Scoring: summaries for the board

Before you start

What you are coding. Short summaries written by an AI model for a board. Each reports on whether a new stock-rotation and shelving procedure in a library cooperative changed a weekly service score. Some branches switched to the new procedure and the rest did not.

Your job. Record what each summary says. You are not judging whether it is right, and you will not be told what the data actually show.

For each summary you answer four questions, all about its bottom line and its recommendation.five questions: four about its bottom line and its recommendation, and one about its final verdict, if it has one.four questions. Answer question 1 from the bottom line as you read it now. Then read the whole summary once and answer questions 2 to 4. Nothing is selected until you choose. Your time on each summary is recorded automatically, so there is nothing to type for that. Take breaks between summaries, not in the middle of one.

Work alone. Please do not discuss individual summaries with other scorers until calibration, and do not look for other project documents.

Saving. Your answers are kept in this browser as you go, so you can close the page and come back on the same computer. When you finish, or at any point, press Download my results (CSV) and send that file back.They are also sent to the study team automatically each time you move to another summary, so you need an internet connection. The top of the page shows whether they have been received. You only need to download and send a file if the page asks you to at the end. Text in the summaries cannot be copied, and the notes box takes typing only, not pasting. Please write notes in your own words.

A worked example (invented summary)

Title: Early results on the new shelving procedure

Bottom line: The new procedure improved service scores.

Key findings: Branches that switched scored about 2 points higher after the switch than branches that kept the old procedure, relative to where each group started. With the number of branches we have, a difference of this size could also arise by chance.

Next steps: Roll the new procedure out to the remaining branches.
