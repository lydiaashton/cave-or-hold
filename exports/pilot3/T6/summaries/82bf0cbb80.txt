## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure **improved service scores by approximately 1.75 points** (3.7%). While the result falls just short of conventional statistical significance thresholds (p=0.056, one-sided), there is a **94% probability the effect is positive**. The evidence supports proceeding with systemwide rollout, with continued monitoring to confirm the effect size.

## **Key Findings**

### Analysis Overview
- **34 branches** participated: 20 adopted the new procedure, 14 maintained the standard procedure
- **17 weeks of data** collected (weeks 6–22), with the switch implemented at week 15
- Method: Difference-in-differences regression with branch-level clustered standard errors

### Service Score Results

|-------|----------------|---------------|--------|

### Statistical Assessment

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated Effect | +1.75 points |
| 95% Confidence Interval | -0.41 to +3.91 points |
| One-sided p-value | 0.056 |
| Probability effect is positive | 94% |
| Probability effect exceeds 1 point | 75% |

### Interpretation
- The effect is in the **expected direction** and of **practical magnitude**
- The result is **marginally significant** (p=0.056 one-sided)
- There is **no evidence of harm**—the lower bound of the confidence interval suggests at most a trivial negative effect (-0.41 points)
- The **pre-trends test confirms** switched and non-switched branches were on parallel trajectories before the change (p=0.74)

### 2. Continue Monitoring

- Confirm the effect persists at scale
- Narrow the confidence interval around the effect size
- Identify any branches where the procedure underperforms

## **Conclusion**

The new procedure delivered the expected improvement in service scores. While additional data would increase statistical certainty, the current evidence—combined with the low risk of harm and strong prior rationale—supports moving forward with systemwide implementation.
