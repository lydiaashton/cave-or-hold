# **REVISED MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD OF DIRECTORS**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure  

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The evidence is inconclusive.** While branches with the new procedure showed higher service scores after implementation, **we cannot confidently attribute this improvement to the procedure itself.** Branches selected for early adoption were already improving faster than other branches before the switch. This selection bias means the observed ~2 point improvement could be entirely due to pre-existing differences rather than the new procedure. **We recommend gathering additional evidence before system-wide rollout.**

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### What We Found

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|---------------|---------------|--------|
| Branches keeping old procedure | 47.8 | 50.0 | +2.2 |
| Branches with new procedure | 48.8 | 52.9 | +4.1 |
| **Observed difference** | | | **+1.9** |

### The Critical Problem: Selection Bias

**Treatment branches were already improving faster before the switch:**
- In the pre-switch period, treatment branches gained **0.24 points per week more** than control branches (p = 0.0003)
- Over 8 post-switch weeks, this pre-existing trend alone would produce **~1.9 points** of apparent improvement
- **This essentially explains the entire observed effect**

This violates the fundamental assumption needed to claim the procedure caused the improvement.

### What's Somewhat Reassuring

- The effect was **not immediate** – it only became statistically significant 6-8 weeks after implementation
- If the improvement were purely due to pre-existing trends, we would expect significant effects right away
- This delayed pattern suggests *some* of the effect may be real, but we cannot quantify how much

---

## **REVISED RECOMMENDATIONS**

### 1. **Do Not Proceed with Full Rollout Yet**
The current evidence does not support a confident system-wide implementation. The observed benefits may reflect branch selection rather than procedure effectiveness.

### 2. **Run a Randomized Trial**
To get credible evidence:
- Randomly assign remaining branches to adopt the procedure (e.g., by lottery)
- This eliminates selection bias
- Track outcomes for 8-12 weeks
- This would provide defensible evidence for or against rollout

### 3. **Alternatively: Phased Rollout with Rigorous Monitoring**
If a randomized trial is not feasible:
- Roll out in additional waves over time
- Compare each new wave to branches not yet converted
- Use staggered adoption to strengthen causal inference
- Set clear success metrics before proceeding

### 4. **Gather Qualitative Evidence**
Interview staff at the 425 early-adopter branches:
- Do they perceive the procedure as beneficial?
- What operational challenges or benefits have they experienced?
- This can complement quantitative evidence

---

## **WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE BOARD**

The library cooperative invested in developing and testing a new procedure with the expectation it would improve service. Unfortunately, **the way branches were selected for early adoption makes it impossible to tell if the procedure actually works.** This is a common problem in program evaluation – not a failure of analysis.

**The responsible course is to obtain cleaner evidence before committing resources to system-wide implementation.** A randomized rollout would take minimal additional time but would give the board confidence that any rollout decision is based on sound evidence.
