**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

## REVISED BOTTOM LINE

**The data do NOT support rolling out the new procedure system-wide.**

The initial analysis suggested a +1.84 point improvement, but this reflected **pre-existing differences between branches**, not a causal effect of the procedure. When we properly account for these differences:

- There was **no immediate effect** when the procedure switched (week 15)
- Post-switch effects are **small and mostly not statistically significant**
- After extrapolating pre-existing trends, branches actually performed **worse than expected** (-1.2 points, though not significant)

**Recommendation: Do NOT proceed with system-wide rollout.** If the board wishes to evaluate this procedure, conduct a proper randomized controlled trial.

---

## KEY FINDINGS

### 1. The Critical Problem: Pre-Existing Differences

Branches selected for the new procedure were **already on a different trajectory** before the switch:

| Week Relative to Switch | Effect (vs. Control) | Statistical Significance |
|------------------------|---------------------|-------------------------|
| Week -8 | -2.47 points | p < 0.001 |
| Week -6 | -1.84 points | p = 0.001 |
| Week -4 | -0.93 points | p = 0.069 |
| Week -2 | -0.51 points | p = 0.185 |

The gap was **narrowing systematically** before the switch (R² = 0.98). This violates the core assumption needed for causal inference.

### 2. No Clear Effect After Switch

| Week Relative to Switch | Effect | P-value |
|------------------------|--------|---------|
| Week 0 (first week after) | +0.49 | 0.206 |
| Week 1 | +0.44 | 0.336 |

**Only weeks 7-8 show statistically significant effects**, and these are small (+1.2 to +1.5 points) and could reflect noise or continuation of pre-existing trends.

## WHY THE INITIAL ANALYSIS WAS MISLEADING

The standard Difference-in-Differences estimate of +1.84 points assumed:
> "Switched and control branches would have followed parallel trends if not for the procedure"

**This assumption is violated.** The event study shows switched branches were already improving faster before the switch. The +1.84 estimate captures this pre-existing advantage, not the procedure's effect.
