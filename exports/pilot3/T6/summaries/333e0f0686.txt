# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 14, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Rotation Procedure  

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence is suggestive but not conclusive.** Branches that switched to the new procedure showed modest service score improvements relative to branches that did not, but the result falls just short of conventional statistical confidence (p=0.09). The 95% confidence interval ranges from -0.3 to +4.1 points, meaning the true effect could be negligible or moderately positive. 

**Recommendation:** Do **not** proceed with immediate systemwide rollout. Instead, **extend the pilot** for 8–12 more weeks to gather additional data. If costs are low and disruption minimal, consider gradual expansion with continued monitoring. If implementation is costly or disruptive, maintain the current mixed approach until evidence is stronger.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Main Result: Positive but Not Statistically Confirmed**

| Metric | Value | Interpretation |
|--------|-------|----------------|
| Estimated effect | **+1.9 points** | Modest improvement |
| Baseline service score | 51.2 | — |
| Relative improvement | 3.7% | Small effect (0.2 SD) |
| P-value | 0.088 | Above 0.05 threshold |
| 95% confidence interval | **-0.3 to +4.1** | Includes zero |

**What this means:** There is approximately a 9% probability of observing this result if the true effect is zero. By conventional scientific standards (5% threshold), this does not constitute sufficient evidence to confirm the procedure works.

### **2. Encouraging Signal: Effect Strengthens Over Time**

| Period | Estimated Effect | P-value |
|--------|-----------------|---------|
| All post-switch weeks (15–22) | +1.9 points | 0.09 |
| Later post-period only (17–22) | **+2.8 points** | **0.03** ✓ |

This pattern is consistent with branches needing time to adapt. However, this is a post-hoc subset analysis and should be confirmed with additional data rather than treated as definitive.

### **3. Control Group Declined Slightly**

| Group | Before | After | Change |
|-------|--------|-------|--------|
| Control (no switch) | 51.36 | 50.67 | **-0.69** |
| Treatment (switched) | 51.10 | 52.20 | **+1.09** |

The treatment effect emerges because switching branches improved while non-switching branches declined slightly. This could reflect seasonal factors, regression to the mean, or other confounding variables.

### **4. Study Limitations**

- **Limited post-switch observations:** Only 8 weeks of post-implementation data
- **Marginal statistical power:** 34 branches with 17 weeks of data provides moderate but not strong power to detect small effects
- **Unknown implementation costs:** Analysis does not account for staff training time, disruption costs, or resource requirements

---

## **Recommendations**

### **Primary Recommendation: Extend the Pilot**

1. **Continue data collection for 8–12 additional weeks** — This will approximately double the post-switch observations and provide greater statistical confidence.

2. **Maintain current mixed approach** — Keep 14 branches on the old procedure as a continuing control group during the extended pilot.

3. **Pre-register decision criteria** — Before collecting more data, establish clear thresholds (e.g., "Roll out if effect is +2 points with p<0.05") to avoid moving goalposts.

### **Conditional Actions**

4. **If implementation costs are low:** Consider gradually converting 5–10 additional branches while monitoring, accepting that evidence is incomplete but directionally positive.

5. **If implementation costs are high:** Maintain the status quo until stronger evidence emerges. A 3–4% improvement may not justify significant expense or disruption.

6. **Document implementation variations:** Track which branches implement the procedure most faithfully to enable future analysis of best practices.

---

## **Decision Framework for the Board**

| If the Board prioritizes... | Then... |
|----------------------------|---------|
| **Certainty before action** | Extend pilot; do not expand yet |
| **Potential upside with low risk** | Gradual expansion with monitoring |
| **Cost minimization** | Maintain status quo until evidence strengthens |
| **Innovation and improvement** | Proceed with rollout, but set clear evaluation checkpoints |

---

## **Conclusion**

The data leans positive but does not meet the standard of evidence typically required for systemwide policy changes. The responsible course is to gather more data before committing all 34 branches to the new procedure. This protects the cooperative from adopting an ineffective change while preserving the option to proceed if additional evidence confirms the initial positive signal.
