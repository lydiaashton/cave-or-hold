# **Revised Memorandum to the Board**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure  
**Date:** November 14, 2026  
**Prepared by:** Data Analysis Team

---

## **Bottom Line**

**We cannot confidently conclude the new procedure caused the improvement.** While treatment branches showed higher scores after the switch, they were already improving faster than control branches *before* the switch. This suggests the branches that adopted the new procedure may have been systematically different (better managed, more resources, or more motivated). **We recommend additional analysis before approving a system-wide rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **What the Data Shows**

| | Treatment Branches | Control Branches |
|---|---|---|
| Number of Branches | 425 | 284 |
| Score Before Switch | 49.8 | 48.2 |
| Score After Switch | 53.3 | 49.6 |
| **Raw Change** | **+3.5** | **+1.4** |

The statistical model attributes **+2.1 points** to the new procedure. **However, this estimate relies on an assumption that does not hold.**

### **The Problem: Pre-Existing Differences**

The gap between treatment and control branches was **already widening before the switch**:

| Week | Gap (Treatment − Control) |
|------|---------------------------|
| 6 | 0.45 points |
| 10 | 1.31 points |
| 14 (week before switch) | 2.68 points |
| 22 (final week) | 4.36 points |

**The gap grew by 2.23 points BEFORE the intervention.** This means treatment branches were already on a better trajectory. The branches that chose to switch may have been:
- Better managed
- Better staffed
- More motivated to improve
- Serving different communities

### **What This Means**

The standard analysis **likely overstates** the treatment effect. The true impact of the new procedure could be:
- Smaller than 2.1 points
- Zero
- Even negative (if the pre-treatment trend would have continued without the procedure)

---

## **Recommendation**

**Do NOT approve system-wide rollout at this time.** Instead:

1. **Investigate why** certain branches chose to adopt the new procedure and others did not
2. **Conduct a randomized trial** where branches are randomly assigned to treatment/control
3. **Gather qualitative feedback** from the 425 treatment branches about their experience
4. **Re-analyze** with methods that account for the pre-existing differences (e.g., synthetic control, matching)

## **Bottom Line (Restated)**

The data shows correlation, not causation. Branches that adopted the new procedure improved more, but they were already improving faster before adoption. **We need stronger evidence before committing resources to a system-wide rollout.**
