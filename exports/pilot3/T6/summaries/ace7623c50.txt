## **Bottom Line**

**Do not proceed with full rollout yet.** While service scores did improve at branches that adopted the new procedure, **we cannot confidently attribute this improvement to the procedure change itself.** The evidence suggests branches that switched were already improving faster before the change. More rigorous testing is needed before committing resources to a system-wide rollout.

---

## **Critical Concerns**

### 1. **Parallel Trends Assumption Violated**

The difference-in-differences method requires that treatment and control branches would have followed similar trajectories absent the intervention. **This assumption does not hold:**

| Period | Gap Widening Rate |
|--------|-------------------|
| Before switch (weeks 6–14) | +0.26 points/week |
| After switch (weeks 15–22) | +0.24 points/week |

**The gap was already widening before the switch.** The rate of improvement did not meaningfully change after the procedure was adopted.

### 2. **Placebo Test Fails**

When we pretend the switch happened at week 12 (instead of week 15), we find an identical "effect":

| Test | Estimated Effect |
|------|------------------|
| Actual switch (week 15) | +2.05 points |
| Placebo switch (week 12) | +2.09 points |

**If the effect appears even when no change occurred, it likely reflects pre-existing differences, not the procedure.**

### 3. **Adjusted Estimate: Essentially Zero**

Accounting for the pre-existing trend:
- Pre-trend: 0.26 points/week × 8 weeks = **2.08 points**
- Raw DiD estimate: **2.05 points**
- **Adjusted effect: -0.03 points**

After removing the pre-existing trend, **there is no detectable treatment effect.**

---

## **Recommendation**

### **Pause the rollout and conduct a randomized trial**

1. **Randomly assign** remaining branches to treatment and control groups
2. **Collect baseline data** for at least 8 weeks before implementation
3. **Verify parallel trends** before proceeding
4. **Monitor for 12+ weeks** post-implementation to assess sustained effects

### **If proceeding anyway, do so cautiously**

- Phase implementation gradually
- Track service scores closely at each newly converted branch
- Be prepared to halt rollout if expected gains do not materialize
- Document implementation costs to assess cost-effectiveness

---

## **Why This Matters**

Rolling out a procedure that does not actually improve service would:
- Waste staff time and training resources
- Disrupt operations without benefit
- Make it harder to identify what actually drives service quality

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Discuss these findings** with the operations team that designed the procedure
2. **Identify why** switched branches were already improving faster (better management? more resources?)
3. **Design a randomized evaluation** before further rollout
4. **Consider alternative metrics** beyond service scores to assess procedure impact

---
