# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

**Prepared for the Library Cooperative Board**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure is associated with higher service scores, but with important caveats.** Our analysis finds a **2-point improvement** (approximately 4%) associated with the new procedure. However, this is observational data—not a randomized trial—and the effect is driven partly by declining scores at branches that kept the old procedure. The evidence suggests the new procedure is beneficial, but we recommend a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than immediate system-wide adoption.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. What the Data Shows

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|---------------|---------------|--------|
| New Procedure (90 branches) | 50.25 | 51.02 | +0.77 |
| Old Procedure (60 branches) | 50.96 | 49.71 | −1.25 |

**Estimated effect: +2.01 points** (95% CI: 0.77 to 3.25)

**Important context:** The treated branches improved modestly (+0.77). The control branches declined (−1.25). The 2-point "effect" reflects both trends.

### 2. Statistical Confidence

- The effect is **statistically significant** (p = 0.0015)
- However, the effect size is **modest**: about 0.2 standard deviations
- Pre-trend analysis shows no significant differences between groups before the switch (p = 0.47), which supports the validity of the comparison

### 3. Important Limitations

| Concern | Implication |
|---------|-------------|
| **Not randomized** | Branches were not randomly assigned to the new procedure. Unobserved differences (e.g., more motivated staff, better management) could explain part of the effect. |
| **Control group decline** | Much of the estimated benefit comes from the control group getting worse, not the treated group getting dramatically better. Something may have negatively affected control branches. |
| **Short follow-up** | Only 8 weeks of post-switch data. We don't know if the effect persists long-term. |
| **Modest effect size** | A 4% improvement may or may not justify rollout costs, depending on implementation expenses. |

### 4. What We Can and Cannot Conclude

| We CAN say | We CANNOT say |
|------------|---------------|
| Branches using the new procedure had higher scores after the switch | The new procedure *caused* the improvement with certainty |
| The association is statistically reliable | The effect will be exactly 2 points at every branch |
| Pre-trends were similar between groups | There are no unobserved differences between groups |

---

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with a phased rollout, not immediate system-wide adoption.**

1. **Expand to 20–30 additional branches** over the next quarter
2. **Monitor closely** to confirm the effect replicates
3. **Track implementation costs** (training, staff time, any equipment)
4. **Gather qualitative feedback** from branch staff about what works and what doesn't
5. **Reconvene in 12 weeks** to decide on full rollout based on new evidence

## **Why This Approach?**

Given the **non-randomized design** and **modest effect size**, a cautious expansion allows us to:
- Confirm the effect replicates in new branches
- Identify implementation challenges before full rollout
- Build a stronger business case with cost data
- Minimize risk if the effect proves smaller at scale
