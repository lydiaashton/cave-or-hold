# **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure produced a small but real improvement in service scores.** Branches that switched saw scores increase by approximately **1.9 points** (about **3.7%**) relative to branches that kept the old procedure. The effect is statistically significant (p = 0.003) and persists after accounting for staffing and visitor volume.

**Recommendation:** The evidence supports rolling out the new procedure, though expectations should be calibrated—the benefit is real but modest, not transformative.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measured Impact
| Metric | Value | Interpretation |
|--------|-------|----------------|
| **Service score improvement** | +1.86 points | Statistically significant |
| **95% Confidence interval** | 0.65 to 3.07 points | Effect is positive, but modest |
| **Effect size** | 0.18 standard deviations | Small-to-moderate |
| **Relative to other factors** | ~3.5× the impact of typical staffing variation | Meaningful in operational context |

### 2. Why We're Confident
- **Parallel pre-trends:** Treatment and control groups moved together before the switch (p = 0.801)
- **Robust analysis:** Results hold with cluster-robust standard errors and controls for staffing/visits
- **Clean design:** 68 branches switched; 82 did not—providing a valid counterfactual

### 3. Context Matters
- Week-to-week score variation (≈3 points) is larger than the treatment effect
- The control group's scores declined slightly after week 15 (−0.62 points); the treatment group's rose (+1.19 points)
- The new procedure appears to have **prevented decline** as much as it drove improvement

| Priority | Action | Rationale |

## **What This Means**

The board's expectation—that this change would not materially affect service scores—was not unreasonable. Operational procedures often have negligible patron-facing impact. However, the data show a **small, reliable benefit**. This suggests:

1. **Back-office changes can ripple forward** to service quality, even when not directly patron-facing
2. **Modest gains accumulate**—this improvement could combine with other initiatives
3. **Testing before scaling** allowed us to detect an effect we might have otherwise dismissed

---

## **Technical Notes**

- **Method:** Difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors (by branch)
- **Controls:** Staffing index, visits index
- **Data:** 2,550 observations, 150 branches, 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- **Pre-trend test:** p = 0.801 (no differential pre-trends detected)
