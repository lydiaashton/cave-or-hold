# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence is promising but not conclusive.** While branches that switched show higher service scores (+1.8 points), **the treatment and control groups were on different trajectories even before the switch**, which undermines confidence that the procedure alone caused the improvement. We recommend a **cautious, phased rollout** with continued monitoring rather than immediate system-wide adoption.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Positive Association, But Causality Uncertain**
- Switching branches show **+1.84 points higher** service scores after the change
- However, **pre-trend analysis reveals a problem**: the treatment group was already improving faster than the control group *before* the switch (p < 0.001)
- This violates a key assumption of the analysis, meaning **some of the observed improvement may reflect pre-existing differences**, not the procedure itself

### **2. Effect Strengthens Over Time**
| Week | Effect Size | Statistical Significance |
|------|-------------|-------------------------|
| 15-17 | +1.2 to +1.4 points | Not significant (p > 0.05) |
| 18-22 | +1.8 to +2.7 points | Significant (p < 0.05) |

The effect was not immediate, which could indicate a learning curve—or continued divergence from pre-existing trends.

### **3. Effect Size Is Modest**
- **3.6% improvement** from baseline
- **Small effect size** (Cohen's d = 0.19)
- Context: The standard deviation of scores is 9.6 points; the effect is about 1/5 of one standard deviation

### **4. Benefits Are Not Universal**
- **66% of switching branches improved**; **34% saw no improvement or declined**
- **Lower-performing branches benefited more** (+2.2 points) than higher-performing branches (+1.0 points)
- This suggests the procedure may be most valuable for struggling branches

### **5. Study Limitations**
- Only **8 weeks of post-switch data**—we don't know if effects persist
- Branches were **not randomly assigned** to switch; selection bias may explain results
- **No cost data**—we cannot assess whether benefits justify implementation costs

---

## **Next Steps**

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| 1 | **Pause immediate system-wide rollout** | Pre-trend violation means we cannot confidently attribute gains to the procedure |
| 2 | **Extend monitoring period** to 6-12 months | Determine if effects persist or were temporary |
| 3 | **Target rollout to lower-performing branches first** | They show larger gains; higher-performing branches may not benefit enough to justify change |
| 4 | **Conduct randomized pilot** in 50-100 additional branches | Random assignment would provide stronger causal evidence |
| 5 | **Collect implementation cost data** | Enable cost-benefit analysis before full commitment |

---

## **Recommendation**

**Do not proceed with immediate system-wide rollout.** The pre-existing differences between switching and non-switching branches mean we cannot rule out that factors other than the procedure caused the observed improvement. A more rigorous evaluation—either through extended monitoring or a randomized pilot—would provide the evidence needed for a confident system-wide decision.
