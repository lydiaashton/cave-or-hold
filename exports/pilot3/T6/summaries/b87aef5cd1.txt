# **Revised Memorandum to the Board of Directors**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 14, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Proceed with caution.** Branches that adopted the new procedure have higher service scores, **but we cannot confidently attribute this entirely to the procedure itself.** Treatment branches were already improving faster than control branches *before* they switched, which clouds the causal interpretation. We recommend a **phased, monitored rollout** rather than immediate system-wide adoption, with continued evaluation to isolate the true effect.

---

## **Key Findings**

### What the Data Shows

| Finding | Implication |
|---------|-------------|
| Treatment branches score ~2 points higher after switching | **Positive association** with the new procedure |
| Treatment branches were already gaining 0.28 points/week MORE than control *before* the switch (p < 0.001) | **Parallel trends assumption violated** – causal claim is weakened |
| Pre-trend divergence could explain ~1.1 of the 1.9 point observed effect | **True effect may be as low as 0.75 points** (or potentially zero) |
| Substantial variation in treatment branch outcomes (25th percentile: 46; 75th percentile: 59) | **Not all branches benefit equally** – some may see little or no improvement |

### The Core Problem

The difference-in-differences method relies on the assumption that treatment and control branches would have followed similar trends absent the intervention. **This assumption does not hold here.** Treatment branches were on an upward trajectory before the switch, suggesting:

- Branches selected for early adoption may have been higher-performing or more motivated
- Unobserved factors (management quality, staffing stability, local demographics) may drive both the decision to switch AND service score improvements
- The observed ~2 point gain likely **overstates** the true causal effect of the procedure

---

## **Recommendation**

**Do not proceed with immediate system-wide rollout.** Instead:

1. **Randomize the next phase:** Randomly assign remaining branches to switch now vs. later. This will restore the ability to make causal claims.

2. **Implement with enhanced monitoring:** Track each branch weekly for 8-12 weeks post-switch. Flag branches that don't show improvement within 4 weeks.

3. **Investigate selection factors:** Understand what distinguished early-adopter branches. Were they better resourced? More motivated? This will help predict which remaining branches will benefit most.

4. **Set realistic expectations:** If the true effect is 0.75 points rather than 1.9 points, the improvement is still positive but more modest (~1.5% rather than ~4%).

---

## **Bottom Line (Restated)**

The new procedure is **associated with** higher service scores, and there is likely **some genuine benefit**. However, the evidence does not support the claim that *all* branches will see a ~2 point improvement. A more careful, evidence-based rollout will protect the cooperative from investing in a change that may deliver smaller benefits than expected—or that may not work uniformly across all branch types.
