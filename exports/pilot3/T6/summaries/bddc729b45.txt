# **LIBRARY COOPERATIVE BOARD MEMORANDUM**

---

## **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

**Date:** November 14, 2026  
**Analysis Period:** Weeks 6–22 (17 weeks)  
**Procedure Switch:** Week 15

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

The new procedure shows a **promising but not statistically conclusive** effect. Service scores improved by **2.2 points (4.2%)** in switched branches relative to control branches, but this result could plausibly be due to chance (p = 0.13). The evidence **does not definitively prove** the procedure works as expected. We recommend **continuing the rollout with explicit success metrics** and a commitment to re-evaluate after 12 weeks of system-wide data.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **What the Data Shows**

| Period | Switched Branches | Control Branches |
|--------|------------------|------------------|
| Before switch (Weeks 6–14) | 52.83 | 51.34 |
| After switch (Weeks 15–22) | 53.61 | 49.95 |
| **Change** | **+0.79** | **-1.40** |

**Net Effect: +2.19 points** (Difference-in-Differences)

### **What the Statistics Tell Us**

| Measure | Value | Interpretation |
|---------|-------|----------------|
| Effect estimate | +2.19 points | Promising direction |
| P-value | 0.13 | **Not statistically significant** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | -0.62 to +4.99 | **Includes zero** – cannot rule out no effect |

### **Branch-Level Reality**

- **9 of 20** switched branches (45%) showed improvement
- **11 of 20** switched branches (55%) showed no improvement or decline
- Control branches declined on average, suggesting external factors may have affected scores system-wide

### **Study Limitations**

- Only **8 weeks** of post-switch data (limited statistical power)
- **34 branches** total (modest sample size)
- High score variability (standard deviation ~10 points)
- The study may be **underpowered** to detect a real but modest effect

---

## **RECOMMENDATIONS**

### **1. Proceed with Cautious Expansion**

The positive direction of results, combined with low risk of harm, supports continuing implementation. However, the board should understand:

- This is **not a proven success** – it's a promising pilot
- The true effect could be zero, or as high as +5 points
- Continued monitoring is essential, not optional

### **2. Define Success Criteria Before Rollout**

The system-wide rollout should be evaluated against clear metrics:

| Metric | Success Threshold | Timeline |
|--------|------------------|----------|
| Average service score increase | +1.5 points or more | 12 weeks post-rollout |
| Branches showing improvement | 60% or more | 12 weeks post-rollout |
| No branch decline >3 points | All branches | 12 weeks post-rollout |

### **3. Commit to Re-evaluation**

Schedule a board review **12 weeks after full implementation** to:

- Confirm the effect persists at scale
- Identify branches needing additional support
- Decide whether to continue, modify, or discontinue the procedure

### **4. Investigate Variation**

Since only 45% of switched branches improved, we recommend:

- Identifying characteristics of branches that benefited most
- Understanding why some branches saw declines
- Developing targeted implementation support

---

## **CONCLUSION**

The data provides **encouraging but inconclusive** evidence that the new procedure improves service scores. The 2.2-point improvement aligns with expectations and carries minimal downside risk. However, the statistical uncertainty means the board should proceed with **measured optimism and clear accountability** rather than treating this as a confirmed success.
