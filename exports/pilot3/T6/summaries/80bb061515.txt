# **REVISED BOARD SUMMARY: Library Branch Procedure Change Analysis**

---

## **TITLE: New Procedure Protects Service Scores During System-Wide Decline**

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure had a real effect—contrary to expectations of no difference.** However, the effect is more nuanced than "improvement." Service scores declined system-wide after week 15. Branches using the new procedure **maintained** their scores (−0.12 points) while control branches **declined** (−1.78 points). The net benefit is **+1.66 points** (p=0.003). 

**Recommendation: Proceed with rollout, but with tempered expectations.** The procedure protects against decline rather than driving improvement. This is still valuable, but the board should understand what the data actually shows.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **What Actually Happened**

| | New Procedure | Old Procedure |
|---|---|---|
| Before switch (weeks 6–14) | 49.91 | 53.34 |
| After switch (weeks 15–22) | 49.79 | 51.57 |
| **Change** | **−0.12** | **−1.78** |

**Net effect: +1.66 points relative to control** (95% CI: 0.57 to 2.74)

### **Critical Context**

1. **Treatment branches started lower.** Before the switch, treatment branches averaged 3.43 points below control branches. This suggests non-random assignment—possibly struggling branches were prioritized for the new procedure.

2. **Something caused system-wide decline.** Both groups faced downward pressure after week 15. The new procedure helped branches resist this pressure, but did not reverse it.

3. **The effect is statistically real.** P-value of 0.003 means we can confidently reject the "no difference" hypothesis.

4. **But absolute scores didn't improve.** No branch group saw gains. The benefit is relative protection, not absolute improvement.

### **Why the Original Expectation Was Wrong**

The board expected no difference. The data shows there **is** a difference—the procedure provides a protective buffer during adverse conditions. This is meaningful, just not what was anticipated.

## **RECOMMENDATION**

**Proceed with rollout, but reframe expectations.**

The new procedure is a **defensive tool**, not an offensive improvement. It helps branches maintain service quality when external factors push scores down. This has value, particularly if the system-wide decline reflects ongoing challenges (seasonal patterns, staffing pressures, budget constraints, etc.).

**However**, if the board's goal is to *raise* service scores above baseline levels, this procedure alone will not achieve that. It should be part of a broader strategy.
