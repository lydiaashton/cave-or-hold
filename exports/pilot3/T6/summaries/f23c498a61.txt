**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure  

---

## **Executive Summary**

**Recommendation: Do NOT proceed with full rollout based on current evidence. The data is inconclusive.**

While the statistical model shows a +1.9 point difference between switched and control branches, this result **cannot be confidently attributed to the new procedure**. The key finding is that switched branches remained stable (+0.35 points) while control branches declined (-1.50 points). The parallel trends assumption required for causal inference is violated (p=0.014), meaning switched branches were already on a different trajectory before the switch. We recommend additional investigation before making a rollout decision.

---

## **Bottom Line**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did the new procedure change service scores? | **Uncertain** |
| Direction of change? | **Unclear** – switched branches stayed flat; controls declined |
| Statistically reliable? | **No** – parallel trends assumption violated |
| Recommend full rollout? | **No** – evidence is inconclusive |

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. The Parallel Trends Assumption Is Violated

Difference-in-differences analysis requires that treatment and control groups would have followed similar trajectories absent the intervention. Our data shows this assumption does not hold:

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Pre-switch trend difference** | 0.24 points/week |
| **P-value** | 0.014 |
| **Interpretation** | Switched branches were already improving relative to controls BEFORE the switch |

This undermines causal claims about the procedure's effect.

### 2. No Clear Discontinuity at Implementation

If the new procedure caused improvement, we would expect a jump in relative performance at Week 15. Instead:

| Week | Gap (Switched − Control) |
|------|--------------------------|
| Week 14 (last pre-switch) | +0.99 |
| Week 15 (first post-switch) | +0.69 |
| **Change at switch point** | **-0.30** |

The gap actually **narrowed** immediately after the switch, not widened.

### 3. The "Effect" Is Driven by Control Branches Declining

| Group | Before | After | Change |
|-------|--------|-------|--------|
| **Switched Branches** | 51.35 | 51.70 | **+0.35** (essentially flat) |
| **Control Branches** | 51.15 | 49.65 | **-1.50** (notable decline) |

The statistical "treatment effect" primarily reflects control branches getting worse, not switched branches getting better. **We do not know why control branches declined** – it could be seasonal factors, staffing issues, or other unmeasured changes.

### 4. Event Study Shows Gradual Divergence, Not Treatment Effect

Post-switch coefficients from the event study:

| Week | Coefficient | P-value |
|------|-------------|---------|
| 15 | -0.30 | 0.777 |
| 16 | 0.65 | 0.545 |
| 17 | 0.89 | 0.405 |
| 18 | 1.03 | 0.335 |
| 19 | 1.66 | 0.120 |
| 20 | 1.88 | 0.079 |
| 21 | 1.39 | 0.193 |
| 22 | 1.33 | 0.213 |

**None of the post-switch weeks show statistically significant effects** (all p>0.05). The gradual widening could reflect continuing pre-existing trends rather than a treatment effect.

### 5. Pre-Switch Pattern Suggests Convergence, Not Divergence

Before the switch, switched branches started ~1.85 points behind controls (Week 6) and nearly caught up by Week 14. This convergence trend could naturally continue post-switch without any intervention effect.

---

## **What We Cannot Rule Out**

1. **Selection bias:** Branches that switched may have been systematically different (e.g., better managed, more motivated staff, different patron demographics).

2. **Confounding factors:** Something else may have caused control branches to decline (regional issues, staffing changes, seasonal patterns affecting certain branch types).

3. **No true effect:** The new procedure may have no impact on service scores, and the observed pattern reflects pre-existing differences.

4. **Small positive effect:** The procedure may provide modest benefit, but the current data cannot distinguish this from confounding.

---

## **Recommended Next Steps**

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| 1 | **Pause full rollout** | Current evidence does not support a cooperative-wide decision |
| 2 | **Investigate control branch decline** | Understand why non-switched branches dropped 1.5 points – this may be the more important issue |
| 3 | **Conduct qualitative interviews** | Speak with managers at switched and control branches about implementation experience, staffing, and challenges |
| 4 | **Consider a randomized trial** | If causal evidence is needed, randomly assign remaining branches to treatment/control |
| 5 | **Extend observation period** | Collect 6-12 more months of data to see if patterns persist or clarify |
| 6 | **Phased rollout with evaluation** | If proceeding, do so in stages with rigorous monitoring to build better evidence |

---

## **Conclusion**

The data does not provide reliable evidence that the new stock rotation procedure improves service scores. The apparent benefit is driven by control branches declining rather than switched branches improving, and the statistical assumptions required for causal inference are not met. 

**We recommend the Board defer a full rollout decision** until additional investigation clarifies (a) why control branches declined, and (b) whether the new procedure has any causal effect on service quality.
