# **MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 14, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure shows a measurable positive effect on service scores, but the Board should weigh this against implementation costs before approving system-wide rollout.** Branches using the new procedure achieved service scores approximately **1.9 points higher** (3.6% improvement) than comparable branches using the old procedure. This finding is statistically reliable. However, we do not have data on implementation costs, staff time, or long-term sustainability—factors the Board should consider before making a final decision.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### What We Studied
- **150 branches** across the cooperative, tracked weekly from week 6 through week 22
- **90 branches** implemented the new procedure starting week 15; **60 branches** maintained the old procedure
- Service scores average approximately 50 points (higher scores indicate better service)

### What We Found

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|----------------|---------------|--------|
| Branches keeping old procedure | 51.32 | 50.70 | −0.62 |
| Branches using new procedure | 50.32 | 51.51 | +1.19 |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | | **+1.81** |

- **Estimated impact: +1.86 service score points** (95% confidence interval: 0.65 to 3.07)
- **Statistical confidence: 99.7%** (p = 0.003)
- Results remain consistent after accounting for staffing levels, visitor volume, and branch-specific characteristics

### Study Limitations
- **Short follow-up period:** Only 8 weeks of post-implementation data; long-term effects are unknown
- **Observational design:** Branches were not randomly assigned to switch; however, pre-switch trends were parallel, supporting the validity of comparisons
- **No cost data:** We cannot assess whether the service score improvement justifies any additional labor, training, or operational costs
- **Unexplained variation:** Service scores vary substantially across branches for reasons unrelated to the procedure change

---

## **RECOMMENDATION**

**The evidence supports proceeding with rollout, contingent on a cost-benefit analysis.** The service score improvement is real and statistically robust. Before approving system-wide implementation, the Board should:

1. Confirm that implementation costs are reasonable relative to the 3.6% service improvement
2. Verify that staff capacity exists to support the transition at remaining branches
3. Consider a phased rollout to monitor for any unintended consequences
