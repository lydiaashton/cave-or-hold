# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Do NOT proceed with system-wide rollout yet.** While service scores improved more at switched branches than control branches, the analysis reveals a critical methodological problem: branches that switched were **already improving faster before the procedure change**. This means we cannot confidently attribute the improvement to the new procedure itself. The observed effect may reflect pre-existing differences between branches rather than a causal impact of the procedure.

**Recommendation:** Gather more evidence before making a rollout decision.

---

## **Key Findings**

### The Core Problem: Non-Parallel Pre-Trends

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Switched branches' pre-switch trend | +0.46 points/week |
| Control branches' pre-switch trend | +0.21 points/week |
| **Difference** | **+0.25 points/week** |
| Statistical significance | p = 0.011 |

**What this means:** Switched branches were already on a steeper improvement trajectory before Week 15. If this trend continued naturally, it would account for approximately **2.0 of the 2.1 points** we observed post-switch—leaving little to no effect attributable to the procedure itself.

### Study Limitations

1. **Selection bias:** Branches that switched may differ systematically from those that didn't (e.g., more motivated leadership, better resources, different patron demographics)
2. **Short observation window:** Only 8 weeks post-switch; effects may change over time
3. **No random assignment:** The quasi-experimental design relies on assumptions that the data does not support

## **Next Steps**

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| 1 | **Pause rollout decision** | Current evidence is insufficient to justify system-wide change |
| 2 | **Investigate why switched branches were different** | Understand what else may have driven their improvement |
| 3 | **Consider a randomized trial** | Randomly assign remaining branches to new vs. old procedure to establish causality |
| 4 | **Collect additional data** | Extend observation period; gather qualitative feedback from staff |
| 5 | **Re-evaluate in 3–6 months** | With better evidence, make an informed rollout decision |

---

## **Conclusion**

The data shows a positive association between the new procedure and service scores, but **association is not causation**. The pre-existing trend difference suggests the 2.1-point estimate likely overstates the true effect—possibly substantially. Rolling out the procedure system-wide based on this evidence risks implementing a change that may not deliver the expected benefits.

A more rigorous evaluation is warranted before committing cooperative-wide resources to the new procedure.
