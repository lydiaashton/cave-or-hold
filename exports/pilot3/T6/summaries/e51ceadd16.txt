# **BOARD MEMORANDUM**

## **Subject: Evaluation of New Shelving & Stock Rotation Procedure**

**Date:** November 2026  
**To:** Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  

---

### **BOTTOM LINE**

**The evidence is inconclusive.** While branches that adopted the new procedure showed higher service scores after implementation, **we cannot confidently attribute this improvement to the procedure itself.** Switched branches were already improving faster than control branches before the change, which undermines our ability to isolate the procedure's effect. We recommend **against a full rollout** until more rigorous evidence is gathered.

---

### **KEY FINDINGS**

**What the Data Shows**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Raw difference-in-differences estimate | +2.30 service score points |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 |
| Switched branches (pre → post) | 50.91 → 52.91 |
| Control branches (pre → post) | 48.88 → 48.59 |

**Critical Limitation: Pre-Existing Trends**

The fundamental assumption for causal inference—that switched and control branches would have followed parallel trajectories absent the intervention—is **violated**:

| Issue | Finding |
|-------|---------|
| Differential pre-trends | **Statistically significant** (p < 0.001) |
| Pre-period gap growth | +0.27 points/week (weeks 6–14) |
| Post-period gap growth | +0.17 points/week (weeks 15–22) |
| **Acceleration attributable to procedure** | **Not detectable** |

The gap between switched and control branches was widening **before** the policy change, suggesting switched branches were on a stronger improvement trajectory regardless of the procedure.

**Possible Explanations**

1. **Selection bias**: Branches that switched may have been systematically different (better management, more motivated staff, favorable demographics)
2. **Volunteer effect**: Branches expecting improvement may have volunteered for the new procedure
3. **Unobserved confounders**: Other simultaneous changes may have affected switched branches differently

---

### **WHAT WE CANNOT CONCLUDE**

❌ We cannot claim the new procedure **caused** the observed improvement  
❌ We cannot estimate the true magnitude of the procedure's effect  
❌ We cannot predict what would happen if all branches adopted the procedure  

---

### **RECOMMENDATIONS & NEXT STEPS**

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| **1** | **Pause system-wide rollout** | Current evidence does not support confident expansion |
| **2** | **Conduct a randomized controlled trial** | Randomly assign branches to new vs. old procedure to eliminate selection bias |
| **3** | **Continue monitoring current switched branches** | Track whether improvements persist or plateau |
| **4** | **Investigate why switched branches were improving faster** | Understand underlying factors before scaling |
| **5** | **Consider qualitative research** | Interview branch staff to understand on-the-ground impacts |

**If Proceeding with Caution:**

Should the Board wish to continue exploration despite the limitations:
- Implement in **small, randomized batches** (not all remaining branches at once)
- Maintain a **control group** for ongoing comparison
- Set **clear success metrics** and **stopping rules**
- Plan for **independent evaluation** at 3, 6, and 12 months

---

### **CONCLUSION**

The observed association between the new procedure and higher service scores is encouraging but **not causally established**. The pre-existing improvement trajectory of switched branches means the 2.3-point estimate likely overstates the procedure's true effect. 

**We recommend investing in more rigorous evaluation before committing resources to system-wide implementation.** A randomized trial with 50–100 branches could provide definitive evidence within 3–6 months.
