## **Bottom Line**

The evidence suggests the new procedure **likely improved service scores by about 2.4 points**, but we **cannot be certain**. The result falls just short of conventional statistical standards (p = 0.095), meaning there's roughly a 10% chance this improvement occurred by random chance rather than due to the procedure.

**Key consideration:** There is **no evidence of harm**, and the cost of adoption is low (procedure change only, no major investment).

**Recommendation:** **Expand to additional branches on a pilot basis** (not full rollout), with rigorous monitoring. If results confirm the initial findings over 6 months, proceed systemwide.

## **Key Findings**

### **1. What the Data Shows**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Branches that switched | 51.6 | 54.7 | +3.1 |
| Branches that did not switch | 49.5 | 50.2 | +0.7 |
| **Estimated effect of new procedure** | | | **+2.4** |

### **2. How Confident Should We Be?**

| Confidence Level | Range of Plausible Effects |
|-----------------|---------------------------|
| 90% confidence | +0.0 to +4.8 points |
| 95% confidence | -0.4 to +5.2 points |

**Interpretation:** At the standard 95% confidence level used in most research, we cannot rule out zero effect—or even a small negative effect. However, the **entire range of plausible values is centered on improvement**, and there is no signal of harm.

### **3. Why We Think the Effect Is Real (Despite Uncertainty)**

- ✓ Treatment and control branches had **parallel trends before the switch**
- ✓ The improvement **emerged after the switch**, not before
- ✓ The effect **persists** across different statistical approaches
- ✓ The gap **widened over time**, suggesting genuine adoption effects
- ✓ **No red flags** in the data that would undermine the comparison

### **4. Why We're Not Fully Confident**

- ✗ Only **8 weeks of post-switch data**—too short for definitive conclusions
- ✗ Only **20 branches** adopted the new procedure—limited statistical power
- ✗ Service scores are **inherently variable** (standard deviation ~10 points)
- ✗ **Unmeasured factors** could have affected switched branches differently after week 15

### **Phase 1: Controlled Expansion (Next 3-6 Months)**

1. **Add 5-7 more branches** to the new procedure (not all 14 remaining)
2. **Stagger the switches** over 4-6 weeks to enable ongoing comparison
3. **Monitor weekly** for the first 8 weeks after each switch
4. **Set a decision checkpoint** at 6 months

### **Phase 2: Decision Point (6 Months)**

| Outcome | Action |
|---------|--------|
| Effect confirmed (≥1.5 pts, consistent) | Proceed with full rollout |
| Effect weak or inconsistent (0-1.5 pts) | Continue monitoring, optional adoption |
| No effect or negative effect | Halt rollout, investigate why |

### **Ongoing**

5. **Document implementation quality** at each branch—variation in execution may explain variation in results

## **Why This Approach?**

| Option | Upside | Downside |
|--------|--------|----------|
| **Full rollout now** | Faster benefits if real | Hard to reverse; may waste effort if no effect |
| **No rollout** | Avoids unnecessary change | Misses potential improvement for 14 branches |

Given the **moderate evidence of benefit** and **low risk of harm**, the pilot expansion balances learning with action.
