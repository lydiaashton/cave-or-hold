# **Revised Board Memorandum**

---

## **Subject: Results of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure Pilot**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**We cannot conclusively determine whether the new procedure improved service scores.** While branches that switched showed better outcomes than those that didn't, the data reveals a critical issue: **the switched branches were already outperforming before the change occurred.** This makes it impossible to separate the procedure's effect from pre-existing differences between branches.

**Recommendation: Proceed with caution.** Additional evidence is needed before system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **What the Data Shows**

| Period | Treatment Group | Control Group | Gap |
|--------|-----------------|---------------|-----|
| **Before Switch** (weeks 6-14) | 51.35 | 51.15 | +0.20 |
| **After Switch** (weeks 15-22) | 51.70 | 49.65 | +2.05 |

### **The Critical Problem**

The gap between groups **was already widening before the switch**:

| Week | Gap (Treatment − Control) |
|------|---------------------------|
| Week 6 | −0.86 |
| Week 9 | +0.24 |
| Week 12 | +0.42 |
| Week 14 | +0.99 |
| Week 18 | +2.02 |
| Week 20 | +2.86 |

**The treatment group went from behind to ahead BEFORE the new procedure started.** This pattern suggests the switched branches may have been systematically different (better managed, more resources, stronger leadership) rather than the procedure itself causing improvement.

---

## **What This Means**

| Possibility | Explanation |
|-------------|-------------|
| **The procedure works** | The new method genuinely improves service, but we can't prove it with this data |
| **Selection bias** | Better-performing branches were more likely to adopt the new procedure |
| **Both factors** | The procedure helps, but the estimated effect is inflated by pre-existing differences |

---

## **Recommendation**

**Do not proceed with full rollout yet.** Instead:

1. **Run a randomized trial** — Randomly assign branches to new vs. old procedure to eliminate selection bias
2. **Gather qualitative data** — Interview branch managers about implementation challenges and perceived benefits
3. **Pilot in similar branches** — Test the procedure in branches comparable to those that didn't switch
4. **Review after 6 months** — Reassess with more data and a stronger study design

---

## **Bottom Line (Restated)**

The switched branches performed better, but **we cannot say the new procedure caused this improvement.** The board should request stronger evidence before committing to system-wide implementation.
