# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD OF DIRECTORS**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Shelving and Rotation Procedure

**From:** Data Analysis Office

---

## **EXECUTIVE SUMMARY**

**Recommendation: Proceed with caution. The data are inconclusive regarding causal impact.**

Branches that adopted the new procedure showed higher service scores after implementation. However, these branches were already outperforming control branches—and improving faster—before the procedure changed. This analysis cannot isolate the procedure's effect from pre-existing differences between branches.

---

## **WHAT THE DATA SHOW**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Raw DiD Estimate** | +2.29 service score points |
| **Statistical Significance** | p < 0.001 |
| **Pre-Switch Trend** | Gap widening by 0.31 points/week |
| **Branches in Pilot** | 425 (new) + 284 (control) |

---

## **CRITICAL LIMITATION**

**The parallel trends assumption is violated.**

For a valid causal comparison, treatment and control branches should have similar trajectories before the intervention. They do not:

- The gap between switched and control branches was **already widening by 0.31 points per week** before the switch
- **7 of 8 pre-period coefficients** in the event study are statistically different from zero
- The pre-switch gap grew from 0.69 points (week 6) to 3.08 points (week 14)

This indicates that branches selected for the pilot were systematically different—they were already on a stronger performance trajectory independent of the procedure change.

---

## **INTERPRETATION**

| Scenario | Likely True Effect |
|----------|-------------------|
| If pre-trends reflected selection bias only | Effect could be near zero |
| If pre-trends partially reflected early adoption benefits | Effect could be positive but smaller than 2.29 |
| If procedure genuinely helps | Effect is positive but unquantified |

**We cannot distinguish among these scenarios with the current data.**

---

## **RECOMMENDED ACTIONS**

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| 1 | **Do not approve immediate system-wide rollout** | Evidence is insufficient to justify full deployment |
| 2 | **Continue pilot with enhanced monitoring** | Gather more data while limiting exposure |
| 3 | **Consider randomizing remaining branches** | Random assignment would enable causal inference |
| 4 | **Investigate why pilot branches differed** | Understand selection criteria for future evaluations |

---

## **CONCLUSION**

The board's confidence in this procedure is not supported by this analysis. While the new procedure may be beneficial, the current evidence cannot confirm it. A more rigorous evaluation design is needed before committing to system-wide implementation.
