# **Memorandum to the Board**

**Subject:** Impact of New Shelving and Rotation Procedure on Service Scores

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure did not improve service scores in any meaningful way.** While our statistical model shows a small positive effect (+1.7 points, p=0.018), this result is driven entirely by control branches declining after week 15—not by treatment branches improving. Treatment branches went from 49.9 to 49.8 (essentially no change). In 6 of 8 post-switch weeks, treatment branches actually performed *worse* than control branches. **We cannot conclude the new procedure adds value.** We recommend against systemwide rollout based on current evidence.

---

## **Key Findings**

### What Actually Happened to Scores

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|---------------|---------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 49.9 | 49.8 | **-0.1** (no change) |
| Non-switched branches | 53.3 | 51.6 | **-1.8** (declined) |

The "treatment effect" exists only because control branches declined more—not because switched branches improved.

### Week-by-Week Performance After Switch

| Week | Treatment vs. Control |
|------|----------------------|
| 15 | Treatment worse (-6.7) |
| 16 | Treatment worse (-2.5) |
| 17 | Treatment worse (-5.2) |
| 18 | Treatment better (+3.8) |
| 19 | Treatment worse (-0.4) |
| 20 | Treatment worse (-2.9) |
| 21 | Treatment worse (-5.3) |
| 22 | Treatment better (+0.2) |

**Treatment branches underperformed in 6 of 8 weeks.**

### Post-Switch Reality Check

Even after the procedure change, switched branches still have **lower absolute service scores** than non-switched branches (49.8 vs. 51.6).

---

## **Interpretation**

The statistically significant result appears to reflect one of the following:

1. **External factors** affected control branches negatively after week 15 (seasonal patterns, staffing changes, etc.)
2. **Random variation** in an 8-week post-switch window
3. **The procedure has no practical effect** on service quality—which aligns with the board's initial expectation

The new procedure did not harm scores, but there is no convincing evidence it helps either.

---

## **Recommendations**

1. **Do not proceed with systemwide rollout** based on current evidence

2. **Continue monitoring** the 90 switched branches over a longer period to see if effects emerge or stabilize

3. **Investigate why control branches declined** after week 15—this may reveal operational issues unrelated to the procedure

4. **Consider a cost-benefit analysis** of the new procedure's implementation costs against the uncertain benefits

5. **If rollout is still desired**, implement gradually with rigorous tracking to confirm any effect before full deployment
