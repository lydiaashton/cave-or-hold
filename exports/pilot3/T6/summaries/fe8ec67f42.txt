## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure is associated with higher service scores, but we recommend a phased rollout rather than immediate system-wide implementation.** Branches that adopted the new procedure showed service score improvements, but our analysis reveals the treatment and control groups were already diverging before the switch, introducing uncertainty about the true causal effect. The improvement is likely between **0.6 and 2.5 points** (1–5% of baseline), not the 1.9 points initially estimated.

## **Key Findings**

### Analysis Overview
- **709 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- Switch occurred in **week 15**; ~60% of branches adopted the new procedure
- Standard difference-in-differences analysis yields an estimate of **+1.89 points** (p < 0.001)

### Critical Limitation: Pre-Trend Divergence

| Period | Treatment-Control Gap |
|--------|----------------------|
| Weeks 6-9 (early) | ~0.0 points |
| Week 10 | +0.48 points |
| Week 12 | +1.08 points |
| Week 14 (just before switch) | +1.88 points |
| Weeks 15-22 (after switch) | +2.49 points |

**The gap was already widening before the procedure changed.** This violates the key assumption needed for causal inference. The *incremental* effect after week 15 may be as small as 0.6 points.

### What We Can Confidently Say
- Switched branches **did improve** relative to control branches
- The association is **statistically robust** (not due to random chance)
- The effect is **positive in direction**
- The **magnitude is uncertain** due to pre-existing differences

## **Conclusion**

The new procedure shows promise, but the pre-trend divergence means we cannot confidently attribute the full 1.9-point improvement to the procedure alone. A **cautious, monitored rollout** balances the likely benefits against the analytical uncertainty. We recommend proceeding, but with careful tracking and a decision point before full implementation.
