# **Revised Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence is inconclusive.** While branches that adopted the new procedure showed higher service scores after implementation, **we cannot confidently attribute this improvement to the procedure itself.** The data reveal that switched branches were already on a more favorable trajectory before the change, which undermines our ability to isolate the procedure's true effect.

**Recommendation:** Do not proceed with system-wide rollout based on this evidence alone. Instead, conduct a more rigorous evaluation before making a cooperative-wide decision.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. The Critical Problem: Pre-Existing Trends**

The fundamental assumption for causal inference—that switched and non-switched branches would have followed similar trends without the intervention—**does not hold**.

| Week | Gap (Switched − Non-Switched) | Period |
|------|-------------------------------|--------|
| 6 | 0.65 | Pre |
| 9 | 1.38 | Pre |
| 12 | 1.81 | Pre |
| 14 | 3.05 | Pre |
| 15 | 2.73 | Post |
| 18 | 3.75 | Post |
| 22 | 4.65 | Post |

**The gap was already widening before the switch.** From week 6 to week 14 (entirely pre-intervention), the advantage of switched branches grew from 0.65 to 3.05 points. Statistical testing confirms switched branches were gaining approximately **0.25 points per week more** than non-switched branches even before the procedure change (p < 0.001).

### **2. What This Means for the Estimated Effect**

The reported +2.02 point "effect" likely **overstates the true impact** because it attributes pre-existing trend differences to the intervention. The actual causal effect could be:
- Smaller than estimated
- Zero
- Possibly even negative (we cannot rule this out)

### **3. Additional Concerns**

- **Non-random assignment**: ~60% of branches switched; selection criteria are unclear
- **Volatile weekly effects**: Some post-switch weeks show negative effects (weeks 17-18)
- **Week 16 anomaly**: An unusually high score across all branches may distort results
- **Short observation window**: Only 8 weeks of post-switch data

### **4. What We Can Say with Confidence**

- Switched branches have higher service scores than non-switched branches
- This difference existed before the procedure change and persisted after
- The new procedure did not appear to harm service quality
- Staffing and visit levels independently affect service scores (both significant predictors)

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended Actions**

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| **High** | Pause system-wide rollout | Current evidence does not support the investment |
| **High** | Conduct a randomized pilot | Randomly assign remaining branches to treatment/control for clean causal inference |
| **Medium** | Extend observation period | Collect 6-12 more months of data to assess sustainability |
| **Medium** | Investigate selection criteria | Understand why certain branches were chosen to switch |
| **Low** | Qualitative assessment | Interview branch managers about implementation experience |

### **Proposed Rigorous Evaluation**

1. **Randomized Controlled Trial**: Randomly assign the ~40% of branches still on the old procedure to either adopt the new procedure or remain as controls
2. **Pre-register Analysis Plan**: Specify outcomes and methods before data collection
3. **Minimum 6-Month Follow-up**: Assess whether effects persist beyond initial implementation
4. **Cost-Benefit Analysis**: Compare implementation costs against measured benefits

---

## **Conclusion**

The initial analysis appeared to show a positive effect, but deeper examination reveals the evidence is not reliable for decision-making. The new procedure **may** improve service scores, but the current data cannot distinguish this from pre-existing differences between branches.

**We recommend the Board defer a rollout decision pending more rigorous evidence.** Investing in a proper randomized evaluation now will prevent potentially costly mistakes and ensure resources are directed toward interventions that truly improve service.
