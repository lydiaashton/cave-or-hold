# **Memorandum to the Board of Directors**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence is suggestive but not definitive.** Branches that adopted the new procedure showed higher service scores after the switch, but our analysis reveals that these branches were already improving relative to others *before* the change took effect. The estimated improvement ranges from **0.6 to 1.9 points** depending on the analytical approach, with meaningful uncertainty about how much is attributable to the procedure itself versus pre-existing trends.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with caution. The data is consistent with a positive effect, but we cannot confidently isolate the causal impact.

---

## **Key Findings**

### What We Analyzed
- **709 branches** across the cooperative
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22)
- Switch occurred in **week 15**: some branches adopted the new procedure, others did not

### The Complication: Pre-Treatment Divergence

| Period | Treatment vs. Control Gap |
|--------|---------------------------|
| Weeks 6–11 | –0.2 to +0.5 points (groups track closely) |
| Weeks 12–14 | +1.1 to +1.9 points (**divergence begins BEFORE switch**) |
| Weeks 15–22 | +1.8 to +3.0 points (gap persists after switch) |

**This pattern violates the key assumption** needed for a clean causal estimate: that treatment and control groups would have continued on parallel trajectories absent the intervention.

### Estimated Effects

| Method | Estimated Effect | Interpretation |
|--------|------------------|----------------|
| Standard DiD (weeks 6–14 baseline) | +1.89 points | Likely **overstates** true effect |
| Conservative (week 14 only as baseline) | +0.61 points | More cautious estimate |
| **Recommended range** | **+0.6 to +1.3 points** | Accounts for pre-trend uncertainty |

### What Might Explain the Pre-Trend?
1. **Selection bias**: Branches chosen for the new procedure may have been better-performing or on an upward trajectory already
2. **Anticipation effects**: Branches may have begun preparing for the change before week 15
3. **Unobserved factors**: Something else may have affected the treatment group starting in week 12

---

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with phased rollout, with continued monitoring.** The data does not show harm, and is consistent with a modest positive effect. However, the analytical limitations mean we should:

1. **Implement gradually** rather than all at once
2. **Track outcomes carefully** during rollout to confirm the effect materializes
3. **Consider a randomized rollout** for remaining branches to generate cleaner evidence

## **Technical Appendix**

**Why the uncertainty?** The difference-in-differences method requires that treatment and control groups follow parallel trends before the intervention. Our data shows the treatment group began outperforming the control group 3 weeks *before* the switch, making it impossible to cleanly separate the procedure's effect from pre-existing differences.

**What we know with confidence:**
- Switched branches averaged 52.56 after the switch vs. 50.07 for non-switched branches
- The gap is statistically significant (p < 0.001)
- No evidence of negative effects

**What remains uncertain:**
- How much of the gap is caused by the procedure vs. pre-existing trends
- Whether the effect will persist or diminish over time
