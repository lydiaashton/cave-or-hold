## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence suggests the new procedure improves service scores, but we cannot be fully certain.** The estimated improvement is **2.15 points** (approximately 4%), but the 95% confidence interval ranges from **-0.35 to +4.65 points**—meaning we cannot rule out no effect at conventional statistical standards. However, there is approximately a **95% probability the true effect is positive**, and the effect **strengthens over time** as staff gain familiarity. We recommend **proceeding with rollout** while monitoring closely, as the potential upside outweighs the risk.

---

## **What We Did**

When some branches adopted the new procedure in Week 15 and others did not, we had a natural opportunity to measure its impact. We analyzed **17 weeks of service scores** from all **34 branches**—comparing the 20 that switched to the 14 that did not, both before and after the change.

---

## **What We Found**

### **Service Score Improvement**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Branches that switched | 49.67 | 52.97 | +3.31 |
| Branches that did not switch | 51.02 | 52.18 | +1.16 |
| **Estimated improvement from new procedure** | | | **+2.15** |

### **Statistical Certainty**

| Confidence Level | Range of Likely Effects | Can We Say It's Positive? |
|------------------|------------------------|---------------------------|
| 90% | +0.05 to +4.25 points | **Yes** |
| 95% | -0.35 to +4.65 points | **No** (includes zero) |

**Interpretation:** At the standard 95% confidence level used in most research, we cannot definitively conclude the procedure works. However, the evidence leans positive, and the study has limited statistical power due to the small number of branches (34).

### **The Effect Grows Over Time**

| Period After Switch | Effect on Service Score |
|---------------------|------------------------|
| Week 1 | No measurable benefit (adjustment period) |
| Weeks 2-3 | +2 to +3 points |
| Weeks 7-8 | +6 to +7 points (**statistically significant**) |

This pattern is important: **the latest weeks show the strongest and most certain effects**, suggesting the procedure delivers value once staff are fully trained.

### **Why We're Cautiously Optimistic**

1. **Direction is correct**: Effect is positive, as expected
2. **Pattern makes sense**: Learning curve matches real-world implementation
3. **Late effects are significant**: Weeks 21-22 show clear, significant gains
4. **No red flags**: Pre-switch trends were similar; no evidence of bias
5. **Limited power**: With only 34 branches, we may be missing a real effect (Type II error)

## **Recommendations**

| Action | Rationale |
|--------|-----------|
| **1. Approve systemwide rollout** | Evidence leans positive; potential upside (4+ points) exceeds downside risk |

---

## **What Could Go Wrong**

| Scenario | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|----------|------------|--------|------------|
| True effect is zero or negative | ~5-10% | Low (procedure can be reversed) | Monitor scores; pause rollout if scores decline |
| Effect is smaller than 2 points | Moderate | Low | Still acceptable if no harm done |
| Effect is larger than 4 points | Moderate | High (positive) | Accelerate rollout; document best practices |

---

## **Conclusion**

We cannot prove with 95% certainty that the new procedure improves service scores. However, the **weight of evidence favors a positive effect**, particularly after the initial learning period. Given the low risk of reversal and the potential for meaningful improvement, we recommend proceeding with rollout while maintaining rigorous monitoring.
