# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 14, 2026  
**Subject:** Findings on New Branch Procedure – Recommendation for Cautious Consideration

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence does not support a clear recommendation for system-wide rollout.** While the statistical analysis shows a significant difference between groups (+1.66 points, p = 0.003), this reflects **relative resilience, not improvement**. Treatment branches still have **lower absolute service scores** than control branches both before and after the switch. The new procedure appears to protect against decline rather than enhance performance. We recommend the board consider additional factors before deciding on rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Critical Context Often Overlooked**

| Metric | Control (Old Procedure) | Treatment (New Procedure) |
|--------|------------------------|---------------------------|
| **Before switch** (Weeks 6–14) | 53.34 | 49.91 |
| **After switch** (Weeks 15–22) | 51.57 | 49.79 |
| **Change** | -1.78 | -0.12 |
| **Gap between groups** | — | Treatment is **1.78 points LOWER** even after switch |

### **What the +1.66 Point "Effect" Actually Means**

The statistically significant result indicates that treatment branches **declined less** than control branches after Week 15—not that they improved. In absolute terms:

- **Neither group improved** – both stayed flat or declined
- **Treatment branches started lower** (by 3.4 points) and **remain lower** (by 1.8 points)
- The "benefit" is that the new procedure appears more **resilient to whatever caused the system-wide decline** at Week 15

### **System-Wide Pattern**

Week 15 shows a sharp drop for **all branches** (both groups), suggesting an external factor affected the entire cooperative simultaneously—possibly seasonal, operational, or related to the transition itself.

### **Study Validity**

✓ Pre-switch trends were parallel (no significant differences in week-to-week changes before the switch)

✓ Analysis uses appropriate statistical methods with cluster-robust standard errors

⚠️ **However**: Treatment branches had systematically lower baseline scores, which may reflect unobserved differences between branches that chose to switch early versus those that didn't

---

## **Reconsidered Recommendation**

Given that:
1. The new procedure did not **improve** service scores in absolute terms
2. Treatment branches still score **below** control branches after the switch
3. The apparent benefit is **protective** (resistance to decline) rather than **enhancing**
4. Branches self-selected into early adoption, which may introduce bias

**We recommend the board:**

1. **Pause the rollout decision** pending further investigation

2. **Interview early-adopter branches** to understand why they switched and whether there are unobserved differences from non-adopters

3. **Investigate the Week 15 system-wide decline** to understand what drove scores down across all branches

4. **Consider a randomized rollout** for remaining branches to eliminate selection bias before committing system-wide

5. **Define success criteria** – if the goal is to prevent decline during disruptions, the new procedure shows promise; if the goal is to raise scores, evidence is lacking

---

## **Bottom Line for Decision-Makers**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did the new procedure improve service scores? | **No** – absolute scores did not increase |
| Did the new procedure show any benefit? | **Yes** – treatment branches were more resilient to decline |
| Is the statistical finding reliable? | **Yes** – the difference is real and significant |
| Should we roll out system-wide? | **Not clearly** – the benefit is modest and contextual |

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Conduct qualitative research** with early-adopter branches to understand selection factors

2. **Analyze the Week 15 shock** – was this seasonal, procedural, or external?

3. **Consider a controlled pilot** with random assignment for remaining branches

4. **Revisit the decision** once selection bias and external factors are better understood
