# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

**Date:** November 14, 2026

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

The new procedure shows a **positive but not statistically conclusive** effect on service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure improved by approximately 1.75 points (3.6%) relative to branches that kept the old procedure. However, with 89% confidence (p=0.11), we cannot rule out that this improvement occurred by chance. 

**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **phased rollout** to additional branches while continuing to collect data. Do not commit to system-wide implementation until we have stronger evidence.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **1. The Effect Points in the Right Direction**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches (20) | 47.9 | 49.5 | **+1.6** |
| Non-switched branches (14) | 48.1 | 47.9 | **-0.2** |
| **Net effect** | | | **+1.8** |

The new procedure is associated with higher service scores, consistent with our original hypothesis.

### **2. Statistical Uncertainty Remains**

- **95% Confidence Interval:** -0.4 to +3.9 points
- **Interpretation:** The true effect could be slightly negative, zero, or moderately positive
- **Why the uncertainty?** We have only 34 branches and 8 weeks of post-switch data

### **3. Results Vary Across Branches**

- **65% of switched branches** improved after adoption
- **50% of non-switched branches** improved over the same period
- Individual branch changes ranged from -6.2 to +9.2 points
- This variation suggests branch-specific factors may influence success

### **4. Analysis Is Methodologically Sound**

- Treatment and control branches followed parallel trends before the switch
- Results hold when accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume
- Standard errors properly account for repeated observations per branch

## **WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE COOPERATIVE**

The new procedure shows promise but does not yet meet the evidentiary standard for a full rollout. A phased approach allows us to:

1. **Capture potential benefits** at additional branches
2. **Limit risk** if the procedure underperforms at scale
3. **Learn which branches benefit most** before committing resources
4. **Make a confident decision** with stronger data in Q2
