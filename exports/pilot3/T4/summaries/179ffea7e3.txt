# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and rotation procedure produced a **small but statistically significant improvement** in service scores. Contrary to expectations of no effect, branches that adopted the new procedure maintained their service scores while control branches declined, resulting in a net benefit of approximately **1.7 points** (3.5% relative improvement). Given the modest effect size and unexpected nature of the finding, we recommend a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than immediate systemwide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15 (treatment group)
- **60 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses difference-in-differences with branch fixed effects and clustered standard errors

### Main Results

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Treatment (new procedure) | 49.9 | 49.8 | -0.1 |
| Control (old procedure) | 53.3 | 51.6 | -1.8 |
| **Difference (Treatment Effect)** | | | **+1.7** |

- **Treatment effect: +1.75 points** (95% CI: 0.63 to 2.86; p = 0.002)
- **Effect size: 0.19 standard deviations** (small by conventional standards)
- The effect appears driven by treatment branches **maintaining stability** while control branches experienced a decline

### Important Context

1. **Unexpected finding**: The analysis detected a statistically significant effect where none was anticipated. This warrants careful interpretation.

2. **Modest practical impact**: At 0.19 standard deviations, the effect is detectable but small in practical terms.

3. **Weekly variability**: Post-switch effects varied considerably week-to-week, ranging from -6.7 to +3.8 points relative to baseline.

4. **No absolute improvement**: Treatment branches did not improve in absolute terms—they held steady while control branches declined.

5. **Potential confounding**: The control group's decline may reflect external factors (seasonal patterns, staffing changes, etc.) that affected them differently than treatment branches.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with cautious, phased rollout**
   - Expand to 20–30 additional branches over the next quarter
   - Prioritize branches with staffing challenges (they showed larger gains)

2. **Extend the evaluation period**
   - Collect 8–12 additional weeks of data to confirm the effect persists
   - Monitor for seasonal patterns that may have influenced the initial results

3. **Investigate the control group decline**
   - Determine whether external factors affected control branches disproportionately
   - This will clarify whether the new procedure actively improves scores or simply protects against deterioration

4. **Establish decision thresholds**
   - Define minimum effect size required for systemwide adoption
   - Consider cost-benefit analysis if implementation requires resources

5. **Report back to the board** in Q2 with updated findings from the expanded rollout

---

*Analysis based on weekly service score data from weeks 6–22. The service score index averages approximately 50 across all branches, with higher scores indicating better service. Statistical models include branch fixed effects with standard errors clustered at the branch level.*
