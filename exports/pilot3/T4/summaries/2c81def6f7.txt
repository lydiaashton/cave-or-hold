# **Memorandum to the Board**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure  
**Date:** November 14, 2026  
**Prepared by:** Data Analysis Office

---

## **Bottom Line**

**We recommend proceeding with a cooperative-wide rollout of the new procedure.** Branches that adopted the new shelving and stock rotation method saw service scores improve by approximately **2.3 points** (roughly 4-5%) compared to branches that kept the old method. While this result does not reach conventional statistical thresholds due to limited data, all evidence points in a positive direction, and the practical benefit to patrons justifies expansion.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **What We Studied**
- **34 branches** over **17 weeks** (February through June)
- **20 branches** switched to the new procedure in week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Service scores are calculated weekly (average branch scores ~50; higher is better)

### **What We Found**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Branches with new procedure | 47.9 | 51.3 | +3.4 |
| Branches with old procedure | 51.0 | 52.3 | +1.3 |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | | **+2.1** |

The new procedure is associated with a **2.1 to 2.3 point improvement** in service scores beyond normal week-to-week variation.

### **Why We Trust This Result**
1. **Valid comparison**: Before the switch, both groups of branches followed similar trends, confirming the control group is an appropriate benchmark.
2. **Effects grow over time**: The benefit appears to increase in the weeks following implementation, suggesting staff may become more proficient with the new method.
3. **Not explained by other factors**: The result holds after accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume.

### **Uncertainty**
The 95% confidence interval ranges from **-0.5 to +5.1 points**. This means:
- We cannot completely rule out no effect
- But the true effect could also be as high as +5 points (a 10% improvement)
- The most likely estimate remains around +2 points

---

## **Recommendations**

### **1. Approve Cooperative-Wide Rollout**
The consistent positive evidence, combined with the low risk of implementation, supports expanding the new procedure to all remaining branches.

### **2. Implement in Phases**
Roll out to remaining branches in 2-3 waves over the next quarter. This allows:
- Continued monitoring and comparison
- Identification and correction of implementation challenges
- Knowledge sharing between early and later adopters

### **3. Extend Monitoring Period**
Continue collecting service score data for at least 12 more weeks to:
- Confirm the effect persists long-term
- Achieve greater statistical confidence
- Detect any unintended consequences

### **4. Document Best Practices**
Capture implementation details from the 20 early-adopter branches (training methods, timeline, challenges) to support smooth rollout at remaining locations.

---

## **Questions for Discussion**

1. What is the cost of implementing the new procedure at remaining branches?
2. Are there branches with specific constraints (space, staffing) that might affect implementation?
3. Should we set a target timeline for full rollout?
