# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 14, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Rotation Procedure  

---

## **Executive Summary**

The new shelving and rotation procedure is associated with a **modest but meaningful improvement in service scores**. Branches that adopted the new procedure showed an average gain of approximately **2 points** (about 4% improvement) compared to branches that maintained the old procedure. The effect appears to strengthen over time, becoming statistically significant in the weeks following the initial transition. 

**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **phased systemwide rollout** while continuing to monitor outcomes and provide transition support to branches.

---

## **Study Overview**

| Component | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Branches studied** | 34 total (20 switched, 14 control) |
| **Observation period** | 17 weeks (weeks 6–22) |
| **Switch date** | Week 15 |
| **Method** | Difference-in-differences analysis with clustered standard errors |
| **Outcome measure** | Weekly service score (baseline average ~51; higher is better) |

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Main Effect Estimate**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated treatment effect | **+1.9 points** |
| Baseline service score | 51.2 |
| Relative improvement | **3.7%** |
| 95% confidence interval | -0.3 to +4.1 points |
| Statistical significance | Marginal (p = 0.09) |

### **2. Effect Strengthens Over Time**

| Period | Estimated Effect | Significance |
|--------|-----------------|--------------|
| All post-switch weeks (15–22) | +1.9 points | p = 0.09 |
| Excluding transition week (16–22) | +2.2 points | p = 0.07 |
| Later post-period only (17–22) | **+2.8 points** | **p = 0.03** ✓ |

This pattern suggests branches require 2–3 weeks to fully realize benefits from the new procedure.

### **3. Pre-Treatment Trends Validate Comparison**

Service score trajectories for switching and non-switching branches were parallel before week 15, confirming that observed post-switch differences are attributable to the procedure change rather than pre-existing branch differences.

### **4. Transition Disruption Observed**

Both groups experienced a service score dip during week 15 (the switch week), indicating implementation disruption. The treatment group recovered more rapidly in subsequent weeks.

### **5. Other Factors Influencing Service Scores**

- **Staffing index:** Each 1-point increase associated with +0.81 service score points (p = 0.01)
- **Visits index:** Each 1-point increase associated with +1.03 service score points (p = 0.02)

These relationships held independent of the procedure change.

## **Visual Summary**

A trend chart (attached) illustrates service score trajectories for both groups. The treatment group's line diverges upward from the control group after week 15, with the gap widening in later weeks.

---

## **Recommendations**

### **Immediate Actions**

1. **Approve phased systemwide rollout** — Begin with 5–10 branches per month to allow for manageable implementation and ongoing evaluation.

2. **Provide transition support** — Allocate temporary additional staffing or training resources during each branch's switchover week to minimize the observed transition dip.

3. **Establish monitoring protocol** — Track service scores for 8–12 weeks post-switch for each newly converted branch.

### **Ongoing Evaluation**

4. **Set performance expectations** — Anticipate average improvements of 2–3 service score points per branch, with full benefits realized after 2–3 weeks of operation under the new procedure.

5. **Identify best practices** — Investigate branches showing above-average gains to develop implementation guidelines for future conversions.

6. **Reassess at 6 months** — Conduct a follow-up analysis after full rollout to confirm systemwide impact and identify any branches requiring additional support.

---

## **Conclusion**

The evidence supports proceeding with the new procedure, though expectations should be calibrated to the modest effect size observed. The strengthening effect over time is encouraging and suggests that continued refinement and branch experience will yield additional gains. A careful, supported rollout maximizes the likelihood of realizing the full potential of this operational change.
