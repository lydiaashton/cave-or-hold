# **Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure Evaluation**

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure shows a **positive but statistically uncertain effect** on service scores. Branches that switched to the new procedure improved by approximately **2.2 points** (about 4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. However, this result is not statistically significant at conventional levels, meaning we cannot confidently rule out that the observed improvement occurred by chance.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with caution. The evidence suggests the new procedure may help, but it is not conclusive. Consider a phased rollout with continued monitoring rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches total:** 20 adopted the new procedure, 14 kept the old procedure (control group)
- **17 weeks of data:** Weeks 6–14 (before switch) and Weeks 15–22 (after switch)
- **Analysis method:** Difference-in-differences, which isolates the effect of the new procedure by comparing changes in switched branches against changes in control branches

### **Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched Branches (New Procedure) | 52.83 | 53.61 | +0.79 |
| Control Branches (Old Procedure) | 51.34 | 49.95 | −1.40 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+2.19** |

### **Statistical Confidence**
- **Estimated effect:** +2.19 points on the service score index
- **95% Confidence Interval:** −0.74 to +5.11 points
- **P-value:** 0.143 (not statistically significant at the 5% level)

**What this means:**
- The data are **consistent with** a meaningful improvement of up to 5 points
- But the data are **also consistent with** no effect or even a small negative effect
- There is approximately a 14% probability of observing this result if the new procedure had no true effect

### **Supporting Evidence**
- **Parallel trends validated:** Before the switch, both groups followed similar trends (p=0.644), which supports the validity of our analysis
- **Control group declined:** Branches keeping the old procedure saw scores drop by 1.4 points after week 15, while switched branches held steady or improved slightly
- **Practical significance:** If the effect is real, a 2.2-point improvement represents about 0.22 standard deviations—a modest but potentially meaningful gain for patron experience

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Continue monitoring:** Extend data collection for another 8–12 weeks to increase statistical power and confirm whether the positive trend persists

2. **Phased rollout:** Consider switching additional branches in stages while tracking outcomes, rather than implementing system-wide immediately

3. **Investigate mechanisms:** Analyze whether the effect varies by branch characteristics (size, location, staffing) to identify where the procedure works best

4. **Cost-benefit analysis:** Weigh the modest potential benefit against implementation costs, staff training time, and any operational disruptions

5. **Set decision thresholds:** Define in advance what level of improvement would justify full rollout (e.g., "We need 95% confidence the effect exceeds 1.0 point")
