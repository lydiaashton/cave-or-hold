# **Memorandum to the Board**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure  
**Date:** November 2025  
**Prepared by:** Data Analysis Team

---

## **Bottom Line**

Branches that adopted the new procedure showed **modest improvement** in service scores compared to branches that retained the old procedure. However, the evidence is **not strong enough to confirm the procedure caused the improvement**. We recommend **proceeding cautiously** with broader rollout while continuing to collect data.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **The Numbers**

| Metric | Control Branches (Old Procedure) | Treatment Branches (New Procedure) |
|--------|----------------------------------|------------------------------------|
| Average score before switch | 47.4 | 46.6 |
| Average score after switch | 47.2 | 48.2 |
| **Change** | **-0.3** | **+1.6** |

**Net effect of new procedure: +1.9 points** (approximately 4% improvement relative to the typical score of 50)

### **What the Statistics Tell Us**

- The 1.9-point advantage for treatment branches is **positive and directionally consistent** with our expectations
- However, the **confidence interval ranges from -0.4 to +4.3 points**, meaning the true effect could be zero or slightly negative
- The result is **marginally significant** (p = 0.10), falling short of the conventional threshold for statistical confidence

### **Why the Uncertainty?**

1. **Limited post-switch data:** Only 8 weeks of observation after the change
2. **Natural variation:** Service scores fluctuate week-to-week due to factors beyond the procedure (staffing, visits, seasonal patterns)
3. **Sample size:** 34 branches provides moderate but not definitive statistical power

### **Encouraging Signs**

- Treatment branches improved while control branches did not
- The effect persisted after accounting for staffing levels and visit volumes
- No negative effects were detected

---

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with a monitored expansion rather than immediate system-wide rollout.**

### **Rationale**

The data show a positive signal consistent with our hypothesis, but the statistical evidence is not yet conclusive. A measured approach allows us to:

- Capture the potential benefits if the procedure does work
- Avoid locking in a system-wide change before we have definitive evidence
- Build a stronger evidence base for future decisions

---

## **Next Steps**

| Action | Timeline | Responsibility |
|--------|----------|----------------|
| Expand new procedure to 5–10 additional branches | Immediate | Operations |
| Continue weekly score tracking for all branches | Ongoing | Data Team |
| Collect 12 additional weeks of data | 3 months | Data Team |
| Re-evaluate statistical evidence | Q1 2026 | Data Team |
| Make final rollout decision | Q2 2026 | Board |

### **Success Criteria for Full Rollout**

We recommend proceeding to system-wide implementation if follow-up analysis shows:
- Effect size remains positive (+1 point or higher)
- Statistical significance reaches p < 0.05
- No adverse operational impacts are identified

---

## **Conclusion**

The new procedure shows **promise but not proof**. The observed improvement aligns with our expectations, and there is no evidence of harm. A phased expansion with continued monitoring represents the most prudent path forward—positioning the cooperative to capture potential benefits while maintaining accountability to evidence-based decision-making.
