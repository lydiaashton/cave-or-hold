# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores—contrary to expectations.** Branches that switched to the new shelving and rotation procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.1 points** (about **4% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and unexpected, suggesting the procedure change has beneficial side effects beyond its intended purpose.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Clear Positive Effect on Service Quality
- The new procedure increased service scores by **2.08 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.0 to 3.2 points)
- This represents a **4.2% improvement** over the baseline average score of ~49.5
- The result is highly statistically significant (p < 0.001)

### 2. Valid Comparison Between Groups
- **150 branches** studied over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure; **60 branches** served as a control group
- Before the switch, both groups showed **similar trends** in service scores, confirming the groups were comparable
- The improvement appeared after week 15 when the switch occurred

### 3. Effect Is Independent of Staffing and Traffic
- The improvement holds even after accounting for:
  - Staffing levels
  - Visitor volume
- This suggests the procedure itself—not external factors—drove the improvement

### 4. Why This May Have Happened
While the procedure change was not expected to affect service scores, possible explanations include:
- **Improved staff organization** leading to better member interactions
- **Reduced time searching for materials**, freeing staff for member services
- **Psychological effect** of implementing visible changes that energized staff

---

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with system-wide rollout.** The evidence shows a clear, unexpected benefit to service quality. Even if the primary goal of the procedure change was operational efficiency, the additional service score improvement strengthens the case for adoption across all branches.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve full rollout** to remaining branches
2. **Investigate the mechanism**—understand why service scores improved to maximize the benefit
3. **Monitor during implementation** to confirm the effect replicates at scale
4. **Share findings with staff**—the unexpected positive outcome may boost morale and buy-in
5. **Document lessons learned** for future operational changes

---

## **Technical Note**

Analysis used a **difference-in-differences** design with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch). The parallel trends assumption was tested and confirmed (p = 0.28), supporting the validity of the causal interpretation.
