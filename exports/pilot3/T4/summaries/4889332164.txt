# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

---

## **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

**Date:** November 14, 2026  
**Analysis Period:** Weeks 6–22 (17 weeks)  
**Switch Implementation:** Week 15

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

The new procedure shows a **positive but statistically inconclusive effect** on service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure experienced an estimated **1.9-point improvement** (approximately 4% relative to baseline), but this result is **not statistically significant** at conventional levels. 

**We cannot confidently conclude the new procedure improves service scores based on current data.** Additional data collection is recommended before a system-wide rollout.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### Study Overview
| | |
|---|---|
| Total branches | 34 |
| Branches using new procedure | 20 (59%) |
| Branches using old procedure | 14 (41%) |
| Weeks of data | 17 (weeks 6–22) |
| Switch point | Week 15 |

### Service Score Results

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| New procedure branches | 50.6 | 51.3 | +0.7 |
| Old procedure branches | 47.7 | 46.5 | −1.2 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+1.9** |

### Statistical Evidence
- **Estimated effect:** +1.9 points on service score index
- **95% confidence interval:** −0.5 to +4.3 points
- **P-value:** 0.11 (not significant at 5% level)
- **Interpretation:** The confidence interval includes zero, meaning we cannot rule out no effect—or even a small negative effect

### Validity Checks
✓ **Parallel trends confirmed:** No significant difference in pre-switch trends between groups (p=0.24)  
✓ **No confounding factors:** Staffing and visitor traffic did not change differentially between groups  
✓ **Model robust:** Results hold when controlling for branch characteristics, staffing levels, and visitor volume

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| 1 | **Extend data collection by 8–12 weeks** | Current sample lacks statistical power; more data will clarify whether the positive trend is real |
| 2 | **Analyze branch-level variation** | Some branches show larger improvements; identifying success factors could improve implementation |
| 3 | **Define success criteria** | Establish minimum effect size needed to justify system-wide implementation costs |
| 4 | **Consider phased rollout** | If proceeding, implement gradually with continued monitoring rather than all at once |

---

## **RECOMMENDATION**

**Do not proceed with immediate system-wide rollout.**

While the point estimate is encouraging (+1.9 points), the statistical evidence is insufficient to justify the costs and operational disruption of changing procedures at all 34 branches. The confidence interval is too wide to support a confident decision.

**Proposed path forward:**
1. Continue monitoring the current 20 pilot branches
2. Collect 8–12 additional weeks of data
3. Re-evaluate with expanded dataset before making final rollout decision

---

*Analysis methodology: Difference-in-differences regression with branch fixed effects and cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch).*
