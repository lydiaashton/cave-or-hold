# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **1.8 points** (approximately 3.6%) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant and robust across multiple analytical approaches. **We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Measurable Improvement in Service Scores**
- Branches using the new procedure improved by **+1.84 points** on the service score index relative to branches using the old procedure
- This represents a **3.6% improvement** from the baseline average score of 50.5
- The result is highly statistically significant (p < 0.001), meaning it is very unlikely to be due to random chance

### **2. Strong Research Design**
- **709 branches** were studied over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **425 branches** switched to the new procedure at week 15; **284 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Before the switch, both groups had nearly identical service scores (difference of only 0.02 points), validating the comparison
- The control group's scores actually declined slightly after week 15 (-0.25 points), while the treatment group's scores increased (+1.59 points)

### **3. Consistent Results Across Analyses**
The finding holds up under multiple rigorous tests:
- Basic comparison: +1.84 points
- Controlling for branch characteristics: +1.84 points
- Controlling for week-to-week trends: +1.84 points
- Accounting for staffing and visit levels: +1.85 points
- 95% confidence interval: **+1.3 to +2.4 points**

### **4. Branch-Level Outcomes**
- **66% of switching branches** (282 of 425) saw their service scores improve
- **34% of switching branches** (143 of 425) saw scores decline or stay flat
- This variation suggests the procedure works well for most branches, but some may need additional support during implementation
