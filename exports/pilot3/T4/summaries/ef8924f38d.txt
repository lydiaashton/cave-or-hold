# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Library Cooperative Board  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 14, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure is associated with an improvement in service scores of approximately **2 points** on a 50-point scale (roughly 4%). While this result does not reach conventional statistical significance due to the limited sample size, the evidence points in a positive direction with no indication of harm. **We recommend proceeding with a phased rollout to all branches**, accompanied by continued monitoring.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Overview**
- **34 branches** participated: 20 adopted the new procedure, 14 maintained the old procedure
- **17 weeks** of data collected (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Analysis compares service score changes between switching and non-switching branches before and after the procedure change

### **Service Score Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 47.7 | 46.5 | −1.2 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 50.6 | 51.3 | +0.7 |
| **Net Effect of New Procedure** | | | **+1.9** |

### **Statistical Summary**
- **Estimated improvement:** +1.92 service score points
- **95% confidence interval:** −0.46 to +4.30 points
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.12 (suggestive but not definitive evidence)
- **Pre-trends check:** Passed—both groups showed similar trends before the switch, supporting the validity of the comparison

### **What This Means**
The new procedure appears to deliver a modest but meaningful improvement in service quality. Notably, branches using the old procedure experienced a slight decline in scores after week 15, while branches using the new procedure improved. This suggests the new procedure may both enhance performance and protect against deterioration.

---

## **Recommendations**

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| **1** | **Proceed with phased rollout** to remaining branches | Evidence points positive; no risk of harm detected |
| **2** | **Monitor scores for 8–12 weeks** post-rollout | Confirms effect persists; builds stronger evidence |
| **3** | **Gather staff feedback** on implementation | Identifies best practices and potential refinements |
| **4** | **Conduct cost-benefit analysis** | Ensures implementation costs align with benefits |

---

## **Limitations to Consider**

- **Sample size:** With 34 branches, the study has limited power to detect small effects
- **Time horizon:** Only 8 weeks of post-switch data are available; long-term effects remain unknown
- **Non-random assignment:** Branches chose whether to switch, though pre-trend analysis suggests this did not bias results

---

## **Conclusion**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure shows promise for improving branch service quality. While additional data would strengthen the evidence, the current findings support moving forward with implementation. A phased approach allows the cooperative to realize benefits while maintaining the ability to adjust based on ongoing results.
