# **Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure Evaluation**

**Date:** November 2025  
**Analysis Period:** Weeks 6–22 (switch implemented Week 15)  
**Branches Studied:** 34 total (20 adopted new procedure, 14 retained old)

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure shows a **positive but statistically uncertain effect** on service scores. Treatment branches improved by approximately **1.4–1.7 points** relative to control branches after accounting for pre-existing differences. However, this effect does not reach conventional statistical significance (p ≈ 0.07–0.14), meaning we cannot confidently rule out random variation as an explanation.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **phased rollout** rather than immediate system-wide adoption, accompanied by extended monitoring to confirm the effect stabilizes and grows over time.

---

## **Key Findings**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated treatment effect | +1.4 to +1.7 points |
| Statistical significance | Not significant at 5% level |
| 95% confidence interval | -0.2 to +3.6 points |
| Average service score (baseline) | ~50 points |
| Relative improvement | ~3% |

**1. Positive Direction, Uncertain Magnitude**
- All model specifications show a positive effect, consistent with expectations.
- The effect size is modest but meaningful if real (1.7 points represents roughly 3% improvement).
- The confidence interval includes zero, so we cannot exclude the possibility of no effect.

**2. Effect Strengthens Over Time**
- Weeks 15–16 (immediately post-switch): Minimal detectable effect
- Weeks 20–22 (later period): Effect grows to 2–3 points
- This pattern suggests staff may need time to fully implement and benefit from the new procedure

**3. Treatment vs. Control Performance**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| New Procedure (20 branches) | 50.2 | 52.8 | +2.6 |
| Old Procedure (14 branches) | 47.6 | 48.8 | +1.2 |
| **Difference** | **2.6** | **4.0** | **+1.4** |

**4. Supporting Factors**
- Higher visit volumes correlate with higher service scores (statistically significant)
- Staffing levels show a positive relationship with scores, though not statistically significant
- The new procedure's effect persists after controlling for both factors

---

## **Recommendations**

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| **1** | Expand to 10–15 additional branches | Increases statistical power to detect the true effect |
| **2** | Extend monitoring period by 12+ weeks | Effect appears to grow over time; need longer horizon |
| **3** | Document implementation best practices | Identify what high-performing branches are doing differently |
| **4** | Conduct cost-benefit analysis before full rollout | Ensure operational costs justify the modest potential gain |
| **5** | Gather staff feedback on the new procedure | May reveal operational benefits not captured in service scores |

---

## **What Would Change Our Conclusion**

We would recommend immediate system-wide rollout if future data shows:
- Statistical significance at p < 0.05 with the current sample
- Effect size stabilizes above 2 points
- Consistent positive effects across diverse branch types

---

**The data are encouraging but not definitive. A measured, evidence-based expansion minimizes risk while allowing the cooperative to capture potential benefits.**
