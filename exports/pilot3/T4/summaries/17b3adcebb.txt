# **MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 14, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure improved service scores—an unexpected but welcome benefit.** While the rotation and shelving changes were not expected to affect service quality, branches that switched saw scores increase by approximately **2.1 points** (about 4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant. The new procedure delivers operational benefits without compromising—and in fact enhancing—service quality. We recommend proceeding with the full rollout.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### Study Design
- **150 branches** total: 90 switched to the new procedure, 60 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** (Weeks 6–22), with the switch implemented in Week 15
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in switched branches against non-switched branches

### Results

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 49.68 | 48.80 | -0.88 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 49.25 | 50.47 | +1.22 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+2.10** |

### Statistical Evidence
- **Treatment effect: +2.10 points** (95% confidence interval: 0.82 to 3.38)
- **Statistically significant at p < 0.01**
- Effect remains significant after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume
- **Parallel trends validated**: Before the switch, both groups showed similar week-to-week patterns, confirming the comparison is valid

### Interpretation
The procedure change was designed to improve stock rotation and shelving efficiency, not to affect service scores. The observed improvement suggests the new procedure may have secondary benefits, such as:
- Staff spending less time searching for materials and more time assisting patrons
- Better-organized shelves making it easier for patrons to find items independently
- Reduced frustration from misplaced or hard-to-locate stock

Importantly, **there is no evidence of negative impact** on service quality—a risk that was reasonable to consider when changing operational procedures.

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

1. **Approve full rollout** of the new procedure to all branches
2. **Monitor service scores** for 8–12 weeks post-rollout to confirm the effect persists at scale
3. **Gather qualitative feedback** from branch staff on how the new procedure affects their daily work and patron interactions
4. **Document best practices** from high-performing branches to support training during rollout
5. **Consider investigating** which aspects of the new procedure drive the service score improvement, to reinforce those elements in training
