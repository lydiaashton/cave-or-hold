# **Board Memorandum**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 14, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure produced a **modest improvement in service scores (+2.4 points, ~5%)**, but the result falls short of statistical certainty. While the direction of the effect is positive and consistent with expectations, we cannot confidently rule out the possibility that chance alone produced this outcome. We recommend **proceeding with the rollout** while extending monitoring to confirm the benefit.

---

## **What We Did**

We analyzed 17 weeks of service score data from 34 branches:
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15
- **14 branches** continued with the old procedure (comparison group)
- This design isolates the procedure's effect from other factors affecting all branches

---

## **What We Found**

| Metric | Result |
|--------|--------|
| **Estimated improvement** | +2.4 points on service score index |
| **Baseline average score** | 50.7 points |
| **Relative improvement** | ~5% |
| **Statistical confidence** | 90% confident the effect is real; 95% confidence interval ranges from -0.4 to +5.2 points |
| **Pre-switch trends** | Treatment and control branches moved in parallel before the switch (validates our method) |

**Visual evidence:** The attached chart shows treatment branches pulling away from control branches after week 15, with the gap widening through weeks 18–19 before narrowing slightly.

---

## **Why the Uncertainty?**

Three factors limit our confidence:

1. **Sample size:** 34 branches over 17 weeks provides moderate statistical power
2. **Natural variation:** Service scores fluctuate week-to-week due to factors beyond the procedure (staffing, visits, seasonal patterns)
3. **Implementation variation:** Not all branches may have adopted the new procedure with equal fidelity

---

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with system-wide rollout, with conditions:**

1. **Continue monitoring** for at least 12 more weeks to strengthen the evidence base
2. **Document implementation** at each branch to identify best practices
3. **Set a review checkpoint** at 6 months post-rollout to confirm the benefit has materialized
4. **Track downstream outcomes** (patron satisfaction, circulation, staff time) to connect score improvements to operational impact

---

## **Bottom Line for Decision-Makers**

The data supports moving forward, but temper expectations. The new procedure likely helps, just not as dramatically as hoped. Continued measurement will tell us whether this modest gain is worth the investment at scale.
