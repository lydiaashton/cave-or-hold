# **Memorandum to the Board**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative
**From:** Data Analysis Team
**Date:** November 14, 2026
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new shelving and stock rotation procedure achieved service scores approximately **1.8 points higher** (a **3.6% improvement**) than branches that kept the old procedure. This result is statistically significant and consistent across multiple analytical approaches.

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout to all remaining branches.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Total branches studied | 709 |
| Branches using new procedure | 425 |
| Branches using old procedure | 284 |
| Study period | 17 weeks (Weeks 6-22) |
| Procedure switch date | Week 15 |

### Treatment Effect Summary

Our preferred model (with branch and week controls) estimates the new procedure increased service scores by:

| Estimate | 95% Confidence Interval | Statistical Significance |
|----------|------------------------|-------------------------|
| **+1.81 points** | **+1.26 to +2.36 points** | **p < 0.001** |

This finding held steady across five different analytical specifications, confirming the result is robust and not driven by model choice.

### Context for Interpretation
- Average service score across all branches: ~50 points
- Typical week-to-week variation: ~10 points (standard deviation)
- The 1.8-point gain represents approximately **one-fifth of a standard deviation**—a meaningful and practically significant improvement
- No branches showed evidence of service score declines attributable to the new procedure

### Supporting Factors
- **Staffing matters**: Higher staffing levels correlate with better service scores
- **Visitor volume matters**: Busier branches tend to have higher service scores
- The procedure effect is **independent** of these factors—the improvement persists after accounting for staffing and visits

---

## **Next Steps**

### Recommended Actions

| Priority | Action | Timeline |
|----------|--------|----------|
| **1** | Approve system-wide rollout of new procedure | Immediate |
| **2** | Develop implementation schedule for remaining 284 branches | 30 days |
| **3** | Prepare training materials based on early adopter experience | 60 days |
| **4** | Establish ongoing monitoring framework | 90 days |

### Monitoring Plan

Post-rollout, we recommend tracking:
1. **Service scores** by branch to confirm effects persist at scale
2. **Implementation fidelity** to ensure the procedure is followed as designed
3. **Branch-level variation** to identify any branches needing additional support

### Future Analysis

Once rollout is complete, we can examine:
- Whether certain branch types benefit more or less from the new procedure
- Whether the improvement sustains, grows, or diminishes over time
- The full cost-benefit profile of the change

---

## **Conclusion**

The data provide strong evidence that the new shelving and stock rotation procedure improves service quality. With 425 branches already successfully using the new approach and a clear, positive effect on service scores, we recommend extending the procedure to all remaining branches.

---

*Analysis based on branches.csv data covering weeks 6-22. All estimates use cluster-robust standard errors at the branch level.*
