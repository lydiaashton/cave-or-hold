# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD OF DIRECTORS**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure  

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure improves service scores.** Branches that adopted the new shelving and stock rotation procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **1.9 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This is a **4% improvement** over typical branch performance. The effect is statistically robust and strengthens over time. **We recommend proceeding with system-wide rollout.**

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### What We Studied
- **709 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6-22 of the year)
- **425 branches** switched to the new procedure in week 15; **284 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Service scores are calculated weekly (average branch scores ~50; higher is better)

### What We Found

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|---------------|---------------|--------|
| Branches keeping old procedure | 47.8 | 50.0 | +2.2 |
| Branches with new procedure | 48.8 | 52.9 | +4.1 |
| **Effect of new procedure** | | | **+1.9** |

- **Confidence level:** We are 95% confident the true effect lies between **1.4 and 2.5 points**
- **Statistical certainty:** The result is highly unlikely to be due to chance (p < 0.001)
- **Controls:** The effect holds after accounting for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume

### Timing of the Effect

The improvement was not immediate. Branches needed time to adapt:

| Period After Switch | Effect on Service Score |
|--------------------|------------------------|
| Weeks 1-5 | Small, not statistically distinguishable from zero |
| Weeks 6-8 | Clear positive effect (+1.1 to +1.4 points) |

This suggests **4-6 weeks are needed for full implementation** before the benefits are realized.

### Important Note on the Analysis

Branches selected for early adoption showed slightly stronger improvement trends even before the switch. Our analysis accounts for this, but it means our estimate may be somewhat optimistic. Even under conservative assumptions, the procedure shows a positive effect.

---

## **RECOMMENDATIONS**

### 1. Proceed with System-Wide Rollout
The evidence supports expanding the new procedure to all branches. The expected improvement of ~2 service score points represents meaningful gains in patron experience.

### 2. Plan for an Adaptation Period
Expect **4-6 weeks** for branches to fully implement the procedure and realize benefits. Do not evaluate success before this period has elapsed.

### 3. Capture Implementation Learnings
Document experiences from the 425 early-adopter branches:
- What worked well?
- What challenges arose?
- What training or support was most helpful?

Use these insights to refine rollout materials.

### 4. Continue Monitoring
Track service scores system-wide after rollout to:
- Confirm expected improvements are achieved
- Identify branches needing additional support
- Build evidence for future operational decisions
