# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 14, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure  

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure shows a **positive but statistically uncertain** effect on service scores. Switched branches improved by approximately **2 points** more than non-switched branches, but this difference could reasonably occur by chance. We **do not recommend a full system-wide rollout at this time**. Instead, we advise extending the evaluation period and gathering additional evidence before making a final decision.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. What We Measured**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22 of the year)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure at week 15; **14 branches** kept the old procedure
- Service scores average around 50 points (higher is better)
- Analysis compares how switched branches changed relative to non-switched branches

### **2. Impact on Service Scores**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Branches with new procedure | 49.7 | 53.0 | +3.3 |
| Branches with old procedure | 51.0 | 52.2 | +1.2 |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | | **+2.15** |

- The new procedure is associated with a **2-point improvement** in service scores
- **Statistical confidence:** The effect is suggestive but not definitive (p ≈ 0.11; conventional threshold for confidence is p < 0.05)
- In practical terms: We estimate roughly a **60–70% probability** the effect is genuinely positive, but we cannot rule out no effect

### **3. Important Context**
- **Both groups improved** after week 15, suggesting seasonal or system-wide factors also influenced scores
- Branches that switched started with **slightly lower scores** than control branches
- **Visitor volume strongly affects scores**—busier branches tend to score higher regardless of procedure
- **Results varied widely** across branches: some switched branches improved by 10+ points, others showed no gain or declined

### **4. What This Means**
The new procedure **may help**, but the evidence is not strong enough to justify the cost and disruption of a full rollout. Other factors—staffing, visitor patterns, local conditions—appear to matter as much or more than the shelving procedure itself.

---

## **Recommendations**

### **Immediate Action**
1. **Pause system-wide rollout** until stronger evidence is available
2. **Continue monitoring** both groups for 8–12 additional weeks
3. **Document operational impacts** (staff time, costs, satisfaction) alongside service scores

### **Further Analysis**
4. **Identify which branches benefited most**—the procedure may work better in specific contexts (e.g., high-traffic branches, certain staffing levels)
5. **Gather staff feedback** from switched branches about practical challenges and benefits
6. **Review cost implications** of the new procedure compared to the old one

### **Decision Framework**
7. If proceeding, consider a **phased rollout** with clear evaluation milestones
8. Establish **success criteria** in advance (e.g., sustained 3-point improvement with p < 0.05)
9. Set a **review date** (e.g., 90 days) to reassess with additional data

---

## **Questions for Board Discussion**

1. What level of confidence is required before committing to a system-wide change?
2. Are there operational benefits (efficiency, staff satisfaction) not captured in service scores?
3. What are the costs of maintaining two procedures versus standardizing?
4. Should we prioritize branches with certain characteristics for early adoption?
