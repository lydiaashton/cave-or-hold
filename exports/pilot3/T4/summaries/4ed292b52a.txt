# **Memorandum to the Board of Directors**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure works.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure achieved service scores **1.9 points higher** than branches that kept the old procedure. This represents a **3.7% improvement** in service quality. The finding is statistically robust and supports proceeding with a system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### What We Analyzed
- **709 branches** across the cooperative
- **17 weeks of data** (February through June)
- Natural experiment: some branches switched to the new procedure in week 15, others did not
- Rigorous difference-in-differences analysis comparing treatment and control groups

### Results Summary

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Estimated improvement** | +1.89 service score points |
| **95% confidence interval** | +1.37 to +2.42 points |
| **Statistical significance** | p < 0.001 |
| **Relative improvement** | 3.7% over baseline |

### What the Numbers Mean

Branches using the new procedure improved by **1.77 points** after the switch. Branches using the old procedure actually declined slightly (**-0.11 points**). The net benefit of the new procedure is **1.89 points**—a meaningful gain on a scale where the average branch scores around 50.

The result holds even after accounting for weekly variations in staffing levels and visitor volume, confirming the improvement is attributable to the procedure itself.

---

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with system-wide implementation.** The data confirms what operational staff expected: the new rotation and shelving procedure improves service quality. There is no statistical evidence of harm, and the magnitude of improvement justifies the investment in training and transition.

## **Appendix: Technical Notes**

- **Method:** Difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch)
- **Controls:** Staffing index, visits index
- **Data quality:** 12,053 observations; no missing values on key variables
- **Assumption check:** Pre-treatment trends between groups were reasonably parallel, supporting causal interpretation
