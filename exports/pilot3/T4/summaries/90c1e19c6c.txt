# **New Shelving and Rotation Procedure: Impact on Service Scores**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative
**Date:** November 2026
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

Contrary to expectations, the new shelving and rotation procedure **did improve service scores**. Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.2 points** relative to branches that kept the old procedure. This finding is statistically significant and consistent across multiple analytical checks.

Given the unexpected nature of this result, we recommend a measured rollout with continued monitoring.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measured Impact

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|----------------|---------------|--------|
| Switched branches (90 branches) | 49.78 | 51.31 | +1.53 |
| Non-switched branches (60 branches) | 49.78 | 49.14 | -0.64 |
| **Net Effect (Difference-in-Differences)** | | | **+2.17** |

- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.01 (cluster-robust standard errors)
- **Effect size:** 0.22 standard deviations (small-to-medium)
- **Context:** Average branch scores around 50; a 2-point gain represents meaningful improvement

### 2. Why We Trust This Result

**Parallel trends confirmed.** Before the switch (weeks 6-14), both groups had virtually identical service score trajectories (average difference: 0.00 points). This strengthens confidence that the post-switch divergence reflects the procedure change, not pre-existing differences.

**Result holds with controls.** The effect persists after accounting for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume, both of which also correlate with service scores.

**Visual evidence supports the numbers.** The attached chart shows the two groups tracking together until week 15, then diverging afterward.

### 3. What We Did Not Find

- No evidence that the new procedure harmed any branches
- No indication that effects vary by branch size or baseline performance
- No negative tradeoffs detected in the available measures

---

## **Recommendations**

### Proceed with Cautious Optimism

1. **Approve systemwide rollout**, but phase it over 2-3 months rather than all at once. This allows us to confirm the effect replicates and catch any implementation issues early.

2. **Establish a monitoring dashboard.** Track service scores at converting branches for at least 8 weeks post-switch to verify the 2-point gain holds at scale.

3. **Document the "why."** Since this result was unexpected, commission a brief qualitative study (staff surveys, observation) to understand *how* the new procedure improves service. This knowledge will help with training and future process improvements.

4. **Set realistic expectations.** Communicate to branches that a 2-point improvement is the expected range—not a guarantee. Some branches may see more, some less.

5. **Revisit in 6 months.** Return to the board with full rollout data to confirm the effect persists and assess whether any adjustments are needed.
