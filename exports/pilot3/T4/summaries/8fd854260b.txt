# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Library Cooperative Board  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 14, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw an average increase of **1.9 points** (approximately **4% improvement**) in their service scores compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and consistent across the majority of branches. 

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout to all branches.**

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### 1. Measured Impact

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Treatment effect | **+1.88 points** on service score index |
| 95% Confidence Interval | 1.2 to 2.6 points |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 (highly significant) |
| Relative improvement | ~3.7% above baseline (avg. score = 51) |

### 2. Before-and-After Comparison

| Group | Branches | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|----------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched to new procedure | 425 | 50.8 | 52.6 | **+1.8** |
| Kept old procedure | 284 | 50.2 | 50.1 | -0.1 |

### 3. Branch-Level Results

- **67.5%** of switched branches improved their scores vs. only **47.9%** of non-switched branches
- Among branches that improved, the average gain was **3.6 points**
- The effect remained significant after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### 4. Analysis Validation

- **Parallel trends confirmed:** Both groups showed similar score patterns before the switch (weeks 6-14)
- **Robust to controls:** Results hold when accounting for week-to-week variation, staffing, and visits
- **Not driven by outliers:** Effect is distributed across the majority of treatment branches

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all 284 branches still using the old method

2. **Monitor implementation** over the next 8-12 weeks to ensure:
   - Consistent adoption of the new procedures
   - Service score improvements match the pilot results
   - No unintended operational burdens on staff

3. **Document best practices** from high-performing switched branches to support training materials

4. **Re-evaluate in Q2 2027** to confirm sustained improvements and assess long-term impact

---

## **METHODOLOGY NOTE**

This analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach comparing 425 branches that switched to the new procedure (treatment group) against 284 branches that did not (control group). Data covers 17 weeks (weeks 6-22) with the switch implemented in week 15. The model controls for branch characteristics, time trends, staffing levels, and visitor volume. Standard errors are robust to heteroscedasticity.
