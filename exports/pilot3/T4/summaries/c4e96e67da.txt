# **Board Memorandum**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2025  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure  

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation procedure shows a **positive but statistically inconclusive effect** on service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure improved by approximately **2 points (4%)** relative to branches that kept the old procedure. However, this result is not statistically significant, meaning we cannot confidently rule out that the observed improvement occurred by chance. 

**Recommendation: Proceed with a phased rollout while continuing to collect data, rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. What the Data Shows**

| Group | Before (Weeks 6–14) | After (Weeks 15–22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------|---------------------|--------|
| Control branches (old procedure) | 47.4 | 47.2 | −0.3 |
| Treatment branches (new procedure) | 46.6 | 48.2 | +1.6 |
| **Net Effect (Difference-in-Differences)** | | | **+1.9 points** |

- **Estimated improvement:** 1.9 points on the service score index (approximately 4%)
- **Statistical confidence:** Not significant at conventional levels (p ≈ 0.10)
- **95% confidence interval:** −0.5 to +4.4 points

### **2. Study Strengths**

- **34 branches** participated (20 switched, 14 did not)
- **17 weeks of data** provided before-and-after comparison
- **Parallel trends validated:** Both groups followed similar patterns before the switch, supporting the validity of our comparison
- **Controlled for other factors:** Results hold after accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume

### **3. What the Chart Shows**

![Service Score Trends](board_summary_chart.png)

- **Left panel:** Weekly trends show treatment branches pulling ahead after week 15, while control branches remained flat
- **Right panel:** The treatment group gained ground relative to control in the post-switch period

### **4. Important Caveats**

- **Uncertainty remains:** The true effect could range from slightly negative to meaningfully positive
- **Small effect size:** Even if real, a 2-point improvement is modest relative to typical week-to-week variation
- **Limited observation window:** Only 8 weeks of post-switch data may be insufficient to detect smaller effects reliably

---

## **Next Steps**

### **Immediate Actions**

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| 1 | **Continue data collection** for 8–12 more weeks | Increases statistical power and reduces uncertainty |
| 2 | **Allow additional branches to adopt** the new procedure voluntarily | Builds momentum while maintaining evaluation rigor |
| 3 | **Monitor implementation fidelity** | Ensure branches are following the new procedure correctly |

### **Decision Triggers**

| Scenario | Recommended Action |
|----------|-------------------|
| Effect becomes statistically significant (p < 0.05) | Proceed with system-wide rollout |
| Confidence interval lower bound exceeds zero | Consider accelerated rollout |
| Effect diminishes or reverses with more data | Reconsider the procedure; investigate why |

### **Questions for Board Discussion**

1. What magnitude of improvement would justify the operational costs of system-wide implementation?
2. Should we set a specific timeline (e.g., 6 months) for a final go/no-go decision?
3. Are there specific branch characteristics (size, location, patron demographics) we should examine for differential effects?

---

## **Conclusion**

The new procedure shows **promise but not proof**. The data trends in the right direction, but we lack the statistical confidence to guarantee the improvement is real. A measured, data-driven approach—continuing to monitor while allowing gradual adoption—balances innovation with fiscal responsibility.

**We recommend revisiting this decision in 3–4 months with additional data.**
