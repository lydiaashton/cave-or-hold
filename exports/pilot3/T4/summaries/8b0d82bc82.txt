# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Library Cooperative Board  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 14, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** We recommend **proceeding with system-wide rollout** to all branches.

Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by **2.1 points** (approximately **4% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and consistent across the majority of branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Clear Positive Impact**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Branches with new procedure (425) | 51.1 | 54.0 | +2.9 |
| Branches with old procedure (284) | 49.9 | 50.7 | +0.8 |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | | **+2.1** |

The 2.1-point improvement is the estimated causal effect of the new procedure, isolated from other factors like seasonal trends or branch-specific characteristics.

### **2. Improvement Across Most Branches**

- **79% of branches** using the new procedure saw their scores improve
- Only **57% of branches** keeping the old procedure saw improvement
- This 22-percentage-point difference confirms the benefit is widespread, not driven by a few outliers

### **3. Results Are Reliable**

- **Statistical confidence:** We are highly confident this effect is real (p < 0.001)
- **Precision:** The true effect is very likely between 1.5 and 2.7 points
- **Robustness:** Results hold when accounting for staffing levels, visitor volume, and branch-specific factors
- **Baseline validity:** Treatment and control branches had similar characteristics before the switch

---

## **Recommendation**

**Approve immediate rollout of the new procedure to all remaining branches.**

The evidence strongly supports that the new shelving and rotation procedure improves service quality. With 425 branches already successfully using the procedure and demonstrating measurable gains, extending it system-wide is a low-risk, high-reward decision.

---

## **Next Steps**

| Action | Timeline | Responsibility |
|--------|----------|----------------|
| Approve system-wide rollout | Board meeting | Board |
| Develop implementation schedule | 2 weeks | Operations |
| Train remaining 284 branches | 4-8 weeks | Training Team |
| Monitor service scores during rollout | Ongoing | Data Team |
| Full evaluation post-rollout | 6 months | Data Team |

### **Implementation Considerations**

1. **Expect variation:** While 79% of branches improved, about 21% did not see gains. These branches may need additional support during adoption.

2. **Track progress:** Weekly monitoring of service scores will help identify branches needing extra assistance early in the rollout.

3. **Capture best practices:** Document what high-performing branches do differently to support struggling adopters.

---

## **Appendix: Analysis Details**

- **Data:** 709 branches, 17 weeks (weeks 6-22), 12,053 observations
- **Method:** Difference-in-differences regression with branch and week fixed effects
- **Standards:** Clustered standard errors at branch level
- **Switch point:** Week 15
