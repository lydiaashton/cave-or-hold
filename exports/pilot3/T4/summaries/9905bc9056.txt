# **Library Cooperative Shelving Procedure Change: Impact Analysis**

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure is associated with a **modest improvement in service scores** (+2.2 points, or approximately 4%), but this result is **not statistically significant** at conventional levels. The data are consistent with both a small positive effect and no effect at all. We recommend a **cautious, phased rollout** with continued monitoring rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure at week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in switched branches against changes in non-switched branches

### Results

| Group | Before Switch (Weeks 6–14) | After Switch (Weeks 15–22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------------|---------------------------|--------|
| Switched Branches | 52.8 | 53.6 | +0.8 |
| Control Branches | 51.3 | 49.9 | −1.4 |
| **Difference** | | | **+2.2** |

- **Point Estimate**: +2.19 points improvement attributable to the new procedure
- **95% Confidence Interval**: −0.6 to +5.0 points
- **Statistical Significance**: p ≈ 0.12 (not significant at the 0.05 level)
- **Effect Size**: Approximately 4% of baseline score; small-to-moderate magnitude

### Validity Checks
- **Pre-trend analysis** shows no evidence that switched and control branches were on different trajectories before the change, supporting the validity of the comparison
- Results are robust to controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

---

## **Interpretation**

The positive point estimate suggests the new procedure may improve service quality, but the statistical uncertainty is substantial. With only 34 branches and 17 weeks of data, the study has **limited power** to detect modest effects. The confidence interval tells us:

- We cannot rule out **no effect** (or even a very small negative effect)
- We also cannot rule out a **meaningful positive effect** of up to 5 points

The control branches experienced a slight decline in scores after week 15, while switched branches maintained their levels—this pattern is consistent with the new procedure helping branches avoid a downward trend.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Continue Monitoring**: Extend data collection for another 8–12 weeks to increase statistical power and confirm whether the positive trend persists

2. **Phased Rollout**: Consider offering the new procedure to additional branches on a voluntary basis while maintaining a control group for ongoing evaluation

3. **Cost-Benefit Analysis**: Assess implementation costs (training, time, materials) against the potential 2–5 point service score improvement to determine whether the benefit justifies system-wide adoption

4. **Qualitative Feedback**: Gather input from branch staff on operational impacts, as service scores alone may not capture all relevant outcomes

5. **Re-evaluate in Q2**: With additional data, conduct a follow-up analysis to make a more definitive recommendation on system-wide implementation
