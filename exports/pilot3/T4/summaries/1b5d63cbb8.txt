# **Memorandum to the Board**

**Subject:** Impact of New Shelving and Rotation Procedure on Service Scores  
**Date:** Analysis of weeks 6–22, switch implemented week 15

---

## **Bottom Line**

Contrary to initial expectations, the new shelving and rotation procedure **did improve service scores**. Branches that adopted the new procedure achieved service scores approximately **1.7 points higher** than would have been expected based on control branch trends. This represents a **3.5% improvement** and is statistically significant (p = 0.002). We recommend proceeding with systemwide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Analysis Overview
- **150 branches** tracked over 17 weeks
- **90 branches** switched to the new procedure at week 15; **60 branches** maintained the old procedure
- Method: Difference-in-differences comparison, isolating the procedure effect from other factors

### Results Summary

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|---------------|---------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 49.9 | 49.8 | -0.1 |
| Non-switched branches | 53.3 | 51.6 | -1.8 |
| **Estimated procedure effect** | | | **+1.7** |

**Interpretation:** While non-switched branches experienced a decline in service scores after week 15, switched branches maintained their performance. The new procedure appears to have **protected against deterioration** in service quality during this period.

### Statistical Confidence
- **Effect estimate:** +1.74 points
- **95% confidence interval:** +0.63 to +2.86 points
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.002 (highly unlikely to occur by chance)
- **Bootstrap validation:** Confirms 95% CI of [+0.79, +2.54]

### Additional Observations
1. **Pre-switch trends were parallel** – the treatment and control groups showed stable relative performance before the change, supporting the validity of the comparison
2. **Effect persists beyond transition** – the benefit remains when excluding the first week after switch (week 15)
3. **Staffing matters** – branches with lower staffing levels saw larger improvements, suggesting the new procedure may reduce operational burden

---

## **Recommendations**

1. **Approve systemwide rollout** of the new procedure to all branches

2. **Prioritize understaffed branches** in the initial rollout phases, as they stand to benefit most

3. **Continue monitoring** service scores quarterly to confirm sustained benefits and identify any branches requiring additional support

4. **Document lessons learned** from the 90 early-adopter branches to support smooth implementation elsewhere

---

## **Caveats**

- The analysis covers an 8-week post-switch period; longer-term effects should be tracked
- Some external factor may have negatively affected control branches after week 15; however, the parallel pre-trends and statistical controls support the conclusion that the procedure change drove the difference
- Effect size is modest but meaningful given the low-cost nature of the procedural change
