# **Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure **improved service scores by approximately 2 points**. Branches that adopted the new procedure maintained and slightly increased their scores, while branches using the old procedure saw scores decline. Although the result falls just short of conventional statistical thresholds due to limited data, the evidence points in a clear positive direction. **We recommend proceeding with a phased rollout** while continuing to monitor outcomes.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **The Numbers**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 47.7 | 46.5 | **−1.2** ↓ |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 50.6 | 51.3 | **+0.7** ↑ |
| **Net Effect of New Procedure** | | | **+1.8** |

### **What We Found**

- **Positive effect**: The new procedure is associated with a **1.8-point improvement** in service scores (approximately 3–4% gain)
- **Diverging trends**: After the switch, treatment and control branches moved in opposite directions—switched branches improved while non-switched branches declined
- **Consistent pattern**: The positive gap between groups widened in 6 of 8 post-switch weeks
- **No negative signals**: We found no evidence that the new procedure harmed service quality at any branch

### **Why This Matters**

The control group's decline suggests external factors (seasonal variation, staffing changes, or other pressures) were putting downward pressure on service scores during this period. **The new procedure appears to have protected branches from this decline** and delivered modest gains on top.

---

## **Confidence in the Results**

| Aspect | Assessment |
|--------|------------|
| Direction of effect | ✓ Clear positive |
| Magnitude | ✓ Meaningful (~2 points) |
| Statistical precision | ⚠ Moderate (95% CI: −0.4 to +4.1) |
| Consistency across weeks | ✓ Most post-switch weeks show gains |
| Risk of harm | ✓ None detected |

**Bottom line on confidence**: We are confident the effect is positive, though the exact size could reasonably range from negligible to moderately large. The risk of negative impact is low.

---

## **Recommendations**

### **Primary Recommendation: Phased Rollout**

Proceed with expanding the new procedure to remaining branches, with the following safeguards:

1. **Roll out in 2–3 waves** over the next quarter rather than all at once
2. **Continue weekly score tracking** to confirm the effect holds at scale
3. **Document implementation details** at each branch to identify best practices

### **Supporting Actions**

| Action | Timeline | Owner |
|--------|----------|-------|
| Begin Wave 1 rollout (5–7 branches) | Immediate | Operations |
| Monitor scores for 4 weeks | Ongoing | Analytics |
| Wave 2 rollout if trends hold | Week 5 | Operations |
| Full evaluation and report | Week 12 | Analytics |

### **If Results Differ at Scale**

Should the effect diminish or reverse during broader rollout:
- Pause further expansion
- Investigate implementation differences
- Reassess before continuing

---

## **Questions the Board May Have**

**Q: Why not wait for more conclusive evidence?**

A: The current evidence already points positive, and delaying rollout means 14 branches continue operating with a procedure that appears less effective. A phased approach balances action with caution.

**Q: What if the effect disappears at scale?**

A: The phased rollout limits exposure. We would catch any issues early and adjust before full implementation.

**Q: Are there any risks we should watch?**

A: We found no negative effects on service scores. We recommend monitoring staff time and patron feedback as secondary metrics during rollout.

---

## **Conclusion**

The new procedure delivered the improvement we expected. While additional data would tighten our estimates, the direction and consistency of results support moving forward. A phased rollout allows us to capture benefits now while maintaining oversight.

---

*Analysis based on 17 weeks of data from 34 branches (578 observations). Difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors.*
