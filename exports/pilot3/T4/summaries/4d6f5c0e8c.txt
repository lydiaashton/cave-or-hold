# **Analysis of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure: Impact on Service Scores**

**Prepared for:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors  
**Date:** November 2026

---

## **Bottom Line**

Contrary to initial expectations, the new stock rotation and shelving procedure **significantly improved service scores**. Branches that adopted the new procedure saw an average increase of **1.65 points** (approximately **3.4%**) in their service scores compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This result is statistically significant and robust across multiple analytical approaches.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22 of the year)
- **90 branches** switched to the new procedure at week 15; **60 branches** continued with the old procedure as a control group
- Analysis compared changes in service scores before and after the switch between the two groups

### Results Summary

| Group | Before Switch (Weeks 6–14) | After Switch (Weeks 15–22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------------|---------------------------|--------|
| New Procedure (90 branches) | 48.49 | 49.58 | **+1.09** |
| Old Procedure (60 branches) | 49.15 | 48.59 | **–0.56** |
| **Net Treatment Effect** | | | **+1.65** |

### Statistical Confidence
- **p-value: 0.004** — less than 1% probability this result occurred by chance
- **95% Confidence Interval:** 0.54 to 2.77 points improvement
- Results hold when controlling for staffing levels and visitor traffic
- Results hold when examining only the weeks immediately surrounding the switch

### Practical Significance
- The cooperative-wide average service score is approximately **49 points**
- A 1.65-point improvement represents a **3.4% gain** in service quality
- This is a meaningful improvement for a key performance metric

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new stock rotation and shelving procedure to all 60 branches still using the old procedure.

2. **Monitor implementation** over the next 8–12 weeks to confirm the effect persists at scale and identify any branches needing additional support.

3. **Document best practices** from the 90 branches that successfully adopted the new procedure to ensure consistent implementation.

4. **Track costs** associated with the new procedure to complete a full cost-benefit analysis, though the service improvement suggests a positive return.

5. **Communicate results** to branch managers to build support and encourage adoption of the new practices.

---

## **Technical Appendix (Summary)**

- **Method:** Difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch)
- **Sample:** 2,550 branch-week observations (150 branches × 17 weeks)
- **Assumption check:** Pre-switch trends between treatment and control groups were not statistically different in the weeks immediately before the switch
- **Software:** Python with statsmodels library
