# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

---

## **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

**Date:** November 2026  
**Data:** 150 branches, 17 weeks of service scores (weeks 6–22)

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Contrary to the expectation that it would make no difference, branches that adopted the new procedure saw their service scores increase by **2.1 points (4.1%)** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and consistent across multiple analytical approaches.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### 1. The New Procedure Had a Positive Effect

| Measure | Value |
|---------|-------|
| Estimated improvement | +2.1 points |
| 95% Confidence Interval | +1.0 to +3.2 points |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 |
| Relative improvement | 4.1% over baseline |

### 2. How We Know This

We compared 90 branches that switched to the new procedure against 60 branches that did not, before and after week 15 when the switch occurred. This "difference-in-differences" approach controls for:
- Pre-existing differences between branches
- System-wide changes affecting all branches simultaneously
- Seasonal or temporal trends

### 3. The Result Holds Up Under Scrutiny

| Analysis | Estimated Effect |
|----------|------------------|
| Main analysis (all weeks) | +2.1 points |
| Excluding anomalous weeks 19–21 | +2.9 points |
| Immediate post-switch period only (weeks 15–18) | +3.0 points |
| Controlling for staffing and visits | +2.1 points |

### 4. Important Context

- **Pre-switch trends were parallel:** Treatment and control branches had stable, consistent differences before the switch, supporting the validity of our comparison.
- **System-wide dip in weeks 19–21:** All branches experienced lower scores during this period (likely an external factor). Treatment branches recovered faster.
- **No evidence of harm:** At no point did the new procedure negatively affect service scores.

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

| Action | Timeline |
|--------|----------|
| **1. Approve full rollout** of the new procedure to remaining 60 branches | Immediate |
| **2. Implement in phases** (20–30 branches per week) to allow for troubleshooting | Weeks 1–4 |
| **3. Monitor service scores weekly** during transition, with attention to weeks 4–6 post-switch | Ongoing |
| **4. Investigate the weeks 19–21 dip** to understand the external factor and prepare contingency plans | Within 30 days |
| **5. Conduct follow-up evaluation** at 3 months to confirm effects are sustained | 3 months post-rollout |

---

## **CAVEATS**

- Analysis covers 17 weeks; longer-term effects remain to be confirmed
- Treatment branches were slightly lower-performing before the switch, though this was accounted for in the analysis
- External disruptions (like the weeks 19–21 dip) may temporarily mask benefits

---

**Conclusion:** The data provide clear evidence that the new stock rotation and shelving procedure improves service quality. The board can proceed with confidence in approving a system-wide rollout.
