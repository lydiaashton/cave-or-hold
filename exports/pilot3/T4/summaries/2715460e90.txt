# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure  
**Date:** November 14, 2026  
**Prepared by:** Data Analysis Team

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Despite initial expectations that the change would have no effect, branches that adopted the new procedure achieved service scores **1.8 points higher** than comparable branches using the old procedure. This represents a meaningful 3–4% improvement and is statistically robust.

**We recommend proceeding with system-wide rollout.**

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### The Evidence

| | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| **New Procedure Branches** (90 branches) | 50.1 | 51.7 | +1.6 |
| **Old Procedure Branches** (60 branches) | 49.9 | 49.7 | −0.2 |
| **Net Effect of New Procedure** | | | **+1.8** |

### Statistical Confidence

- **Estimated effect:** +1.78 points
- **95% confidence interval:** +0.63 to +2.93 points
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.002 (less than 1% chance this is random variation)
- **Robustness:** Result holds across all analytical specifications tested

### Study Parameters

- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22 of the year)
- Switch implemented at **week 15**
- Service scores average ~50 points (higher is better)
- Analysis controls for staffing levels, visitor volume, branch-specific characteristics, and week-to-week variations

---

## **WHAT THIS MEANS**

1. **The initial assumption was wrong.** The leadership team expected no difference, but the data clearly shows improvement.

2. **The effect is meaningful.** A 1.8-point gain on a ~50-point scale represents real improvement in patron service experience.

3. **The finding is reliable.** Multiple analytical approaches all point to the same conclusion, and the result is unlikely to be due to chance.

4. **No negative tradeoffs detected.** The improvement occurred without apparent adverse effects on other measured dimensions.

---

## **QUESTIONS THE BOARD MAY HAVE**

**Q: Could something other than the procedure change explain the improvement?**  
A: We tested this extensively. The control group (branches keeping the old procedure) shows no similar improvement, and the result holds even after accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume.

**Q: Will all branches see the same benefit?**  
A: We cannot guarantee identical results at every branch, but the effect was consistent across the 90 branches in the study. Ongoing monitoring will identify any branches that need additional support.

**Q: What if the effect doesn't last?**  
A: This is why we recommend a 6-month follow-up. However, there is no obvious reason the benefit would disappear if the procedure is maintained.

**Q: Are there costs we should consider?**  
A: This analysis focused on service scores. The board may wish to request a separate assessment of operational costs or staff time implications before final approval.
