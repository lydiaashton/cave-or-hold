# **Library Stock Rotation Procedure: Impact on Service Scores**

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure is associated with a **1.75-point improvement** in service scores (approximately 3.7% increase), but this result is **not statistically significant** at conventional levels. The data suggests a positive trend, but we cannot rule out the possibility that the observed improvement is due to random variation. The board should consider proceeding with caution—either implementing the new procedure with continued monitoring or extending the evaluation period to gather more evidence.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **34 branches** total: 20 switched to the new procedure, 14 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in switched branches against changes in non-switched branches

### Results Summary

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Non-Switched Branches (n=14) | 48.13 | 47.95 | -0.19 |
| Switched Branches (n=20) | 47.93 | 49.50 | +1.57 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+1.75** |

### Statistical Assessment
- **Point estimate**: +1.75 points on the service score index
- **95% confidence interval**: -0.41 to +3.91 points
- **P-value**: 0.11 (not statistically significant at the 5% level)
- **Pre-trends check**: No significant difference in trends between groups before the switch (p=0.74), supporting the validity of the comparison

### Interpretation
- The confidence interval includes zero, meaning we cannot confidently conclude the procedure had a real effect
- However, the interval also allows for improvements as large as 4 points, which would be operationally meaningful
- Service scores in switched branches improved while non-switched branches remained flat or slightly declined

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Continue monitoring**: If the board approves broader rollout, track service scores for an additional 8–12 weeks to increase statistical power and confirm the effect.

2. **Consider practical factors**: Evaluate the new procedure on dimensions beyond service scores, including:
   - Staff time and labor costs
   - Patron feedback and satisfaction
   - Implementation complexity

3. **Phased rollout option**: Consider expanding to additional branches while maintaining a control group for ongoing evaluation.

4. **Investigate heterogeneity**: Analyze whether the procedure works better in certain types of branches (e.g., by size, location, or traffic volume) to target implementation more effectively.

---

*Analysis prepared using difference-in-differences regression with branch-level clustered standard errors. Data covers weeks 6–22 with the procedure switch at week 15.*
