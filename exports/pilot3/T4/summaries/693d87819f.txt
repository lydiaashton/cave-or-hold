# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**TO:** Library Cooperative Board  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 14, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure experienced service score increases of approximately **2 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant and meaningful. We recommend approving a system-wide rollout.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **Study Overview**
- Analyzed 17 weeks of data (weeks 6–22) from all **709 library branches**
- **425 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **284 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Used rigorous difference-in-differences analysis to isolate the procedure's effect

### **Results**

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|----------------|---------------|--------|
| Old procedure (control) | 48.15 | 49.59 | +1.44 |
| New procedure (treatment) | 49.80 | 53.31 | +3.51 |
| **Effect attributable to new procedure** | | | **+2.07** |

- **Estimated improvement:** 2.07 service score points
- **95% confidence interval:** 1.53 to 2.61 points
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001 (highly significant)
- **Practical significance:** ~4% improvement relative to the average score of 50

### **Effect Over Time**
The benefit of the new procedure was not immediate but grew steadily:
- Weeks 1–4 after switch: Small, statistically insignificant effects
- Weeks 5–8 after switch: Clear positive effects of 1.4 to 1.9 points

This pattern suggests staff required approximately one month to fully realize the benefits of the new procedure.

### **Important Context**
Branches that adopted the new procedure had slightly higher service scores even before the switch (49.80 vs. 48.15). Our analysis accounts for this baseline difference. However, this means the estimated effect should be interpreted with appropriate caution.

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all 284 branches currently using the old procedure

2. **Plan for a transition period** of 4–5 weeks per branch, as full benefits take approximately one month to materialize

3. **Monitor service scores** for 8–10 weeks following each branch's conversion to confirm expected improvements

4. **Capture implementation learnings** from the 425 early-adopter branches to support smooth rollout

5. **Consider phased rollout** to allow staff training and adjustment time without overwhelming support resources

---

## **RECOMMENDATION**

**Proceed with system-wide implementation.** The evidence indicates the new procedure produces a real, measurable improvement in service quality. The 2-point gain, while modest in absolute terms, represents meaningful progress on a metric where the average branch scores around 50. With proper training and transition support, we expect similar improvements across all branches.

---

*Technical note: Analysis uses difference-in-differences regression with standard errors clustered by branch. Models control for week effects and account for baseline differences between switching and non-switching branches.*
