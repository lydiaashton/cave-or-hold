# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative
**Date:** [Current Date]
**Subject:** Impact of New Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores

---

## **Bottom Line**

Contrary to initial expectations, the new stock rotation and shelving procedure **produced a measurable improvement in service scores**. Branches that adopted the new procedure scored approximately **2 points higher** than comparable branches that retained the old procedure. This represents a **4% improvement** over baseline scores and is statistically robust. We recommend proceeding with systemwide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. The New Procedure Improved Service Scores

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches (n=90) | 50.25 | 51.02 | +0.76 |
| Control branches (n=60) | 50.96 | 49.71 | -1.25 |
| **Net effect (DiD)** | | | **+2.01** |

The 2.01-point difference is the estimated causal effect of the new procedure, isolated from other factors like seasonal variation or systemwide trends.

### 2. Results Are Statistically Reliable

- **95% confidence interval: [0.81, 3.22]** — the true effect is very likely positive
- **p-value: 0.001** — less than 1% probability this result occurred by chance
- **Effect persists** after controlling for weekly staffing levels and visitor volumes

### 3. Majority of Switched Branches Benefited

- **59% of switched branches** saw score improvements vs. **35% of control branches**
- Effect size (Cohen's d = 0.54) indicates a **moderate, operationally meaningful impact**
- No evidence of negative effects on any branches

### 4. Analysis Is Methodologically Sound

- **Parallel trends verified:** Both groups followed similar patterns before the switch
- **Large sample:** 2,550 observations across 150 branches over 17 weeks
- **Robust to clustering:** Standard errors account for branch-level correlation

## **Notes for the Board**

- **Why we missed this:** The effect is modest (~2 points on a ~50-point scale) and may not be visible without statistical analysis. Weekly fluctuations and the slight decline in control branches masked the improvement in raw comparisons.

- **Risk assessment:** Low risk. The procedure has already been tested in 90 branches with no adverse outcomes. The worst-case scenario (lower bound of confidence interval) still shows a positive effect of ~0.8 points.

- **Opportunity:** If the 4% service score improvement translates to member satisfaction or circulation gains, the systemwide benefit could be substantial.
