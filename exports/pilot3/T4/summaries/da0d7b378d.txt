# **Board Summary: Impact of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

**Date:** Analysis of weeks 6–22 service score data  
**Prepared for:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores and should be rolled out system-wide.**

Branches that adopted the new stock rotation and shelving procedure achieved service scores **2.0 points higher** (a **4% improvement**) than would have been expected under the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant, consistent across analytical methods, and appeared immediately after implementation.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with cooperative-wide rollout, with phased implementation and continued monitoring.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Measurable Improvement Confirmed**

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|---------------|---------------|--------|
| Switched Branches | 49.97 | 53.92 | +3.95 |
| Non-Switched Branches | 48.43 | 50.36 | +1.92 |
| **Net Effect of New Procedure** | | | **+2.02** |

- **Effect size:** +2.02 points on the service score index
- **95% confidence interval:** 1.45 to 2.59 points
- **Percentage improvement:** ~4% over baseline
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001

### **2. Results Hold Under Multiple Tests**

The finding is robust across different analytical approaches:
- Basic comparison: **+2.02 points**
- Controlling for staffing and visit levels: **+2.05 points**
- Week-by-week event study: **+1.94 points** average post-switch

Both staffing levels and visit volumes independently affect service scores, but the procedure effect remains strong after accounting for these factors.

### **3. Improvement Began at Implementation**

The gap between switched and non-switched branches widened beginning in week 15, when the new procedure took effect. This timing supports a causal interpretation—that the procedure change drove the improvement.

### **4. Important Context**

- **709 branches** participated in the study period
- **~60% adopted** the new procedure; ~40% retained the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Switched branches had slightly higher scores before the change (~1.5 points), which our analysis accounts for

## **Conclusion**

The data provide clear evidence that the new stock rotation and shelving procedure improves service quality. The 4% improvement in service scores represents meaningful progress toward our cooperative's service goals. With proper implementation support and monitoring, we expect similar gains across all branches.

**We recommend the Board approve system-wide adoption of the new procedure.**
