# **Memorandum to the Board of Directors**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure  
**Date:** November 2025  
**Prepared by:** Data Analysis Team

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence is inconclusive.** Branches that adopted the new procedure showed a 2-point improvement relative to non-adopting branches, but this result is **not statistically significant** at conventional standards. We cannot confidently determine whether the new procedure actually caused the improvement or whether it reflects random variation. **We do not recommend a system-wide rollout based on this evidence alone.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Overview**
- **34 branches**: 20 adopted the new procedure, 14 maintained the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22), with implementation beginning week 15
- Only **8 weeks of post-implementation data** per adopting branch

### **Service Score Results**

| Group | Before | After | Change |
|-------|--------|-------|--------|
| Branches with old procedure | 47.4 | 47.2 | -0.3 |
| Branches with new procedure | 46.6 | 48.2 | +1.6 |
| **Net difference** | | | **+1.9** |

### **Statistical Assessment**

| Metric | Value | Interpretation |
|--------|-------|----------------|
| Point estimate | +1.9 points | Observed improvement |
| p-value | 0.10 | **Not significant** at 5% threshold |
| 95% Confidence Interval | -0.4 to +4.3 | **Includes zero** |

### **What This Means**

The data are consistent with **three equally plausible scenarios**:
1. The new procedure provides a meaningful benefit (up to +4 points)
2. The new procedure has no effect whatsoever (0 points)
3. The new procedure has a small negative effect (down to -0.4 points)

**We cannot distinguish among these possibilities with the current data.**

---

## **Recommendations**

### **1. Do Not Proceed with System-Wide Rollout Yet**
The current evidence does not support a confident recommendation. A full rollout based on inconclusive results risks implementing a procedure that may not deliver expected benefits.

### **2. Extend the Evaluation Period**
Collect **at least 12–16 additional weeks** of data. This will:
- Improve statistical precision
- Reveal whether the observed effect is sustained or temporary
- Allow detection of smaller but meaningful effects

### **3. Maintain the Current Study Design**
Continue tracking both adopting and non-adopting branches. The control group is essential for isolating the procedure's effect from other factors (seasonal changes, staffing variations, etc.).

### **4. Reconsider in Q2 2026**
With approximately 6 months of post-implementation data, we can provide a statistically reliable assessment and a confident rollout recommendation.

---

## **Conclusion**

While the observed direction of effect is encouraging, **the data do not provide reliable evidence that the new procedure improves service scores.** The board should wait for additional data before committing to a system-wide rollout. This cautious approach protects the cooperative from implementing changes that may not deliver expected benefits.
