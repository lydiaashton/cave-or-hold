# **New Shelving Procedure: Impact on Service Scores**
## Analysis for the Board of Directors

---

### **BOTTOM LINE**

**We cannot confidently conclude that the new procedure caused the improvement in service scores.** While switched branches showed higher scores after the change, the same branches were already improving relative to others *before* the switch. This suggests the observed difference may reflect which branches were selected to pilot the procedure, not the procedure's actual effect. **We do not recommend a full rollout based on this evidence alone.**

---

### **KEY FINDINGS**

**1. What the Raw Data Shows**
- Branches that switched saw scores increase by 2.0 points after the switch
- Control branches saw scores decline slightly (-0.3 points)
- Simple difference-in-differences estimate: **+2.29 points**

**2. The Critical Problem: Pre-Existing Trends**
- **The gap between switched and control branches was already widening BEFORE the intervention**
- Pre-switch trend: gap increased by **0.31 points per week** (p < 0.001)
- Over 8 weeks, this pre-trend alone would predict a **2.51 point** gap increase
- The observed post-switch gap increase was **2.29 points**
- **After accounting for pre-trends, the estimated treatment effect is approximately zero (possibly negative)**

**3. What This Means**
- The parallel trends assumption required for causal inference is **violated**
- Switched branches appear to have been systematically different—possibly better-managed or better-resourced branches were chosen to pilot first
- The 2.29-point "effect" likely reflects this selection bias, not the procedure itself

**4. What We Can Say With Confidence**
- Branches using the new procedure have higher service scores (52.9 vs. 48.6 post-switch)
- No negative effects were detected on staffing or visits
- The procedure does not appear harmful

---

### **NEXT STEPS**

1. **Do NOT proceed with system-wide rollout** based on current evidence

2. **Investigate the selection process**: Why were certain branches chosen to switch? Were they systematically different (better staff, more resources, higher baseline performance)?

3. **Consider a randomized trial**: Randomly assign remaining branches to new vs. old procedure to get an unbiased estimate of the true effect

4. **If proceeding despite uncertainty**:
   - Implement phased rollout with careful monitoring
   - Set clear success metrics and stopping rules
   - Be prepared to reverse course if scores don't improve

5. **Gather qualitative data**: Interview branch managers at switched vs. control locations to understand what else might explain the differences

---

### **BOTTOM LINE (REVISED)**

**The data is inconclusive.** The apparent benefit of the new procedure cannot be distinguished from pre-existing differences between branches. More rigorous evidence is needed before committing to a system-wide rollout.
