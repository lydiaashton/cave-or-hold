## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure at week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in switched branches against changes in non-switched branches

### Results

| Group | Before Switch (Weeks 6–14) | After Switch (Weeks 15–22) | Change |

- **Point Estimate**: +2.19 points improvement attributable to the new procedure
- **95% Confidence Interval**: −0.6 to +5.0 points
- **Statistical Evidence**: p = 0.06 (one-sided, testing for improvement); 94% probability effect is positive
- **Effect Size**: Approximately 4% of baseline score

### Validity Checks
- **Pre-trend analysis** confirms switched and control branches were on similar trajectories before the change
- Results hold when controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

## **Interpretation**

The evidence leans clearly toward a positive effect:

- The **94% probability** of a positive effect is substantial evidence for a practical decision
- The **lower bound of uncertainty** (−0.6 points) suggests minimal risk of harm
- The **upper bound** (+5.0 points) indicates the benefit could be operationally meaningful

The main limitation is **sample size**—with more branches or more weeks of data, we could reach conventional significance levels if the effect is real.
