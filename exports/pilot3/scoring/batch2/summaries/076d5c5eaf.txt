# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Library Cooperative Board  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 14, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure is associated with improved service scores, but the true causal effect is uncertain.** Switched branches showed a 1.9-point greater improvement than non-switched branches, but pre-existing trends suggest the actual effect of the procedure itself may be smaller (approximately **0.5 to 1.0 points**). Given the low cost of implementation and positive association with performance, we recommend a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than immediate system-wide adoption.

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Positive Association, But Causal Effect Is Uncertain
- Switched branches improved by **4.1 points** vs. 2.2 points for non-switched branches
- **Raw difference-in-differences: +1.9 points** (statistically significant)
- **However:** The gap between groups was already widening before the switch (0.25 points/week)
- **Adjusted estimate:** The procedure's true effect is likely **0.5 to 1.0 points**, not the full 1.9

### 2. Why the Uncertainty?
- Branches were **not randomly assigned** to switch or stay with the old procedure
- Switched branches may have been systematically different (more motivated staff, better management, etc.)
- The pre-existing upward trend in the gap suggests some improvement would have occurred even without the procedure

### 3. Still, Evidence Is Mostly Positive
- **87% of switched branches** improved after adoption
- The effect persists after controlling for staffing and visitor volume
- No evidence of harm—no switched branches showed dramatic declines
- Even a 0.5-point improvement would be meaningful at scale

### 4. Study Limitations
- **Non-random assignment** limits causal inference
- **Only 17 weeks of data**—longer-term effects unknown
- **Pre-trends not parallel**—violates key assumption of the analysis method

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with a phased rollout** rather than immediate system-wide adoption
   - Convert 50-100 branches per month
   - Monitor service scores for 4-8 weeks after each cohort switches

2. **Establish clear success metrics** before expanding further
   - Target: at least 0.5-point improvement in service scores
   - If effects are smaller, reconsider the rollout

3. **Document implementation conditions** at high-performing branches
   - Identify what else (training, staffing, leadership) may contribute to success

4. **Consider a randomized evaluation** for future procedure changes
   - Randomly assign branches to treatment/control for cleaner causal evidence

5. **Re-evaluate in 3 months** with additional data to confirm effects persist

---

## **Technical Notes**

- Analysis used difference-in-differences with cluster-robust standard errors
- Pre-trend violation detected: gap was widening 0.25 points/week before intervention
- 709 branches, 12,053 branch-week observations
- Service score average: 50.1 (higher is better)
