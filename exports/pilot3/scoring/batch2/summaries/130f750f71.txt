# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 14, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure produced a modest but statistically significant improvement in service scores.** Branches using the new procedure scored approximately **2 points higher** (about 4%) than they would have under the old procedure. This contradicts the initial expectation of no difference.

**Verdict: The data supports rollout, but the board should weigh this modest gain against implementation costs.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Measurable Improvement in Service Scores**

| Group | Branches | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|----------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched to new procedure | 90 | 50.25 | 51.02 | +0.76 |
| Kept old procedure | 60 | 50.96 | 49.71 | −1.25 |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | — | — | — | **+2.01** |

### **2. Statistical Confidence**
- The 2-point improvement is **statistically significant** (p = 0.001)
- 95% confidence interval: **0.81 to 3.22 points**
- Results hold after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume
- Analysis accounts for branch-specific factors and seasonal trends

### **3. Practical Significance**
- Effect size: **0.21 standard deviations** — a modest but meaningful improvement
- Equivalent to the impact of a ~4-unit increase in staffing or visitor volume
- **59% of switched branches** improved vs. 35% of non-switched branches

### **4. Valid Comparison**
- Before the switch (weeks 6–14), both groups followed parallel trends
- This confirms the comparison is valid and supports causal interpretation

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did the new procedure change service scores? | **Yes** — statistically significant improvement |
| Was the effect large? | **Modest** — approximately 4% gain |
| Did it match expectations? | **No** — we expected no difference |
| Should we roll out system-wide? | **Yes, if costs are low** — the gain is real but modest |

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rollout if implementation costs are minimal. If significant resources are required, the board should conduct a cost-benefit analysis to determine whether a 4% service score improvement justifies the investment.
