# **Revised Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence is inconclusive.** While branches that switched to the new procedure had higher service scores after week 15, this difference **cannot be confidently attributed to the procedure change**. The treatment group was already improving relative to the control group *before* the switch, which undermines the validity of the comparison. **I cannot recommend a system-wide rollout based on this evidence alone.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Critical Problem: Non-Parallel Pre-Trends**

The difference-in-differences method requires that treatment and control groups follow similar trends before the intervention. **This assumption is violated in our data:**

| Week | Treatment − Control Difference |
|------|-------------------------------|
| 6 | −0.86 (treatment behind) |
| 9 | +0.24 (treatment ahead) |
| 14 | +0.99 (treatment further ahead) |

**The treatment group gained 0.24 points per week relative to control *before* the switch.** This pre-existing trend explains much of the post-switch difference.

### **What the Data Actually Shows**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Simple DiD estimate | +1.85 points |
| Pre-treatment trend | +0.24 points/week |
| Expected difference at week 22 (if pre-trend continued) | ~2.9 points |
| **Actual average post-treatment difference** | **2.05 points** |

The post-treatment gap (2.05 points) is actually **smaller** than what the pre-trend would have predicted (~2.9 points). This suggests the new procedure may have had **no additional benefit** beyond the existing trajectory.

### **Alternative Explanation**

The control group's scores declined after week 15 (from 51.15 to 49.65). The treatment group's scores remained stable. This could mean:
- The new procedure protected against a system-wide decline, OR
- The treatment group was already on a different trajectory for unrelated reasons (e.g., better management, more resources, different community demographics)

We cannot distinguish between these explanations with the current data.

---

## **Recommendations**

### **Do NOT proceed with system-wide rollout yet.**

### **Next Steps**

1. **Gather more evidence before deciding:**
   - Collect additional weeks of data to see if the gap widens or narrows
   - Consider a randomized rollout for remaining branches to enable cleaner causal inference

2. **Investigate why branches were selected to switch:**
   - Were higher-performing branches chosen first?
   - Are there systematic differences between switched and non-switched branches?

3. **Control for confounding factors:**
   - Include staffing_index and visits_index in the analysis
   - Check if branch characteristics (size, location, demographics) differ between groups

4. **Consider a pilot expansion:**
   - Randomly assign 50-100 additional branches to switch
   - Compare outcomes against a true control group

---

## **Bottom Line for the Board**

The current analysis **does not provide reliable evidence** that the new procedure causes improved service scores. The branches that switched were already outperforming the control group before the change. **Additional evidence is needed before committing to a system-wide rollout.**
