# **Board Memorandum (Revised)**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** [Current Date]  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure  

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence suggests the new procedure improved service scores, but with important caveats.** Our primary analysis estimates a **1.85-point improvement**, however, branches that adopted the new procedure were already outperforming control branches before the switch. After accounting for pre-existing differences, the **true effect is likely smaller—approximately 0.8 to 1.0 points**. We recommend proceeding with rollout, but with continued monitoring to confirm the effect.

---

## **What We Found**

### **The Complication**

Branches that switched to the new procedure were already on a stronger trajectory before the change:

| Period | Treatment Group | Control Group | Gap |
|--------|-----------------|---------------|-----|
| Weeks 6-8 (early) | Behind by 0.64 pts | — | -0.64 |
| Week 14 (just before switch) | Ahead by 0.99 pts | — | +0.99 |
| Weeks 15-22 (after switch) | Ahead by 2.05 pts | — | +2.05 |

The treatment group went from **behind** to **ahead** even before the procedure changed. This suggests branches that volunteered for the new procedure may have been more motivated, better managed, or otherwise different from those that didn't.

### **Adjusted Estimate**

| Estimate Type | Effect Size | Confidence |
|---------------|-------------|------------|
| Raw DiD estimate | +1.85 points | Statistically significant |
| Adjusted for pre-trends | **+0.8 to +1.0 points** | Less certain |

Even the conservative estimate represents meaningful improvement on the service score index.

---

## **Why We Still Recommend Rollout**

1. **No negative effects observed:** The new procedure did not harm service scores at any branch.

2. **Treatment group maintained performance:** While control branches declined after week 15, treatment branches held steady or improved.

3. **Consistent with expectations:** The direction of effect matches what we anticipated.

4. **Low risk:** If the procedure is not costly to implement, even a modest gain justifies adoption.

---

## **Key Risks & Uncertainties**

| Risk | Likelihood | Mitigation |
|------|------------|------------|
| Effect is smaller than estimated | Moderate | Monitor closely post-rollout |
| Effect doesn't generalize to all branches | Moderate | Phase rollout; track by branch type |
| Pre-existing motivation drove results, not procedure | Possible | Provide training/support during rollout |

---

## **Next Steps**

| Action | Timeline |
|--------|----------|
| Approve phased rollout (not all branches at once) | Current meeting |
| Prioritize branches similar to original treatment group | 2 weeks |
| Track service scores weekly during rollout | Ongoing |
| Re-evaluate after 12 weeks of additional data | 3 months |
| Decide on full system-wide adoption based on results | 3-6 months |

---

## **Bottom Line (Restated)**

The new procedure **likely helps**, but we cannot be as confident in the magnitude as we initially hoped. A **phased rollout with careful monitoring** is the prudent path forward.
