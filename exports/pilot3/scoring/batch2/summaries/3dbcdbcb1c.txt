# **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The data shows a small, statistically significant improvement in service scores, but the effect is modest and inconsistent.** While the result is unexpected (no effect was anticipated), the evidence is **not strong enough to confidently recommend system-wide rollout based on service scores alone**. The board should consider other factors (cost, staff feedback, operational efficiency) before deciding.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Statistical Result
- **Estimated effect: +1.86 points** (95% CI: 0.65 to 3.07; p = 0.003)
- **However**: This is smaller than typical week-to-week variation (3.34 points average)
- **Effect size**: 0.18 standard deviations—modest in practical terms

### 2. Inconsistent Pattern
The treatment effect varies considerably week-to-week after the switch:

| Week | Treatment Advantage |
|------|---------------------|
| 15 | +0.55 |
| 16 | +2.27 |
| 17 | **-0.41** |
| 18 | +1.25 |
| 19 | +1.46 |
| 20 | +0.57 |
| 21 | +0.56 |
| 22 | +0.21 |

The effect appears to fade over time and was negative in week 17.

### 3. Potential Selection Bias
Treatment branches scored lower than control branches in 8 of 9 pre-switch weeks. This suggests branches that adopted the new procedure may have been systematically different (possibly lower-performing branches were more willing to try changes).

### 4. Study Limitations
- Only 8 weeks of post-switch data
- Service scores are inherently volatile
- Model explains <1% of score variation

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Criterion | Assessment |
|-----------|------------|
| Statistical significance | ✓ Significant (p = 0.003) |
| Practical significance | ⚠ Small effect (0.18 SD) |
| Consistency over time | ⚠ Variable week-to-week |
| Study design validity | ⚠ Possible selection bias |
| **Overall confidence** | **LOW TO MODERATE** |

**Verdict: The evidence does not support a service-score-based business case for rollout.** The procedure may still be worthwhile for other reasons, but service score improvement alone is insufficient justification.

---

## **Recommendation**

**Do not base the rollout decision solely on service score improvements.** The statistical evidence, while significant, shows a small and inconsistent effect that may not be practically meaningful.

**Instead:**
1. **Evaluate other metrics** that the procedure was designed to affect (e.g., shelving efficiency, staff time, material availability)
2. **Gather qualitative feedback** from staff at switched branches
3. **Consider cost-benefit** of rollout independent of service scores
4. **If proceeding, monitor closely** with a longer evaluation period

- **Method**: Difference-in-differences with cluster-robust standard errors
- **Result**: Statistically significant (p=0.003) but small and inconsistent effect
- **Caveat**: Pre-existing differences between groups suggest possible selection bias
- **Conclusion**: Evidence is inconclusive for decision-making purposes
