# **REVISED MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 14, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence is inconclusive. Do not proceed with full rollout based on this analysis alone.** While branches that switched to the new procedure show higher service scores, we cannot confidently attribute this to the procedure itself. The branches that adopted the new procedure were already improving faster than other branches **before** the switch occurred, suggesting selection bias rather than a causal effect.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Critical Problem: Pre-Existing Differences

The branches that switched to the new procedure were **not comparable** to those that didn't:

| Metric | Treatment Branches | Control Branches |
|--------|-------------------|------------------|
| Baseline service score (weeks 6-14) | 48.8 | 47.6 |
| Gap at week 6 | -0.2 | — |
| Gap at week 14 (before switch) | +2.7 | — |

**The gap between groups widened by 3.0 points BEFORE the new procedure was implemented.** This violates the fundamental assumption needed to claim the procedure caused the improvement.

### What Happened at the Switch Point?

When analyzing only the weeks immediately surrounding the switch (weeks 13-17):

- **Estimated effect:** 0.22 points
- **95% Confidence Interval:** -0.56 to 1.00 (includes zero)
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.58 (not significant)

There was **no detectable discontinuous jump** in service scores when the new procedure was implemented.

### What the Original 1.86 Point Estimate Actually Captures

The initially reported 1.86 point "treatment effect" likely reflects:
1. **Selection bias** — branches that chose (or were chosen) to switch may have had more motivated staff, better management, or other advantages
2. **Pre-existing trends** — these branches were already improving faster before the switch
3. **NOT a causal effect** of the new procedure itself

---

## **Recommendations**

### 1. Do Not Roll Out System-Wide (Yet)
The current evidence does not support a confident recommendation to expand the procedure to all 284 remaining branches.

### 2. Run a Proper Randomized Trial
To determine whether the new procedure actually works:
- Randomly assign remaining branches to treatment and control groups
- Implement the new procedure only in the treatment group
- Compare outcomes with proper causal inference

### 3. If Proceeding Anyway, Monitor Closely
If the board decides to proceed despite inconclusive evidence:
- Implement in phases with careful tracking
- Establish clear success metrics in advance
- Be prepared to halt rollout if benefits don't materialize

### 4. Investigate Why Some Branches Were First to Adopt
Understanding what distinguished early-adopting branches (management quality, staff training, resources, etc.) may reveal more actionable insights than the procedure change itself.

---

## **Questions for Board Discussion**

1. How were branches selected to switch? Was it voluntary, or based on specific criteria?
2. What are the costs of implementing the new procedure at remaining branches?
3. Would a randomized pilot at 50-100 branches be feasible before full rollout?
4. Are there other outcomes we should track (staff satisfaction, operational efficiency, patron feedback)?
