# **MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** [Current Date]  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure  

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure **does improve service scores** by approximately 2.2 points (4-5% improvement), and this finding is statistically reliable. However, **whether to proceed with system-wide rollout depends on implementation costs**, which were not included in this analysis.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. The New Procedure Improved Service Scores

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches (90) | 49.8 | 51.3 | +1.5 |
| Non-switched branches (60) | 49.8 | 49.1 | -0.6 |
| **Net effect** | | | **+2.2** |

The 2.2-point improvement is **statistically significant** (p < 0.001), with a 95% confidence interval of 0.9 to 3.5 points.

### 2. The Result Is Reliable

- **Parallel pre-trends:** Before the switch, both groups had nearly identical service score trends, validating the comparison
- **Controls applied:** The finding holds after accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume
- **Consistent over time:** Positive effects observed in 7 of 8 post-switch weeks

### 3. Important Limitations

- **Short observation window:** Only 8 weeks of post-switch data; long-term effects unknown
- **No cost data:** Training, transition time, and operational disruption costs not measured
- **Modest effect size:** 2 points on a 50-point scale may or may not justify system-wide change depending on costs

---

## **Analysis Details**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Branches studied | 150 |
| Treatment branches | 90 (switched to new procedure) |
| Control branches | 60 (kept old procedure) |
| Observation period | Weeks 6-22 |
| Pre-switch weeks | 9 (weeks 6-14) |
| Post-switch weeks | 8 (weeks 15-22) |
| Total observations | 2,550 |

**Method:** Difference-in-Differences regression with standard errors clustered by branch.

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did the new procedure change service scores? | **Yes** – improved by ~2.2 points |
| Is this finding statistically reliable? | **Yes** – p < 0.001, robust to controls |
| Should the board roll this out system-wide? | **Conditional recommendation** (see below) |

### Recommendation Framework

| If implementation costs are... | Then... |
|-------------------------------|---------|
| **Low** (minimal training, no disruption) | **Proceed with rollout** – the benefit is clear and free |
| **Moderate** (some training, brief disruption) | **Proceed cautiously** – 4-5% improvement may justify costs |
| **High** (extensive training, significant disruption) | **Delay decision** – collect 3-6 more months of data and conduct formal cost-benefit analysis |

### Suggested Next Steps

1. **Quantify implementation costs** per branch (training hours, staff time, materials)
2. **Compare costs to benefits** – is a 2-point service score improvement worth the investment?
3. **Continue monitoring** the 90 switched branches to confirm effects persist beyond 8 weeks
4. **Reconvene in 90 days** with cost data and updated performance metrics for final rollout decision

---

## **Conclusion**

The data reject the null hypothesis – the new procedure **does** improve service scores. The statistical evidence is strong. The policy decision now rests on whether a ~4% service improvement justifies the cost of changing procedures at 60 additional branches.
