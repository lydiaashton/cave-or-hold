# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Library Cooperative Board  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 14, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure shows **promising evidence of improvement** with no signal of harm. Switched branches improved by approximately **1.8 points** relative to non-switched branches. While this doesn't meet conventional statistical significance thresholds (p = 0.11), the evidence points clearly in a positive direction. **We recommend a phased rollout** to remaining branches, with continued monitoring to confirm benefits at scale.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Overview**
- **34 branches** participated: 20 adopted the new procedure, 14 continued with the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** collected (Weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at Week 15
- Service scores average around 50 points (higher is better)

### **Results Summary**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Control Branches (old procedure) | 47.7 | 46.5 | −1.2 |
| Switched Branches (new procedure) | 50.6 | 51.3 | +0.7 |
| **Net Effect (Difference-in-Differences)** | | | **+1.8** |

### **Statistical Confidence**
- **Estimated effect:** +1.82 service score points (approximately 3–4% improvement)
- **95% confidence interval:** −0.4 to +4.1 points
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.11

### **Interpretation**
- **Direction is clear:** The effect is positive; there is no evidence the new procedure harms service quality
- **Magnitude is uncertain:** The true effect is likely between 0 and +4 points
- **Context matters:** Control branches declined slightly while switched branches held steady or improved

---

## **Final Verdict**

### **Recommendation: Proceed with Phased Rollout**

**Rationale:**

| Consideration | Assessment |
|---------------|------------|
| Direction of effect | ✅ Positive |
| Risk of harm | ✅ None detected |
| Magnitude of benefit | ⚠️ Modest but meaningful |
| Statistical certainty | ⚠️ Inconclusive but suggestive |
| Implementation risk | ✅ Low (phased approach manages this) |

**Why not wait for more data?**
- The evidence already points positive with no red flags
- Branches using the old procedure showed declining scores
- A phased rollout allows real-world learning while spreading benefits
- We can halt or adjust if problems emerge at scale

**Why not full immediate rollout?**
- Uncertainty remains about the exact magnitude of benefit
- Phased approach allows for course correction
- Operational capacity may limit how quickly all branches can transition

---

## **Implementation Plan**

### **Phase 1 (Next 4–6 weeks)**
- Roll out to 5–7 additional branches
- Monitor service scores weekly
- Gather staff feedback on implementation

### **Phase 2 (Weeks 7–12)**
- Expand to remaining branches if Phase 1 shows consistent results
- Continue monitoring for 8 weeks post-rollout

### **Success Criteria**
- Service scores at newly switched branches do not decline
- Average effect remains positive (≥ +1.0 points)
- No significant operational issues reported by staff

### **Stop/Adjust Triggers**
- Service scores decline by more than 2 points at multiple branches
- Staff report significant increases in workload or errors
- Patron complaints increase noticeably

---

## **Questions for the Board**

1. Does the board approve the phased rollout approach?
2. What is the operational capacity for training/supporting branches during transition?
3. Should we establish a formal review checkpoint after Phase 1?
