# **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence suggests the new procedure has a positive average effect, but with important caveats.** Branches that switched saw service scores increase by approximately **1.9 points** on average compared to branches that didn't switch. However, the effect is modest, varies across branches, and 39% of switching branches actually saw scores decline. **A phased rollout with close monitoring is recommended** rather than immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Criterion | Assessment |
|-----------|------------|
| **Statistical Evidence** | ✅ Significant (p = 0.003) |
| **Effect Size** | ⚠️ Modest (+1.86 points, ~3.7%) |
| **Consistency** | ⚠️ Variable (61% positive, 39% negative) |
| **Study Design** | ⚠️ Observational (not randomized) |
| **Data Sufficiency** | ⚠️ Limited (8 weeks post-switch) |

**VERDICT: Proceed with Cautious Expansion**

The new procedure should move forward, but not as an unconditional system-wide mandate. The positive average effect is real and statistically robust, yet the variation in outcomes suggests the procedure may work better in some contexts than others. A **phased rollout with active monitoring** balances the opportunity for improvement against the risk of disappointing results at some branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

| Finding | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Average Treatment Effect** | +1.86 points (95% CI: 0.65 to 3.07) |
| **Statistical Significance** | p = 0.003 |
| **Relative Improvement** | ~3.7% above baseline |
| **Branches with Positive Effect** | 61% of switching branches |
| **Branches with Negative Effect** | 39% of switching branches |
| **Pre-Trend Validation** | Passed—groups tracked similarly before switch |

**What the data shows:**
- The average effect is positive and statistically significant
- However, there is substantial variation: some branches improved by 7+ points, others declined by similar amounts
- The effect does not appear to depend on staffing levels or visitor volume
- Control branches (old procedure) saw a slight average decline (-0.62 points)

---

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with a phased rollout**, not immediate system-wide implementation:

1. **Expand to additional branches** in stages (e.g., 10-15 branches at a time)
2. **Monitor each cohort** for 4-6 weeks before expanding further
3. **Identify characteristics** of branches that benefit most vs. least
4. **Be prepared to adjust** the procedure or provide additional support to branches that struggle

---

## **Next Steps**

| Priority | Action |
|----------|--------|
| **High** | Document the procedure in detail to ensure consistent implementation |
| **High** | Establish monitoring dashboard to track service scores during rollout |
| **Medium** | Interview branches with strong positive/negative outcomes to understand why |
| **Medium** | Set a review point (e.g., after 50% rollout) to reassess before continuing |
| **Low** | Consider a formal randomized rollout for remaining branches to strengthen evidence |

---

## **Limitations to Acknowledge**

- **Observational study**: Branches were not randomly assigned to switch
- **Short timeframe**: Only 8 weeks of post-switch data
- **Modest effect**: Average improvement is ~2 points on a ~50 point scale
- **Heterogeneous effects**: Not all branches benefit equally

---

## **Technical Appendix**

- **Method**: Difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors
- **Controls**: Staffing index, visits index
- **Data**: 2,550 observations, 150 branches, 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- **Parallel trends test**: p = 0.801 (no evidence of differential pre-trends)
