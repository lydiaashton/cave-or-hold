# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure did not improve service scores.** While the statistical analysis shows a significant difference between treatment and control groups, this reflects that control branches declined while treatment branches held steady—not that the new procedure produced gains. Since the board expected no effect on service scores, and treatment branches showed no actual improvement, **we do not recommend systemwide rollout based on service score benefits alone.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### What Actually Happened

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Treatment (new procedure) | 49.9 | 49.8 | **-0.1** |
| Control (old procedure) | 53.3 | 51.6 | **-1.8** |

**Critical insight:** Treatment branches did not improve. They remained essentially flat. The statistical "effect" (+1.7 points) exists only because control branches declined more sharply.

### Why This Matters

1. **No absolute improvement**: Branches using the new procedure did not achieve higher service scores than they had before the change.

2. **Control group anomaly**: The control group experienced unusual volatility after week 15, including a -5.6 point drop in week 15 alone. This suggests external factors may have affected control branches disproportionately.

3. **Weekly gaps narrowed, but treatment never led**: The treatment-control gap went from -3.4 (pre) to -1.8 (post), but treatment branches remained below control branches throughout the study period.

4. **Effect is relative, not absolute**: The new procedure may have helped branches maintain stability during a period when other branches struggled, but it did not enhance service quality.

### Statistical Summary

- DiD estimate: +1.75 points (p = 0.002)
- However, this reflects **differential decline**, not improvement
- Effect size: 0.19 standard deviations (small)

---

## **Final Verdict**

**DO NOT ROLLOUT** the new procedure systemwide based on service score evidence.

| Criterion | Finding | Verdict |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| Expected outcome | No effect on service scores | ✓ Confirmed |
| Absolute improvement | Treatment branches flat (-0.1 pts) | ✗ Not achieved |
| Statistical significance | p = 0.002 | ✓ Significant, but driven by control decline |
| Practical significance | 0.19 SD (small effect) | ✗ Not meaningful |
| Consistency | High week-to-week variability | ✗ Unstable |

**Rationale:** The board's expectation—that this procedural change would not affect service scores—was essentially correct. Treatment branches showed no meaningful improvement. The statistically significant result reflects that control branches declined more sharply, likely due to external factors unrelated to the procedure itself. Rolling out the new procedure systemwide would incur implementation costs without delivering measurable service quality gains.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Do not proceed with systemwide rollout** based on service score evidence—the data does not support the claim that the new procedure improves service.

2. **Investigate the control group decline**: Determine what happened to control branches after week 15. Was there a systemwide issue, seasonal effect, or measurement change that affected them disproportionately?

3. **Consider other evaluation criteria**: If the new procedure has other benefits (efficiency, staff satisfaction, cost savings, shelf organization), evaluate those separately. The service score alone does not justify rollout.

4. **Extend monitoring**: If rollout proceeds for other reasons, continue tracking service scores to confirm treatment branches maintain stability over a longer period.

5. **Report to the board**: The expected outcome (no effect on service scores) is largely what the data shows. Treatment branches neither improved nor declined meaningfully.
