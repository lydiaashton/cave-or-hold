# **Board Memorandum (Revised)**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors

**Date:** January 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure shows modest average improvement, but results vary significantly across branches.** While the statistical analysis indicates a positive effect of approximately 2 points, this masks important concerns:

- Switched branches improved by only **0.76 points on average** (the larger DiD estimate reflects control branches declining)
- **41% of switched branches saw no improvement or got worse**
- Branches that volunteered to switch had **lower scores before the change**, suggesting possible selection bias

**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **phased, monitored rollout** rather than immediate systemwide implementation. Prioritize branches similar to those that benefited most, and establish clear success metrics before full adoption.

---

## **Key Findings**

### What the Data Shows

| Measure | Branches with New Procedure | Branches with Old Procedure |
|---------|----------------------------|----------------------------|
| Number of branches | 90 | 60 |
| Average score change | +0.76 points | -1.25 points |
| Branches that improved | 59% | 35% |
| Branches with meaningful gain (>2 pts) | 36% | — |

### Important Caveats

1. **Modest absolute improvement**: Treated branches improved by less than 1 point on their own. The 2-point "treatment effect" largely reflects control branches declining during the same period.

2. **Substantial variation**: Among switched branches, score changes ranged from -9.6 to +8.3 points. The median improvement was 0.92 points, but the 25th percentile actually declined (-1.61 points).

3. **Possible selection bias**: Branches that switched had lower average scores before the change (-0.71 points vs. control). Some apparent improvement may reflect regression to the mean rather than the procedure itself.

4. **Limited observation window**: Only 8 weeks of post-switch data. Longer-term effects remain unknown.

---

## **Recommendations**

### 1. Conditional Rollout (Not Immediate Systemwide)
- Extend the procedure to additional branches in **phases of 20-30 branches**
- Require **4-6 weeks of monitoring** before proceeding to the next phase
- Establish a **minimum improvement threshold** (e.g., 1+ points) to continue rollout

### 2. Identify Which Branches Benefit Most
- Analyze branch characteristics (size, location, staffing, baseline scores) associated with improvement
- Prioritize rollout to branches with similar profiles to the 36% that saw meaningful gains

### 3. Investigate Non-Responders
- Conduct qualitative interviews at branches where scores declined
- Identify implementation barriers or contextual factors that limit effectiveness

### 4. Cost-Benefit Assessment
- Quantify staff time, training, and operational costs of the new procedure
- Compare against the modest service score gains to confirm net value

### 5. Report Back to Board
- Provide updated analysis after 2-3 rollout phases
- Recommend continuation, modification, or halt based on accumulated evidence
