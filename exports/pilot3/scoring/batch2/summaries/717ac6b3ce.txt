# REVISED MEMORANDUM

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 14, 2026  
**Subject:** Impact of New Branch Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## Bottom Line

**We cannot determine whether the new procedure caused the observed differences in service scores.** The analysis has a fundamental limitation: branches that switched to the new procedure were already on a different trajectory than those that did not, before the switch occurred. This makes it impossible to isolate the procedure's effect from pre-existing differences.

**Recommendation: Do not proceed with system-wide rollout based on this evidence alone.** Consider a controlled pilot or randomized evaluation before expanding.

---

## Key Findings

### 1. The Core Problem: Non-Comparable Groups

The branches that chose to switch were **already improving relative to control branches** before the new procedure began:

| Week | Treatment vs. Control Gap |
|------|--------------------------|
| Week 6 | −0.86 points (treatment behind) |
| Week 10 | +0.15 points (treatment ahead) |
| Week 14 | +0.99 points (treatment ahead) |

**This 1.85-point swing occurred BEFORE the intervention.** The groups were not on parallel trends, which violates the fundamental assumption needed to claim causation.

### 2. What Happens When We Account for Pre-Existing Trends?

If the pre-switch trend had continued without any procedure change, we would have **expected** the treatment-control gap to reach approximately **2.50 points** in the post-period. The **actual** gap was **2.05 points**.

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Expected gap (if pre-trend continued) | +2.50 points |
| Actual observed gap | +2.05 points |
| **Effect beyond pre-trend** | **−0.45 points** |

This suggests the new procedure may have had **no positive effect** beyond what was already happening—or possibly even a slight negative effect.

### 3. What We Can Confidently Say

| Finding | Confidence |
|---------|------------|
| Switched branches maintained service scores | ✓ Yes |
| Control branches declined in the same period | ✓ Yes |
| The two groups were different before the switch | ✓ Yes |
| The new procedure **caused** the difference | ✗ **Cannot determine** |

### 4. Likely Explanation

Branches that volunteered to switch may differ systematically from those that did not:
- More motivated staff or leadership
- Better resources or management
- Different patron demographics
- Other unobserved factors

These differences—not the procedure itself—may explain the diverging outcomes.

## Next Steps

### Recommended Actions

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| **1** | **Pause system-wide rollout** | Current evidence does not support causal claim |
| **2** | Design a proper evaluation | Use randomized assignment or matched comparison |
| **3** | Run a controlled pilot | Randomly assign some branches to new procedure, others to status quo |
| **4** | Gather qualitative data | Interview staff at switched branches about implementation |

### Proper Evaluation Design

To determine whether the procedure actually works:

1. **Randomized Controlled Trial**: Randomly assign branches to new vs. old procedure
2. **Matched Comparison**: Pair similar branches and assign one of each pair to treatment
3. **Stepped Wedge Design**: Roll out gradually with random timing across branches
4. **Continue monitoring**: Track all branches for several more months regardless of decision

---

## Technical Notes

**Why the original analysis was misleading:**

The difference-in-differences method requires that treatment and control groups would have followed similar trends absent the intervention (the "parallel trends assumption"). Our event study analysis shows this assumption is **violated**:

- 7 of 8 pre-period coefficients were statistically significant
- Pre-period trend slope: +0.27 points per week favoring treatment
- Post-period effect beyond pre-trend: −0.45 points (t = −3.00, p < 0.01)

**Data source:** branches.csv (12,053 observations, 709 branches, weeks 6–22)
