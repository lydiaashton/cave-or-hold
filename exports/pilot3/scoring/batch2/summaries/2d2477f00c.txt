# **Board Summary: Impact of New Branch Procedure on Service Scores**

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure is associated with a **2.4-point increase in service scores**, but this result is **not statistically significant at conventional levels** (p=0.095). The 95% confidence interval ranges from -0.4 to +5.2 points, meaning the true effect could be zero, slightly negative, or meaningfully positive.

**Recommendation:** Do not proceed with full rollout yet. Instead, **extend the pilot period** to gather more data, or proceed with a **phased rollout with continued evaluation**. The evidence is suggestive but not conclusive enough to justify system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
- **34 branches** tracked over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure at week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Analysis uses difference-in-differences to isolate the procedure's effect

### Service Score Impact

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|----------------|---------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 49.5 | 50.2 | +0.7 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 51.6 | 54.7 | +3.1 |
| **Net Effect of New Procedure** | | | **+2.4** |

### What the Statistics Tell Us

| Metric | Value | Interpretation |
|--------|-------|----------------|
| Point Estimate | +2.4 points | Best guess of the true effect |
| P-value | 0.095 | Not significant at 5% level |
| 95% Confidence Interval | -0.4 to +5.2 | True effect could be zero or negative |
| Statistical Significance | **No** | Cannot confidently distinguish from random variation |

### Why the Uncertainty?
- Only **8 weeks of post-switch data** limits statistical power
- Service scores have natural week-to-week variation (standard deviation ~8-12 points)
- The effect size (+2.4) is meaningful but modest relative to this variation

### Validity Checks
✓ **Parallel pre-trends confirmed:** Treatment and control branches moved together before the switch  
✓ **No confounding detected:** Effect persists after controlling for staffing and visitor volume  
✓ **No evidence of harm:** Lower bound of confidence interval is only slightly below zero

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

### **Do not approve full rollout at this time.**

**Reasoning:**
1. **The evidence is inconclusive.** A p-value of 0.095 means there is roughly a 1 in 10 chance this result occurred by random variation—even if the procedure had no real effect. This does not meet the standard of evidence typically required for system-wide policy changes.

2. **The confidence interval includes zero.** We cannot rule out that the true effect is zero or slightly negative. Committing all 34 branches to the new procedure based on this evidence would be premature.

3. **There is no urgency or evidence of harm.** The lower bound of the confidence interval (-0.4 points) suggests minimal risk of negative impact. This allows time to gather better evidence without pressing operational need.

4. **More data will clarify the picture.** Extending the observation period by 8-12 weeks would substantially narrow the confidence interval and provide a clearer basis for decision-making.

**Path Forward:**
- **Option A (Recommended):** Continue the pilot for 8-12 more weeks, then reconvene with updated analysis
- **Option B (Acceptable):** Proceed with phased rollout to 5-7 additional branches while maintaining evaluation
- **Not Recommended:** Full immediate rollout to all remaining branches
