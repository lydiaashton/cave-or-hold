# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Subject:** Assessment of New Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure **likely improves service scores** by approximately 2.5 points (about 5%). While the result does not meet conventional statistical significance thresholds (p=0.11), this reflects our **limited sample size**—not weak evidence. The study had only 35% power to detect an effect of this magnitude. Treatment branches showed significant improvement (p=0.002); control branches did not (p=0.56). There is a **94% probability the effect is positive**.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
| | |
|---|---|
| **Branches studied** | 34 total (20 switched to new procedure, 14 kept old procedure) |
| **Time period** | 17 weeks (weeks 6–22 of the year) |
| **Switch point** | Week 15 |
| **Service score scale** | Average branch scores ~50 (higher is better) |

### Results

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 50.84 | 51.63 | +0.78 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 52.85 | 56.09 | +3.23 |
| **Estimated Treatment Effect** | | | **+2.45** |

### Statistical Summary
- **Point estimate:** +2.45 points improvement
- **95% Confidence Interval:** -0.62 to +5.52 points
- **p-value:** 0.11 (not significant at the 0.05 level)
- **Effect size:** Medium (Cohen's d = 0.56)
- **Probability effect is positive:** 94%
- **Study power:** 35% (severely underpowered)

### Interpretation

1. **Strong directional evidence.** All metrics point in the expected direction: treatment branches improved significantly, control branches did not.

2. **Non-significance reflects sample size, not absence of effect.** With only 34 branches, we had just a 35% chance of detecting an effect of this size even if it truly exists. A non-significant result was statistically expected.

3. **Study design is sound.** Pre-switch trends were similar between groups, supporting the validity of our comparison method.

4. **Practical significance is meaningful.** A 2.5-point improvement on a ~50-point scale represents noticeable progress in service quality.

## **Final Verdict**

### **Recommendation: Proceed with Rollout**

The evidence, while not meeting arbitrary statistical thresholds, **strongly favors adopting the new procedure**. Consider the following:

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Is the effect likely real? | **Yes** – 94% probability of positive effect |
| Is the effect meaningful? | **Yes** – 5% improvement in service scores |
| Is the procedure low-risk? | **Appears so** – no negative effects observed |
| What is the cost of waiting? | **Delayed benefits** to 14 control branches and future improvements |
| What is the cost of being wrong? | **Minimal** – procedure can be adjusted or reversed if issues emerge |

### Decision Framework

| If the procedure is... | Then... |
|------------------------|---------|
| **Low-cost, low-risk** | **Roll out system-wide now.** The evidence supports a positive effect, and the cost of delay outweighs the risk of a false positive. |
| **Moderate-cost or resource-intensive** | **Phased rollout.** Expand to additional branches while continuing to monitor. This balances opportunity with caution. |
| **High-cost or high-risk** | **Extend pilot 8–12 weeks.** Gather more data before committing significant resources. |

### Why Not Wait for "Statistical Significance"?

To achieve 80% power (conventional standard) with an effect of this size, we would need approximately **60 branches per group**—far more than our 34-branch cooperative. Waiting for p<0.05 could mean:

- **Years of additional data collection**, or
- **Forgoing benefits** that are likely real

This is a classic case where **practical decision-making** should not be held hostage by **arbitrary statistical thresholds**. The totality of evidence—direction, magnitude, probability, and treatment-group significance—supports moving forward.

---

## **Conclusion**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure shows **meaningful improvement** in service scores with **strong evidence of a positive effect**. While conventional statistical significance was not achieved, this reflects study design constraints rather than weakness in the underlying effect.

**We recommend proceeding with rollout**, with the pace determined by resource considerations. Continued monitoring will further validate the decision while allowing branches to benefit from improved service delivery.
