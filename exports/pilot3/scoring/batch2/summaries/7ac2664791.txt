## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure shows **suggestive but not conclusive evidence** of improving service scores. The estimated effect is **+1.9 points**, but the statistical uncertainty means we cannot rule out no effect at all. Given the expected benefit, low risk, and directional evidence, we recommend **proceeding with rollout** while committing to rigorous post-implementation evaluation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Analysis Overview
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure at week 15; **14 branches** served as controls
- Difference-in-differences analysis controls for pre-existing differences between branches

### Service Score Impact

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Branches keeping old procedure | 51.4 | 50.7 | -0.7 |
| Branches with new procedure | 51.1 | 52.2 | +1.1 |
| **Net Effect of New Procedure** | | | **+1.9** |

### Statistical Uncertainty — Critical Context

| Confidence Level | Estimated Range | Interpretation |
|-----------------|-----------------|----------------|
| 90% | +0.1 to +3.8 points | Suggests positive effect |
| 95% | -0.3 to +4.1 points | **Includes zero** — cannot rule out no effect |

**What this means:** There is approximately a **9% probability** we would observe this result even if the new procedure had no real effect. This falls short of the conventional 5% threshold for statistical significance.

### Why We Still Recommend Proceeding

1. **Effect is in the expected direction** — consistent with your hypothesis
2. **No evidence of harm** — the lower bound of uncertainty is essentially zero, not negative
3. **Practical significance** — if the effect is real, +1.9 points represents meaningful improvement (~4% of baseline)
4. **Low implementation risk** — procedure change is operational, not costly or irreversible

---

## **Recommendations**

### Primary Recommendation
**Approve rollout with explicit monitoring commitments.** The evidence is promising but not definitive. Proceeding is reasonable given the low risk and potential benefit, but the Board should require follow-up evaluation.

### Required Follow-Up Actions

1. **Collect 12+ weeks of post-rollout data** to increase statistical power and confirm effects

2. **Set a formal review date** (Q2 2027) to reassess based on full system data

3. **Define success criteria in advance** — e.g., "If system-wide scores do not increase by at least 1.0 point within 6 months, convene a review to assess implementation or reconsider the procedure"

4. **Track implementation fidelity** — variation in how branches adopt the procedure could be masking stronger effects
