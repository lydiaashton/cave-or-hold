# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

---

## **Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

**Date:** Analysis of weeks 6–22  
**Prepared for:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors

---

### **BOTTOM LINE**

**The evidence suggests the new procedure improved service scores, but with important caveats.** Branches that adopted the new procedure showed service score gains of approximately **2.1 points** relative to branches that kept the old procedure. However, the control group was already on a declining trajectory before the switch, which may inflate the apparent effect size.

> **Recommendation: Proceed with caution.** Consider a **phased rollout** with rigorous monitoring rather than immediate system-wide implementation. Additional data collection would strengthen the evidence base.

---

### **KEY FINDINGS**

**Main Result**

| Group | Pre-Switch Average | Post-Switch Average | Change |
|-------|-------------------|---------------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 49.91 | 50.73 | +0.82 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 51.12 | 54.04 | +2.92 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+2.10** |

- Effect is **statistically significant** (p < 0.001)
- **95% confidence interval:** 1.37 to 2.83 points

**Critical Limitation: Parallel Trends Violation**

The difference-in-differences method assumes both groups would have followed similar trajectories absent the intervention. **This assumption is not met:**

| Group | Pre-Period Trend |
|-------|------------------|
| Control | **-0.36 points/week** (declining, p < 0.001) |
| Treatment | -0.03 points/week (stable, p = 0.68) |

The control group was already deteriorating before the switch while the treatment group held steady. This means:
- Some portion of the 2.1-point "effect" may reflect the control group's continued decline rather than genuine improvement from the new procedure
- The **true effect is likely smaller than 2.1 points**, though probably still positive
- We cannot precisely quantify how much of the effect is real vs. artifactual

**Supporting Evidence**
- Treatment branches maintained their advantage throughout all 8 post-switch weeks
- No evidence of the effect diminishing over time
- Result is robust to controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

---

### **WHAT WE CAN AND CANNOT CONCLUDE**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did service scores improve in switching branches? | **Yes** – absolute scores rose from 51.1 to 54.0 |
| Did the new procedure cause the improvement? | **Probably, but not certainly** – alternative explanations exist |
| How large is the true effect? | **Uncertain** – likely positive but smaller than 2.1 points |
| Should we roll out system-wide? | **Proceed with caution** – monitor closely, consider phased approach |

---

### **FINAL VERDICT**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Statistical Significance** | ✓ Passed (p < 0.001) |
| **Effect Size** | ⚠ Uncertain (2.1 pts is likely an upper bound) |
| **Parallel Trends** | ✗ Failed (control group was declining) |
| **Evidence of Harm** | ✓ None detected |
| **Practical Significance** | ⚠ Modest (≈4% improvement, possibly less) |

**Overall Assessment: PROCEED WITH MONITORED ROLLOUT**

The balance of evidence favors adoption, but confidence in the estimated benefit is moderate rather than high. The new procedure appears to help and does no harm, but the true magnitude of improvement is uncertain. A phased implementation with continued measurement will allow the cooperative to capture likely benefits while building stronger evidence for the full business case.

---

### **NEXT STEPS**

| Action | Rationale | Timeline |
|--------|-----------|----------|
| **Phased rollout** (50% of remaining branches) | Limits risk if effect is smaller than estimated | 1–2 months |
| **Extend observation window** | More post-switch data will improve estimate precision | Ongoing |
| **Track individual branch trajectories** | Identify whether effect varies by branch characteristics | Ongoing |
| **Re-analyze after 12 weeks of additional data** | Better evidence for full rollout decision | Quarter 2 |
| **Consider randomized rollout** for remaining branches | Would provide cleaner causal evidence for future decisions | Future |

---

### **CONCLUSION**

The new procedure is associated with higher service scores, and there is no evidence of harm. However, the methodological limitations mean we cannot be confident in the magnitude of the benefit. A **cautious, monitored rollout** balances the likely upside against the uncertainty in our estimates.
