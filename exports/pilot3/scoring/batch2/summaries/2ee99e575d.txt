# **Memorandum to the Board**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure  
**Date:** November 2026

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence is inconclusive.** While branches using the new procedure have higher service scores, we cannot confidently attribute this to the procedure itself. The treatment branches were already outperforming control branches **before** the switch was implemented, suggesting systematic differences between the groups that bias our estimates.

---

## **Key Findings**

### The Core Problem: Non-Parallel Trends

| Week | Treatment - Control Gap |
|------|-------------------------|
| 6 (baseline) | -0.23 |
| 14 (pre-switch) | +2.73 |
| 15-22 (post-switch) | +3.05 (average) |

**The gap widened by 2.96 points BEFORE the switch, but only 0.32 points AFTER.**

### What This Means

The standard difference-in-differences estimate of +1.86 points likely **overstates** the true effect because:
1. Treatment branches were already on a stronger improvement trajectory
2. The switch itself did not produce a visible "jump" in the gap
3. Weeks 15-16 actually showed the gap narrowing slightly

### Alternative Explanations

- Branches selected for early adoption may have been better-managed or better-staffed
- Unobserved factors (not captured by staffing_index or visits_index) may drive both the selection to switch AND the score improvements
- The new procedure may have no effect, a smaller effect, or even a negative effect masked by pre-existing trends

---

## **Recommendation**

**Do NOT proceed with system-wide rollout based on current evidence.** Instead:

1. **Pause the rollout** until we have clearer evidence
2. **Investigate why** treatment branches were improving faster before the switch
3. **Consider a randomized trial** where branches are randomly assigned to switch, ensuring comparable groups
4. **Extend the observation period** to see if effects emerge over longer time horizons
5. **Gather qualitative feedback** from branches about the new procedure's practical impact

---

## **Final Verdict**

**Cannot recommend rollout.** The analysis fails to demonstrate that the new procedure causes improved service scores. The observed association is confounded by pre-existing differences between branches that adopted the procedure and those that did not. Approving system-wide implementation on this basis would be investing in an unproven change with uncertain returns.
