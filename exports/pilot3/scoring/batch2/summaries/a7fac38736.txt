# **Memorandum to the Board**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative
**From:** Data Analysis Team
**Date:** November 14, 2026
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**We cannot confidently conclude the new procedure caused the improvement.** While branches using the new procedure do show higher service scores after the switch, the study design has a critical limitation: branches that switched were already on different trajectories than those that didn't **before** the change occurred. This means the observed ~1.8 point difference may reflect pre-existing branch differences rather than the procedure's effect.

**Recommendation: Do not proceed with system-wide rollout based on this evidence alone.** Consider a randomized trial or gather additional evidence before making a system-wide decision.

---

## **Key Findings**

### What the Data Show

| Observation | Finding |
|-------------|---------|
| Post-switch scores | Branches with new procedure averaged **1.8 points higher** |
| Statistical association | Highly significant (p < 0.001) |
| Consistency | Pattern holds across multiple weeks after switch |

### The Critical Problem: Pre-Existing Differences

The difference-in-differences method requires that treatment and control groups follow **parallel trends** before the intervention. Our data show this assumption is **violated**:

| Issue | Evidence |
|-------|----------|
| **Pre-trend divergence** | Treatment and control groups had significantly different week-to-week patterns before the switch (p = 0.0006) |
| **All pre-periods significant** | All 8 pre-switch weeks show statistically significant differences between groups |
| **Week 13 anomaly** | An unusual spike in week 13 affected both groups but differently |

### What This Means

The branches selected to switch procedures were **systematically different** from those that kept the old procedure. These differences existed before the switch and likely explain at least part of the observed post-switch gap. Possible explanations include:

- Better-managed branches were chosen to pilot the new procedure
- Branches with more resources self-selected into the switch
- Community characteristics differ between the two groups

---

## **What We Can and Cannot Conclude**

| We CAN Say | We CANNOT Say |
|------------|---------------|
| ✓ Branches using the new procedure have higher scores | ✗ The procedure **caused** the improvement |
| ✓ The association is statistically significant | ✗ Rollout **will** improve scores at other branches |
| ✓ The pattern is consistent over time | ✗ The true effect size is 1.8 points |

---

## **Next Steps**

### Recommended Actions

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| **1** | **Pause rollout decision** | Current evidence doesn't support causal claim |
| **2** | **Investigate selection process** | Understand why certain branches switched |
| **3** | **Consider randomized trial** | Random assignment would enable causal inference |
| **4** | **If proceeding, monitor closely** | Track whether non-pilot branches see similar gains |

### If the Board Chooses to Proceed Anyway

Given the positive association (even if causality is uncertain), the board may still wish to proceed. In that case:

1. **Set realistic expectations**: Effects may be smaller than 1.8 points
2. **Implement rigorous monitoring**: Track service scores at each branch during rollout
3. **Be prepared to adjust**: Have a plan to modify or halt rollout if benefits don't materialize
4. **Document implementation**: Ensure the procedure is actually being followed as designed

---

## **Conclusion**

The data show a positive **association** between the new procedure and service scores, but the non-random assignment of branches to the new procedure means we cannot attribute this to the procedure itself. The branches that switched may have been better positioned to achieve higher scores regardless of the procedure change.

**We recommend gathering stronger evidence before committing to a system-wide rollout.**
