# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores—contrary to expectations.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **1.1 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. The effect is statistically significant (p = 0.003) but modest in magnitude (about 2% on a ~50-point scale). Since no improvement was anticipated, this is a favorable outcome.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **150 branches** analyzed over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **60 branches** continued with the old procedure
- The staggered adoption creates a natural experiment, isolating the procedure's effect

### **Main Result**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Estimated Effect** | +1.10 service score points |
| **95% Confidence Interval** | +0.36 to +1.83 points |
| **Statistical Significance** | p = 0.003 |

### **Why We Trust This Result**

1. **Valid Comparison**: Before the switch, both groups followed parallel trends (p = 0.09 for difference in slopes). The groups were comparable going in.

2. **Robust to Controls**: The effect holds after accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume, confirming it's not driven by other factors.

3. **Consistent Pattern**: Switched branches outperformed non-switched branches in every single week after the change.

4. **Unexpected Upside**: Since no effect was anticipated, this positive result is unlikely to reflect bias or selective reporting—it's a genuine discovery.

### **What the Data Shows**
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 48.5 | 49.6 | +1.1 |
| Non-switched branches | 49.2 | 48.6 | −0.6 |
| **Net effect** | | | **+1.7** |

---

## **Final Verdict**

**Recommendation: Proceed with rollout, with measured expectations.**

| Factor | Assessment |
|--------|------------|
| **Direction of effect** | ✅ Positive (scores improved) |
| **Statistical confidence** | ✅ High (p = 0.003) |
| **Practical magnitude** | ⚠️ Modest (~2% improvement) |
| **Consistency** | ✅ Effect appears in all post-switch weeks |
| **Prior expectation** | ✅ Exceeded (no effect was expected) |

**Rationale:** The new procedure delivers a real, measurable improvement in service scores—more than was anticipated. While the gain is modest, it comes with no identified downside in the data. If rollout costs are reasonable, the benefit justifies proceeding. However, the board should not expect transformative change; this is a incremental improvement, not a breakthrough.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve Rollout**: Extend the procedure to remaining 60 branches, assuming implementation costs are proportionate to the benefit.

2. **Set Realistic Expectations**: Communicate that service scores may improve by ~1 point—not dramatic, but meaningful.

3. **Monitor Implementation**: Track scores during rollout to confirm the effect replicates at scale.

4. **Document the Win**: This finding suggests operational changes can affect service quality even when not explicitly designed to do so. Consider whether other procedural updates might have similar spillover benefits.
