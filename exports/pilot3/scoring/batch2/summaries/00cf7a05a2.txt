# **Library Stock Rotation Procedure: Impact on Service Scores**

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure shows a **positive effect of 1.75 points** (3.7% improvement) on service scores. While this result falls just short of conventional statistical significance (p=0.11), the evidence is **suggestive of a real benefit**. The study has limited statistical power—we lack enough data to detect an effect of this size with high confidence. Given the positive direction, sound methodology, and meaningful effect size, the board may reasonably approve rollout **with continued monitoring** to confirm the benefit.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **34 branches** total: 20 switched to the new procedure, 14 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in switched branches against changes in non-switched branches

### Results Summary

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Non-Switched Branches (n=14) | 48.13 | 47.95 | -0.19 |
| Switched Branches (n=20) | 47.93 | 49.50 | +1.57 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+1.75** |

### Statistical Assessment
- **Point estimate**: +1.75 points on the service score index
- **95% confidence interval**: -0.41 to +3.91 points
- **P-value**: 0.11
- **Effect size**: 0.19 standard deviations (small-to-moderate)
- **Pre-trends check**: Passed (p=0.74)—groups were on parallel trajectories before the switch

### Why the Result Isn't Statistically Significant
The study is **underpowered**, not the effect necessarily absent:
- To detect an effect of this size with 80% confidence, we'd need ~448 observations per group
- We currently have 160 (switched) and 112 (non-switched) post-switch observations
- The confidence interval includes zero but also includes improvements up to 4 points

### Interpretation
- Switched branches improved (+1.57 points); non-switched branches declined slightly (-0.19 points)
- The methodology is valid (pre-trends check confirms comparable groups)
- The true effect is likely positive but could plausibly be zero or as high as 4 points

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with rollout, monitor closely**: The positive signal and sound methodology support broader implementation, provided the board commits to ongoing evaluation.

2. **Extend data collection**: Track service scores for an additional 8–12 weeks post-rollout to increase statistical power and narrow the confidence interval.

3. **Evaluate cost-effectiveness**: Assess whether the ~4% improvement justifies implementation costs (staff training, workflow changes, etc.).

4. **Maintain a comparison group**: If rolling out phased, keep some branches on the old procedure temporarily to continue rigorous evaluation.
