## **BOTTOM LINE**

The new procedure shows a **positive but statistically uncertain effect** on service scores. The estimated improvement is +1.8 points (~3.5%), but the data is also consistent with no effect at all. We recommend a **phased rollout with continued evaluation** rather than an immediate system-wide mandate, allowing the cooperative to gather more evidence before full commitment.

### What the Statistics Tell Us

| Metric | Value | Interpretation |
|--------|-------|----------------|
| Estimated effect | +1.79 points | Best estimate from the data |

### Important Considerations

1. **Limited post-switch data**: Only 8 weeks of observation after the change may be insufficient to detect a true effect with confidence

2. **Non-random assignment**: Branches were not randomly assigned to switch, which could introduce bias (though pre-switch trends were similar between groups)

3. **Modest effect size**: Even if the +1.8 point effect is real, it represents a ~3.5% improvement—meaningful but not transformative

4. **Control group decline**: The control group's scores dropped slightly post-switch while treatment branches improved, suggesting the procedure may help buffers against external pressures

## **RECOMMENDATION**

**Proceed cautiously with a structured evaluation:**

1. **Phase the rollout** over 2–3 months rather than mandating immediate system-wide adoption

2. **Continue data collection** for at least 12 additional weeks to strengthen statistical confidence

3. **Establish clear success criteria** before rollout (e.g., "proceed if effect remains positive and p-value drops below 0.10")

4. **Monitor implementation costs** — training time, staff hours, disruption — to ensure benefits outweigh costs

5. **Schedule a decision point** at 90 days to either (a) confirm full rollout, (b) modify the procedure, or (c) halt expansion
