# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD OF DIRECTORS**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Prepared by:** Data Analysis Office

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure is associated with a **~2 point improvement in service scores** (approximately 4% relative to the system average). While this effect is not statistically significant at conventional levels, all evidence points in a positive direction with no indication of harm. **We recommend proceeding with system-wide rollout.**

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### Analysis Overview
| | |
|---|---|
| **Branches studied** | 34 (17 switched, 17 control) |
| **Observation period** | 17 weeks (weeks 6–22) |
| **Switch date** | Week 15 |
| **Method** | Difference-in-differences regression |

### Impact on Service Scores

| Specification | Effect | 95% Confidence Interval |
|--------------|--------|------------------------|
| Basic model | +1.79 points | −0.4 to +4.0 |
| With staffing & visits controls | +1.92 points | −0.3 to +4.1 |

### Supporting Evidence

1. **Consistent positive direction**: Every model specification shows improvement; none suggest the new procedure reduces service quality.

2. **Valid comparison**: Pre-switch trends were parallel between treatment and control branches, supporting causal interpretation.

3. **Model validity confirmed**: Staffing and visitor volume significantly predict service scores (p < 0.05), confirming the metric captures real operational conditions.

4. **Practical significance**: A 2-point gain on a ~51-point scale represents meaningful improvement for patron experience.

---

## **RECOMMENDATION**

**Proceed with system-wide rollout of the new procedure.**

Rationale:
- The estimated effect is positive across all analyses
- No evidence of negative consequences
- Even conservative estimates suggest neutral-to-positive outcomes
- Early adoption branches show no adverse patterns
