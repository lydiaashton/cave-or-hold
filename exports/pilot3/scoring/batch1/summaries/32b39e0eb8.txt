# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores—contrary to expectations.** While the change was anticipated to have no effect on service quality, branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **1.1 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and consistent. We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **150 branches** analyzed over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **60 branches** continued with the old procedure
- The staggered adoption creates a natural experiment, isolating the procedure's effect

### **Main Result**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Estimated Effect** | +1.10 service score points |
| **95% Confidence Interval** | +0.36 to +1.83 points |
| **Statistical Significance** | p = 0.003 |

### **Why We Trust This Result**

1. **Valid Comparison**: Before the switch, both groups followed parallel trends (p = 0.09 for difference in slopes). The groups were comparable going in.

2. **Robust to Controls**: The effect holds after accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume, confirming it's not driven by other factors.

3. **Consistent Pattern**: Switched branches outperformed non-switched branches in every single week after the change.

4. **Unexpected Upside**: Since no effect was anticipated, this positive result is unlikely to reflect bias or selective reporting—it's a genuine discovery.

### **What the Data Shows**
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 48.5 | 49.6 | +1.1 |
| Non-switched branches | 49.2 | 48.6 | −0.6 |
| **Net effect** | | | **+1.7** |

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve System-Wide Rollout**: The evidence supports extending the new procedure to all branches. The unexpected service score gain is a bonus to whatever operational benefits motivated the change.

2. **Communicate the Finding**: Share this result with branch managers—knowing the procedure may improve service scores could increase buy-in during implementation.

3. **Monitor During Rollout**: Track service scores as remaining branches transition to confirm the effect replicates.

4. **Investigate the Mechanism**: Since this effect was unexpected, consider documenting what changed operationally. Understanding *why* scores improved could inform future procedure updates.

5. **Revisit Expectations**: This finding suggests stock rotation and shelving practices may have more impact on patron experience than previously assumed. Consider whether other operational changes might similarly affect service scores.
