# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 14, 2026  
**Subject:** Recommendation on System-Wide Rollout of New Branch Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Approve the system-wide rollout.** The new stock rotation and shelving procedure significantly improved service scores. Contrary to the initial expectation of no difference, branches that adopted the new procedure achieved service scores **1.66 points higher** than comparable branches using the old procedure (p = 0.003). This is a statistically significant and operationally meaningful improvement.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Overview**
| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| Branches studied | 150 total |
| Treatment group | 90 branches (switched to new procedure at Week 15) |
| Control group | 60 branches (kept old procedure throughout) |
| Observation period | 17 weeks (Weeks 6–22) |
| Analysis method | Difference-in-differences with cluster-robust standard errors |

### **Main Result**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Effect of new procedure** | **+1.66 service score points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | [0.58, 2.74] |
| P-value | 0.003 |
| Statistically significant | **Yes** (at 1% level) |

### **What Happened in Practice**
- **Control branches** (old procedure): Service scores **declined 1.78 points** after Week 15
- **Treatment branches** (new procedure): Service scores **remained stable** (declined only 0.12 points)
- **Net benefit**: The new procedure prevented the decline and delivered a **~1.7 point advantage**

### **Confidence in the Result**
✓ **Parallel trends verified**: No significant differences between groups before the switch (all pre-period comparisons p > 0.24)

✓ **Robust to other factors**: Effect persists (+1.74 points, p = 0.002) after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volumes

✓ **Consistent over time**: Positive effects observed across most post-switch weeks

---

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches. The evidence indicates the new procedure improves service quality without the operational disruption that was initially anticipated.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Schedule rollout** to the 60 branches currently using the old procedure

2. **Develop training materials** based on experiences from the 90 early-adopter branches

3. **Monitor service scores** during and after rollout to confirm effects persist at scale

4. **Document operational lessons** to streamline implementation

5. **Conduct follow-up review** in 3 months to assess long-term impact

---

## **Technical Appendix**

- Service scores average ~50 across all branches; a 1.66-point improvement represents approximately a 3% gain
- Standard errors are clustered at the branch level to account for within-branch correlation over time
- Analysis controls for week-specific effects and branch-level fixed characteristics
- Data source: `branches.csv` (2,550 observations: 150 branches × 17 weeks)
