# **SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure is associated with **higher service scores**, consistent with our expectations. Branches that adopted the new procedure improved their scores by approximately **1.8 points** relative to branches that kept the old procedure. While this result is encouraging and moves in the expected direction, the evidence is not yet strong enough to rule out chance variation. We recommend **continuing the pilot while planning for a phased rollout**, pending additional data collection.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Overview**
- **34 branches** participated: 20 adopted the new procedure, 14 continued with the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** collected (Weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at Week 15
- Service scores average around 50 points; higher scores indicate better service

### **Results Summary**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Control Branches (old procedure) | 47.7 | 46.5 | −1.2 |
| Switched Branches (new procedure) | 50.6 | 51.3 | +0.7 |
| **Net Effect (Difference-in-Differences)** | | | **+1.8** |

### **Statistical Evidence**

| Measure | Value |
|---------|-------|
| Estimated effect | +1.82 points |
| 95% confidence interval | −0.4 to +4.1 points |
| P-value | 0.11 |
| Direction of effect | ✓ Matches expectation (positive) |

### **Interpretation**

1. **The effect is in the expected direction.** Switched branches improved relative to control branches, consistent with our hypothesis that the new procedure would enhance service.

2. **The evidence is suggestive but not definitive.** The p-value of 0.11 means there is an 11% probability of observing this result by chance alone—above the conventional 5% threshold for statistical significance, but still indicative of a meaningful trend.

3. **The confidence interval is informative.** We can be 95% confident the true effect lies between −0.4 and +4.1 points. The interval is mostly positive, and even the lower bound represents a negligible negative effect.

4. **Control branches declined slightly.** Branches keeping the old procedure saw scores drop by 1.2 points, while switched branches gained 0.7 points. This 2-point spread suggests the new procedure may help branches maintain service quality during challenging periods.

---

## **Recommendations**

### **Primary Recommendation: Proceed with Cautious Expansion**

Given that the effect is positive, aligns with expectations, and shows no evidence of harm, we recommend the board approve a **phased rollout** of the new procedure while continuing to collect data.

### **Implementation Plan**

| Phase | Action | Timeline |
|-------|--------|----------|
| 1 | Continue current pilot; monitor switched branches | Immediate |
| 2 | Offer new procedure to additional interested branches | 4–8 weeks |
| 3 | Collect 8–12 more weeks of data across all participating branches | Ongoing |
| 4 | Re-evaluate with expanded dataset; decide on full rollout | Quarter 2, 2027 |

### **Success Metrics for Continued Evaluation**

- **Primary:** Service score improvement of ≥1.5 points
- **Secondary:** Staff efficiency measures, patron satisfaction scores
- **Monitoring:** Any branches experiencing score declines after switching

### **Risk Assessment**

| Risk | Likelihood | Mitigation |
|------|------------|------------|
| No true effect (result was chance) | Moderate | Continued data collection will clarify |
| Effect smaller than expected | Low–Moderate | Even a 1-point gain may justify adoption |
| Implementation challenges at new branches | Moderate | Provide training and support materials |
| Negative effect on some branches | Low | Monitor individually; allow opt-out if needed |

---

## **Conclusion**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure shows **promise**. The positive effect on service scores aligns with our expectations, and there is no evidence of adverse impacts. While additional data would strengthen the evidence base, the current results support moving forward with a phased expansion. We recommend the board authorize continued implementation while committing to a formal re-evaluation after one additional quarter of data collection.
