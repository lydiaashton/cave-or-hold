# **Evaluation of New Shelving Procedure on Library Service Scores**

**Date:** November 14, 2026  
**Prepared for:** Library Cooperative Board  
**Analysis Period:** Weeks 6–22 (17 weeks of data)

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Contrary to the expectation of no difference, branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.2 points** (about 4.4% relative to the average score of 50). This improvement is statistically significant and consistent across multiple analytical approaches.

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

| Metric | Result |
|--------|--------|
| **Treatment Effect** | +2.18 service score points |
| **Statistical Significance** | p = 0.0009 (significant at 1% level) |
| **95% Confidence Interval** | +0.89 to +3.46 points |
| **Branches Analyzed** | 150 total (90 switched, 60 control) |
| **Observation Period** | 17 weeks (weeks 6–22), switch at week 15 |

### What the Evidence Shows

1. **Clear Positive Impact**: Branches using the new procedure achieved higher service scores than comparable branches that kept the old procedure. The difference is not due to chance.

2. **Valid Comparison**: Before the switch (weeks 6–14), treatment and control branches followed parallel trends with no systematic differences. This confirms the improvement is attributable to the procedure change.

3. **Robust Results**: The finding holds across multiple analytical approaches, with and without controls for staffing levels and visit volumes.

4. **Broad-Based Improvement**: Among the 90 branches that switched, 53 (59%) showed score improvements after the change.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve System-Wide Rollout**: Extend the new procedure to all remaining branches.

2. **Support Underperforming Branches**: Since 37 of 90 switching branches did not improve, identify common characteristics and provide targeted support.

3. **Continue Monitoring**: Track service scores post-rollout to confirm the effect persists and identify any emerging issues.

4. **Document Best Practices**: Branches with the largest gains may have implementation insights worth sharing across the cooperative.

---

*Methodology: Difference-in-differences regression with branch and week fixed effects; standard errors clustered at branch level.*
