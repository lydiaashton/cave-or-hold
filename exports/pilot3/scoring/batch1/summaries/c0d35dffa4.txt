# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD OF DIRECTORS**

---

## **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

**Date:** November 2026  
**Prepared by:** Data Analysis Team

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure is associated with a **modest improvement in service scores** (+1.8 points, or approximately 3.5%). While the result falls just short of conventional statistical significance, the evidence points in a positive direction. We recommend **proceeding with a phased rollout** to all branches while continuing to monitor outcomes.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### Study Overview
| | |
|---|---|
| **Study Period** | 17 weeks (weeks 6–22) |
| **Switch Date** | Week 15 |
| **Branches Analyzed** | 34 total |
| **Treatment Group** | 20 branches (new procedure) |
| **Control Group** | 14 branches (old procedure) |
| **Total Observations** | 578 weekly records |

### Results Summary

| Group | Pre-Switch Average | Post-Switch Average | Change |
|-------|-------------------|---------------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 51.4 | 50.7 | −0.7 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 51.1 | 52.2 | +1.1 |
| **Net Treatment Effect** | | | **+1.8** |

### Statistical Assessment
- **Estimated effect:** +1.79 service score points
- **Confidence level:** 90% (p = 0.088)
- **Relative improvement:** 3.5% above baseline
- **Parallel trends verified:** Pre-switch trajectories were similar between groups, supporting the validity of the comparison

### Important Context
- The control group experienced a slight decline after week 15, while treatment branches improved. This suggests the new procedure may help branches resist external pressures affecting scores system-wide.
- Staffing levels and visit volumes are positively associated with service scores; the treatment effect holds even after accounting for these factors.

---

## **RECOMMENDATION**

**Proceed with rollout, with safeguards:**

1. **Implement in phases** over the next 2–3 months rather than all at once, allowing for adjustments if issues arise

2. **Extend monitoring** for an additional 8–12 weeks to strengthen statistical confidence and assess whether effects persist or grow

3. **Verify implementation fidelity** at each branch to ensure the procedure is being followed consistently

4. **Track complementary metrics** such as staff time expenditure, patron wait times, and material availability

5. **Schedule a formal review** at 90 days post-rollout to evaluate whether to continue, modify, or expand the procedure
