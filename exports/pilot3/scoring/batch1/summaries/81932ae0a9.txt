# MEMORANDUM

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 14, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## Bottom Line

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw their service scores increase by approximately **2.1 points** (about 4% improvement) compared to branches that retained the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and holds up under multiple validation tests. **We recommend proceeding with a full rollout to all branches.**

---

## Key Findings

### 1. Measurable Improvement in Service Quality

| Group | Pre-Switch Average | Post-Switch Average | Change |
|-------|-------------------|---------------------|--------|
| Non-switched branches (control) | 50.1 | 51.0 | +0.9 |
| Switched branches (treatment) | 49.1 | 52.1 | +3.0 |
| **Net Treatment Effect** | | | **+2.1** |

The switched branches started with slightly lower scores but pulled ahead after adopting the new procedure. The 2.1-point improvement represents a **4% gain** over the baseline average service score of 49.5.

### 2. Statistical Confidence

- **Effect size:** +2.1 points on the service score index
- **95% confidence interval:** 0.5 to 3.6 points (does not include zero)
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001 (highly significant)
- **Validation:** Results confirmed through 1,000 bootstrap simulations

### 3. Study Design & Data Quality

- **150 branches** total: 90 adopted the new procedure, 60 retained the old (control group)
- **17 weeks of data:** Weeks 6–22, with the switch occurring in week 15
- **2,550 observations:** One weekly score per branch
- **Method:** Difference-in-differences analysis, the standard approach for evaluating policy changes with treatment and control groups

### 4. Validity Checks Passed

- **Parallel trends confirmed:** Before the switch (weeks 6–14), both groups followed similar trajectories (p = 0.28 for trend difference)
- **Robust to controls:** Adding staffing levels and visit volumes as controls does not change the estimated effect
- **No pre-existing differences:** The switched and non-switched branches showed comparable performance before the intervention

---

## Recommendation

**Proceed with full rollout of the new procedure to all branches.**

The evidence is clear and statistically robust: the new rotation and shelving procedure improves service scores without any identified negative effects. The 4% improvement is meaningful for patron experience and aligns with the cooperative's service quality goals.

### Additional Considerations

1. **Cost-benefit review:** If the new procedure carries additional costs (training, equipment, staff time), quantify these against the 4% service improvement.

2. **Implementation support:** Provide training and resources to ensure consistent adoption across all branches.

3. **Mechanism investigation:** Consider interviewing staff at high-performing switched branches to identify best practices that could enhance the rollout.

4. **Continued monitoring:** Track service scores for at least 6 months post-rollout to confirm the effect persists at scale.
