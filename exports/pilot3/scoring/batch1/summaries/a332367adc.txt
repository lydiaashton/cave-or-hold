# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

## **Subject: Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

### **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure produced an unexpected but measurable improvement.** Although we anticipated no change in service scores, branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **1.9 points** (about **4% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant (p = 0.001). 

Given the positive outcome with no apparent downside, we recommend proceeding with the system-wide rollout while continuing to monitor results.

---

### **KEY FINDINGS**

**1. Study Design**
- 150 branches tracked over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- 90 branches switched to the new procedure at week 15; 60 branches kept the old procedure
- This natural experiment allows us to isolate the effect of the new procedure from other factors

**2. Main Result**
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches (new procedure) | 51.1 | 53.4 | +2.3 |
| Non-switched branches (old procedure) | 50.6 | 50.9 | +0.3 |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | | **+2.0** |

After adjusting for staffing levels and visitor volume, the estimated effect is **+1.91 points** (95% confidence interval: 0.78 to 3.03; p = 0.001).

**3. Why This Is Notable**
- **Unexpected finding**: We hypothesized the procedure change would have no effect on service scores
- **Statistically robust**: The result is unlikely to be due to chance (less than 1 in 1,000 probability)
- **Consistent pattern**: All eight post-switch weeks showed positive effects; none showed negative effects
- **No pre-existing trend**: Groups were on parallel trajectories before the switch, supporting causal interpretation

**4. Possible Explanations** (for further investigation)
- Staff may work more efficiently under the new procedure, freeing time for patron assistance
- Better-organized shelves may help patrons find materials faster, improving their experience
- The change may have signaled attentiveness to service quality, affecting staff behavior

---

### **NEXT STEPS**

1. **Proceed with rollout** to remaining 60 branches—the data shows benefit with no identified harm

2. **Investigate the mechanism**: Since this result was unexpected, conduct qualitative follow-up to understand *why* service scores improved:
   - Interview staff at switched branches about workflow changes
   - Survey patrons about their experience
   - Observe operations to identify specific improvements

3. **Monitor during implementation**:
   - Track service scores for 8–12 weeks after each branch switches
   - Flag any branches that don't show expected gains for additional support

4. **Document and share learnings**: Once the mechanism is clearer, incorporate findings into training materials to maximize benefits at remaining branches

5. **Consider broader implications**: If the procedure change improved scores through unexpected channels, evaluate whether other operational changes might have similar positive spillovers
