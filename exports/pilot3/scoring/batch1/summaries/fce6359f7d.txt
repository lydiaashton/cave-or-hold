# **Board Summary: Impact of New Branch Procedure on Service Scores**

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure is associated with a **2.4-point increase in service scores** (approximately 5% improvement). The effect is **marginally statistically significant** (p=0.095), with the 95% confidence interval ranging from -0.4 to +5.2 points. 

**Recommendation:** Proceed with broader rollout while continuing to monitor results. The evidence points to a positive impact, and the risk of harm appears minimal.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
- **34 branches** tracked over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure at week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure
- This design isolates the effect of the procedure change from other factors affecting all branches

### Service Score Impact

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|----------------|---------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 49.5 | 50.2 | +0.7 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 51.6 | 54.7 | +3.1 |
| **Net Effect of New Procedure** | | | **+2.4** |

### Statistical Confidence
- **95% Confidence Interval:** -0.4 to +5.2 points
- **Interpretation:** The true effect is most likely positive (around +2.4 points), but we cannot rule out zero effect with high certainty
- **Practical significance:** A 2.4-point gain represents roughly 5% improvement on the service score index

### Validity Checks Passed
✓ **Parallel pre-trends:** Treatment and control branches moved together before the switch, supporting causal interpretation  
✓ **Robust to controls:** Effect persists after accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume  
✓ **No red flags:** No evidence of negative impacts or adverse effects
