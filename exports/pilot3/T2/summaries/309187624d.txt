# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new shelving and rotation procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **1.7 points** (about **3.5%**) relative to branches that kept the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant and represents a meaningful gain in service quality. We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15 (treatment group)
- **60 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in treatment branches to changes in control branches

### Main Results
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Treatment (new procedure) | 49.9 | 49.8 | -0.1 |
| Control (old procedure) | 53.3 | 51.6 | -1.8 |
| **Difference (Treatment Effect)** | | | **+1.7** |

- **Treatment effect: +1.74 points** (95% CI: 0.63 to 2.86; p = 0.002)
- Effect size: 0.19 standard deviations
- The control group experienced a notable decline in service scores after week 15, while treatment branches remained stable—suggesting the new procedure **protected against deterioration** in service quality

### Supporting Evidence
1. **Parallel trends validated**: Before the switch, the gap between treatment and control branches was stable (averaging -3.4 points), supporting the validity of our comparison
2. **Robust to controls**: The effect holds when accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume
3. **Consistent over time**: The benefit persists beyond the initial transition week
4. **Stronger for understaffed branches**: Branches with below-median staffing saw larger gains (+2.3 points vs. +1.2 points)

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve systemwide rollout** of the new shelving and rotation procedure to all 150 branches

2. **Prioritize branches with staffing challenges**, as they appear to benefit most from the new procedure

3. **Monitor implementation** during the first 4–6 weeks at each new branch to ensure proper adoption

4. **Track service scores quarterly** to confirm the benefit persists at scale and identify any branches needing additional support

5. **Document the procedure** formally and incorporate into branch manager training materials
