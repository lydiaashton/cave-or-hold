# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact Analysis**

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and rotation procedure is associated with a **modest improvement in service scores**, but the evidence is not yet conclusive. Branches that switched showed an average gain of approximately **2 points** (about 4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect appears to strengthen over time, becoming statistically significant when looking at weeks 3-8 after the switch. **We recommend proceeding with a cautious, phased rollout while continuing to monitor results.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6-22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure at week 15; **14 branches** kept the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in switching branches against non-switching branches

### **Main Results**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect of new procedure | **+1.9 points** on service score |
| Baseline service score (pre-switch) | 51.2 |
| Relative improvement | **~3.7%** |
| Statistical significance | Marginal (p=0.09) |
| 95% confidence interval | -0.3 to +4.1 points |

### **Important Patterns**

1. **Effect strengthens over time**: When examining only the later post-switch period (weeks 17-22), the effect grows to **+2.8 points** and becomes statistically significant (p=0.03). This suggests branches may need time to fully realize benefits from the new procedure.

2. **Parallel trends confirmed**: Before the switch, treatment and control branches followed similar service score trajectories, supporting the validity of our comparison.

3. **Both groups dipped at week 15**: Service scores dropped for all branches during the switch week, likely reflecting transition disruption. The treatment group recovered more quickly afterward.

4. **Staffing and visits matter**: Higher staffing levels and visitor volumes are both associated with better service scores, independent of the procedure change.

---

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended Actions**

1. **Proceed with phased rollout** — The positive direction of the effect, combined with strengthening results over time, supports expanding the new procedure. However, the modest effect size suggests managing expectations.

2. **Continue monitoring for 8-12 weeks** — Allow newly switched branches adequate time to realize the full benefit. Track whether the 2-3 point improvement materializes consistently.

3. **Investigate high-performing branches** — Some switching branches showed larger gains than others. Understanding what distinguishes them could help optimize implementation.

4. **Consider transition support** — The week-15 dip affected all branches. Providing additional staffing or training during the switchover period may minimize disruption.

5. **Set realistic targets** — If rolled out systemwide, expect an average service score improvement of approximately **2 points** (from ~51 to ~53), with potential for greater gains as branches gain experience.

---

## **Analytical Notes**

- Standard errors are clustered at the branch level to account for repeated observations
- Results control for weekly staffing and visitor volume fluctuations
- The confidence interval includes zero at the 95% level, meaning we cannot rule out no effect with high certainty — but the preponderance of evidence points to a positive impact
