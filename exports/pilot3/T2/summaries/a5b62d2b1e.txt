# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new shelving and stock rotation procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.2 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and consistent across the post-switch period. Given that the average service score is around 50, this represents a meaningful **4-5% improvement** in service quality.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measurable Improvement in Service Scores
- **Treatment branches** (90 branches that switched): Service scores increased by an average of 1.5 points after the switch
- **Control branches** (60 branches that kept old procedure): Service scores actually declined by 0.6 points during the same period
- **Net effect**: The new procedure is associated with a **2.2-point improvement** in service scores (95% confidence interval: 0.9 to 3.5 points)

### 2. Statistical Confidence
- The result is **highly statistically significant** (p < 0.001), meaning there's less than a 0.1% chance this result occurred by random variation
- The finding holds even after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume
- The effect is robust across different analytical approaches

### 3. Consistent Positive Effects Over Time
The improvement was observed in every week after the switch:
| Week | Effect Size | Statistical Significance |
|------|-------------|-------------------------|
| 15 | +3.0 points | Significant |
| 16 | +2.9 points | Significant |
| 17 | +2.8 points | Significant |
| 18 | +1.9 points | Not significant |
| 19 | +1.7 points | Not significant |
| 20 | +2.3 points | Marginal |
| 21 | +2.9 points | Significant |
| 22 | +3.2 points | Significant |

### 4. Valid Comparison
- Pre-switch trends were **parallel** between treatment and control groups, validating the comparison
- Both groups had nearly identical average service scores before the switch (~49.8 points)
- The analysis accounts for branch-specific characteristics and weekly fluctuations in staffing and visits

---

## **Next Steps**

### Recommended Actions

1. **Proceed with System-Wide Rollout**
   - The evidence strongly supports expanding the new procedure to all 150 branches
   - Expected benefit: ~2-point improvement in service scores across the cooperative

2. **Monitor Implementation**
   - Track service scores during rollout to ensure effects replicate at scale
   - Pay attention to weeks 18-19 where effects were smaller—investigate whether seasonal factors or implementation fatigue played a role

3. **Document Best Practices**
   - Capture what switching branches did during their transition
   - Create implementation guidance based on early adopters' experience

4. **Consider Cost-Benefit Analysis**
   - While service scores improved, evaluate whether the new procedure has any associated costs (training, time, equipment)
   - The 4-5% service improvement should be weighed against implementation costs

### Caveats

- This analysis covers 8 weeks post-switch; longer-term effects should be monitored
- The effect, while statistically significant, is modest in absolute terms (~2 points on a ~50-point scale)
- Continue collecting data to confirm the effect persists beyond the initial implementation period
