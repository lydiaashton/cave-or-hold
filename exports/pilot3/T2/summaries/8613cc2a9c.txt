# **Board Summary: Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **1.8 points** (approximately **3.5%**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and represents a meaningful improvement in service quality. We recommend proceeding with a cooperative-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Study Design
- **150 branches** participated in the study over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **60 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis used a **Difference-in-Differences** approach, which compares changes over time between treatment and control groups while accounting for underlying trends

### 2. Main Result: Positive Treatment Effect
| Group | Before Switch (Weeks 6–14) | After Switch (Weeks 15–22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------------|---------------------------|--------|
| Control (Old Procedure) | 49.9 | 49.7 | -0.2 |
| Treatment (New Procedure) | 50.1 | 51.7 | +1.6 |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | | | **+1.8 points** |

- The estimated effect of **+1.8 points** is statistically significant (p < 0.01)
- **95% confidence interval: +0.6 to +2.9 points**
- The effect remains significant after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volumes

### 3. Parallel Trends Validated
A critical assumption of this analysis is that treatment and control branches were on similar trajectories before the switch. This assumption holds:
- Pre-switch trends were statistically indistinguishable between groups (p = 0.68)
- Both groups showed similar week-to-week variation before week 15
- This supports the validity of our causal interpretation

### 4. Effect Persistence
- The positive effect was observed throughout the post-switch period
- Early post-switch period (weeks 15–18): +2.4 points
- Later post-switch period (weeks 19–22): +1.5 points
- While there is some week-to-week variation (normal for service metrics), the effect remained positive in all post-switch weeks

### 5. Context
- Average service score across all branches: **50.4 points**
- Standard deviation: **9.5 points**
- The 1.8-point improvement represents roughly **0.19 standard deviations**—a modest but meaningful effect size for an operational change
