# **Analysis of New Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure shows a **positive but statistically uncertain** effect on service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure improved by approximately 2 points relative to branches that kept the old procedure, but this difference could plausibly be due to random variation. **We cannot confidently conclude the new procedure caused an improvement.** The board should consider a longer evaluation period or a phased rollout with continued monitoring before committing to system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** total: 20 adopted the new procedure, 14 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in treatment branches against changes in control branches

### **Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 47.4 | 47.2 | -0.3 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 46.6 | 48.2 | +1.6 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+1.9** |

- **Estimated effect:** +1.9 points on the service score index
- **95% confidence interval:** -0.4 to +4.3 points
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.10 (marginally significant at 10% level, not significant at the conventional 5% level)

### **What This Means**
- The data suggest the new procedure may improve service scores by about 2 points (roughly 4% of the average score of 50)
- However, the confidence interval includes zero and extends to negative values, meaning we **cannot rule out no effect or even a small negative effect**
- The pre-switch trends for both groups were reasonably parallel, supporting the validity of the comparison

### **Additional Observations**
- Control branches showed essentially no change after week 15 (-0.3 points)
- Treatment branches showed improvement (+1.6 points), but some of this could reflect normal variation
- Staffing and visit levels did not meaningfully change the results when included in the analysis

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Extend the evaluation period.** Eight weeks post-switch may be insufficient to detect a stable effect. Recommend collecting 8–12 additional weeks of data.

2. **Consider a phased rollout.** Rather than system-wide implementation, expand the new procedure to additional branches while maintaining a control group for continued comparison.

3. **Monitor implementation fidelity.** Ensure branches using the new procedure are following it consistently; variation in implementation could mask true effects.

4. **Track secondary outcomes.** Consider whether the new procedure affects other metrics (e.g., operational costs, staff satisfaction, patron wait times) that might justify adoption even if service score effects are modest.

5. **Revisit the decision in 3 months.** With additional data, the statistical precision will improve, allowing a more confident recommendation.
