# **Board Summary: Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure is associated with a **2.15-point improvement in service scores** (approximately 4% relative to baseline). The effect is **marginally statistically significant** (p=0.09) and appears to **strengthen over time**, with the largest gains occurring in weeks 3-4 after implementation. We recommend proceeding with a **phased rollout** while continuing to monitor results.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Study Design**
- **34 branches** total: 20 adopted the new procedure (treatment), 14 kept the old procedure (control)
- **17 weeks of data**: Weeks 6-14 (pre-switch) and Weeks 15-22 (post-switch)
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in the treatment group to changes in the control group

### **2. Main Results**

| Group | Pre-Switch Average | Post-Switch Average | Change |
|-------|-------------------|---------------------|--------|
| Control (No Switch) | 51.02 | 52.18 | +1.16 |
| Treatment (Switched) | 49.67 | 52.97 | +3.31 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+2.15** |

- **Point estimate**: 2.15 points improvement attributable to the new procedure
- **95% Confidence Interval**: -0.35 to 4.65 points
- **Statistical significance**: p=0.09 (marginally significant at the 10% level)

### **3. Effect Evolves Over Time**

The treatment effect was not immediate but grew stronger over the post-switch period:

| Week After Switch | Effect (points) | Significance |
|-------------------|-----------------|--------------|
| Week 1 (Week 15) | -1.03 | Not significant |
| Week 2 (Week 16) | +3.29 | **Significant** |
| Week 3-4 | Mixed | Variable |
| Week 7-8 (Weeks 21-22) | +6.0 to +6.9 | **Highly significant** |

This pattern suggests a **learning curve**: branches needed 1-2 weeks to adjust to the new procedure before realizing benefits, with effects strengthening thereafter.

### **4. Validity Checks**
- **Parallel trends assumption supported**: Pre-switch trends were statistically similar between groups (p=0.18)
- **Control variables balanced**: Staffing and visit patterns were comparable between groups before the switch
- **Results robust**: Effect size remains ~2.15 points after controlling for staffing and visit levels

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended Actions**

1. **Proceed with rollout, but phase it**
   - The positive effect, while marginally significant, is consistent across specifications
   - The strengthening effect over time suggests branches need support during the transition

2. **Provide enhanced training and support during Weeks 1-2**
   - The data shows no benefit (and possibly a slight dip) in the first week
   - Consider assigning "procedure champions" or extra staff during the transition period

3. **Monitor service scores closely for the first 8 weeks post-implementation**
   - Track whether the pattern of strengthening effects replicates at other branches
   - Set up weekly dashboards to identify branches struggling with the transition

4. **Consider a formal pilot evaluation**
   - If resources allow, run a more controlled rollout with random assignment
   - This would provide stronger causal evidence and narrower confidence intervals

### **Caveats to Consider**

- The confidence interval includes zero, so we cannot rule out no effect with 95% certainty
- The study covers only 8 post-switch weeks; longer-term effects are unknown
- External factors (seasonal patterns, staffing changes) could influence results

---

## **Conclusion**

The evidence suggests the new procedure **likely improves service quality**, with benefits becoming clearer after branches have time to adjust. The estimated 2-point improvement is meaningful given the ~50-point scale. A careful, supported rollout with close monitoring is warranted.
