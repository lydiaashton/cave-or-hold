# **Analysis of New Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Contrary to the initial expectation that the change would make no difference, branches that switched to the new procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **1.8 points** (approximately 3.6%) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and consistent across multiple analytical approaches.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **60 branches** continued with the old procedure
- This natural experiment allows us to isolate the effect of the new procedure using a difference-in-differences approach

### Main Results

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Treatment Effect** | +1.78 points |
| **95% Confidence Interval** | +0.63 to +2.93 points |
| **Statistical Significance** | p = 0.002 (highly significant) |
| **Relative Improvement** | ~3.6% increase from baseline (~50 points) |

### What the Numbers Mean
- **Treatment branches** (new procedure): Scores increased from 50.1 to 51.7 (+1.6 points)
- **Control branches** (old procedure): Scores remained stable, 49.9 to 49.7 (−0.2 points)
- The **net effect** attributable to the new procedure is +1.8 points

### Robustness Checks
The result holds consistently across different analytical specifications:
- Simple comparison: +1.78 points (p=0.002)
- With staffing and visits controls: +1.74 points (p=0.003)
- With branch fixed effects: +1.78 points (p=0.003)
- With branch + week fixed effects: +1.78 points (p=0.003)

### Additional Insights
- **Staffing and visits matter:** Both staffing index and visits index are positively associated with service scores (p<0.01), but the procedure effect remains significant even after accounting for these factors
- **Effect timing:** The improvement was visible immediately in the first week after implementation and persisted through the study period

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new shelving and rotation procedure to all remaining branches

2. **Monitor implementation** during rollout:
   - Track service scores at newly converted branches
   - Ensure the ~1.8 point improvement is replicated across different branch types and sizes

3. **Document the procedure** thoroughly to ensure consistent implementation

4. **Consider a follow-up study** after 6–12 months to assess:
   - Whether the effect persists long-term
   - Any operational cost implications
   - Whether certain branch characteristics moderate the effect

5. **Communicate success** to branch staff to encourage adoption and reinforce the value of the change

---

## **Technical Note**

This analysis uses a quasi-experimental difference-in-differences design, which compares changes in outcomes between branches that adopted the new procedure and those that did not, before and after the switch. The parallel trends assumption—that treatment and control branches would have followed similar trajectories absent the intervention—is reasonably supported by the pre-period data, though some week-to-week variation exists. The consistency of results across multiple model specifications increases confidence in the findings.
