# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

## **Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

---

### **BOTTOM LINE**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.1 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents a **4% improvement** relative to the average service score of 50. The effect is statistically significant and robust across multiple analytical approaches.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

### **KEY FINDINGS**

**1. Study Design**
- **709 branches** total: 425 switched to the new procedure (treatment group), 284 kept the old procedure (control group)
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in service scores between switching and non-switching branches before and after the procedure change

**2. Main Result**
| Group | Pre-Switch Avg | Post-Switch Avg | Change |
|-------|---------------|-----------------|--------|
| Control (no switch) | 49.91 | 50.73 | +0.82 |
| Treatment (switched) | 51.12 | 54.04 | +2.92 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+2.10** |

**3. Statistical Confidence**
- The 2.10-point improvement is **highly statistically significant** (p < 0.001)
- **95% confidence interval:** 1.37 to 2.83 points
- The result holds after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume, and after accounting for inherent differences between individual branches

**4. Effect Size Context**
- A 2.1-point increase represents approximately **4% improvement** over the average service score
- For comparison: the control group's natural week-to-week variation was less than 1 point, making this a meaningful operational improvement

**5. Timeline Pattern**
- Service scores for switching branches rose immediately after week 15 and remained elevated through week 22
- No evidence of the effect diminishing over the 8-week post-switch period

---

### **LIMITATIONS & CAVEATS**

1. **Non-random assignment:** Branches were not randomly assigned to switch; some self-selected or were selected based on unstated criteria. However, the difference-in-differences design accounts for pre-existing differences between groups.

2. **Pre-existing trends:** There was some week-to-week volatility in service scores before the switch, though no clear diverging trend between groups that would invalidate the comparison.

3. **Short-term evidence:** We have 8 weeks of post-switch data. Longer-term monitoring is recommended to confirm the effect persists.

---

### **NEXT STEPS**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all remaining branches

2. **Continue monitoring** service scores for at least 6–12 months post-rollout to confirm sustained improvement

3. **Document implementation details** from the 425 early-adopter branches to support training and change management during rollout

4. **Consider complementary investments** in staffing, as our analysis shows staffing levels are positively associated with service scores (independent of the procedure change)
