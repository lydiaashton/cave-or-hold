# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new shelving and rotation procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.1 points** (about **4% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and robust to controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measurable Improvement in Service Quality
- The new procedure increased service scores by **2.08 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.0 to 3.2 points)
- This represents a **4.2% improvement** over the baseline average score of ~49.5
- The result is highly statistically significant (p < 0.001)

### 2. Strong Research Design Validates the Result
- **150 branches** were studied over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure; **60 branches** kept the old procedure as a control group
- The switch occurred at **week 15**, providing 9 weeks of before-data and 8 weeks of after-data
- **Parallel trends assumption holds**: Before the switch, treatment and control branches showed similar trends in service scores (p = 0.28), supporting the validity of the comparison

### 3. Effect Persists After Accounting for Other Factors
- The improvement remains significant even after controlling for:
  - **Staffing index** (staffing levels)
  - **Visits index** (visitor volume)
- Both staffing and visits independently affect service scores, but the procedure change effect is separate from these factors

### 4. Visual Evidence
The trend chart (saved as `service_score_trends.png`) shows:
- Treatment and control groups tracked closely before week 15
- After week 15, treatment branches pulled ahead and maintained higher scores

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** — The evidence strongly supports expanding the new procedure to all branches

2. **Monitor implementation** — Track service scores during rollout to ensure the effect replicates at scale

3. **Document the procedure** — Capture the specific practices that make the new procedure effective for training purposes

4. **Consider timing** — The effect appeared immediately after implementation; no extended ramp-up period appears necessary

5. **Evaluate cost-benefit** — If the new procedure requires additional resources, the 4% service improvement provides a benchmark for ROI calculations

---

## **Technical Note**

This analysis used a **difference-in-differences** approach, the standard method for evaluating policy changes when random assignment isn't feasible. Standard errors were clustered at the branch level to account for repeated observations over time.
