# **Memorandum to the Board**

## **Title: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw their service scores increase by approximately **2.1 points (4.1%)** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and robust across multiple analytical approaches. We recommend proceeding with a full rollout, with attention to monitoring during the transition period.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Main Result: Positive Impact on Service Scores
- **Treatment effect:** +2.1 points on the service score index (95% CI: 1.0 to 3.2 points)
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001 (highly significant)
- **Practical significance:** Approximately 4% improvement over the baseline average of 50 points

### 2. Analysis Approach
We used a **difference-in-differences** design, comparing:
- **90 treatment branches** that switched to the new procedure
- **60 control branches** that kept the old procedure
- **Pre-period:** Weeks 6–14 (before the switch)
- **Post-period:** Weeks 15–22 (after the switch)

This approach isolates the effect of the new procedure from other factors affecting all branches (like seasonal changes or system-wide issues).

### 3. Robustness Checks
The positive effect holds across multiple specifications:
- **Excluding anomalous weeks (19–21):** Effect increases to +2.9 points
- **Immediate post-period only (weeks 15–18):** Effect is +3.0 points
- **With staffing and visit controls:** Effect remains at +2.1 points

### 4. Important Observations
- **Pre-existing differences:** Treatment branches scored about 1 point lower than control branches before the switch, but this gap was stable over time.
- **Common shock in weeks 19–21:** All branches experienced a temporary dip in service scores during this period (likely due to an external factor affecting the entire system). The treatment group recovered faster.
- **No negative effects detected:** The new procedure did not harm service quality at any point.

---

## **Next Steps**

### Recommended Actions

1. **Proceed with Full Rollout**
   - The evidence supports expanding the new procedure to all 150 branches
   - Expected system-wide improvement: ~2 points on service scores

2. **Monitor Implementation Closely**
   - Track service scores weekly during the transition
   - Pay special attention to weeks 4–6 post-switch (when we observed the system-wide dip)

3. **Investigate the Weeks 19–21 Dip**
   - Determine what caused the temporary service score decline across all branches
   - Develop contingency plans to mitigate similar disruptions in the future

4. **Consider Phased Implementation**
   - Roll out in batches of 20–30 branches per week
   - Allows for troubleshooting and adjustment before full deployment

5. **Follow-up Evaluation**
   - Conduct a 3-month post-rollout assessment
   - Measure whether the 2-point improvement is sustained over time

---

## **Caveats and Limitations**

- The analysis covers 17 weeks of data; longer-term effects remain uncertain
- Treatment branches were slightly lower-performing before the switch, which may affect generalizability
- The external shock in weeks 19–21 introduces some uncertainty about effects during disruptive periods
