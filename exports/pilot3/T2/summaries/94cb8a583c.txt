# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure is associated with a **~2 point improvement in service scores** (approximately 4% relative to the system average of 51). While this positive effect is not statistically significant at conventional levels (p ≈ 0.10), the evidence consistently points in a favorable direction. **We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout while continuing to monitor outcomes.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **17 branches** adopted the new procedure at week 15; **17 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach to isolate the procedure's effect

### Results

| Model | Effect on Service Score | Statistical Significance |
|-------|------------------------|-------------------------|
| Basic analysis | +1.79 points | p = 0.12 |
| Controlling for staffing & visits | +1.92 points | p = 0.10* |

*Marginally significant

- **95% Confidence Interval**: The true effect likely falls between **-0.3 and +4.1 points**
- **Practical significance**: A 2-point gain represents meaningful improvement in a system where scores average ~51

### Supporting Evidence

1. **Staffing and visits matter**: Both factors significantly predict service scores (p < 0.05), confirming the service score captures real operational conditions

2. **Consistent direction**: The estimated effect is positive across all model specifications—no analysis suggests the new procedure harms service quality

3. **Parallel pre-trends**: Before the switch, both groups had similar service score trajectories, supporting the validity of the comparison

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with rollout**: The weight of evidence favors the new procedure. Even if the true effect is at the lower end of the confidence interval, there is no indication of harm.

2. **Continue monitoring**: Track service scores for 8–12 weeks post-rollout to confirm the effect stabilizes at the expected level.

3. **Investigate heterogeneity**: Some branches may benefit more than others. Analyze whether branch size, location, or baseline performance moderates the effect.

4. **Consider complementary improvements**: Since staffing and visitor volume strongly influence service scores, pairing the new procedure with targeted staffing adjustments could amplify gains.
