# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Recommend proceeding with system-wide rollout.** The new procedure produced a statistically significant improvement in service scores, increasing them by an average of **2.1 points** (approximately 4% improvement). This effect is robust across multiple analytical approaches and has been consistent across all 8 weeks since implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **425 branches** adopted the new procedure starting Week 15
- **284 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Data covers 17 weeks (Weeks 6–22), with 9 weeks before and 8 weeks after the switch

### Impact on Service Scores

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 49.8 | 53.3 | +3.5 |
| Control branches | 48.2 | 49.6 | +1.4 |
| **Net effect (DiD)** | | | **+2.1** |

### Statistical Confidence
- **Effect size:** +2.1 points (95% CI: +1.5 to +2.6)
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001 (highly significant)
- The result holds even after controlling for:
  - Branch-specific characteristics (fixed effects)
  - Week-to-week fluctuations affecting all branches
  - Staffing levels and visit volumes

### Additional Observations
1. **Effect appears sustained:** The gap between switched and control branches remained stable or grew slightly over the 8-week post-switch period (from +2.9 points in Week 15 to +4.4 points in Week 22).
2. **No negative side effects:** The improvement in service scores was not accompanied by deterioration in staffing or visit metrics.
3. **Baseline difference:** Switched branches scored slightly higher than control branches even before the switch (+1.6 points), but the analysis accounts for this pre-existing difference.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to remaining 284 branches.

2. **Monitor implementation** during rollout:
   - Track service scores for newly switched branches to confirm they achieve similar gains
   - Watch for any operational challenges during transition

3. **Consider a formal evaluation** 3–6 months after full rollout to:
   - Confirm the effect persists long-term
   - Assess whether there are additional benefits (e.g., staff efficiency, patron satisfaction)
   - Identify any branches that may need additional support

4. **Document the procedure** thoroughly to ensure consistent implementation across all branches.
