# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure is associated with an improvement in service scores of approximately **1.9 points** (on a scale where the average branch scores about 50). This represents roughly a **4% improvement**. While the result is not statistically significant at conventional levels when accounting for branch-level clustering (p = 0.12), the evidence is suggestive of a positive effect, and there is no evidence of harm. We recommend proceeding with a **phased rollout** while continuing to monitor outcomes.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** total: 20 adopted the new procedure, 14 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks** of data (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in service scores between switching and non-switching branches before and after the procedure change

### **Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 47.7 | 46.5 | −1.2 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 50.6 | 51.3 | +0.7 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+1.9** |

- **Estimated effect**: +1.92 service score points (95% CI: −0.46 to +4.30)
- **Statistical significance**: p = 0.12 (not significant at 5% level, but directionally consistent with expectations)
- **Parallel trends assumption**: Supported—no significant difference in pre-switch trends between groups (p = 0.29)

### **Additional Observations**
- Control branch scores **declined** slightly after week 15, while treatment branch scores **increased**
- The new procedure appears to have both improved treatment branch performance and protected against the decline seen in control branches
- Higher visitor traffic (visits_index) is associated with higher service scores, but this does not explain the treatment effect

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with cautious rollout**: The evidence suggests the new procedure improves service scores without negative consequences. A full rollout is reasonable, but consider phasing to allow for monitoring.

2. **Continue monitoring**: Track service scores for an additional 8–12 weeks post-rollout to confirm the effect persists and gather more statistical power.

3. **Investigate mechanisms**: The ~2-point improvement could stem from better stock availability, faster shelving, or improved staff efficiency. Consider qualitative feedback from branch staff to understand what's working.

4. **Consider branch heterogeneity**: Some branches may benefit more than others. Analyze whether branch size, location, or baseline performance moderates the effect.

5. **Cost-benefit analysis**: If the new procedure requires additional training or resources, weigh the ~4% service score improvement against implementation costs.

---

## **Limitations**

- **Statistical power**: With 34 branches and 17 weeks, the study has limited power to detect small effects. The confidence interval includes both modest negative effects and meaningful positive effects.
- **Short time horizon**: Only 8 weeks of post-switch data are available; longer-term effects are unknown.
- **Non-random assignment**: Branches self-selected into switching, which could introduce bias (though the parallel trends test suggests this is not a major concern).
