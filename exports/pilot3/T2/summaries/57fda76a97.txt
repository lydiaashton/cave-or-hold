# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2 points** (a 4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and robust across multiple analytical approaches. We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measurable Improvement in Service Scores
- **Treatment effect: +2.01 points** (95% confidence interval: 0.77 to 3.25)
- **Statistical significance: p = 0.0015** (highly significant)
- This represents roughly a **4% improvement** relative to the average service score of 50.5

### 2. What the Data Shows
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| New Procedure (90 branches) | 50.25 | 51.02 | +0.77 |
| Old Procedure (60 branches) | 50.96 | 49.71 | -1.25 |

The difference-in-differences estimate isolates the procedure's effect by comparing how the two groups changed. While the new-procedure branches improved slightly, the control branches actually declined—suggesting the new procedure may also protect against external factors that were depressing scores system-wide.

### 3. Analysis is Robust
The 2-point effect holds consistently across:
- Basic difference-in-differences regression
- Models accounting for branch-specific characteristics (fixed effects)
- Models accounting for week-to-week variation
- Models controlling for staffing and visit levels

### 4. Pre-Trends Support Validity
Before the switch (weeks 6–14), both groups followed similar weekly patterns with no systematic divergence. This supports the validity of the comparison and suggests the observed effect is attributable to the procedure change rather than pre-existing differences.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all 150 branches.

2. **Monitor implementation** during the rollout to ensure consistent adoption and identify any branches needing additional support.

3. **Track service scores** for 8–12 weeks post-rollout to confirm the effect persists at scale.

4. **Investigate mechanisms**: Consider qualitative follow-up with branch staff to understand *why* the new procedure works (e.g., faster restocking, better organization, reduced staff time on shelving).

5. **Cost-benefit analysis**: If the new procedure involves additional costs (training, equipment, staff time), weigh these against the 4% service score improvement to confirm net benefit.

---

## **Methodology Note**

This analysis uses a difference-in-differences design with data from 150 branches over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22). The procedure switch occurred at week 15. Standard errors are clustered at the branch level to account for within-branch correlation. A visualization of the trends is attached (`service_scores_trend.png`).
