# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Library Cooperative Board  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 14, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw an average increase of **1.9 points** (approximately **4% improvement**) in their service scores compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and consistent across the majority of branches. **We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measured Impact
- **Treatment effect:** +1.88 points on the service score index (95% confidence interval: 1.2 to 2.6 points)
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001 (highly significant)
- **Relative improvement:** ~3.7% above the baseline average score of 51

### 2. Before-and-After Comparison
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches (425) | 50.8 | 52.6 | **+1.8** |
| Non-switched branches (284) | 50.2 | 50.1 | -0.1 |

### 3. Branch-Level Results
- **67.5%** of switched branches improved their scores vs. only **47.9%** of non-switched branches
- Among branches that improved, the average gain was **3.6 points**
- The effect remained significant after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### 4. Robustness Checks
- The parallel trends assumption is satisfied: both groups showed similar patterns before the switch
- Results hold when controlling for week-to-week variation, staffing, and visits
- The effect is not driven by a few outlier branches

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all 284 branches still using the old method

2. **Monitor implementation** over the next 8-12 weeks to ensure:
   - Consistent adoption of the new procedures
   - Service score improvements match the pilot results
   - No unintended operational burdens on staff

3. **Document best practices** from high-performing switched branches to support training

4. **Re-evaluate in Q2 2027** to confirm sustained improvements and assess long-term impact

---

## **Methodology Note**

This analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach comparing 425 branches that switched to the new procedure (treatment group) against 284 branches that did not (control group), using 17 weeks of data (weeks 6-22) with the switch implemented in week 15. The model controls for branch characteristics, time trends, staffing levels, and visitor volume.
