# Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

## Bottom Line

**The new procedure is associated with higher service scores, but we cannot confidently attribute this improvement to the procedure itself.** Branches that adopted the new procedure showed service scores approximately **1.8 points higher** (about 4% improvement) than comparable branches after the switch. However, these branches were already on an upward trajectory before the change, which complicates our ability to claim the procedure caused the improvement.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **phased rollout** accompanied by rigorous monitoring, rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.

### 2. Important Caveat: Pre-Existing Differences

The analysis reveals a significant concern for causal interpretation:

- **Branches selected to switch were already improving faster** than control branches *before* the procedure change
- The gap between switched and control branches widened from **-0.2 points** (week 6) to **+2.7 points** (week 14) during the pre-switch period
- This suggests branches were not randomly assigned to the new procedure—selection bias may explain part of the observed effect

## Next Steps

### Immediate Actions
1. **Do not halt the current implementation**—branches using the new procedure are performing well
2. **Document the selection criteria** used to choose which branches switched—this will help understand the pre-trend issue

### Recommended Rollout Strategy
1. **Phased expansion**: Roll out to additional branches in waves (e.g., 50-100 branches per month)
2. **Randomized assignment**: For the next phase, randomly assign branches to treatment/control to enable cleaner causal inference
3. **Enhanced monitoring**: Track service scores weekly for all branches during rollout

### Evaluation Plan
1. **Continue data collection** with the same weekly frequency
2. **Set success threshold**: Define minimum acceptable improvement (e.g., +1.0 point sustained over 8 weeks)
3. **Interim review**: Board review after 8 weeks of expanded rollout with fresh DiD analysis

### Risk Mitigation
- If scores decline at newly-switched branches, pause and investigate
- Branches showing sustained negative effects should revert to old procedure
- Budget for potential training/support if implementation quality varies across branches
