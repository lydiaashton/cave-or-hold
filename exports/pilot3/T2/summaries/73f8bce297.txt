## **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

### **Title: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

### **Bottom Line**

The new procedure is associated with a **positive improvement in service scores of approximately 2.5 points** (about 5% relative to baseline). However, this effect is **not statistically significant at conventional levels** (p ≈ 0.10), meaning we cannot rule out that the observed improvement occurred by chance. The evidence is suggestive but not conclusive. We recommend a **cautious, phased rollout** with continued monitoring rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. Study Design**
- 34 branches total: 20 adopted the new procedure (treatment), 14 kept the old procedure (control)
- Data covers 17 weeks (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in treatment branches to changes in control branches

**2. Estimated Effect**
- Treatment branches improved by an average of **3.2 points** after the switch
- Control branches improved by an average of **0.8 points** over the same period
- **Net effect of the new procedure: +2.5 points** on the service score index
- This represents approximately a **5% improvement** relative to typical branch scores (~50)

**3. Statistical Confidence**
- The effect is **not statistically significant at the 5% level** (p-value ≈ 0.10)
- The 95% confidence interval ranges from approximately **-0.5 to +5.6 points**
- This means the true effect could be slightly negative, zero, or moderately positive

**4. Pre-Trend Validation**
- Before the switch, treatment and control branches showed **parallel trends** in service scores
- This supports the validity of the comparison (no evidence treatment branches were already improving faster)

**5. Variation Across Branches**
- Considerable variation exists: some treatment branches saw declines (-3.4 points), while others saw large gains (+10 points)
- This suggests the procedure's effectiveness may depend on branch-specific factors (size, staffing, layout, etc.)

---

### **Next Steps**

1. **Continue monitoring** the current treatment branches for at least 8–12 more weeks to see if the effect strengthens with more data

2. **Investigate branch-level heterogeneity** to understand why some branches benefited more than others (e.g., correlate effects with branch size, staffing levels, or visit volume)

3. **Consider a phased rollout**: Implement the new procedure in additional branches incrementally, measuring results before expanding further

4. **Set a decision threshold**: Define what magnitude of improvement would justify full rollout (e.g., if the effect stabilizes at +3 points with tighter confidence intervals)

5. **Document implementation details**: Ensure consistent training and procedure application across branches to reduce variation in outcomes
