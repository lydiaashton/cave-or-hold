# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new shelving and rotation procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **1.8 points** (95% confidence interval: 0.6 to 3.0 points) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents approximately a **3.6% improvement** over the average service score of 50. The result is statistically significant (p = 0.004), and we recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15 (treatment group)
- **60 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in switched branches to changes in non-switched branches

### Results
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Treatment effect | **+1.81 points** |
| 95% Confidence interval | **[0.59, 3.03]** |
| p-value | **0.004** |
| Improvement vs. mean | **+3.6%** |

### What Changed
- **Switched branches:** Service scores increased from 50.3 (before) to 51.5 (after) — a +1.2 point change
- **Non-switched branches:** Service scores decreased from 51.3 (before) to 50.7 (after) — a -0.6 point change
- **Net effect:** The new procedure added 1.8 points beyond what would have happened otherwise

### Robustness Checks
✓ **Parallel trends confirmed:** Before the switch, both groups followed similar trends (p = 0.80)  
✓ **Controls don't change result:** Effect remains significant after accounting for staffing and visitor volume  
✓ **Effect persists over time:** Positive effects observed in most weeks after the switch, particularly weeks 18–22  
✓ **Not driven by outliers:** Result holds even excluding an anomalous week (week 17)

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with rollout:** The evidence supports expanding the new procedure to all 150 branches.

2. **Monitor implementation:** Track service scores during rollout to ensure similar gains are realized across all branches.

3. **Investigate mechanisms:** Consider studying *why* the new procedure improved scores (e.g., faster item location, better organization) to optimize training.

4. **Cost-benefit analysis:** If the new procedure has additional costs (training, labor), weigh them against the 3.6% service improvement.

5. **Long-term follow-up:** Continue monitoring to confirm effects are sustained beyond the initial 8-week post-switch period.
