# **MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure shows a **positive but statistically uncertain effect** on service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure improved their service scores by approximately **2 points (about 4%)** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. However, this improvement is **not statistically significant** at conventional levels, meaning we cannot rule out that the observed difference occurred by chance. **We recommend a cautious, phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** total: 20 adopted the new procedure, 14 kept the old procedure
- Data covers **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in service scores between switching and non-switching branches before and after the intervention

### **Results**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated treatment effect | **+2.04 points** |
| 95% Confidence interval | **-0.74 to +4.81 points** |
| P-value | **0.15** |
| Effect size (vs. baseline) | **~4% improvement** |

### **What the Numbers Mean**

1. **Positive direction**: The new procedure is associated with higher service scores, consistent with expectations.

2. **Statistical uncertainty**: The confidence interval includes zero, and the p-value (0.15) exceeds the conventional 0.05 threshold. This means the evidence is **suggestive but not conclusive**.

3. **Parallel trends validated**: Before the switch, both groups showed similar trends in service scores, supporting the validity of our comparison.

4. **Control group also improved**: Branches keeping the old procedure saw a modest 1.3-point increase after week 15, suggesting other factors (seasonal effects, system-wide initiatives) may have influenced scores.

5. **Treatment group improvement**: Branches with the new procedure improved by about 3.3 points from their pre-switch baseline, narrowing the gap with the control group.

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended Actions**

1. **Proceed with phased rollout**: Given the positive (though uncertain) effect and no evidence of harm, expand the new procedure to additional branches while continuing to collect data.

2. **Extend the evaluation period**: The current post-switch window is only 8 weeks. Service score improvements may take time to fully materialize. Collect at least 3–6 more months of data.

3. **Monitor implementation fidelity**: Ensure branches adopting the new procedure are implementing it consistently. Variation in execution could dilute the measured effect.

4. **Track secondary outcomes**: The new procedure may affect operational efficiency, staff satisfaction, or patron experience in ways not captured by the service score alone.

5. **Re-evaluate in 6 months**: With more data, statistical power will increase, allowing for a more definitive conclusion about the procedure's effectiveness.

### **Decision Framework**

| Scenario | Recommendation |
|----------|----------------|
| Effect remains ~2 points but becomes significant | Proceed with full rollout |
| Effect grows larger with more data | Accelerate rollout |
| Effect shrinks or becomes negative | Halt rollout and investigate |
| Effect remains uncertain after extended period | Consider cost-benefit analysis of implementation costs vs. modest gains |
