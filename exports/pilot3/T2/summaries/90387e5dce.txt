# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

## **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

### **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure **improved service scores by approximately 2.15 points (4.3%)**, but the evidence is **not statistically conclusive** at conventional confidence levels. The effect is suggestive of a real benefit, but we cannot rule out the possibility that the observed improvement occurred by chance. We recommend a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. Measured Impact**
- Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by **2.15 points more** than branches that kept the old procedure
- This represents a **4.3% improvement** relative to baseline scores (~50 points)
- The 95% confidence interval ranges from **-0.3 to +4.6 points**, meaning the true effect could be negligible or as high as a 9% improvement

**2. Statistical Significance**
- The p-value of **0.087** indicates the result is *marginally significant*—stronger evidence than random chance, but not meeting the conventional 0.05 threshold
- With more data or a longer observation period, we may achieve greater statistical certainty

**3. Parallel Trends Validated**
- Before the switch (weeks 6-14), treatment and control branches followed similar trends (p=0.18 for differential pre-trends)
- This supports the validity of our comparison and strengthens confidence in the estimated effect

**4. Study Design**
- **34 branches** total: 20 adopted the new procedure, 14 kept the old
- **17 weeks** of data (weeks 6-22), with the switch implemented in week 15
- Analysis controls for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume

---

### **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with Cautious Expansion**: Roll out the new procedure to an additional 10-15 branches over the next quarter to gather more evidence

2. **Extend Monitoring Period**: Continue tracking service scores for at least 8-12 more weeks to improve statistical power and assess whether effects persist or grow

3. **Track Secondary Outcomes**: Monitor whether the procedure affects operational costs, staff time, or patron satisfaction beyond the service score index

4. **Set Decision Threshold**: Establish a clear criterion for full rollout (e.g., if the effect remains positive and p-value drops below 0.05 after expanded implementation)

5. **Document Implementation**: Capture any branch-level variations in how the procedure was implemented to identify best practices
