# **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new procedure saw their service scores increase by approximately **1.9 points** (about **3.7% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and robust to controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume. The evidence supports rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect
- **Estimated improvement: +1.86 points** on the service score index (95% confidence interval: 0.65 to 3.07)
- **Statistical significance: p = 0.003** (highly significant)
- This represents a **3.7% improvement** relative to the pre-switch baseline of ~50 points

### 2. Study Design Strength
- **102 branches** adopted the new procedure (treatment group); **48 branches** kept the old procedure (control group)
- Data covers **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- **Pre-trend validation:** Statistical testing confirms the treatment and control groups had parallel trends before the switch (p = 0.801), supporting a causal interpretation

### 3. Robustness Checks
- The effect remains significant after controlling for **staffing levels** and **visitor volume**, both of which independently affect service scores
- Cluster-robust standard errors (accounting for repeated observations per branch) were used, making the inference conservative and reliable

### 4. Visual Evidence
The trend chart shows that:
- Before week 15, both groups tracked closely together
- After the switch, the treatment group's scores diverged upward relative to the control group

## **Technical Notes**

- **Method:** Difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch)
- **Controls included:** Staffing index, visits index
- **Data:** 2,550 observations across 150 branches over 17 weeks
- **Assumption check:** Parallel pre-trends confirmed (no evidence of differential trends before the intervention)
