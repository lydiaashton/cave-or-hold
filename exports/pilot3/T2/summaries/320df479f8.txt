# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Library Cooperative Board  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 14, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **1.9 points more** than branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and represents approximately a **4% improvement** relative to the typical branch score of 50. We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measurable Improvement in Service Quality
- Switched branches improved by **4.1 points** on average (from 48.8 to 52.9)
- Non-switched branches improved by **2.2 points** on average (from 47.8 to 50.0)
- **Net effect of the new procedure: +1.9 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.4 to 2.5 points)
- This difference is highly statistically significant (p < 0.001)

### 2. Broad-Based Benefits
- **87% of switched branches** saw their service scores improve after adopting the new procedure
- Only **13% of switched branches** experienced a decline
- By comparison, 73% of non-switched branches improved (suggesting some system-wide factors also contributed to gains)

### 3. Effect Persists After Accounting for Other Factors
The improvement remains unchanged when controlling for:
- **Staffing levels** (staffing_index)
- **Branch traffic** (visits_index)

This indicates the gain is attributable to the procedure itself, not to changes in staffing or visitor volume.

### 4. Study Design Strengths
- **709 branches** total: 425 adopted the new procedure (60%), 284 kept the old (40%)
- **17 weeks of data**: 9 weeks before the switch (weeks 6-14), 8 weeks after (weeks 15-22)
- Pre-switch trends between the two groups were reasonably parallel, supporting the validity of the comparison

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all remaining branches

2. **Monitor implementation** at newly converted branches to ensure they achieve similar gains

3. **Investigate the 13% of switched branches** that did not improve to identify any implementation barriers or branch-specific factors

4. **Consider a follow-up evaluation** in 3-6 months to assess whether the gains are sustained over time

5. **Document best practices** from high-performing switched branches to support training during the rollout

---

## **Technical Notes**

- Analysis used a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in switched vs. non-switched branches before and after week 15
- Standard errors clustered by branch to account for repeated observations
- Service score average across all observations: 50.1 (higher is better)
- Effect size: approximately 0.20 standard deviations
