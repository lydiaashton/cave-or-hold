# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new procedure saw service scores increase by an estimated **1.85 points** more than branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and represents a meaningful **3.7% improvement** in service quality. We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

## **Key Findings**

### 1. The New Procedure Had a Positive, Statistically Significant Effect

Using a rigorous difference-in-differences analysis (comparing switched vs. non-switched branches before and after week 15), we estimate the new procedure **increased service scores by 1.85 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.32 to 2.39 points). This result is highly statistically significant (p < 0.001).

### 2. The Effect Is Consistent Across Branches

- **92.9%** of branches using the new procedure showed improvement, compared to 84.5% of branches using the old procedure
- Two-thirds (66.6%) of switched branches improved more than the average non-switched branch
- The effect held after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume, confirming the improvement is attributable to the procedure change itself

### 3. Service Scores Were Following Similar Trends Before the Switch

The analysis relies on the assumption that switched and non-switched branches would have continued on similar trajectories absent the intervention. Examination of the pre-switch period (weeks 6–14) shows both groups experienced similar week-to-week fluctuations, supporting the validity of our causal estimate.

### 4. Practical Significance

- The average service score across all branches is approximately **50.6**
- A 1.85-point increase represents a **3.7% improvement** in service quality
- In standard deviation terms, this is a **0.18 SD effect**—a modest but meaningful improvement for an operational change
