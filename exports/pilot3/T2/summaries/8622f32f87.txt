# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Contrary to the initial expectation of no difference, branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores that were **1.66 points higher** than would have been expected based on the control group trend (p = 0.003). This represents a meaningful improvement, and we recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **150 branches** tracked over 17 weeks (Weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** switched to the new procedure at Week 15 (treatment group)
- **60 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach with cluster-robust standard errors

### **Main Result**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect of new procedure | **+1.66 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | [0.58, 2.74] |
| Statistical significance | **p = 0.003** (significant at 1% level) |

### **What This Means in Practice**
- Control group (old procedure) scores **declined by 1.78 points** after Week 15
- Treatment group (new procedure) scores remained **essentially stable** (declined only 0.12 points)
- The new procedure **prevented the decline** seen in control branches and provided a net benefit of ~1.7 points

### **Validity Checks**
✓ **Parallel trends confirmed**: No statistically significant differences in trends between groups before the switch (all pre-period p-values > 0.24)

✓ **Robust to controls**: Effect remains significant (+1.74 points, p = 0.002) after accounting for staffing levels and visit volumes

✓ **Consistent post-switch effects**: Positive effects observed in most weeks after the switch, with several weeks showing statistically significant improvements
