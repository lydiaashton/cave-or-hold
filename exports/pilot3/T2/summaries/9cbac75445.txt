# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

## **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

### **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **2.3 points** (approximately 4.6% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and consistent across all post-switch weeks. We recommend proceeding with a full rollout.

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. Study Design & Data**
- 709 branches total: 425 adopted the new procedure, 284 kept the old procedure
- 17 weeks of data (weeks 6–22), with the switch implemented in week 15
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in treatment branches against control branches

**2. Treatment Effect**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated improvement | **+2.30 points** |
| 95% Confidence interval | 1.71 to 2.90 points |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 (highly significant) |
| Relative improvement | ~4.6% above baseline |

**3. Week-by-Week Performance After Switch**
The treatment group outperformed the control group in every single week after the switch:

| Week | Treatment − Control Gap |
|------|------------------------|
| 15 | +3.82 points |
| 16 | +3.67 points |
| 17 | +4.22 points |
| 18 | +4.30 points |
| 19 | +4.11 points |
| 20 | +4.58 points |
| 21 | +4.68 points |
| 22 | +5.17 points |

The widening gap suggests the benefits may accumulate over time as staff become more familiar with the new procedure.

**4. Robustness**
- Results hold when controlling for staffing levels and visit volumes
- Results hold when accounting for week-to-week variations affecting all branches
- The effect is economically meaningful: a 2.3-point gain on a ~50-point scale

---

### **Caveats & Limitations**

1. **Baseline differences**: Treatment branches had slightly higher service scores than control branches before the switch (about 2 points higher). The analysis accounts for this, but it's worth noting.

2. **Study duration**: We have only 8 weeks of post-switch data. Longer-term monitoring would confirm whether effects persist or grow.

3. **Implementation variation**: We cannot assess whether some branches implemented the procedure more faithfully than others.

---

### **Next Steps**

1. **Approve full rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches.

2. **Develop implementation support**: Create training materials and assign implementation coaches to help branches adopt the procedure smoothly.

3. **Continue monitoring**: Track service scores for the next 3–6 months to confirm the effect persists and identify any branches that may need additional support.

4. **Investigate mechanisms**: The analysis shows *that* the procedure works. A follow-up study could examine *why*—whether it's faster stock retrieval, better organization, or reduced errors.

5. **Cost-benefit analysis**: Quantify the operational costs of the new procedure against the service score gains to calculate ROI.
