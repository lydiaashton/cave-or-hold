### **Title: Impact Assessment of New Branch Procedure on Service Scores**

---

### **Bottom Line**

The new rotation and shelving procedure shows a **positive but statistically uncertain effect** on service scores. Switched branches improved by approximately **1.7 points** (about 3.5% above baseline) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. However, this improvement is not statistically significant at conventional confidence levels, meaning we cannot rule out that the observed difference occurred by chance. **We recommend a cautious, phased rollout with continued monitoring rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.**

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. Treatment Effect Estimate**
- Branches that adopted the new procedure showed an average service score improvement of **1.7 points** relative to branches that kept the old procedure
- The 95% confidence interval ranges from **-1.6 to +5.0 points**, indicating substantial uncertainty about the true effect
- Statistical significance: p = 0.08 (marginally significant when accounting for repeated measurements per branch)

**2. Service Score Trends**

**3. Important Context**
- **Both groups improved** after week 15, suggesting external factors (seasonal effects, system-wide initiatives) may have boosted scores across all branches
- The parallel trends assumption is satisfied: switched and non-switched branches followed similar trajectories before the procedure change
- The effect size (3.5% improvement) is modest relative to the typical branch score of ~50

**4. Statistical Limitations**
- Only 8 weeks of post-switch data limits our ability to detect smaller effects
- With 34 branches total, the study has limited statistical power
- The confidence interval includes both negative and positive values, so we cannot confidently conclude the procedure helps or harms service quality

---

### **Next Steps**

**1. Continue Monitoring (Recommended)**
- Collect at least 8-12 additional weeks of data to improve statistical precision
- This would narrow the confidence interval and provide a clearer answer on effectiveness

**2. Consider Phased Rollout**
- If the board wishes to proceed, implement the new procedure in additional branches gradually
- Use a staggered rollout design to continue generating rigorous evidence
- Monitor service scores closely during expansion

**3. Investigate Mechanisms**
- Analyze whether the procedure affects certain branch types differently (e.g., by size, location, or traffic volume)
- Examine secondary outcomes (staffing efficiency, patron satisfaction) beyond the service score

**4. Cost-Benefit Assessment**
- Before full rollout, quantify the operational costs of the new procedure
- Weigh these costs against the uncertain 1.7-point service score benefit

---

### **Conclusion**

The new procedure shows promise but lacks conclusive evidence of effectiveness. A patient, evidence-based approach—collecting more data while considering a measured expansion—would best serve the cooperative's interests and the communities we serve.
