## **Analysis of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

### **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure **improved service scores** by an average of 1.8 points (approximately 3.5% relative to the baseline average of 50). This improvement is statistically significant and was not expected based on prior assumptions. The evidence supports rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

### **Key Findings**

**Study Design**
- 150 branches total: 90 adopted the new procedure, 60 continued with the old procedure
- Data covers weeks 6–22, with the switch occurring at week 15
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in switched branches against changes in non-switched branches

**Results**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Treatment effect | +1.78 points |
| 95% confidence interval | [0.63, 2.93] |
| p-value | 0.002 |
| Relative improvement | 3.5% |

**Validity Checks**
- **Parallel trends confirmed**: Service score trends for switched and non-switched branches were statistically indistinguishable before the switch (all pre-treatment comparisons p > 0.50)
- **Robust to controls**: The effect remains significant (p = 0.003) when controlling for weekly staffing and visitor volume
- **Consistent over time**: Positive effects observed in most post-switch weeks, with the strongest effects in weeks 16–18

**What This Means**
- The average branch scores about 50 on the service index; the new procedure lifted scores by nearly 2 points
- The effect is modest but meaningful and statistically reliable
- There is no evidence of negative consequences from the procedure change

### **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** – The data supports extending the new procedure to all 150 branches
2. **Monitor implementation** – Track service scores during rollout to ensure consistent effects across all branches
3. **Document best practices** – Capture what switched branches did during implementation to support training
4. **Consider cost-benefit** – If the new procedure has additional costs, weigh them against the 3.5% service improvement

---

**Technical Note**: Analysis used cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch) to account for repeated weekly observations. The event study design confirms no pre-existing differences in trends between treatment and control groups.
