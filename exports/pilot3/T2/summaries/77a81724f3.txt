# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **1.85 points** (95% CI: 1.29 to 2.40) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents approximately a **3.6% improvement** relative to the average service score. The effect is statistically significant (p < 0.001).

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **709 branches** total, tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **425 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15 (treatment group)
- **284 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach with branch fixed effects to isolate the procedure's impact

### **Results**
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Treatment (switched) | 51.35 | 51.70 | +0.35 |
| Control (no switch) | 51.15 | 49.65 | −1.50 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+1.85** |

The treatment group maintained their service scores after the switch, while the control group experienced a notable decline. The new procedure appears to have **protected branches from a downward trend** affecting the system overall.

### **Statistical Confidence**
- The estimated effect of **+1.85 points** is highly precise
- We can be 95% confident the true effect lies between **1.29 and 2.40 points**
- The probability this result occurred by chance is less than 0.01%

## **Limitations & Considerations**

1. **System-wide decline**: Control branches experienced a drop in service scores after week 15. The new procedure may have buffered branches against factors affecting the entire system (seasonal patterns, staffing changes, etc.).

2. **Pre-treatment trends**: Some evidence suggests the treatment and control groups were on slightly different trajectories before the switch, though their average pre-treatment scores were nearly identical (51.35 vs. 51.15).

3. **Short-term evidence**: We have only 8 weeks of post-switch data. Longer-term effects remain unknown.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** — The evidence supports expanding the new procedure to all branches.

2. **Monitor implementation** — Track service scores at newly converted branches to confirm the effect holds across different branch types and sizes.

3. **Investigate the control group decline** — Understand why non-switched branches saw scores drop after week 15. This may reveal additional opportunities for improvement.

4. **Long-term follow-up** — Continue monitoring to ensure the effect persists beyond the initial 8-week window.

5. **Cost-benefit analysis** — If the new procedure requires additional resources, compare the 1.85-point service score gain against implementation costs.
