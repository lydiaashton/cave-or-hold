# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure is associated with a **modest improvement in service scores** (+1.8 points, or ~3.5%). While the result is not statistically definitive, the evidence suggests the new procedure has a positive effect. We recommend **proceeding with a cautious rollout** while continuing to monitor outcomes.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure; **14 branches** kept the old procedure (control group)
- Switch occurred at **week 15**
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in treatment branches against control branches

### Results

| Group | Pre-Switch Avg | Post-Switch Avg | Change |
|-------|---------------|-----------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 51.4 | 50.7 | -0.7 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 51.1 | 52.2 | +1.1 |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | | | **+1.8** |

- **Estimated treatment effect:** +1.79 service score points (95% CI: approximately -0.3 to +3.9)
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.088 (marginally significant; 90% confidence)
- **Parallel trends check:** Pre-switch trends were similar between groups, supporting the validity of the comparison

### Additional Insights
- Branches with higher staffing and visit levels tend to have higher service scores (both statistically significant predictors)
- The control group experienced a slight decline post-switch, while treatment branches improved—suggesting the new procedure may also help branches resist external pressures that were affecting scores system-wide

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with rollout** to remaining branches, but implement in phases to allow for ongoing monitoring

2. **Extend the evaluation period**—collect 8–12 more weeks of data to increase statistical confidence and assess whether effects persist or grow over time

3. **Monitor implementation fidelity**—ensure branches adopting the new procedure follow it consistently; variation in implementation could be masking stronger effects

4. **Track secondary outcomes**—examine whether the procedure affects operational efficiency, staff satisfaction, or patron wait times, not just service scores

5. **Set a review checkpoint** at 3 months post-rollout to reassess the decision based on accumulated data
