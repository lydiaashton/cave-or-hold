# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

## **Subject: Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

### **BOTTOM LINE**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **1.9 points** (about **4% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and consistent across the post-switch period. We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

### **KEY FINDINGS**

**1. Study Design**
- 150 branches tracked over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- 90 branches switched to the new procedure at week 15; 60 branches kept the old procedure
- This natural experiment allows us to isolate the effect of the new procedure from other factors

**2. Main Result**
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches (new procedure) | 51.1 | 53.4 | +2.3 |
| Non-switched branches (old procedure) | 50.6 | 50.9 | +0.3 |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | | **+2.0** |

After adjusting for staffing levels and visitor volume, the estimated effect is **+1.91 points** (95% confidence interval: 0.78 to 3.03; p = 0.001).

**3. The Effect Is Real and Reliable**
- **Parallel trends confirmed**: Before week 15, both groups followed similar trajectories—no pre-existing differences that would bias the result
- **Consistent post-switch gains**: Every week after the switch showed positive effects, ranging from +1.8 to +3.4 points
- **Robust to controls**: The effect holds even after accounting for weekly staffing and visit fluctuations

**4. Practical Significance**
- Service scores average around 50; a 2-point gain represents meaningful improvement
- The improvement is comparable to moving a branch from the 50th percentile to roughly the 58th percentile of performance

---

### **NEXT STEPS**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all 60 branches still using the old method

2. **Monitor implementation** during the transition:
   - Track service scores for 8–12 weeks after each branch switches
   - Expect similar ~2-point gains if the pilot results hold

3. **Document best practices** from the 90 early-adopter branches to support training at remaining branches

4. **Consider follow-up analysis** after full rollout to:
   - Confirm effects persist at scale
   - Identify whether certain branch types benefit more or less
   - Assess any unintended consequences (e.g., staff time, costs)
