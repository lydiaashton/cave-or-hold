# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure is associated with an improvement in service scores of approximately **2.5 points** (about 5% relative to baseline). However, this result is **not statistically significant** at conventional levels, meaning we cannot rule out that the observed improvement occurred by chance. The evidence is suggestive but not conclusive. We recommend a cautious approach: continue monitoring while gathering more data before committing to a system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **34 branches** participated in the evaluation (20 switched to the new procedure, 14 kept the old procedure)
- Data covers **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Service scores average around 50 points (higher is better)

### Results Summary

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 50.84 | 51.63 | +0.78 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 52.85 | 56.09 | +3.23 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+2.45** |

### Statistical Analysis
- **Estimated effect**: +2.45 points on the service score
- **95% Confidence Interval**: -0.62 to +5.52 points
- **p-value**: 0.13 (not significant at the 0.05 level)
- **Effect size**: Medium (Cohen's d = 0.57)

### What This Means
1. **Positive direction**: Branches that switched showed greater improvement than those that didn't, consistent with our hypothesis.
2. **Uncertain magnitude**: The confidence interval includes zero, meaning the true effect could be anywhere from slightly negative to substantially positive.
3. **Parallel trends validated**: Before the switch, both groups showed similar trends, supporting the validity of our comparison.
4. **Practical significance**: If the effect is real, a 2.5-point improvement represents meaningful progress on a ~50-point scale.

### Why Isn't It Statistically Significant?
- **Limited sample size**: Only 34 branches total
- **Short observation window**: Just 8 weeks of post-switch data
- **Natural variability**: Service scores fluctuate week-to-week due to many factors

## **Next Steps**

### Recommended Actions

1. **Continue the pilot for 8–12 more weeks**
   - Additional data will narrow the confidence interval and provide greater certainty
   - Target: achieve statistical significance or rule out meaningful effects

2. **Monitor implementation fidelity**
   - Ensure switched branches are fully adopting the new procedure
   - Document any variations in how the procedure is applied

3. **Track secondary metrics**
   - Staffing index and visits index may provide additional insights
   - Consider cost-benefit analysis if the procedure requires additional resources

4. **Plan for phased rollout**
   - If results remain positive after extended monitoring, consider rolling out in waves
   - This allows continued evaluation while expanding benefits

### Decision Framework

| Scenario | Recommendation |
|----------|----------------|
| Effect becomes significant (p < 0.05) with continued positive direction | Proceed with system-wide rollout |
| Effect remains positive but non-significant after extended monitoring | Consider rollout with ongoing evaluation |
| Effect diminishes or becomes negative | Re-evaluate the procedure before any expansion |

---

## **Conclusion**

The new procedure shows promise, with branches that adopted it experiencing greater service score improvements than those that didn't. However, the evidence is not yet strong enough to justify immediate system-wide implementation. A modest extension of the evaluation period will provide the board with greater confidence for this important decision.
