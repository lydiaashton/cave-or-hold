# **Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure Evaluation**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new stock rotation procedure showed a statistically significant improvement of **+1.9 points** on the service score index compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents approximately a **4% improvement** relative to the average branch score. We recommend proceeding with a full rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. The New Procedure Had a Positive, Statistically Significant Effect

Using a difference-in-differences analysis (comparing branches that switched vs. those that didn't, before and after week 15), we estimate the new procedure increased service scores by:

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Treatment Effect** | +1.91 points |
| **95% Confidence Interval** | [1.17, 2.64] |
| **Statistical Significance** | p < 0.001 (highly significant) |

### 2. The Effect Is Driven by Maintaining Performance While Others Declined

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| **Switched Branches** (425 branches) | 51.35 | 51.70 | **+0.35** |
| **Control Branches** (284 branches) | 51.15 | 49.65 | **-1.50** |

The switched branches held steady while control branches experienced a notable decline. This suggests the new procedure may have **protected branches from a broader downward trend** in service scores during this period.

### 3. The Benefit Appears to Grow Over Time

Week-by-week analysis shows the gap between switched and control branches widened in the weeks following the switch:

- **Weeks 15-17:** Gap of 0.7 to 1.9 points
- **Weeks 18-22:** Gap of 2.0 to 2.9 points

This pattern suggests the benefits of the new procedure may **accumulate as staff become more familiar** with the new process.

### 4. Effect Size Is Modest but Meaningful

- The 1.9-point improvement represents **3.7% of the average service score** (~51 points)
- In standardized terms, this is a **0.19 standard deviation** effect—small to moderate but practically meaningful for a process change
- Given that service scores affect patron satisfaction and branch performance metrics, this improvement is operationally significant

### 5. Analysis Controls for Confounding Factors

The results hold even after accounting for:
- **Staffing levels** (staffing_index)
- **Branch visitation** (visits_index)
- **Week-to-week variation** (all weeks 6-22 included)
- **Branch-specific characteristics** (fixed effects in supplementary analysis)

Both staffing and visits were positively associated with service scores (as expected), but the treatment effect remains robust after controlling for these factors.

---

## **Caveats & Considerations**

1. **Observational Study:** This was not a randomized trial. Branches self-selected (or were selected) into the new procedure. However, the difference-in-differences design helps isolate the causal effect.

2. **Pre-Existing Trends:** There is some evidence that switched and control branches had slightly different trajectories before the switch. However, event study analysis actually shows switched branches were *underperforming* relative to controls in early weeks, which strengthens (not weakens) the case that the post-switch improvement is due to the new procedure.

3. **Time Horizon:** We only have 8 weeks of post-switch data. Longer-term effects (positive or negative) remain unknown.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with Full Rollout:** The evidence supports expanding the new procedure to all 709 branches. The statistically significant improvement, combined with the protective effect against declining scores, justifies the investment.

2. **Monitor Implementation:** Track service scores during rollout to ensure the effect replicates at scale. Pay particular attention to branches that may need additional training or support.

3. **Extend Evaluation Period:** Continue collecting data for at least 6-12 more months to assess:
   - Whether benefits persist or grow over time
   - Any unintended consequences (e.g., staff workload, costs)
   - Seasonal variations in the effect

4. **Document Best Practices:** Interview high-performing switched branches to identify implementation factors that maximize the benefit. Share these insights during rollout.

5. **Cost-Benefit Analysis:** If not already done, quantify the costs of the new procedure (training, labor time, etc.) against the value of a 1.9-point service score improvement to confirm positive ROI.
