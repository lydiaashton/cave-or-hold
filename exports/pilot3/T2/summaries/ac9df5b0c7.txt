# **Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure Evaluation**

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation procedure shows a **positive but statistically inconclusive effect** on service scores. Branches that switched to the new procedure improved by approximately **1.9 points (4.1%)** relative to branches that kept the old procedure. However, this result is not statistically significant at conventional levels, meaning we cannot rule out that the observed improvement occurred by chance. **We recommend a cautious, phased rollout with continued monitoring rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Treatment Effect Estimate**
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 47.44 | 47.16 | -0.28 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 46.61 | 48.23 | +1.62 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+1.90 points** |

- The new procedure is associated with a **1.9-point increase** in service scores (approximately 4% improvement)
- **95% confidence interval: -0.5 to +4.4 points**
- **p-value: 0.10–0.12** (not statistically significant at the 0.05 level)

### **2. Study Design & Validity**
- **34 branches total**: 20 switched to the new procedure, 14 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data**: Weeks 6–14 (before), Weeks 15–22 (after)
- **Parallel trends assumption supported**: No significant difference in pre-switch trends between groups (p = 0.38), validating our comparison approach

### **3. Practical Significance**
- Effect size is **small** (Cohen's d = 0.20)
- The confidence interval includes both negative and positive values, ranging from a slight decline to a meaningful 4-point improvement
- Service scores naturally fluctuate week-to-week (standard deviation: 9.6 points), making it harder to detect modest improvements

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended Actions**

1. **Do NOT proceed with immediate system-wide rollout.** The evidence is suggestive but not conclusive.

2. **Extend the observation period.** Collect 8–12 more weeks of data to:
   - Increase statistical power
   - Determine if the effect is sustained over time
   - Reduce uncertainty in the estimate

3. **Consider a stepped-wedge rollout.** Gradually switch remaining branches while continuing to monitor, allowing for ongoing evaluation.

4. **Investigate heterogeneity.** Analyze whether the procedure works better for certain branch types (e.g., by size, location, or baseline performance).

5. **Review cost-benefit.** Even if the 1.9-point improvement is real, assess whether the operational costs of the new procedure justify this gain.

### **What Would Constitute Success?**

For a confident rollout recommendation, we would want to see:
- A statistically significant effect (p < 0.05)
- A lower bound of the confidence interval above zero (e.g., +0.5 points or higher)
- Evidence that the effect is sustained over multiple months
