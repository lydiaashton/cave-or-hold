# Analysis of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

## Bottom Line

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new procedure saw their service scores increase by approximately **2.1 points** (about 4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and robust to alternative specifications. We recommend proceeding with a full rollout.

## Key Findings

### 1. Treatment Effect
- **Estimated impact**: +2.1 points on the service score index (95% confidence interval: 0.5 to 3.6 points)
- **Statistical significance**: p < 0.001 (highly significant)
- **Relative improvement**: Approximately 4% increase over the baseline average score of 49.5

### 2. Study Design
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure starting in week 15
- **60 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Data covers **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22), with 9 weeks before and 8 weeks after the switch
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in switched branches to changes in non-switched branches

### 3. Validity Checks
- **Parallel trends assumption holds**: Before the switch (weeks 6–14), both groups showed similar trends in service scores (interaction term p = 0.28)
- **Results are robust**: Adding controls for staffing levels and visit volumes does not change the estimated effect (remains ~2.1 points)
- **Bootstrap validation**: 1,000 bootstrap samples confirm the 95% confidence interval excludes zero
