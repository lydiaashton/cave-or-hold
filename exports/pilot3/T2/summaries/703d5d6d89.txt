# **Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure shows a **positive but not statistically significant** effect on service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores improve by approximately **1.8 points** relative to branches that kept the old procedure. However, due to limited data and natural variation in scores, we cannot confidently rule out that this improvement occurred by chance. The board may wish to continue the pilot and gather more data before making a system-wide rollout decision.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** total: 20 adopted the new procedure, 14 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data**: Weeks 6–14 (before switch), Weeks 15–22 (after switch)
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in switched branches against changes in non-switched branches

### **Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 47.7 | 46.5 | −1.2 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 50.6 | 51.3 | +0.7 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+1.8** |

- **Estimated effect**: +1.82 service score points
- **95% confidence interval**: −0.4 to +4.1 points
- **Statistical significance**: p = 0.11 (not significant at conventional 0.05 level)

### **What This Means**
- The new procedure is associated with higher service scores, but the evidence is **suggestive rather than conclusive**
- The confidence interval includes zero, meaning we cannot rule out no effect
- The effect size (1.8 points) represents about a **3–4% improvement** relative to the average score of ~50
- Control branches actually saw a slight decline in scores, while switched branches maintained or slightly improved

### **Additional Observations**
- **Visits index** is a significant predictor of service scores (higher visits → higher scores)
- **Staffing index** showed no significant relationship with service scores
- Pre-switch trends showed the gap between groups was narrowing, which slightly complicates interpretation

---

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended Actions**

1. **Continue the pilot for 8–12 more weeks** to increase statistical power and confirm whether the positive trend holds

2. **Consider expanding the pilot** to include more branches if operationally feasible, which would improve our ability to detect true effects

3. **Monitor implementation fidelity** at switched branches to ensure the new procedure is being applied consistently

4. **Track secondary outcomes** such as:
   - Staff time spent on shelving
   - Patron satisfaction surveys
   - Item findability metrics

5. **Conduct a follow-up analysis** after additional data is collected; if the effect remains around +1.5 to +2.0 points with improved precision, a broader rollout would be justified

### **Decision Framework**

| If... | Then... |
|-------|---------|
| Effect stays positive and becomes statistically significant (p < 0.05) | Recommend system-wide rollout |
| Effect remains positive but imprecise (p between 0.05–0.20) | Consider phased rollout with continued monitoring |
| Effect shrinks or turns negative | Re-evaluate the procedure before any expansion |

---

## **Technical Notes**

- Analysis used difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch)
- Results were robust to including branch fixed effects and controlling for staffing and visits indices
- The study had approximately 80% power to detect effects of 1.4 points or larger
