# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure **improved service scores by approximately 2.4 points** (about 5% relative to the average score of 50). This effect is **marginally statistically significant** (p≈0.095), with a 95% confidence interval ranging from roughly -0.4 to +5.2 points. While the evidence is not definitive at conventional significance levels, all analytical checks point in the same positive direction, and the practical improvement is meaningful. **We recommend proceeding with a phased rollout to all branches, with continued monitoring.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. The New Procedure Increased Service Scores**
- Branches that adopted the new procedure saw their average service score rise from **51.6 to 54.7** (+3.1 points)
- Branches that kept the old procedure saw a smaller increase from **49.5 to 50.2** (+0.7 points)
- After accounting for the underlying trend (via difference-in-differences), the **net effect of the new procedure is +2.4 points**

### **2. The Result Is Statistically Suggestive, Not Definitive**
- The p-value of 0.095 means there is about a 9.5% chance we'd see this result even if the procedure had no effect
- This falls just short of the conventional 5% threshold but exceeds the 10% threshold often used for policy decisions
- The confidence interval (-0.4 to +5.2) includes zero but is predominantly positive

### **3. The Analysis Is Methodologically Sound**
- **Parallel pre-trends confirmed**: Before week 15, treatment and control branches followed similar trajectories, validating our comparison approach
- **Robust to controls**: The effect persists when accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume
- **Robust to branch differences**: The effect holds even when controlling for fixed differences between individual branches
- **Event study supports causality**: The gap between groups widened after the switch, not before

### **4. The Effect Appears to Grow Over Time**
- In the first week after switching, the effect was about +3 points
- By weeks 2-4 after switching, the effect grew to 4-6 points
- This pattern suggests branches may need time to fully realize the benefits of the new procedure

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve phased rollout**: Begin extending the new procedure to remaining branches over the next 1-2 quarters, prioritizing branches with lower current service scores

2. **Continue monitoring**: Track service scores for 8-12 weeks after each branch switches to confirm the effect materializes as expected

3. **Document implementation**: Capture best practices from the 20 early-adopter branches to support training at remaining locations

4. **Re-evaluate in 6 months**: Conduct a follow-up analysis with more post-switch data to obtain more precise estimates of the long-term effect

5. **Consider complementary measures**: Since staffing and visitor volume showed weak relationships with service scores in this analysis, explore whether additional operational changes could amplify the procedure's benefits
