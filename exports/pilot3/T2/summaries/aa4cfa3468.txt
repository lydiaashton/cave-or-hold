# **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, roll out the new procedure to all branches.** The evidence shows the new procedure significantly improved service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by **1.94 points more** than branches that kept the old procedure—a **3.9% improvement** that is highly statistically significant (p < 0.001).

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. The New Procedure Improved Service Scores
- **Treatment effect**: +1.94 points on the service score index (95% CI: 1.39 to 2.48)
- **Statistical significance**: p < 0.001 (highly confident this is not due to chance)
- **Practical significance**: Approximately 3.9% improvement relative to the average score of 50

### 2. Strong Evidence from the Natural Experiment
The analysis used a difference-in-differences approach, comparing:
- **425 branches** that switched to the new procedure (treatment group)
- **284 branches** that kept the old procedure (control group)
- **Before period**: Weeks 6-14
- **After period**: Weeks 15-22

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 48.78 | 52.89 | +4.12 |
| Non-switched branches | 47.82 | 50.00 | +2.18 |
| **Difference** | | | **+1.94** |

### 3. Results Are Robust
- **Parallel trends confirmed**: Before the switch, both groups had similar trends in service scores (no pre-existing divergence)
- **Controls don't change results**: The effect holds even after accounting for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume
- **Broad-based improvement**: 87% of switched branches saw score improvements, compared to 73% of non-switched branches

### 4. No Obvious Downsides Detected
The analysis found no evidence that the new procedure negatively affected any subset of branches. The improvement was consistent across the treatment group.

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all 284 branches still using the old method.

2. **Monitor implementation** over the next 8-12 weeks to ensure the expected ~2-point service score improvement materializes across all branches.

4. **Consider further analysis** to identify whether certain branch characteristics (size, location, staffing patterns) predict larger or smaller gains, which could inform targeted support during rollout.

---

## **Methodology Note**

This analysis used a quasi-experimental difference-in-differences design with standard errors clustered at the branch level to account for repeated observations. The parallel trends assumption—the key requirement for this method—was verified by examining pre-treatment trends, which showed no significant divergence between groups before the switch occurred.
