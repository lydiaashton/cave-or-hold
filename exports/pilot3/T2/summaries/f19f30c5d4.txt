# **Analysis of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new procedure saw service scores increase by an estimated **1.86 points** (95% CI: 1.30 to 2.41) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents approximately a **4% improvement** over baseline performance. The effect is statistically significant (p < 0.001) and consistent across the 425 branches that adopted the new procedure.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect
- **Estimated impact:** +1.86 points on the service score index
- **Statistical significance:** Highly significant (p < 0.001)
- **Practical significance:** ~4% improvement relative to baseline scores averaging ~48 points

### 2. Study Design
- **709 total branches** analyzed over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- **425 treatment branches** switched to the new procedure at week 15
- **284 control branches** continued with the old procedure
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach with branch and week fixed effects, controlling for staffing and visit levels

### 3. Before/After Comparison
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 47.6 | 51.3 | +3.7 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 48.8 | 54.4 | +5.6 |
| **Net effect** | | | **+1.9** |

Both groups improved over time (likely due to seasonal factors or system-wide initiatives), but the treatment group improved significantly more.

### 4. Consistency Across Branches
- **92.9%** of treatment branches showed improvement after switching
- **84.5%** of control branches showed improvement in the same period
- The treatment effect remained robust after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### 5. Parallel Trends Validation
Analysis of pre-switch trends (weeks 6–14) shows no statistically significant differential trend between groups before the switch, supporting the validity of the comparison.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Recommend rollout:** The evidence supports expanding the new procedure to all remaining branches.

2. **Monitor implementation:** Track service scores during rollout to ensure the effect replicates at scale.

3. **Investigate mechanisms:** Consider analyzing which specific aspects of the new procedure (rotation vs. shelving) drive the improvement.

4. **Cost-benefit analysis:** Compare the 4% service score improvement against any additional costs of the new procedure to confirm net benefit.

5. **Long-term tracking:** Continue monitoring to ensure the effect persists beyond the initial 8-week post-switch window.

---

*Analysis based on 12,053 branch-week observations. Statistical models control for branch-specific characteristics, week-specific factors, staffing levels, and visitor volume.*
