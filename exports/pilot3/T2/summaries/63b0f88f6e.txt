# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and rotation procedure **improved service scores by approximately 2 points** (on a scale where the average branch scores about 50). This improvement is statistically significant and was not anticipated. The evidence supports rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect
- Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by **1.9 to 2.0 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure.
- This effect is **statistically significant** (p < 0.01), with a 95% confidence interval of **0.8 to 3.0 points**.
- The effect represents about **19% of one standard deviation** in service scores—a modest but meaningful improvement.

### 2. Study Design & Validity
- **150 branches** were analyzed over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22).
- **90 branches** switched to the new procedure at week 15; **60 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group).
- **Parallel trends assumption holds**: Before the switch (weeks 6–14), both groups followed similar trajectories (p = 0.23 for pre-trend difference). This supports the validity of the comparison.

### 3. Not Driven by Other Factors
- The improvement is **not explained by changes in staffing or visitor volume**. Neither the staffing index nor the visits index changed differently between the two groups after the switch.
- Controlling for these factors in the analysis does not meaningfully change the estimated treatment effect.

### 4. Consistency Over Time
- The treatment group outperformed the control group in **every post-switch week** (weeks 15–22).
- Weekly differences ranged from **1.6 to 3.3 points**, with an average of **2.5 points** after the switch (compared to only 0.6 points before).

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches. The data shows a clear, positive impact on service quality.

2. **Monitor implementation** during rollout to ensure the effect is replicated across all branches. Track service scores for at least 8 weeks post-implementation at each branch.

3. **Investigate the mechanism**: Since the improvement was unexpected, consider qualitative follow-up (e.g., staff surveys) to understand *why* the new procedure improved service scores. This could reveal additional best practices.

4. **Cost-benefit analysis**: If the new procedure has any associated costs (training, equipment, time), weigh them against the benefit of a ~2-point service score improvement across the system.

5. **Continue evaluation**: After full rollout, maintain ongoing monitoring to ensure the effect persists over time and does not diminish as the novelty wears off.

## **Technical Notes**

- Analysis used a **difference-in-differences** approach with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch).
- Pre-switch period: weeks 6–14; Post-switch period: weeks 15–22.
- Service score average across all observations: ~50.5; Standard deviation: ~10.3.
