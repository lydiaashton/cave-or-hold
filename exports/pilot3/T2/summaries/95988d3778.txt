# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure **improved service scores by approximately 1.75 points** (about 3.5% relative to the baseline average of 50). This improvement is **statistically significant** using standard robust methods, though the evidence is less conclusive when accounting for the small number of branches. The data supports proceeding with a broader rollout, but we recommend monitoring results closely during implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Treatment Effect**
Branches that adopted the new procedure showed a **1.75-point increase** in service scores compared to branches that kept the old procedure, after accounting for:
- Branch-specific characteristics (some branches consistently score higher or lower)
- Week-to-week fluctuations (common trends affecting all branches)
- Staffing and visitor volume changes

### **2. Statistical Confidence**
| Method | Effect Size | Standard Error | P-value | 95% Confidence Interval |
|--------|-------------|----------------|---------|------------------------|
| Robust SE | +1.75 | 0.79 | 0.026 | [0.21, 3.29] |
| Cluster-Robust SE* | +1.75 | 1.15 | 0.127 | [-0.50, 4.00] |

*Cluster-robust standard errors are more conservative and appropriate for panel data with a small number of branches.

### **3. Parallel Trends Assumption Validated**
A critical requirement for this analysis is that treatment and control branches were on similar trajectories before the switch. Our analysis confirms:
- **No significant pre-treatment differences** in trends between groups (p = 0.82)
- Treatment and control branches showed comparable score patterns in weeks 6-14

### **4. Visual Evidence**
The attached chart (`service_scores_trend.png`) shows:
- Treatment branches (green) pulled ahead of control branches (blue) after week 14
- The gap widened in the post-switch period, particularly in weeks 15-16 and 21-22
- Some week-to-week variability exists, but the overall pattern supports a positive treatment effect

### **5. Context**
- **34 branches total**: 20 adopted the new procedure, 14 kept the old
- **17 weeks of data**: Weeks 6-14 (before), Weeks 15-22 (after)
- **Baseline**: Average service score is approximately 50 points
- **Improvement magnitude**: 1.75 points represents roughly a 3.5% gain

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended Actions**
1. **Proceed with rollout** to remaining branches, but implement in phases to allow for monitoring and adjustment
2. **Establish ongoing monitoring** with monthly service score tracking for at least 6 months post-rollout
3. **Document implementation details** at each branch to identify any factors that might enhance or diminish the effect

### **Additional Analysis Opportunities**
1. **Heterogeneity analysis**: Examine whether the effect varies by branch size, location, or baseline performance
2. **Longer-term tracking**: Determine if the improvement is sustained or diminishes over time
3. **Cost-benefit analysis**: Compare the service score gains against any additional costs of the new procedure

### **Caveats**
- The cluster-robust analysis (more appropriate for this data structure) yields wider confidence intervals that include zero. This reflects uncertainty due to the limited number of branches (34), not necessarily absence of an effect.
- Service scores fluctuate week-to-week; continued monitoring will help distinguish sustained improvement from temporary variation.
