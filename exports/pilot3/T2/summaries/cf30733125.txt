# **Board Summary: Impact of New Branch Procedure on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure is associated with a **2.4-point increase in service scores** (approximately 5% improvement). This effect is **marginally statistically significant** (p=0.095), with a 95% confidence interval ranging from -0.4 to +5.2 points. The evidence suggests a positive impact, though we cannot rule out the possibility of no effect with high confidence. We recommend proceeding with a broader rollout while continuing to monitor results.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Study Design
- **34 branches** participated in the analysis over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15 (treatment group)
- **14 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- This "natural experiment" design allows us to isolate the effect of the new procedure

### 2. Main Results

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 49.5 | 50.2 | +0.7 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 51.6 | 54.7 | +3.1 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+2.4** |

- The treatment group improved by **3.1 points** after switching
- The control group improved by only **0.7 points** over the same period
- The **net effect of the new procedure is +2.4 points** on the service score index

### 3. Statistical Confidence
- **P-value: 0.095** — marginally significant (significant at 10% level, not quite at the conventional 5% level)
- **95% Confidence Interval: -0.4 to +5.2 points**
- Interpretation: We are 95% confident the true effect lies somewhere between a trivial negative effect and a meaningful 5-point gain. The most likely estimate is +2.4 points.

### 4. Validity Checks
- **Parallel trends assumption holds**: Before the switch, treatment and control branches showed similar week-to-week trends (p=0.95 for differential pre-trend test). This supports the credibility of our causal estimate.
- **Results are robust** to controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume, which did not materially change the estimated effect.

### 5. Post-Switch Pattern
The treatment-control gap widened in weeks 18–19 (6+ points) before narrowing slightly in the final weeks. This could suggest:
- A learning curve as branches mastered the new procedure
- Possible seasonal or external factors affecting later weeks
- Worth monitoring in future rollouts

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with cautious rollout**: The evidence points to a positive effect, and the cost of the procedure change appears low relative to the potential benefit.

2. **Extend the observation period**: Continue tracking service scores for another 8–12 weeks to:
   - Confirm the effect persists over time
   - Narrow the confidence interval with more data
   - Observe whether the effect stabilizes or grows

3. **Monitor implementation fidelity**: Ensure branches adopting the new procedure are following it consistently. Variation in implementation could be masking a larger true effect.

4. **Consider a phased rollout**: If resources allow, roll out to remaining branches in waves and continue using the same difference-in-differences approach to refine the effect estimate.

5. **Track secondary outcomes**: Monitor whether the service score improvement translates to tangible outcomes like patron satisfaction, circulation, or staff efficiency.
