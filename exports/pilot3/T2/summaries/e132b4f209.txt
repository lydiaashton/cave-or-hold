# **Analysis of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

Contrary to initial expectations, the new stock rotation and shelving procedure **significantly improved service scores**. Branches that adopted the new procedure saw an average increase of **1.65 points** (approximately **3.4%**) in their service scores compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This result is statistically significant (p < 0.01) and holds across multiple analytical approaches.

**Recommendation:** The evidence supports rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Study Design
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** switched to the new procedure at week 15; **60 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis used a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in service scores before and after the switch between treated and control branches

### 2. Main Results

| Group | Before Switch (Weeks 6–14) | After Switch (Weeks 15–22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------------|---------------------------|--------|
| New Procedure (90 branches) | 48.49 | 49.58 | **+1.09** |
| Old Procedure (60 branches) | 49.15 | 48.59 | **–0.56** |
| **Difference (Treatment Effect)** | | | **+1.65** |

### 3. Statistical Significance
- **p-value: 0.004** (highly significant; less than 1% chance this result occurred by random variation)
- **95% Confidence Interval:** 0.54 to 2.77 points improvement
- Results remain significant when:
  - Controlling for staffing levels and visitor traffic
  - Using only the 5 weeks immediately before and after the switch (effect: +2.03 points, p = 0.003)
  - Analyzing branch-level changes directly (p = 0.004)

### 4. Practical Significance
- The average service score across all branches is approximately **49 points**
- A 1.65-point improvement represents a **3.4% gain** in service quality
- Given that service scores are a key performance metric for the cooperative, this is a meaningful improvement

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new stock rotation and shelving procedure to all remaining branches.

2. **Monitor implementation** over the next 8–12 weeks to confirm the effect persists at scale and identify any branches that may need additional support during the transition.

3. **Document the new procedure** in detail, capturing the specific practices that drove the improvement, to ensure consistent implementation across all branches.

4. **Consider cost-benefit analysis** if the new procedure involves additional labor or resource costs, though the service score improvement suggests a positive return.

5. **Share findings** with branch managers to build support for the change and encourage adoption of best practices.

## **Technical Notes**

- Analysis used cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch) to account for repeated observations within branches
- The parallel trends assumption appears reasonable when examining the 5 weeks immediately preceding the switch
- Service score is an index where higher values indicate better service; the cooperative average is approximately 50
