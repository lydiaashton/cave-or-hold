## **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

### **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure is associated with a **modest improvement in service scores** (+1.6 points, or approximately 3% above baseline). However, this effect is **not statistically significant at conventional levels** (p=0.084), meaning we cannot rule out that the observed improvement occurred by chance. The evidence is **promising but not conclusive**. We recommend a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. Study Design**
- Analyzed 34 branches over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- 20 branches adopted the new procedure starting week 15; 14 branches continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Used difference-in-differences analysis with branch fixed effects to isolate the procedure's impact

**2. Service Score Impact**
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 47.6 | 48.8 | +1.2 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 50.2 | 52.8 | +2.6 |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | | | **+1.6** |

- **Estimated effect:** +1.62 points on the service score index
- **95% Confidence Interval:** -0.22 to +3.45 points
- **Interpretation:** The true effect could range from slightly negative to moderately positive

**3. Statistical Confidence**
- P-value of 0.084 indicates **marginal significance** (significant at the 10% level, but not at the 5% level)
- The confidence interval includes zero, so we cannot confidently conclude the procedure causes improvement
- Pre-trend analysis confirms the treatment and control groups were following similar trajectories before the switch, supporting the validity of our comparison

**4. Additional Insights**
- **Staffing levels** and **visit volumes** are positively associated with service scores, suggesting these factors also influence performance
- Branches that switched started with slightly higher baseline scores (50.2 vs. 47.6), though our analysis accounts for this

### **Next Steps**

1. **Continue the pilot** for an additional 8–12 weeks to gather more data and improve statistical precision

3. **Consider a staggered rollout** to remaining branches, treating it as a phased implementation with ongoing evaluation rather than a binary go/no-go decision
