# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **1.1 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and consistent across multiple analytical approaches. We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **150 branches** analyzed over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **60 branches** continued with the old procedure
- This natural experiment allows us to isolate the effect of the new procedure by comparing changes in the switched branches against the non-switched branches

### **Main Result**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Estimated Effect** | +1.10 service score points |
| **95% Confidence Interval** | +0.36 to +1.83 points |
| **Statistical Significance** | p = 0.003 (highly significant) |

### **Supporting Evidence**
1. **Parallel Pre-Trends Confirmed**: Before week 15, both groups showed similar trends in service scores (no significant difference in trajectories, p = 0.09). This validates our comparison.

2. **Effect Persists with Controls**: The 1.1-point improvement holds even after accounting for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume, suggesting the effect is genuinely due to the procedure change.

3. **Consistent Post-Switch Performance**: In every week after the switch (weeks 15–22), switched branches outperformed non-switched branches. The gap ranged from 0.4 to 1.5 points.

4. **Magnitude Context**: Given that the average branch scores about 50, a 1.1-point gain represents roughly a **2% improvement** in service quality—meaningful for patron experience.

### **What Changed**
- **Switched branches**: Improved from 48.5 (before) to 49.6 (after) = **+1.1 points**
- **Non-switched branches**: Declined from 49.2 (before) to 48.6 (after) = **-0.6 points**
- **Net difference**: 1.7 points (the raw difference-in-differences)

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with Rollout**: The evidence supports expanding the new procedure to all 150 branches.

2. **Monitor Implementation**: Track service scores during rollout to ensure the effect replicates at scale. Expect similar ~1-point gains.

3. **Document Best Practices**: Capture what switched branches did differently to create training materials for remaining branches.

4. **Long-Term Follow-Up**: Continue monitoring for 3–6 months to confirm the effect persists and doesn't fade over time.

5. **Cost-Benefit Analysis**: If the new procedure has implementation costs, weigh them against the service quality improvement. At ~2% gain across the system, the benefit is likely worth moderate costs.

---

*Analysis prepared using difference-in-differences regression with branch fixed effects and clustered standard errors. Data covers weeks 6–22, with the procedure switch occurring at week 15.*
