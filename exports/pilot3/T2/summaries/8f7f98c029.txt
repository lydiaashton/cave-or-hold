# **Board Summary: New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure Evaluation**

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure is associated with a **modest improvement in service scores** (approximately +2.7 points on a ~50-point scale), but the evidence is **not statistically conclusive** at conventional confidence levels. The effect appears to emerge gradually after implementation, with stronger signals in weeks 2 and 6 post-switch. We recommend a **cautious expansion** with continued monitoring rather than an immediate system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** analyzed over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure; **14 branches** kept the old procedure (control group)
- Switch occurred at **week 15**
- Analysis uses difference-in-differences methodology with cluster-robust standard errors

### **Main Results**

| Metric | Control Group | Treatment Group |
|--------|---------------|-----------------|
| Pre-switch average | 50.84 | 52.85 |
| Post-switch average | 51.63 | 56.09 |
| Change | +0.78 | +3.23 |

**Estimated Treatment Effect: +2.67 points** (95% CI: -0.39 to +5.74, p=0.087)

### **Supporting Evidence**

1. **Parallel Trends Validated**: Pre-switch trends between treatment and control groups show no significant divergence (p=0.84), supporting the validity of our comparison.

2. **Event Study Pattern**: 
   - All pre-switch weeks show small, statistically insignificant differences
   - Post-switch weeks show consistently positive effects
   - Weeks 2 and 6 post-switch show statistically significant improvements (p<0.05)
   - Effect appears to build gradually rather than appearing immediately

3. **Practical Significance**: A 2.7-point increase represents approximately **5% improvement** relative to the average branch score of 50, which could meaningfully impact patron experience.

4. **Controls**: The effect persists after accounting for staffing levels and visit volumes, suggesting the procedure itself—not external factors—drives the improvement.

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended Actions**

1. **Pilot Expansion**: Roll out the new procedure to an additional 5–10 branches over the next quarter to gather more data and increase statistical power.

2. **Extended Monitoring**: Continue tracking service scores for at least 8–12 more weeks to determine if effects stabilize, grow, or diminish over time.

3. **Cost-Benefit Analysis**: Quantify implementation costs (training, equipment, staff time) against the service score improvement to assess ROI before full rollout.

4. **Qualitative Feedback**: Survey branch staff and patrons at switched branches to understand *why* scores improved and identify any unintended consequences.

5. **Decision Timeline**: Revisit the board in 3 months with expanded data to make a final system-wide rollout decision.

### **Risk Considerations**

- **Statistical uncertainty**: The confidence interval includes zero, meaning we cannot rule out no effect
- **Implementation variability**: Effects may differ across branches depending on local conditions
- **Long-term sustainability**: Unknown whether improvements persist beyond the initial 8-week post-switch period

---

## **Conclusion**

The new procedure shows **promising but not definitive** evidence of improved service scores. Given the positive direction of effects, validated study design, and meaningful potential impact on patron experience, we recommend proceeding with **careful expansion and continued evaluation** rather than either immediate full rollout or abandonment of the initiative.
