# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

## **New Shelving & Stock Rotation Procedure: Impact on Library Service Scores**

---

### **BOTTOM LINE**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new shelving and stock rotation procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **2.3 points** (approximately **4.7% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and has grown over time since implementation. We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

### **KEY FINDINGS**

**1. Study Design & Data**
- Analyzed 709 branches over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22 of the year)
- 425 branches switched to the new procedure in week 15; 284 branches continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Used a difference-in-differences approach to isolate the effect of the new procedure

**2. Main Result**
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched Branches | 50.91 | 52.91 | +2.00 |
| Control Branches | 48.88 | 48.59 | -0.29 |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | | | **+2.29** |

- **Treatment effect: +2.30 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.7 to 2.9 points)
- **Statistical significance: p < 0.001** (highly significant)
- Effect remains significant after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

**3. Effect Over Time**
- The improvement was not immediate but grew over the 8 weeks following implementation
- Early weeks showed modest gains; later weeks showed stronger effects
- This pattern suggests staff needed time to master the new procedure

**4. Important Context**
- Switched branches had slightly higher service scores even before the change (about 2 points higher on average)
- However, the *acceleration* in scores after week 15 is attributable to the new procedure
- Control branch scores remained essentially flat throughout the study period

---

### **NEXT STEPS**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new shelving and stock rotation procedure to all 709 branches

2. **Plan implementation support:**
   - Allow 4–6 weeks for staff training and adjustment at each branch
   - Expect full benefits to materialize after this learning period

3. **Monitor outcomes:**
   - Continue tracking service scores post-rollout
   - Target: maintain or exceed the 2.3-point improvement observed in this study

4. **Consider additional analysis:**
   - Investigate whether certain branch types (size, location, traffic) benefit more or less
   - Examine whether staffing or visitor volume interacts with the procedure's effectiveness
