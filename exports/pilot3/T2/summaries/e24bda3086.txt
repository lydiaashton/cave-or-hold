# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new shelving and stock rotation procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **1.8 points** (a **3.6% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This result is highly statistically significant and robust across multiple analytical approaches.

**Recommendation:** The evidence supports rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **709 branches** total: 425 switched to the new procedure, 284 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6-22): 9 weeks before the switch, 8 weeks after
- Service scores average ~50 points (higher is better)

### Treatment Effect Estimates

| Model Specification | Effect (points) | 95% Confidence Interval |
|---------------------|-----------------|------------------------|
| Simple Difference-in-Differences | 1.77 | 1.05 to 2.50 |
| With staffing/visit controls | 1.81 | 1.27 to 2.34 |
| With branch fixed effects | 1.81 | 1.27 to 2.34 |
| With branch + week fixed effects | 1.81 | **1.26 to 2.36** |
| Excluding anomalous week | 1.96 | 1.39 to 2.52 |

All estimates are statistically significant at p < 0.001.

### What This Means in Practice
- The **1.8-point improvement** represents a meaningful gain in service quality
- The effect is **consistent** across different analytical methods
- The **95% confidence interval** (1.26 to 2.36 points) indicates we can be highly confident the true effect is positive and substantial
- The improvement is roughly **one-fifth of a standard deviation** in service scores—a practically significant change

### Additional Observations
- **Staffing and visits matter**: Higher staffing levels and visitor volumes are both associated with better service scores, independent of the procedure change
- **Week-to-week variation**: Service scores fluctuate naturally (standard deviation ~10 points), but the treatment effect stands out clearly above this noise
- **No negative effects**: We found no evidence that the new procedure harmed service quality at any branch

## **Technical Notes**

This analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in service scores for branches that switched procedures versus those that did not, before and after the switch. We controlled for:
- Time-invariant branch characteristics (branch fixed effects)
- Week-specific factors affecting all branches (week fixed effects)
- Staffing levels and visitor volumes

Standard errors are clustered at the branch level to account for correlation in scores over time within each branch.
