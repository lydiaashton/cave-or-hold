# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.0 points** (95% confidence interval: 0.8 to 3.2 points) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents roughly a **4% improvement** over baseline scores. The result is statistically significant (p=0.001) and holds after accounting for staffing levels and visitor volumes.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect
- **Difference-in-differences estimate: +2.01 points** (p=0.001)
- Switched branches improved by an average of 0.76 points from before to after
- Control branches (old procedure) declined by an average of 1.25 points over the same period
- The net effect of the new procedure is the difference: **+2.01 points**

### 2. Branch-Level Impact
| Metric | Switched Branches | Control Branches |
|--------|------------------|------------------|
| Number of branches | 90 | 60 |
| % with score improvement | 58.9% | 35.0% |
| Average score change | +0.76 | -1.25 |

### 3. Statistical Confidence
- **95% confidence interval: [0.81, 3.22]** — we can be confident the true effect is positive
- **Effect size (Cohen's d): 0.54** — a moderate, practically meaningful improvement
- Results are robust when controlling for weekly staffing and visitor volume fluctuations

### 4. Parallel Trends Check
Before the switch (weeks 6-14), both groups showed similar week-to-week patterns, supporting the validity of the comparison. The treatment group scored slightly lower on average before the switch, making the post-switch improvement even more notable.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Recommend rollout** — The evidence supports extending the new procedure to all branches. The 2-point service score gain is both statistically significant and operationally meaningful.

2. **Monitor implementation** — Track service scores during rollout to ensure the effect generalizes across all branches. About 41% of switched branches did not see improvement, so identify and address branch-specific barriers.

3. **Investigate variation** — Analyze which branch characteristics (size, location, staffing) predict stronger or weaker responses to the new procedure to optimize implementation.

4. **Cost-benefit analysis** — Quantify the operational costs of the new procedure against the service score gains to confirm net benefit before full rollout.

---

*Analysis based on 17 weeks of data (weeks 6-22) from 150 branches. The switch occurred at week 15. Service scores average ~50 points, with higher scores indicating better service.*
