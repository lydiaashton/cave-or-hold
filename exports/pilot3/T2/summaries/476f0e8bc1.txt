# Library Cooperative Branch Procedure Change: Impact Analysis

## Bottom Line

**Yes, the new shelving and stock rotation procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **1.9 points** (about 4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and robust across multiple analytical approaches. We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## Key Findings

### 1. Treatment Effect Estimate
- **Average improvement: +1.9 points** on the service score index (95% confidence interval: 1.4 to 2.4 points)
- Given that the average branch scores about 50 points, this represents roughly a **4% improvement** in service quality
- The effect is **highly statistically significant** (p < 0.001)

### 2. What the Data Shows

| Group | Before Switch (Weeks 6-14) | After Switch (Weeks 15-22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------------|---------------------------|--------|
| Switched Branches | 51.35 | 51.70 | +0.35 |
| Control Branches | 51.15 | 49.65 | -1.50 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+1.85** |

The switched branches maintained their service scores after the change, while control branches experienced a notable decline. This suggests the new procedure may have helped branches **avoid deterioration** in service quality during this period.

### 3. Robustness Checks
The finding holds across multiple analytical approaches:
- **Basic DiD model:** +1.85 points (p < 0.001)
- **With staffing and visits controls:** +1.91 points (p < 0.001)
- **Branch fixed effects model:** +1.90 points (p < 0.001)

All models account for branch-specific characteristics and use cluster-robust standard errors.

### 4. Effect Over Time
The treatment effect was not uniform across all post-switch weeks:
- **Week 15:** Large initial jump (+6.5 points, possibly implementation enthusiasm)
- **Weeks 16-18:** Sustained positive effect (+2 to +5 points)
- **Weeks 19-20:** Effect diminished (not statistically significant)
- **Weeks 21-22:** Mixed results

This pattern suggests the benefit may require ongoing attention to maintain, or there could be seasonal factors at play.

## Methodology Notes

This analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing branches that adopted the new procedure (treatment group) to those that did not (control group), before and after the switch in week 15. The design controls for:
- Time-invariant branch characteristics (via branch fixed effects)
- Overall time trends (via after_switch indicator)
- Weekly staffing and visitor volume fluctuations (via control variables)

Standard errors are clustered at the branch level to account for correlation within branches over time.
