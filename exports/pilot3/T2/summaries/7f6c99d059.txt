# **New Shelving Procedure: Impact on Service Scores**
## Analysis for the Board of Directors

---

### **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure is associated with a statistically significant improvement in service scores of approximately 2.3 points (about 4.6% relative to the baseline of ~50).** The evidence supports rolling out the new procedure to all branches, with continued monitoring to confirm the effect persists.

---

### **KEY FINDINGS**

**1. Treatment Effect**
- Branches that switched to the new procedure saw service scores **increase by 2.0 points** after the switch (from 50.9 to 52.9)
- Control branches (keeping the old procedure) saw scores **decline slightly by 0.3 points** (from 48.9 to 48.6)
- **Difference-in-differences estimate: +2.29 points** (p < 0.001)
- **95% confidence interval: 1.69 to 2.89 points**

**2. Study Design & Sample**
- 709 branches total: 425 adopted the new procedure, 284 kept the old
- 17 weeks of data per branch (weeks 6–22)
- Switch occurred at week 15, providing 9 weeks of "before" and 8 weeks of "after" data
- Total of 12,053 branch-week observations

**3. Specificity of Effect**
- No significant changes were detected in staffing or visits indices between groups
- This suggests the improvement is specific to service quality, not driven by changes in staffing levels or customer traffic

**4. Important Caveat**
- Switched branches already had somewhat higher service scores before the procedure change (average gap of ~2 points)
- The gap between groups was widening even before the intervention, suggesting possible selection bias (better-performing branches may have been chosen to pilot the new procedure first)
- The true causal effect may be somewhat smaller than the 2.3-point estimate, but the direction of the effect is clear

---

### **NEXT STEPS**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new shelving and rotation procedure to all 709 branches

2. **Establish ongoing monitoring** to track service scores after full implementation:
   - Continue weekly service score tracking
   - Compare post-rollout scores to the baseline established in this study
   - Watch for any decay in the effect over time

3. **Document implementation details** from the pilot branches to ensure consistent adoption:
   - Training materials used
   - Timeline for transition
   - Any branch-specific adaptations

4. **Consider a follow-up evaluation** in 6–12 months to confirm the effect persists at scale and identify any branches that may need additional support
