# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure is associated with a **statistically significant improvement in service scores of approximately 1.9 points** (on a scale where the average branch scores about 50). This represents roughly a **4% improvement** over baseline scores. The effect appears to strengthen over time, becoming clearly evident 6-8 weeks after implementation. We recommend proceeding with system-wide rollout, with continued monitoring to confirm these results.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **709 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6-22)
- **425 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15 (treatment group)
- **284 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach to isolate the procedure's effect

### Main Results

| Group | Before Switch (Weeks 6-14) | After Switch (Weeks 15-22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------------|---------------------------|--------|
| Control (Old Procedure) | 47.82 | 50.00 | +2.18 |
| Treatment (New Procedure) | 48.78 | 52.89 | +4.12 |
| **Difference** | | | **+1.94** |

- **Estimated effect: +1.94 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.4 to 2.5 points)
- **Statistical significance: p < 0.001** (highly significant)
- Effect remains robust after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### Effect Over Time

The impact of the new procedure was not immediate:
- **Weeks 15-19:** No statistically significant difference from control branches
- **Weeks 20-22:** Clear positive effects emerge (+1.1 to +1.4 points, statistically significant)

This pattern suggests branches needed **4-6 weeks to fully implement** the new procedure and realize its benefits.

### Important Caveat

Treatment branches showed slightly faster improvement than control branches even *before* the switch was implemented. This suggests the branches selected for early adoption may have been on a more positive trajectory already. Our analysis accounts for this statistically, but it means the true effect could be somewhat smaller than our estimate.

## **Next Steps**

### Recommended Actions

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new procedure
   - The evidence supports a positive effect on service quality
   - The ~2 point improvement is meaningful for patron experience

2. **Implement with training and support**
   - Allow 4-6 weeks for branches to fully adapt before expecting full benefits
   - Provide implementation guidance based on early-adopter experience

3. **Continue monitoring**
   - Track service scores for all branches post-rollout
   - Compare actual outcomes to these projections
   - Identify branches that struggle with implementation for targeted support

4. **Document implementation learnings**
   - Gather qualitative feedback from the 425 early-adopter branches
   - Identify best practices and common challenges
   - Incorporate into rollout training materials

### Further Analysis Opportunities

If the board desires additional evidence before full rollout:
- Extend the study period to capture longer-term effects
- Conduct branch-level interviews to understand implementation factors
