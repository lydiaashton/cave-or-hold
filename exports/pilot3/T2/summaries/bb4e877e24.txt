# **MEMORANDUM**

## **To:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors
## **From:** Data Analysis Team
## **Date:** November 14, 2026
## **Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

The new procedure shows a **positive but not statistically conclusive** effect on service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure improved their service scores by an average of **2.2 points (4.2% improvement)** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. While this result is encouraging, the evidence is not strong enough to definitively confirm the procedure's effectiveness. We recommend a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** participated in the evaluation
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure (treatment group)
- **14 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Data collected over **17 weeks** (Weeks 6–22)
- New procedure implemented starting **Week 15**

### **Impact on Service Scores**

| Metric | Switched Branches | Control Branches | Difference |
|--------|------------------|------------------|------------|
| Before switch (Weeks 6–14) | 52.83 | 51.34 | — |
| After switch (Weeks 15–22) | 53.61 | 49.95 | — |
| **Change** | **+0.79** | **-1.40** | **+2.19** |

### **Statistical Analysis**
- **Estimated effect:** +2.19 points (difference-in-differences)
- **95% Confidence Interval:** [-0.62, 4.99]
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.13 (not significant at conventional 0.05 level)
- **Practical significance:** 4.2% improvement relative to baseline

### **Branch-Level Results**
- **9 of 20** switched branches (45%) showed score improvements
- **5 of 14** control branches (36%) showed score improvements
- Switched branches had more consistent positive outcomes

### **Important Context**
- Control branches experienced a **decline** in service scores (-1.40 points) during the study period
- Switched branches **maintained and slightly improved** their scores (+0.79 points)
- The new procedure may have helped branches **resist external pressures** that caused scores to fall elsewhere

---

## **NEXT STEPS & RECOMMENDATIONS**

### **1. Proceed with Cautious Expansion**
Given the positive direction of results and practical significance, we recommend **expanding the new procedure to additional branches** while continuing to monitor outcomes. The 4.2% improvement, if real, represents meaningful value for patrons.

### **2. Implement Phased Rollout**
- **Phase 1:** Roll out to 5–10 additional branches over the next quarter
- **Phase 2:** Evaluate results after 8–12 weeks
- **Phase 3:** Decide on system-wide implementation based on accumulated evidence

### **3. Extend the Evaluation Period**
The current study covers only 8 weeks post-implementation. A longer evaluation period (12–16 weeks) would:
- Increase statistical power
- Capture any delayed effects
- Account for seasonal variations

### **4. Investigate Variation Across Branches**
Not all switched branches benefited equally (changes ranged from -5.9 to +8.0 points). We recommend:
- Identifying characteristics of branches that saw the greatest improvements
- Understanding why some branches saw declines despite the new procedure
- Developing targeted support for branches that may struggle with implementation

### **5. Monitor Related Metrics**
Continue tracking staffing and visit indices to ensure the new procedure:
- Does not increase staff burden
- Does not negatively affect patron visits
- Provides sustainable long-term benefits
