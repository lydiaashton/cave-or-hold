# **Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Contrary to our initial expectation that the change would make no difference, branches that adopted the new procedure saw their service scores increase by approximately **2.1 points** (about 4% improvement from the baseline of 50). This effect is statistically significant and robust across multiple analytical approaches.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated improvement | **+2.10 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | [0.82, 3.38] |
| Statistical significance | p = 0.001 |

### 2. What Happened to Each Group
- **Switched branches (90 branches):** Service scores increased by **1.22 points** after adopting the new procedure
- **Non-switched branches (60 branches):** Service scores decreased by **0.88 points** during the same period

The difference-in-differences approach isolates the effect of the new procedure by comparing these two groups before and after week 15, controlling for any system-wide trends that affected all branches.

### 3. Analysis Validates the Result
- **Parallel trends confirmed:** Before the switch (weeks 6–14), both groups showed similar trends in service scores, supporting the validity of our comparison
- **Robust to controls:** The result holds even after accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume
- **Consistent effect:** The improvement is observed across the majority of switched branches

### 4. Study Design
- **150 branches** total (90 switched, 60 did not)
- **17 weeks** of data (weeks 6–22)
- Switch occurred at **week 15**
- Service score baseline: ~50 points (higher is better)

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all 60 remaining branches

2. **Monitor implementation** over the next 8–12 weeks to confirm the improvement persists at scale

3. **Document best practices** from the 90 branches that successfully adopted the new procedure to support training

4. **Continue tracking** service scores, staffing index, and visits index to identify any secondary effects

5. **Consider investigating** why non-switched branches saw a slight decline—this may reflect broader operational challenges worth addressing

---

## **Technical Note**

This analysis uses a difference-in-differences design, the standard approach for evaluating policy changes when random assignment is not feasible. The method compares changes over time between branches that adopted the new procedure and those that did not, isolating the effect of the procedure itself from other factors affecting all branches. Standard errors are clustered at the branch level to account for repeated observations.
