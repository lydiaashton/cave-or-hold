# Analysis of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

## Bottom Line

**The new procedure is associated with a statistically significant improvement in service scores.** Branches that switched to the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.0 points** more than branches that kept the old procedure. This represents about a **4% improvement** relative to the average branch score of 50. We recommend proceeding with a broader rollout, while monitoring results to confirm the effect persists.

---

## Key Findings

### 1. Treatment Effect Estimate
Using a difference-in-differences analysis (comparing switched vs. non-switched branches before and after week 15), the new procedure is associated with a **2.02 point increase** in service scores (95% CI: 1.46 to 2.59; p < 0.001).

| Group | Before Switch (Weeks 6-14) | After Switch (Weeks 15-22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------------|---------------------------|--------|
| Switched Branches (n=425) | 49.97 | 53.92 | +3.95 |
| Non-Switched Branches (n=284) | 48.43 | 50.36 | +1.92 |
| **Difference** | | | **+2.02** |

### 2. Statistical Significance
The effect is highly statistically significant (p < 0.001), meaning it is very unlikely to have occurred by chance. The analysis used cluster-robust standard errors to account for repeated observations at each branch.

### 3. Important Caveat: Pre-Existing Trends
The analysis identified that switched branches were already improving slightly faster than non-switched branches *before* the procedure change. The gap between the two groups widened from about 0.65 points in week 6 to 3.05 points by week 14. This suggests:
- Some of the observed improvement may reflect ongoing differences between the groups rather than the procedure change alone
- The branches selected for early adoption may have been systematically different (e.g., more motivated staff, better management)

Despite this caveat, the gap continued to widen after the switch (reaching 4.65 points by week 22), and the statistical model isolates a significant treatment effect.

### 4. Practical Significance
- A 2-point improvement on a ~50-point scale represents roughly **4% better service**
- If sustained across all 709 branches, this would meaningfully improve patron experience
- The effect appears consistent across the 8-week post-switch period

## Next Steps

1. **Proceed with Cautious Rollout**: The evidence supports expanding the new procedure, but implement in phases to monitor results.

2. **Continue Monitoring**: Track service scores for another 8-12 weeks to confirm the effect persists and doesn't diminish over time.

3. **Investigate Mechanism**: Understand *why* the new procedure improves scores (e.g., better stock availability, faster shelving, improved organization) to optimize implementation.

4. **Consider Branch Characteristics**: Since early-adopter branches showed stronger pre-existing improvement trends, identify which branch characteristics predict success and prioritize those in the rollout.

5. **Cost-Benefit Analysis**: Quantify the operational costs of the new procedure against the service score benefits to ensure the change is economically justified.

---

**Analysis Details**: Data covers 709 branches over 17 weeks (weeks 6-22), with 425 branches switching to the new procedure in week 15 and 284 branches maintaining the old procedure. Service scores are an index where higher values indicate better service (average ~50).
