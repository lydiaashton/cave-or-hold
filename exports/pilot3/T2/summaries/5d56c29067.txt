# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure is associated with a **statistically significant improvement in service scores**. Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2 points (4%)** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This improvement is consistent across multiple analytical approaches and grows over time. We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout, while continuing to monitor outcomes.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Treatment Effect**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated improvement | +2.05 points |
| Percentage improvement | ~4% |
| 95% Confidence Interval | [1.48, 2.62] |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 |

### **2. Study Design**
- **709 branches** total: 425 adopted the new procedure, 284 kept the old
- **17 weeks** of data (weeks 6–22)
- Switch occurred at **week 15**
- Analysis uses difference-in-differences methodology with branch-level controls

### **3. Service Score Changes**
| Group | Before Switch (weeks 6–14) | After Switch (weeks 15–22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------------|---------------------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 49.97 | 53.92 | +3.95 |
| Non-switched branches | 48.43 | 50.36 | +1.92 |
| **Net effect (DiD)** | | | **+2.02** |

### **4. Effect Grows Over Time**
- Early post-switch period (weeks 15–18): **+1.66 points**
- Late post-switch period (weeks 19–22): **+2.44 points**

This pattern suggests branches may need time to fully realize the benefits of the new procedure.

### **5. Robustness Checks**
- Effect remains significant after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume
- Effect remains significant with branch fixed effects (controlling for time-invariant branch characteristics)
- Standard errors clustered at the branch level to account for repeated observations

---

## **Caveats and Limitations**

1. **Pre-existing differences**: Branches that switched already had slightly higher service scores before the change (about 1.5 points higher on average). The analysis accounts for this, but it cannot rule out all unobserved differences.

2. **Underlying trends**: Both groups showed improving scores over time. The analysis isolates the *additional* improvement for switched branches, but external factors affecting all branches cannot be fully separated.

3. **Short observation window**: We have only 8 weeks of post-switch data. Longer-term effects (positive or negative) remain unknown.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with rollout**: The evidence supports expanding the new procedure to all branches, with an expected service score improvement of approximately 2 points.

2. **Monitor implementation**: Track service scores for newly converted branches to ensure they achieve similar gains.

3. **Investigate mechanisms**: The improvement may be driven by better staffing utilization or visitor experience. Further analysis could identify which aspects of the new procedure matter most.

4. **Long-term follow-up**: Continue collecting data to assess whether the effect persists, grows, or diminishes over time.

5. **Cost-benefit analysis**: If the new procedure has implementation costs, compare them against the value of a 4% service score improvement.

---

*Analysis prepared using difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors. Data covers weeks 6–22 with the procedure switch occurring at week 15.*
