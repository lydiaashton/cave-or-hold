# **Board Summary: Impact of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new stock rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.0 points** (a **4% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and consistent across multiple analytical approaches. We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout, with continued monitoring.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. The New Procedure Delivered Measurable Improvement**

| Group | Pre-Switch Score | Post-Switch Score | Change |
|-------|-----------------|-------------------|--------|
| Switched Branches | 49.97 | 53.92 | +3.95 |
| Non-Switched Branches | 48.43 | 50.36 | +1.92 |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | | | **+2.02** |

- The **2.02-point improvement** is the estimated causal effect of the new procedure
- 95% confidence interval: **1.45 to 2.59 points**
- This represents approximately a **4% improvement** in service quality

### **2. Results Are Statistically Robust**

The finding holds across multiple specifications:
- **Basic model**: +2.02 points (p < 0.001)
- **With staffing/visits controls**: +2.05 points (p < 0.001)
- **Event study average**: +1.94 points post-switch

The effect remains significant after controlling for:
- Staffing levels (which independently affect scores)
- Visit volumes (which independently affect scores)
- Branch-specific characteristics

### **3. Effect Appeared Immediately After Implementation**

Service scores for switched branches showed improvement beginning in week 15 (the switch week), with the gap between switched and non-switched branches widening from an average of 1.5 points pre-switch to 3.6 points post-switch.

### **4. Important Context and Limitations**

- **Not a randomized trial**: Branches self-selected (or were selected) into the new procedure, which introduces some uncertainty
- **Pre-existing differences**: Switched branches had slightly higher scores even before the change (~1.5 points), though our analysis accounts for this
- **Weekly variation**: The effect fluctuates week-to-week, likely reflecting normal operational variability
- **Sample**: 709 branches, 17 weeks of data (weeks 6–22), with ~60% adopting the new procedure

## **Appendix: Methodology Notes**

This analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing the change in service scores for branches that adopted the new procedure against those that did not, before and after week 15. Standard errors are clustered at the branch level to account for repeated observations. The parallel trends assumption is reasonably satisfied, though not perfectly—switched branches had a modest pre-existing advantage that our models control for.
