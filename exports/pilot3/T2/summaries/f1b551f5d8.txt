# **Analysis of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **1.8 points** (approximately **3.6% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and consistent across all weeks after the switch. We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all remaining branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. **The New Procedure Had a Positive Effect**
- Switched branches improved by **1.8 points** on the service score index relative to non-switched branches
- 95% confidence interval: **1.3 to 2.3 points**
- The result is highly statistically significant (p < 0.001)
- This represents a meaningful **3.6% improvement** over the average service score of ~50

### 2. **The Effect Was Consistent Over Time**
- Positive effects appeared immediately in week 15 (when the switch occurred) and persisted through week 22
- Weekly treatment effects ranged from +1.5 to +2.6 points after the switch
- All post-switch weeks showed positive effects; most were statistically significant

### 3. **The Comparison Is Valid**
- Before the switch (weeks 6–14), switched and non-switched branches followed similar trends
- There was no systematic divergence between groups prior to the intervention
- Switching was not correlated with baseline branch characteristics (service scores, staffing, or visit levels), suggesting the assignment was effectively random

### 4. **Results Hold After Accounting for Other Factors**
- The positive effect remains after controlling for weekly staffing levels and visitor volumes
- Both staffing and visits independently affect service scores (as expected), but the procedure effect is separate from these factors

### 5. **Scale of the Pilot**
- **425 branches** (60% of all 709 branches) adopted the new procedure
- **284 branches** continued with the old procedure, providing a valid control group
- Data covers **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with System-Wide Rollout** — The evidence strongly supports extending the new procedure to all remaining 284 branches.

2. **Monitor Implementation** — Track service scores at newly converted branches to ensure they achieve similar gains.

3. **Document Best Practices** — Investigate whether certain branches achieved larger improvements and identify any implementation factors that contributed to success.

4. **Consider Cost-Benefit** — If the new procedure requires additional resources (training, labor, equipment), weigh the 3.6% service score improvement against those costs.

5. **Long-Term Follow-Up** — Continue monitoring to confirm the effect persists beyond the initial 8-week post-switch period.
