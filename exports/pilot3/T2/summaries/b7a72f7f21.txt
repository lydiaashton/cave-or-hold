# **Analysis of New Shelving Procedure Impact on Library Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new shelving and stock rotation procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **1.65 points** (approximately 3.3% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant (p = 0.005), and we recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Study Design**

We analyzed weekly service scores from 150 library branches over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22 of the year):

| Group | Branches | Procedure | Weeks Observed |
|-------|----------|-----------|----------------|
| Treatment | 90 | New procedure (from week 15) | 9 pre + 8 post |
| Control | 60 | Old procedure (throughout) | 9 pre + 8 post |

The analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing how service scores changed for switching branches relative to non-switching branches before and after week 15.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. **The New Procedure Improved Service Scores**

| Metric | Control (Old) | Treatment (New) |
|--------|---------------|-----------------|
| Pre-switch average | 49.15 | 48.49 |
| Post-switch average | 48.59 | 49.58 |
| Change | -0.56 | +1.09 |

**Treatment Effect: +1.65 points** (95% CI: +0.50 to +2.80)

### 2. **The Effect Is Statistically Significant**

- **p-value: 0.005** (significant at the 1% level)
- The 95% confidence interval excludes zero, indicating we can be confident the effect is real
- Results hold when controlling for branch-specific characteristics and weekly trends

### 3. **The Effect Is Consistent Post-Switch**

After the switch (weeks 15–22), treatment branches consistently outperformed control branches every single week, with the gap ranging from 0.4 to 1.5 points. Before the switch, the gap fluctuated and was often negative (treatment branches scoring lower).

### 4. **Not Driven by Staffing or Visits**

- Staffing levels did not change differently between groups (p = 0.49)
- Visit volumes showed a small decline in treatment branches (p = 0.03), but this does not explain the service score improvement
- The service score improvement persists even after controlling for these factors

## **Next Steps**

2. **Monitor implementation.** Track service scores during rollout to ensure the effect replicates at remaining branches.

3. **Document the procedure.** Capture the specific practices that make the new procedure effective for training purposes.
