# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure shows a **positive but statistically uncertain effect** on service scores. Branches that switched to the new procedure improved by approximately **2.3 points more** than branches that kept the old procedure. However, this difference is not statistically significant at conventional levels (p=0.11), meaning we cannot rule out that the observed improvement occurred by chance. The 95% confidence interval ranges from **-0.5 to +5.1 points**, indicating the true effect could be negligible or meaningfully positive.

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** (59%) adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in switched branches to changes in non-switched branches while controlling for branch-specific characteristics and week-to-week variation

### Results
| Group | Before Switch (Weeks 6–14) | After Switch (Weeks 15–22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------------|---------------------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 47.95 | 51.26 | +3.31 |
| Non-switched branches | 51.02 | 52.29 | +1.28 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+2.28** |

- **Treatment effect estimate:** +2.28 points (on a scale where average ≈ 50)
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.11 (not significant at the 5% level)
- **95% confidence interval:** -0.5 to +5.1 points
- **Pre-trends check:** Both groups showed similar trends before the switch, supporting the validity of the comparison

### Additional Observations
- Both groups improved after week 15, suggesting broader factors (seasonal, organizational) may have influenced scores
- Staffing levels and visit volumes are positively associated with service scores, but the procedure effect persists after controlling for these factors
- The point estimate (+2.3 points) represents roughly a **4–5% improvement** relative to the average score

## **Next Steps**

1. **Continue monitoring:** Extend the observation period to increase statistical power. With more weeks of data, we could detect smaller effects with greater confidence.

2. **Consider phased rollout:** Given the positive point estimate and absence of negative effects, a cautious expansion to additional branches could be warranted while continuing to collect data.

3. **Investigate heterogeneity:** Analyze whether the procedure works better in certain types of branches (e.g., by size, location, or baseline performance) to identify where it may be most effective.

4. **Cost-benefit analysis:** Before full rollout, assess whether the operational costs of the new procedure are justified by the potential service score improvements.

5. **Set decision threshold:** The board may wish to define what magnitude of improvement would justify systemwide adoption (e.g., "proceed if we can confirm at least a 2-point improvement with 95% confidence").
