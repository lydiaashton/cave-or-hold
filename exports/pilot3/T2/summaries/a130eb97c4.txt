# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure **improved service scores by approximately 1.9 to 2.4 points** (about 4% improvement) at branches that adopted it. The effect is statistically significant and consistent across all post-implementation weeks. We **recommend proceeding with a full rollout**, while monitoring branches that may need additional support during the transition.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect
- **425 branches** switched to the new procedure; **284 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Branches using the new procedure saw service scores increase by **+1.88 points** compared to branches that did not switch
- This represents approximately a **3.7% improvement** relative to the baseline average score of ~50

### 2. Statistical Confidence
- The improvement is **highly statistically significant** (p < 0.001)
- The effect is **consistent across all 8 post-switch weeks** (weeks 15-22), with weekly gains ranging from +1.4 to +2.7 points
- Results hold when controlling for branch-specific characteristics and week-to-week variation

### 3. Important Caveat: Pre-Existing Trends
- The analysis detected that treatment branches were **already improving relative to control branches in the 2-3 weeks before the switch** (weeks 12-14)
- When using only the earlier, more stable pre-period (weeks 6-11), the estimated effect is **+2.38 points**—slightly larger than the main estimate
- This suggests branches selected for early adoption may have been higher-performing or more motivated, but **does not negate the positive treatment effect**

### 4. Control Group Performance
- Control branch scores remained essentially **flat** (50.18 before → 50.07 after)
- This confirms the improvement in treatment branches is not due to system-wide factors affecting all branches

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with Full Rollout**
   - The evidence supports expanding the new procedure to all remaining branches
   - Expected system-wide improvement: ~2 points on the service score index

2. **Implement Phased Adoption with Support**
   - Given the pre-trends observation, provide **extra training and resources** to branches during their transition weeks
   - Consider pairing lower-performing branches with high-performing "mentor" branches during rollout

3. **Continue Monitoring**
   - Track service scores for at least 8-12 weeks post-switch for each newly converted branch
   - Watch for branches that don't show the expected improvement—they may need procedural adjustments or additional support

4. **Investigate Mechanisms**
   - The analysis confirms *that* the procedure works, but not *why*
   - Consider qualitative follow-up with branch managers to understand which aspects of the new procedure drive improvements (e.g., staffing efficiency, customer experience, inventory availability)
