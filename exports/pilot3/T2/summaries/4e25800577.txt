# **Board Summary: New Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure is associated with a **2.3-point improvement in service scores** (on a scale where the average branch scores about 50). This represents approximately a **4-5% improvement** in service quality. While the result is not statistically significant at conventional levels when accounting for branch-level variation (p = 0.11), the evidence consistently points in a positive direction, and the practical benefit may justify broader rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6-22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in the treatment group against changes in the control group

### **Results**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated treatment effect | **+2.28 points** |
| 95% Confidence interval | **-0.5 to +5.1 points** |
| Statistical significance | Not significant at p < 0.05 (p = 0.11) |

### **Supporting Evidence**
1. **Parallel trends verified**: Before the switch (weeks 6-14), treatment and control branches followed similar trends, validating our comparison approach.

2. **Consistent positive effects**: All post-switch weeks show positive effects, with larger gains in later weeks (weeks 6-7 post-switch show +5.8 and +6.3 point effects).

3. **Robust to controls**: The result holds after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume, suggesting the effect is attributable to the procedure itself.

### **What This Means**
- Treatment branches improved from **47.9 to 51.3** (pre to post)
- Control branches improved from **51.0 to 52.3** (pre to post)
- The **extra 2.3-point gain** for treatment branches is the estimated effect of the new procedure

---

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended Actions**
1. **Proceed with cautious rollout**: The positive point estimate and consistent direction of effects suggest the new procedure is beneficial. A full rollout could yield meaningful service improvements across the cooperative.

2. **Continue monitoring**: Track service scores for an additional 8-12 weeks to:
   - Confirm the effect persists
   - Determine if benefits continue to grow over time (early event study results suggest they may)
   - Achieve greater statistical precision with more data

3. **Consider a phased expansion**: Roll out to remaining branches in waves, allowing continued comparison and learning.

4. **Document implementation details**: Capture any branch-specific factors that might affect outcomes (staff training, physical layout, etc.) to optimize the rollout.

### **Caveats**
- The confidence interval includes zero, so we cannot rule out no effect
- However, it also includes effects as large as +5 points, which would be substantial
- The cost of the new procedure should be weighed against the potential benefit

*Analysis used difference-in-differences regression with branch and week fixed effects, controlling for staffing and visitor indices. Standard errors clustered at the branch level. Pre-trend test confirms parallel trends assumption (p = 0.82 for differential pre-trends).*
