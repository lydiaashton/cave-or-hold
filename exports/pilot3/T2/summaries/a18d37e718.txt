# **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure shows a **positive but statistically uncertain** effect on service scores. Branches that switched to the new procedure improved by an average of 1.75 points (approximately 3.6%) relative to branches that kept the old procedure. However, this result is not statistically significant at conventional levels (p=0.11), meaning we cannot rule out that the observed improvement is due to random variation. **We recommend a cautious, phased rollout with continued monitoring rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. **Direction of Effect is Positive**
- **Treatment branches** (switched to new procedure): Service scores increased by an average of **+1.57 points** after the switch
- **Control branches** (kept old procedure): Service scores decreased by an average of **-0.19 points** over the same period
- **Net effect (Difference-in-Differences)**: **+1.75 points** (95% confidence interval: -0.41 to +3.91)

### 2. **Statistical Uncertainty**
- The p-value of 0.11 means there is an 11% probability of observing this result even if the new procedure has no real effect
- The confidence interval includes zero, so we cannot confidently conclude the effect is different from no effect
- This uncertainty is primarily due to the relatively small sample (34 branches over 17 weeks)

### 3. **Branch-Level Variation**
- **65% of treatment branches** (13 of 20) showed improvement after switching
- **50% of control branches** (7 of 14) showed improvement over the same period
- Some branches saw substantial gains (up to +9.2 points), while others declined (down to -4.1 points)
- This variation suggests the procedure may work better in some contexts than others

### 4. **Validity Checks Passed**
- **Parallel trends assumption**: Before the switch, treatment and control branches followed similar trends (no pre-existing differences in trajectory)
- **Controls**: The result holds when accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume, both of which positively affect service scores
