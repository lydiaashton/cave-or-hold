# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by **1.8 points** (approximately 3.7% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and has been growing over time. **We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect
- **Switched branches** improved from 50.12 to 51.71 (+1.59 points)
- **Control branches** declined slightly from 50.10 to 49.85 (-0.25 points)
- **Net effect (Difference-in-Differences): +1.84 points** (95% CI: 1.30 to 2.40)

### 2. Statistical Confidence
- The effect is **highly statistically significant** (p < 0.001)
- Results hold after controlling for staffing levels and visit volumes
- The parallel trends assumption is satisfied—switched and control branches followed similar trajectories before the change

### 3. Effect Over Time
The improvement appears to be **strengthening** as branches gain experience with the new procedure:
- Weeks 15-18 (early): +1.46 points
- Weeks 19-22 (late): +2.25 points

This suggests the full benefit may not yet be realized.

### 4. Scale of Impact
- Baseline service score: ~50 points
- Improvement: ~1.8 points (3.7%)
- With 709 branches system-wide, this represents a meaningful system-level gain

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all remaining branches.

2. **Develop implementation support** including:
   - Training materials based on successful switched branches
   - A transition timeline that allows branches to build proficiency

3. **Continue monitoring** service scores post-rollout to:
   - Confirm the effect persists at scale
   - Identify whether the improvement continues to grow with experience

4. **Document best practices** from high-performing switched branches to accelerate adoption.

---

*Analysis based on 12,053 weekly observations from 709 branches, weeks 6-22. The switch occurred in week 15. Statistical models use standard errors clustered by branch.*
