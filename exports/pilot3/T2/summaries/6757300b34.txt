# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure **improved service scores by approximately 2.1 points** (on a scale where the average branch scores about 50). This represents a **4% improvement** and is statistically significant. We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout, while monitoring results to confirm the effect persists.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **709 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **425 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **284 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in the treatment group to changes in the control group

### **Results**

| Group | Before Switch (Weeks 6–14) | After Switch (Weeks 15–22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------------|---------------------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 49.9 | 50.7 | +0.8 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 51.1 | 54.0 | +2.9 |
| **Estimated Effect** | | | **+2.1** |

- **Treatment effect: +2.1 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.4 to 2.8)
- **Statistical significance: p < 0.001** (highly significant)
- Effect remains robust after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### **Trends Over Time**
The event study analysis shows:
- Before the switch, both groups followed similar trends with some variation
- After the switch, branches using the new procedure maintained higher service scores relative to the control group
- The improvement appears sustained across all 8 post-switch weeks

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with rollout** — The evidence supports expanding the new procedure to all branches

2. **Monitor implementation** — Track service scores for the first 8–12 weeks after rollout to confirm the effect holds at scale

3. **Document best practices** — Work with the 425 early-adopter branches to capture any implementation tips that may help other branches maximize the benefit

4. **Consider complementary factors** — Staffing and visitor volume also affect service scores; ensure adequate resourcing during the transition

---

*Analysis based on weekly branch data from weeks 6–22. The switch occurred at week 15. Service scores are an internal index where higher values indicate better performance.*
