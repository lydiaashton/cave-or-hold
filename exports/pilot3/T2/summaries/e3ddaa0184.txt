# **Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by **2.4 points** (approximately 5% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This result is statistically significant and robust. **We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measurable Improvement in Service Quality
- Branches using the new procedure experienced a **2.4-point increase** in service scores after the switch
- 95% confidence interval: **0.7 to 4.2 points**
- Statistical significance: **p = 0.007** (less than 1% chance this result is due to random variation)

### 2. Strong Research Design Validates the Result
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure; **14 branches** kept the old procedure as a control group
- The switch occurred at **week 15**, allowing clear before/after comparison
- **Parallel trends confirmed**: Before the switch, treatment and control branches were moving in lockstep (p = 0.93), validating our comparison

### 3. Results Are Robust
- The 2.4-point improvement holds even after accounting for:
  - Branch-specific characteristics (some branches naturally score higher/lower)
  - Weekly fluctuations across all branches
  - Staffing levels and visitor volume

### 4. Practical Significance
- Average service score is approximately 50 points
- A 2.4-point gain represents a **~5% improvement** in service quality
- Control branches (old procedure) saw only a 0.7-point increase over the same period
- Treatment branches (new procedure) saw a 3.1-point increase

2. **Monitor implementation** over the next 8–12 weeks to ensure:
   - Consistent adoption across all branches
