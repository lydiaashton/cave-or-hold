# **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2 points** (about 4%) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This result is statistically significant and contradicts the initial expectation that the change would make no difference. We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Measurable Improvement in Service Scores**
- **Switched branches** (90 branches): Service scores increased by an average of **0.76 points** after adopting the new procedure
- **Non-switched branches** (60 branches): Service scores **decreased by 1.25 points** during the same period
- **Net effect**: **+2.01 points** attributable to the new procedure (95% confidence interval: 0.81 to 3.22 points)

### **2. Statistical Confidence**
- The improvement is **statistically significant** (p = 0.001), meaning there is less than a 0.1% probability this result occurred by chance
- The analysis used a difference-in-differences approach, which isolates the effect of the procedure change from other factors affecting all branches (such as seasonal variations)
- Results are robust to controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### **3. Consistent Across Branches**
- **59% of switched branches** saw their service scores improve after the change
- Only **35% of non-switched branches** saw improvement during the same period
- The effect was consistent regardless of branch size or baseline performance

### **4. Valid Comparison**
- Before the switch (weeks 6-14), switched and non-switched branches followed **parallel trends**—there was no systematic difference in how their scores were changing
- This validates the comparison and supports the conclusion that the procedure change caused the improvement

### **5. Context**
- Average service score across all branches is approximately 50 points
- A 2-point improvement represents roughly a **4% gain** in service quality
- The non-switched branches experienced a slight decline during the study period, possibly due to seasonal factors or other system-wide influences

## **Methodology Note**

This analysis used 2,550 weekly observations from 150 branches over 17 weeks (weeks 6-22). The switch occurred in week 15. We employed a difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch) to account for repeated measurements on the same branches. The parallel trends assumption was tested and confirmed for the pre-switch period.
