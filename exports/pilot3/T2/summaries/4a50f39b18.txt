# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **2.1 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.5 to 2.6 points) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents approximately a **4% improvement** and is statistically significant. We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **709 branches** tracked over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- **425 branches** adopted the new procedure (treatment group)
- **284 branches** kept the old procedure (control group)
- The switch occurred in **week 15**
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in treatment branches to changes in control branches

### **Results**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Treatment Effect** | +2.08 points |
| **95% Confidence Interval** | [1.53, 2.64] |
| **Statistical Significance** | p < 0.001 (highly significant) |
| **Relative Improvement** | ~4% above baseline |

### **What This Means**
- Treatment branches improved by **3.5 points** from before to after the switch
- Control branches improved by **1.4 points** over the same period (likely due to seasonal or system-wide factors)
- The **additional 2.1-point gain** in treatment branches is attributable to the new procedure
- The effect is **robust** across different statistical models, including when controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### **Supporting Evidence**
- Pre-treatment trends were stable, supporting the validity of the comparison
- Post-treatment effects were **consistently positive** in every week after the switch
- The improvement persisted throughout the 8-week post-switch observation period

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all 284 branches currently using the old procedure

2. **Monitor implementation** to ensure the new procedure is adopted consistently across all branches

3. **Track service scores** for the next 3–6 months to confirm the effect persists at scale

4. **Document best practices** from the 425 early-adopter branches to support training at remaining branches

5. **Consider cost-benefit analysis** if the new procedure has different resource requirements than the old one

## **Technical Notes**

- Analysis controls for branch-specific characteristics (branch fixed effects)
- Standard errors are clustered at the branch level to account for repeated observations
- Results are robust to inclusion of staffing and visitor volume controls
- Average service score across all branches is approximately 50 points (higher is better)
