# **Evaluation of New Shelving Procedure on Library Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Contrary to the expectation of no difference, branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.2 points** (about 4.4% relative to the average score of 50). This improvement is statistically significant and consistent across multiple analytical approaches. **We recommend proceeding with system-wide rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

| Metric | Result |
|--------|--------|
| **Treatment Effect** | +2.18 service score points |
| **Statistical Significance** | p = 0.0009 (significant at 1% level) |
| **95% Confidence Interval** | +0.89 to +3.46 points |
| **Branches Analyzed** | 150 total (90 switched, 60 control) |
| **Observation Period** | 17 weeks (weeks 6–22), switch at week 15 |

### What the Analysis Shows

1. **Clear Positive Impact**: The difference-in-differences analysis—with controls for branch-specific characteristics, week-to-week variation, staffing levels, and visit volumes—shows the new procedure increased service scores by 2.2 points on average.

2. **Valid Comparison**: Before the switch (weeks 6–14), treatment and control branches followed parallel trends with no systematic differences. This validates our causal interpretation.

3. **Robust Results**: The finding holds across multiple specifications:
   - With and without control variables
   - Excluding extreme outliers
   - Different statistical approaches

4. **Broad-Based Improvement**: Among the 90 branches that switched, 53 (59%) showed score improvements after the change, while 37 saw declines—indicating the benefit is widespread, though not universal.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve System-Wide Rollout**: The evidence supports extending the new procedure to all 150 branches.

2. **Support Underperforming Branches**: Since 37 of 90 switching branches did not improve, consider:
   - Identifying common characteristics of non-responders
   - Providing additional training or resources to struggling branches
   - Monitoring implementation fidelity

3. **Continue Monitoring**: Track service scores post-rollout to confirm the effect persists at scale and identify any emerging issues.

4. **Document Best Practices**: Branches with the largest gains may have implementation insights worth sharing across the cooperative.

---

*Analysis prepared using difference-in-differences regression with branch and week fixed effects, clustered standard errors at the branch level.*
