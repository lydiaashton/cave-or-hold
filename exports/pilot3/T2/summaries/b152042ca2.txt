# **Memorandum to the Board**

## **Title: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure shows a **positive but statistically inconclusive effect** on service scores. Branches that switched experienced an estimated **1.9-point improvement** (approximately 4% relative to baseline), but this result is not statistically significant at conventional levels. **We cannot confidently conclude the new procedure improves service scores based on current data.** Additional data collection is recommended before a system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **34 branches** total: 20 adopted the new procedure, 14 continued with the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in switching branches against non-switching branches while controlling for branch-specific characteristics

### Service Score Results

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 50.6 | 51.3 | +0.7 |
| Non-switched branches | 47.7 | 46.5 | −1.2 |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | | | **+1.9** |

- **Estimated effect: +1.9 points** (95% confidence interval: −0.5 to +4.3)
- **P-value: 0.11** — not statistically significant at the 5% level
- The confidence interval includes zero, meaning we cannot rule out no effect (or even a small negative effect)

### Supporting Analyses
- **Parallel trends assumption holds**: No significant difference in trends between groups before the switch (p=0.24)
- **No confounding from staffing or visits**: These factors did not change differentially between groups
- **Branch-level variation is substantial**: Some branches show larger improvements than others, suggesting the effect may depend on local conditions

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Extend the observation period**: Collect 8–12 additional weeks of data to increase statistical power and determine whether the positive trend persists

2. **Investigate branch-level heterogeneity**: Analyze which branches benefited most from the new procedure to identify implementation best practices

3. **Consider a phased rollout**: If proceeding, implement in stages with continued monitoring rather than system-wide adoption

4. **Establish clear success criteria**: Define the minimum effect size that would justify the costs of system-wide implementation

---

## **Recommendation**

**Do not proceed with immediate system-wide rollout.** The point estimate is encouraging, but the evidence is not strong enough to justify the costs and disruption of changing procedures at all 34 branches. Continue monitoring the current pilot branches and gather more data before making a final decision.
