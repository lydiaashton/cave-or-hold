# **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw their service scores increase by approximately **1.9 points** (95% CI: 1.4 to 2.4) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents a **3.7% improvement** over baseline performance. The result is statistically significant (p < 0.001) and robust to controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **709 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **Switch happened in week 15**: branches were split into treatment (adopted new procedure) and control (kept old procedure) groups
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in switched branches against changes in non-switched branches

### Results

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| **Switched branches** (treatment) | 50.79 | 52.56 | +1.77 |
| **Non-switched branches** (control) | 50.18 | 50.07 | -0.11 |
| **Net effect (DiD)** | | | **+1.89** |

### Statistical Confidence
- **Estimated effect**: +1.89 service score points
- **95% confidence interval**: 1.37 to 2.42 points
- **P-value**: < 0.001 (highly significant)
- Results hold after controlling for weekly staffing and visitor volume

### Parallel Trends Check
Before the switch (weeks 6–14), the average gap between switched and non-switched branches was 0.61 points. After the switch (weeks 15–22), this gap widened to 2.49 points, consistent with a positive treatment effect.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** — The evidence strongly supports expanding the new procedure to all branches.

2. **Monitor implementation** — Track service scores during rollout to ensure the ~1.9 point improvement is realized across all branches.

3. **Investigate mechanisms** — The analysis controlled for staffing and visits, but further study could identify *why* the new procedure works (e.g., faster checkout, better organization, reduced wait times).

4. **Cost-benefit analysis** — Quantify the operational costs of the new procedure against the service improvement to confirm net benefit.

5. **Long-term follow-up** — Continue monitoring to ensure the effect persists beyond the initial 8-week post-switch period.
