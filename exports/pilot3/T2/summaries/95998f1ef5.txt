# **MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 14, 2026  
**Subject:** Impact of New Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Contrary to the expectation that the change would make no difference, branches that adopted the new procedure saw their service scores increase by approximately **1.9 points** (about 3.6%) relative to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and robust across multiple analytical approaches.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### Study Design
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** switched to the new procedure at week 15; **60 branches** continued with the old procedure
- This natural experiment allows us to isolate the effect of the procedure change

### Main Result
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 51.32 | 50.70 | −0.62 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 50.32 | 51.51 | +1.19 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+1.81** |

- **Treatment effect: +1.86 points** (95% confidence interval: 0.65 to 3.07)
- **p-value: 0.003** — highly unlikely to occur by chance
- Effect holds after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### Validity Checks
- **Parallel trends confirmed:** Before the switch, treatment and control branches showed no differential trends (p=0.80), supporting the validity of the comparison
- **Consistent across specifications:** Results are stable whether we use simple comparisons, add control variables, or account for branch-specific characteristics

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

1. **Recommend rollout:** The evidence supports expanding the new procedure to all branches. The improvement is meaningful (nearly 4% gain) and statistically reliable.

2. **Monitor implementation:** As the procedure rolls out, continue tracking service scores to confirm the effect persists at scale.

3. **Investigate mechanisms:** Consider studying *why* the new procedure works—was it faster retrieval, better organization, or something else? This could inform future improvements.

4. **Cost-benefit analysis:** If the new procedure has implementation costs, weigh them against the 1.9-point service score improvement to confirm net benefit.
