# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

---

## **Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

**Date:** Analysis of weeks 6–22  
**Prepared for:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors

---

### **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure experienced service score increases of approximately **2.1 points** compared to branches that retained the old procedure. This represents a **4% improvement** relative to the typical service score of 50. The effect is statistically significant and consistent across analytical methods.

> **Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout of the new procedure.**

---

### **KEY FINDINGS**

**Study Scope**
- **709 branches** analyzed: 425 adopted the new procedure, 284 retained the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22), with the procedure change implemented at week 15
- Analysis employs a **difference-in-differences** design, isolating the procedure's effect from other factors

**Main Result**

| Group | Pre-Switch Average | Post-Switch Average | Change |
|-------|-------------------|---------------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 49.91 | 50.73 | +0.82 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 51.12 | 54.04 | +2.92 |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | | | **+2.10** |

**Statistical Confidence**
- Effect is **highly statistically significant** (p < 0.001)
- **95% confidence interval:** 1.37 to 2.83 points
- Result is robust after controlling for staffing levels, visitor volume, and branch-specific characteristics

**Practical Significance**
- A 2.1-point gain equals approximately **4% improvement** over baseline
- The effect emerged immediately after implementation and persisted through all 8 weeks of post-switch observation
- No evidence of diminishing returns over the observed period

---

### **LIMITATIONS**

1. **Non-random assignment:** Branches were not randomly assigned to adopt the new procedure. The analytical design accounts for pre-existing differences, but unobserved factors cannot be fully ruled out.

2. **Observation window:** Eight weeks of post-implementation data provides strong short-term evidence but cannot confirm long-term sustainability.

3. **Pre-existing volatility:** Service scores showed week-to-week variation before the switch, though no systematic diverging trend between groups was detected.

---

### **NEXT STEPS**

| Action | Timeline | Owner |
|--------|----------|-------|
| **Approve system-wide rollout** | Immediate | Board |
| **Develop implementation playbook** from early adopters | 4–6 weeks | Operations |
| **Monitor service scores** post-rollout | Ongoing (6–12 months) | Analytics |
| **Review staffing-service score relationship** for complementary improvements | Next quarter | HR/Operations |

---

### **CONCLUSION**

The evidence supports rolling out the new rotation and shelving procedure to all branches. The 2.1-point service score improvement is meaningful, statistically reliable, and shows no signs of fading over the observation period. Continued monitoring will ensure the effect sustains at scale.
