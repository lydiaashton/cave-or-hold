# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 14, 2025  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure shows a **positive but statistically uncertain** effect on branch service scores. While branches using the new procedure improved by approximately 2 points relative to those using the old procedure, this result is not statistically significant at conventional levels. **We recommend against immediate system-wide rollout** and instead propose extended evaluation with continued monitoring.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Overview**
- **34 branches** participated: 20 adopted the new procedure, 14 maintained the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** collected (weeks 6–22), with the procedure change occurring at week 15
- Analysis compares service score changes between the two groups using standard difference-in-differences methods

### **Service Score Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 47.4 | 47.2 | −0.3 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 46.6 | 48.2 | +1.6 |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | | | **+1.9** |

### **Statistical Assessment**
- **Estimated effect:** +1.9 points on the service score index (approximately 4% of the average score of 50)
- **95% confidence interval:** −0.4 to +4.3 points
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.10 (not significant at the 5% level)

### **Interpretation**
The data suggest the new procedure may improve service scores, but the evidence is not strong enough to rule out random variation. The confidence interval indicates the true effect could range from slightly negative to moderately positive. Pre-switch trends were similar between groups, supporting the validity of the comparison.

---

## **Recommendation**

**Do not proceed with system-wide rollout at this time.** The current evidence is insufficient to justify the costs and disruption of cooperative-wide implementation.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Extend data collection by 8–12 weeks.** This will improve statistical precision and reveal whether the observed effect stabilizes or diminishes over time.

2. **Maintain the current study design.** Continue tracking both treatment and control branches to preserve the ability to measure causal effects.

3. **Document implementation practices.** Survey branches to ensure consistent application of the new procedure and identify any operational challenges.

4. **Evaluate complementary metrics.** Assess whether the new procedure affects staffing efficiency, patron satisfaction, or operational costs—factors not captured in the service score.

5. **Reconvene in Q1 2026.** With additional data, the board will be positioned to make a more confident decision.
