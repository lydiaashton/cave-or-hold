# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 14, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure **improves service scores by approximately 2 points** (about 4% relative to baseline). While statistical certainty is moderate (90% confidence), the effect is consistent, grows stronger over time, and shows no negative consequences. We recommend **proceeding with a phased system-wide rollout**, accompanied by enhanced transition support.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. The New Procedure Improves Service Quality**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Branches that switched | 49.67 | 52.97 | +3.31 |
| Branches that did not switch | 51.02 | 52.18 | +1.16 |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | | **+2.15** |

- **Estimated improvement:** 2.15 points on the service score index
- **Confidence range:** We are 95% confident the true effect lies between -0.35 and +4.65 points
- **Confidence level:** 90% certainty the effect is positive

### **2. Benefits Increase Over Time**

The effect was not immediate. Branches showed a learning curve:

- **Weeks 1-2 after switch:** Minimal or no measurable benefit
- **Weeks 3-4:** Clear positive effects emerge (+2 to +3 points)
- **Weeks 7-8:** Strongest effects observed (+6 to +7 points)

This pattern indicates branches need **1-2 weeks to adjust** before realizing the procedure's full benefits.

### **3. Analysis Is Methodologically Sound**

- **34 branches** studied over **17 weeks** (9 weeks before, 8 weeks after the switch)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure; **14 branches** served as a comparison group
- Pre-switch trends were similar between groups, supporting the validity of the comparison
- Results hold after accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume

---

## **Recommendations**

### **Proceed with Rollout—With Support**

| Action | Rationale |
|--------|-----------|
| **1. Approve system-wide implementation** | Evidence points to meaningful service improvement |
| **2. Phase the rollout over 8-10 weeks** | Allows staff to provide hands-on support to each cohort |
| **3. Assign transition coaches for Weeks 1-2** | Data shows this is the critical adjustment period |
| **4. Monitor weekly service scores** | Track whether the learning-curve pattern replicates |
| **5. Re-evaluate after 6 months** | Assess whether benefits sustain long-term |

### **Success Metrics**

- **Short-term (Weeks 1-4):** Service scores should not decline more than 2 points during transition
- **Medium-term (Weeks 5-8):** Scores should exceed pre-switch levels by at least 1.5 points
- **Long-term (6+ months):** Maintain or exceed the 2-point improvement observed in this study

---

## **Risks and Mitigations**

| Risk | Likelihood | Mitigation |
|------|------------|------------|
| Effect smaller than estimated | Moderate | Phased rollout allows course correction |
| Transition disruption | Moderate | Transition coaches and extra staffing in Weeks 1-2 |
| Benefits fade over time | Low | Schedule 6-month follow-up evaluation |
| Some branches struggle | Moderate | Identify laggards early; provide targeted support |

---

## **Conclusion**

The new rotation and shelving procedure **likely delivers meaningful service improvements**. The evidence is strong enough to justify implementation, particularly given the absence of negative effects and the pattern of strengthening benefits over time. A supported, phased rollout minimizes risk while positioning the cooperative to realize sustained gains in service quality.
