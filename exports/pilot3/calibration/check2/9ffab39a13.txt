# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 14, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure  

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure shows a **positive but not statistically definitive** effect on service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure achieved service scores approximately **2.2 points higher (4.2% improvement)** than would have been expected based on control branch trends. While this result is encouraging, statistical uncertainty means we cannot rule out the possibility that the observed improvement occurred by chance. We recommend a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **Study Overview**
| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| Total branches | 34 |
| Adopted new procedure | 20 branches |
| Kept old procedure (control) | 14 branches |
| Study period | 17 weeks (Weeks 6–22) |
| Switch implemented | Week 15 |

### **Service Score Impact**

| Period | Switched Branches | Control Branches |
|--------|------------------|------------------|
| Before switch (Weeks 6–14) | 52.83 | 51.34 |
| After switch (Weeks 15–22) | 53.61 | 49.95 |
| **Change** | **+0.79** | **-1.40** |

**Difference-in-Differences Estimate: +2.19 points**

### **Statistical Confidence**
- **95% Confidence Interval:** -0.62 to +4.99 points
- **P-value:** 0.13 (not statistically significant at the 0.05 level)
- **Interpretation:** The data are consistent with both a small negative effect and a moderate positive effect, though the most likely value is a ~2-point improvement

### **Branch-Level Patterns**
- **45%** of switched branches (9 of 20) showed score improvements
- **36%** of control branches (5 of 14) showed score improvements
- Switched branch changes ranged from -5.9 to +8.0 points
- Control branch changes ranged from -7.9 to +6.0 points

### **Important Context**
Control branches experienced a notable **decline** in service scores during the study period (-1.40 points). Switched branches not only avoided this decline but achieved a modest improvement (+0.79 points). This suggests the new procedure may help branches **maintain service quality** even when external conditions are challenging.

---

## **NEXT STEPS & RECOMMENDATIONS**

### **1. Approve Phased Expansion**
Given the positive direction of results and meaningful practical significance, we recommend expanding the new procedure to **5–10 additional branches** over the next quarter. This balances potential benefits against implementation costs and uncertainty.

### **2. Extend the Evaluation Period**
The current study covers only 8 weeks post-implementation. We recommend collecting **at least 12–16 weeks** of post-implementation data for the next phase to:
- Increase statistical confidence
- Capture any delayed or cumulative effects
- Account for seasonal variations in library usage

### **3. Investigate Branch-Level Variation**
Not all branches benefited equally. We recommend analyzing which branch characteristics (size, location, staffing levels, patron demographics) correlate with stronger outcomes to:
- Identify best practices for implementation
- Target support to branches that may struggle
- Refine the procedure for different branch types

### **4. Monitor Operational Metrics**
Continue tracking staffing_index and visits_index to ensure the new procedure:
- Does not increase staff workload unsustainably
- Does not negatively affect patron visit patterns
- Delivers net positive value across all dimensions

### **5. Decision Timeline**
| Milestone | Target Date |
|-----------|-------------|
| Phase 1 expansion begins | Q1 2027 |
| Interim review (8 weeks) | Q2 2027 |
| Full evaluation (16 weeks) | Q3 2027 |
| System-wide rollout decision | Q4 2027 |

---

## **CONCLUSION**

The evidence suggests the new procedure has a **modest positive effect** on service scores, but additional data are needed before committing to system-wide implementation. A phased approach allows the cooperative to capture potential benefits while managing risk and building a stronger evidence base for the final decision.
