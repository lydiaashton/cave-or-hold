# MEMORANDUM

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 14, 2026  
**Subject:** Impact of New Branch Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## Bottom Line

**The new shelving and stock rotation procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by **1.9 points** (approximately 4%) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant and consistent across multiple analytical methods.

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout.**

---

## Key Findings

### 1. Measured Impact

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Average improvement** | +1.9 points on service score index |
| **Percentage improvement** | ~4% (based on average score of 50) |
| **Statistical confidence** | 95% confidence interval: 1.4 to 2.4 points |
| **Statistical significance** | p < 0.001 (highly significant) |

### 2. What the Data Shows

The analysis compared 413 branches that switched to the new procedure against 296 branches that continued with the old procedure, across 17 weeks (weeks 6–22). The switch occurred in week 15.

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched Branches | 51.35 | 51.70 | +0.35 |
| Control Branches | 51.15 | 49.65 | −1.50 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+1.85** |

**Important insight:** Switched branches maintained their service quality, while control branches experienced a notable decline. The new procedure appears to have **protected against deterioration** in service scores during this period.

### 3. Robustness of Findings

The positive effect holds across all analytical approaches:

- **Basic model:** +1.85 points (p < 0.001)
- **With staffing/visits controls:** +1.91 points (p < 0.001)
- **Branch fixed effects model:** +1.90 points (p < 0.001)

### 4. Effect Over Time

The improvement was not uniform across all post-switch weeks:

- **Weeks 15–18:** Strong positive effects (+2 to +6 points)
- **Weeks 19–20:** Effect diminished (not statistically significant)
- **Weeks 21–22:** Mixed results

This pattern suggests the benefit may require ongoing reinforcement or that external factors (such as seasonal variations) influenced later weeks.

---

## Technical Notes

This analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** research design, the standard approach for evaluating policy changes when random assignment is not feasible. The methodology controls for:

- Permanent differences between branches (branch fixed effects)
- Overall time trends affecting all branches
- Weekly variations in staffing and visitor volume

Standard errors are clustered at the branch level to account for repeated observations within each branch.

**Data source:** branches.csv (12,053 observations, 709 branches, weeks 6–22)
