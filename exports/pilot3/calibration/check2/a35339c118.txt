# **Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure Evaluation**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **2.1 points** (approximately **4% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and consistent across multiple analytical approaches. **We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measurable Improvement in Service Quality
- **Treatment effect:** +2.1 points on the service score index (95% confidence interval: 0.8 to 3.3)
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.01 (highly significant)
- **Practical significance:** Represents a 4.2% improvement over the baseline average of ~50 points

### 2. Clear Divergence Between Groups

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| **Switched branches** (90 branches) | 49.7 | 50.7 | **+1.0** |
| **Non-switched branches** (60 branches) | 49.9 | 48.8 | **-1.1** |

The difference-in-differences estimate of +2.1 points captures that switched branches improved while non-switched branches actually declined slightly during the same period.

### 3. Broad-Based Benefits
- **59% of switched branches** saw their scores improve after adoption
- Only **35% of non-switched branches** saw improvement during the same period
- The effect remains significant even after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### 4. Analysis is Methodologically Sound
- **Parallel trends verified:** Before the switch, both groups followed similar trends (p = 0.13), validating our comparison approach
- **Robust results:** The finding holds across multiple statistical specifications
- **Large sample:** 150 branches tracked over 17 weeks (2,550 observations)

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all remaining 60 branches

2. **Monitor implementation** during the rollout to ensure:
   - Staff receive adequate training on the new procedure
   - Benefits are realized across all branch types and sizes

3. **Track service scores** for the next 8-12 weeks post-rollout to confirm the effect persists at scale

4. **Document best practices** from the 90 early-adopter branches to support training at remaining locations

5. **Consider complementary investments** in staffing, as our analysis shows staffing levels also positively correlate with service scores
