# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

## **Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

---

### **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by **2.1 points** compared to branches that retained the old procedure. This represents a **4% improvement** and is statistically significant (p < 0.001).

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout.**

---

### **KEY FINDINGS**

**Study Overview**
- **709 branches** analyzed over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- **425 branches** switched to the new procedure at week 15; **284 branches** maintained the old procedure
- Analysis controls for staffing levels, visitor volume, and branch-specific characteristics

**Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Branches that switched | 51.12 | 54.04 | +2.92 |
| Branches that did not switch | 49.91 | 50.73 | +0.82 |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | | **+2.10** |

**Statistical Confidence**
- **95% confidence interval:** 1.4 to 2.8 points
- Result is robust across multiple model specifications
- Effect sustained over all 8 weeks of post-switch observation

**Context**
- Average service score across all branches: ~50 points
- The 2.1-point gain exceeds normal week-to-week variation
- Both staffing and visitor volume independently correlate with higher scores

---

### **NEXT STEPS**

1. **Approve full rollout** of the new procedure to all branches

2. **Monitor service scores** quarterly for 12 months to confirm sustained improvement

3. **Capture implementation learnings** from the 425 early-adopter branches to support training

4. **Review staffing allocations** alongside rollout, as adequate staffing amplifies the procedure's benefits

---

*Analysis based on weekly branch data, weeks 6–22. Difference-in-differences methodology with branch fixed effects.*
