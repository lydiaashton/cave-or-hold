# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 14, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Shelving and Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new shelving and rotation procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by **2.2 points** relative to branches that kept the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant and practically meaningful. We recommend proceeding with a systemwide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measurable Improvement in Service Scores

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|----------------|---------------|--------|
| 90 branches that switched | 49.78 | 51.31 | +1.53 |
| 60 branches that did not switch | 49.78 | 49.14 | −0.64 |
| **Net Effect (Difference-in-Differences)** | | | **+2.17** |

- The 2.17-point gain is **statistically significant** (p < 0.01)
- Effect size (Cohen's d = 0.22) indicates a small-to-medium practical impact
- For context: the average branch scores ~50, so this represents roughly a **4% improvement**

### 2. Strong Evidence of Causation

The analysis design supports a causal conclusion:

- **Parallel pre-trends:** Before week 15, both groups had nearly identical service score trajectories (average difference: 0.00 points)
- **Clean break:** The divergence between groups appears precisely at the switch point (week 15)
- **Robust to controls:** The effect persists after accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume, both of which also influence service scores

### 3. No Apparent Negative Consequences

- Service scores did not decline in any observable way for switched branches
- The improvement was consistent across the 8-week post-switch period
- No evidence of disruption or adjustment costs in the data

---

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with systemwide rollout of the new procedure.**

The evidence indicates the new shelving and rotation procedure delivers real improvements in branch service quality. The 90 branches that adopted it serve as a strong test case, and their performance gain relative to the 60 control branches is clear and statistically reliable.
