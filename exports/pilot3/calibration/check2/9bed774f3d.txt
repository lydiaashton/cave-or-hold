# **LIBRARY COOPERATIVE BOARD MEMORANDUM**

---

## **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

**Prepared for:** Library Cooperative Board  
**Date:** January 2026  
**Analysis Period:** Weeks 6-22 (17 weeks of data)  
**Branches Studied:** 34 total (20 switched, 14 did not)

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

The new procedure shows a **positive but inconclusive effect** on service scores. Switched branches improved by approximately **1.7 points** (3.5% above baseline) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. However, this result is **not statistically significant** at conventional confidence levels—we cannot rule out that the observed difference occurred by chance.

**Recommendation:** Do not proceed with immediate system-wide rollout. Instead, continue monitoring and consider a phased expansion while collecting additional data.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **1. What the Data Shows**

| Metric | Switched Branches | Non-Switched Branches |
|--------|------------------|----------------------|
| Average score before switch (weeks 6-14) | 50.21 | 47.58 |
| Average score after switch (weeks 15-22) | 52.84 | 48.81 |
| **Change** | **+2.63** | **+1.23** |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | **+1.40 points** |

### **2. Statistical Confidence**

- **Point estimate:** 1.7 points improvement
- **95% Confidence Interval:** -1.6 to +5.0 points
- **Interpretation:** The true effect could reasonably be slightly negative, zero, or moderately positive
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.08 (marginally significant; conventional threshold is p < 0.05)

### **3. Important Context**

- **Both groups improved** after week 15, suggesting external factors (seasonal patterns, system-wide initiatives, or other changes) boosted scores across all branches
- **Pre-switch trends were parallel:** Switched and non-switched branches followed similar trajectories before the procedure change, validating our comparison method
- **Modest effect size:** Even if the 1.7-point improvement is real, it represents only a 3.5% gain relative to the typical branch score of ~50

### **4. Study Limitations**

- Only 8 weeks of post-switch data limits our ability to detect smaller effects
- With 34 branches total, the study has limited statistical power
- We cannot yet distinguish the procedure's effect from other factors that may have influenced scores after week 15

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

### **1. Continue Data Collection (High Priority)**
- Collect 8-12 additional weeks of data to improve statistical precision
- This would narrow the confidence interval and provide a clearer answer on effectiveness

### **2. Consider Phased Rollout (If Board Wishes to Proceed)**
- Implement the new procedure in additional branches gradually rather than all at once
- Use a staggered rollout to continue generating rigorous evidence
- Monitor service scores closely during expansion

### **3. Conduct Cost-Benefit Analysis**
- Quantify the operational costs of the new procedure (staff training, implementation time, ongoing expenses)
- Weigh these costs against the uncertain 1.7-point service score benefit
- Determine whether the improvement, if real, justifies the investment

### **4. Investigate Differential Effects**
- Analyze whether the procedure affects certain branch types differently (by size, location, or traffic volume)
- Examine secondary outcomes beyond the service score (staff satisfaction, patron feedback, operational efficiency)

---

## **CONCLUSION**

The new rotation and shelving procedure shows **promise but lacks conclusive evidence** of effectiveness. The observed improvement is consistent with a modest positive effect, but the data does not yet support a confident conclusion.

A **patient, evidence-based approach** would best serve the cooperative's interests:

> **Continue monitoring, collect more data, and consider gradual expansion rather than immediate system-wide implementation.**

This approach minimizes risk while allowing the cooperative to build a stronger evidence base before committing resources to a full rollout.

---

*Technical Note: Analysis used difference-in-differences regression with controls for staffing and visit indices. Standard errors clustered by branch to account for repeated weekly observations per branch. The parallel trends assumption was tested and satisfied.*
