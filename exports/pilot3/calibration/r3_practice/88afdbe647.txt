# **Revised Memorandum to the Board**

**Subject:** New Branch Procedure – Service Score Impact Analysis

---

## **Bottom Line**

**We cannot determine whether the new procedure caused the improvement.** While service scores did increase more at switched branches than non-switched branches, the data shows the switched branches were **already improving faster before the change occurred**. This makes it impossible to separate the procedure's effect from pre-existing differences between the two groups.

**Recommendation: Do not proceed with system-wide rollout based on this evidence alone.**

---

## **What the Data Shows**

| Observation | Interpretation |
|-------------|----------------|
| Switched branches improved by +2 points more than non-switched | **Association exists** |
| But switched branches were already pulling ahead before the switch | **Causation unclear** |
| Gap grew by 2.4 points during pre-switch weeks | **Same magnitude as "effect"** |
| Placebo test (fake switch date) shows similar results | **Suggests bias, not causation** |

### **The Critical Problem: Pre-Existing Trends**

| Week | Gap (Switched − Non-Switched) | Period |
|------|-------------------------------|--------|
| 6 | 0.65 points | Before |
| 10 | 1.48 points | Before |
| 14 | 3.05 points | Before |
| 18 | 3.75 points | After |
| 22 | 4.65 points | After |

**The gap was already widening by 0.26 points per week before the switch.** This trend accounts for most of the observed "effect."

---

## **Why This Matters**

The standard analysis method (difference-in-differences) requires that treatment and control groups would have followed **parallel trends** absent the intervention. This assumption is **violated** in our data.

Possible explanations for the observed pattern:
1. **Selection bias**: Higher-performing or better-managed branches may have been chosen to switch first
2. **Unobserved factors**: Something else (regional differences, management quality, etc.) drives both the decision to switch and score improvements
3. **The procedure may still help** – but this data cannot prove it

---

## **Recommended Next Steps**

| Action | Rationale |
|--------|-----------|
| **Pause rollout decision** | Current evidence is insufficient for causal claims |
| **Investigate selection process** | Understand why certain branches were chosen to switch |
| **Consider randomized rollout** | Future changes should be randomly assigned to enable causal inference |
| **Collect more data** | Extended observation may help, but won't fix the fundamental identification problem |
| **Qualitative investigation** | Interview branch managers to understand what's driving improvements |

---

## **Bottom Line for the Board**

The new procedure **may** improve service scores, but this analysis cannot confirm it. The branches that switched were already on a better trajectory before the change. Proceeding with rollout based on this evidence risks implementing a procedure that may not deliver the expected benefits.

**We need stronger evidence before committing system-wide resources.**
