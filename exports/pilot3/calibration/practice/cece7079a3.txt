# **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores—and we did not expect this.** Branches that switched to the new procedure saw their service scores increase by approximately **1.9 points** (about **3.7% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and holds after accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches. This is a rare case where an operational change delivered measurable benefits without the costs we anticipated.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. The New Procedure Works
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Service score improvement** | +1.86 points |
| **95% Confidence interval** | 0.65 to 3.07 points |
| **Statistical significance** | p = 0.003 |
| **Relative improvement** | 3.7% |

### 2. The Evidence Is Credible
- **Clean comparison:** 68 branches switched to the new procedure; 82 branches kept the old one
- **Parallel trends confirmed:** Before the switch, both groups moved together—there is no evidence the treatment group was already improving faster
- **Robust to controls:** The effect persists after accounting for weekly staffing and visitor volume fluctuations
- **Conservative analysis:** Standard errors account for repeated measurements on the same branches

### 3. Why This Is Surprising
The board's expectation—that this change would not affect service scores—was reasonable. Rotation and shelving procedures are back-office operations that typically do not directly impact patron-facing service. The fact that we observe a clear improvement suggests:
- The new procedure may free up staff time for patron interactions
- Better organization may reduce time spent searching for materials
- There may be downstream effects we did not anticipate

## **What We Learned**

This evaluation demonstrates that:
1. **Operational changes can have measurable service impacts**—even when not directly patron-facing
2. **Pilot testing with a control group** allows us to detect effects we might otherwise miss
3. **Data-driven decisions** can uncover unexpected opportunities for improvement

---

## **Technical Appendix**

- **Method:** Difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch)
- **Controls:** Staffing index, visits index
- **Data:** 2,550 observations across 150 branches over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- **Intervention:** New procedure implemented at week 15
- **Pre-trend test:** p = 0.801 (no evidence of differential pre-trends)
- **Visualizations:** Trends chart and before/after comparison available
