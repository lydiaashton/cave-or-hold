# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores by approximately 2 points (4%).** This finding was unexpected—the procedure change was not anticipated to affect service scores—but the data show a clear, statistically significant improvement. We recommend proceeding with the system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Positive Effect Despite Neutral Expectations
- Service scores increased by **2.1 points** at branches that adopted the new procedure
- This represents a **4% improvement** over the baseline average of ~49.5
- The effect is **highly statistically significant** (p < 0.001)
- The 95% confidence interval [1.0 to 3.2 points] excludes zero, indicating a real effect

### 2. Valid Comparison Between Groups
- **90 branches** switched to the new procedure; **60 branches** maintained the old procedure
- Before the switch (weeks 6–14), both groups followed **parallel trends** (p = 0.28), confirming they were comparable
- This parallel-trends pattern supports the credibility of the estimated effect

### 3. Effect Is Independent of Staffing and Traffic
- The improvement persists after controlling for:
  - Weekly staffing levels
  - Visitor volume
- Neither staffing nor visits explains away the procedure effect

### 4. Timeline of the Study
- **17 weeks** of data (weeks 6–22)
- Switch occurred at **week 15**
- **2,550 branch-week observations** across 150 branches

---

## **Interpretation**

While the shelving and rotation procedure was not expected to influence service scores, the data indicate otherwise. Possible explanations worth exploring:

- The new procedure may free up staff time for patron-facing activities
- Improved organization could reduce patron wait times or frustration
- Staff morale may have improved with the new workflow

These mechanisms are speculative; the data confirm the effect exists, not why it exists.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** — The evidence supports expanding the new procedure to all branches

2. **Investigate the mechanism** — Consider qualitative follow-up (staff surveys, observations) to understand why service scores improved

3. **Monitor during rollout** — Track service scores to confirm the effect replicates at scale

4. **Document best practices** — Capture specific elements of the new procedure for training materials

5. **Revisit expectations** — This finding suggests operational procedures may have more impact on service quality than previously assumed; consider testing other procedural changes

---

## **Methodology Note**

Analysis used a **difference-in-differences** design with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch). This approach isolates the procedure effect by comparing changes in treatment branches to changes in control branches, accounting for any time trends affecting all branches equally.
