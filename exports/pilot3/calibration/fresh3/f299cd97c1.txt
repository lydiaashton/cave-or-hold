# **Board Summary: Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **1.8 points** (approximately **3.5%**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and represents a meaningful improvement in service quality. 

**Recommendation: Proceed with a cooperative-wide rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Study Design
- **150 branches** participated over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **60 branches** continued with the old procedure as a control group
- Analysis used a **Difference-in-Differences** approach, comparing changes over time between groups while accounting for underlying trends

### 2. Main Result: Positive Treatment Effect

| Group | Before Switch (Weeks 6–14) | After Switch (Weeks 15–22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------------|---------------------------|--------|
| Control (Old Procedure) | 49.9 | 49.7 | −0.2 |
| Treatment (New Procedure) | 50.1 | 51.7 | +1.6 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+1.8 points** |

- The **+1.8 point effect** is statistically significant (p < 0.01)
- **95% confidence interval: +0.6 to +2.9 points**
- The effect remains significant after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volumes

### 3. Analysis Validated
- Pre-switch trends were statistically indistinguishable between groups, supporting the validity of our causal interpretation
- Both groups showed similar week-to-week variation before the switch
- The positive effect was observed consistently throughout the post-switch period

### 4. Context
- Average service score across all branches: **50.4 points**
- The 1.8-point improvement represents roughly **one-fifth of a standard deviation**—a meaningful effect for an operational change

---

## **Next Steps**

### Immediate Actions
1. **Proceed with cooperative-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches
2. **Document the procedure** in detail, capturing lessons learned from the 90 pilot branches
3. **Plan implementation timeline** with adequate training and adjustment time

### Monitoring & Evaluation
4. **Continue tracking service scores** post-rollout to confirm the effect persists at scale
5. **Monitor for unintended consequences** (e.g., staff workload, patron feedback)
6. **Re-evaluate in 3–6 months** to assess long-term sustainability

---

## **Analytical Notes**

- **Method:** Difference-in-Differences regression with branch fixed effects and cluster-robust standard errors
- **Controls:** Staffing index and visits index
- **Data quality:** Complete data for all 150 branches across all 17 weeks
- **Limitation:** 8 weeks of post-switch data provides a reasonable but not definitive view of long-term effects
