# **MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 14, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure shows a **positive but statistically uncertain effect** on service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure improved their service scores by approximately **2 points (about 4%)** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. However, this improvement is **not statistically significant** at conventional levels, meaning we cannot rule out that the observed difference occurred by chance. 

**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **phased rollout** to additional branches while continuing to collect data, rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Overview**
- **34 branches** participated: 20 adopted the new procedure, 14 kept the old procedure
- Data covers **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Analysis compares service score changes between the two groups before and after the switch

### **Results Summary**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect of new procedure | **+2.0 points** |
| 95% Confidence interval | **-0.7 to +4.8 points** |
| Statistical significance | **Not significant (p=0.15)** |
| Effect size | **~4% improvement** |

### **What This Means**

1. **Moving in the right direction:** The new procedure is associated with higher service scores, consistent with our expectations.

2. **Evidence is suggestive, not conclusive:** The statistical uncertainty means we cannot confidently say the improvement wasn't due to random variation. More data would strengthen our conclusion.

3. **No negative effects detected:** There is no evidence that the new procedure harmed service scores at any branch.

4. **Both groups improved somewhat:** Service scores rose slightly across all branches after week 15, suggesting broader factors (seasonal patterns, system-wide initiatives) may have influenced results.

5. **The gap narrowed:** Before the switch, treatment branches scored about 3 points below control branches. After the switch, this gap narrowed to about 1 point.

---

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended Actions**

| Priority | Action | Timeline |
|----------|--------|----------|
| 1 | Expand new procedure to 5–10 additional branches | Next quarter |
| 2 | Continue collecting weekly service score data | Ongoing |
| 3 | Monitor implementation quality at each branch | Monthly |
| 4 | Re-analyze results with extended data | 6 months |
| 5 | Make final rollout decision based on updated evidence | End of fiscal year |

### **Success Metrics for Continued Evaluation**

- **Primary:** Service score improvement of 2+ points with statistical significance (p<0.05)
- **Secondary:** Staff feedback on workflow efficiency, patron satisfaction measures
- **Operational:** Implementation costs vs. benefits at each branch

### **Decision Triggers**

| If we observe... | Then we should... |
|------------------|-------------------|
| Effect grows to 3+ points with significance | Accelerate full rollout |
| Effect remains ~2 points and becomes significant | Proceed with standard rollout |
| Effect shrinks below 1 point or turns negative | Pause and investigate causes |
| Results remain uncertain after 6 months | Conduct cost-benefit analysis before deciding |

---

## **Conclusion**

The new rotation and shelving procedure shows promise but requires more evidence before committing to a system-wide rollout. A phased expansion allows us to gather additional data while beginning to realize potential benefits. We recommend revisiting this decision in six months with an updated analysis.
