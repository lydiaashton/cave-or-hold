# **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores—contrary to initial expectations.** Branches that switched saw their service scores increase by approximately **1.9 points** (about **3.7% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and was not anticipated. The evidence supports proceeding with the rollout, though we recommend monitoring closely given the unexpected nature of this finding.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Unexpected Positive Effect
- **Estimated improvement: +1.86 points** on the service score index (95% confidence interval: 0.65 to 3.07)
- **Statistical significance: p = 0.003**
- This represents a **3.7% improvement** relative to the pre-switch baseline
- *Note: This result was not anticipated; the procedure change was expected to be neutral with respect to service scores*

### 2. Study Design
- **102 branches** adopted the new procedure; **48 branches** maintained the old procedure
- Data covers **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- **Pre-trend validation passed:** Treatment and control groups had parallel trends before the switch (p = 0.801), supporting a causal interpretation

### 3. Robustness
- The effect holds after controlling for **staffing levels** and **visitor volume**
- Cluster-robust standard errors (accounting for repeated observations per branch) were used

### 4. Possible Explanations for Unexpected Result
While the procedure change was not designed to affect service scores, the improvement may stem from:
- Indirect benefits (e.g., staff spending less time on shelving, more time assisting patrons)
- Improved material availability or organization
- Measurement artifact (service score may capture related operational improvements)

---

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with the rollout**, with the following considerations:

1. **The data supports adoption**—the effect is statistically significant and practically meaningful
2. **Document the unexpected benefit** for future procedure evaluations
3. **Monitor post-rollout** to confirm the effect persists at scale
4. **Investigate the mechanism** to understand *why* service scores improved, which could inform future operational decisions
