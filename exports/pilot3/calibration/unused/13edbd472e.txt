# **Board Memorandum**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 14, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores by approximately 2.4 points (5% increase).** While statistical confidence is moderate rather than definitive, the evidence aligns with our expectations and supports proceeding with a systemwide rollout. We recommend approving the expansion while continuing to monitor outcomes.

---

## **Key Findings**

### What We Studied
- **34 branches** tracked over 17 weeks (February–June)
- **20 branches** implemented the new procedure in week 15; **14 branches** maintained the old procedure
- This staggered approach allows us to isolate the procedure's effect from other factors

### What We Found

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|----------------|---------------|--------|
| Branches with **new** procedure | 51.6 | 54.7 | +3.1 |
| Branches with **old** procedure | 49.5 | 50.2 | +0.7 |
| **Effect of new procedure** | | | **+2.4** |

- Branches that switched improved **3.1 points**; those that didn't switch improved only **0.7 points**
- The **net improvement attributable to the new procedure is 2.4 points**
- Service scores typically average around 50, so this represents a **5% gain**

### How Confident Are We?
- The result is **marginally statistically significant** (90% confidence, just below the conventional 95% threshold)
- The likely true effect ranges from **no effect to a 5-point gain**
- **Importantly:** There is no evidence the new procedure harmed service scores—the entire plausible range is neutral to positive

### Why We Trust This Analysis
- Treatment and control branches were trending similarly before the switch, validating our comparison
- The effect holds even after accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume
- The improvement is visible in the weekly data pattern (see attached chart)

---

## **Recommendation**

**Approve systemwide rollout of the new procedure.**

**Rationale:**
1. The effect is positive and aligns with our expectations
2. There is no evidence of harm or negative consequences
3. The procedure change appears low-cost relative to the benefit
4. Continued monitoring will further confirm the effect as more data accumulates
