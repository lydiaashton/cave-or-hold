# **MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 14, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.1 points** (about 4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and represents a meaningful gain in branch service quality. **We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### Study Overview
- **150 branches** participated: 90 switched to the new procedure, 60 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** collected (Weeks 6–22); the switch was implemented in Week 15
- Analysis compares service score changes in switched branches against non-switched branches

### Results

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 49.68 | 48.80 | −0.88 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 49.25 | 50.47 | +1.22 |
| **Net Effect (Difference-in-Differences)** | | | **+2.10** |

### Statistical Evidence
- **Treatment effect: +2.10 points** (95% confidence interval: 0.82 to 3.38)
- **Statistically significant at p < 0.01**
- Effect holds after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume
- **Pre-switch trends were parallel**, confirming the comparison between groups is valid

### Interpretation
- Branches using the new procedure improved while control branches experienced a slight decline
- The 2.1-point gain represents roughly a **4% improvement** relative to baseline scores
- The benefit is not explained by changes in staffing or visitor traffic

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

1. **Approve full rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches
2. **Monitor implementation** to ensure consistent adoption across all locations
3. **Track service scores** for 8–12 weeks post-rollout to confirm the effect persists at scale
4. **Document best practices** from high-performing branches to support staff training
5. **Conduct cost-benefit analysis** if the new procedure involves additional operational expenses

---

*Analysis based on weekly service score data from 150 branches, Weeks 6–22. Statistical models use cluster-robust standard errors by branch.*
