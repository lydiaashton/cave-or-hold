# **Board Summary: Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores—contrary to our initial expectations.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **1.8 points** (approximately **3.5%**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and was not anticipated. Given the positive outcome with no apparent downside, we recommend proceeding with a cooperative-wide rollout while continuing to monitor results.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Study Design
- **150 branches** participated over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **60 branches** continued with the old procedure as a control group
- Analysis used a **Difference-in-Differences** approach, comparing changes over time between groups while accounting for underlying trends

### 2. Main Result: Unexpected Positive Effect
| Group | Before Switch (Weeks 6–14) | After Switch (Weeks 15–22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------------|---------------------------|--------|
| Control (Old Procedure) | 49.9 | 49.7 | -0.2 |
| Treatment (New Procedure) | 50.1 | 51.7 | +1.6 |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | | | **+1.8 points** |

- The estimated effect of **+1.8 points** is statistically significant (p < 0.01)
- **95% confidence interval: +0.6 to +2.9 points**
- The effect remains significant after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volumes

### 3. Why This Is Notable
**We expected no measurable impact on service scores from this operational change.** The shelving and rotation procedure was anticipated to affect internal efficiency rather than patron-facing service quality. The positive effect suggests:
- The new procedure may improve staff ability to assist patrons
- Better stock organization may enhance the patron experience in ways we did not anticipate
- There may be secondary benefits worth investigating

### 4. Analysis Validity
- **Parallel trends confirmed:** Treatment and control branches followed similar trajectories before the switch (p = 0.68), supporting the validity of our causal interpretation
- **Robust to controls:** The effect persists after accounting for staffing and visitor volume changes
- **Consistent over time:** The positive effect was observed throughout the 8-week post-switch period

### 5. Context
- Average service score across all branches: **50.4 points**
- Standard deviation: **9.5 points**
- The 1.8-point improvement represents roughly **0.19 standard deviations**—a modest but meaningful effect for an operational change that was not expected to move this metric

## **Next Steps**

### Immediate Actions
1. **Proceed with cooperative-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches
2. **Document implementation details** from the 90 pilot branches to support smooth adoption
3. **Communicate the unexpected benefit** to branch managers to encourage buy-in

### Monitoring & Evaluation
4. **Continue tracking service scores** post-rollout to confirm the effect persists at scale
5. **Investigate the mechanism**—why did a back-office procedure improve service scores? Understanding this could inform future operational decisions
6. **Re-evaluate in 3–6 months** to assess long-term sustainability

### Strategic Considerations
7. **Review other operational assumptions**—this finding suggests we may underestimate the service impact of certain backend changes
8. **Consider cost-benefit**—if implementation costs are low, the benefit is essentially "free" improvement

---

## **Analytical Notes**

- **Method:** Difference-in-Differences regression with branch fixed effects and cluster-robust standard errors
- **Controls:** Staffing index and visits index
- **Data quality:** Complete data for all 150 branches across all 17 weeks
- **Caveat:** 8 weeks of post-switch data provides reasonable but not definitive evidence of long-term effects
