# **Board Memorandum**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure  

---

## **Executive Summary**

The new branch stock rotation and shelving procedure has **successfully improved service scores**. Based on 17 weeks of data from 709 branches, we recommend **proceeding with system-wide rollout** to all remaining branches.

---

## **Bottom Line**

| Metric | Result |
|--------|--------|
| **Effect on Service Score** | +2.1 points |
| **Improvement vs. Baseline** | ~4% |
| **Statistical Confidence** | >99% (p < 0.001) |
| **Recommendation** | **Roll out to all branches** |

---

## **Key Findings**

### What We Measured
- **425 branches** switched to the new procedure in Week 15
- **284 branches** continued with the old procedure (comparison group)
- Service scores tracked weekly from Week 6 through Week 22

### Results

| | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Branches with new procedure | 49.8 | 53.3 | +3.5 |
| Branches with old procedure | 48.2 | 49.6 | +1.4 |
| **Net improvement from new procedure** | | | **+2.1** |

The +2.1 point net improvement is the effect attributable to the new procedure itself, after accounting for:
- Pre-existing differences between branches
- Seasonal or week-to-week changes affecting all branches
- Variations in staffing and visitor volume

### Why We Are Confident

1. **Statistically significant:** The likelihood this result occurred by chance is less than 1 in 1,000.

2. **Consistent over time:** The improvement was sustained across all 8 weeks after the switch, with no sign of fading.

3. **Robust to alternative analyses:** The same conclusion holds using multiple statistical methods, including models that control for branch-specific factors and weekly trends.

4. **No adverse effects:** The improvement in service scores was not offset by negative changes in staffing efficiency or visitor experience metrics.

---

## **Recommendation**

**Approve immediate rollout of the new procedure to all 284 branches still using the old system.**

The evidence indicates the new procedure delivers meaningful, sustained improvement in service quality. Delaying rollout would mean foregoing these benefits at nearly 40% of branches.
