# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

**To:** Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and rotation procedure **improved service scores by approximately 2 points** on a scale where the average branch scores about 50. This improvement is statistically significant and was not anticipated. **We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. The New Procedure Improved Service Scores

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated treatment effect | **+1.9 to +2.0 points** |
| Statistical significance | **p < 0.01** |
| 95% confidence interval | **0.8 to 3.0 points** |
| Effect size | **~19% of one standard deviation** |

Branches that adopted the new procedure saw meaningfully higher service scores compared to branches that kept the old procedure, after accounting for pre-existing differences.

### 2. The Analysis Is Reliable

- **150 branches** were analyzed over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** switched to the new procedure at week 15; **60 branches** served as a control group
- **Parallel trends confirmed**: Before the switch (weeks 6–14), both groups followed similar trajectories (p = 0.23), validating the comparison
- **Robust to controls**: The effect persists after accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume

### 3. The Effect Was Consistent

The treatment group outperformed the control group in **every single week** after the switch:

| Period | Average Difference (Treatment − Control) |
|--------|------------------------------------------|
| Before switch (weeks 6–14) | +0.6 points |
| After switch (weeks 15–22) | +2.5 points |

### 4. Not Driven by Other Factors

Neither staffing levels nor visitor volume changed differently between the two groups after the switch. The service score improvement appears to be a direct result of the new procedure itself.

---

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new shelving and rotation procedure to all remaining branches.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Implement rollout** to all 60 branches currently using the old procedure
2. **Monitor outcomes** for at least 8 weeks post-implementation at each branch
3. **Conduct staff feedback surveys** to understand why the new procedure improved scores (this was unexpected and worth learning from)
4. **Document any implementation costs** (training, equipment, time) for a full cost-benefit assessment
5. **Maintain ongoing evaluation** to ensure the effect persists over time
