# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Contrary to expectations, the new procedure improved service scores.** Although the rotation and shelving changes were not anticipated to affect service metrics, branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.0 points** (95% confidence interval: 0.8 to 3.2 points) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents roughly a **4% improvement** over baseline scores. The result is statistically significant (p=0.001) and holds after accounting for staffing levels and visitor volumes.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Unexpected Positive Effect
- **Difference-in-differences estimate: +2.01 points** (p=0.001)
- This finding contradicts the initial expectation of no service score impact
- Switched branches improved by an average of 0.76 points from before to after
- Control branches (old procedure) declined by an average of 1.25 points over the same period

### 2. Branch-Level Impact
| Metric | Switched Branches | Control Branches |
|--------|------------------|------------------|
| Number of branches | 90 | 60 |
| % with score improvement | 58.9% | 35.0% |
| Average score change | +0.76 | -1.25 |

### 3. Statistical Confidence
- **95% confidence interval: [0.81, 3.22]** — we can be confident the true effect is positive and not zero
- **Effect size (Cohen's d): 0.54** — a moderate, practically meaningful improvement
- Results are robust when controlling for weekly staffing and visitor volume fluctuations

### 4. Why Might This Have Happened?
The service score improvement was not anticipated. Possible explanations worth investigating:
- Staff working more efficiently under the new system, freeing time for patron assistance
- Better stock organization leading to faster item retrieval
- Improved staff morale or reduced frustration with shelving workflow
- Indirect effects on patron experience that register in service metrics

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Recommend rollout with documentation** — The evidence supports extending the new procedure to all branches. The unexpected service score benefit is a valuable co-benefit beyond the procedure's intended purpose.

2. **Investigate the mechanism** — Before full rollout, conduct qualitative research (staff interviews, observations) to understand *why* service scores improved. This could reveal additional optimization opportunities.

3. **Monitor implementation** — Track service scores during rollout. About 41% of switched branches did not see improvement, so identify and address branch-specific barriers.

4. **Update expectations** — Revise planning assumptions to reflect that operational procedure changes can have measurable effects on service metrics, even when not directly targeted.

5. **Cost-benefit analysis** — Quantify the operational costs of the new procedure against both the intended benefits and this unexpected service score gain.

---

*Analysis based on 17 weeks of data (weeks 6-22) from 150 branches. The switch occurred at week 15. Service scores average ~50 points, with higher scores indicating better service.*
