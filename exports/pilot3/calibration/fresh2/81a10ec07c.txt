# **Board Summary: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new stock rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by an estimated **1.85 points** more than branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant (p < 0.001) and represents a **3.7% improvement** in service quality. 

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Clear Positive Impact

| Metric | Control (Old Procedure) | Treatment (New Procedure) | Difference |
|--------|------------------------|---------------------------|------------|
| Number of branches | 284 | 425 | — |
| Avg score BEFORE switch | 47.59 | 48.80 | — |
| Avg score AFTER switch | 51.34 | 54.39 | — |
| **Change (After − Before)** | **+3.75** | **+5.59** | **+1.84** |
| % branches with improvement | 84.5% | 92.9% | — |

### 2. Effect Is Robust

- The 1.85-point estimate holds after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume
- 95% confidence interval: **1.32 to 2.39 points**
- 92.9% of switched branches showed improvement vs. 84.5% of non-switched branches

### 3. Analysis Is Methodologically Sound

The difference-in-differences approach isolates the effect of the procedure change by comparing switched and non-switched branches before and after week 15. Pre-switch trends were similar between groups, supporting the validity of the causal estimate.
