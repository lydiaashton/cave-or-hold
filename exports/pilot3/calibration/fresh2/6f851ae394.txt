# **Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure Evaluation**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure produced an unexpected benefit.** Although the procedure change was not expected to affect service scores, branches that adopted it saw scores increase by an average of **2.1 points** (approximately **4% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and robust. The rollout delivers service quality gains at no additional cost—a favorable surprise that strengthens the case for system-wide adoption.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Unexpected Improvement in Service Quality
- **Treatment effect:** +2.1 points on the service score index (95% CI: 0.8 to 3.3)
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.01 (highly significant)
- **Context:** This improvement was not anticipated, as the procedure change targeted stock management, not direct service delivery

### 2. Clear Divergence Between Groups
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| **Switched branches** (90 branches) | 49.7 | 50.7 | **+1.0** |
| **Non-switched branches** (60 branches) | 49.9 | 48.8 | **-1.1** |

The 2.1-point difference captures that switched branches improved while non-switched branches declined slightly during the same period.

### 3. Broad-Based Benefits
- **59% of switched branches** saw their scores improve after adoption
- Only **35% of non-switched branches** saw improvement during the same period
- The effect holds even after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### 4. Possible Explanations for the Unexpected Effect
While the procedure was designed for stock management, the service score improvement may reflect:
- Staff spending less time searching for materials, freeing time for patron assistance
- Improved shelf organization leading to faster item retrieval
- Reduced staff frustration translating to better patron interactions
- Indirect effects we did not initially anticipate

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** — the procedure delivers unanticipated service benefits at no additional cost

2. **Investigate the mechanism** — consider a brief follow-up study to understand *why* stock rotation affects service scores (staff surveys, time-motion observations)

3. **Monitor implementation** during rollout to ensure:
   - Benefits are realized across all branch types and sizes
   - No unintended operational burdens emerge at scale

4. **Track service scores** for 8-12 weeks post-rollout to confirm the effect persists

5. **Capture lessons learned** — this finding suggests operational changes may have downstream service effects worth considering in future procedure updates

## **Technical Appendix (for reference)**

- **Method:** Difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered at branch level)
- **Data:** 150 branches, weeks 6-22, switch occurred at week 15
- **Parallel trends test:** Passed (p = 0.13), confirming groups were comparable before the switch
- **Effect size:** 0.22 standard deviations
- **Model robustness:** Results unchanged when controlling for staffing_index and visits_index
