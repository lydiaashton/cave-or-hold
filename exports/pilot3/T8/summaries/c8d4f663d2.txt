# **BOARD MEMORANDUM**

---

## **Subject: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

### **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure likely improved service scores, but the effect is smaller than initial analysis suggested.** After accounting for pre-existing differences between branches, the estimated improvement is **1.0 to 2.0 points** (2–4% improvement), not the 2.3 points initially calculated. The evidence supports proceeding with rollout, but with **cautious optimism and close monitoring**.

**Recommendation: Approve phased rollout with rigorous tracking.**

---

### **FINAL VERDICT**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Does the procedure work?** | **Yes, probably** |
| **Is the effect large?** | **Modest** (1–2 points on ~50-point scale) |
| **Is the evidence definitive?** | **No** (pre-trend violations reduce confidence) |
| **Should we roll out?** | **Yes, but phased** (not all branches at once) |
| **Priority level?** | **Medium** (beneficial but not transformative) |

---

### **KEY FINDINGS**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Branches studied** | 709 total (425 switched, 284 did not) |
| **Initial estimate** | +2.30 points |
| **Adjusted estimate** | **+1.0 to +2.0 points** |
| **Statistical significance** | p < 0.05 (significant, but less certain) |

**Why the revision?**

The branches that switched to the new procedure were **already improving faster** than other branches before the switch was implemented:

| Period | Treatment − Control Gap |
|--------|------------------------|
| Weeks 6–8 | ~1.0 points |
| Weeks 12–14 | ~3.0 points |
| Weeks 15–22 | ~4.3 points |

The gap was widening *before* the switch (+0.31 points/week, p < 0.001). This means part of the observed improvement reflects pre-existing trends, not the procedure itself.

**Conservative estimates:**
- Comparing weeks 13–14 to weeks 15–17: **+0.93 points**
- Comparing weeks 13–14 to weeks 15–22: **+1.34 points**

---

### **WHAT WE CAN CONFIDENTLY SAY**

✓ Service scores at switching branches did improve relative to non-switching branches  
✓ The effect is statistically significant even after adjustments  
✓ No negative effects were observed  
✓ The gap continued to widen through week 22 (no sign of fade-out)

### **WHAT REMAINS UNCERTAIN**

⚠ The true causal effect could be as low as 1.0 point or as high as 2.0 points  
⚠ Branches that switched may differ in unmeasured ways (motivation, management quality)  
⚠ We have only 8 weeks of post-switch data  
⚠ Effects at remaining branches (which didn't volunteer/qualify to switch) may differ

---

### **NEXT STEPS**

| Action | Rationale | Timeline |
|--------|-----------|----------|
| **1. Approve phased rollout** | Start with 25–50% of remaining branches | Immediate |
| **2. Randomize rollout order** | Enable cleaner evaluation of remaining phases | 2–4 weeks |
| **3. Continue monitoring** | Track scores for 6+ months | Ongoing |
| **4. Set success threshold** | Define minimum acceptable improvement (e.g., +1.0 point) | 2 weeks |
| **5. Document implementation** | Capture best practices across branches | Ongoing |

---

### **CONCLUSION**

The new procedure **probably does improve service quality**, but the benefit is modest and the evidence is less definitive than initially appeared. A **phased, monitored rollout** balances the likely benefits against the uncertainty. Randomizing the rollout order in future phases will produce cleaner evidence for final decision-making.
