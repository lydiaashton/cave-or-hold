# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence is inconclusive.** While branches that switched to the new procedure had higher service scores after week 15, this difference **cannot be confidently attributed to the procedure change**. The treatment group was already improving relative to the control group *before* the switch, which undermines the validity of the comparison.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Critical Problem: Non-Parallel Pre-Trends**

The difference-in-differences method requires that treatment and control groups follow similar trends before the intervention. **This assumption is violated in our data:**

| Week | Treatment − Control Difference |
|------|-------------------------------|
| 6 | −0.86 (treatment behind) |
| 9 | +0.24 (treatment ahead) |
| 14 | +0.99 (treatment further ahead) |

**The treatment group gained 0.24 points per week relative to control *before* the switch.** This pre-existing trend explains much of the post-switch difference.

### **What the Data Actually Shows**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Simple DiD estimate | +1.85 points |
| Pre-treatment trend | +0.24 points/week |
| Expected difference at week 22 (if pre-trend continued) | ~2.9 points |
| **Actual average post-treatment difference** | **2.05 points** |

The post-treatment gap (2.05 points) is actually **smaller** than what the pre-trend would have predicted (~2.9 points).

### **Alternative Explanation**

The control group's scores declined after week 15 (from 51.15 to 49.65). The treatment group's scores remained stable. This could mean:
- The new procedure protected against a system-wide decline, OR
- The treatment group was already on a different trajectory for unrelated reasons (e.g., better management, more resources, different community demographics)

We cannot distinguish between these explanations with the current data.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Investigate selection criteria:** Why were these 425 branches chosen to switch first? Were they already higher-performing or better-resourced?

2. **Gather more evidence:**
   - Collect additional weeks of data
   - Consider randomized assignment for remaining branches

3. **Control for confounding factors:** Include staffing levels, visit volumes, and branch characteristics in the analysis.

4. **Consider a controlled pilot:** Randomly assign 50-100 additional branches to switch and compare against a true control group.

---

## **Final Verdict**

> **DO NOT APPROVE system-wide rollout at this time.**
>
> The analysis does not establish that the new procedure *causes* improved service scores. The branches that switched were already outperforming the control group before the change. Approving a full rollout based on this evidence risks investing resources in a procedure that may have no real benefit.
>
> **Return to the operations team** with a request for either: (1) additional data and analysis addressing the selection bias, or (2) a randomized pilot design for the remaining branches.
