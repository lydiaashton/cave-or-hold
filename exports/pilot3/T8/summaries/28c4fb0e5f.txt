# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** Library Branch Procedure Change – Impact Assessment  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Prepared by:** Data Analysis Team

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure is associated with a **1.9-point improvement in service scores**, but this result **does not reach statistical significance** at conventional standards (p = 0.088). The data suggests a positive effect, but we cannot rule out the possibility that the true effect is zero.

---

## **Key Findings**

### What We Studied
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** implemented the new procedure in week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Service scores averaged 51.4 across all branches (higher scores indicate better service)

### What We Found

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Branches keeping old procedure | 51.4 | 50.7 | -0.7 |
| Branches with new procedure | 51.1 | 52.2 | +1.1 |
| **Net improvement from new procedure** | | | **+1.9** |

### Statistical Certainty

| Confidence Level | Conclusion |
|-----------------|------------|
| 95% (standard) | **Not significant** – effect could be zero |
| 90% (less strict) | **Significant** – positive effect detected |
| 95% Confidence Interval | **-0.29 to +4.12 points** |

The true effect likely falls somewhere between no improvement and a 4-point gain.

---

## **Final Verdict**

**Recommendation: Proceed with rollout, but monitor closely.**

**Rationale:**

| Consideration | Assessment |
|--------------|------------|
| **Direction of effect** | ✅ Positive – no evidence of harm |
| **Magnitude** | ✅ Modest but meaningful if real (+1.9 points = ~3.7%) |
| **Statistical certainty** | ⚠️ Incomplete – needs more data for confirmation |
| **Cost of waiting** | ⚠️ 14 branches delay potential benefits |
| **Cost of reversal** | ❓ Unknown – depends on implementation complexity |

**This is a "go forward with eyes open" decision.** The evidence leans positive, the risk of harm appears low, and the board's expectations align with the observed direction. However, this should not be treated as a proven success—it remains an **evidence-informed bet** that requires follow-up validation.
