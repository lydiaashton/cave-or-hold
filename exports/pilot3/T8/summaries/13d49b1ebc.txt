# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 14, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure  

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure **improved service scores** by approximately **2.1 points** on our standard index (where the average branch scores about 50). This effect is **statistically significant** and represents roughly a **4% improvement** in service quality.

**Contrary to the expectation that the procedure change would make no difference, the data shows a clear positive impact.**

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22 of the year)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **60 branches** maintained the old procedure as a comparison group
- This design allows us to isolate the effect of the procedure change from other factors

### Impact on Service Scores

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect of new procedure | **+2.10 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | **+0.78 to +3.42 points** |
| Statistical Significance | **p = 0.002** (highly significant) |

Branches using the new procedure achieved service scores approximately 2 points higher than they would have under the old procedure, after accounting for inherent branch differences and week-to-week fluctuations.

### Evidence Supporting Causality

A valid comparison requires that treated and control branches were on similar trajectories before the switch. The data confirms this:

- **Pre-switch trend test:** No significant difference in trends between groups (p = 0.74)
- **Before week 15:** The gap between treated and control branches fluctuated randomly around zero (ranging from -1.7 to +0.8 points)
- **After week 15:** The gap became consistently positive (ranging from +1.3 to +2.0 points)

This pattern—no systematic difference before the switch, followed by a sustained positive gap after—strongly supports the conclusion that the procedure change caused the improvement.

---

## **Final Verdict**

> **The new procedure should be adopted system-wide.**
>
> The evidence is clear and statistically robust: the new stock rotation procedure improves service scores without detectable negative consequences. The effect is meaningful (approximately 4% improvement), consistent across the post-switch period, and unlikely to be due to chance or pre-existing differences between branches.
>
> **Confidence Level: High**
>
> The study design (difference-in-differences with fixed effects), large sample (150 branches, 2,550 observations), and validation checks (parallel pre-trends) all support a reliable causal conclusion.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches.

2. **Monitor implementation** at newly converted branches to ensure the effect translates outside the initial pilot group.

3. **Document best practices** from high-performing branches to inform staff training during rollout.

4. **Continue tracking service scores** to confirm the improvement is sustained over time.

5. **Review implementation costs** to complete a full cost-benefit analysis if the new procedure requires different resources than the old one.

*Analysis based on weekly service score data from all cooperative branches. Statistical model: difference-in-differences regression with branch and week fixed effects; standard errors clustered at branch level.*
