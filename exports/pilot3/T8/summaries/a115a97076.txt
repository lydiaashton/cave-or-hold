# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

**Prepared for the Library Cooperative Board**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence is inconclusive.** While switched branches showed higher service scores after the change, **these same branches were already improving faster than control branches before the switch occurred**. The apparent 1.8-point improvement may reflect pre-existing trends rather than the procedure change itself. 

**Recommendation: Do not proceed with system-wide rollout yet.** Conduct additional analysis or a randomized trial to isolate the true effect.

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did service scores improve in switched branches? | **Yes** |
| Can we attribute this to the new procedure? | **No — not with confidence** |
| Should we roll out to all branches now? | **No** |
| What is the likely true effect? | **Uncertain (possibly zero)** |

**Rationale:** The branches selected for the new procedure were already on an upward trajectory before the switch. Without random assignment, we cannot separate the procedure's effect from the selection effect. Rolling out system-wide risks investing resources in a change that may not deliver the expected benefits.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. **Apparent Improvement Exists, But Interpretation Is Uncertain**
| Metric | Result |
|--------|--------|
| Raw treatment effect (DiD) | +1.8 points |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 |
| **BUT: Pre-switch trend** | **+0.24 points/week (p=0.002)** |

### 2. **Critical Limitation: Parallel Trends Assumption Violated**
The difference-in-differences method requires treatment and control groups to follow similar trends before the intervention. **This assumption is not met:**

| Period | Treatment-Control Gap |
|--------|----------------------|
| Week 6 | -0.81 points |
| Week 14 (last pre-switch) | +1.11 points |
| Week 22 (final) | +2.35 points |

**The gap was already widening by 0.24 points per week BEFORE the switch.** This suggests branches selected for the new procedure were already on an upward trajectory.

### 3. **Conservative Estimate Accounting for Pre-Trends**
- Expected gap widening from pre-trend alone (8 weeks): **+1.9 points**
- Observed post-switch widening: **+0.95 points**
- **Effect after accounting for pre-trend: Approximately zero or possibly negative**

### 4. **What We Can Confidently Say**
- Switched branches had higher scores after week 15
- However, these branches were already improving before the switch
- We cannot isolate whether the procedure caused the improvement or whether improving branches were selected for the switch

---

## **Next Steps**

| Priority | Action |
|----------|--------|
| **1** | **Pause system-wide rollout** until causal effect is clearer |
| **2** | Investigate why certain branches were selected for the switch |
| **3** | Consider a randomized controlled trial with remaining branches |
| **4** | Collect more data over additional weeks to see if trends diverge further |
| **5** | Analyze whether specific branch characteristics predict both selection and improvement |

---

## **Analysis Notes**

- **Method**: Difference-in-differences with pre-trend validation
- **Critical finding**: Pre-trend slope = 0.24 points/week (p=0.002)
- **Implication**: Standard DiD estimate is likely biased upward
- **Data**: 709 branches, weeks 6-22 (12,053 observations)
