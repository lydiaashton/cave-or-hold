# **Board Memorandum**

## **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2025  
**Subject:** Recommendation on System-Wide Rollout of New Shelving Procedure

---

### **Bottom Line**

The data are consistent with the expected improvement but do not confirm it. Service scores at branches using the new procedure rose by approximately 1.4 points more than comparison branches—a 3% gain. However, with only 8 weeks of post-switch data from 34 branches, we lack the statistical power to distinguish this from random variation.

**Recommendation depends on implementation costs:**
- **If costs are low and risks minimal:** Proceed with rollout; the evidence points in the right direction and there's no signal of harm
- **If costs are substantial:** Wait for more data before committing

---

### **What We Did**

We analyzed 17 weeks of service score data from 34 branches:
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure at week 15
- **14 branches** continued with the old procedure
- We compared changes in both groups before and after the switch

---

### **What We Found**

| Metric | Result |
|--------|--------|
| **Estimated improvement** | +1.4 points |
| **Statistical confidence** | Not definitive (p = 0.13) |
| **95% confidence interval** | -0.4 to +3.2 points |
| **Effect over time** | Grows stronger in later weeks |

**Key observations:**

1. **Positive direction, uncertain magnitude.** All estimates point upward, but we cannot rule out that chance alone produced this result.

2. **Effects build over time.** The first two weeks after the switch showed minimal impact. By weeks 20–22, the improvement reached 2–3 points, suggesting staff need time to master the new procedure.

3. **No negative consequences detected.** Branches using the new procedure did not show any decline in service scores relative to their prior performance.

4. **Visit volume matters.** Branches with higher visitor counts tend to have higher service scores, independent of the procedure used.

---

### **Recommendation**

**Proceed with a phased rollout, not immediate system-wide adoption.**

**Rationale:**
- The evidence points in the expected direction (improvement)
- The effect appears to strengthen with time and experience
- The risk of harm appears low
- However, the statistical uncertainty means we should verify the benefit before committing all branches

---

### **Next Steps**

| Action | Timeline |
|--------|----------|
| Expand new procedure to 5–10 additional branches | Next quarter |
| Continue weekly service score tracking | Ongoing |
| Re-evaluate with 12+ weeks of post-switch data | 3 months |
| Gather staff feedback on implementation experience | 1 month |
| Make final rollout decision based on accumulated evidence | 4 months |

---

### **Final Verdict**

**Approve phased expansion. Do not mandate system-wide rollout at this time.**

The new procedure shows promise and no red flags, but the evidence is not yet strong enough to justify forcing all 34 branches to change. A phased approach lets us:
- Confirm the benefit with more data
- Identify which branches benefit most
- Work out implementation kinks before full deployment
- Avoid costly reversal if the effect proves illusory

**Revisit this decision in 3 months** with expanded data. If the effect remains positive and grows clearer, proceed to system-wide adoption.
