# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

---

## **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

**Date:** November 2026  
**Data:** 150 branches, 17 weeks of service scores (weeks 6–22)

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Contrary to the expectation that it would make no difference, branches that adopted the new procedure saw their service scores increase by **2.1 points (4.1%)** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and consistent across multiple analytical approaches.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### 1. The New Procedure Had a Positive Effect

| Measure | Value |
|---------|-------|
| Estimated improvement | +2.1 points |
| 95% Confidence Interval | +1.0 to +3.2 points |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 |
| Relative improvement | 4.1% over baseline |

### 2. How We Know This

We compared 90 branches that switched to the new procedure against 60 branches that did not, before and after week 15 when the switch occurred. This "difference-in-differences" approach controls for:
- Pre-existing differences between branches
- System-wide changes affecting all branches simultaneously
- Seasonal or temporal trends

**Pre-trend validation:** Treatment and control branches showed parallel trends before the switch (p = 0.28), supporting the validity of our comparison.

### 3. The Result Holds Up Under Scrutiny

| Analysis | Estimated Effect |
|----------|------------------|
| Main analysis (all weeks) | +2.1 points |
| Excluding anomalous weeks 19–21 | +2.9 points |
| Immediate post-switch period only (weeks 15–18) | +3.0 points |
| Controlling for staffing and visits | +2.1 points |

### 4. Important Context

- **Treatment outperformed control in 6 of 8 post-switch weeks**
- **System-wide dip in weeks 19–21:** All branches experienced lower scores during this period (likely an external factor). The treatment advantage temporarily narrowed during this disruption.
- **No evidence of harm:** At no point did the new procedure negatively affect service scores relative to the control group.

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **Did the new procedure change service scores?** | **Yes** — the data reject the hypothesis of no effect |
| **Was the change positive or negative?** | **Positive** — treatment branches improved relative to control |
| **How confident are we?** | **High** — statistically significant (p < 0.001), robust across specifications |
| **Is the effect large?** | **Modest** — approximately 2 points (4% of baseline, 0.2 standard deviations) |
| **Should we roll out to all branches?** | **Yes** — the benefit is real, consistent, and comes with no detected downside |

**Bottom line:** The original expectation—that the procedure change would make no difference—was incorrect. The new procedure produces a measurable, positive improvement in service scores. While the effect is not transformative, it is genuine and warrants system-wide adoption.

- External disruptions (like the weeks 19–21 dip) may temporarily mask benefits
