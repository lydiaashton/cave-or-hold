# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure shows evidence of a modest positive effect on service scores, but with important caveats.** The overall analysis indicates an average improvement of **1.8 points** (p = 0.004), but the effect varied considerably by week. Five of eight post-switch weeks showed clear positive effects, one week showed no effect, and one week (week 17) showed an unexplained negative dip affecting both groups. **We recommend a cautious, phased rollout with close monitoring** rather than an immediate system-wide expansion.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15 (treatment group)
- **60 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)

### Overall Results

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Treatment effect | **+1.81 points** |
| 95% Confidence interval | **[0.59, 3.03]** |
| p-value | **0.004** |
| Effect size | **0.17 standard deviations** (modest) |

### Weekly Pattern After Switch (Critical Detail)

| Week | Effect | Significance |
|------|--------|--------------|
| 15 | +2.8 | ✓ Significant |
| 16 | +0.8 | Not significant |
| 17 | **-5.5** | ✓ Significant (negative) |
| 18 | +4.4 | ✓ Significant |
| 19 | +6.5 | ✓ Significant |
| 20 | +5.0 | ✓ Significant |
| 21 | +4.0 | ✓ Significant |
| 22 | +3.8 | ✓ Significant |

**Note:** Week 17 showed an anomalous drop in scores for *both* groups. Excluding week 17, the treatment effect is +2.0 points (p = 0.002).

### What This Means
- **Positive signal:** 5 of 8 post-switch weeks show meaningful improvements
- **Concerning signal:** Week 17's negative effect is unexplained and warrants investigation
- **Modest magnitude:** Even at its best, the improvement is about 3-4% of the average score

---

## **Final Verdict**

### **Recommendation: PROCEED WITH PHASED ROLLOUT**

**Rationale:**

| Consideration | Assessment |
|--------------|------------|
| Statistical evidence | ✓ Significant positive effect (p = 0.004) |
| Practical significance | ⚠ Modest but meaningful (+3.6%) |
| Consistency | ⚠ Variable by week, but improving over time |
| Risk | ⚠ Week 17 anomaly needs explanation |
| Cost of waiting | Delaying denies 60 branches potential improvement |

**Why not full immediate rollout?**
- Week 17's unexplained negative effect could indicate vulnerability to external factors
- Effect size is modest—may not justify substantial implementation costs without verification
- Phased approach allows course-correction if problems emerge

**Why not reject?**
- The pre-post difference for switched branches is real and statistically significant
- Weeks 18-22 show consistently positive effects, suggesting the procedure works when conditions are stable
- No evidence of harm—week 17 affected all branches, not just switched ones

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Investigate week 17:** Before any rollout, determine what caused the score drop (system-wide issue? data problem? seasonal factor?).

2. **Phased rollout:** Expand to 20-30 additional branches first, monitoring weekly scores for 6-8 weeks.

3. **Set success criteria:** Define what improvement level justifies full rollout (e.g., consistent +1.5 points across 4+ weeks).

4. **Document implementation:** Track how faithfully each branch adopts the new procedure—variation in implementation may explain week-to-week differences.

5. **Reassess after 8-12 weeks:** Make a final go/no-go decision on system-wide rollout based on expanded data.
