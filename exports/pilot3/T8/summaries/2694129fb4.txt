# **Board Summary: Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Contrary to initial expectations that the change would have no effect, branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.0 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents a **3.9% improvement** and is highly statistically significant (p < 0.001).

**Verdict: Approve system-wide rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
- **150 branches** participated: 90 adopted the new procedure, 60 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks** of data collected (weeks 6–22); the switch occurred at week 15
- Analysis compares treatment branches to control branches before and after the switch, accounting for branch-specific and week-specific factors

### Results Summary

| Period | Treatment Branches | Control Branches | Difference |
|--------|-------------------|------------------|------------|
| Before switch (weeks 6–14) | 51.13 | 50.57 | +0.56 |
| After switch (weeks 15–22) | 53.42 | 50.87 | +2.55 |
| **Change attributable to new procedure** | | | **+1.99** |

### Statistical Confidence
- **Estimated effect:** +1.99 points
- **95% confidence interval:** 1.22 to 2.75 points
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001
- The effect is distinguishable from zero with high confidence

### Validation of Results
- **Pre-switch trends were parallel:** Treatment and control branches showed similar patterns before the switch, supporting a causal interpretation
- **Effects emerged after implementation:** Score improvements appeared starting week 15 and persisted through week 22
- **Results are robust:** Findings hold across multiple statistical specifications

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did the new procedure change service scores? | **Yes** |
| Was the change positive or negative? | **Positive** (+2.0 points) |
| Is the result statistically reliable? | **Yes** (p < 0.001) |
| Should we roll out to all branches? | **Yes** |

**Rationale:** The evidence is clear and robust. The new procedure improved service scores by a meaningful margin, the statistical confidence is high, and pre-switch trends support a causal interpretation. While the effect size is modest (~4%), service quality improvements of this magnitude are valuable for patron satisfaction. There is no statistical basis for withholding the procedure from remaining branches.
