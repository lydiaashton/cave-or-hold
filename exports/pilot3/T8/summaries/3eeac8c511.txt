# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Library Cooperative Board  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 14, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The evidence is **inconclusive but leans positive**. The new procedure is associated with an estimated 2-point improvement in service scores (~4%), but statistical uncertainty is too large to confirm this effect is real (p = 0.12). The 95% confidence interval ranges from a slight decline (-0.5 points) to a meaningful gain (+4.3 points). There is no evidence of harm.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Overview**
- **34 branches** participated: 20 adopted the new procedure, 14 maintained the old procedure
- **17 weeks** of data collected (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach with branch fixed effects and cluster-robust standard errors

### **Service Score Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 47.7 | 46.5 | −1.2 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 50.6 | 51.3 | +0.7 |
| **Net Effect of New Procedure** | | | **+1.9** |

### **Statistical Summary**
- **Estimated improvement:** +1.92 service score points
- **95% confidence interval:** −0.46 to +4.30 points
- **P-value:** 0.12 (not statistically significant at 5% level)
- **Probability effect is positive:** ~94%
- **Pre-trends check:** Passed—both groups showed similar trends before the switch

### **What This Means**
Branches using the new procedure improved slightly while control branches declined. However, the sample size is too small to rule out chance as an explanation. The data cannot confidently answer whether the new procedure works.

---

## **Final Verdict**

**We cannot recommend a full rollout based on this evidence alone.** The analysis does not demonstrate that the new procedure reliably improves service scores. However, it also does not suggest the procedure is harmful.

**Our recommendation depends on implementation costs:**

| If Implementation Costs Are... | Then... |
|-------------------------------|---------|
| **Low** (minimal training, no equipment) | Proceed with rollout while monitoring. The evidence leans positive and downside risk is small. |
| **Moderate to High** (significant training, staffing, or equipment costs) | Extend the pilot 8–12 weeks. Additional data would provide the precision needed to justify the investment. |

**Regardless of decision:**
- Continue tracking service scores for all branches
- Gather qualitative feedback from staff on what's working
- Re-evaluate after one quarter with expanded data

---

## **Limitations**

- **Sample size:** 34 branches provides limited statistical power
- **Time horizon:** Only 8 weeks of post-switch data; long-term effects unknown
- **Non-random assignment:** Branches chose whether to switch, though pre-trend analysis suggests minimal bias

---

## **Conclusion**

The new procedure shows promise but lacks definitive evidence. The Board faces a choice between acting on suggestive evidence (if costs are low) or gathering more data before committing resources (if costs are significant). Either approach is defensible; the key is to continue monitoring and remain willing to adjust based on emerging results.
