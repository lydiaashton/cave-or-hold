# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

---

## **New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure: Impact Assessment**

---

### **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure is associated with higher service scores, but we cannot be certain it caused the improvement.** Branches that switched showed a 2.1-point gain relative to non-switching branches. However, our analysis reveals the two groups were already on different trajectories before the switch, which clouds causal interpretation.

**Verdict: Proceed with rollout, but implement safeguards to verify the effect.**

---

### **KEY FINDINGS**

| | **Control Group**<br>(284 branches, old procedure) | **Treatment Group**<br>(425 branches, new procedure) |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-switch average | 49.91 | 51.12 |
| Post-switch average | 50.73 | 54.04 |
| **Change** | +0.82 | **+2.92** |

**Observed difference: +2.10 points** (95% CI: 1.37 to 2.83)

---

### **WHAT WORKED IN OUR FAVOR**

✓ **Large sample**—709 branches, 17 weeks of data
✓ **Statistically precise**—the 2.1-point gap is unlikely to be random noise
✓ **No fading**—effect persisted across all 8 post-switch weeks
✓ **Controls applied**—results hold after accounting for staffing and visitor volume

---

### **WHAT GIVES US PAUSE**

⚠ **Parallel trends violated**—treatment and control branches showed significantly different patterns *before* the switch, suggesting they were not comparable to begin with

⚠ **Non-random assignment**—branches that switched may have been systematically different (more motivated, better managed, etc.) from those that didn't

⚠ **Unobserved factors**—something other than the procedure change may explain the divergence

---

### **FINAL VERDICT**

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Did service scores improve in switching branches? | **Yes, clearly** |
| Can we prove the new procedure caused it? | **Not definitively** |
| Is rollout still worth pursuing? | **Yes, with conditions** |

**Rationale:** Even if the true effect is smaller than 2.1 points, the procedure shows no negative effects, requires no additional resources, and 425 branches have already adopted it successfully. The risk of rollback is low; the risk of leaving potential gains on the table is real.

---

### **RECOMMENDED ACTIONS**

1. **Approve rollout** to remaining 284 branches

2. **Stage the rollout** in 2–3 waves over 6 months to enable ongoing comparison

3. **Track service scores monthly** for all branches post-rollout; flag any that underperform

4. **Commit to evaluation**—report to the Board in 12 months with full-system data to confirm the effect holds at scale

5. **Document implementation practices** from high-performing early adopters to support training

---

*Analysis based on 12,053 weekly observations from 709 branches (weeks 6–22). Difference-in-differences regression with branch fixed effects. Event study analysis reveals pre-treatment divergence between groups, limiting causal confidence.*
