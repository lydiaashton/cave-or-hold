# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure  
**Date:** Analysis of weeks 6–22 service data  
**Prepared by:** Data Analysis Office

---

## **Bottom Line**

**We cannot confidently attribute the observed improvement to the new procedure.** While branches that switched show higher service scores (+2.1 points), the treatment and control groups were already on different trajectories *before* the switch occurred. This undermines our ability to isolate the procedure's effect. **We recommend against a full rollout until additional evidence is gathered.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **What the Data Shows**
- Branches that adopted the new procedure have service scores **2.1 points higher** than what a simple comparison would predict
- This difference is statistically significant (p < 0.001)

### **The Critical Limitation**
- **Parallel trends assumption is violated** (p = 0.0005)
- The treatment and control groups were already diverging *before* week 15:

| Week | Treatment – Control Gap |
|------|------------------------|
| 6    | 0.01 points |
| 10   | 1.93 points |
| 14   | 2.37 points |

- The gap grew by **2.4 points during the pre-period alone**—before any branches switched procedures
- If this pre-existing trend continued, we would expect a gap of ~4.7 points by week 22; the actual gap was 3.3 points

### **What This Means**
The +2.1 point "effect" likely reflects **pre-existing differences** between branches that chose to switch versus those that didn't, rather than a causal impact of the new procedure. Branches that opted into the new procedure may have been:
- Better-managed overall
- More motivated to improve
- Already experiencing positive momentum

---

## **Recommendations**

| Priority | Action |
|----------|--------|
| **1** | **Do not proceed with system-wide rollout** based on current evidence |
| **2** | **Conduct a randomized trial** — Randomly assign remaining branches to treatment/control to establish causality |
| **3** | **Gather qualitative evidence** — Interview staff at switched branches about implementation experience and perceived benefits |
| **4** | **Review branch selection process** — Understand why certain branches were chosen to switch; this may reveal confounding factors |
| **5** | **Consider additional metrics** — Examine circulation, patron satisfaction, and staff workload alongside service scores |

---

## **Limitations**

- **Non-random assignment**: Branches were not randomly assigned to switch, creating selection bias
- **Short observation window**: Only 8 weeks of post-implementation data
- **Single outcome measure**: Service score is one index; broader impact assessment needed

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

> **DO NOT ROLL OUT SYSTEM-WIDE AT THIS TIME**
>
> The current evidence is **insufficient to support a causal claim** that the new procedure improves service scores. The observed association is confounded by pre-existing differences between branches. Proceeding with a full rollout risks:
> - Implementing a procedure that may not deliver expected benefits
> - Incurring implementation costs without guaranteed return
> - Disrupting operations at branches that may not benefit
>
> **Path forward:** Conduct a randomized controlled trial with 50–100 branches to establish causal evidence before making a system-wide decision.
