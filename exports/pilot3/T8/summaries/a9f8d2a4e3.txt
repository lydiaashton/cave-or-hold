# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure  
**Date:** November 14, 2026  
**Prepared by:** Data Analysis Team

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**We cannot determine whether the new procedure caused the observed improvement.** While branches that switched to the new procedure showed higher service scores after the change, the analysis reveals that treatment and control branches were **already on different trajectories before the switch**. This means we cannot confidently attribute the improvement to the procedure itself rather than pre-existing differences between the groups.

**Recommendation: Do not proceed with system-wide rollout based on this evidence alone.** Additional investigation is needed.

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

> **INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE FOR ROLLOUT**
>
> The data shows an association between the new procedure and higher service scores, but **does not establish causation**. The fundamental assumption required for causal inference—that treatment and control branches would have followed parallel trends absent the intervention—is violated. 
>
> **Decision:** Pause rollout. Investigate selection bias. Consider randomized pilot.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### What the Data Shows

| | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| **New Procedure Branches** | 50.1 | 51.7 | +1.6 |
| **Old Procedure Branches** | 49.9 | 49.7 | −0.2 |
| **Apparent Effect** | | | **+1.8** |

### The Critical Problem: Non-Parallel Pre-Trends

For a valid comparison, treatment and control groups must follow similar trends *before* the intervention. **They did not.**

| Pre-Period Week | Gap (Treatment − Control) | Statistically Significant? |
|-----------------|---------------------------|---------------------------|
| Week 6 | −0.18 | No |
| Week 7 | +0.45 | **Yes** |
| Week 8 | +0.70 | **Yes** |
| Week 9 | −0.11 | No |
| Week 10 | −1.00 | **Yes** |
| Week 11 | −0.13 | No |
| Week 12 | +0.33 | **Yes** |
| Week 13 | +0.56 | No |
| Week 14 | +1.21 | **Yes** |

**5 of 8 pre-period weeks show significant differences** (62%), when we would expect only 5% by chance if the groups were comparable. This is strong evidence that the groups were systematically different.

### What This Means

1. **Branches were not randomly assigned** to switch procedures. Some systematic factor determined which branches adopted the new procedure.

2. **The apparent +1.8 point effect may reflect pre-existing differences**, not the procedure itself.

3. **We cannot rule out either possibility:**
   - The new procedure genuinely improves scores, OR
   - Higher-performing branches were selected to pilot the change

---

## **RECOMMENDED NEXT STEPS**

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| **1** | **Pause rollout decision** | Current evidence is insufficient for a causal conclusion |
| **2** | **Investigate selection process** | Understand why certain branches were chosen to switch |
| **3** | **Analyze branch characteristics** | Compare treatment vs. control branches on size, location, staffing, baseline performance |
| **4** | **Consider a randomized trial** | Randomly assign remaining branches to treatment/control for cleaner evidence |
| **5** | **Collect qualitative feedback** | Interview staff at switched branches about perceived effects |
| **6** | **Re-evaluate in 4–6 weeks** | With additional analysis, make an informed rollout decision |

---

## **WHAT WE CAN SAY WITH CONFIDENCE**

✓ Branches using the new procedure *did* have higher scores after week 15  
✓ The statistical association is robust (p < 0.01)  
✓ No obvious negative effects were detected  

## **WHAT WE CANNOT SAY**

✗ The new procedure *caused* the improvement  
✗ All branches would see similar benefits  
✗ The effect will persist long-term  

---

## **BOTTOM LINE FOR THE BOARD**

The initial expectation—that the procedure change would make no difference—has **not been disproven**. The observed improvement is equally consistent with selection bias as with a genuine treatment effect. 

**Prudent course:** Investigate further before committing resources to system-wide rollout.
