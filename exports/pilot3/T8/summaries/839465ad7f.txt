# **MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 14, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure shows a positive, statistically significant effect on service scores.** Switched branches improved by approximately **2.1 points** (about 4%) relative to branches that kept the old procedure. However, we only have 8 weeks of post-switch data, and we lack information on implementation costs.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **phased rollout** while continuing to monitor service scores and tracking any additional operational costs. Reassess after 3–6 months before committing to system-wide implementation.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### Study Overview
- **150 branches** participated: 90 switched to the new procedure, 60 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** collected (Weeks 6–22); the switch was implemented in Week 15
- Analysis compares service score changes in switched branches against non-switched branches

### Results

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 49.68 | 48.80 | −0.88 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 49.25 | 50.47 | +1.22 |
| **Net Effect (Difference-in-Differences)** | | | **+2.10** |

### Statistical Evidence
- **Treatment effect: +2.10 points** (95% confidence interval: 0.82 to 3.38)
- **Statistically significant at p < 0.01**
- Effect holds after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume
- **Pre-switch trends were parallel**, confirming the comparison between groups is valid

### Interpretation
- Branches using the new procedure improved while control branches experienced a slight decline
- The 2.1-point gain represents roughly a **4% improvement** relative to baseline scores
- The benefit is not explained by changes in staffing or visitor traffic

---

## **LIMITATIONS TO CONSIDER**

| Limitation | Why It Matters |
|------------|----------------|
| Only 8 weeks of post-switch data | We cannot confirm whether the effect is sustainable long-term |
| Modest effect size | A 4% improvement may or may not justify system-wide change depending on costs |
| No cost data available | Additional staff time, training, or resources could offset the benefit |
| Low model explanatory power (R² ≈ 1%) | Most variation in service scores is driven by factors we did not measure |

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did the new procedure improve service scores? | **Yes** – the effect is real and statistically significant |
| Is the improvement large enough to matter? | **Uncertain** – 4% is modest; operational impact depends on branch priorities |
| Should we roll out to all branches immediately? | **No** – insufficient data on long-term effects and costs |
| **Recommended action** | **Phased rollout with monitoring** |

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

1. **Approve phased rollout** to an additional 30–50 branches over the next quarter
2. **Track implementation costs** (staff hours, training time, any disruptions)
3. **Monitor service scores** for 12+ weeks to confirm the effect persists
4. **Reconvene in 6 months** to review cost-benefit analysis before full system-wide adoption
5. **Document best practices** from high-performing branches to support staff training

---

*Analysis based on weekly service score data from 150 branches, Weeks 6–22. Statistical models use cluster-robust standard errors by branch.*
