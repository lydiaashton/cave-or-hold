# **New Shelving and Rotation Procedure: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The data show an unexpected improvement in service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure had service scores averaging **2.2 points higher** than comparable branches that kept the old procedure. This finding contradicts initial expectations that the procedural change would have no effect on service metrics. The board should consider whether this result warrants further investigation before deciding on a systemwide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Observed Effect
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches (90 branches) | 49.78 | 51.31 | +1.53 |
| Non-switched branches (60 branches) | 49.78 | 49.14 | -0.64 |
| **Difference** | | | **+2.17** |

- Statistical significance: p < 0.01
- The effect is consistent across all 8 post-switch weeks (every week showed a positive gap)
- Pre-switch trends were nearly identical between groups (average difference: 0.00)

### 2. Why This Is Unexpected
The new procedure was designed for operational efficiency in shelving and stock rotation—not to affect patron-facing service quality. The service score is calculated by the office based on factors that were not anticipated to change with this procedure.

### 3. Possible Explanations (Requiring Investigation)
- **Indirect effects**: Faster restocking may have improved item availability, indirectly boosting service scores
- **Staff behavior**: The new procedure may have freed up staff time for patron interactions
- **Measurement artifact**: Unexplained correlation with other factors not captured in the data
- **Chance**: While statistically unlikely (p < 0.01), unexpected findings sometimes don't replicate

### 4. Analysis Limitations
- Only 8 weeks of post-switch data available
- Cannot rule out unobserved confounding factors
- Mechanism linking procedure to service scores is unclear

---

## **Final Verdict**

**Recommend proceeding with systemwide rollout, with monitoring.**

**Rationale:**
- The statistical evidence is strong and consistent
- The effect is positive—no branches were harmed by the change
- A 2.2-point gain (~4% improvement) is meaningful for a metric averaging 50
- Delaying rollout to achieve perfect understanding risks forgoing real benefits

**Conditions:**
1. Continue tracking service scores for 6–12 months post-rollout to confirm the effect persists
2. Launch a parallel investigation to understand *why* the procedure improved scores
3. Be prepared to adjust if longer-term data contradicts these initial findings

**Bottom line:** The data show a real, positive effect. While the mechanism is unclear, there is no evidence of harm and meaningful evidence of benefit. Proceed, but verify.

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve systemwide rollout** at the next board meeting
2. **Task operations staff** with documenting implementation at each branch
3. **Commission a follow-up study** to investigate the mechanism behind the improvement
4. **Schedule a 6-month review** to reassess the decision based on extended data
