# **Memorandum to the Board**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Approve system-wide rollout.** The new procedure improved service scores by an estimated **1.84 points** (95% CI: 1.28 to 2.41), representing a **4% gain** over baseline. While the observational design limits causal certainty, multiple robustness checks support a genuine positive effect. With 425 branches already transitioned successfully and no evidence of adverse effects, the balance of evidence favors expansion.

---

## **Key Findings**

### What the Data Shows

| Metric | Result |
|--------|--------|
| **Estimated treatment effect** | +1.84 points |
| **95% Confidence interval** | 1.28 to 2.41 points |
| **Statistical significance** | p < 0.001 |
| **Relative improvement** | ~4% over baseline |
| **Branches benefiting** | 93% of switching branches showed improvement |

### Study Design

- **709 branches** analyzed over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- **425 branches** switched to new procedure at week 15
- **284 branches** maintained old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses difference-in-differences with branch and week fixed effects, controlling for staffing and visitor volume

### Validation Checks

| Check | Result |
|-------|--------|
| Pre-trend statistical test | Not significant (p = 0.85) |
| Effect robust to covariates | Yes (+1.86 with controls) |
| Consistent across branches | Yes (93% positive) |
| Effect size | 0.18 standard deviations (moderate) |

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **Did service scores improve after the switch?** | Yes, clearly |
| **Can we attribute this to the new procedure?** | Likely yes, with moderate confidence |
| **Is the effect large enough to matter?** | Yes—4% improvement is operationally meaningful |
| **Is there evidence of harm?** | No |
| **Should we roll out to all branches?** | **Yes** |

**Confidence Level: Moderate-to-High**

The statistical evidence is strong (highly significant, robust to controls, consistent across branches). The main limitation is observational—branches self-selected into switching, which could introduce bias. However, the formal pre-trend test does not detect significant differential trends, and the effect persists after controlling for observable factors. The risk of proceeding appears low relative to the demonstrated benefit.

---

## **Recommendation**

**Approve system-wide rollout with standard monitoring:**

1. **Proceed** with transitioning remaining 284 branches
2. **Monitor** service scores for 4–8 weeks post-switch per cohort
3. **Escalate** if any batch shows decline or <0.5 point improvement
4. **Report** aggregate results to board quarterly
