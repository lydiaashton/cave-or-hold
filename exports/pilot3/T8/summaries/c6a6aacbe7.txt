# **Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure Evaluation**

---

## **Final Verdict**

**PROCEED WITH CAUTION** — The new procedure shows a positive effect on service scores, but the evidence is not strong enough to justify immediate system-wide implementation. Approve a **phased expansion with continued evaluation**, with a decision on full rollout deferred until additional data confirms the benefit.

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure shows a **modest positive effect on service scores** (+1.6 points, approximately 3% improvement). However, this result is **not statistically conclusive** (p=0.084), meaning we cannot confidently rule out chance as an explanation. The evidence is **promising but not definitive**. We recommend a **phased continuation with enhanced monitoring** rather than an immediate system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Overview**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure at week 15; **14 branches** maintained the old procedure as a control group
- Analysis used difference-in-differences methodology with branch fixed effects to isolate the procedure's true impact

### **Service Score Results**

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|----------------|---------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 47.6 | 48.8 | +1.2 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 50.2 | 52.8 | +2.6 |
| **Net Effect of New Procedure** | | | **+1.6** |

- **Estimated impact:** +1.62 points on the service score index
- **95% Confidence Interval:** -0.22 to +3.45 points
- **Practical meaning:** The true effect could range from negligible to moderately beneficial

### **Statistical Assessment**
- **P-value: 0.084** — marginally significant (passes 10% threshold, not 5%)
- **Pre-trend validation:** Treatment and control groups showed parallel trajectories before the switch, confirming our comparison is valid
- **Baseline difference:** Switching branches started with slightly higher scores, but our analysis accounts for this

### **Supporting Factors**
- Higher staffing levels and visit volumes correlate with better service scores
- The new procedure may work better when combined with adequate staffing

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
