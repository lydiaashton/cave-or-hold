# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative
**From:** Data Analysis Team
**Date:** November 14, 2026
**Subject:** Recommendation on System-Wide Rollout of New Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores, but the effect is modest.** Branches that adopted the new procedure gained approximately **1.1 points** on the service score index (on a ~50-point scale). This effect is statistically reliable and consistent across all post-switch weeks. However, at roughly 2% improvement, the practical impact is modest.

**Verdict: Proceed with rollout only if implementation costs are low.** If the new procedure requires minimal additional staff time, training, or resources, the improvement justifies adoption. If costs are significant, the board should conduct a formal cost-benefit analysis before committing to system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Analysis Overview**
| Element | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| Branches Analyzed | 150 |
| Analysis Period | 17 weeks (weeks 6–22) |
| Switched Branches | 90 (treatment group) |
| Non-Switched Branches | 60 (control group) |
| Procedure Switch | Week 15 |

### **Main Result**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Estimated Effect** | +1.10 service score points |
| **95% Confidence Interval** | +0.36 to +1.83 points |
| **Statistical Significance** | p = 0.003 |
| **Effect Size** | 0.11 standard deviations (~2% of mean) |

### **Evidence Summary**

1. **Valid Comparison**: Before the switch, both groups followed parallel trends in service scores, confirming the control group provides a reliable counterfactual.

2. **Consistent Effect**: Switched branches outperformed non-switched branches in **all 8 post-switch weeks**, with differences ranging from 0.4 to 1.5 points.

3. **Robust to Controls**: The effect persists after accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume.

4. **Modest Magnitude**: At an average baseline of ~50 points, the 1.1-point gain represents approximately **2% improvement**—real but not transformative.

### **Score Changes by Group**
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched Branches | 48.5 | 49.6 | +1.1 |
| Non-Switched Branches | 49.2 | 48.6 | −0.6 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+1.7** |

*Note: The new procedure appears to both improve scores and protect against the decline observed in control branches.*

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did the procedure change service scores? | **Yes** — statistically significant improvement |
| Is the effect large? | **No** — modest (~2%) improvement |
| Should we roll out system-wide? | **Conditional** — depends on implementation costs |

**Recommendation**: The procedure works, but whether it's worth adopting system-wide depends on costs that this analysis cannot assess. We recommend:

- **If low-cost**: Proceed with rollout; the improvement is real and consistent.
- **If moderate/high-cost**: Conduct cost-benefit analysis; consider targeted rollout to underperforming branches first.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Quantify Implementation Costs**: Determine staff time, training, and resource requirements per branch.

2. **Define Success Threshold**: Establish what minimum improvement justifies the investment (is 1 point enough?).

3. **Consider Phased Rollout**: Implement at 20–30 branches initially to confirm effects and refine training before full deployment.

4. **Supplement with Patron Feedback**: Add direct patron satisfaction measures to assess whether the score improvement translates to noticeable service quality.

5. **Monitor Long-Term**: Track whether effects persist beyond the initial 8-week post-switch window.
