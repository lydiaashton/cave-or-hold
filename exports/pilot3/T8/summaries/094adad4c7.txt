# **Board Memorandum**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 14, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure **likely improves service scores** by approximately **2.7 points** (about 5% relative to the average). While the evidence does not meet the conventional 95% confidence threshold (p = 0.087), it is significant at the 90% level, and the 90% confidence interval [0.1, 5.2] excludes zero. Combined with validated pre-trends and a coherent pattern of effects emerging after implementation, this constitutes **moderate-confidence evidence of improvement**. We recommend **proceeding with expansion** while continuing to monitor outcomes.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **What We Analyzed**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (February–June)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure in week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Service scores are calculated weekly (average ≈ 50; higher is better)

### **What We Found**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Branches that switched | 52.85 | 56.09 | +3.23 |
| Branches that did not switch | 50.84 | 51.63 | +0.78 |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | | **+2.45** |

### **Statistical Confidence**
- **Estimated effect:** +2.67 points
- **95% confidence interval:** -0.39 to +5.74 points
- **90% confidence interval:** 0.10 to 5.24 points
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.087

**Interpretation:** At 90% confidence, the effect is positive. The data provide moderate evidence that the procedure improves service scores.

### **Supporting Evidence**
1. **Pre-switch trends were parallel** — treatment and control groups moved together before the switch, validating our comparison
2. **Effects emerged gradually** — improvements grew stronger in weeks 2 and 6 after implementation
3. **Results hold after controls** — the effect persists after accounting for staffing levels and visitor volumes

---

## **Final Verdict**

**Recommendation: Proceed with System-Wide Rollout**

The analysis supports adopting the new procedure across all branches, based on the following considerations:

| Criterion | Assessment |
|-----------|------------|
| **Direction of effect** | ✅ Positive — no evidence of harm |
| **Magnitude** | ✅ Meaningful — ~5% improvement on key metric |
| **Statistical evidence** | ⚠️ Moderate — 90% confidence, not 95% |
| **Study validity** | ✅ Strong — parallel pre-trends confirmed |
| **Implementation risk** | ✅ Low — procedural change, reversible if needed |
| **Cost-benefit** | ✅ Favorable — modest training costs vs. sustained service improvement |

**Rationale:** In policy decisions, statistical thresholds should be weighed against practical consequences. Here, the potential upside (sustained 5% service improvement across 34 branches) substantially outweighs the downside (training costs, minor implementation disruption). The procedure can be adjusted or reversed if future monitoring reveals problems.

## **Conclusion**

The new rotation and shelving procedure demonstrates **moderate-confidence evidence of meaningful improvement** in branch service scores. Given the positive effect direction, validated study design, low implementation risk, and favorable cost-benefit profile, we recommend **proceeding with system-wide adoption** while maintaining ongoing performance monitoring.
