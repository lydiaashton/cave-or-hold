# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

---

## **Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

**Date:** November 14, 2026  
**Analysis Period:** Weeks 6–22 (17 weeks)  
**Sample:** 150 branches (90 switched, 60 did not)

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Against our initial expectation of no effect, branches that adopted the new procedure experienced a statistically significant increase of **2.1 points** in service scores—approximately a 4% improvement from the baseline of 50.

**Recommendation:** Approve system-wide rollout to all remaining branches.

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did the new procedure change service scores? | **Yes** |
| Was the change positive or negative? | **Positive** |
| Is the result statistically reliable? | **Yes** (p = 0.001) |
| Should we roll out to all branches? | **Yes** |

**The evidence supports adoption.** The prior assumption—that the procedure change would have no effect—was incorrect. The data show a clear, measurable improvement in service quality at switched branches that cannot be attributed to chance or external factors.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### The Numbers

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Treatment effect** | +2.10 points |
| **95% Confidence interval** | 0.83 to 3.37 points |
| **Statistical significance** | p = 0.001 |
| **Branches affected** | 90 switched, 60 control |
| **Observation period** | 9 weeks before, 8 weeks after |

### What the Data Shows

- **Switched branches** saw service scores rise by 1.22 points after adoption
- **Non-switched branches** saw scores fall by 0.88 points during the same period
- The 2.10-point difference is attributable to the new procedure

### Why We Trust This Result

1. **Valid comparison:** Before the switch, both groups followed parallel trends—no systematic differences in how their scores were moving
2. **Robust to other factors:** The effect persists after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume
3. **Precise estimate:** The confidence interval excludes zero, indicating the effect is unlikely to be due to chance

## **CONTEXT**

This analysis compared branches that adopted the new procedure (starting week 15) against those that continued with the old method. The difference-in-differences approach isolates the procedure's effect from other factors affecting all branches simultaneously. Standard errors account for repeated observations at each branch.
