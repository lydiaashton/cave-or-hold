# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure  
**Date:** November 14, 2026  
**Analysis Period:** Weeks 6–22 (17 weeks of data)

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Contrary to the initial expectation that the change would make no difference, branches that adopted the new procedure experienced a statistically significant increase in service scores of 2.1 points (approximately 4%) compared to branches that retained the old procedure.

**Verdict: Recommend full rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Impact on Service Scores**

| Measure | Value |
|---------|-------|
| **Estimated effect of new procedure** | **+2.09 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | 0.84 to 3.34 points |
| Statistical Significance | **p = 0.001** |
| Baseline average score | 49.8 |
| **Relative improvement** | **~4%** |

### **What Happened**

- **Branches that switched**: Service scores **increased** by approximately 1.0 point after the change
- **Branches that did not switch**: Service scores **decreased** by approximately 1.1 point over the same period
- **Net effect attributable to the new procedure**: +2.1 points

The new procedure appears to have both improved service quality and protected branches from a system-wide decline observed in the control group during the same period.

### **Study Strength**

- **150 branches** participated (90 adopted new procedure; 60 retained old procedure)
- **17 weeks** of observations (9 weeks before switch, 8 weeks after)
- **Parallel trends verified**: No pre-existing differences between groups before the switch (p = 0.28), supporting causal interpretation
- **Robust methods**: Analysis accounts for branch-level variation using cluster-robust standard errors

---

## **Context**

The board initially expected this procedural change would have **no effect** on service scores. The data clearly contradict this expectation. The new procedure is associated with meaningful, statistically reliable improvements in service quality as measured by our service score index.

While the effect is modest (~4%), it is:
- **Statistically significant** at the 1% level (highly unlikely to be due to chance)
- **Consistent** across the 8-week post-implementation period
- **Achieved without reported implementation problems** from switching branches

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did the new procedure change service scores? | **Yes** |
| Was the change positive or negative? | **Positive** |
| Is the result statistically reliable? | **Yes** (p = 0.001) |
| Should we roll out to all branches? | **Yes** |

**Recommendation: Approve system-wide rollout of the new rotation and shelving procedure.**

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve full rollout** to all remaining branches

2. **Develop implementation timeline** with branch managers (suggested: 4–6 weeks)

3. **Continue monitoring** service scores for 8–12 weeks post-rollout to confirm effects persist at scale

4. **Gather qualitative feedback** from staff to understand mechanisms and identify any operational considerations

5. **Report back to the board** in one quarter with full rollout results
