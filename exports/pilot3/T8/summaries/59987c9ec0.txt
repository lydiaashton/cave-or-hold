## **Final Verdict**

**We cannot conclude with confidence that the new procedure caused the improvement in service scores.**

While branches using the new procedure did achieve higher scores after the switch, the same branches were already outperforming the control group in the weeks *before* the change. This pre-existing trend makes it impossible to determine how much of the observed improvement is due to the procedure itself versus other factors (such as branch selection, management quality, or local conditions).

**The honest assessment:**
- **Best case:** The procedure provides a modest benefit (~0.6 to 1.3 points)
- **Likely case:** The procedure provides little to no additional benefit beyond what was already happening
- **Worst case:** The apparent benefit is entirely due to pre-existing differences, and the procedure has no effect

**For the Board's decision:** If the new procedure is low-cost and low-risk, a cautious rollout may be reasonable. However, if significant investment is required, we recommend generating cleaner evidence first—ideally through a randomized rollout design that would allow us to isolate the true causal effect.
