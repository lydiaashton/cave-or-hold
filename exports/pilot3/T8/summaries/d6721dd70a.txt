# MEMORANDUM

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 14, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## Bottom Line

**The new procedure likely improved service scores, with moderate confidence.** Branches that adopted the new procedure showed an average improvement of approximately **2.1 points** (about 4%) compared to branches that retained the old procedure. The effect is statistically significant (p < 0.001), though week-to-week results varied. **We recommend proceeding with rollout, with continued monitoring to confirm the effect persists.**

---

## Final Verdict

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did the new procedure change service scores? | **Yes** — a positive change of ~2 points |
| Was this change expected? | **No** — the hypothesis was "no difference" |
| Is the evidence strong enough to act on? | **Yes** — statistically significant with consistent direction |
| Should we roll out to all branches? | **Yes** — with phased implementation and monitoring |

**Decision: Approve phased rollout of the new procedure to all branches.**

---

## Key Findings

### 1. Measurable Improvement with Some Variability

| Group | Pre-Switch Average | Post-Switch Average | Change |
|-------|-------------------|---------------------|--------|
| Non-switched branches (control) | 50.1 | 51.0 | +0.9 |
| Switched branches (treatment) | 49.1 | 52.1 | +3.0 |
| **Net Treatment Effect** | | | **+2.1** |

The 2.1-point improvement represents approximately **4%** over the baseline average service score of 49.5.

### 2. Statistical Evidence

- **Effect size:** +2.1 points on the service score index
- **95% confidence interval:** 1.0 to 3.2 points
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.0002
- **Interpretation:** We can be confident the effect is positive, though the exact magnitude may range from 1 to 3 points

### 3. Week-to-Week Variability

The effect was not uniform across all post-switch weeks:
- **6 of 8 weeks** showed treated branches outperforming control
- **2 of 8 weeks** (weeks 20 and 21) showed treated branches slightly behind control
- This variability suggests external factors (staffing, visits, seasonal effects) also influence scores

### 4. Study Design

- **150 branches:** 90 adopted the new procedure, 60 served as controls
- **17 weeks of data:** Weeks 6–22, with the switch in week 15
- **Method:** Difference-in-differences analysis with standard errors clustered by branch

### 5. Validity Assessment

- **Parallel trends:** Reasonably satisfied—both groups followed similar trajectories before the switch, though treated branches converged toward control just before switching
- **Robustness:** Effect persists when controlling for staffing levels and visit volumes
- **Limitation:** Only 8 weeks of post-switch data; longer-term effects remain uncertain

---

## Recommendation

**Proceed with rollout, but implement with monitoring.**

The evidence supports a positive effect from the new procedure, though the magnitude may be modest (1–3 points). Given that:
- The original expectation was "no difference"
- The observed effect is positive and statistically significant
- No negative consequences were identified

The new procedure appears to be a low-risk improvement. However, the week-to-week variability suggests the effect may depend on implementation quality or interacting factors.

### Risk Mitigation

1. **Phased approach:** Roll out to 25% of remaining branches per month rather than all at once, allowing for course correction.

2. **Implementation support:** Ensure consistent training and resources to minimize variability in how the procedure is adopted.

3. **Continue monitoring:** If service scores decline in any branch post-switch, investigate implementation issues promptly.

4. **Re-evaluate in 6 months:** With more data, confirm the effect size and adjust expectations accordingly.

---

*Analysis based on 2,550 weekly observations from 150 branches. Standard errors clustered by branch. The confidence interval reflects uncertainty in the effect size, not uncertainty about the direction of the effect.*
