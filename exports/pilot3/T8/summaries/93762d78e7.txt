# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Contrary to expectations, the new procedure did improve service scores.** Branches that switched to the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **1.1 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and robust across multiple analytical approaches. We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **150 branches** analyzed over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **60 branches** continued with the old procedure
- This natural experiment allows us to isolate the effect by comparing switched branches against non-switched branches

### **Main Result**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Estimated Effect** | +1.10 service score points |
| **95% Confidence Interval** | +0.36 to +1.83 points |
| **Statistical Significance** | p = 0.003 |

### **Why We Can Trust This Result**

1. **Valid Comparison Group**: Before the switch, both groups followed parallel trends—there was no systematic difference in how their scores were moving. This confirms the non-switched branches are an appropriate control group.

2. **Effect Holds Under Scrutiny**: The 1.1-point improvement persists even after:
   - Accounting for weekly staffing levels
   - Accounting for visitor volume
   - Controlling for branch-specific characteristics

3. **Consistent Pattern**: In all 8 weeks after the switch, switched branches outperformed non-switched branches. The advantage never disappeared.

4. **Unlikely to Be Chance**: The probability of observing this result if the procedure had no real effect is less than 1 in 200 (p = 0.003).

### **What the Data Shows**
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 48.5 | 49.6 | **+1.1** |
| Non-switched branches | 49.2 | 48.6 | **-0.6** |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+1.7** |

### **Context**
- Average branch service score is approximately 50
- A 1.1-point gain represents roughly a **2% improvement**
- While modest, this is a meaningful improvement in patron experience at no apparent cost to other metrics

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve System-Wide Rollout**: The evidence supports expanding the new procedure to all branches.

2. **Phase the Implementation**: Consider rolling out in waves to monitor for any implementation challenges.

3. **Track Key Metrics**: Continue monitoring service scores, staffing efficiency, and patron satisfaction during rollout.

4. **Capture Learnings**: Document what worked well in the 90 switched branches to support training for remaining branches.

5. **Re-Evaluate in 6 Months**: Confirm the effect persists at scale and assess whether additional benefits emerge over time.

---

## **Why This May Have Been Unexpected**

The analysis was designed to detect any effect—positive or negative. The fact that we found a positive effect despite expectations suggests:

- The new procedure may improve staff efficiency in ways not immediately visible
- Better stock organization may help patrons find materials faster
- Small operational changes can have measurable downstream effects on service quality

This is a favorable surprise: a low-cost operational change delivering measurable service improvement.

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|

| Direction of change? | **Positive** |

While the magnitude is modest (~2%), it represents a real gain in patron experience with no identified downside. This is exactly the kind of evidence-based improvement that justifies system-wide adoption.
