# **Subject: Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence suggests the new procedure improves service scores, but we cannot be statistically certain.** The estimated effect is **+2.15 points**, but the 95% confidence interval (-0.35 to +4.65) includes zero. At 90% confidence, the effect is positive. The pattern of results—particularly strengthening effects in later weeks—is encouraging. We recommend **proceeding with rollout** while acknowledging the uncertainty and committing to continued evaluation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **What the Data Shows**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Branches that switched | 49.67 | 52.97 | +3.31 |
| Branches that did not switch | 51.02 | 52.18 | +1.16 |
| **Estimated effect of new procedure** | | | **+2.15** |

### **What the Statistics Tell Us**

| Measure | Value | Interpretation |
|---------|-------|----------------|
| Point estimate | +2.15 points | Best guess of the true effect |
| p-value | 0.09 | 9% chance of this result if there's no real effect |
| 95% Confidence Interval | -0.35 to +4.65 | **Includes zero**—cannot rule out no effect |
| 90% Confidence Interval | +0.05 to +4.25 | **Excludes zero**—suggests positive effect |

**Bottom line on statistics:** The evidence leans positive but falls short of conventional scientific certainty (p < 0.05). For business decisions, this level of evidence is often considered **suggestive but not definitive**.

### **Encouraging Patterns**

Despite statistical uncertainty, several factors support proceeding:

1. **Effects strengthen over time:** Weeks 1-2 showed little benefit; Weeks 7-8 showed +6 to +7 point gains (statistically significant)
2. **Consistent direction:** All post-switch weeks show positive point estimates after Week 2
3. **Plausible mechanism:** Learning curve pattern matches what we'd expect from procedural change
4. **No red flags:** Pre-switch trends were similar between groups; control variables are balanced

---

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with system-wide rollout, with appropriate caveats:**

### **Why Proceed?**
- The **preponderance of evidence points positive**
- A 2-point improvement (~4% gain) is **operationally meaningful** if real
- The **learning curve pattern** suggests effects may grow with experience
- **Waiting for more certainty** means delaying potential benefits across all branches

### **How to Proceed Responsibly**

| Action | Rationale |
|--------|-----------|
| **Phase the rollout** (4-6 branches/week) | Allows support staff to assist; enables course correction |
| **Provide extra support in Weeks 1-2** | Data shows no benefit during adjustment period |
| **Track scores weekly for 8 weeks post-implementation** | Verify the strengthening pattern replicates |
| **Set a review point at 6 months** | Decide whether to continue, modify, or reverse based on accumulated data |

---

## **Risks and Mitigation**

| Risk | Likelihood | Mitigation |
|------|------------|------------|
| True effect is zero or negative | ~30-40% (based on CI) | Monitor closely; be prepared to pause or reverse |
| Implementation varies across branches | Moderate | Standardize training; share best practices |
| Initial disruption affects staff morale | Moderate | Communicate transparently; celebrate early wins |

---

## **What Would Change Our Recommendation**

We would advise **pausing or reversing** the rollout if:
- Service scores decline (rather than strengthen) after Week 4 at multiple branches
- Staff feedback indicates unmanageable workflow disruption
- The 6-month review shows no sustained improvement

---

## **Final Verdict**

> **ROLL OUT THE NEW PROCEDURE—WITH CONDITIONS.**
>
> The data does not prove the new procedure works, but it does not need to. This is a business decision, not a scientific proof. The evidence leans positive, the potential upside is meaningful, and the risks are manageable with proper monitoring.
>
> **Vote: Proceed with phased rollout, extra transition support, and a 6-month review commitment.**
>
> If the effect is real, we gain ~2 points in service quality across all branches. If the effect is not real, we will know within 6 months and can adjust. The cost of waiting exceeds the cost of learning by doing.

---

## **Conclusion**

The board faces a decision under uncertainty—a common situation in operational improvements. The data does not provide definitive proof, but it does provide **actionable evidence leaning positive**. Given the modest cost of rollout relative to potential service gains, we recommend proceeding with appropriate monitoring and willingness to adjust.

**This is a "proceed with eyes open" decision, not a "guaranteed win" decision.**
