# **Analysis of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure: Impact on Service Scores**

**Prepared for:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors  
**Date:** November 2026

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure shows **modest evidence of improvement**, but the findings are **less definitive than initially apparent**. The estimated effect is +1.65 points on service scores, but this effect is not statistically significant in any individual post-switch week when examined separately. Pre-existing differences between branches and week-to-week variability create uncertainty about whether the observed improvement is causal or temporary.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with **cautious, phased rollout** rather than immediate system-wide adoption. Continue monitoring to confirm the effect persists.

---

## **Key Findings**

### What the Data Shows

| Metric | Result |
|--------|--------|
| Estimated treatment effect | **+1.65 points** (3.4% of average) |
| Statistical significance (pooled) | **p = 0.004** |
| Significance in individual post-switch weeks | **None** (all p > 0.35) |
| Pre-switch trend differences | Marginally significant (p = 0.09) |

### Important Caveats

1. **No consistent week-by-week effect**: When examining each post-switch week individually, none shows a statistically significant difference between treated and control branches. The pooled significance comes from combining all weeks, which may mask variability.

2. **Pre-existing differences**: Treated and control branches showed different patterns in the weeks before the switch (p = 0.09 for trend differences). This weakens confidence that the observed effect is purely due to the procedure change.

3. **Effect size is modest**: A 1.65-point improvement on a ~50-point scale is meaningful but not transformative.

4. **Unknown sustainability**: With only 8 weeks of post-switch data, we cannot determine if this effect will persist long-term.

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **Did the new procedure change service scores?** | Probably yes, but we cannot be certain. |
| **Is the effect large enough to matter?** | Modestly—about 3% improvement. |
| **Should we roll out to all branches immediately?** | **No.** The evidence does not support full confidence. |
| **Should we abandon the new procedure?** | **No.** The signal is positive, not negative. |
| **What should we do?** | **Expand carefully and keep measuring.** |

**Rationale:** The data shows a positive signal that is statistically detectable when all weeks are combined, but the lack of consistent week-by-week significance and the marginal pre-trend differences mean we cannot rule out that some of the observed effect is due to factors other than the procedure change. A phased rollout allows the cooperative to capture potential benefits while limiting risk if the effect proves temporary or smaller than estimated.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Phased rollout**: Expand the new procedure to 20-30 additional branches rather than all 60 remaining branches at once.

2. **Extended monitoring**: Track service scores for at least 12-16 weeks post-implementation to assess whether effects persist.

3. **Implementation fidelity**: Document whether branches are actually following the new procedure as intended—variation in implementation could explain inconsistent results.

4. **Cost tracking**: Before full rollout, quantify any additional labor or resource costs to ensure the service improvement justifies the expense.

5. **Re-evaluate in 3 months**: With more data, reassess whether to proceed with system-wide adoption or modify the procedure.

---

## **Technical Notes**

- **Method**: Difference-in-differences with cluster-robust standard errors
- **Sample**: 2,550 branch-week observations (150 branches × 17 weeks)
- **Concern**: Parallel trends assumption is not fully satisfied (pre-period differences marginally significant)
- **Interpretation**: Results suggest a positive effect but with meaningful uncertainty
