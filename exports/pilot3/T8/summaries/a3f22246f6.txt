# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores by approximately 2 points (4%).** This was unexpected—the change was not anticipated to affect service scores—but the data show a clear, statistically significant improvement. The 95% confidence interval [1.0 to 3.2 points] excludes zero. We recommend proceeding with the system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Effect size | +2.1 points |
| Confidence interval | 1.0 to 3.2 points |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 |
| Baseline score | ~49.5 |
| Percent improvement | 4.2% |

- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure; **60 branches** served as controls
- **Parallel trends confirmed** before the switch (p = 0.28), validating the comparison
- Effect persists after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

---

## **Final Verdict**

**ROLL OUT THE NEW PROCEDURE SYSTEM-WIDE.**

The evidence is clear and statistically robust. While the improvement was unexpected, the data demonstrate that the new shelving and rotation procedure produces a measurable gain in service quality at no apparent cost. With 150 branches studied over 17 weeks and a well-validated research design, we have high confidence in this result. Delaying rollout would mean withholding a proven improvement from patrons and staff.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** — Evidence supports expansion to all branches
2. **Monitor implementation** — Verify the effect replicates at scale
3. **Investigate the mechanism** — Understand why a shelving procedure affected service scores (staff time savings? patron experience?)
4. **Document the procedure** — Capture specifics for training

---

## **Methodology**

Difference-in-differences analysis with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch). This design isolates the procedure effect by comparing treatment branches to control branches before and after the switch.
