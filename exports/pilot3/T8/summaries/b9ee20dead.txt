# **Library Cooperative Branch Procedure Change: Impact Analysis**

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure **significantly improved service scores**. Branches that adopted the new procedure saw their service scores increase by approximately **2.2 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents a **4.3% improvement** over the average service score. The evidence supports rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measurable Improvement in Service Scores

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched Branches (90 branches) | 49.78 | 51.31 | +1.53 |
| Non-Switched Branches (60 branches) | 49.78 | 49.14 | -0.64 |
| **Net Effect (Difference-in-Differences)** | | | **+2.17** |

The statistical analysis confirms this effect is **highly significant** (p = 0.001), with a 95% confidence interval of [0.87, 3.48] points.

### 2. Strong Evidence of Causation

The analysis design allows us to confidently attribute the improvement to the new procedure:

- **Parallel trends confirmed**: Before the switch (weeks 6-14), switched and non-switched branches had virtually identical service scores (average difference: -0.004 points). This validates our comparison.
- **Consistent post-switch gains**: In every week after the switch (weeks 15-22), switched branches outperformed non-switched branches by 1.3 to 2.8 points.
- **Robust to controls**: The result holds even when accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume.

### 3. Effect Size in Context

- The 2.2-point improvement equals about **one-fifth of a standard deviation** in service scores
- Given the cooperative's scale (150 branches), this improvement would meaningfully elevate system-wide service quality
- No negative effects were detected on staffing efficiency or visitor patterns

### 4. What We Expected vs. What We Found

The note indicated you expected **no difference** in service scores. The data shows otherwise—the new procedure produced a **statistically significant positive effect**. This is welcome news: the procedure change improved service without the disruption you may have anticipated.

---

## **Final Verdict**

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout.**

| Criterion | Assessment |
|-----------|------------|
| Statistical evidence | ✅ Strong (p = 0.001) |
| Effect direction | ✅ Positive |
| Negative side effects | ✅ None detected |
| Practical significance | ⚠️ Modest but meaningful |
| Evidence durability | ⚠️ Only 8 weeks observed |

**Rationale:** The data provides clear evidence that the new procedure improves service scores without adverse consequences. While the effect is modest rather than transformative, the absence of any negative findings and the consistency of results across all post-switch weeks supports extending the procedure to remaining branches.

**Caveats:** The board should set realistic expectations (incremental gains, not transformation) and plan follow-up evaluation to confirm effects persist beyond the initial 8-week window.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** to the 60 branches still using the old procedure.

2. **Monitor implementation** by tracking service scores during rollout to ensure consistent results across all branches.

3. **Document best practices** to understand what specifically about the new procedure drives improvement (e.g., faster restocking, better organization) and optimize training accordingly.

4. **Schedule follow-up evaluation** in 3-6 months to confirm long-term effects and identify any branches that may need additional support during transition.

---

## **Analysis Notes**

- **Data**: 2,550 observations from 150 branches over 17 weeks (weeks 6-22)
- **Method**: Difference-in-differences regression with branch fixed effects and clustered standard errors
- **Switch date**: Week 15
- **Treatment group**: 90 branches (60% of cooperative)
- **Control group**: 60 branches (40% of cooperative)
