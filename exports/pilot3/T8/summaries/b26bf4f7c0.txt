# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

## **Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

---

### **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure is associated with improved service scores.** Branches that switched saw scores rise by approximately **2 points** relative to branches that did not switch. The effect is statistically significant and sustained over 8 weeks of observation.

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout.**

---

### **KEY FINDINGS**

**Study Overview**
- **709 branches** analyzed over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- **425 branches** switched to the new procedure at week 15; **284 branches** maintained the old procedure
- Analysis uses difference-in-differences methodology with controls for staffing and visitor volume

**Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Branches that switched | 51.12 | 54.04 | +2.92 |
| Branches that did not switch | 49.91 | 50.73 | +0.82 |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | | **+2.10** |

**Statistical Confidence**
- **95% confidence interval:** 1.4 to 2.8 points
- **p-value:** < 0.001
- Effect persists across all 8 post-switch weeks with no decay

**Important Context**
- Treatment branches showed some improvement relative to control branches even before week 15 (gap widened from ~0 to ~2 points in weeks 6–14)
- This suggests branches selected to switch early may have been on a more positive trajectory
- Even accounting for this, the post-switch gap (~3.3 points) exceeds the late pre-period gap (~2.1 points), indicating additional improvement attributable to the procedure change

---

### **VERDICT**

**The evidence supports rollout, with moderate confidence.**

The data are consistent with a genuine positive effect of the new procedure, though we cannot rule out that some portion of the observed improvement reflects pre-existing differences between early-adopter and other branches. The 2-point estimate is likely an upper bound; a more conservative expectation would be **1 to 1.5 points** of improvement per branch.

Given that:
- The effect is in the expected direction
- There is no evidence of harm or score degradation
- The procedure change is unlikely to have meaningful downside risk
- Even a 1-point improvement represents meaningful service enhancement across 709 branches

**The board should approve system-wide rollout.**

---

### **NEXT STEPS**

1. **Approve full rollout** of the new procedure to all branches

2. **Monitor service scores** quarterly for 12 months post-rollout to confirm sustained improvement

3. **Track implementation fidelity** to ensure the procedure is applied consistently across branches

4. **Re-evaluate at 6 months**—if scores do not show expected improvement, investigate implementation barriers

---

*Analysis based on weekly branch data, weeks 6–22. Difference-in-differences methodology with controls for staffing index, visits index, and branch characteristics.*
