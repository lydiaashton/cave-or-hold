# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

## **Subject: Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

**Date:** November 14, 2026  
**Prepared by:** Data Analytics Office

---

### **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure improved service scores—and we did not expect it to.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **1.9 points** (about **4% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant, consistent across the post-switch period, and robust to alternative explanations. 

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all remaining branches. This is a rare opportunity where an operational change delivered measurable benefits without the costs we anticipated.

---

### **KEY FINDINGS**

**1. Study Design**
- 150 branches tracked over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- 90 branches switched to the new procedure at week 15; 60 branches kept the old procedure
- This natural experiment allows us to isolate the effect of the new procedure from other factors

**2. Main Result**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches (new procedure) | 51.1 | 53.4 | +2.3 |
| Non-switched branches (old procedure) | 50.6 | 50.9 | +0.3 |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | | **+2.0** |

After adjusting for staffing levels and visitor volume, the estimated effect is **+1.91 points** (95% confidence interval: 0.78 to 3.03; p = 0.001).

**3. Why We Can Trust This Result**

| Concern | How We Addressed It |
|---------|---------------------|
| Were the groups different to start? | No—pre-switch trends were parallel; no significant differences before week 15 |
| Could staffing or visits explain it? | No—effect persists after controlling for both factors |
| Is this just random noise? | No—p-value of 0.001 indicates less than 0.1% chance this is random |
| Did the effect fade quickly? | No—positive effects persisted across all 8 post-switch weeks |

**4. Practical Significance**
- Service scores average around 50; a 2-point gain represents meaningful improvement
- The improvement is comparable to moving a branch from the 50th percentile to roughly the 58th percentile of performance
- No additional investment was required beyond the procedural change itself

---

### **FINAL VERDICT**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **Did the new procedure change service scores?** | **Yes** |
| **Direction of change?** | **Positive** (+1.91 points) |
| **Statistically significant?** | **Yes** (p = 0.0009) |
| **Confidence in result?** | **High** (95% CI: 0.78 to 3.03; robust to controls) |
| **Recommendation?** | **Roll out to all branches** |

**Confidence Level: HIGH** ✓

This conclusion rests on three pillars:
1. **Strong research design** – treatment and control groups followed parallel trends before the switch
2. **Consistent evidence** – every post-switch week showed positive effects; results hold with controls
3. **Low risk of false positive** – less than 1 in 1,000 chance this result is random noise

---

### **A NOTE ON THE SURPRISE**

We entered this evaluation expecting no measurable impact on service scores. The positive result suggests the new procedure may have unintended benefits—perhaps faster item retrieval, improved shelf organization, or staff workflow efficiencies that translate into better patron experiences. We recommend capturing qualitative feedback from staff at switched branches to understand *why* this worked, which could inform future operational improvements.
