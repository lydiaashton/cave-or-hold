# **Board Summary: Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure produced a measurable improvement in service scores.** Contrary to our expectation that this operational change would have no effect on service quality, branches that adopted the new procedure saw scores increase by an average of **1.7 points** (approximately **3.5%**) relative to branches that kept the old procedure. The effect is statistically significant (p = 0.003) with a 95% confidence interval of 0.6 to 2.9 points. While modest in magnitude, this represents a genuine improvement with no identified downside. We recommend proceeding with the rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Study Design
- **150 branches** over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure at week 15; **60 branches** served as controls
- **Difference-in-Differences** analysis isolates the effect of the procedure change from other factors

### 2. Treatment Effect
| Group | Before (Weeks 6–14) | After (Weeks 15–22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------|---------------------|--------|
| Control (Old Procedure) | 49.9 | 49.7 | -0.2 |
| Treatment (New Procedure) | 50.1 | 51.7 | +1.6 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+1.7 points** |

- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.003
- **95% confidence interval:** 0.6 to 2.9 points
- **Effect size:** 0.18 standard deviations (small-to-moderate)
- Result holds after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volumes

### 3. Why This Matters
We expected this back-office procedural change to have **no measurable impact** on patron-facing service scores. The positive effect suggests the new procedure may:
- Improve staff efficiency in assisting patrons
- Enhance stock accessibility in ways that benefit service delivery
- Produce secondary benefits we did not anticipate

### 4. Analysis Validity
- **Parallel trends confirmed:** Pre-switch trajectories were statistically indistinguishable (p = 0.68)
- **Robust specification:** Effect persists with branch fixed effects and clustered standard errors
- **Consistent signal:** Positive effect observed throughout the 8-week post-switch period
