# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Library Cooperative Board  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 14, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**We cannot confidently conclude the new procedure caused the improvement.** While branches that switched show higher scores, the data reveals they were **already improving relative to other branches BEFORE the switch**. This means the observed improvement may reflect pre-existing differences, not the procedure change itself.

**Recommendation: Pause system-wide rollout and gather more evidence.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. The Apparent Effect (Initial Analysis)**
- Branches that switched: Scores increased from 51.1 to 54.0 (+2.9 points)
- Branches that didn't switch: Scores increased from 49.9 to 50.7 (+0.8 points)
- **Initial estimate: +2.1 point effect** (statistically significant)

### **2. The Critical Problem: Pre-Existing Trends**

| Period | Treatment-Control Gap |
|--------|----------------------|
| Week 6 | 0.01 points |
| Week 9 | 1.44 points |
| Week 12 | 1.91 points |
| Week 14 (before switch) | 2.37 points |
| Week 15+ (after switch) | 3.31 points |

**The gap was already widening BEFORE week 15.** Statistical test confirms this pre-trend is significant (p = 0.001). This violates the key assumption needed to claim causation.

### **3. What Happens When We Account for This?**

When analyzing only weeks closer to the switch (where pre-trends were more stable):

| Analysis Window | Estimated Effect | Statistical Significance |
|-----------------|------------------|-------------------------|
| Weeks 6-22 (full) | 2.10 points | Significant (p < 0.001) |
| Weeks 10-22 | 1.33 points | **Not significant** (p = 0.71) |
| Weeks 12-22 | 1.23 points | **Not significant** (p = 0.89) |

**The effect disappears when we use a more appropriate comparison window.**

### **4. What We Can Say**
- There IS a positive association between switching and service scores
- 79% of switching branches improved vs. 57% of non-switching branches
- BUT we cannot determine if the procedure caused this, or if switching branches were already on a better trajectory

---

## **Why This Matters**

The branches selected to switch may have differed systematically from those that didn't—perhaps they were:
- Better managed
- Had more motivated staff
- Were already experimenting with improvements
- Had different community characteristics

Without random assignment, we cannot separate these factors from the procedure's actual effect.

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **Did service scores improve in switching branches?** | Yes |
| **Can we attribute this to the new procedure?** | No — evidence is insufficient |
| **Should we roll out to all branches now?** | No — too much uncertainty |
| **What should we do instead?** | Pause, monitor, and gather better evidence |

**The switching branches ARE performing better, but we do not know WHY.** Rolling out system-wide based on this evidence risks:
- Implementing a procedure that may not actually work
- Missing the real drivers of improvement
- Wasting resources on an ineffective change

---

## **Recommendation**

**Do NOT proceed with system-wide rollout at this time.** Instead:

| Action | Timeline | Rationale |
|--------|----------|-----------|
| **Pause full rollout** | Immediate | Evidence is insufficient |
| **Continue monitoring** | Ongoing | Track whether switching branches maintain their advantage |
| **Gather qualitative data** | 4 weeks | Interview staff about what's actually driving improvements |
| **Consider random assignment** | Next pilot | For future tests, randomly assign branches to isolate causal effects |
| **Re-evaluate** | 3 months | More post-switch data may clarify the picture |

---

## **If the Board Chooses to Proceed Anyway**

Should the Board decide the potential benefit outweighs the uncertainty:

1. **Implement phased rollout** with careful monitoring
2. **Set clear success metrics** (e.g., must see 1.5+ point improvement sustained for 8 weeks)
3. **Be prepared to reverse course** if effects don't materialize
4. **Document implementation details** to identify what works

---

## **Bottom Line (Restated)**

The data shows switching branches have higher scores, but we cannot prove the new procedure caused this. The branches that switched were already outperforming before the change. **More rigorous evidence is needed before committing to system-wide rollout.**
