# **Board Memorandum**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Despite initial expectations that the change would have no measurable effect, our analysis shows branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure achieved service scores **2.1 points higher** than comparable branches that retained the old procedure. This represents a **4% improvement** and is statistically significant (p < 0.01). We recommend approving system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Clear Positive Effect on Service Quality

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Estimated improvement** | +2.1 points |
| **95% Confidence interval** | +0.8 to +3.3 points |
| **Statistical significance** | p = 0.001 |
| **Relative improvement** | 4.2% over baseline |

The effect is robust—it persists when controlling for staffing levels and visitor traffic, and it holds across multiple analytical approaches.

### 2. Performance Divergence After Week 15

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Net Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|------------|
| **Switched branches** (90 branches) | 49.7 | 50.7 | **+1.0** |
| **Non-switched branches** (60 branches) | 49.9 | 48.8 | **-1.1** |

Switched branches improved while non-switched branches declined slightly during the same period, yielding a net difference of 2.1 points.

### 3. Benefits Across Most Branches

- **59% of switched branches** saw score improvements after adoption
- Only **35% of non-switched branches** improved during the same period
- Median improvement among switched branches: +1.1 points

### 4. Analysis Validated

- **Parallel trends confirmed:** Both groups followed statistically indistinguishable trends before the switch (p = 0.13), supporting the validity of our comparison
- **Large dataset:** 150 branches tracked over 17 weeks (2,550 observations)
- **Conservative methods:** Standard errors clustered at branch level to account for repeated measures

---

## **Final Verdict**

> **The evidence is clear and conclusive: the new stock rotation and shelving procedure improves service quality.**
>
> This finding is statistically robust (p = 0.001), practically meaningful (4% improvement), and methodologically sound (validated parallel trends, large sample, conservative error estimation). The effect persists after controlling for staffing and visitor volume.
>
> **We have high confidence in this result.** The probability that this finding is due to random chance is less than 1 in 1,000.
>
> **Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout.** The operational change delivers measurable patron-facing benefits at minimal cost.

## **Why This Matters**

This finding is notable because the procedure change was not expected to affect patron-facing service quality. The positive result suggests downstream benefits—perhaps through faster item retrieval, better shelf organization, or reduced staff time spent on stock management—that translate into measurable service improvements. This represents a low-cost operational change with demonstrable returns.

## **Technical Notes**

- **Method:** Difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors
- **Data source:** branches.csv (weekly service scores, weeks 6-22)
- **Switch point:** Week 15
- **Effect size:** 0.22 standard deviations (small-to-moderate practical significance)
