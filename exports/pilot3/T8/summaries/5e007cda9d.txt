# **Memorandum to the Board**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** [Current Date]  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure  

---

## **Bottom Line**

**We cannot confidently conclude the new procedure caused the improvement.** While branches that switched show higher service scores after the change, this pattern was already emerging *before* the switch occurred. The data is consistent with the new procedure having a positive effect, but it is equally consistent with pre-existing differences between branches driving the results. **We do not recommend a full rollout based on this evidence alone.**

---

## **Final Verdict**

> **DO NOT APPROVE SYSTEM-WIDE ROLLOUT AT THIS TIME**
>
> **Reason:** The analysis cannot establish causation. The observed improvement likely reflects pre-existing differences between branches rather than the procedure change itself.
>
> **What would change our verdict:**
> - A randomized controlled trial showing similar results, OR
> - An additional 8–12 weeks of data demonstrating the gap continues to widen beyond pre-existing trends, OR
> - Qualitative evidence from staff confirming operational benefits independent of score trends

---

## **Key Findings**

### **What the Data Shows**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Observed score difference (post-switch) | +2.0 points |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 |
| **BUT: Pre-existing trend in gap** | **+0.26 points/week (p < 0.001)** |

### **The Critical Problem: Parallel Trends Violated**

The standard analysis method assumes that, without the intervention, treatment and control branches would have followed similar trajectories. **This assumption does not hold:**

| Period | Gap Trend (Treatment vs. Control) |
|--------|-----------------------------------|
| Weeks 6–14 (before switch) | Widening by 0.26 points/week |
| Weeks 15–22 (after switch) | Widening by 0.24 points/week |
| **Change in trend** | **Essentially zero** |

The gap continued widening at the same rate after the switch as before. This suggests the observed improvement may reflect pre-existing differences rather than the procedure change.

### **Supporting Evidence of the Problem**

1. **Placebo test**: When we pretend the switch happened at week 12 (when it didn't), we still find a "significant effect" of +2.1 points. This indicates the methodology detects effects even where none exist.

2. **Event study**: The treatment-control gap was already significantly different from zero in every pre-treatment week, and growing larger over time.

3. **Restricted sample**: Using only the 4 weeks immediately before the switch, the estimated effect drops from +2.0 to +1.5 points.

---

## **What We Can Say**

| Statement | Supported? |
|-----------|------------|
| Switching branches have higher scores than non-switching branches | ✓ Yes |
| The gap widened after week 15 | ✓ Yes |
| The new procedure *caused* the improvement | ✗ **Uncertain** |
| Rolling out system-wide will improve scores | ✗ **Uncertain** |

---

## **Recommendations**

| Priority | Action |
|----------|--------|
| **1** | **Do not proceed with full rollout** based on current evidence |
| **2** | **Extend the evaluation period** – collect 8–12 more weeks of data |
| **3** | **Consider a randomized rollout** – randomly assign remaining branches to switch now vs. later to enable cleaner causal inference |
| **4** | **Investigate branch selection** – understand why certain branches chose to switch; this may reveal confounding factors |
| **5** | **Gather qualitative feedback** – survey staff at switching branches about perceived benefits and challenges |

---

## **Path Forward**

If the cooperative wishes to make a confident recommendation, we suggest:

1. **Randomized controlled trial**: Randomly assign half the remaining branches to adopt the procedure immediately and half to wait 8–12 weeks. Compare outcomes.

2. **Extended monitoring**: Continue tracking all branches for another quarter to see if the gap stabilizes, grows, or shrinks.

3. **Cost-benefit consideration**: If the new procedure has minimal costs and staff report positive experiences, a cautious phased rollout may be reasonable despite uncertain evidence. If costs are significant, stronger evidence is warranted before proceeding.

---

## **Bottom Line Restated**

The observed 2-point improvement is **real** but its **cause is uncertain**. The data cannot distinguish between:
- The new procedure genuinely improving service, versus
- Branches that chose to switch already being on an upward trajectory

**Additional evidence is needed before committing to system-wide implementation.**
