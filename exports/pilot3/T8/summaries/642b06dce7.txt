# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 14, 2025  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure shows a **modest positive effect** on service scores (+1.9 points, or ~4%), though the evidence falls just short of conventional statistical significance (p = 0.10). There is **no evidence of harm**, and the data suggest the true effect is likely neutral to moderately positive.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Overview**
- **34 branches** participated: 20 adopted the new procedure, 14 maintained the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** collected (weeks 6–22), with the procedure change occurring at week 15
- Analysis compares service score changes between the two groups using standard difference-in-differences methods

### **Service Score Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 47.4 | 47.2 | −0.3 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 46.6 | 48.2 | +1.6 |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | | | **+1.9** |

### **Statistical Assessment**
- **Estimated effect:** +1.9 points on the service score index (approximately 4% of the average score of 50)
- **95% confidence interval:** −0.4 to +4.3 points
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.10 (not significant at the 5% level)

### **Interpretation**
The data suggest the new procedure may improve service scores, but the evidence is not strong enough to rule out random variation. The confidence interval indicates the true effect could range from slightly negative to moderately positive. Pre-switch trends were similar between groups, supporting the validity of the comparison.

---

## **Final Verdict**

**Approve a phased rollout with continued monitoring.**

The evidence leans positive—there is no signal of harm, and the observed effect is meaningful in practical terms (~4% improvement). While statistical certainty would require more data, waiting for definitive proof may delay benefits unnecessarily. A phased approach balances opportunity with prudence:

- **Proceed** with offering the new procedure to additional interested branches
- **Retain** some branches on the old procedure as an ongoing control group
- **Re-evaluate** after 12 additional weeks of data

This allows the cooperative to capture potential benefits while maintaining the ability to detect problems or confirm the effect with greater precision.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Identify 5–10 additional branches** for Phase 2 rollout (volunteers preferred)
2. **Maintain 10+ branches** on the old procedure for continued comparison
3. **Document implementation** at each branch to ensure consistency
4. **Collect 12 more weeks of data** and reconvene in Q1 2026
5. **Track secondary metrics** (staff time, patron feedback, operational costs)
