# **MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 14, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **1.85 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents a **3.7% improvement** over the baseline average. The effect is statistically significant and has grown stronger over time since implementation.

**Recommendation: Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### Study Overview
- **709 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **425 branches** adopted the new procedure; **284 branches** kept the old procedure
- Switch occurred at **week 15**
- Analysis compares changes in treatment branches to changes in control branches

### Main Result

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect | **+1.85 points** |
| 95% Confidence interval | **1.30 to 2.41 points** |
| Statistical significance | **p < 0.001** |
| Relative improvement | **3.7%** |

### Effect Over Time

The benefit of the new procedure **increased over time**:

| Period | Average Improvement |
|--------|---------------------|
| Weeks 15–17 (immediate) | ~1.2 points |
| Weeks 18–22 (later) | ~2.2 points |

This pattern suggests branches needed a few weeks to fully realize the benefits of the new procedure.

### Validity Checks

✓ **Comparable groups:** Service score trends for treatment and control branches were similar before the switch (7 of 8 pre-treatment weeks showed no significant difference)

✓ **Robust to controls:** Result holds after adjusting for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume

✓ **Consistent estimates:** Multiple analytical approaches yield similar results (1.8 to 1.9 points)

✓ **All post-treatment effects positive:** Every week after the switch showed improvement

### Practical Significance

- The 1.85-point improvement equals about **0.19 standard deviations**—a modest but meaningful effect for a service quality metric
- Given the cooperative serves 709 branches, this improvement aggregates to substantial system-wide gains
- The growing effect over time suggests continued benefits as staff gain familiarity

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

**Confidence Level: HIGH**

The evidence strongly supports adopting the new procedure system-wide:

| Criterion | Assessment |
|-----------|------------|
| Statistical significance | ✓ Passed (p < 0.001) |
| Confidence interval excludes zero | ✓ Passed [1.30, 2.41] |
| Consistent direction of effect | ✓ Passed (8/8 weeks positive) |
| Plausible mechanism | ✓ Passed (effect grows with experience) |
| Robust across methods | ✓ Passed (DiD, event study, bootstrap agree) |

**Limitations to Monitor:**
- Effect size is modest (~0.2 standard deviations)
- One pre-treatment week showed baseline differences between groups
- Long-term sustainability beyond 8 weeks is untested

**Overall:** The analytical evidence is strong and consistent. The risk of proceeding with rollout is low, and the potential system-wide benefit is substantial.

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to the remaining 284 branches

2. **Develop implementation timeline** with consideration for:
   - Staff training requirements
   - Phased rollout to manage operational disruption
   - Target completion within 2–3 months

3. **Continue monitoring** service scores after full rollout to:
   - Confirm the effect persists at scale
   - Identify branches that may need additional support
   - Track whether benefits continue to grow with experience

4. **Document best practices** from the 425 early-adopter branches to support training

5. **Consider cost-benefit analysis** if implementation costs vary across branches, to prioritize rollout sequence
