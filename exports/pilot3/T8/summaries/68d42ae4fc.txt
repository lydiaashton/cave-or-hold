# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 14, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure  

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The evidence is inconclusive.** We cannot confirm that the new procedure improves service scores. The observed 2.2-point difference could reflect a real benefit, but it could also be due to random variation. **We recommend collecting more data before making any rollout decision.**

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did the new procedure improve service scores? | **Cannot determine** |
| Is the effect statistically significant? | **No** (p = 0.13) |
| Should we roll out to all branches now? | **No** |
| Should we abandon the new procedure? | **No** — evidence is insufficient, not negative |
| Recommended action | **Continue monitoring; decide after more data** |

**Rationale:** The 95% confidence interval (-0.62 to +4.99) includes zero, meaning the data are compatible with no effect, a small negative effect, or a moderate positive effect. With current evidence, neither rollout nor abandonment is justified.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **Study Overview**
| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| Total branches | 34 |
| Adopted new procedure | 20 branches |
| Kept old procedure (control) | 14 branches |
| Study period | 17 weeks (Weeks 6–22) |
| Switch implemented | Week 15 |

### **Service Score Impact**

| Period | Switched Branches | Control Branches |
|--------|------------------|------------------|
| Before switch (Weeks 6–14) | 52.83 | 51.34 |
| After switch (Weeks 15–22) | 53.61 | 49.95 |
| **Change** | **+0.79** | **-1.40** |

**Difference-in-Differences Estimate: +2.19 points**

### **Statistical Confidence**
| Metric | Value | Interpretation |
|--------|-------|----------------|
| P-value | 0.13 | Not statistically significant |
| 95% Confidence Interval | -0.62 to +4.99 | Includes zero (no effect) |
| Effect Size (Cohen's d) | 0.22 | Small |

**What this means:** The data are compatible with:
- No effect at all
- A small negative effect (up to -0.6 points)
- A moderate positive effect (up to +5.0 points)

We cannot distinguish among these possibilities with current data.

### **Branch-Level Patterns**
- **45%** of switched branches showed score improvements (9 of 20)
- **36%** of control branches showed score improvements (5 of 14)
- Wide variation in outcomes across all branches

### **Important Context**
Control branches experienced a notable **decline** in service scores during the study period (-1.40 points). Switched branches avoided this decline and achieved a modest improvement (+0.79 points). This pattern is encouraging but not statistically reliable with current sample size.

---

## **NEXT STEPS & RECOMMENDATIONS**

### **1. Do Not Expand Yet — Gather More Evidence**
Given the statistical uncertainty, we recommend **pausing any expansion** until stronger evidence is available. Premature rollout risks investing in a procedure that may not deliver benefits.

### **2. Extend the Current Evaluation**
Continue monitoring all 34 branches for an additional **12–16 weeks** to:
- Increase statistical power
- Narrow the confidence interval
- Determine whether the observed pattern persists

### **3. Pre-Specify a Decision Rule**
Before collecting more data, establish clear criteria for rollout, such as:
- P-value < 0.05, OR
- 95% CI entirely above zero, OR
- Minimum effect size of X points with 80% confidence

### **4. Investigate Implementation Variation**
The wide variation in branch-level outcomes suggests implementation quality may matter. Document:
- How faithfully each branch adopted the new procedure
- Staff training levels
- Any operational challenges encountered

### **5. Decision Timeline**
| Milestone | Target Date |
|-----------|-------------|
| Continue current monitoring | Q1 2027 |
| Interim analysis (8 additional weeks) | Q2 2027 |
| Final analysis (16 additional weeks) | Q3 2027 |
| Rollout decision | Q3–Q4 2027 |

---

## **CONCLUSION**

The current data show an encouraging pattern but do not provide sufficient evidence to justify system-wide implementation. The responsible course is to **continue monitoring and collect more data** before committing cooperative resources to a full rollout.
