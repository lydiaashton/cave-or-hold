# **Board Memorandum**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Office  
**Date:** [Current Date]  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new rotation and shelving procedure shows a **positive effect on service scores**, consistent with our expectations. Branches that adopted the new procedure improved by approximately **1.7 points** relative to those that kept the old procedure. While this result is not statistically significant at conventional levels, the evidence points in the right direction with no signs of negative impact. We recommend **continuing the rollout while extending the evaluation period** to confirm the effect with greater precision.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Overview**
| Element | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| Branches studied | 34 total (20 switched, 14 did not) |
| Study period | 17 weeks (Weeks 6–22) |
| Switch date | Week 15 |
| Method | Difference-in-Differences analysis |

### **Service Score Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| **New Procedure** | 53.45 | 53.96 | +0.50 |
| **Old Procedure** | 49.03 | 47.84 | −1.19 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+1.69 points** |

**Interpretation:** Branches using the new procedure maintained their service scores while control branches experienced a slight decline. The net benefit of **+1.7 points** represents approximately a **3% improvement** relative to the baseline average of 50.

### **Statistical Confidence**
- **Primary estimate:** +1.69 points (p = 0.16)
- **95% confidence interval:** −0.7 to +4.1 points
- **Conclusion:** The effect is positive but we cannot rule out chance as an explanation with high confidence

### **Validity Assessment**

| Check | Result | Implication |
|-------|--------|-------------|
| **Parallel trends** | ✅ Passed (p = 0.996) | Treatment and control groups were on similar trajectories before the switch |
| **Pre-trend coefficients** | ✅ All insignificant | No evidence of diverging trends prior to implementation |
| **Post-trend pattern** | ✅ Consistently positive | All 8 post-switch weeks show positive effects |
| **Control variables** | ✅ Robust | Results hold when accounting for staffing and visit levels |

The attached visualization (`did_analysis.png`) illustrates two key points:

## **Recommendations**

### **Immediate Actions**

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| **1** | **Continue the rollout** | Effect is in the expected direction with no evidence of harm |
| **2** | **Extend data collection 8–12 weeks** | Would increase power to detect modest effects with confidence |
| **3** | **Monitor implementation fidelity** | Ensure branches are following the new procedure consistently |

### **Further Analysis**

1. **Subgroup analysis:** Examine whether certain branch types (size, location, patron volume) benefit more from the new procedure

2. **Cost-benefit assessment:** Compare the service score gains against implementation and training costs

3. **Complementary metrics:** Track operational efficiency, staff satisfaction, and patron wait times alongside service scores

---

## **Decision Framework**

| Scenario | Recommendation |
|----------|----------------|
| **Low implementation cost** | Proceed with full rollout; continue monitoring |
| **High implementation cost** | Pause and gather more data before expanding |
| **Operational benefits beyond scores** | Strong case for proceeding regardless of statistical significance |

---

## **Conclusion**

The new procedure is delivering results **in line with expectations**. While we would prefer stronger statistical confirmation, the combination of a positive effect size, clean validity checks, and no adverse signals supports moving forward. Extending the evaluation period will provide the Board with greater confidence in the magnitude of the benefit.
