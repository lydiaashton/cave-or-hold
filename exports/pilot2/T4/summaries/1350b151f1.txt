# **Board Memorandum**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** January 2025  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure is associated with **higher service scores**. Branches that adopted the new procedure maintained their performance while control branches experienced a decline. The estimated improvement is **+1.8 points** on the service score index. Although this result does not reach conventional statistical significance with the current data, the direction of the effect aligns with expectations, and there is no evidence of harm. We recommend proceeding with broader implementation while continuing to monitor outcomes.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Overview**
| | |
|---|---|
| Branches analyzed | 34 |
| Treatment branches (new procedure) | 20 |
| Control branches (old procedure) | 14 |
| Observation period | Weeks 6–22 |
| Switch date | Week 15 |

### **Impact on Service Scores**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Treatment (new procedure) | 54.4 | 54.6 | **+0.2** |
| Control (old procedure) | 48.1 | 46.6 | **-1.5** |
| **Net Effect (DiD Estimate)** | | | **+1.8** |

### **Statistical Summary**
- **Estimated effect:** +1.82 points
- **95% confidence interval:** -0.71 to +4.36 points
- **Interpretation:** We estimate the true effect is likely between slightly negative and moderately positive, with the best estimate being a meaningful improvement

### **Supporting Evidence**
1. **Pre-trends validated:** No systematic differences between groups before the switch, confirming the analysis is sound
2. **Effects strengthened over time:** Weeks 21–22 showed larger gains (+4.9 to +7.4 points, statistically significant), suggesting benefits may grow as staff gain experience
3. **Consistent across specifications:** The +1.8 point estimate holds whether we account for staffing levels, visitor traffic, or branch-specific factors

---

## **Recommendations**

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| **High** | Continue current rollout | Positive effect direction with no evidence of harm |
| **High** | Extend monitoring period | Effects appear to grow over time; more data will increase confidence |
| **Medium** | Gather staff feedback | Identify which aspects of the new procedure drive improvements |
| **Medium** | Plan full implementation | Current evidence supports expansion to remaining branches |
| **Low** | Re-evaluate in 6 months | Larger sample will provide more precise estimates |

---

## **Context for Decision-Making**

- The average branch service score is approximately **50 points**; a 1.8-point gain represents roughly **3–4% improvement**
- Control branches declined after week 15, suggesting external factors may have affected service quality system-wide; the new procedure appears to have **protected treatment branches from this decline**
- The lack of statistical significance reflects limited data (17 weeks, 34 branches), not necessarily a lack of real effect

---

## **Conclusion**

The evidence supports moving forward with the new procedure. While we cannot claim definitive proof of improvement with the current data, the positive signal, growing effects over time, and absence of negative outcomes justify continued implementation. Additional monitoring will strengthen the evidence base for future decisions.
