# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Office  
**DATE:** [Current Date]  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure **has not demonstrably improved service scores**. While branches using the new procedure showed a modest 1.7-point advantage compared to expectations, this difference is not statistically distinguishable from random variation. Based on the available data, we cannot confirm that the new procedure has enhanced service quality.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. No Statistically Significant Impact**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect | +1.7 points on service score index |
| Statistical significance | Not significant (p = 0.16) |
| 95% confidence interval | -0.6 to +4.0 points |

The confidence interval includes zero, meaning the true effect could be slightly negative, neutral, or modestly positive.

### **2. Valid Comparison Between Groups**
- Treatment branches (new procedure): 20 branches
- Control branches (old procedure): 14 branches
- Pre-switch trends were parallel, confirming the groups are comparable
- No evidence that switching branches were already improving faster before the change

### **3. Study Limitations**
- **Sample size is small**: Only 34 branches limits our ability to detect modest effects
- **Minimum detectable effect**: Approximately 14 points with 80% confidence
- **Observed effect**: 1.7 points—well below the detection threshold
- **Conclusion**: A real but small benefit could exist that this analysis cannot confirm

### **4. Stable Group Differences**
Branches that adopted the new procedure consistently score 4–5 points higher than control branches, but this gap existed *before* the switch and reflects inherent branch characteristics rather than the procedure itself.

---

## **Recommendations**

| Priority | Action | Timeline |
|----------|--------|----------|
| **High** | Continue collecting service score data to extend the post-switch observation period | Ongoing |
| **High** | Survey branch staff on workflow efficiency, time savings, and ease of implementation | 1–2 months |
| **Medium** | Analyze whether effects vary by branch size, location, or circulation volume | 2–3 months |
| **Medium** | Evaluate alternative outcome measures (hold fulfillment time, shelf accuracy, staff hours) | 3–6 months |
| **Low** | Reconduct formal analysis with 6–12 months of additional data | 6–12 months |

---

## **Decision Considerations**

Given the inconclusive results, the Board may wish to consider:

1. **Continue the rollout**: If the procedure offers operational benefits not captured by service scores (e.g., staff efficiency, inventory accuracy), proceeding may still be warranted.

2. **Pause and refine**: If implementation challenges were reported, consider gathering feedback and adjusting the procedure before broader adoption.

3. **Maintain flexibility**: Allow branches to opt in or out based on local conditions and staff preference until clearer evidence emerges.

---

## **Appendix: Analysis Summary**

- **Method**: Difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors
- **Observation period**: Weeks 6–22 (17 weeks total; switch occurred at week 15)
- **Total observations**: 578 branch-week records
- **Statistical software**: Python with statsmodels package
