# **BOARD MEMORANDUM**

## **Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

**Date:** November 2026  
**Analysis Period:** Weeks 6–22 (17 weeks of data)  
**Scope:** 709 branches (425 switched to new procedure; 284 retained old procedure)

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure improved service scores by 2.1 points—a 4% gain that is statistically significant and growing over time.** We recommend expanding the procedure to all branches with appropriate training and support.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### 1. Clear Positive Impact
| Metric | Switched Branches | Control Branches | Net Effect |
|--------|------------------|------------------|------------|
| Before (Weeks 6–14) | 51.59 | 50.95 | — |
| After (Weeks 15–22) | 52.77 | 50.00 | — |
| **Change** | **+1.18** | **-0.95** | **+2.13** |

The 2.1-point improvement is highly statistically significant (p < 0.001) and holds after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume.

### 2. Benefits Accumulate Over Time
The gap between switched and control branches widened from 2.3 points in week 15 to 3.7 points by week 22. This suggests branches continue gaining proficiency and realizing additional benefits as they gain experience with the new procedure.

### 3. Control Branches Declined Slightly
Branches retaining the old procedure experienced a modest decline (~1 point) after week 15. This may reflect seasonal patterns or operational pressures that the new procedure helps mitigate.

### 4. Selection Bias Noted
Branches that opted into the new procedure had slightly higher baseline service scores (~0.6 points) before the switch. Our analysis accounts for this pre-existing difference, though the true effect could be marginally smaller than estimated.
