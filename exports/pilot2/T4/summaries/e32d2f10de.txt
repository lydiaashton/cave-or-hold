# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

---

## **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

**Date:** [Current Date]  
**Prepared by:** Data Analysis Team

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure is associated with **improved service scores**, but benefits took time to emerge. During the first month after implementation, there was no detectable effect. However, in the second month, branches using the new procedure showed **significantly higher service scores—approximately 3.5 points higher** than comparable branches that kept the old procedure. This represents roughly a **7% improvement** relative to the baseline.

**Recommendation:** Continue the rollout, but invest in stronger initial training to accelerate the learning curve.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### 1. Study Overview
| Component | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| Branches studied | 34 total (20 switched, 14 did not) |
| Observation period | 17 weeks (weeks 6-22) |
| Switch date | Week 15 |
| Outcome measure | Service score index (baseline ~50; higher is better) |

### 2. Impact on Service Scores

| Time Period | Effect Estimate | Statistical Significance |
|-------------|-----------------|-------------------------|
| **Overall** (weeks 15-22) | +2.1 points | Not significant (p=0.19) |
| **Early** (weeks 15-18) | +0.7 points | Not significant (p=0.66) |
| **Late** (weeks 19-22) | **+3.5 points** | **Significant (p=0.04)** |

### 3. What the Data Shows

- **Before the switch** (weeks 6-14): Service score trends were parallel between switching and non-switching branches, validating our comparison approach.

- **After the switch**: 
  - Switched branches improved from 50.1 to 50.9 (+0.8)
  - Non-switched branches declined from 50.9 to 49.3 (-1.6)
  - **Net attributable effect: +2.4 points**

### 4. Important Context

- **Staffing changes**: Branches that switched showed a modest increase in staffing levels (p=0.05). This may reflect additional labor needs during implementation or could partially explain the service score gains.

- **Visitor volume**: No meaningful difference in visitor trends between groups, suggesting the effect is not driven by changes in demand.

## **TECHNICAL APPENDIX**

**Methodology:** Difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch). Models control for weekly staffing and visitor volume indices.

**Data:** 578 branch-week observations from branches.csv

**Limitations:**
- Relatively short post-implementation window (8 weeks)
- Potential confounding from staffing changes
- Cannot rule out unobserved time-varying factors
