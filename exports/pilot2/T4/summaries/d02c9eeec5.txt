# **Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

**Date:** November 2026  
**Analysis Period:** Weeks 6–22  
**Prepared for:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure **improved service scores by approximately 2 points** (about 4% relative to the 50-point scale average). This effect is statistically significant and strengthens over time as branches gain experience. We recommend continuing the rollout while providing implementation support during the transition period.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Total branches | 709 |
| Branches switched to new procedure | 425 (60%) |
| Branches kept old procedure | 284 (40%) |
| Study period | 17 weeks (weeks 6–22) |
| Switch implemented | Week 15 |

### Service Score Results

| Group | Pre-Switch | Post-Switch | Change |
|-------|------------|-------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 51.0 | 50.0 | −1.0 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 51.6 | 52.8 | +1.2 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+2.1** |

### Statistical Confidence
- **Estimated treatment effect:** +2.08 points
- **95% confidence interval:** 1.55 to 2.61 points
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001

### Effect Timeline
The benefit grows as branches gain familiarity with the new system:

| Period | Average Effect |
|--------|----------------|
| Weeks 15–17 (early) | +0.6 points |
| Weeks 18–22 (later) | +1.6 points |

This learning curve suggests staff proficiency drives fuller realization of benefits.

### Additional Observations
- Branches retaining the old procedure experienced a slight decline (−1.0 points), while switched branches improved—indicating the new procedure may also buffer against operational headwinds.
- Staffing levels and visit volume both correlate positively with service scores, confirming these are relevant operational factors.

---

## **Next Steps**

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| **1** | Continue rollout to remaining branches | Evidence supports ~2-point service score gain per branch |
| **2** | Provide enhanced training in weeks 1–4 post-switch | Effects grow over time; early support accelerates benefits |
| **3** | Document practices from top-performing switched branches | Identify implementation factors driving strongest results |
| **4** | Monitor scores through week 30+ | Confirm effects persist and do not fade |
| **5** | Conduct cost-benefit analysis | Weigh implementation costs against 4% service improvement |

---

## **Methodology Note**

Analysis used difference-in-differences regression with branch fixed effects, controlling for staffing levels and visit volume. Standard errors clustered at the branch level. This approach isolates the causal effect of the procedure change by comparing switched branches to non-switched branches before and after week 15.
