# **Memorandum to the Board**

**Subject:** Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores

**Date:** November 2026

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure **delivered the expected improvement** in service quality. Branches that switched saw service scores rise by **2.1 points** (approximately 4% above the system average), while branches keeping the old procedure experienced a slight decline. The evidence is strong and statistically conclusive.

---

## **Key Findings**

### What We Measured
- **709 branches** tracked over 17 weeks
- **425 branches** implemented the new procedure (starting week 15)
- **284 branches** maintained the old procedure (comparison group)
- Service scores averaged ~50 points system-wide (higher is better)

### Results

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Net Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|------------|
| New Procedure Branches | 51.6 | 52.8 | +1.2 |
| Old Procedure Branches | 51.0 | 50.0 | −1.0 |
| **Effect of New Procedure** | | | **+2.1** |

### Confidence in the Results
- **Effect size:** +2.08 points
- **Confidence range:** 1.55 to 2.61 points (95% certainty)
- **Statistical reliability:** p < 0.001

### Important Pattern
Benefits **increased over time**. Early weeks showed modest gains; later weeks showed stronger improvements. This indicates staff proficiency with the new system drives better outcomes.

---

## **Recommendations**

| Action | Rationale |
|--------|-----------|
| **1. Complete the rollout** to remaining 284 branches | Proven 4% service improvement with high confidence |
| **2. Invest in training during first 6 weeks** | Effects grow as staff gain experience; accelerate the learning curve |
| **3. Document best practices** from top-performing switched branches | Capture implementation insights for future rollouts |
| **4. Continue monitoring through Q1 2027** | Confirm effects persist and identify any branches needing support |

---

## **Conclusion**

The data confirms what leadership expected: the new procedure improves service quality. The effect is meaningful, reliable, and strengthens with experience. Proceeding with full implementation is supported by strong evidence.
