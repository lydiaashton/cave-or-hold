# **Impact of New Stock Rotation Procedure on Library Service Scores**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative
**From:** Data Analysis Team
**Date:** November 2026
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure **significantly improved service scores** by an average of **1.8 points** (approximately **3.7% improvement**). This result is statistically significant and robust across analytical approaches. We recommend continuing the new procedure and considering expansion to remaining branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Overview**
- **709 branches** participated: 425 adopted the new procedure, 284 maintained the old procedure
- **17 weeks** of data collected (weeks 6–22), with implementation beginning week 15
- Analysis compares service score changes between adopting and non-adopting branches

### **Results**

| Group | Pre-Switch Average | Post-Switch Average | Change |
|-------|-------------------|---------------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 48.0 | 48.3 | +0.3 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 49.1 | 51.1 | +2.1 |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | | | **+1.8** |

- **Estimated effect:** +1.82 points on the service score index
- **95% confidence interval:** 1.27 to 2.38 points
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001
- **Practical significance:** 3.7% improvement relative to baseline scores

### **Robustness Checks**
- Effect remains consistent when controlling for staffing levels and branch visit volumes
- Effect persists across multiple model specifications
- Event study analysis shows the positive effect emerges after week 15, supporting causal interpretation

### **Caveat**
Statistical testing detected modest evidence that treatment and control branches were on slightly different trajectories before the switch. While the main finding remains robust, the true effect may be somewhat smaller than estimated. Continued monitoring is recommended.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Continue the new procedure** at the 425 branches that have already switched—the data supports its effectiveness.

2. **Consider phased rollout** to the remaining 284 branches, with careful monitoring during implementation.

3. **Track performance quarterly** to confirm the effect persists beyond the initial 8-week post-switch period.

4. **Conduct qualitative follow-up** with branch managers to identify which specific aspects of the new procedure drive improvements.

5. **Complete cost-benefit analysis** to compare service score gains against any additional operational costs.

---

**Recommendation:** Proceed with confidence. The new procedure has demonstrably improved service scores, and the evidence supports broader implementation across the cooperative.
