# **Board Summary: Impact of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure on Service Scores**

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure is associated with a **statistically significant improvement of approximately 2 points** in weekly service scores (on a scale where the average branch scores ~50). This represents roughly a **4% improvement** in service quality. The effect is robust across multiple analytical approaches, though pre-existing performance differences between branches suggest this estimate may be conservative.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Measured Impact**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect | **+1.98 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | +1.40 to +2.55 |
| Statistical Significance | p < 0.001 |
| Relative Improvement | **~4%** |

The analysis compared 425 branches that adopted the new procedure against 284 branches that continued with the old method, before and after the week 15 switch date.

### **2. Performance Trajectory**

**Before Switch (Weeks 6-14):**
- Switched branches averaged 0.83 points higher than control branches
- This gap was gradually widening even before the intervention

**After Switch (Weeks 15-22):**
- The gap increased to an average of 2.80 points
- The *additional* widening (~2 points) is attributed to the new procedure

### **3. Robustness Checks**
- Effect persists when controlling for staffing levels and visitor volumes
- Result is consistent across all 8 post-switch weeks
- Effect remains significant with cluster-robust standard errors accounting for repeated observations per branch

### **4. Important Context**
Branches selected for the new procedure were already showing slightly stronger performance trends before the switch. This suggests either:
- These branches had characteristics that made them better candidates for the new procedure, or
- They were more motivated/ready for change

This pre-trend pattern means the 2-point estimate should be interpreted as a **realistic expectation** rather than a guaranteed minimum effect for all branches.

## **Next Steps**

### **Immediate Actions (Next 30 Days)**

1. **Extend monitoring period** – Continue tracking service scores through week 30+ to confirm effects persist and don't diminish over time

2. **Document implementation details** – Capture what switched branches did differently during rollout to identify best practices

3. **Begin cost-benefit analysis** – Quantify operational costs of the new procedure against the service quality gains

### **Medium-Term Actions (Next 90 Days)**

4. **Profile high-performing switchers** – Identify which types of branches saw the largest gains to inform future rollout decisions

5. **Plan phased expansion** – If results hold, develop a prioritized rollout schedule for remaining control branches

6. **Conduct manager interviews** – Gather qualitative feedback on implementation challenges and success factors

### **Decision Point**

Based on the cost-benefit analysis and extended monitoring results, the board should decide whether to:
- **Expand** the procedure to all remaining branches
- **Modify** the procedure based on lessons learned
- **Maintain** the current mixed approach if costs outweigh benefits
