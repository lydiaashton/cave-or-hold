# MEMORANDUM

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Office  
**Date:** November 14, 2026  
**Subject:** Impact of New Stock Rotation Procedure on Service Scores

---

## Bottom Line

**The new stock rotation procedure improved service scores by approximately 2 points.** This improvement is statistically significant and represents a meaningful gain in branch service quality. The effect strengthened over time, suggesting branches benefit more as they gain experience with the new system.

---

## Key Findings

### Study Overview
- **709 branches** participated: 425 adopted the new procedure, 284 continued with the old method
- Data spans **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22), with the switch implemented in week 15
- Analysis compares service score changes between adopting and non-adopting branches

### Results Summary

| | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Branches keeping old procedure | 50.2 | 52.9 | +2.7 |
| Branches with new procedure | 50.1 | 54.7 | +4.6 |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | | **+1.9** |

- The **1.9-point improvement** is highly reliable (p < 0.001)
- Results account for differences in staffing levels and visitor volume
- By weeks 18–22, the effect reached approximately **2.3 points**

### Important Context
- Before the switch, both groups had nearly identical service scores, confirming a valid comparison
- The improvement was not immediate—branches showed modest gains in the first 2–3 weeks, with stronger effects emerging thereafter
- This pattern suggests a learning curve; branches perform better as they become familiar with the new procedure

---

## Next Steps

1. **Roll out the new procedure to all remaining branches.** The evidence supports expanding implementation to the 284 branches still using the old method.

2. **Provide enhanced support during the transition.** Since benefits accumulate over 3–4 weeks, consider offering additional training or on-site assistance during the first month after switching.

3. **Continue monitoring performance.** Track service scores over the coming months to confirm the improvement is sustained long-term.

4. **Document best practices.** Identify high-performing branches and capture their implementation approaches to share with others during rollout.

5. **Evaluate cost-effectiveness.** Compare the operational costs of the new procedure against the service quality gains to inform future resource decisions.
