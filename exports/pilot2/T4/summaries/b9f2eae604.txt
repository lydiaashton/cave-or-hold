# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 14, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Impact of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure on Service Scores

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure is associated with a **statistically significant increase of approximately 2 points** in service scores (95% confidence interval: 1.4 to 2.6 points). This represents roughly a **4% improvement** relative to the average branch score of 51. 

**Important caveat:** Branches that adopted the new procedure were already showing stronger score improvements before the switch. The true causal effect is likely positive but may be somewhat smaller than our point estimate.

**Recommendation:** Continue the new procedure while monitoring outcomes and investigating which branches benefit most.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### 1. Treatment Effect Summary

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect | **+1.98 points** |
| 95% Confidence interval | **1.40 to 2.55 points** |
| Statistical significance | **p < 0.001** |
| Relative improvement | **~4% of average score** |

### 2. Score Changes by Group

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Treatment branches (new procedure) | 50.2 | 53.8 | +3.6 |
| Control branches (old procedure) | 49.3 | 51.0 | +1.7 |
| **Net effect (Difference-in-Differences)** | | | **+2.0** |

### 3. Scope of Analysis

- **709 total branches** across the cooperative
- **425 branches (60%)** adopted the new procedure
- **284 branches (40%)** continued with the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22), with switch implemented in week 15
- **12,053 branch-week observations**

### 4. Important Context: Pre-Existing Trends

Our analysis detected that treatment branches were already improving faster than control branches *before* the procedure change:

- In week 6, the gap between groups was essentially zero (−0.17 points)
- By week 14 (just before the switch), the gap had grown to ~2 points
- This suggests the branches selected for the new procedure may have been on a stronger improvement trajectory regardless of the change

**Implication:** The 2-point estimate likely includes both the procedure effect and underlying branch differences. The true causal effect is positive but may be closer to 1–1.5 points rather than the full 2 points.

### 5. Supporting Analysis

- **Staffing and visit levels** were similar between groups at baseline (no significant differences)
- Both staffing and visits positively correlate with service scores, but controlling for these factors does not change the main result
- The effect appears **persistent** across the 8 weeks post-switch, with no signs of fading

## **ANALYTICAL APPROACH**

This analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** design, the standard method for evaluating policy changes when randomized assignment is not feasible. The approach:

- Compares score changes in treatment branches against score changes in control branches
- Controls for time-invariant branch characteristics and common time trends
- Uses cluster-robust standard errors (clustered at branch level) to account for repeated observations

**Limitations:** Non-random assignment of branches to the new procedure introduces potential selection bias, as evidenced by the pre-trend divergence. Future evaluations should consider randomized or stepped-wedge designs where feasible.

---

## **CONCLUSION**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure is associated with meaningful improvements in service scores. While selection bias prevents us from claiming the full 2-point effect is causal, the evidence strongly supports a **positive impact** warranting continued implementation. We recommend proceeding with the new procedure while pursuing the next steps above to refine our understanding and maximize benefits across the cooperative.
