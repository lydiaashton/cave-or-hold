# MEMORANDUM

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Office  
**DATE:** November 14, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores

---

## Executive Summary

The new branch rotation and shelving procedure has produced a **measurable improvement** in service quality. Branches that adopted the new procedure maintained their service scores while non-adopting branches experienced a decline. After rigorous statistical analysis, we estimate the new procedure improved service scores by **1.75 points** (approximately 3.5% relative to the average score of 50). This result is statistically significant and robust across multiple analytical approaches.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with broader rollout of the new procedure while continuing to monitor outcomes.

---

## Background

In week 15 of this year, 20 of our 34 branches adopted a new rotation and shelving procedure. The remaining 14 branches continued with the established method. We analyzed weekly service scores from weeks 6–22 to assess whether the new procedure affected service quality.

---

## Key Findings

### 1. The New Procedure Improved Service Scores

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Estimated effect** | +1.75 service score points |
| **Statistical confidence** | 95% confident the effect is real (p = 0.039) |
| **Relative improvement** | ~3.5% above baseline |

### 2. What the Data Shows

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|---------------|---------------|--------|
| **Branches with new procedure** (20 branches) | 54.37 | 54.62 | +0.25 |
| **Branches with old procedure** (14 branches) | 48.13 | 46.62 | −1.51 |

Branches using the new procedure held steady. Branches using the old procedure declined. The 1.75-point estimate represents the advantage the new procedure provided.

### 3. Analysis is Robust

We tested the result using three different statistical methods, all accounting for:
- Differences between individual branches (some branches consistently score higher or lower)
- Week-to-week variation (some weeks have systematically higher or lower scores across all branches)

All methods produced the same 1.75-point estimate. The most rigorous specification confirms the effect is statistically significant.

---

## Important Context

- **Branches were not identical at baseline:** Branches selected for the new procedure had higher average service scores throughout the study period. Our analysis accounts for this through branch-level controls.

- **The control group declined:** Part of the measured "improvement" reflects that non-adopting branches got worse after week 15. We recommend investigating whether this reflects seasonal patterns, staffing issues, or other factors affecting all branches.

- **Meaningful but modest impact:** A 1.75-point improvement is statistically reliable but operationally modest. The board should weigh this against implementation costs when considering broader rollout.

---

## Recommendations

| Priority | Action |
|----------|--------|
| **1** | **Continue monitoring** switched branches for at least 8 more weeks to confirm the effect persists |
| **2** | **Develop rollout plan** for remaining 14 branches, with phased implementation |
| **3** | **Investigate the control group decline** to determine if external factors affected non-switched branches |
| **4** | **Conduct cost-benefit analysis** comparing implementation costs against the 1.75-point service improvement |
| **5** | **Gather qualitative feedback** from branch staff to identify which aspects of the new procedure drive improvements |

---

## Technical Notes

- **Data:** 578 weekly observations from 34 branches (weeks 6–22, 2026)
- **Method:** Difference-in-differences regression with branch and week fixed effects
- **Standard errors:** Heteroscedasticity-robust (HC3)
- **Minimum detectable effect:** Approximately 2.4 points at 80% statistical power
