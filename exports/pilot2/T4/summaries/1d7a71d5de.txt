# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD OF DIRECTORS**

---

## **Subject: Impact of New Shelving Procedure on Library Service Scores**

**Date:** Analysis of weeks 6–22  
**Prepared by:** Data Analytics Team

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new shelving and stock rotation procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure achieved service scores **1.96 points higher** (a **4% improvement**) than would have been expected based on trends at branches keeping the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant and has been sustained throughout the post-implementation period.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with system-wide rollout of the new procedure.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### 1. Clear Positive Impact

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Treatment effect | +1.96 points |
| 95% Confidence interval | 1.39 to 2.54 points |
| Relative improvement | 3.9% |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 |

### 2. Robust Analysis

- **Sample:** 709 branches over 17 weeks (12,053 observations)
- **Treatment group:** 425 branches adopted the new procedure in week 15
- **Control group:** 284 branches continued with the old procedure
- **Method:** Difference-in-Differences regression with branch-level controls
- **Validation:** Pre-switch trends were parallel between groups, confirming valid comparison

### 3. Effect Has Strengthened Over Time

The improvement was immediate and has grown in subsequent weeks:
- Weeks 15–18: ~2.0 point average improvement
- Weeks 19–22: ~3.4 point average improvement

This suggests branches are mastering the new procedure and realizing increasing benefits.

### 4. Results Hold After Accounting for Other Factors

The improvement persists after controlling for:
- Staffing levels (staffing_index)
- Visitor volume (visits_index)

Both control variables were well-balanced between treatment and control groups, strengthening confidence in the causal interpretation.
