# **BOARD MEMORANDUM**

---

## **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative
**From:** Data Analysis Team
**Date:** December 2025
**Subject:** Impact Assessment of New Shelving Procedure on Service Scores

---

## **Executive Summary**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure **does improve service scores**, but branches require approximately one month to realize the full benefits. After an initial adjustment period, branches using the new procedure achieved service scores **3.5 points higher** than comparable branches using the old procedure—a statistically significant improvement representing roughly **7% gain** over baseline performance.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with systemwide rollout, accompanied by enhanced first-month training and support to accelerate the learning curve.

---

## **Analysis Overview**

| Study Parameter | Details |
|-----------------|---------|
| Study Period | 17 weeks (weeks 6-22) |
| Branches Analyzed | 34 total |
| Treatment Group | 20 branches (adopted new procedure week 15) |
| Control Group | 14 branches (continued old procedure) |
| Method | Difference-in-Differences with cluster-robust standard errors |

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Service Score Improvement Confirmed

| Period | Effect vs. Control | Statistical Significance |
|--------|-------------------|-------------------------|
| **Weeks 15-18** (immediate) | +0.7 points | Not significant |
| **Weeks 19-22** (stabilized) | **+3.5 points** | **Significant (p=0.04)** |
| **Overall average** | +2.1 points | Not significant (p=0.19) |

The overall average appears muted because it includes the adjustment period. The late-period result—when staff had become proficient with the new procedure—is the more meaningful indicator of the procedure's true impact.

### 2. Learning Curve Pattern

```
Service Score Trend (Simplified)

Treated Branches:    ────────┐
                           │ Adjustment │  ▲▲▲▲  ← Clear improvement
Control Branches:    ────────┴──────────  ─────
                      Week 14    Week 18   Week 22
                      (Switch)
```

Branches that switched showed no immediate gain in weeks 15-18, but demonstrated clear, sustained improvement by weeks 19-22. This pattern is consistent with a learning curve rather than a procedure failure.

### 3. Pre-Switch Trends Were Comparable

Before week 15, service score trends at switching and non-switching branches moved in parallel (p=0.16 for trend difference). This validates our comparison and supports the conclusion that the late-period improvement is attributable to the new procedure.

### 4. Staffing Consideration

Branches that switched showed a modest increase in staffing levels relative to control branches (p=0.05). This warrants attention:
- The new procedure may require additional staff time initially
- Alternatively, better-staffed branches may have been prioritized for early adoption
- **Action needed:** Clarify whether the service score gains persist without elevated staffing

---

## **Recommendations**

### **Proceed with Rollout**
The evidence supports expanding the new procedure to all branches. The 3.5-point improvement in the stabilized period is both statistically significant and operationally meaningful.

### **Accelerate the Learning Curve**
To capture benefits sooner:
- Develop a **structured 4-week training program** for new adopters
- Assign **peer mentors** from early-adopter branches
- Create **quick-reference guides** addressing common week 1-4 challenges

### **Monitor and Evaluate**
- Track service scores for **12 additional weeks** to confirm sustained gains
- Survey staff at weeks 2, 4, and 8 post-switch to identify friction points
- Analyze whether staffing levels can normalize after the adjustment period

### **Cost-Benefit Review**
Before full rollout, quantify:
- Training and implementation costs per branch
- Any permanent staffing implications
- Expected service score gains translated to patron satisfaction metrics

---

## **Conclusion**

The board's expectation that the new procedure would improve service scores was correct. The data confirms a **real, positive effect** that becomes evident once staff gain proficiency. With proper training and support during the transition, all branches should be able to realize these gains within one month of adoption.
