# **Library Cooperative Shelving Procedure Change: Service Score Impact Analysis**

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure is associated with a **2.15-point improvement** in service scores (approximately 4.5% relative to baseline). However, this improvement is **not statistically significant** at conventional levels (p = 0.16), meaning we cannot confidently rule out that the observed difference occurred by chance. The data show a positive trend in the expected direction, but the evidence is not strong enough to conclusively demonstrate the procedure's effectiveness.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Overall Impact Estimate**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect of new procedure | **+2.15 points** |
| 95% Confidence interval | **-0.84 to +5.13 points** |
| Statistical significance (p-value) | **0.158** (not significant) |
| Relative improvement | **~4.5%** vs. pre-switch average |

### **2. What Happened to Service Scores**

Both groups improved after week 15, but switched branches improved more:

| Group | Pre-Switch Average | Post-Switch Average | Change |
|-------|-------------------|---------------------|--------|
| **Switched branches** (20 branches) | 47.97 | 53.25 | **+5.28** |
| **Non-switched branches** (14 branches) | 48.35 | 51.49 | **+3.13** |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | — | — | **+2.15** |

### **3. Branch-Level Patterns**

- **95% of switched branches** (19 of 20) showed improvement from pre- to post-switch
- **71% of non-switched branches** (10 of 14) showed improvement in the same period
- This suggests the new procedure may benefit most branches, even if the average effect is modest

### **4. Validity Checks**

- **Parallel trends assumption holds**: Pre-switch trends between groups were not significantly different (p = 0.25), supporting the validity of our comparison
- **Results are robust**: Adding controls for staffing levels and visit volumes did not meaningfully change the estimate (2.16 points)

### **5. Why Isn't This Statistically Significant?**

The study may be **underpowered** to detect modest effects:
- With 34 branches and 17 weeks of data, we can reliably detect effects of ~3.5 points or larger
- The observed effect (2.15 points) is meaningful but falls below this threshold
- The confidence interval includes both small negative effects and moderate positive effects

---

## **Next Steps**

### **Short-Term Recommendations**

1. **Continue monitoring**: Collect additional weeks of data to increase statistical power. Each additional month of data will improve our ability to detect the true effect.

2. **Implement across all branches**: Given the positive direction of the effect, the low risk of the procedure change, and the fact that 95% of switched branches improved, proceeding with system-wide implementation is reasonable.

3. **Track branch-level variation**: Investigate why one switched branch saw a decline (-1.68 points) while others improved. There may be implementation differences or branch-specific factors worth understanding.

### **Medium-Term Recommendations**

4. **Extend the evaluation period**: Continue tracking for at least 6-12 more months to:
   - Confirm whether the effect persists or grows over time
   - Achieve statistical significance if the true effect is around 2 points
   - Identify any seasonal patterns in the procedure's effectiveness

5. **Consider complementary measures**: Service scores are one metric. Consider tracking:
   - Patron satisfaction surveys
   - Item findability metrics
   - Staff time spent on shelving

### **Long-Term Considerations**

6. **Plan for iterative improvement**: If the procedure is adopted system-wide, establish a feedback mechanism to refine the approach based on branch-level experiences.

7. **Document implementation details**: Variations in how branches implement the new procedure may explain differences in outcomes. Standardized implementation guidance could maximize benefits.

---

## **Technical Notes**

- **Method**: Difference-in-Differences (DiD) regression with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch)
- **Data**: 578 observations across 34 branches, weeks 6-22
- **Switch point**: Week 15
- **Analysis controls**: Staffing index and visits index (neither significantly affected the main result)
