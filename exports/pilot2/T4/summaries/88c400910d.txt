# **Board Summary: Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure is associated with a **statistically significant improvement of approximately 1.9 points** in service scores (on a scale where the average branch scores about 50). This represents roughly a **4% improvement** relative to baseline performance. The effect is highly significant (p < 0.001).

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **Data:** 709 branches over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- **Treatment:** 425 branches (60%) adopted the new procedure starting week 15
- **Control:** 284 branches (40%) continued with the old procedure
- **Method:** Difference-in-differences analysis with branch-level clustering

### Results

| Group | Pre-Switch Average | Post-Switch Average | Change |
|-------|-------------------|---------------------|--------|
| Switched Branches | 50.9 | 51.0 | +0.1 |
| Non-Switched Branches | 50.4 | 48.5 | −1.9 |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | | | **+1.9** |

- **Effect size:** +1.94 points (95% CI: 1.37 to 2.52)
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001
- **Practical significance:** ~0.2 standard deviations, or 4% above baseline

### Important Context

The improvement appears driven by the new procedure **preventing a decline** rather than producing absolute gains. Non-switched branches experienced a notable drop in service scores after week 14, while switched branches maintained their performance levels.

**Caveat:** Branches selected for the new procedure showed higher service scores than non-switched branches in the weeks immediately before the transition (weeks 9–14). This suggests branches may have been selected based on existing positive trajectories. While our analysis controls for baseline differences, this pre-existing trend means the true causal effect could be somewhat smaller than estimated.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Continue monitoring** – The post-switch period covers only 8 weeks. Track whether the effect persists, grows, or diminishes over time.

2. **Investigate selection factors** – Examine why certain branches were chosen for the new procedure to better interpret the results.

3. **Consider phased rollout** – Given that non-switched branches showed declining scores, consider extending the new procedure to remaining branches.

4. **Examine mechanisms** – Analyze whether the effect varies by branch characteristics (size, location, staffing levels) to identify where the new procedure works best.

5. **Conduct cost-benefit analysis** – Pair these service score gains with implementation costs to assess overall return on investment.

---

*Analysis based on weekly branch-level data from weeks 6–22. Standard errors clustered at the branch level to account for repeated observations.*
