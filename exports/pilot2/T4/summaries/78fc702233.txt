# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD OF DIRECTORS**

**Subject:** Impact Assessment of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure  
**Date:** November 2025  
**Prepared by:** Data Analysis Team

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure shows a **positive but statistically inconclusive effect** on service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure scored approximately **1.8 points higher** than expected, but this result is not statistically distinguishable from zero. The data cannot confidently confirm whether the procedure improved service or had no effect.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **Study Overview**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure at week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Service scores average around 50 points (higher is better)

### **Results Summary**

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|---------------|---------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 48.1 | 46.6 | **-1.5** |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 54.4 | 54.6 | **+0.3** |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | | | **+1.8** |

### **Statistical Assessment**
- **Point estimate:** +1.75 points (~3.6% improvement)
- **95% confidence interval:** -0.9 to +4.4 points
- **Statistical significance:** Not significant (p ≈ 0.20)
- **Interpretation:** The true effect could be zero, slightly negative, or as high as +4 points

### **Critical Observation**
Treatment branches **maintained their scores** while control branches **declined** after week 15. The new procedure may have **prevented deterioration** rather than driving active improvement.

### **Analysis Validity**
Pre-switch trends show no significant divergence between groups (p = 0.36), supporting the validity of the comparison. Results hold when controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume.

---

## **RECOMMENDATIONS / NEXT STEPS**

1. **Extend the evaluation period.** Additional weeks of data would narrow the confidence interval and provide a more definitive answer.

2. **Investigate the control group decline.** Understanding why scores dropped in branches keeping the old procedure (seasonal factors, staffing, external pressures) is essential.

3. **Consider phased expansion.** Given no evidence of harm and potential benefit, expanding the new procedure to remaining branches is reasonable while continuing monitoring.

4. **Gather qualitative feedback.** Supplement scores with staff and patron input on workflow efficiency, satisfaction, and practical implementation.

5. **Schedule follow-up review.** Re-assess with 8–12 additional weeks of data for a more conclusive determination.
