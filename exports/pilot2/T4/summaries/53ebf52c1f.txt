# **Memorandum to the Board**

**Subject:** Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores

**Date:** November 2026

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure **delivered the expected improvement** in service quality. Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2 points (4% improvement)** compared to branches that maintained the old procedure. The effect is statistically robust and strengthens over time. We recommend proceeding with full system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### What We Studied
- **709 branches** across the cooperative, tracked weekly from weeks 6–22
- **425 branches** implemented the new procedure starting week 15
- **284 branches** continued with the standard procedure (providing a comparison group)
- Service scores are calculated weekly by our office; the systemwide average is approximately 50 points

### Results

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Branches with new procedure | 51.6 | 52.8 | +1.2 |
| Branches with old procedure | 51.0 | 50.0 | −1.0 |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | | **+2.1** |

The new procedure is associated with a **+2.08 point improvement** in service scores, with a 95% confidence interval of 1.55 to 2.61 points. This result is highly statistically significant (p < 0.001).

### Important Observations

1. **Effects accumulate over time.** The improvement was modest in the first three weeks after implementation (+0.4 to +0.8 points) but grew substantially by weeks 19–22 (+1.0 to +2.3 points). This suggests staff proficiency with the new system drives additional gains.

2. **The new procedure appears protective.** While control branches experienced a 1-point decline in the post-switch period, treatment branches improved. This suggests the new procedure may help branches maintain service quality amid operational pressures.

3. **Results hold after accounting for other factors.** The effect remains significant when controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume, and when accounting for inherent differences between individual branches.

---

## **Recommendations**

| Action | Rationale |
|--------|-----------|
| **1. Proceed with full rollout** | The procedure delivers measurable service improvements consistent with expectations. |
| **2. Invest in early implementation support** | Since effects grow over time, dedicated training and troubleshooting during the first 4–6 weeks will accelerate benefits. |
| **3. Document best practices** | Branches showing the strongest gains may have implementation approaches worth sharing systemwide. |
| **4. Continue monitoring** | Track scores through the remainder of the fiscal year to confirm effects persist and identify any branches needing additional support. |

---

## **Conclusion**

The data confirm that the new rotation and shelving procedure improves service quality as intended. The 2-point gain represents meaningful progress on our service quality objectives, and the growing effect over time suggests even greater benefits as staff become fully proficient. Full implementation is warranted.
