# **Library Cooperative Branch Procedure Change: Impact Analysis**

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure shows a **positive but statistically inconclusive effect** on service scores. Branches that switched improved by approximately **2.4 points** (about 5% of the average score) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. However, with only 34 branches in the study, we lack sufficient statistical power to confirm this improvement was not due to chance. The analysis provides no evidence of harm and suggestive evidence of modest benefit.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Estimated Impact**
| Measure | Value |
|---------|-------|
| Treatment effect | +2.4 service score points |
| 95% Confidence interval | -0.8 to +5.5 points |
| Statistical significance | p = 0.15 (not significant) |
| Effect size | 5% of average score |

### **2. What the Data Shows**

| Metric | Treatment Branches (Switched) | Control Branches (No Switch) |
|--------|------------------------------|------------------------------|
| Pre-switch average score | 50.1 | 50.9 |
| Post-switch average score | 50.9 | 49.3 |
| **Average change** | **+0.8** | **-1.6** |
| % of branches improved | 38% | 12% |

Treatment branches were **three times more likely** to show improvement than control branches.

### **3. Study Limitations**

- **Low statistical power:** With 20 treatment and 14 control branches, the study has only 9.5% power to detect the observed effect. A detectable effect at standard power levels would need to exceed 10 points.
- **Limited post-switch data:** Only 8 weeks of observations after the change limits precision.
- **One pre-trend deviation:** Treatment and control branches differed significantly in one pre-period week (week 8), though overall pre-period trends were reasonably parallel.

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended Actions**

1. **Continue the new procedure** — No evidence of harm exists, and the point estimate indicates modest benefit.

2. **Extend data collection** — Gather 6-12 additional months of service scores to improve statistical power and assess whether effects persist or grow.

3. **Consider full rollout** — Switching remaining branches would eliminate the control group limitation and enable stronger before/after analysis with more data.

4. **Investigate branch-level variation** — Treatment branch outcomes ranged from -7.5 to +7.7 points. Identifying factors associated with stronger gains could improve implementation support.

5. **Monitor complementary metrics** — Track staffing efficiency and visit patterns alongside service scores to capture the full operational impact.

---

## **Technical Notes**

- **Method:** Difference-in-differences regression with branch fixed effects and cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch)
- **Data:** 34 branches × 17 weeks (weeks 6-22) = 578 observations
- **Switch date:** Week 15
- **Controls:** Branch fixed effects, week fixed effects, staffing index, visits index
