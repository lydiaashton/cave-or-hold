# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD OF DIRECTORS**

---

## **Subject: Impact of New Shelving Procedure on Library Service Scores**

**Date:** Analysis of weeks 6–22  
**Prepared by:** Data Analytics Team

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new shelving and stock rotation procedure is associated with improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure achieved service scores approximately **2 points higher** (a **4% improvement**) compared to branches keeping the old procedure. This result is statistically significant.

**Important caveat:** Treatment branches showed stronger performance trends even before the switch, which means the true causal effect may be somewhat smaller than estimated. Even accounting for this, the evidence points to a **modest but real positive impact**.

**Verdict:** **Proceed with rollout, but monitor closely.**

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### 1. Measured Impact

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated treatment effect | +1.96 to +1.98 points |
| 95% Confidence interval | 1.39 to 2.55 points |
| Relative improvement | 3.9% |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 |

### 2. Study Design

- **Sample:** 709 branches over 17 weeks (12,053 observations)
- **Treatment group:** 425 branches adopted the new procedure in week 15
- **Control group:** 284 branches continued with the old procedure
- **Method:** Difference-in-Differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors

### 3. Important Limitation: Pre-Treatment Trends

**The treatment and control groups were not on perfectly parallel trends before the switch:**

| Period | Average Treatment-Control Gap |
|--------|-------------------------------|
| Pre-switch (weeks 6–14) | 0.83 points |
| Post-switch (weeks 15–22) | 2.80 points |

The gap was already widening before the intervention (from -0.17 in week 6 to +1.97 in week 14). This suggests treatment branches may have been systematically different—perhaps more motivated staff, better management, or other unobserved factors.

**What this means:** The 1.96-point estimate likely includes both the true treatment effect AND some pre-existing differences. The actual causal effect is probably **positive but modest**—likely in the range of 1 to 2 points.

### 4. Supportive Evidence

- The post-switch gap (1.85 to 3.82) is **consistently higher** than most of the pre-switch period
- Results hold after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume
- The effect has **strengthened over time**, suggesting branches are mastering the procedure
- Effect size (~0.2 standard deviations) is meaningful for an operational change

## **FINAL VERDICT**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did the new procedure improve service scores? | **Yes, likely** |
| Is the effect large? | **Modest (~2 points, 4%)** |
| Can we be certain of the exact magnitude? | **No—pre-trends complicate interpretation** |
| Should we roll out system-wide? | **Yes, with monitoring** |

**Rationale:** Even if the true effect is at the lower end of our estimates (~1 point), the procedure appears to provide genuine value with no identified downsides. The cost of rollout should be weighed against sustained service improvements across 709 branches.
