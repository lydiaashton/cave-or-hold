# **REVISED Board Memorandum**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 14, 2026  
**Subject:** Impact Assessment: New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence is inconclusive.** While branches that adopted the new procedure showed higher service score improvements, **pre-existing trends suggest these branches were already improving faster before the change**. The observed 1.8-point difference could be entirely explained by these pre-existing trends rather than the procedure itself. **We cannot confidently attribute the improvement to the new procedure.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. What the Data Shows**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Raw DiD estimate | +1.82 service score points |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 |
| **BUT: Pre-existing trend difference** | **+0.28 points/week** (p < 0.001) |

### **2. The Critical Problem: Violated Assumption**

Difference-in-differences analysis requires that treatment and control groups were on **parallel trends** before the intervention. Our data shows this assumption is **violated**:

| Group | Pre-Switch Weekly Trend |
|-------|------------------------|
| Treatment branches | +0.17 points/week |
| Control branches | -0.11 points/week |
| **Difference** | **+0.28 points/week** |

Over 8 post-switch weeks, this pre-existing trend alone would create ~**2.24 points** of apparent improvement—**more than the entire observed effect of 1.82 points**.

### **3. Visual Evidence**

The attached chart (`trends_with_extrapolation.png`) shows:
- Treatment branches were already on an upward trajectory before week 15
- Control branches were relatively flat or declining
- The "effect" may simply be continuation of pre-existing patterns

### **4. What We Can Say**

- Branches that adopted the new procedure **did** achieve higher service scores post-switch (51.1 vs 48.3)
- However, we **cannot determine** whether this was caused by the procedure or by other factors (e.g., management quality, staff motivation, resources) that also influenced the adoption decision

---

## **Recommendations & Next Steps**

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| **1** | **Do NOT expand rollout yet** | Insufficient evidence of causal impact |
| **2** | **Investigate adoption criteria** | Understand what distinguished branches that switched vs. those that didn't |
| **3** | **Consider randomized trial** | For future changes, randomly assign branches to ensure valid causal inference |
| **4** | **Gather qualitative evidence** | Staff/patron feedback may provide insights the quantitative data cannot |
| **5** | **Continue monitoring** | Additional post-period data may help disentangle trends from treatment effects |

---

## **Conclusion**

The new procedure **cannot be credited with causing the observed improvement** based on this analysis. Branches that adopted it were already improving faster beforehand. We recommend against broad rollout until more rigorous evidence is available.
