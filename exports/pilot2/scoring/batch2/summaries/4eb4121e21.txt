# MEMORANDUM

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Office  
**Date:** November 14, 2026  
**Subject:** Impact of New Stock Rotation Procedure on Service Scores

---

## Bottom Line

**The new stock rotation procedure improved service scores by approximately 1.9 points.** This improvement is statistically significant (p < 0.001) and represents a meaningful gain in branch service quality. The effect strengthened over time, suggesting branches benefit more as they gain experience with the new system.

---

## Final Verdict

**Proceed with system-wide implementation.** The evidence is clear and reliable: the new procedure causes a real improvement in service scores. The 1.9-point gain (95% confidence interval: 1.3 to 2.4 points) is both statistically significant and operationally meaningful. With 284 branches still using the old method, expanding the new procedure is a low-risk, evidence-based decision.

---

## Key Findings

### Study Overview
- **709 branches** participated: 425 adopted the new procedure, 284 continued with the old method
- Data spans **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22), with the switch implemented in week 15
- Analysis uses a rigorous difference-in-differences design

### Results Summary

| | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Branches keeping old procedure | 50.2 | 52.9 | +2.7 |
| Branches with new procedure | 50.1 | 54.7 | +4.6 |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | | **+1.9** |

- Effect is highly reliable (p < 0.001; 95% CI: 1.3 to 2.4 points)
- Results hold after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume
- By weeks 18–22, the effect reached approximately **2.3 points**

### Why We Trust These Results
- **Valid comparison:** Before the switch, treated and control branches had nearly identical scores (difference of only 0.04 points)
- **Clean identification:** The staggered timing and clear treatment/control split isolate the procedure's effect
- **Robust to controls:** The effect persists after accounting for staffing and visitor volume changes
- **Sensible pattern:** Effects grew over time, consistent with a learning curve rather than random noise

---

## Next Steps

1. **Roll out the new procedure to all remaining branches.** Begin with a phased approach, grouping branches by region or size.

2. **Provide enhanced support during transition.** Since benefits accumulate over 3–4 weeks, offer additional training or on-site assistance during the first month after switching.

3. **Continue monitoring performance.** Track service scores for at least 6 months post-implementation to confirm long-term sustainability.

4. **Document best practices.** Identify high-performing branches and capture their implementation approaches to share during rollout.

5. **Evaluate cost-effectiveness.** Compare operational costs against service quality gains to inform future procedural changes.
