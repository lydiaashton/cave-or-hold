# **Board Memorandum**

## **Title: New Stock Rotation Procedure — Early Results Are Encouraging**

---

## **Bottom Line**

The data show a **positive trend in the expected direction**, but the evidence is not yet statistically definitive. Branches using the new procedure averaged **2.1 points higher** on service scores, but this difference could plausibly be due to chance (p=0.19). 

However, the effect **strengthened over time**: in weeks 19-22, the improvement reached **3.5 points and was statistically significant**. This pattern suggests the procedure may deliver real benefits once staff gain proficiency.

**Recommendation**: Continue the rollout with enhanced monitoring. The potential upside is meaningful (~4-7% improvement), the procedure appears safe (no negative effects detected), and additional weeks of data will provide greater certainty.

---

## **Final Verdict**

> **PROCEED WITH CAUTIOUS OPTIMISM**
>
> The new procedure shows **promising results consistent with our expectations**, but we do not yet have conclusive proof. The positive direction of the effect, the absence of any detected harm, and the strengthening trend over time all support continued implementation. However, the board should understand that:
>
> - **Confidence level**: Moderate (not yet definitive)
> - **Risk**: Low (no evidence of negative impact)
> - **Potential reward**: Moderate to high (4-7% service score improvement)
> - **Decision**: Continue rollout while gathering more data
>
> This is a **Type II error risk situation** — the greater danger is abandoning a beneficial procedure due to insufficient data, not implementing one that doesn't work.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. **Point Estimate Is Positive, But Uncertainty Remains**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect (overall) | +2.1 points |
| 95% confidence interval | -1.0 to +5.3 points |
| P-value | 0.19 |
| Statistical significance | Not at conventional levels |

The confidence interval tells us the true effect could be zero, modestly negative, or as high as +5 points. We need more data to narrow this range.

### 2. **Benefits Appeared After a Learning Period**

| Period | Effect | P-value |
|--------|--------|---------|
| Weeks 15-18 (early) | +0.7 points | 0.66 |
| Weeks 19-22 (late) | **+3.5 points** | **0.04** |

This pattern is consistent with staff needing time to master the new procedure. It also raises a caution: the late-period result comes from splitting the data, so it should be confirmed with additional observations.

### 3. **No Evidence of Harm**

At no point did switching branches show significantly *worse* service scores than control branches. The lower bound of our confidence interval (-1.0 points) represents a relatively small potential downside.

### 4. **Study Limitations**

- Only 8 weeks of post-switch data (limited statistical power)
- 34 branches total (20 treated, 14 control)
- Possible staffing differences between groups (switching branches had slightly higher staffing increases)

---

## **Recommendations**

### 1. **Continue Monitoring Before Full Expansion**
Collect 8-12 additional weeks of data. If the late-period gains persist, statistical confidence will increase substantially.

### 2. **Support the Learning Curve**
- Document best practices from branches that showed the strongest week 19-22 improvements
- Provide targeted support during the first 4 weeks at newly switching branches
- Consider pairing new adopters with experienced branches

### 3. **Investigate Staffing Relationship**
Switching branches showed a modest staffing increase. Determine whether this is:
- A temporary implementation effect, or
- An ongoing requirement that should be factored into cost-benefit analysis

### 4. **Revisit Decision in 2-3 Months**
With additional data, we can provide a more definitive assessment. If the effect remains around +2 to +3 points with narrower confidence intervals, a stronger recommendation for full rollout would be warranted.
