## **BOTTOM LINE**

**We cannot conclude with statistical confidence that the new procedure changed service scores.** The best estimate suggests a modest improvement of about 2 points (~4%), but the data is also compatible with no effect or even a small decline. The 95% confidence interval ranges from -1.0 to +5.3 points.

There is **suggestive evidence** that benefits may emerge over time—the effect appears larger in weeks 19-22 than in weeks 15-18—but this finding needs confirmation with additional data before drawing firm conclusions.

**Recommendation:** Continue monitoring for 8-12 more weeks before making a cooperative-wide rollout decision.

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **Did the new procedure improve service scores?** | Not proven |

**Bottom line for decision-makers:** The new procedure shows **promise but not proof**. Proceed with caution, not conviction.

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### 1. Study Overview
| Component | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| Branches studied | 34 total (20 switched, 14 did not) |
| Observation period | 17 weeks (weeks 6-22) |
| Switch date | Week 15 |
| Outcome measure | Service score index (baseline ~50; higher is better) |

### 2. Impact on Service Scores

| Time Period | Effect Estimate | 95% Confidence Interval | Statistical Significance |
|-------------|-----------------|------------------------|-------------------------|
| **Overall** (weeks 15-22) | +2.1 points | -1.0 to +5.3 | Not significant (p=0.19) |
| **Early** (weeks 15-18) | +0.7 points | -2.5 to +4.0 | Not significant (p=0.66) |
| **Late** (weeks 19-22) | +3.5 points | +0.1 to +7.0 | Significant (p=0.04) |

### 3. What This Means

- **The main analysis does not show a statistically reliable effect.** We cannot rule out that the new procedure had zero impact on service scores.

- **However, the data leans positive.** The point estimate is +2.1 points, and the confidence interval excludes only effects larger than -1.0 (negative) or +5.3 (positive). A meaningful negative effect is unlikely.

- **The late-period finding is promising but preliminary.** The stronger effect in weeks 19-22 could indicate a learning curve, but subgroup analyses are more prone to false positives. More weeks of data would clarify whether this pattern persists.

### 4. Important Context

- **Pre-trends were parallel:** Before the switch, service score trends were similar between switching and non-switching branches, supporting the validity of our comparison.

- **Staffing changes:** Branches that switched showed a modest increase in staffing levels (p=0.05). This may have contributed to service score changes and should be factored into cost-benefit calculations.

- **Visitor volume:** No meaningful difference between groups, suggesting the effect is not driven by changes in demand.

## **TECHNICAL APPENDIX**

**Methodology:** Difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch). Models control for weekly staffing and visitor volume indices.

**Data:** 578 branch-week observations from branches.csv

**Why the uncertainty?** With 34 branches and only 8 post-switch weeks, the study has limited statistical power. The minimum detectable effect at 80% power is approximately 3-4 points, meaning smaller but meaningful effects may go undetected.

**Limitations:**
- Relatively short post-implementation window
- Potential confounding from staffing changes
- Cannot rule out unobserved time-varying factors
