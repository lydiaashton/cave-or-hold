# **Board Summary: Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure is associated with a **2.3-point improvement** in service scores (approximately 5% above baseline). However, this result is **not statistically significant** at the conventional 5% level (p=0.10; 95% confidence interval: -0.5 to +5.1 points). This means the data are consistent with both a modest positive effect and no effect at all. The evidence **leans positive** but is not conclusive. Given there is no evidence of harm, the board may consider proceeding with the rollout while continuing to monitor outcomes.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure
- The switch occurred at **week 15**
- Analysis used a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in switched branches against changes in non-switched branches

### Service Score Changes

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 44.8 | 45.3 | +0.6 |
| Switched (new procedure) | 49.9 | 52.8 | +2.9 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+2.3** |

### Statistical Results
- **Estimated effect:** +2.3 service score points
- **95% Confidence Interval:** -0.5 to +5.1 points
- **P-value:** 0.10 (not significant at 5% level)
- The confidence interval includes zero, indicating uncertainty about whether the true effect is positive

### Validity Checks
- **Parallel trends confirmed:** Before the switch, both groups showed similar trends in service scores (p=0.89), supporting the validity of the comparison
- **Control variables:** Accounting for staffing levels and visit volumes did not substantially change the results
- **Visit volume matters:** Higher branch visits are significantly associated with higher service scores (p<0.001)

---

## **Final Verdict**

**Recommendation: Proceed with Cautious Expansion**

The analysis provides **suggestive but not definitive** evidence that the new procedure improves service scores. We recommend:

1. **Continue the rollout** to remaining branches—the point estimate is meaningfully positive, there is no evidence of harm, and waiting for statistical certainty may delay benefits unnecessarily.

2. **Implement enhanced monitoring** for the next 12–16 weeks to gather more data and narrow the confidence interval around the effect estimate.

3. **Set a decision checkpoint** at Week 30 (15 weeks post-switch) to reassess. If the effect remains positive but still inconclusive, consider whether the operational benefits (e.g., efficiency, staff satisfaction) justify full adoption regardless of statistical significance.

4. **Document branch-level outcomes** to identify whether certain branches benefit more or less, which could inform future training or support needs.

**Risk Assessment:** Low. The worst-case scenario (no true effect) means the cooperative has invested in a change with neutral impact on service scores. The best-case scenario (true effect ~2–5 points) represents a meaningful service improvement across the system.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Continue Monitoring** — Collect additional weeks of data to increase statistical power.

2. **Track Related Metrics** — Monitor staffing and visit volumes, and consider adding patron satisfaction measures.

3. **Branch-Level Analysis** — Identify whether certain branch characteristics predict better or worse outcomes under the new procedure.

4. **Schedule Review** — Return to the board at Week 30 with updated analysis.
