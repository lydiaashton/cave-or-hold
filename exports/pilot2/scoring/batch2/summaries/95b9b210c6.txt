# **Board Summary: Impact of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure on Service Scores**

---

## **Bottom Line**

Branches that adopted the new procedure show **higher service scores after the switch** compared to branches that didn't, with an estimated difference of approximately 2 points (~4% improvement). **However, because switched branches were already improving faster before the change, we cannot confidently attribute this improvement to the new procedure itself.** The observed association may reflect pre-existing differences between branches rather than a causal effect of the procedure change.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Measured Association**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated difference | **+1.98 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | +1.40 to +2.55 |
| Statistical Significance | p < 0.001 |
| Relative Difference | **~4%** |

This represents the *association* between procedure adoption and service scores—not a proven causal effect.

### **2. Critical Limitation: Pre-Existing Trends**

| Week | Switched Branches | Control Branches | Gap |
|------|-------------------|------------------|-----|
| 6 | 51.2 | 51.4 | -0.2 |
| 9 | 48.7 | 48.1 | +0.6 |
| 12 | 50.0 | 48.3 | +1.6 |
| 14 | 48.7 | 46.7 | +2.0 |
| **15 (switch)** | — | — | — |
| 22 | 50.7 | 47.4 | +3.3 |

**The gap was already widening before the switch.** Statistical testing confirms switched branches had significantly different (stronger) pre-trends (p = 0.003). This violates the key assumption needed to interpret the 2-point estimate as a causal effect.

### **3. What We Can Say With Confidence**
- ✓ Switched branches outperformed control branches after week 15
- ✓ The performance gap widened after the switch date
- ✓ The association is statistically significant and robust to controls

### **4. What We Cannot Say With Confidence**
- ✗ The new procedure *caused* the improvement
- ✗ Rolling out the procedure to other branches will produce similar gains
- ✗ The effect size is exactly 2 points (could be smaller, larger, or zero)

---

## **Next Steps**

### **Immediate Actions (Next 30 Days)**

1. **Pause expansion decisions** – Do not commit to system-wide rollout until causal evidence is stronger

2. **Extend monitoring** – Continue tracking both groups through week 30+ to see if gaps persist, widen, or narrow

3. **Document implementation** – Capture what switched branches did differently; implementation quality may explain variation

### **Medium-Term Actions (Next 90 Days)**

4. **Analyze branch characteristics** – Determine whether switched branches differed systematically (size, staffing, location, management) from control branches

5. **Consider randomized rollout** – If expanding, randomly assign remaining branches to treatment vs. continued control for cleaner causal inference

6. **Conduct manager interviews** – Gather qualitative data on whether staff perceive the procedure as beneficial

### **Decision Framework**

| Evidence Status | Recommended Action |
|-----------------|-------------------|
| Current (associational only) | Limited pilot expansion; continue evaluation |
| If causal effect confirmed | Proceed with phased system-wide rollout |
| If effect diminishes over time | Revisit procedure design or implementation support |

---

## **Final Verdict**

> **The data show a positive association between the new procedure and service scores, but they do not establish that the procedure caused the improvement.**
>
> **Verdict: Proceed with caution.** The new procedure is a **promising candidate** for expansion, but the cooperative should invest in more rigorous evaluation before committing significant resources to system-wide rollout. The safest path forward is a **randomized or staggered expansion** with continued monitoring, which would generate the causal evidence needed for a confident investment decision.
>
> **Risk of acting now:** Expanding based on associational evidence risks investing in a procedure that may not deliver expected benefits if the observed gains were driven by branch characteristics rather than the procedure itself.
>
> **Risk of waiting:** Delaying expansion postpones potential benefits if the procedure is genuinely effective. Given the modest costs of monitoring relative to rollout costs, the balance favors **continued evaluation before full commitment.**
