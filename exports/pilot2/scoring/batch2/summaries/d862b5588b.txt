# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD OF DIRECTORS**

---

## **Impact of New Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure on Service Scores**

**Date:** Analysis of Weeks 6-22  
**Prepared by:** Data Analytics Team

---

### **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure is associated with a 1.9-point service score advantage, but causality cannot be confirmed with high confidence.** Treatment branches maintained their scores while control branches declined. However, pre-existing trends suggest some of this gap may have emerged regardless of the procedure.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with **phased rollout** and continued monitoring rather than immediate system-wide adoption.

---

### **KEY FINDINGS**

| Finding | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Estimated Effect** | +1.94 points (95% CI: 1.35 to 2.54) |
| **Statistical Significance** | p < 0.001 |
| **Sample Size** | 709 branches (425 adopted, 284 did not) |
| **Treatment Branches** | 50.92 → 51.00 (essentially flat) |
| **Control Branches** | 50.36 → 48.50 (−1.86 points) |

**1. The Gap Widened After the Switch**
The treatment-control gap grew from ~1.2 points (pre-period average) to ~2.5 points (post-period average). However, this gap was already increasing before week 15.

**2. Pre-Existing Trends Are a Concern**
Treatment branches started 2 points below control branches in weeks 6-7, then converged to parity by week 14—**before the switch occurred**. This suggests selection bias: branches chosen for the new procedure may have already been on an upward trajectory.

**3. Post-Switch Effects Strengthen Over Time**
Weeks 15-16 showed modest, non-significant effects. Weeks 17-22 showed clearer separation (1.2 to 2.2 points, statistically significant). This pattern is consistent with a genuine treatment effect but could also reflect continued mean reversion.

**4. No Indirect Effects Detected**
The procedure did not change staffing levels or visitor volume. Any benefits stem from the procedure itself.

**5. Control Group Decline Needs Investigation**
Non-adopting branches lost nearly 2 points post-week-14. Understanding why is critical—this could reflect seasonal factors, broader operational issues, or regression to the mean.

---

### **FINAL VERDICT**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **Did the procedure help?** | Likely yes, but we cannot rule out pre-existing trends |
| **How large is the effect?** | Best estimate: +1.5 to +2.0 points; true effect may be smaller |
| **Is the evidence strong enough for full rollout?** | No—insufficient to justify immediate system-wide adoption |
| **Is the evidence strong enough to continue?** | Yes—the procedure shows promise and no harm |

**Confidence Level: MODERATE**

The analysis is methodologically sound (difference-in-differences with branch fixed effects and cluster-robust standard errors), but the **parallel trends assumption is imperfect**. The pre-period convergence between groups means the DiD estimate likely captures both the treatment effect and ongoing mean reversion.

---

### **APPENDIX: METHODOLOGY & LIMITATIONS**

- **Approach:** Difference-in-Differences with branch fixed effects
- **Standard Errors:** Cluster-robust at branch level
- **Data:** 12,053 observations (weekly scores per branch)
- **Period:** Weeks 6-22 (9 weeks pre-switch, 8 weeks post-switch)

**Key Limitation:** Treatment branches were already improving relative to control branches before the switch. This violates the ideal parallel trends assumption and means the 1.94-point estimate is likely an **upper bound** on the true causal effect.
