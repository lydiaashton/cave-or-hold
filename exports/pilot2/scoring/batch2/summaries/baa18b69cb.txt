## **Final Verdict**

**The new procedure shows promise but lacks conclusive evidence of effectiveness.**

We recommend **continuing the pilot with enhanced monitoring** rather than either a full rollout or termination. The data shows a positive direction of effect with no evidence of harm, but the sample size and study duration are insufficient to confirm the improvement is real rather than due to chance.

**Decision Summary:**
| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did service scores improve? | Possibly (+1.7 points), but not statistically confirmed |
| Is the analysis trustworthy? | Yes — methodology is sound |

| Should we abandon the procedure? | **No** — results are encouraging |

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new rotation and shelving procedure shows a **positive but statistically uncertain effect** on branch service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure improved by approximately **1.7 points** relative to those that kept the old procedure. However, this difference is not statistically significant at conventional levels (p=0.16), meaning we cannot confidently rule out chance as an explanation. The analysis is methodologically sound, with strong evidence that treatment and control branches were on similar trajectories before the switch.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Overview**
| Element | Details |
|---------|---------|
| Branches Studied | 34 total (20 switched, 14 did not) |
| Study Period | 17 weeks (Weeks 6–22) |
| Switch Date | Week 15 |
| Method | Difference-in-Differences Regression |

### **Service Score Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (Old Procedure) | 49.03 | 47.84 | −1.19 |
| Treatment (New Procedure) | 53.45 | 53.96 | +0.50 |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | | | **+1.69** |

- **Point Estimate:** +1.69 service score points
- **Statistical Significance:** p = 0.16 (not significant at 0.05 level)
- **95% Confidence Interval:** −0.64 to +4.02 points
- **Practical Meaning:** If real, the effect represents about a 3% improvement relative to the average branch score of ~50

### **Analysis Quality Checks**

| Check | Result | Interpretation |
|-------|--------|----------------|
| Parallel Trends Test | p = 0.996 | ✅ Passed — groups were on similar trajectories before the switch |
| Staffing Controls | Significant (p < 0.01) | ✅ Staffing affects scores, but controlling for it doesn't change the main result |
| Event Study Pattern | Consistent positive post-effects | ✅ No evidence of pre-trends; post-effects stable over time |

## **Next Steps**

### **Immediate Actions (Next 30 Days)**

1. **Extend data collection 8–12 weeks** to improve statistical power and detect smaller effects with greater confidence.

2. **Branch-level review:** Identify whether certain branch characteristics (size, location, baseline performance) are associated with larger improvements.

3. **Operational metrics audit:** Track staffing hours, patron wait times, and implementation costs alongside service scores.

### **Strategic Decisions**

| If... | Then Consider... |
|-------|------------------|
| Implementation costs are low | Proceed with phased rollout while continuing evaluation |
| Implementation costs are high | Wait for additional data before committing resources |
| Some branches show strong gains | Prioritize rollout to similar branch types first |

### **Success Metrics for Continued Evaluation**

- Target: Detect effects as small as 1.0 point with 80% statistical power
- Timeline: 12 additional weeks of post-switch data
- Sample: All 34 branches continuing weekly reporting

---

## **Conclusion**

The evidence suggests the new procedure may modestly improve service quality, but current data cannot confirm this with statistical certainty. Given the positive direction, strong methodology, and absence of negative effects, a cautious continuation with enhanced monitoring is warranted.
