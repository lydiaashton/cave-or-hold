# **Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

**Date:** November 2026  
**Analysis Period:** Weeks 6–22 (17 weeks)  
**Branches Analyzed:** 709

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure protected service quality during a period of decline.** While branches using the old procedure saw scores fall by 1.9 points, branches using the new procedure held steady. The net benefit is **1.9 points**—not because scores improved, but because they **did not deteriorate** like others did. This is still a meaningful advantage, but expectations should be calibrated accordingly.

---

## **What We Found**

| Metric | Result |
|--------|--------|
| **Treatment group change** | 50.92 → 51.00 (**+0.08**, essentially flat) |
| **Control group change** | 50.36 → 48.50 (**−1.86**, significant decline) |
| **Net protective effect** | **+1.9 points** (statistically significant, p < 0.001) |
| **Relative advantage** | Treatment branches scored ~4% higher than they otherwise would have |

**Critical context:** Service scores declined system-wide after week 15. The new procedure appears to have **shielded branches from this downturn** rather than driving absolute improvement.

---

## **Why This Matters**

1. **Resilience:** The new procedure may make branches more robust during challenging periods (seasonal dips, staffing pressures, etc.)

2. **Risk mitigation:** Rolling out the procedure cooperative-wide could prevent future score declines

3. **Caveat:** We should not expect the new procedure to raise scores above baseline—only to help maintain them

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **Did the new procedure improve service scores?** | **No** — scores remained flat |
| **Did the new procedure provide value?** | **Yes** — it protected against decline |
| **Is the effect statistically reliable?** | **Yes** — p < 0.001, consistent across all post-switch weeks |
| **Should we roll out cooperative-wide?** | **Yes, with caveats** — proceed if the goal is stability and risk mitigation, not score elevation |

**Bottom-line verdict:** **Approve the rollout, but reframe expectations.** The new procedure is a **defensive improvement**, not an offensive one. It belongs in the cooperative's toolkit for maintaining service quality during challenging periods. However, if the board's objective is to raise scores above current levels, additional initiatives beyond this procedure will be needed.
