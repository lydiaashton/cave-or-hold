# **Memorandum to the Board**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence suggests the new procedure improved service scores, but we cannot be fully certain.** Branches that adopted the new procedure showed service score gains of approximately 1.0-1.5 points in the weeks following implementation, emerging after a 3-week adjustment period. However, because treatment branches were already trending upward before the switch, some of the observed improvement may reflect pre-existing momentum rather than the procedure itself. **We recommend cautious expansion with continued monitoring.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### What the Data Shows
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Simple comparison (post - pre) | +1.82 points |
| Event study (weeks 4-8 post-switch) | +1.0 to +1.5 points |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.05 for later weeks |
| Timing | Effects emerged after 3-week delay |

### Important Caveat
**Treatment branches were already improving before the switch.** From weeks 6-14, they gained 1.76 points relative to control branches—before any procedure change. This means:
- The true effect could be smaller than observed
- Some improvement may reflect pre-existing trends, not the new procedure
- We cannot rule out that part of the gain would have happened anyway

### What Supports a Causal Interpretation
- Effects were **near-zero in the first 3 weeks** after switching, then became positive
- This timing pattern is consistent with staff learning the new procedures
- Results hold after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume
- Branch fixed effects account for inherent differences between branches

## **Recommendations**

| Priority | Action |

| **4** | **Document implementation quality** — branches with better training may show stronger effects |
| **5** | **Re-evaluate after 6 months** with more data to confirm sustained benefits |

## **Conclusion**

The new procedure is associated with improved service scores, and the timing pattern suggests a real effect. However, pre-existing trends mean we cannot be fully confident in the magnitude. A cautious, monitored expansion is warranted rather than immediate system-wide adoption.
