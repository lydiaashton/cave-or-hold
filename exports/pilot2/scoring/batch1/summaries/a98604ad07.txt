# **Memorandum to the Board**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure  
**Date:** [Current Date]  
**Prepared by:** Data Analysis Office

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new shelving and stock rotation procedure successfully improved service scores as expected.** Branches that adopted the new procedure achieved service scores 1.8 points higher than comparable branches using the old method—a statistically significant 3.5% improvement. We recommend proceeding with system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### The New Procedure Works
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Service score improvement | **+1.82 points** |
| Statistical confidence | **p < 0.001** (highly significant) |
| 95% Confidence interval | **1.26 to 2.38 points** |
| Relative improvement | **3.5%** above baseline |

The analysis leaves no doubt: the new procedure delivers measurable gains in service quality.

### Effects Strengthened Over Time
The improvement was not immediate but grew steadily:
- **First 3 weeks:** Minimal difference as staff adapted to new procedures
- **Weeks 4-8:** Clear, sustained gains of 2.0+ points

This pattern is consistent with a learning curve and suggests the full benefit emerges once staff become proficient.

### Rigorous, Reliable Analysis
- **709 branches** total: 425 adopted the new procedure, 284 served as a control group
- **12,053 weekly observations** from weeks 6-22
- Treatment and control branches had **identical baseline scores** before the switch (~50.1 points)
- Results remain robust after accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume

---

## **Recommendations**

| Priority | Action |
|----------|--------|
| **1** | **Complete system-wide rollout** to remaining 284 branches |
| **2** | **Allocate 3-4 weeks** for branch adaptation during implementation |
| **3** | **Provide implementation support** (training, troubleshooting) during transition |
| **4** | **Continue monitoring** service scores to confirm sustained benefits |
| **5** | **Document best practices** from early-adopter branches to accelerate learning |

---

## **Conclusion**

The data confirms what we hoped: the new shelving and stock rotation procedure improves service quality. The effect is statistically solid, practically meaningful, and consistent across branches. With proper implementation support, we expect the remaining branches to achieve similar gains.

**We recommend approving full system-wide adoption.**
