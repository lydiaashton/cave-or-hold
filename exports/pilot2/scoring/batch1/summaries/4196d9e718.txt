# **Memorandum to the Board of Directors**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Subject:** Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new rotation and shelving procedure shows a **positive effect on service scores**, but the evidence is not yet statistically conclusive. Branches that adopted the new procedure improved by an estimated **2.1 points more** than branches that kept the old procedure. While this suggests the change is beneficial, we cannot rule out the possibility that this result occurred by chance. Continued monitoring is recommended before making a systemwide decision.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Overall Impact

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated treatment effect | **+2.15 points** |
| 95% Confidence interval | **-0.8 to +5.1 points** |
| Statistical significance | **p = 0.16** (not significant at p < 0.05) |

**What this means:** Switched branches improved from 48.0 to 53.2 after the change (+5.3 points). Non-switched branches also improved, from 48.4 to 51.5 (+3.1 points), likely due to seasonal factors or systemwide trends. The *additional* improvement attributable to the new procedure is 2.1 points—positive, but with uncertainty.

### Week-by-Week Performance

The effect varies by week. Three of the eight post-switch weeks showed **statistically significant improvements** in switched branches:

- **Week 15** (first week after switch): +6.2 points (p = 0.025)
- **Week 17:** +8.1 points (p = 0.005)
- **Week 21:** +6.6 points (p = 0.024)

Other weeks showed positive but non-significant gains. This pattern suggests the procedure *does* improve service, but the magnitude depends on other factors (e.g., staffing levels, visitor volume, or implementation consistency).

### Pre-Trend Check

Importantly, switched and non-switched branches followed **similar trends before the change**, supporting the validity of our comparison. There is no evidence that switched branches were already on a different trajectory.

### Study Limitations

- **34 branches** (20 switched, 14 did not)
- **17 weeks of data** (9 before, 8 after the switch)
- With this sample size, we can only reliably detect effects larger than **3.0 points**
- The observed 2.1-point effect may be real but too small to confirm with current data

---

## **Recommendations**

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| **1** | **Continue data collection** for at least 8–12 more weeks | Increases statistical power to confirm or refute the observed effect |
| **2** | **Investigate week-to-week variation** with branch managers | Identify conditions under which the procedure works best |
| **3** | **Consider phased expansion** to additional branches | The positive point estimate and significant weeks suggest potential benefit |
| **4** | **Gather staff feedback** from switched branches | Qualitative insights can reveal implementation challenges or best practices |

---

## **Conclusion**

The new procedure is **promising but not yet proven**. The data point in a positive direction, and there is no evidence of harm. We recommend continuing the pilot while collecting more data and working with branches to understand when and why the procedure delivers the strongest results.
