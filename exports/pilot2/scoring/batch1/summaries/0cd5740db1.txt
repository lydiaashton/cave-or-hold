# **Board Summary: Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure is associated with a **2.4-point improvement** in service scores (approximately 5% above baseline). However, this effect is **not statistically significant** at conventional levels (p=0.075), meaning we cannot rule out that the observed improvement occurred by chance. The evidence suggests a positive trend, but more time or data is needed to confirm the benefit with confidence.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure
- The switch occurred at **week 15**
- Analysis used a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in switched branches against changes in non-switched branches

### Service Score Changes

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 44.8 | 45.3 | +0.6 |
| Switched (new procedure) | 49.9 | 52.8 | +2.9 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+2.4** |

### Statistical Results
- **Estimated effect:** +2.43 service score points
- **95% Confidence Interval:** -0.25 to +5.10 points
- **P-value:** 0.075 (marginally significant)
- The confidence interval includes zero, indicating uncertainty about whether the true effect is positive

### Validity Checks
- **Parallel trends confirmed:** Before the switch, both groups showed similar trends in service scores (p=0.89), supporting the validity of the comparison
- **Control variables:** Accounting for staffing levels and visit volumes did not substantially change the results
- **Visit volume matters:** Higher branch visits are significantly associated with higher service scores (p=0.001)

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Continue Monitoring** — Collect additional weeks of data to increase statistical power. The current 8 weeks post-switch may be insufficient to detect a modest but real effect.

2. **Consider Expanded Rollout** — Given the positive (though not definitive) results and no evidence of harm, proceeding with broader implementation may be reasonable. Monitor closely for any branches that struggle with the new procedure.

3. **Investigate Heterogeneous Effects** — Analyze whether certain types of branches (by size, location, or baseline performance) benefit more from the new procedure to inform targeted implementation strategies.

4. **Track Related Metrics** — Continue monitoring staffing and visit volumes, as these are associated with service scores. Consider whether the new procedure affects operational efficiency or patron satisfaction beyond the service score.

5. **Set a Review Date** — Revisit this analysis after 12–16 weeks of post-switch data to reassess statistical significance. A larger sample may narrow the confidence interval and provide a clearer answer.
