# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new shelving and stock rotation procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **1.82 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.25 to 2.39) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents a **3.7% improvement** and is highly statistically significant (p < 0.001). The change is working as intended.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Clear Positive Effect
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Treatment effect | +1.82 service score points |
| 95% Confidence interval | +1.25 to +2.39 points |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 |
| Percentage improvement | 3.7% |

### 2. Broad-Based Improvement
- **71.8% of switched branches** (305 out of 425) showed score improvements after adopting the new procedure
- Average improvement for switched branches: **+2.05 points**
- Average change for non-switched branches: **+0.28 points** (minimal change)

### 3. Effect Persists Over Time
The improvement was sustained throughout the 8-week post-switch period (weeks 15-22), with switched branches consistently outperforming non-switched branches by 2-4 points each week.

### 4. Robust Results
The finding holds across multiple analytical approaches, including controls for staffing levels, visit volumes, and branch-specific characteristics.

---

## **Methodology Note**

This analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing 425 branches that switched to the new procedure against 284 branches that kept the old procedure. By examining changes in both groups before and after week 15 (when the switch occurred), we isolate the effect of the procedure change from other factors that might affect all branches. The analysis controls for staffing levels, visit volumes, and branch-specific characteristics.
