# **Impact of New Shelving Procedure on Library Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new shelving and stock rotation procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by **1.96 points** (approximately **4% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This result is statistically significant with high confidence, and the improvement has been sustained throughout the post-implementation period.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measurable Improvement in Service Quality
- **Treatment effect:** +1.96 points on the service score index (95% confidence interval: 1.39 to 2.54)
- **Relative improvement:** 3.9% increase from the treatment group's baseline of 50.17
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001 (highly significant)

### 2. Strong Research Design
- **709 branches** analyzed over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **425 branches** adopted the new procedure; **284 branches** continued with the old procedure
- The switch occurred in **week 15**, providing 9 weeks of pre-switch and 8 weeks of post-switch data
- Pre-switch trends were parallel between groups, validating the comparison

### 3. Effect Is Robust
- The improvement persists after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume
- The effect has been **consistent and even growing** in the weeks following implementation
- Control variables (staffing and visits) were well-balanced between treatment and control groups

## **Next Steps**

1. **Roll out the new procedure to remaining branches.** With 284 branches still using the old procedure, extending the new approach could yield system-wide service improvements.

2. **Monitor for sustained effects.** Continue tracking service scores to ensure the improvement persists beyond the initial 8-week post-implementation window.

3. **Investigate mechanisms.** Consider qualitative research to understand *why* the new procedure works—whether it's faster stock retrieval, better organization, or improved staff efficiency—to optimize implementation.

4. **Cost-benefit analysis.** Compare the operational costs of the new procedure against the value of a 4% service score improvement to confirm the change is economically justified.

5. **Share best practices.** Document the implementation process from the 425 early-adopter branches to support smooth rollout to remaining locations.
