# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure **improved service scores by approximately 1.9 points** (about 4% relative improvement). This effect is statistically significant and consistent across all branches that adopted the new procedure. We recommend expanding the new procedure to remaining branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Study Design
- **709 branches** analyzed over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **425 branches** switched to the new procedure (treatment group)
- **284 branches** kept the old procedure (control group)
- Switch occurred at **week 15**

### 2. Main Result: Positive Impact on Service Quality
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 50.4 | 48.5 | **-1.9** |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 50.9 | 51.0 | **+0.1** |

**Net effect of new procedure: +1.9 service score points**

The control group's scores actually declined after week 15, while branches using the new procedure maintained their performance. This suggests the new procedure may have helped branches resist broader downward pressures on service quality.

### 3. Statistical Confidence
- **95% confidence interval:** 1.5 to 2.3 points
- **p-value:** < 0.001 (highly statistically significant)
- Results hold after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume
- Results hold when accounting for individual branch characteristics

### 4. Effect Over Time
The benefit of the new procedure was immediate and grew over the 8-week post-switch period:
- Week 15: +1.6 points
- Week 22: +3.5 points

This suggests branches may still be realizing additional benefits as staff gain experience with the new procedure.

### 5. Parallel Trends Validated
Before the switch (weeks 6–14), both groups followed similar service score trends, supporting the validity of the comparison.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Expand rollout:** Consider implementing the new procedure at the remaining 284 branches, given the clear positive impact.

2. **Monitor implementation:** Track whether newer adopters show similar gains, and whether the effect continues to grow with experience.

3. **Investigate mechanisms:** The analysis controlled for staffing and visitor volume, but further research could identify *why* the new procedure improves scores (e.g., faster item location, better shelf organization, reduced staff time on restocking).

4. **Cost-benefit analysis:** Quantify the operational costs of the new procedure against the service quality improvement to determine overall value.

5. **Longer-term follow-up:** Continue monitoring to confirm the effect persists beyond the initial 8-week post-switch period.
