## **Analysis of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure: Impact on Service Scores**

### **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure **did not produce a statistically significant improvement** in service scores. While branches using the new procedure showed a modest 1.7-point higher score compared to what would have been expected, this effect is not distinguishable from random variation given our sample size. We cannot conclude that the new procedure improved service quality.

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. No Statistically Significant Effect**
- The estimated impact of the new procedure is **+1.7 points** on the service score index
- However, this effect is **not statistically significant** (p=0.16), meaning we cannot rule out that the observed difference is due to chance
- The 95% confidence interval ranges from approximately **-0.6 to +4.0 points**, including zero

**2. Parallel Trends Assumption Holds**
- Before the switch (weeks 6–14), treatment and control branches followed similar trends
- Statistical tests confirm no pre-existing differential trends between groups (p=0.70)
- This validates our comparison approach

**3. Limited Statistical Power**
- With only 34 branches (20 switched, 14 did not), the analysis can only reliably detect effects larger than about **14 points**
- The observed effect of 1.7 points is well below this threshold
- A much larger sample or longer observation period would be needed to detect modest effects

**4. Consistent Group Differences**
- Branches that switched to the new procedure consistently score about **4–5 points higher** than control branches, both before and after the switch
- This reflects underlying branch characteristics rather than the procedure change itself

**5. No Clear Pattern Over Time**
- Week-by-week analysis shows no systematic divergence between groups after the switch
- Service scores fluctuate similarly for both groups throughout the observation period

---

### **Next Steps**

1. **Continue Monitoring**: Extend the observation period to capture more post-switch data. Effects may emerge with more time as staff become fully accustomed to the new procedure.

2. **Qualitative Feedback**: Gather direct feedback from branch staff about the new procedure's impact on workflow, efficiency, and patron experience. Service scores may not capture all relevant outcomes.

3. **Branch-Level Analysis**: Investigate whether the procedure works better in certain types of branches (e.g., by size, location, or patron volume). Aggregate analysis may mask heterogeneous effects.

4. **Consider Alternative Metrics**: Evaluate whether other measures (e.g., circulation, hold fulfillment time, staff time savings) better capture the procedure's benefits.

5. **Revisit in 6–12 Months**: Conduct a follow-up analysis with additional data to determine whether effects emerge over a longer horizon.

### **Technical Notes**

- **Method**: Difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch)
- **Data**: 34 branches, 17 weeks (weeks 6–22), 578 observations
- **Controls**: Staffing index and visits index included in main specification
- **Switch Point**: Week 15
