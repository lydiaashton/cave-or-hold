# **Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure **improved service scores by approximately 2 points** (about 4% relative to the 50-point average). This effect is statistically significant and appears to strengthen over time as branches gain experience with the new system. We recommend continuing the rollout while monitoring implementation quality.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **709 branches** tracked over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- **425 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15 (treatment group)
- **284 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- This natural experiment allows us to isolate the effect of the procedure change

### Main Results

| Group | Pre-Switch Avg | Post-Switch Avg | Change |
|-------|---------------|-----------------|--------|
| Control (no switch) | 51.0 | 50.0 | −1.0 |
| Treatment (switched) | 51.6 | 52.8 | +1.2 |
| **Difference** | **+0.6** | **+2.8** | **+2.1** ★ |

★ The +2.1 point difference-in-differences estimate represents the causal effect of the new procedure.

### Statistical Confidence
- **Estimated effect: +2.08 points**
- **95% confidence interval: 1.55 to 2.61 points**
- **Statistical significance: p < 0.001** (highly reliable)

### Effect Over Time
The benefit of the new procedure **grows as branches gain experience**:
- Weeks 15–17: Modest improvement (+0.4 to +0.8 points)
- Weeks 18–22: Stronger improvement (+1.0 to +2.3 points)

This pattern suggests a learning curve—branches realize more benefit as staff become proficient with the new system.

### Additional Insights
- **Staffing and visits matter**: Both staffing levels and visitor volume positively correlate with service scores (each 1-unit increase associates with ~0.5 point higher scores)
- **Control group declined slightly**: Branches keeping the old procedure saw scores drop by 1 point post-week-14, while switched branches improved—suggesting the new procedure may also protect against seasonal or operational headwinds

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Continue the rollout**: The evidence supports expanding the new procedure to remaining branches, with expected service score gains of ~2 points per branch.

2. **Support implementation**: Since effects grow over time, invest in training and onboarding support during the first 4–6 weeks after switch to accelerate the learning curve.

3. **Monitor high-performers**: Branches showing the strongest gains (weeks 19–22) may have implementation practices worth documenting and sharing.

4. **Track long-term effects**: Continue monitoring to confirm effects persist beyond the 8-week post-switch window and don't fade over time.

5. **Consider cost-benefit**: If the new procedure has implementation costs, weigh them against the value of a 4% service score improvement (which may correlate with patron satisfaction, circulation, or other outcomes).

---

*Analysis conducted using difference-in-differences regression with branch fixed effects and clustered standard errors. Data covers weeks 6–22 with procedure switch at week 15.*
