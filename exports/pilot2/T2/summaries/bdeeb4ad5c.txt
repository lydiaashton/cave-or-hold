# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 14, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Impact of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure on Service Scores

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure is associated with a **2.3-point improvement in service scores** (approximately 4.6% relative improvement). While this effect is in the expected direction and consistent across all analytical approaches, the statistical evidence is **moderate rather than definitive** (p-value ~0.08-0.12). We recommend continuing the new procedure while gathering additional data to confirm the effect.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### 1. Service Score Improvement
| Metric | Treatment Branches (Switched) | Control Branches (Not Switched) |
|--------|------------------------------|--------------------------------|
| Before switch (Weeks 6-14) | 49.9 | 44.8 |
| After switch (Weeks 15-22) | 52.8 | 45.3 |
| **Change** | **+2.9 points** | **+0.6 points** |

**Net effect (Difference-in-Differences): +2.3 points**

### 2. Study Design
- **34 branches total:** 20 adopted the new procedure, 14 continued with the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data:** Weeks 6-22, with the switch implemented in Week 15
- **578 observations:** Weekly service scores for all branches
- **Analysis method:** Difference-in-Differences with branch fixed effects and cluster-robust standard errors

### 3. Statistical Confidence
- The estimated effect is **positive and consistent** across all model specifications
- Statistical significance is **marginal** (p-values between 0.075 and 0.124)
- The **parallel trends assumption holds**: treatment and control branches showed similar trends before the switch
- Controlling for staffing and visit levels does not materially change the result

### 4. Practical Significance
- A 2.3-point increase represents meaningful improvement on a ~50-point scale
- Treatment branches improved nearly **5 times more** than control branches (+2.9 vs +0.6 points)
- The effect is visible in the weekly trend data, with the gap between groups widening after Week 15

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

1. **Continue the new procedure** — The evidence points to improvement, and there is no indication of negative effects.

2. **Extend the observation period** — Collect 8-12 more weeks of data to increase statistical power and confirm the effect persists.

3. **Consider expansion** — If the effect is confirmed with additional data, roll out the new procedure to remaining branches.

4. **Investigate mechanisms** — Analyze which aspects of the new procedure (rotation frequency, shelving organization, etc.) drive the improvement.

5. **Monitor cost-benefit** — Track any operational costs associated with the new procedure against the service score gains.
