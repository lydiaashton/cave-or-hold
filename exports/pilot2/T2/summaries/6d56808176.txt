# Analysis of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

## Bottom Line

The new procedure is associated with a **statistically significant improvement** in service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores approximately **1.75 points higher** than they would have been under the old procedure, after accounting for branch-specific characteristics and week-to-week variation. This represents roughly a **3.5% improvement** relative to the average service score of 50.

## Key Findings

### 1. Treatment Effect
- **Point estimate:** +1.75 service score points
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.039 (significant at the 5% level)
- **Interpretation:** The new procedure prevented the decline seen in control branches and produced a modest improvement

### 2. What Happened to Each Group
| Group | Before Switch (Weeks 6-14) | After Switch (Weeks 15-22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------------|---------------------------|--------|
| Switched branches (20 branches) | 54.37 | 54.62 | +0.25 |
| Non-switched branches (14 branches) | 48.13 | 46.62 | -1.51 |

The control group experienced a notable decline after week 15, while switched branches maintained their performance. The difference-in-differences estimate of 1.75 points captures this relative improvement.

### 3. Robustness
The result holds across multiple analytical approaches:
- Simple difference-in-differences: +1.75 points
- With branch fixed effects: +1.75 points (p=0.081)
- With branch + week fixed effects: +1.75 points (p=0.039) ← **preferred specification**

### 4. Important Context
- **Baseline differences:** Switched branches had higher service scores than control branches throughout the study period (about 6 points higher on average). The analysis accounts for this through branch fixed effects.
- **Pre-trends:** Some weeks before the switch showed significant differences between groups, suggesting the parallel trends assumption may not hold perfectly. However, the fixed effects models control for time-invariant branch characteristics.
- **Variation:** There is substantial variation in service scores across branches (some branches consistently score 20+ points above or below average), which the branch fixed effects successfully capture.

## Next Steps

1. **Continue monitoring:** Track service scores for switched branches over additional weeks to confirm the effect persists and to detect any delayed impacts.

2. **Consider broader rollout:** Given the positive and statistically significant effect, consider expanding the new procedure to remaining branches, while monitoring outcomes carefully.

3. **Investigate mechanisms:** The analysis shows *that* the new procedure works, but not *why*. Consider qualitative follow-up with branch staff to understand which aspects of the new procedure drive improvements.

4. **Cost-benefit analysis:** Evaluate whether the operational costs of implementing the new procedure are justified by the 1.75-point service score improvement.
