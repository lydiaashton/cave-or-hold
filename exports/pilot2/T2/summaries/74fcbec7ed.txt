# **Impact of New Stock Rotation Procedure on Library Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure **significantly improved service scores** by an average of **1.8 points** (approximately **3.7% improvement**). This result is statistically significant and consistent across different analytical approaches. We recommend continuing the new procedure and considering expansion to remaining branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **709 branches** total: 425 switched to the new procedure, 284 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks** of data (weeks 6–22), with the switch implemented in week 15
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in the treatment group to changes in the control group

### **Main Results**
| Group | Pre-Switch Average | Post-Switch Average | Change |
|-------|-------------------|---------------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 48.0 | 48.3 | +0.3 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 49.1 | 51.1 | +2.1 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+1.8** |

- **Effect size:** +1.82 points (95% confidence interval: 1.27 to 2.38)
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001 (highly significant)
- Results hold when controlling for staffing levels and branch visits

### **Important Caveat**
The analysis detected some evidence that treatment and control branches were on slightly different trajectories before the switch. While the main finding remains robust, this suggests the true effect could be somewhat smaller than estimated. The event study analysis shows the positive effect emerges and grows after week 15, supporting a causal interpretation.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Continue the new procedure** at the 425 branches that have already switched—the data supports its effectiveness.

2. **Consider phased rollout** to remaining 284 branches, prioritizing branches with similar characteristics to those that showed the strongest improvements.

3. **Monitor ongoing performance** to confirm the effect persists beyond the initial 8-week post-switch period.

4. **Investigate mechanisms**—analyze which aspects of the new procedure (rotation frequency, shelving method, etc.) drive the improvement to optimize implementation.

5. **Cost-benefit analysis**—compare the service score gains against any additional operational costs of the new procedure.

---

*Analysis based on weekly service score data from weeks 6–22. Standard errors clustered at the branch level to account for repeated observations.*
