# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure is associated with a **modest improvement in service scores** (+2.3 points, or approximately 5%), but the evidence is **not statistically conclusive** with the current data. We recommend continuing the new procedure while collecting additional data to confirm the effect.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **34 branches** participated in the evaluation (20 adopted the new procedure, 14 kept the old)
- **17 weeks** of data collected (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Rigorous difference-in-differences analysis controls for branch-specific characteristics and underlying trends

### Service Score Results

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (Old Procedure) | 44.8 | 45.3 | +0.6 |
| Treatment (New Procedure) | 49.9 | 52.8 | +2.9 |
| **Estimated Effect** | | | **+2.3 points** |

- Branches using the new procedure improved by **2.9 points** after the switch
- Control branches improved by only **0.6 points** over the same period
- The **net effect of the new procedure is +2.3 points** (approximately 5% improvement)

### Statistical Assessment
- **P-value: 0.11** — The result does not meet the conventional threshold for statistical significance (p < 0.05)
- **95% confidence interval: -0.5 to +5.1 points** — The true effect could range from negligible to moderately positive
- **Pre-trend validation passed** — Treatment and control branches showed parallel trends before the switch, supporting the validity of our analysis

### Interpretation
The lack of statistical significance likely reflects **limited data** (only 8 weeks post-switch) rather than absence of a real effect. The consistent positive point estimate across all model specifications, combined with validated pre-trends, suggests the new procedure is probably beneficial.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Continue the new procedure** at the 20 branches that have adopted it — the evidence points toward improvement with no indication of harm.

2. **Collect additional data** — Extend monitoring for 8–12 more weeks to increase statistical power and narrow the confidence interval.

3. **Consider phased rollout** — If continued data confirms the positive effect, expand the new procedure to remaining branches.

4. **Monitor operational metrics** — Track staffing efficiency and visit patterns alongside service scores to assess broader operational impacts.

5. **Re-evaluate in Q2** — Conduct a follow-up analysis with the expanded dataset to make a definitive recommendation on system-wide adoption.

---

*Analysis prepared using difference-in-differences methodology with branch fixed effects and clustered standard errors. Data covers weeks 6–22; procedure switch occurred week 15.*
