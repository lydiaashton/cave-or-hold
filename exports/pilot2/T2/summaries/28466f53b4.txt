# **Board Summary: Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure shows a **positive but statistically uncertain effect** on service scores. Branches that switched to the new procedure improved by approximately **1.7 points** relative to branches that kept the old procedure. However, this difference is not statistically significant at conventional levels (p=0.16), meaning we cannot confidently rule out that the observed improvement occurred by chance. The data quality is strong, with no evidence that the treatment and control groups were on different trajectories before the switch.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** total: 20 switched to the new procedure (treatment), 14 kept the old procedure (control)
- **17 weeks** of data (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring in week 15
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in service scores before and after the switch between the two groups

### **Results**
| Group | Before Switch (Weeks 6–14) | After Switch (Weeks 15–22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------------|---------------------------|--------|
| Control (Old Procedure) | 49.03 | 47.84 | −1.19 |
| Treatment (New Procedure) | 53.45 | 53.96 | +0.50 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+1.69** |

- The **DiD estimate of +1.69 points** suggests the new procedure may improve service scores
- **Statistical significance**: p = 0.16 (not significant at the 0.05 level)
- **95% confidence interval**: approximately −0.7 to +4.1 points

### **Validity Checks**
- **Parallel trends assumption**: ✅ **Passed** (p = 0.996). Before the switch, treatment and control branches showed no systematic differences in their service score trends, supporting the validity of the comparison.
- **Control variables**: Staffing levels were positively associated with service scores (p < 0.01), but including these controls did not change the main result.
- **Event study analysis**: Post-switch coefficients are consistently positive but individually not statistically significant, with no clear pattern of increasing or decreasing effects over time.

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended Actions**

1. **Continue monitoring for 8–12 more weeks**
   - The current sample may be underpowered to detect a modest but meaningful effect. Additional post-switch data would improve precision and allow detection of effects as small as 1 point with greater confidence.

2. **Consider a phased rollout evaluation**
   - If resources allow, randomly assign remaining branches to switch in different weeks. This would strengthen causal inference and provide more data points for analysis.

3. **Investigate heterogeneous effects**
   - Analyze whether the procedure works better for certain branch types (e.g., by size, location, or baseline performance). Some branches may benefit more than others.

4. **Track complementary metrics**
   - Monitor staffing efficiency, patron wait times, and operational costs alongside service scores to assess the full impact of the new procedure.

### **Decision Guidance**

Given the **positive direction** of the effect, **strong validity checks**, and **no evidence of harm**, the cooperative may consider:
- **Proceeding with the rollout** if the procedure has low implementation costs and operational benefits beyond service scores
- **Continuing the evaluation** alongside rollout to gather more evidence before making a final determination
