# **Board Summary: New Branch Rotation Procedure Impact Analysis**

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure shows a **positive but statistically inconclusive effect** on service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure scored approximately **1.8 points higher** than expected, but this result is not statistically significant at conventional levels. The data cannot confidently distinguish between no effect and a meaningful improvement of up to 4 points.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** switched to the new procedure at week 15 (treatment group)
- **14 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in treatment vs. control branches before and after the switch

### **Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 48.1 | 46.6 | **-1.5** |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 54.4 | 54.6 | **+0.3** |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+1.8** |

- **Point estimate**: +1.75 points (approximately +3.6% relative to baseline)
- **95% confidence interval**: -0.9 to +4.4 points
- **Statistical significance**: Not significant (p ≈ 0.20)

### **What This Means**
1. **Treatment branches held steady** while **control branches declined** after week 15
2. The new procedure may have **prevented a decline** rather than driving improvement
3. We cannot rule out that the true effect is zero—or that it could be as high as 4 points

### **Validity Check**
- Pre-treatment trends between groups show **no significant divergence** before the switch (p = 0.36), supporting the validity of our comparison
- Results are robust to controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Continue monitoring**: Extend the observation period to increase statistical power. More post-switch data would narrow the confidence interval.

2. **Investigate the control group decline**: Service scores dropped in branches that kept the old procedure. Understanding why (seasonal effects, staffing changes, external factors) would clarify whether the new procedure prevented deterioration.

3. **Consider a phased rollout**: Since the new procedure shows no negative effects and potential benefits, consider expanding it to remaining branches while continuing to track outcomes.

4. **Supplement with qualitative feedback**: Service scores are one metric. Gather staff and patron feedback on the new procedure's practical impacts (efficiency, satisfaction, workflow).

5. **Re-evaluate in 8–12 weeks**: With additional data, we can provide a more definitive assessment of the procedure's impact.
