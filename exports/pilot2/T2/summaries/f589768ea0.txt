## **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

### **Title: Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

---

### **Bottom Line**

The new procedure **improved service scores by approximately 2.1 points** (on a scale where the average branch scores ~50). This represents a **4% improvement** and is statistically significant. The effect appears to strengthen over time, with switched branches pulling further ahead of control branches in the weeks following implementation.

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. Measurable Improvement in Service Quality**
- Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by **2.1 points** relative to branches that kept the old procedure
- This effect is highly statistically significant (p < 0.001)
- The improvement persisted after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

**2. Effect Strengthens Over Time**
- In the first week after the switch, the gap between switched and control branches was about 2.3 points
- By week 22 (the final week of data), the gap had grown to 3.7 points
- This suggests branches continue to realize benefits as they gain experience with the new procedure

**3. Control Group Performance**
- Branches that kept the old procedure saw a slight decline in service scores after week 15 (about 0.95 points)
- This may reflect seasonal patterns or increased demand that the new procedure helps branches manage

**4. Important Caveat**
- Branches that chose to switch were already performing slightly better than control branches before the change (pre-existing gap of ~0.6 points)
- This suggests some selection bias—more motivated or better-resourced branches may have opted into the new procedure
- The 2.1-point estimate accounts for this pre-existing difference, but the true effect could be somewhat smaller

---

### **Next Steps**

1. **Expand the new procedure to remaining branches** — The evidence supports rolling out the procedure system-wide, with attention to providing adequate training and support

2. **Monitor implementation quality** — Since benefits appear to grow over time, ensure branches receive ongoing support as they adopt the new procedure

3. **Conduct follow-up analysis** — Track service scores for an additional 8-12 weeks to confirm the effect persists and to better understand the learning curve

4. **Investigate why control branches declined** — Understanding this pattern could reveal additional opportunities for improvement

5. **Consider a formal randomized rollout** — For future procedural changes, random assignment would eliminate selection bias and provide cleaner causal estimates

---

**Analysis Details:**
- Data: 709 branches, 17 weeks (weeks 6-22)
- Method: Difference-in-differences regression with branch-level clustering
- Treatment: 425 branches switched; 284 branches kept old procedure
- Switch date: Week 15
