# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new shelving and stock rotation procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **1.82 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.25 to 2.39) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents a **3.7% improvement** and is highly statistically significant (p < 0.001). The change is working as intended.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Clear Positive Effect
- **Treatment effect:** +1.82 service score points
- **Confidence:** We are 95% confident the true effect lies between +1.25 and +2.39 points
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001 (less than 0.1% chance this result is due to random variation)

### 2. Broad-Based Improvement
- **71.8% of switched branches** (305 out of 425) showed score improvements after adopting the new procedure
- Average improvement for switched branches: **+2.05 points**
- Average change for non-switched branches: **+0.28 points** (minimal change)

### 3. Effect Persists Over Time
The improvement was sustained throughout the 8-week post-switch period (weeks 15-22), with switched branches consistently outperforming non-switched branches by 2-4 points each week.

### 4. Robust Results
The finding holds across multiple analytical approaches:
- Basic comparison: +1.76 points
- Adjusted for staffing and visit levels: +1.82 points
- Controlling for branch-specific and week-specific factors: +1.82 points

---

## **Methodology Note**

This analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, which compares:
- **Treatment group:** 425 branches that switched to the new procedure
- **Control group:** 284 branches that kept the old procedure

By comparing changes in both groups before and after week 15 (when the switch occurred), we isolate the effect of the procedure change from other factors that might affect all branches (such as seasonal patterns or system-wide events). The analysis controls for staffing levels, visit volumes, and branch-specific characteristics.
