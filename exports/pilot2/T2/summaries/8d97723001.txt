# **Board Summary: Impact of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure shows a **positive effect on service scores**, with branches that adopted the new procedure scoring approximately **1.8 points higher** than expected based on pre-switch trends. While this result is not statistically significant at conventional levels (p=0.16), the evidence is suggestive of a meaningful benefit—particularly because control branches experienced a decline in scores after week 15, while treatment branches maintained their performance. We recommend continuing to monitor the results and consider broader implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Study Design**
- **34 branches** analyzed over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure (treatment group); **14 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- The switch occurred at **week 15**
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in service scores before and after the switch between the two groups

### **2. Main Results**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated treatment effect | **+1.82 points** on service score |
| 95% Confidence Interval | -0.71 to +4.36 points |
| Statistical significance | Not significant at 5% level (p=0.159) |

### **3. What the Numbers Mean**
- **Treatment branches**: Service scores remained stable (54.4 → 54.6, +0.2 points)
- **Control branches**: Service scores declined (48.1 → 46.6, -1.5 points)
- The new procedure appears to have **helped branches maintain service quality** while control branches experienced a downturn

### **4. Supporting Evidence**
- **Parallel trends confirmed**: No significant differences in trends between groups before the switch (validates our analysis approach)
- **Effects grew over time**: The treatment effect was stronger in later weeks (weeks 21–22 showed statistically significant improvements of +4.9 to +7.4 points)
- **Robust across models**: The +1.8 point estimate is consistent whether we control for staffing, visitor traffic, or branch-specific characteristics

### **5. Important Context**
- Average service score across all branches is approximately **50 points**
- A 1.8-point improvement represents roughly a **3–4% gain** relative to the average
- The confidence interval suggests the true effect could range from slightly negative (-0.7) to moderately positive (+4.4)

## **Next Steps**

### **1. Continue Monitoring (High Priority)**
- Track service scores for an additional 8–12 weeks to determine if the positive trend continues
- The growing effect in weeks 21–22 suggests benefits may accumulate over time as staff become more proficient with the new procedure

### **2. Consider Phased Expansion (Medium Priority)**
- Given the positive point estimate and lack of negative effects, consider offering the new procedure to remaining branches on a voluntary basis
- Prioritize branches with similar characteristics to the current treatment group

### **3. Investigate Mechanisms (Medium Priority)**
- Conduct qualitative interviews with treatment branch staff to understand what aspects of the new procedure are working well
- Examine whether the effect varies by branch size, location, or baseline performance

### **4. Formal Re-evaluation (Low Priority)**
- Re-run this analysis after 6 months of data to assess whether statistical significance improves with a larger sample
- Consider a randomized rollout to remaining branches for more definitive causal evidence
