# **Memorandum to the Board**

## **Subject: Impact of New Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

---

### **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **2.2 points** (approximately 4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and represents a meaningful improvement in branch service quality.

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. Study Design**
- Analyzed 34 branches over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- 20 branches adopted the new procedure starting week 15; 14 branches continued with the old procedure
- Used a difference-in-differences approach, which compares changes over time between switching and non-switching branches while controlling for branch-specific characteristics

**2. Main Result**
- The new procedure increased service scores by **2.16 points** (95% confidence interval: 0.32 to 4.00 points)
- This effect is statistically significant (p = 0.022)
- The improvement represents approximately a **4% gain** relative to the average service score of 50

**3. Trends Over Time**
- Before the switch (weeks 6–14), both groups showed similar service score trends, supporting the validity of the comparison
- After the switch (weeks 15–22), branches using the new procedure pulled ahead of those using the old procedure
- Both groups improved after week 15, but the switching branches improved more

**4. Additional Factors**
- Staffing levels and visit volumes also affect service scores (both positively associated)
- The estimated effect of the new procedure holds even after accounting for these factors and inherent differences between branches

---

### **Next Steps**

1. **Consider expanding the new procedure** to remaining branches, given the positive and statistically significant impact on service scores

2. **Monitor implementation quality** – the 2-point average improvement suggests the procedure works, but there may be variation across branches worth investigating

3. **Track longer-term effects** – this analysis covers 8 weeks post-switch; continued monitoring will show whether gains are sustained

4. **Investigate mechanisms** – consider qualitative feedback from staff at switching branches to understand why the new procedure improves service (e.g., faster restocking, better organization, reduced customer wait times)

5. **Cost-benefit analysis** – weigh the 4% service score improvement against any additional costs or training requirements of the new procedure
