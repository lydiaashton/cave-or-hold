# **Memorandum to the Board**

## **Title: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure is associated with a **modest improvement in service scores**, but the effect took time to materialize. During the first month after implementation (weeks 15-18), there was no detectable benefit. However, in the second month (weeks 19-22), branches using the new procedure showed **significantly higher service scores—about 3.5 points higher** than would be expected based on trends at branches that kept the old procedure. This represents roughly a **7% improvement** relative to the baseline.

The overall estimated effect across the entire post-switch period is **+2.1 points**, though this average is not statistically significant at conventional levels due to the weak early results.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. **Study Design**
- **34 branches** were tracked over 17 weeks (weeks 6-22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure
- This "natural experiment" design allows us to isolate the effect of the new procedure by comparing changes in the switching branches against changes in the non-switching branches

### 2. **Main Results**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Overall effect estimate | +2.1 service score points |
| 95% confidence interval | -1.0 to +5.3 points |
| Statistical significance | Not significant overall (p=0.19) |
| Effect size | ~4% of baseline score |

### 3. **Timing Matters: A Learning Curve**
The benefits of the new procedure **emerged gradually**:

| Period | Effect Estimate | Statistical Significance |
|--------|-----------------|-------------------------|
| Weeks 15-18 (early) | +0.7 points | Not significant (p=0.66) |
| Weeks 19-22 (late) | +3.5 points | **Significant (p=0.04)** |

This pattern suggests staff needed time to become proficient with the new procedure before its benefits were realized.

### 4. **Parallel Trends Assumption Holds**
Before the switch (weeks 6-14), service score trends were statistically similar between branches that would switch and those that wouldn't. This supports the validity of our comparison.

### 5. **Staffing Changes**
Branches that switched showed a modest increase in staffing index relative to control branches (p=0.05). This could indicate the new procedure required additional staffing, or that better-staffed branches were more likely to adopt it. Further investigation is warranted.

---

## **Next Steps**

### Immediate Actions
1. **Continue monitoring** service scores for another 8-12 weeks to confirm the late-period gains are sustained
2. **Survey branch staff** at switched locations to identify implementation challenges during weeks 15-18 that may have delayed benefits

### Strategic Considerations
3. **Consider rolling out the new procedure to remaining branches**, given the positive late-period results
4. **Develop enhanced training materials** focused on the first month of implementation to accelerate the learning curve
5. **Review staffing implications**—if the new procedure requires more staff, factor this into cost-benefit calculations

### Further Analysis
6. **Investigate the staffing relationship** to determine whether increased staffing drove the service score improvements, or whether both resulted from the procedure change
7. **Examine branch-level heterogeneity** to identify whether certain types of branches benefit more from the new procedure

- Analysis uses difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch)
- Models control for weekly staffing and visitor volume indices
- Service score baseline average: ~50 points (higher is better)
- Data source: branches.csv, 578 branch-week observations
