# **Board Summary: Impact of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure is associated with a **statistically significant improvement of approximately 2 points** in weekly service scores (on a scale where the average branch scores ~50). This represents roughly a **4% improvement** in service quality. However, branches that adopted the new procedure were already showing slightly stronger performance trends before the switch, so the true effect may be somewhat smaller than estimated.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Treatment Effect Estimate**
- Using a difference-in-differences analysis comparing switched vs. non-switched branches before and after week 15:
  - **Estimated effect: +1.98 points** (95% confidence interval: +1.40 to +2.55)
  - **Statistical significance: p < 0.001** (highly significant)
  - Results are robust to controlling for staffing levels and visit volumes

### **2. Pre-Existing Differences (Important Caveat)**
- Branches that switched to the new procedure were already performing slightly better before the change:
  - Average pre-switch gap: 0.83 points higher for switched branches
  - This gap was widening even before the switch (from -0.17 in week 6 to +1.97 in week 14)
- **Implication:** The parallel trends assumption required for clean causal inference is not fully satisfied. The 2-point estimate may overstate the true effect if switched branches were already on a stronger improvement trajectory.

### **3. Post-Switch Performance**
- After the switch (weeks 15-22), the performance gap widened to an average of 2.80 points
- The increase in the gap from pre- to post-period (~2 points) is consistent with our main DiD estimate
- Effects appear sustained across all 8 post-switch weeks

### **4. Scope of Analysis**
- 709 branches total (425 switched, 284 control)
- 17 weeks of data (weeks 6-22)
- 12,053 branch-week observations

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended Actions**

1. **Continue monitoring** – Track service scores for another 8-12 weeks to confirm the effect persists and doesn't fade over time

2. **Investigate pre-trend drivers** – Understand why switched branches were already improving faster before the procedure change. Were these branches systematically different (e.g., larger, better staffed, more motivated)?

3. **Consider phased rollout analysis** – If additional branches will switch in the future, use a staggered adoption design for cleaner causal estimates

4. **Cost-benefit analysis** – Quantify the operational costs of the new procedure against the ~4% service score improvement to determine ROI

5. **Qualitative follow-up** – Interview branch managers at switched vs. control locations to understand implementation differences and contextual factors

### **Methodological Note**
The statistical analysis accounts for clustering of observations within branches and controls for time-invariant branch characteristics. However, the pre-trend violation suggests the estimate should be treated as an upper bound on the true effect.
