# **Impact of New Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure **improved service scores by approximately 1.9 points** (3.8% relative to baseline), with high statistical confidence. This represents a meaningful gain for the cooperative, particularly because the control group's scores declined during the same period while treatment branches maintained and improved their performance.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. **Positive Treatment Effect**
- Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by **+1.94 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure
- 95% confidence interval: **1.35 to 2.54 points**
- Result is highly statistically significant (p < 0.001)
- Analysis covered **709 branches** (425 treatment, 284 control) over 17 weeks

### 2. **The Control Group Declined—Treatment Branches Held Steady**
- Control branches dropped from 50.36 to 48.50 (−1.86 points) after week 14
- Treatment branches remained stable, moving from 50.92 to 51.00 (+0.08 points)
- The new procedure appears to have **protected branches from a broader downward trend** in service quality

### 3. **Benefits Accumulated Over Time**
- The effect was not immediate: weeks 15-16 showed modest, non-significant gains
- By weeks 17-22, the treatment effect became clearly positive and statistically significant
- This suggests staff needed **1-2 weeks to adapt** to the new procedure before realizing full benefits

### 4. **Parallel Trends Validated**
- Pre-switch trends (weeks 6-14) showed no systematic differences between groups (p = 0.057)
- This supports the validity of the comparison and strengthens confidence in the causal interpretation

### 5. **Direct Effect, Not Driven by Staffing or Visits**
- The procedure did not significantly change staffing levels or visitor volume
- Improvements appear to come from the procedure itself (e.g., better organization, faster item retrieval) rather than secondary factors

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Expand rollout to remaining branches**: With 284 branches still on the old procedure, there is substantial opportunity to raise system-wide service scores

2. **Document and share best practices**: Since benefits grew over time, branches that successfully adapted may have implementation insights worth sharing

3. **Monitor for sustained effects**: Continue tracking service scores to confirm benefits persist beyond the initial 8-week post-switch window

4. **Investigate the control group decline**: Understand why non-switching branches saw scores drop—this may reveal systemic issues the new procedure helps mitigate

5. **Consider cost-benefit analysis**: Quantify the operational costs of the new procedure against the 3.8% service score improvement to assess ROI
