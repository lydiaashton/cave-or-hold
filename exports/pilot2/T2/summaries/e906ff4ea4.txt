# **Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure Evaluation**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores, but the effect is modest and took time to materialize.** Branches that switched to the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **1.2 points (2.4% improvement)** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant but smaller than the initial 1.8-point estimate suggested.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Treatment Effect**
| Estimate Method | Service Score Improvement |
|-----------------|---------------------------|
| Simple comparison | +1.8 points |
| **Adjusted for pre-trends (recommended)** | **+1.2 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | +0.3 to +2.1 points |
| Statistical significance | p = 0.008 (significant) |

### **2. Effect Timing**
The improvement was **not immediate**:
- **Weeks 15-17** (first 3 weeks after switch): No measurable effect
- **Weeks 18-22** (weeks 4-8 after switch): Clear positive effect of 1.1–1.7 points

This suggests branches needed time to adapt to the new procedure before benefits appeared.

### **3. Important Caveat**
Our analysis detected **different pre-switch trends** between branches that switched and those that didn't. In early weeks (6-8), switching branches scored 1.8–2.2 points *lower* than non-switching branches, then caught up by weeks 13-14. This means:
- The simple 1.8-point estimate is likely **inflated**
- The 1.2-point adjusted estimate is more credible
- We cannot fully rule out that some improvement was already underway before the switch

### **4. Data Overview**
- **709 branches** analyzed over 17 weeks (weeks 6-22)
- **425 branches** switched to new procedure; **284** kept old procedure
- Switch occurred at **week 15**
- Average service score across all branches: **52.0**

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Continue monitoring** for at least 8-12 more weeks to confirm the effect stabilizes and doesn't fade.

2. **Investigate the delayed effect**: Why did benefits only appear 4 weeks after implementation? Consider:
   - Staff training period
   - Time needed to reorganize inventory
   - Patron adjustment period

3. **Understand the pre-trend difference**: Why were switching branches underperforming early on? This could indicate:
   - Selection bias (struggling branches were prioritized for the new procedure)
   - Other unmeasured factors affecting those branches

4. **Cost-benefit analysis**: At ~2.4% improvement, does the new procedure justify its implementation costs? Compare against:
   - Staff time for reorganization
   - Any additional operational costs
   - Alternative improvements that could be made

5. **Consider phased rollout**: If expanding to remaining branches, implement gradually and monitor closely to isolate the true effect.
