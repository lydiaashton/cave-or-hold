# Analysis of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

## Bottom Line

**The new stock rotation procedure improved service scores by approximately 1.8–2.3 points on average.** This is a statistically significant improvement (p < 0.001). The effect was not immediate but grew over time, with branches showing stronger gains 3–4 weeks after implementation.

---

## Key Findings

### 1. Study Design
- **709 branches** total: 425 adopted the new procedure (treatment), 284 kept the old procedure (control)
- Data covers **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring in week 15
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in treated branches to changes in control branches

### 2. Main Results

| Period | Control Branches | Treated Branches | Difference |
|--------|-----------------|-----------------|------------|
| Before switch (weeks 6–14) | 50.18 | 50.14 | -0.04 |
| After switch (weeks 15–22) | 52.91 | 54.68 | +1.77 |
| **Treatment Effect (DiD)** | | | **+1.85** |

- The **1.85-point improvement** is highly statistically significant (p < 0.001)
- Results hold after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume
- In the later weeks (18–22), the effect grew to approximately **2.3 points**

### 3. Parallel Trends Check
Before the switch, treated and control branches had nearly identical service scores (difference of only 0.04 points), supporting the validity of the comparison. The event study analysis shows the treatment effect emerged gradually after week 15, with statistically significant gains appearing by weeks 18–19.

### 4. Effect Over Time
The improvement was not immediate:
- Weeks 15–17: Small, not statistically significant effects
- Weeks 18–22: Clear, significant improvements of 1.3–1.8 points per week

This pattern suggests branches needed time to fully implement and benefit from the new procedure.

---

## Next Steps

1. **Expand the new procedure to remaining branches.** The evidence supports rolling out the new stock rotation procedure to all 284 branches still using the old method.

2. **Monitor implementation quality.** The delayed effect suggests proper training and adjustment time is important. Consider providing additional support during the first 3–4 weeks after switching.

3. **Track long-term sustainability.** Continue monitoring service scores to ensure the improvement persists beyond the initial 8-week post-switch period.

4. **Investigate mechanisms.** The effect appears independent of staffing and visit volume changes. Consider qualitative follow-up to understand *why* the new procedure works (e.g., faster item location, better organization, staff efficiency).

5. **Cost-benefit analysis.** Quantify the operational costs of the new procedure against the service score gains to assess overall value.
