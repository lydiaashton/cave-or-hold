# **Analysis of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure shows a **positive but statistically uncertain effect** on service scores. Switched branches improved by an estimated **2.1 points** more than non-switched branches after the change, but this difference is not statistically significant at conventional levels (p=0.16). However, week-by-week analysis reveals **significant improvements in specific weeks** following the switch, suggesting the procedure may be beneficial but with variable impact.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Overall Impact
- **Estimated effect:** +2.15 service score points (95% CI: -0.8 to +5.1)
- Switched branches went from 48.0 to 53.2 (+5.3 points)
- Non-switched branches went from 48.4 to 51.5 (+3.1 points)
- The additional improvement in switched branches (the "treatment effect") is positive but not statistically distinguishable from zero with our current data

### 2. Week-by-Week Patterns (Event Study)
The effect is not uniform across all weeks:

| Period | Pattern |
|--------|---------|
| **Before switch** (weeks 6-14) | No consistent differences between groups; trends were roughly parallel |
| **After switch** (weeks 15-22) | Three weeks showed statistically significant improvements: Week 15 (+6.2 pts), Week 17 (+8.1 pts), and Week 21 (+6.6 pts) |

### 3. Study Design & Data
- **34 branches total:** 20 switched to new procedure, 14 kept old procedure
- **17 weeks of data:** Weeks 6-14 (before), Weeks 15-22 (after)
- **578 observations** with cluster-robust standard errors
- Service scores average ~50; a 2-point change represents about 4% of the typical score

### 4. Why the Result is Uncertain
With 34 branches and only 8 weeks of post-switch data, the study has **limited statistical power**. To detect an effect as statistically significant, we would need an impact larger than 3.0 points. The observed 2.1-point effect could be real but too small to confirm with this sample size.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Continue monitoring:** Collect additional weeks of data to increase statistical power and determine if the positive trend persists

2. **Investigate variation:** The week-by-week analysis shows the effect is not consistent. Work with branch managers to understand what factors (staffing, visitor volume, implementation fidelity) may explain why some weeks show strong gains and others do not

3. **Consider expansion:** Given the positive point estimate and significant effects in several weeks, consider a phased rollout to additional branches while continuing to track outcomes

4. **Supplement with qualitative feedback:** Survey staff at switched branches about their experience with the new procedure to identify implementation challenges or best practices
