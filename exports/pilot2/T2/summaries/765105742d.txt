# **Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new shelving and stock rotation procedure improved service scores by 1.8 points (approximately 3.5%), with effects becoming clear 3-4 weeks after implementation. The change is statistically significant and represents a meaningful system-wide improvement.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Positive Impact on Service Quality
- Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by **1.82 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure
- This effect is **highly statistically significant** (p < 0.001)
- The 95% confidence interval ranges from **1.26 to 2.38 points**, indicating we can be confident the true effect is positive

### 2. Effects Built Over Time
- **Weeks 1-3 after switch:** Minimal visible impact (branches were likely adjusting)
- **Weeks 4-8 after switch:** Clear, sustained improvements of 2.0+ points
- This pattern suggests staff needed a few weeks to become proficient with the new procedures before benefits materialized

### 3. Strong Research Design
- **425 branches** switched to the new procedure; **284 branches** continued with the old method (control group)
- Before the switch, both groups had nearly identical service scores (~50.1 points), confirming they were comparable
- Results hold even after accounting for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume

### 4. Practical Significance
- The 1.8-point improvement represents about **0.18 standard deviations**—a modest but meaningful effect
- Given that the average branch scores around 52 points, this is a **3.5% improvement**
- Applied across 425 branches, this represents substantial system-wide gains in service quality

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Complete the rollout:** Consider extending the new procedure to the remaining 284 branches, given the demonstrated benefits

2. **Monitor implementation quality:** The gradual emergence of effects suggests proper training and adaptation time matters. Ensure branches receive adequate support during transition

3. **Track long-term effects:** Continue monitoring to confirm benefits persist beyond the initial 8-week post-switch period

4. **Investigate mechanisms:** Consider studying *why* the new procedure works (e.g., faster item location, better stock availability, improved customer experience) to refine future operational changes

5. **Cost-benefit analysis:** If the new procedure has associated costs (training, labor, materials), compare them against the value of a 1.8-point service score improvement to assess ROI

---

## **Technical Notes**

- **Method:** Difference-in-differences regression with branch and week fixed effects
- **Data:** 12,053 weekly observations from 709 branches (weeks 6-22)
- **Switch point:** Week 15
- **Standard errors:** Clustered at the branch level to account for repeated observations
