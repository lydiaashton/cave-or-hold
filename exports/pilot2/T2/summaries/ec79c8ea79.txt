# **Library Cooperative Branch Procedure Change: Impact Analysis**

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure **improved service scores by approximately 1.9 points** (about 4% relative to baseline). This effect is statistically significant and suggests the procedural change achieved its intended goal of enhancing branch service quality.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in switching branches against non-switching branches while controlling for branch-specific characteristics

### **Results**

| Group | Before Switch (Weeks 6–14) | After Switch (Weeks 15–22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------------|---------------------------|--------|
| Switching branches | 53.45 | 53.96 | +0.50 |
| Non-switching branches | 49.03 | 47.84 | −1.19 |
| **Net effect (DiD)** | | | **+1.69** |

After accounting for branch-specific factors and other weekly measures (staffing and visit levels), the estimated improvement is **+1.86 points** (95% confidence interval: 0.03 to 3.68; p=0.046).

### **Supporting Evidence**
- **Parallel trends validated**: Before the switch, both groups showed similar week-to-week trends with no systematic divergence (p=0.75), supporting the validity of the comparison
- **Control group declined**: Non-switching branches saw service scores drop by 1.2 points after week 15, while switching branches maintained and slightly improved their scores
- **Staffing matters**: Higher staffing levels were associated with better service scores, but the procedure effect remains after controlling for this

## **Next Steps**

1. **Consider broader rollout**: Given the positive and statistically significant effect, evaluate expanding the new procedure to remaining branches

2. **Monitor sustained impact**: Continue tracking service scores to confirm the improvement persists beyond the initial 8-week post-switch period

3. **Investigate mechanisms**: Explore why the new procedure worked—was it faster retrieval, better organization, or improved staff workflow? This could help optimize implementation

4. **Cost-benefit analysis**: Weigh the 1.9-point service score gain against any additional costs or training requirements of the new procedure

5. **Share best practices**: Branches with the strongest improvements under the new system may offer insights for training and implementation at other locations
