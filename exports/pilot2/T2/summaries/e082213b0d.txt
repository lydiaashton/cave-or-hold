# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

## **Subject: Impact of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure on Service Scores**

---

### **BOTTOM LINE**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure is associated with a **statistically significant increase of approximately 2 points** in service scores (95% confidence interval: 1.4 to 2.6 points). This represents roughly a **4% improvement** relative to the average branch score of 51. However, we note an important caveat: branches that adopted the new procedure were already showing stronger score improvements before the switch, which means the true causal effect may be somewhat smaller than our estimate.

---

### **KEY FINDINGS**

**1. Measured Treatment Effect**
- Branches using the new procedure showed service scores **1.98 points higher** than expected based on control branch trends
- This effect is **highly statistically significant** (p < 0.001)
- The 95% confidence interval ranges from **1.4 to 2.6 points**, indicating we can be confident the effect is positive and meaningful

**2. Adoption and Scope**
- **425 of 709 branches (60%)** switched to the new procedure
- Analysis covers **17 weeks** of data (weeks 6–22), with the switch implemented in week 15
- Approximately **8,500 branch-week observations** in the dataset

**3. Score Patterns**
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Treatment (new procedure) | 50.2 | 53.8 | +3.6 |
| Control (old procedure) | 49.3 | 51.0 | +1.7 |
| **Difference** | | | **+2.0** |

**4. Important Caveat: Pre-Existing Trends**
- The treatment group branches were already outperforming control branches *before* the switch
- The gap between groups grew from near-zero in week 6 to about 2 points by week 14
- This suggests branches selected for the new procedure may have been on a stronger improvement trajectory regardless
- **The true causal effect of the procedure change is likely smaller than 2 points**, though still positive

**5. Supporting Factors**
- Staffing and visit levels were similar between groups (no significant baseline differences)
- Both staffing and visits positively correlate with service scores, but controlling for these does not change the main result

---

### **NEXT STEPS**

1. **Continue Monitoring**: Track service scores for another 8–12 weeks to determine if the effect persists, grows, or diminishes over time

2. **Investigate Selection Bias**: Analyze why certain branches were chosen for the new procedure to better understand the pre-trend divergence

3. **Consider Phased Rollout Evaluation**: For future procedure changes, use randomized assignment where feasible to enable cleaner causal inference

4. **Cost-Benefit Analysis**: Quantify the operational costs of the new procedure against the ~4% service score improvement to assess ROI

5. **Branch-Level Heterogeneity**: Examine whether the effect varies by branch size, location, or other characteristics to identify where the procedure works best

---

### **ANALYTICAL APPROACH**

This analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** design, comparing the change in service scores for branches that adopted the new procedure against branches that continued with the old procedure. This method controls for:
- Time-invariant differences between branches
- Common time trends affecting all branches

Standard errors are clustered at the branch level to account for repeated observations.
