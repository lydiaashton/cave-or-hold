# **Library Cooperative Branch Procedure Change: Impact Analysis**

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure shows a **positive but statistically inconclusive effect** on service scores. Branches that switched to the new procedure improved by approximately **2.4 points** (about 5% of the average score) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. However, due to limited data, we cannot rule out that this improvement occurred by chance. The analysis does not provide strong enough evidence to confirm the expected improvement, but it also does not suggest the change was harmful.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Estimated Impact**
- **Treatment effect:** +2.4 service score points (95% confidence interval: -0.8 to +5.5)
- **Statistical significance:** Not significant at conventional levels (p = 0.15)
- **Practical significance:** Represents approximately 5% improvement relative to the average score of 50

### **2. What the Data Shows**
| Metric | Treatment Branches (Switched) | Control Branches (No Switch) |
|--------|------------------------------|------------------------------|
| Pre-switch average score | 50.1 | 50.9 |
| Post-switch average score | 50.9 | 49.3 |
| **Change** | **+0.8** | **-1.6** |
| % of branches with improvement | 38% | 12% |

Treatment branches were **3 times more likely** to show improvement than control branches.

### **3. Study Limitations**
- **Low statistical power:** With only 34 branches (20 treatment, 14 control), the study has just 9.5% power to detect the observed effect. We would need approximately 100+ branches to confidently detect a 2-3 point effect.
- **One pre-trend concern:** Treatment and control branches showed a statistically significant difference in one pre-period week (week 8), though trends were otherwise parallel.
- **Short observation window:** Only 8 weeks of post-switch data limits our ability to detect longer-term effects.

### **4. Visual Evidence**

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended Actions**

1. **Continue the new procedure** — There is no evidence of harm, and the point estimate suggests modest benefit.

2. **Extend the observation period** — Collect at least 6-12 additional months of data to improve statistical power and assess whether effects persist or grow over time.

3. **Consider expanding the rollout** — If operationally feasible, switching remaining branches would:
   - Eliminate the control group limitation
   - Allow before/after comparison with more data
   - Provide clearer evidence of impact

4. **Investigate branch-level variation** — Some treatment branches showed substantial gains (up to +7.7 points) while others declined. Understanding what distinguishes high-performing switchers could inform implementation support.

5. **Monitor operational metrics** — Track staffing efficiency and visit patterns alongside service scores to capture full impact of the procedure change.

- **Method:** Difference-in-differences regression with branch fixed effects and cluster-robust standard errors
- **Data:** 34 branches × 17 weeks (weeks 6-22), 578 observations

- **Covariates controlled:** Staffing index, visits index, branch-specific characteristics, week effects
