# **Board Summary: Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

The new rotation and shelving procedure **significantly improved service scores**. Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **1.8 points** (about **3.8% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and robust after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. **Measurable Positive Impact**
- **Treatment effect**: +1.82 points on the service score index (95% confidence interval: 1.27 to 2.38)
- **Statistical significance**: p < 0.001 (highly significant)
- **Relative improvement**: Approximately 3.8% above baseline performance

### 2. **Study Design & Data**
- **709 branches** total: 425 adopted the new procedure, 284 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22), with the switch implemented in week 15
- **12,053 branch-week observations** analyzed
- Analysis controlled for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume

### 3. **Performance Comparison**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| New Procedure (425 branches) | 49.05 | 51.10 | **+2.05** |
| Old Procedure (284 branches) | 47.99 | 48.28 | +0.29 |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | | | **+1.76** |

### 4. **Supporting Factors**
- Both staffing levels and visitor volume positively correlate with service scores (as expected)
- The effect holds even after accounting for these factors, suggesting the procedure change itself drove the improvement

### 5. **Important Caveat**
Pre-switch trend analysis indicates the treatment branches were already improving slightly faster than control branches before the procedure change. This suggests the true effect may be somewhat smaller than estimated, though the positive impact remains clear and significant.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Expand Implementation**: Consider rolling out the new procedure to remaining branches, given the demonstrated benefit.

2. **Monitor Long-Term Effects**: Continue tracking service scores to ensure the improvement is sustained beyond the initial 8-week post-switch period.

3. **Investigate Mechanisms**: Conduct qualitative research to understand *why* the new procedure works (e.g., staff interviews, patron feedback) to optimize implementation.

4. **Cost-Benefit Analysis**: Evaluate whether the service score gains justify any additional costs or training associated with the new procedure.

5. **Address Pre-Existing Differences**: Future evaluations should account for branch characteristics that may influence both adoption decisions and performance trends.
