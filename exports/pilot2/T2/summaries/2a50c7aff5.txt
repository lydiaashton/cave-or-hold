# **Board Summary: Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure is associated with a **statistically significant improvement of approximately 1.9 points** in service scores (on a scale where the average branch scores about 50). This represents roughly a **4% improvement** relative to baseline performance. The effect is robust and highly significant (p < 0.001).

## **Key Findings**

**Study Design**
- Analyzed 709 branches over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- 425 branches (60%) adopted the new procedure starting week 15; 284 branches (40%) continued with the old procedure
- Used a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in switched branches against non-switched branches before and after the transition

**Results**
| Group | Pre-Switch Average | Post-Switch Average | Change |
|-------|-------------------|---------------------|--------|
| Switched Branches | 50.9 | 51.0 | +0.1 |
| Non-Switched Branches | 50.4 | 48.5 | -1.9 |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | | | **+1.9** |

- **Effect size:** +1.94 points (95% confidence interval: 1.37 to 2.52)
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001
- **Practical significance:** Approximately 0.2 standard deviations, or 4% above baseline

**Important Context**
- The improvement appears to come from the new procedure *preventing a decline* rather than driving absolute gains. Non-switched branches experienced a notable drop in service scores after week 14, while switched branches maintained their performance levels.
- **Caveat:** Branches that switched showed slightly higher service scores than non-switched branches in the weeks immediately before the transition (weeks 9–14). This suggests branches selected for the new procedure may have already been on a more positive trajectory. While our analysis controls for baseline differences, this pre-existing trend means the true causal effect could be somewhat smaller than estimated.

## **Next Steps**

1. **Continue monitoring** – The post-switch period covers only 8 weeks. Track whether the effect persists, grows, or diminishes over time.

2. **Investigate selection factors** – Examine why certain branches were chosen for the new procedure. Understanding whether higher-performing branches were preferentially selected will help interpret the results.

3. **Consider phased rollout** – If resources allow, consider extending the new procedure to remaining branches, particularly given that non-switched branches showed declining scores.

4. **Examine mechanisms** – Analyze whether the effect varies by branch characteristics (size, location, staffing levels) to identify where the new procedure works best.

5. **Cost-benefit analysis** – Pair these service score gains with implementation costs to assess the overall return on investment.

---

*Analysis based on weekly branch-level data from weeks 6–22. Standard errors clustered at the branch level to account for repeated observations.*
