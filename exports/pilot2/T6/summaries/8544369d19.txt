## **Bottom Line**

**The data is inconclusive.** While branches that adopted the new procedure show higher service scores after the switch, we cannot confidently attribute this improvement to the procedure itself. The treatment and control groups were on different trajectories before the switch, which prevents us from isolating the causal effect. We recommend additional data collection before making expansion decisions.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **What the Data Shows**
- Branches that switched to the new procedure have **higher average service scores** than those that kept the old procedure (both before and after the switch)
- The simple comparison suggests a **~1.8 point improvement** associated with the new procedure
- However, this estimate **cannot be interpreted as causal**

### **Why We Can't Draw Firm Conclusions**

| Issue | Finding | Implication |
|-------|---------|-------------|
| **Pre-trend violation** | 6 of 8 pre-switch weeks show significant differences between groups | Treatment and control branches were already different before the procedure changed |
| **Post-treatment pattern** | Only 1 of 7 post-switch weeks shows significant effect | No clear immediate impact; effect only emerges in final week |
| **Both groups improved** | Control group scores also rose after week 15 | External factors (seasonal, system-wide) may explain the improvement |

### **Event Study Visualization**
The attached event study plot shows that treatment and control groups diverged before week 15, and the post-switch effect only becomes clear in week 22.

## **Next Steps**

1. **Do not expand the procedure** to remaining branches until we have clearer evidence of its effectiveness.

2. **Collect additional data** - extend the observation period to 6-12 months to see if effects stabilize.

3. **Consider a randomized rollout** for future procedure changes to ensure treatment and control groups are comparable.

4. **Investigate confounding factors** - what else changed around week 15 that might explain the score increases across all branches?

5. **Conduct branch-level qualitative interviews** to understand whether staff perceive the new procedure as beneficial.
