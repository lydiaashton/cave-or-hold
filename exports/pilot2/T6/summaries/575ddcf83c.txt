# **BOARD MEMORANDUM**

## **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** December 2025

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence is inconclusive.** The new procedure is associated with a **positive point estimate (+2.1 service score points)**, but this effect is **not statistically distinguishable from zero** given our sample size and variability. The data are consistent with three possibilities:

1. No meaningful effect
2. A modest improvement (up to ~5 points)
3. A small negative effect (down to ~1 point)

**Recommendation:** Continue the pilot and collect more data before making a systemwide rollout decision. If proceeding, treat it as an ongoing evaluation rather than a confirmed improvement.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Primary Result: Not Statistically Significant

| Statistic | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Point Estimate | +2.1 points |
| 95% Confidence Interval | -1.0 to +5.3 points |
| P-value | 0.19 |
| Statistical Significance | **No** |

The confidence interval includes zero, meaning we cannot confidently say the procedure had any effect—positive or negative.

### 2. Study Limitations

- **34 branches** over **17 weeks** provides limited statistical power
- The minimum detectable effect with this design is approximately **±3.2 points**
- Our observed effect (2.1 points) falls below this threshold
- **More time or more branches would be needed** to detect smaller effects reliably

### 3. Suggestive Patterns (Not Confirmatory)

| Observation | Interpretation |
|-------------|----------------|
| Late-period scores (weeks 19-22) showed larger gains | Could indicate a learning curve—or random variation |
| Point estimate is positive | Consistent with expected improvement, but not proven |
| Pre-switch trends were parallel | Validates our comparison method |

### 4. Staffing Correlation

Branches that switched showed slightly higher staffing levels post-switch (p=0.05). This confounds interpretation: we cannot tell whether any score changes reflect the procedure itself or staffing differences.

---

## **Recommendations**

### **Do Not Yet Declare Success**
The data do not support a definitive conclusion that the new procedure improves service scores. Declaring victory now risks implementing a change that may not deliver expected benefits.

### **Extend the Evaluation Period**
- Continue tracking all branches for **8-12 additional weeks**
- This would roughly double the post-switch observations and improve statistical power
- Re-analyze with the extended dataset before making rollout decisions

### **If Proceeding with Rollout, Do So Cautiously**
- Frame it as a **continued pilot with evaluation**, not a proven improvement
- Set clear success metrics and decision points (e.g., "Reassess at week 30")
- Be prepared to modify or reverse the procedure if later data show no benefit

### **Control for Staffing**
- Document whether the new procedure requires additional staff time
- If so, factor staffing costs into the cost-benefit analysis
- Consider whether score gains (if any) justify the staffing investment

---

## **What This Means for the Board**

Your expectation that the procedure would improve scores was reasonable. The **direction** of the effect is positive, which is encouraging. However, the **magnitude and certainty** of that effect remain unclear. 

This is not evidence that the procedure *doesn't work*—it's evidence that we **don't yet have enough data to know**. Making a systemwide decision now would be premature.

---

## **Next Decision Point**

**Recommended:** Reconvene in 8-12 weeks with extended data to make a fully informed rollout decision.

**Alternative:** If operational pressures require a decision now, proceed with caution and commit to rigorous ongoing evaluation with predefined success criteria.
