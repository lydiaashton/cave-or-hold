You're right to question this. The data reveals a significant problem with my analysis.

**The key issue:** The treatment and control branches were on **different trajectories before the switch even happened**. The treatment group was already improving relative to the control group during the pre-period. This violates the core assumption of difference-in-differences analysis.

**What the numbers show:**
- Pre-treatment, the treatment group was declining **more slowly** than control (-0.25 vs -0.50 points/week)
- The gap between groups was already **widening before week 15**
- From week 14 to 15 specifically, the gap only increased by **0.85 points**—not the 2.08 points the full model suggests
- Some (possibly much) of the observed "effect" may simply be continuation of pre-existing trends

# **Board Memorandum (Revised)**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 14, 2026  
**Subject:** Impact of New Branch Rotation & Shelving Procedure on Service Scores

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Branches that adopted the new procedure showed higher service scores than those that did not, but we cannot confidently attribute this improvement to the procedure itself.** The analysis is compromised because the two groups were on different trajectories before the switch. **We recommend a more rigorous evaluation before proceeding with full rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### What the Data Shows
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Raw DiD estimate | +2.08 points |
| **Reliability of estimate** | **Low—pre-trends differ** |
| Treatment branches | 425 (60%) |
| Control branches | 284 (40%) |

### The Problem: Groups Were Not Comparable
| Period | Treatment Group | Control Group | Gap |
|--------|-----------------|---------------|-----|
| **Pre-switch trend** | −0.25 pts/week | −0.50 pts/week | Widening |
| **Week 14 (last before)** | 50.31 | 48.83 | +1.47 |
| **Week 15 (first after)** | 50.83 | 48.50 | +2.33 |
| **Gap change at switch** | | | **+0.85** |

The gap increased by only 0.85 points at the switch point, but the overall model estimates 2.08 points. This discrepancy suggests pre-existing differences are inflating the estimated effect.

---

## **Recommendations**

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| **1** | **Pause full rollout** | Cannot confirm the procedure caused the improvement |
| **2** | **Conduct randomized trial** | Randomly assign remaining branches to treatment/control to ensure comparability |
| **3** | **Extend observation period** | Track both groups for 6–12 more months to see if patterns persist |
| **4** | **Investigate selection bias** | Determine why certain branches were chosen to switch first; were they already higher-performing? |
| **5** | **Gather qualitative feedback** | Survey branch staff about whether the new procedure feels like an improvement |

---

## **What We Can and Cannot Conclude**

| We CAN say | We CANNOT say |
|------------|---------------|
| Switched branches outperformed non-switched branches after week 15 | The new procedure **caused** the improvement |
| The association is statistically significant | The effect size is 2.08 points |
| There is suggestive evidence of benefit | Full rollout is justified based on this analysis alone |

---

## **Next Steps for Rigorous Evaluation**

1. **Randomized controlled trial**: Randomly assign remaining 284 branches to switch or not switch
2. **Longer follow-up**: Track outcomes for at least 6 months post-switch
3. **Pre-registration**: Document the analysis plan before collecting more data
4. **Mechanism analysis**: Identify *why* the procedure might work (faster shelving? better organization?)
