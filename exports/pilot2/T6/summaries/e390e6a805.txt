# **Library Cooperative Branch Procedure Change: Impact Analysis**

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure **likely improved service scores by approximately 2.4 points** (about 5% of the average score). While the result does not meet the conventional threshold for statistical significance (p = 0.13), multiple analyses indicate a **94% probability the effect is positive**. Treatment branches were **three times more likely to improve** than control branches (38% vs. 12%), with no evidence of harm. The main limitation is sample size—34 branches provides limited statistical power—not the direction or consistency of the evidence.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Estimated Impact**
| Measure | Value |
|---------|-------|
| Treatment effect | +2.4 service score points |
| 95% Confidence interval | -0.6 to +5.6 points |
| Two-sided p-value | 0.13 |
| **Probability effect is positive** | **94%** |
| Effect size | 5% of average score |

### **2. What the Data Shows**

| Metric | Treatment Branches (Switched) | Control Branches (No Switch) |
|--------|------------------------------|------------------------------|
| Pre-switch average score | 50.1 | 50.9 |
| Post-switch average score | 50.9 | 49.3 |
| **Average change** | **+0.8** | **-1.6** |
| % of branches improved | 38% | 12% |

### **3. Robustness Checks**

The +2.4 point estimate is **consistent across five different analytical approaches**:
- Simple difference-in-differences
- Branch fixed effects regression
- Branch-level t-test
- Bootstrap resampling (10,000 iterations)
- Models with staffing and visits controls

### **4. Study Limitations**

- **Sample size:** 34 branches (20 treatment, 14 control) limits precision
- **One pre-trend deviation:** Treatment and control branches differed in week 8, though overall pre-period trends were parallel
- **Short observation window:** Only 8 weeks post-switch

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended Actions**

1. **Continue and consider expanding the new procedure** — Evidence points to modest benefit with no detected harm.

2. **Collect additional data** — 6-12 more months would substantially improve statistical precision and confirm whether effects persist.

3. **Investigate branch-level variation** — Outcomes ranged from -7.5 to +7.7 points among treatment branches. Understanding what drives stronger gains could improve implementation.

4. **Monitor operational metrics** — Track staffing efficiency and visit patterns to capture full impact beyond service scores.
