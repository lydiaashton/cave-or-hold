# **Memorandum to the Board**

## **Subject: Impact of New Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

---

### **Bottom Line**

**The evidence is suggestive but inconclusive.** Branches that switched to the new procedure showed an average service score improvement of about **2.2 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. However, this difference is not statistically significant when properly accounting for the data structure (p = 0.17). The procedure may help, but we cannot rule out that the observed improvement was due to chance.

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. Study Design**
- Analyzed 34 branches over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- 20 branches adopted the new procedure starting week 15; 14 branches continued with the old procedure
- Used a difference-in-differences approach to isolate the procedure's effect

**2. Main Result**
- Estimated effect: **+2.2 points** on the service score index
- 95% confidence interval: **-0.9 to +5.2 points**
- P-value: **0.17** (not statistically significant at conventional thresholds)
- The confidence interval includes both modest harm and meaningful benefit

**3. Interpretation**
- The point estimate suggests a positive effect (roughly 4% improvement)
- However, with only 8 weeks of post-switch data and 34 branches, the study lacks precision
- We cannot confidently distinguish a real effect from random variation

**4. Supporting Evidence**
- Pre-switch trends were similar between groups, supporting the validity of the comparison
- Both staffing levels and visit volumes positively correlate with service scores

---

### **Next Steps**

1. **Continue monitoring** – Collect more weeks of data to increase statistical power and narrow the confidence interval

2. **Consider a phased rollout** – Given the suggestive (though inconclusive) positive effect, expanding the procedure to additional branches could be reasonable if costs are low

3. **Gather qualitative feedback** – Survey staff at switching branches about perceived benefits or challenges with the new procedure

4. **Re-analyze in 3–6 months** – With more post-switch data, we can provide a more definitive answer

5. **Track implementation fidelity** – Ensure branches are actually following the new procedure as designed

---

*Analysis prepared using weekly branch data from weeks 6–22. Standard errors clustered by branch to account for within-branch correlation. The inconclusive result reflects limited statistical power, not evidence of no effect.*
