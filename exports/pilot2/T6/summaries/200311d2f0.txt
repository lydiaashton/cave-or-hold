**SUBJECT:** Impact of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure on Service Scores

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**We cannot confidently conclude the new procedure improved service scores.** While branches using the new procedure showed higher scores after the switch, these same branches were already improving faster than others *before* the change. After accounting for this pre-existing trend, there is **no clear evidence** the procedure itself caused additional improvement. The observed ~2 point difference likely reflects which branches were selected for the new procedure, not the procedure's effect.

**Recommendation:** Pause broader rollout until we can conduct a more rigorous evaluation (e.g., randomized assignment).

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### 1. The Core Problem: Violated Assumption

Standard impact evaluation requires that treatment and control groups were on **parallel trajectories** before the intervention. Our data shows this assumption is **violated**:

| Period | Treatment-Control Gap |
|--------|----------------------|
| Week 6 | -0.17 points |
| Week 14 (last pre-switch) | +1.97 points |
| **Pre-trend slope** | **+0.29 points/week** |

The gap grew by **2.14 points** during the 9 weeks *before* the switch was implemented.

### 2. What the Pre-Trend Predicts

If the pre-switch trend continued naturally (without any procedure effect):

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Expected post-switch gap | 3.31 points |
| Actual post-switch gap | 2.80 points |
| **Difference** | **-0.50 points** |

The actual gap is **lower** than what the pre-trend predicts, suggesting the new procedure may have had **no positive effect** (and possibly a slight negative effect).

### 3. What We Can Say

| Finding | Confidence |
|---------|------------|
| Branches using the new procedure have higher scores | **Certain** |
| These branches were already improving faster before the switch | **Certain** |
| The procedure itself caused improvement | **Uncertain** |
| The procedure had any effect at all | **Uncertain** |

### 4. Why This Matters

The branches selected for the new procedure appear to have been:
- Better-performing or faster-improving branches to begin with
- Possibly managed by stronger branch managers
- Possibly in more favorable locations or demographics

This **selection bias** makes it impossible to isolate the procedure's effect from the branches' underlying characteristics.

## **ANALYTICAL APPROACH & LIMITATIONS**

**Method:** Difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors.

**Critical Limitation:** The parallel trends assumption is violated. The treatment group was improving 0.29 points/week faster than control *before* the intervention. This makes causal inference unreliable.

**What would have helped:**
- Random assignment of branches to new vs. old procedure
- A phased/stepped-wedge rollout with random timing
- More pre-intervention data to better establish baseline trends

---

## **CONCLUSION**

The data does **not support** the conclusion that the new stock rotation procedure improved service scores. The observed improvement is better explained by pre-existing differences between branches that were selected for the new procedure versus those that were not.

We recommend pausing further rollout and investing in a more rigorous evaluation design before making cooperative-wide implementation decisions.
