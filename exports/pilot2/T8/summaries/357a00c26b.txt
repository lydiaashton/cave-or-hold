# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure is associated with significantly higher service scores.** Our best estimate is that branches switching to the new procedure gained **1.8 points** on the service score (95% CI: 1.3 to 2.4), representing roughly a **3.7% improvement**. This result is highly statistically significant (p < 0.001).

**Caveat:** The switched branches showed slightly stronger improvement trends even before the change, which means the true causal effect could be modestly smaller than estimated. However, the magnitude and consistency of the post-switch improvement strongly suggest the new procedure contributed meaningfully to better service scores.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Clear Positive Effect
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Treatment effect | +1.8 service score points |
| 95% Confidence interval | +1.3 to +2.4 points |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 |
| Percentage improvement | 3.7% |

### 2. Broad-Based Improvement
- **71.8% of switched branches** (305 out of 425) showed score improvements after adopting the new procedure
- Average improvement for switched branches: **+2.05 points**
- Average change for non-switched branches: **+0.28 points** (minimal change)

### 3. Effect Persists Over Time
The improvement was sustained throughout the 8-week post-switch period (weeks 15-22), with switched branches consistently outperforming non-switched branches by 2-4 points each week.

### 4. Robust Results
The finding holds across multiple analytical approaches, including controls for staffing levels, visit volumes, and branch-specific characteristics.

---

## **Methodology Note**

This analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing 425 branches that switched to the new procedure against 284 branches that kept the old procedure. By examining changes in both groups before and after week 15 (when the switch occurred), we isolate the effect of the procedure change from other factors that might affect all branches. The analysis controls for staffing levels, visit volumes, and branch-specific characteristics.

---

## **Final Verdict**

**Proceed with system-wide rollout, but monitor closely.**

The evidence supports expanding the new procedure to all remaining branches. The effect is statistically robust, practically meaningful, and benefits the majority of branches that adopted it. However, given the pre-existing trend differential, we recommend:

1. **Phased implementation** — Roll out in batches to allow for course correction if needed
2. **Enhanced monitoring** — Track service scores for 12+ weeks post-implementation at each new branch
3. **Implementation support** — Provide additional training to the ~28% of branches that did not improve initially

The risk of proceeding appears low relative to the demonstrated upside.
