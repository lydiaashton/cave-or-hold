# **Board Memorandum (Final)**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 14, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**We cannot confidently conclude the new procedure caused the improvement.** While branches using the new procedure did achieve higher service scores (+1.8 points), these branches were already improving faster than others *before* the switch. The data is consistent with either a modest positive effect or no effect at all. **More evidence is needed before system-wide rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### What the Data Shows
| Metric | Result |
|--------|--------|
| Raw difference-in-differences | **+1.8 points** |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 |
| **BUT: Pre-existing trend** | **+0.28 pts/week** (p < 0.001) |
| **Implied causal effect** | **Uncertain (possibly zero)** |

### Why This Matters
The standard analysis method (difference-in-differences) assumes treatment and control branches would have followed similar trends without the intervention. **This assumption is not met.** Treatment branches were already on a stronger upward trajectory before adopting the new procedure.

### Weekly Service Scores

| Week | Control | Treatment | Gap |
|------|---------|-----------|-----|
| 6-14 (Pre) | 47.99 | 49.05 | 1.06 |
| 15-22 (Post) | 48.28 | 51.10 | 2.82 |
| **Change in Gap** | | | **+1.76** |

The gap widened, but treatment branches were already pulling ahead before week 15.

---

## **Recommendations**

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| **1** | **Pause system-wide rollout** | Causal effect is unproven |
| **2** | **Extend evaluation period** | Collect more post-switch data to see if trends diverge further |
| **3** | **Investigate selection bias** | Understand why certain branches adopted the new procedure—were they already higher-performing? |
| **4** | **Consider randomized rollout** | For future changes, randomly assign branches to ensure valid comparison |
| **5** | **Gather qualitative evidence** | Interview staff at high-performing branches to understand what's driving scores |

---

## **Final Verdict**

> **DO NOT PROCEED WITH SYSTEM-WIDE ROLLOUT AT THIS TIME.**
>
> The evidence is **inconclusive**. The apparent improvement cannot be reliably attributed to the new procedure rather than pre-existing differences between branches. Approving full implementation now would risk investing resources in a change that may deliver no benefit.
>
> **Path forward:** Either (a) collect more data and re-evaluate, or (b) implement a randomized controlled rollout for the remaining 284 branches to generate defensible evidence before committing further resources.

---

## **Questions for Discussion**

1. What criteria determined which branches adopted the new procedure first?
2. Should we randomize the remaining rollouts to enable cleaner evaluation?
3. What other metrics (beyond service score) might capture the procedure's impact?
