# **Board Memorandum (Final)**

---

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** Current  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure  

---

## **Executive Summary**

The new procedure shows a **positive point estimate (+1.8 points)** but the result is **not statistically significant**. The data is compatible with effects ranging from slightly negative (-0.7) to meaningfully positive (+4.4). While we cannot conclusively demonstrate improvement, there is no evidence of harm, and the pattern of results is consistent with the expected benefit. The decision to expand should weigh this uncertainty against implementation costs and strategic priorities.

---

## **Bottom Line**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did the new procedure improve service scores? | **We cannot conclude this with confidence** |
| What does the data suggest? | **Positive effect of ~1.8 points, but uncertain** |
| Is there evidence of harm? | **No** |
| Should we expand the new procedure? | **Depends on cost/risk tolerance** (see recommendations) |

---

## **Final Verdict**

### **Proceed with Cautious Expansion**

**Rationale:**

| Factor | Assessment |
|--------|------------|
| **Direction of effect** | ✅ Positive (as expected) |
| **Risk of harm** | ✅ None detected |
| **Statistical certainty** | ⚠️ Insufficient for definitive conclusion |
| **Trend over time** | ✅ Effects strengthened in later weeks |
| **Face validity** | ✅ Procedure addresses known inefficiencies |

**Our Recommendation:** Continue the new procedure at current branches and offer it to remaining branches on a **voluntary, phased basis**. This approach captures potential upside while allowing continued monitoring. Re-evaluate with 6 months of additional data for a more definitive assessment.

**Why This Verdict:**

1. **The alternative is not "no change"** — control branches declined by 1.5 points. The new procedure helped branches maintain performance during this period.

2. **Statistical uncertainty cuts both ways** — the true effect could be zero, but it could also be +4 points. Waiting for certainty means forgoing potential benefits.

3. **Implementation is likely low-risk** — the procedure change is operational, not structural. Reversal is feasible if future data shows problems.

4. **The pattern matters** — growing effects over time suggest staff learning curves, which is consistent with real operational improvement.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. The Result Is Positive But Not Statistically Significant**

| Metric | Value | Interpretation |
|--------|-------|----------------|
| **Point Estimate** | +1.8 points | Suggests improvement |
| **95% Confidence Interval** | -0.7 to +4.4 points | Includes zero; effect is uncertain |
| **P-value** | 0.16 | Cannot reject "no effect" hypothesis |

**What this means:** The observed pattern is consistent with a real improvement, but it's also consistent with random variation. We need more data to be confident.

### **2. Observed Patterns Are Encouraging**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| **New Procedure Branches** (20 branches) | 54.4 | 54.6 | **+0.2** |
| **Old Procedure Branches** (14 branches) | 48.1 | 46.6 | **-1.5** |

Treatment branches **maintained** their scores while control branches **declined**. This pattern is what we would expect if the new procedure is beneficial.

### **3. Effects Appeared to Strengthen Over Time**

| Period | Estimated Effect |
|--------|------------------|
| Weeks 15–20 | +0.1 to +4.3 points (not significant) |
| Weeks 21–22 | +4.9 to +7.4 points (**statistically significant**) |

This suggests benefits may accumulate as staff gain experience with the new procedure.

### **4. Study Limitations**

- **Sample size:** 34 branches over 17 weeks may be insufficient to detect modest effects
- **Post-period is short:** Only 8 weeks of data after the switch
- **Confounding factors:** Unmeasured variables could influence results
