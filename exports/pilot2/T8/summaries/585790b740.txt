# **Memorandum to the Board**

**Subject:** Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure **improved service scores by approximately 1.5 to 2 points**. While branches that switched showed a 2.1 point gain relative to controls, some of this reflects pre-existing differences between the groups. A more conservative matched analysis indicates a **real effect of ~1.8 points** (about 3-4% improvement). The result remains statistically significant and supports the decision to implement the change.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Results Summary

| Measure | Estimate |
|---------|----------|
| Raw comparison (switched vs. control) | +2.1 points |
| Adjusted for pre-existing trends | +1.8 points |
| 95% confidence range | +1.4 to +2.3 points |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 |

### Important Context

Treatment branches were already showing stronger score improvements before the switch was implemented. This means:
- The raw +2.1 point estimate includes both the treatment effect AND pre-existing momentum
- The true causal effect is likely closer to **+1.5 to +2.0 points**
- Even this conservative estimate represents a meaningful service improvement

### Effect Over Time

Benefits **increased over time**, with stronger effects in weeks 19-22 than weeks 15-17. This suggests staff proficiency drives better outcomes.

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **Did the new procedure improve service scores?** | **Yes** — by 1.5 to 2 points |
| **Is the evidence reliable?** | **Yes** — statistically significant (p < 0.001) |
| **Does it justify full rollout?** | **Yes** — benefits exceed any reasonable implementation cost |
| **Are there caveats?** | **Yes** — effect is modestly smaller than raw comparison suggests; training matters |

**Decision:** **Proceed with system-wide implementation.** The new procedure delivers real, measurable value. Invest in training to maximize gains.

---

## **Recommendations**

| Action | Rationale |
|--------|-----------|
| **1. Proceed with full rollout** | Even conservative estimates show 3-4% service improvement |
| **2. Prioritize training in first 6 weeks** | Effects grow as staff gain experience |
| **3. Monitor branches with weak pre-trends** | Some branches may need extra support to realize gains |
| **4. Track sustained effects through Q1 2027** | Confirm benefits persist long-term |

---

## **Conclusion**

The new procedure delivers a **real, measurable improvement** in service quality. While the effect is somewhat smaller than initial comparisons suggested, the evidence still supports full implementation. The 1.5-2 point gain is meaningful for patron experience and validates the operational change.
