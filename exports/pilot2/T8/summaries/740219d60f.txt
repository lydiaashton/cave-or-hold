# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Office  
**DATE:** [Current Date]  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure shows a **modest positive effect** on service scores (+1.7 points, or 3.3% improvement), but the evidence is **not statistically conclusive**. There is a 92% probability the true effect is positive, yet we cannot rule out no effect at conventional confidence levels. The data suggest the procedure may be beneficial, but the study lacks sufficient power to confirm this with certainty.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Estimated Impact**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect | +1.7 points on service score index |
| Direction | Positive (as hypothesized) |
| Statistical significance | Not significant at p < 0.05 (p = 0.16) |
| One-sided p-value | 0.08 |
| 95% confidence interval | -0.6 to +4.0 points |
| Probability effect is positive | 92% |

### **2. Valid Comparison Between Groups**
- Treatment branches (new procedure): 20 branches
- Control branches (old procedure): 14 branches
- Pre-switch trends were parallel, confirming the groups are comparable
- No evidence that switching branches were already improving faster before the change

### **3. Study Limitations**
- **Sample size is small**: Only 34 branches limits our ability to detect modest effects
- **Minimum detectable effect**: Approximately 3.3 points with 80% confidence
- **Observed effect**: 1.7 points—below the detection threshold
- **Conclusion**: A real but small benefit likely exists that this analysis cannot confirm with high confidence

### **4. Stable Group Differences**
Branches that adopted the new procedure consistently score 4–5 points higher than control branches, but this gap existed *before* the switch and reflects inherent branch characteristics rather than the procedure itself.

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **Did the procedure improve service scores?** | Probably yes, but we cannot confirm with high confidence |
| **Should we conclude the procedure failed?** | **No** — the effect is in the expected direction with no evidence of harm |
| **Should we conclude the procedure succeeded?** | **Not yet** — evidence is suggestive but not definitive |
| **Recommended action** | **Continue with monitoring** — proceed while gathering more data |

**Rationale:**
1. The effect is positive and meaningful in magnitude (3.3% improvement)
2. There is no evidence of negative impact
3. The main limitation is statistical power, not effect direction
4. Operational benefits may exist beyond what service scores capture
5. Reversing the procedure would discard potential gains without evidence of harm

---

## **Recommendations**

| Priority | Action | Timeline |
|----------|--------|----------|
| **High** | Continue the procedure at switched branches | Immediate |
| **High** | Extend data collection to 6–12 months for follow-up analysis | Ongoing |
| **High** | Survey branch staff on workflow efficiency and implementation experience | 1–2 months |
| **Medium** | Analyze whether effects vary by branch size, location, or circulation volume | 2–3 months |
| **Medium** | Evaluate alternative outcome measures (hold fulfillment time, shelf accuracy, staff hours) | 3–6 months |

---

## **Decision Considerations**

The Board may wish to consider:

1. **Proceed with cautious optimism**: The weight of evidence leans toward a modest benefit. Continuing the rollout is reasonable while acknowledging uncertainty.

2. **Avoid premature conclusions**: Neither declaring success nor failure is warranted at this stage. More data will clarify the picture.

3. **Consider operational context**: If staff report positive experiences or efficiency gains, these qualitative factors may justify continuation even without statistical confirmation.

4. **Plan for follow-up**: Commit to a formal re-evaluation once 6–12 months of additional data are available.

---

## **Appendix: Analysis Summary**

- **Method**: Difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch)
- **Observation period**: Weeks 6–22 (17 weeks total; switch occurred at week 15)
- **Total observations**: 578 branch-week records
- **Statistical software**: Python with statsmodels package
