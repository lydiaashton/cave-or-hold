# MEMORANDUM

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Office  
**DATE:** November 14, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores

---

## Final Verdict

> **The analysis does not provide sufficient evidence to conclude the new procedure improved service scores.**
>
> While branches using the new procedure performed better than those that did not, we cannot determine whether this reflects the procedure's effectiveness or pre-existing differences between the groups. **Do not proceed with system-wide rollout based on this analysis alone.**

---

## Executive Summary

**The evidence is suggestive but not conclusive.** Branches that adopted the new procedure maintained their service scores while non-adopting branches experienced a decline. However, because branches were not randomly assigned to the new procedure and significant pre-existing differences exist between the two groups, we cannot definitively attribute this difference to the procedure itself.

**Recommendation:** Continue monitoring and gather more evidence before committing to a broader rollout.

---

## Bottom Line

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did service scores improve after the switch? | **Uncertain** |
| Is there a detectable difference between groups? | **Yes** (~1.75 points) |
| Can we attribute this to the new procedure? | **Not confidently** |
| Should we roll out to all branches now? | **No** |

---

## Key Findings

### 1. What the Data Shows

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|---------------|---------------|--------|
| **Branches with new procedure** (20 branches) | 54.37 | 54.62 | +0.25 |
| **Branches with old procedure** (14 branches) | 48.13 | 46.62 | −1.51 |

The 1.75-point difference is **statistically detectable** in our most rigorous model (p = 0.039), but **not significant** in simpler specifications (p = 0.309).

### 2. Critical Limitations

| Issue | Why It Matters |
|-------|---------------|
| **Pre-existing differences** | Treated branches scored 6.2 points (13%) higher than control branches *before* the switch. This suggests non-random assignment. |
| **Parallel trends violated** | Three pre-period weeks show statistically significant differences between groups, undermining the key assumption needed for causal inference. |
| **Effect driven by control decline** | Treated branches barely changed (+0.25). The "improvement" reflects that control branches got worse, not that treated branches got better. |
| **Borderline significance** | Results depend on model specification. Simple analysis shows no significant effect. |

### 3. What We Can and Cannot Say

| We CAN say | We CANNOT say |
|------------|---------------|
| Adopting branches held steady while others declined | The new procedure *caused* the difference |
| There is a 1.75-point association with adoption | The effect is robust across all analytical approaches |
| The pattern is consistent with a positive effect | We have definitive proof the procedure works |

---

## Recommendations

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| **1** | **Extend monitoring period** | More weeks of data will improve statistical power and help distinguish treatment effects from random variation |
| **2** | **Investigate control group decline** | Understand why non-adopting branches declined. Was this seasonal? Staffing-related? This affects interpretation. |
| **3** | **Document implementation details** | Capture what the new procedure actually entails to enable replication and identify which components matter |
| **4** | **Consider randomized rollout** | For future changes, randomly assign branches to treatment/control to enable cleaner causal inference |
| **5** | **Revisit rollout decision in 8-12 weeks** | With more data and better understanding of the control group decline, make a more informed decision |

---

## Technical Notes

- **Data:** 578 weekly observations from 34 branches (weeks 6–22, 2026)
- **Method:** Difference-in-differences with branch and week fixed effects
- **Key limitation:** Parallel trends assumption is violated (pre-period differences are statistically significant)
- **Interpretation:** Results are correlational, not causal
