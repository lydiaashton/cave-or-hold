# **Board Memorandum**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** [Current Date]  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Executive Summary**

Branches that adopted the new stock rotation and shelving procedure showed improved service scores compared to branches that did not. However, **branches that switched were already improving faster before the change**, which complicates attribution. The analysis provides **suggestive but not definitive evidence** that the procedure contributed to service score gains.

---

## **Bottom Line**

| Metric | Estimate |
|--------|----------|
| **Standard DiD Effect** | +1.98 points (p < 0.001) |
| **Trend-Adjusted Effect** | +0.5 to +1.0 points (uncertain) |
| **Statistical Confidence** | Limited by pre-trend violation |
| **Branches Affected** | 425 switched branches |

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Switched Branches Outperformed After Implementation**
- Post-switch gap averaged 2.80 points (switched ahead of control)
- Pre-switch gap averaged 0.83 points
- Standard difference-in-differences estimate: **+1.98 points**

### **2. Critical Limitation: Pre-Trends Were Not Parallel**
- Switched branches were already improving faster before the switch
- Gap was widening by **0.29 points per week** in the pre-period (p = 0.0001)
- If this trend continued, expected post-period gap would be ~2.28 points
- Actual gap was 2.80 points, suggesting **true treatment effect may be only ~0.5 points**

### **3. Unusual Week-15 Pattern**
- Event study shows a **large spike in week 15** (+11 points relative to reference)
- Subsequent weeks show much smaller effects (+0.8 to +8.4 points)
- This pattern is inconsistent with a stable treatment effect and warrants investigation

### **4. Analysis Scope**
- 709 branches total (425 switched, 284 control)
- 17 weeks of data (weeks 6-22)
- 12,053 branch-week observations

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Verdict |
|----------|---------|
| **Did switched branches improve?** | **Yes** – clear performance gain |
| **Did the new procedure cause it?** | **Uncertain** – pre-existing trends confound attribution |
| **Is the effect meaningful?** | **Possibly** – but likely smaller than initial estimates |
| **Should we proceed with rollout?** | **Yes, with monitoring** – low risk, potential benefit |

**Bottom Line:** The new procedure **has not been proven to cause the observed improvement**, but there is **no evidence of harm** and **some evidence of benefit**. Given the low implementation cost and positive (if uncertain) returns, proceeding with the rollout is reasonable—but with enhanced monitoring to confirm effects.

---

## **Recommendations**

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| **High** | Continue planned rollout | No evidence of harm; potential benefit |
| **High** | Extend data collection 8-12 weeks | Confirm effects persist beyond initial period |
| **High** | Investigate week-15 spike | Rule out measurement artifact or one-time factor |
| **Medium** | Randomize remaining branch rollout | Enable cleaner causal inference |
| **Medium** | Conduct branch manager interviews | Identify implementation success factors |
| **Low** | Re-estimate effect with extended data | Update board once more evidence is available |

---

## **Technical Appendix**

*Analysis employs difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch). The parallel trends assumption is violated (pre-period interaction p = 0.0001), meaning the standard DiD estimate likely overstates the true causal effect. Event study analysis reveals an anomalous week-15 spike that requires further investigation. Trend-adjusted estimates suggest the true effect may be 50-75% smaller than the headline DiD estimate.*
