# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure shows a **positive but not statistically significant** effect on service scores. Branches that switched to the new procedure improved by approximately **1.7 points** relative to branches that kept the old procedure. However, with our current data, we cannot rule out that this result occurred by chance (p = 0.16). The study was not large enough to reliably detect effects of this size.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in treated branches against changes in control branches

### **Results**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect of new procedure | **+1.69 points** |
| 95% Confidence interval | **-0.64 to +4.02 points** |
| p-value | **0.16** |
| Effect relative to average score | **+3.3%** |

### **What the Numbers Mean**
- Treated branches improved slightly (+0.5 points) after switching, while control branches declined (-1.2 points) during the same period
- The **1.7-point advantage** for treated branches is consistent with a beneficial effect, but the confidence interval includes zero, meaning we cannot be confident the effect is real
- If the effect is genuine, it represents a modest improvement of about one-sixth of a standard deviation

### **Validity Checks**
- **Parallel trends confirmed**: Before the switch, treated and control branches followed similar trajectories with no systematic differences in trends
- **No pre-trends detected**: Event study analysis shows no significant differences between groups in the weeks leading up to the switch, supporting the credibility of the research design

### **Study Limitations**
- **Limited statistical power**: With 34 branches and 8 post-switch weeks, the study could only reliably detect effects of **3.3 points or larger** (80% power)
- The observed 1.7-point effect would only be detected as statistically significant about **24% of the time** even if it were real
- Short post-implementation period (8 weeks) may not capture longer-term effects or adjustment periods

---

## **Final Verdict**

> **The analysis is inconclusive. We cannot confirm the new procedure improves service scores, but we also cannot rule out a meaningful benefit. The positive point estimate, combined with a sound research design and low statistical power, suggests the procedure should remain in place while additional data is collected. Do not expand system-wide yet; do not abandon the procedure either.**

| If follow-up shows | Recommended action |
