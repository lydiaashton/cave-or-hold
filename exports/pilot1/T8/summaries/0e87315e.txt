# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Library Cooperative Board  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** [Current Date]  
**SUBJECT:** Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores

---

## **Bottom Line**

Branches that adopted the new procedure showed **2.2-point higher service scores** than expected based on control branch trends (approximately 4.5% above baseline). However, with only 34 branches in the study, we cannot distinguish this effect from random variation with conventional statistical confidence (p = 0.17). The evidence is **suggestive but not conclusive**.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22 of the year)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure as a control group
- Analysis isolates the procedure's effect from other factors like seasonal trends and branch-specific characteristics

### **Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 48.4 | 51.5 | +3.1 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 48.0 | 53.2 | +5.3 |
| **Estimated Procedure Effect** | | | **+2.2** |

### **Statistical Confidence**
- **Point estimate:** +2.16 points on the service score index
- **95% confidence interval:** -0.9 to +5.2 points
- **Interpretation:** The true effect could reasonably range from a small decline to a meaningful 5-point gain

### **Validation**
- Pre-switch trends were similar between treatment and control branches, supporting the validity of the comparison
- Results account for weekly variations in staffing and visitor volume

---

## **Final Verdict**

**The data provides inconclusive evidence of a modest positive effect.**

We cannot recommend expanding or discontinuing the procedure based solely on this analysis. The point estimate favors the new procedure, but the study lacks sufficient statistical power to confirm the effect is real rather than due to chance. 

**Practical guidance:** If the new procedure has low implementation costs and no reported operational problems, continuing it is reasonable while we gather more evidence. If it requires significant resources, we recommend additional data collection before making a system-wide commitment.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Continue monitoring.** Additional weeks of data will increase our ability to detect a significant effect if one exists.

2. **Gather qualitative feedback.** Supplement scores with staff and patron input to understand how the procedure affects day-to-day operations.

3. **Assess operational benefits.** Even if score improvements are modest, evaluate whether efficiency gains or staff satisfaction justify continued implementation.

4. **Consider targeted rollout.** Analyze whether certain branch types (by size, location, or baseline performance) benefit more than others.
