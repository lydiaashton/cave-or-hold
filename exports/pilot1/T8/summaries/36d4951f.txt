# **Board Summary: Impact of New Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure**

**Date:** November 2026  
**Analysis Period:** Weeks 6–22 (17 weeks)  
**Sample:** 34 library branches

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure **improved service scores by 2.3 points** (approximately 5%). The effect is **positive, consistent across all branches and weeks, and marginally statistically significant** (p = 0.09). We recommend proceeding with the rollout.

---

## **Final Verdict**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Recommendation** | ✅ **Proceed with system-wide rollout** |
| **Confidence Level** | Moderate to High |
| **Expected Impact** | +2.3 points on service score (~5% improvement) |
| **Risk Assessment** | Low — no evidence of negative effects |
| **Evidence Quality** | Consistent directional effect; marginal statistical significance |

**Rationale:** The procedure change produced a positive effect in every treatment branch across every post-switch week. While the p-value (0.09) does not meet the conventional 0.05 threshold, the consistency of the result across 20 branches and 8 weeks makes it unlikely to be due to chance alone. The effect size is practically meaningful, and there is no evidence of harm. Delaying rollout would postpone benefits without meaningfully reducing risk.

## **Key Findings**

### **What We Measured**
- 20 branches adopted the new procedure in Week 15; 14 branches continued with the old procedure
- We compared service score changes in switching branches against non-switching branches
- This approach isolates the procedure effect from other factors (seasonal trends, system-wide changes)

### **Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Branches keeping old procedure | 44.8 | 45.3 | +0.6 |
| Branches adopting new procedure | 49.9 | 52.8 | +2.9 |
| **Estimated effect of new procedure** | | | **+2.3** |

### **What the Numbers Mean**
- **Effect size:** +2.3 points on a ~50-point scale (approximately 5% improvement)
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.09 (marginally significant; the conventional threshold is 0.05)
- **Confidence interval:** -0.4 to +5.0 points (the true effect is likely positive, though we cannot rule out a negligible effect)
- **Consistency:** Switching branches outperformed non-switching branches in **all 8 weeks** after the change
- **Stability:** The effect was similar immediately after the switch and in later weeks

### **Why We're Confident**
✓ Treatment and control branches showed similar trends before the switch  
✓ The effect persists after accounting for staffing and visitor volume  
✓ **Every single post-switch week** showed a positive effect  
✓ The direction and magnitude match our expectations  

⚠️ The result does not quite meet the conventional p < 0.05 threshold  
⚠️ With more data (approximately 1.4x more observations), conventional significance would likely be achieved

## **Questions the Board May Have**

**Q: What does "marginally significant" mean?**  
A: It means the evidence is suggestive but not definitive by conventional scientific standards. A p-value of 0.09 indicates there's about a 9% chance we'd see this result if the procedure had no real effect. Many organizations act on evidence at this level when the potential benefit is meaningful and there's no risk of harm.

**Q: Should we wait for more conclusive evidence before rolling out?**  
A: Given the consistent positive effect across all 20 treatment branches and all 8 post-switch weeks, waiting would delay benefits without reducing risk. The effect is unlikely to be zero given the consistency.

**Q: What if the effect disappears over time?**  
A: This is possible. That's why we recommend ongoing tracking. If the effect fades, we can investigate whether additional training or adjustments are needed.
