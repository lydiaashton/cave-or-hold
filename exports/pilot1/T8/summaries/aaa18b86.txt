# **Board Memorandum (FINAL)**

---

## **Evaluation of New Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** Based on data through Week 22  
**Subject:** Impact Assessment of New Branch Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**We cannot confidently attribute service score changes to the new procedure.** While switched branches outperformed non-switched branches after the change, this pattern was already emerging *before* the switch occurred. Branches that opted into the new procedure were already on a more favorable trajectory, which compromises our ability to isolate the procedure's true effect. **More rigorous evaluation is needed before expanding adoption.**

---

## **Final Verdict**

> **The evidence is insufficient to conclude the new procedure caused improved service scores.**
>
> **Recommendation: Do not proceed with system-wide rollout at this time.** Instead, (1) investigate why non-switched branches declined, (2) consider a randomized pilot for remaining branches, and (3) gather qualitative evidence from switched branches. Revisit the expansion decision after 6+ months of additional data and more rigorous testing.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Branches analyzed | 709 |
| Switched to new procedure | 425 (60%) |
| Kept old procedure | 284 (40%) |
| Study period | 17 weeks (Weeks 6–22) |
| Switch implemented | Week 15 |

### Service Score Outcomes

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| **Switched branches** | 50.9 | 51.0 | +0.1 |
| **Non-switched branches** | 50.4 | 48.5 | −1.9 |
| **Observed difference** | | | **+2.0** |

### Critical Methodological Concern

**The parallel trends assumption is violated.** For a valid comparison, switched and non-switched branches should have been on similar trajectories before the switch. They were not:

| Week | Gap (Switched − Non-switched) |
|------|-------------------------------|
| 6 | −0.88 (switched were *behind*) |
| 10 | +0.76 |
| 14 (pre-switch) | +1.23 |
| 22 (post-switch) | +3.45 |

**The gap was already widening by 0.34 points per week BEFORE the switch** (p < 0.001). This suggests branches that chose to switch differed systematically from those that didn't—perhaps more motivated, better-resourced, or better-managed.

### What This Means

The observed +1.9 point "effect" likely reflects **pre-existing differences between branches**, not the procedure itself. If the pre-switch trend had continued, we would have expected a gap of ~4.6 points by week 22; the actual gap was 3.45 points.

---

## **Limitations**

1. **Self-selection bias**: Branches voluntarily chose whether to switch. This is not a randomized experiment.

2. **Violated assumptions**: The key assumption for causal inference (parallel trends) does not hold in this data.

3. **Unobserved confounders**: We cannot rule out that other factors (management quality, local demographics, staffing stability) explain both the decision to switch and the service score patterns.

4. **Control group decline**: Non-switched branches declined substantially. Understanding *why* is as important as evaluating the new procedure.

---

## **Recommendations**

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| **1** | **Do NOT expand adoption yet** | Evidence is insufficient to justify system-wide rollout |
| **2** | Investigate why non-switched branches declined | This decline drove much of the observed "effect" |
| **3** | Consider randomized pilot for remaining branches | Random assignment would enable valid causal inference |
| **4** | Gather qualitative data from switched branches | Understand implementation experience and perceived benefits |
| **5** | Re-evaluate after 6+ months of additional data | More time may reveal clearer patterns |

---

## **Conclusion**

The data show an **association** between adopting the new procedure and better service score outcomes, but we cannot establish **causation**. The branches that switched were already outperforming those that didn't before the procedure change. We recommend against system-wide expansion until more rigorous evidence is available.
