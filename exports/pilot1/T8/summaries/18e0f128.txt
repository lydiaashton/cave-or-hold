# **BOARD MEMORANDUM**

---

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Subject:** Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores  

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure likely improved service scores, but the evidence is more nuanced than initially apparent.** The analysis suggests a positive effect of roughly **1 to 2 points**, but this benefit only emerged 5+ weeks after implementation. Importantly, branches that adopted the new procedure started the study period with lower scores than control branches, raising questions about whether the full estimated effect reflects the procedure itself or pre-existing differences. **The safest conclusion: there is evidence of improvement, but we should monitor results for another 4–8 weeks before committing to a full rollout.**

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

| Metric | Result |
|--------|--------|
| **Treatment Effect (DiD)** | +1.97 points on service score index |
| **95% Confidence Interval** | 1.37 to 2.56 points |
| **Statistical Significance** | p < 0.001 |
| **Event Study Average (Weeks 19–22)** | +1.3 to +1.9 points |
| **Branches Studied** | 709 total (425 switched, 284 control) |
| **Study Period** | 17 weeks (switch occurred week 15) |

### **Effect Timeline**

| Period | Weeks | Effect | Significance |
|--------|-------|--------|--------------|
| Pre-switch | 6–14 | — | Baseline |
| Immediate post-switch | 15–18 | 0.2 to 0.4 points | Not significant |
| Delayed post-switch | 19–22 | 1.3 to 1.9 points | **Significant** |

### **Pre-Trend Concern**

Treatment branches had **significantly lower scores** in weeks 6–11 (ranging from -1.0 to -2.1 points vs. controls, all statistically significant). By weeks 12–14, this gap had closed. This pattern violates the "parallel trends" assumption required for clean causal inference and suggests the +1.97 point estimate may be **upwardly biased**.

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

| Consideration | Assessment |
|---------------|------------|
| **Direction of effect** | ✅ Positive — no evidence of harm |
| **Statistical evidence** | ⚠️ Significant but with caveats |
| **Pre-trend validity** | ⚠️ Not perfectly parallel; some concern |
| **Effect timing** | ⚠️ Delayed 4–5 weeks; not immediate |
| **Practical significance** | ✅ Meaningful if sustained (~4% improvement) |
| **Recommendation** | **Proceed with caution** |

**Verdict: PROVISIONAL APPROVAL**

The data provides **moderate-confidence evidence** that the new procedure improves service scores, but not with the certainty we initially expected. We recommend:

1. **Continue** the procedure at current 425 branches
2. **Delay full rollout** to remaining 284 branches until we have 4–8 more weeks of data
3. **Re-assess** at that point to confirm effects are sustained and not regressing
4. **Investigate** why treatment branches started with lower pre-period scores (selection bias?)

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

### **Immediate Actions**
1. **Extend data collection** through week 30 to capture longer-term effects
2. **Document implementation quality** at each branch to identify best practices
3. **Communicate tempered expectations** to stakeholders: improvements are likely but not guaranteed

### **Analysis to Complete Before Full Rollout**
1. **Re-run analysis** with additional 4–8 weeks of data
2. **Test for selection bias**: Why did certain branches switch early? Were they systematically different?
3. **Heterogeneity analysis**: Do certain branch types (size, urban/rural, staffing) benefit more?
4. **Cost-benefit assessment**: Do service score gains justify operational costs?

---

## **CONCLUSION**

The new procedure shows **promising but not definitive** evidence of improvement. The delayed effect pattern and pre-trend concerns warrant additional monitoring before full commitment. With continued data collection and careful implementation tracking, the cooperative can make a more confident decision within 2 months.

---

*Analysis conducted on weekly service scores from 709 branches over 17 weeks. Difference-in-differences regression with branch fixed effects and clustered standard errors. Event study design revealed delayed effects and pre-trend concerns.*
