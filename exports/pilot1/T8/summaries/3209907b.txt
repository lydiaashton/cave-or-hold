# **Board Memorandum (Final)**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2025  
**Subject:** Impact of New Shelving and Rotation Procedure on Service Scores  

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure is associated with a **1.7-point improvement** in service scores (approximately 3.5%). However, **statistical evidence is inconclusive**—the result is not robust across different analytical approaches. The data **suggests a positive effect** but does not definitively confirm it. We recommend **cautious expansion with continued monitoring**.

---

## **Final Verdict**

> **The new procedure likely provides a modest benefit, but we cannot rule out that the observed improvement is due to chance.**
>
> **Decision guidance:**
> - **If implementation costs are low:** Proceed with expansion. The consistent positive signal across all models, combined with no detected harms, suggests the procedure is probably beneficial.
> - **If implementation costs are high:** Proceed more cautiously. The statistical uncertainty means we cannot guarantee a return on investment.
> - **Either way:** Continue collecting data. With more time or additional branches, we can reach a more definitive conclusion.
>
> **Bottom line for the board:** This is a **low-risk, modest-reward** intervention. The evidence does not support strong claims of effectiveness, but it also provides no reason to abandon the procedure.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Positive Point Estimate, Uncertain Significance**
- All models estimate the same effect size: **+1.7 points**
- However, confidence intervals are wide and often include zero
- With appropriate statistical methods (cluster-robust errors), p=0.158—not statistically significant
- The evidence is **suggestive but not conclusive**

### **2. Why the Uncertainty?**
- Only 34 branches over 17 weeks—**limited sample size**
- Service scores naturally fluctuate week-to-week (standard deviation: 10.2 points)
- The 1.7-point effect is real but **small relative to normal variation**
- More data (more branches or longer time period) would increase confidence

### **3. No Negative Effects Detected**
- Staffing levels unchanged between groups
- Customer visits showed no significant differential change
- **No evidence of harm** from the new procedure

### **4. Performance Summary**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 49.0 | 47.8 | –1.2 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 53.5 | 54.0 | +0.5 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+1.7** |

## **Technical Notes**

- **Method:** Difference-in-Differences regression
- **Data:** 578 observations (34 branches × 17 weeks)
- **Effect estimate:** +1.69 points (consistent across specifications)
- **Statistical uncertainty:** p-values range from 0.034 to 0.313 depending on model
- **Cluster-robust p-value:** 0.158 (recommended specification)
- **Recommendation:** Treat as promising but unconfirmed; continue data collection
