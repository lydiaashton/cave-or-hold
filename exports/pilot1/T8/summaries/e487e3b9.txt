# **Board Summary: New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure **produced a modest but real improvement** in service scores. After accounting for system-wide gains that affected all branches, the new procedure added approximately **1.5 to 2.0 points** to service scores. This is statistically significant and worth scaling, though not transformational.

---

## **What We Found**

### **Service Score Changes**

| | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| New procedure branches | 50.1 | 54.7 | +4.5 |
| Old procedure branches | 50.2 | 52.9 | +2.7 |
| **Attributable to new procedure** | | | **~+1.8** |

### **Important Context**

- **All branches improved** after week 15, suggesting seasonal or system-wide factors were at play
- The new procedure's contribution is the **incremental gain** above that general improvement
- **Caveat:** Branches selected for the new procedure were already trending slightly upward, so the true effect may be closer to 1.5 than 2.0 points
- Effect size: **0.18 standard deviations** (modest but meaningful)

---

## **Final Verdict**

**Proceed with the rollout.** The new procedure delivers a genuine, statistically significant improvement in service quality. While the effect is modest rather than dramatic, it represents meaningful progress for patrons at no apparent downside. The investment in training and transition is justified.

---

## **Recommendations**

1. **Proceed with rollout**, but set realistic expectations (~1.5–2.0 point gain, not 4+ points)

2. **Investigate what drove system-wide improvement**—if replicable, this could benefit all branches regardless of procedure

3. **Monitor for 8–12 weeks** post-switch to confirm effects persist

4. **Consider cost-benefit** before mandating the change system-wide

---

*Analysis: Difference-in-differences regression, weeks 6–22, 709 branches. Pre-trend differences suggest estimates may be slightly optimistic.*
