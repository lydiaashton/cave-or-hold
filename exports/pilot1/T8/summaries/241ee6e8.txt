# **Board Memorandum**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors
**From:** Data Analysis Office
**Date:** November 13, 2026
**Subject:** Impact Assessment of New Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Executive Summary**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure is associated with **improved branch service scores**. Branches that adopted the new procedure showed service scores approximately **1.8 points higher** than comparable branches that retained the old procedure. However, pre-existing trends suggest the true effect may be **smaller than this estimate**. We recommend a **phased rollout** with close monitoring rather than immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Bottom Line**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did the new procedure work? | **Likely yes** |
| How much improvement? | **Up to +1.8 points** (true effect may be lower) |
| How confident are we? | **Moderate**—positive effect is clear, but magnitude is uncertain |
| Should we expand it? | **Yes, but phased** with monitoring |

---

## **Key Findings**

### **The Evidence**

Our analysis tracked **709 branches** over **17 weeks**, comparing 425 branches that switched to the new procedure against 284 that did not.

**Service Score Changes After Week 15:**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched Branches | 49.05 | 51.10 | **+2.05** |
| Non-Switched Branches | 47.99 | 48.28 | +0.28 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+1.76** |

After controlling for branch characteristics, staffing levels, and visitor volume, the estimated treatment effect is **+1.82 points** (95% CI: 1.27 to 2.37).

### **Important Limitation**

**The treatment and control groups were already diverging before the switch:**

| Pre-Period | Treatment-Control Gap |
|------------|----------------------|
| Weeks 6-10 (early) | 0.56 points |
| Weeks 11-14 (late) | 1.68 points |
| **Pre-switch divergence** | **+1.12 points** |

This pre-existing trend (p < 0.001) suggests our 1.8-point estimate **likely overstates** the true treatment effect. The actual improvement may be **closer to 0.5–1.0 points**.

### **What This Means**

- The **direction** of the effect is clear: switched branches improved relative to non-switched branches
- The **magnitude** is uncertain: pre-existing trends reduce confidence in the exact size
- The effect appears **sustained** across all 8 weeks of post-switch observation
- **Staffing and visitor volume** independently affect service scores and should be monitored

---

## **Final Verdict**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Effect Direction** | ✅ Positive |
| **Statistical Significance** | ✅ Yes (p < 0.001) |
| **Parallel Trends Assumption** | ⚠️ Violated |
| **Confidence in Magnitude** | ⚠️ Moderate |
| **Recommendation** | ✅ **Phased Rollout** |

**Bottom Line Verdict:** The new procedure shows a **positive effect on service scores**, but the pre-existing divergence between treatment and control branches means we cannot confidently claim the full 1.8-point improvement is due to the procedure alone. The true effect is likely **positive but more modest**.

---

## **Recommendations**

### **Immediate Actions**

1. **Approve phased rollout** to 50-75 additional branches (not system-wide)
2. **Establish 8-week evaluation window** for each phase before proceeding
3. **Set conservative expectations**: Plan for 0.5–1.0 point improvement, not 1.8
4. **Allocate implementation resources** for training and transition support

### **Monitoring Protocol**

1. Track service scores weekly at each new branch during rollout
2. Compare against matched control branches where possible
3. **Stop or adjust** rollout if effects do not materialize within 8 weeks
4. Document implementation fidelity (was the procedure actually followed?)

### **Follow-Up Analysis**

1. **12-month impact assessment**: Confirm effects persist long-term
2. **Cost-benefit analysis**: Quantify implementation costs versus service improvements
3. **Mechanism study**: Identify which elements of the new procedure drive improvements

---

## **Methodology Note**

Analysis used difference-in-differences and fixed effects regression with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered at branch level). The **parallel trends assumption is violated**, meaning the estimated effect should be interpreted as an **upper bound** rather than a precise causal estimate.
