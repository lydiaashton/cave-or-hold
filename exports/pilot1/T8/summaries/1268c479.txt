**SUBJECT:** Impact of New Branch Shelving and Rotation Procedure on Service Scores

---

## **Executive Summary**

We analyzed whether the new shelving and rotation procedure improved branch service scores. While branches that adopted the new procedure showed greater score improvements than those that did not, **the difference is not statistically distinguishable from chance** given our current data. We cannot conclude the procedure change caused a meaningful improvement in service scores.

---

## **Bottom Line**

| Finding | Result |
|---------|--------|
| Estimated effect of new procedure | **+2.2 points** on service score index |
| Statistical confidence | **Not significant** (p = 0.17) |
| Range of plausible effects | **-0.9 to +5.2 points** |

The data suggests a positive trend, but we lack sufficient evidence to confirm the procedure change drove the improvement.

---

## **Final Verdict**

**The analysis is inconclusive.** 

The new procedure shows a positive association with service scores, but the evidence is not strong enough to confirm causation. This is a **promising but unproven** intervention.

**What this means for decision-making:**

| If you prioritize... | Recommended action |
|---------------------|-------------------|
| **Evidence certainty** | Continue the pilot and collect more data before expanding |
| **Potential upside** | Proceed with rollout—the effect is likely positive, and there's no evidence of harm |
| **Resource conservation** | Pause expansion until results are clearer; invest in other proven improvements |

**Our recommendation:** Given the positive point estimate, absence of negative effects, and potential operational benefits, **continue the new procedure while collecting additional data**. Set a review date (e.g., 6 months) to reassess with more observations.

---

## **Analysis Overview**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** tracked weekly from week 6 through week 22
- **20 branches** switched to the new procedure at week 15; **14 branches** continued standard practice
- Compared score changes between switching and non-switching branches, controlling for branch characteristics, staffing levels, and visitor volume

### **What We Found**

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|----------------|---------------|--------|
| Branches keeping old procedure | 48.4 | 51.5 | +3.1 |
| Branches adopting new procedure | 48.0 | 53.2 | +5.3 |
| **Attributable to new procedure** | | | **+2.2** |

**Important context:** All branches improved after week 15. The critical question is whether switching branches improved *more* than non-switching branches. The answer is yes—by about 2 points—but this difference falls within the range of normal variation.

### **Why We Cannot Confirm an Effect**

1. **Limited data:** Only 8 weeks of post-switch observations across 20 branches provides modest statistical power
2. **Natural variation:** Service scores fluctuate week-to-week (standard deviation ≈ 10 points); the observed difference could reflect random variation
3. **Confidence interval:** The true effect could plausibly range from slightly negative (-0.9) to meaningfully positive (+5.2)

---

## **Recommendations**

### **Immediate Actions**

1. **Continue the new procedure.** The point estimate is positive, and there is no evidence of harm. Operational benefits (efficiency, staff workflow) may still justify continuation.

2. **Extend data collection.** Continue tracking for an additional 8–12 weeks. Doubling the post-switch observation period would substantially improve our ability to detect a true effect.

3. **Document implementation fidelity.** Verify that switching branches are consistently applying the new procedure as intended. Variation in implementation could mask true effects.

### **Medium-Term Actions**

4. **Conduct branch-level reviews.** Identify whether certain branch types (by size, location, or staffing) show stronger effects. Targeted rollout may maximize impact.

5. **Gather qualitative feedback.** Survey branch staff and patrons about perceived changes in service quality, wait times, and material availability.

6. **Review cost implications.** Compare any training or operational costs against the potential service score benefits.

3. Are there non-score benefits (staff satisfaction, efficiency) that should factor into the decision?

## **Technical Appendix (Summary)**

- **Method:** Difference-in-differences regression with branch fixed effects
- **Standard errors:** Clustered at branch level
- **Pre-trend validation:** Treatment and control groups showed parallel trends before the switch (p = 0.11)
- **Controls:** Staffing index, visits index, branch-specific characteristics
- **Effect size:** 0.21 standard deviations (modest)
- **Software:** Python with statsmodels
