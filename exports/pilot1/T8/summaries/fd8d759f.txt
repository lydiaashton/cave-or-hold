# **LIBRARY COOPERATIVE BOARD MEMORANDUM**

---

**To:** Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 13, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**We did not find statistically significant evidence that the new procedure improves service scores** (p = 0.17). However, the data suggests a positive effect of approximately 2.5 points (~5% improvement), with no evidence of harm. The recommendation depends on implementation costs—see Final Verdict below.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### Treatment Effect Summary

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect of new procedure | **+2.5 points** on service score |
| Baseline average score | 50.3 points |
| Effect size | **4.5% improvement** |
| Statistical significance | Not significant (p = 0.17) |
| 95% Confidence Interval | **-1.0 to +5.5 points** |

### What Changed After the Switch

| Group | Before (Weeks 6-14) | After (Weeks 15-22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------|---------------------|--------|
| Switched branches (20) | 50.1 | 50.9 | +0.8 |
| Non-switched branches (14) | 50.9 | 49.3 | -1.6 |
| **Net effect (DiD)** | | | **+2.5** |

### Study Validity

✓ **Parallel trends verified**: Both groups showed similar score patterns before the switch  
✓ **Controls applied**: Results account for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume  
✓ **Robust methods**: Branch and week fixed effects with cluster-robust standard errors  

### Why Results Are Inconclusive

The study has **limited statistical power**:
- 34 branches over 17 weeks provides 578 observations
- The observed 2.5-point effect is meaningful but below our detection threshold
- The true effect could range from slightly negative (-1.0) to substantially positive (+5.5)
- Approximately 88% probability the true effect is positive (but not statistically certain)

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

| Evidence Status | Recommendation |
|-----------------|----------------|
| ❌ **Not proven** | The data does not meet the conventional threshold (p < 0.05) to conclude the new procedure works |
| ⚠️ **Suggestive** | The point estimate (+2.5 points) and confidence interval mostly favor a positive effect |
| ✓ **No harm detected** | Even the lower bound of uncertainty is only -1 point—no evidence of damage |

### Decision Matrix

| If New Procedure Is... | Board Should... |
|------------------------|-----------------|
| **Low-cost, easy to maintain** | **Continue** while collecting 8-12 more weeks of data for a definitive answer |
| **High-cost, operationally burdensome** | **Reconsider or pause** — the evidence does not justify significant expense |
| **Already rolled out to most branches** | **Complete the rollout** — switching back would add disruption, and evaluate with before/after data for all branches |
| **Not yet widely adopted** | **Pilot for 3 more months** — treat as an experiment, not a proven improvement |

### Our Recommendation

**If implementation costs are modest, continue the new procedure and extend evaluation.** The potential upside (5% service improvement) outweighs the risk, and a definitive answer is achievable with additional data. However, the board should understand this is a **judgment call under uncertainty**, not a data-driven proof of effectiveness.

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

### Immediate Actions (Next 30 Days)

1. **Assess implementation costs** — quantify staff time, training, and operational burden of the new procedure
2. **Decide on continuation** based on the decision matrix above
3. **Plan extended data collection** — commit to at least 8-12 more weeks if continuing

### Medium-Term Recommendations (Next Quarter)

4. **Monitor key drivers**: Staffing levels and visitor volume both significantly affect service scores
5. **Verify implementation fidelity**: Ensure all switched branches are fully adopting the new procedures
6. **Re-analyze in Q1 2027**: With extended data, determine whether the effect becomes statistically significant

---

## **METHODOLOGY SUMMARY**

- **Design**: Difference-in-differences with branch and week fixed effects
- **Sample**: 34 branches, 17 weeks (weeks 6-22), 578 observations
- **Treatment**: 20 branches adopted new procedure starting week 15; 14 branches maintained old procedure
- **Outcome**: Service score index (average ~50, higher is better)
- **Analysis**: Cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch)
