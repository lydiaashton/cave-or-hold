# **Board Summary: Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Service scores improved more at switched branches than at non-switched branches, but we cannot definitively attribute this to the new procedure.** Switched branches showed a 2.1-point greater improvement after the change, but these branches were already trending upward relative to others before the switch. The evidence is **suggestive but not conclusive** that the new procedure caused the improvement.

**Recommendation:** Proceed cautiously. Consider a phased rollout with rigorous monitoring, or a randomized trial for remaining branches.

---

## **Final Verdict**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Did scores improve at switched branches?** | ✅ **Yes** — clearly and significantly |
| **Was the improvement caused by the new procedure?** | ⚠️ **Uncertain** — pre-existing trends confound the result |
| **Should we roll out system-wide immediately?** | ❌ **No** — evidence is insufficient for full commitment |
| **Should we abandon the new procedure?** | ❌ **No** — results are promising, warrant further testing |

**Bottom Line:** The new procedure is **promising but unproven**. Invest in rigorous evaluation before full-scale implementation.

- Week 6: Treatment was **0.7 points BELOW** control

---

## **Limitations**

⚠️ **Parallel Trends Violation:** The fundamental assumption for causal inference—that treatment and control branches would have followed similar trends absent the intervention—is not satisfied. Treatment branches were already improving faster before the procedure change.

⚠️ **Possible Explanations:**
1. The new procedure genuinely improved scores (our hope)
2. Branches chosen to switch were already on an upward trajectory (selection bias)
3. Unmeasured factors affected switched branches differently (confounding)

## **Technical Appendix**

**Methodology:** Difference-in-differences with branch fixed effects and clustered standard errors. The parallel trends test revealed a statistically significant pre-trend (coefficient = 0.25, p = 0.012), indicating treatment branches were improving ~0.25 points/week faster than control branches even before the switch. Over the 9-week pre-period, this accounts for approximately 2.2 points of the observed effect.

**Bottom Line Interpretation:** The 2.1-point DiD estimate should be viewed as an **upper bound** on the true causal effect. The actual effect of the procedure change could be substantially smaller, or even zero.
