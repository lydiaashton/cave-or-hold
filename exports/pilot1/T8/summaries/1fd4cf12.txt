# **Board Memorandum**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** [Current Date]  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure is associated with a **2-point improvement in service scores** (4% gain), with suggestive but not definitive statistical evidence. The data are compatible with a true effect ranging from slightly negative to +5 points. **This is promising early evidence, but not yet conclusive proof** of effectiveness. We recommend continuing implementation while gathering more data to reach statistical certainty.

---

## **Key Findings**

| Metric | Result |
|--------|--------|
| **Estimated Effect** | +2.1 points (4% improvement) |
| **95% Confidence Interval** | -1.0 to +5.3 points |
| **Statistical Significance** | Not significant at 5% level (p = 0.19) |
| **Branches Studied** | 34 total (20 new procedure, 14 old procedure) |
| **Observation Period** | 17 weeks (9 before, 8 after switch) |

### **What the Data Show**

1. **Positive Direction, Uncertain Magnitude**: All model specifications yield positive estimates (2.1–2.5 points). The confidence interval includes zero but also includes effects as large as +5 points.

2. **Effects May Grow Over Time**: Point estimates in weeks 19–22 (+2.7 to +4.4 points) are larger than in weeks 15–18, suggesting staff may need time to master the new procedure.

3. **No Pre-Existing Differences**: Treatment and control branches followed parallel trends before the switch, validating our comparison approach.

4. **Staffing Matters**: Higher staffing levels are significantly associated with better service scores (p = 0.04), independent of the procedure change.

---

## **Why We May Not See Statistical Significance Yet**

| Factor | Impact |
|--------|--------|
| **Limited Post-Switch Data** | Only 8 weeks after implementation; effects may strengthen over time |
| **Sample Size** | 34 branches provides moderate power; more branches or weeks would help |
| **Natural Variation** | Service scores vary week-to-week (SD ≈ 10 points), masking smaller effects |
| **Implementation Learning Curve** | Staff proficiency with new procedures likely improves over time |

---

## **Recommendations**

### **Immediate Actions**

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| **1** | **Continue current rollout** | Point estimates are positive; no evidence of harm |
| **2** | **Extend data collection 8–12 weeks** | More post-switch observations will narrow confidence intervals |
| **3** | **Monitor weeks 19–22 pattern** | Growing effects suggest full benefit not yet realized |

### **Strategic Considerations**

| If... | Then... |
|-------|---------|
| New procedure is **low-cost/low-burden** | Proceed with full rollout; 4% improvement may justify costs |
| New procedure is **costly/disruptive** | Pause expansion; gather more evidence before committing |
| Effects **strengthen in next 8 weeks** | Accelerate rollout and document best practices |
| Effects **remain inconclusive after 20 weeks** | Consider alternative interventions or procedure refinements |

---

## **Final Verdict**

> **"Proceed with Cautious Optimism"**

The evidence does not meet the conventional threshold for statistical proof, but it also provides **no reason to stop**. Three considerations support continuing the rollout:

| Consideration | Assessment |
|---------------|------------|
| **Risk of Harm** | Low — no negative effects detected; confidence interval excludes large negative impacts |
| **Potential Benefit** | Moderate — 2–5 point improvement would be meaningful for patron experience |
| **Cost of Waiting** | Moderate — delaying rollout forfeits potential benefits while uncertainty persists |

**Our Recommendation:** Continue the current implementation plan. Re-evaluate in 8–12 weeks with additional data. If effects remain in the 2–3 point range with improved precision, proceed to full rollout. If estimates drift toward zero or negative, pause and reassess.

**Decision Rule for Next Review:**
- **Expand** if effect ≥2 points with p < 0.10
- **Continue monitoring** if effect 0–2 points or p > 0.10
- **Pause/Revise** if effect <0 or confidence interval excludes positive values

## **Conclusion**

The data are **consistent with a modest positive effect** but do not yet provide statistical certainty. Given the positive direction of results, the absence of negative effects, and the likelihood that benefits grow with staff proficiency, we recommend **continuing the current implementation while planning a follow-up evaluation in 8–12 weeks**. This balanced approach allows the cooperative to capture potential benefits while maintaining accountability for evidence-based decision-making.
