# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD OF DIRECTORS**

---

## **Subject: Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

**Date:** Analysis of weeks 6–22  
**Data:** 709 branches, 12,053 weekly observations

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**Branches that adopted the new procedure showed service score gains of approximately 1.8 points more than branches that kept the old procedure.** However, because switching branches were already on a stronger improvement trajectory before the change, we cannot definitively attribute all of this gain to the new procedure. The evidence suggests the procedure **likely helped**, but the true effect is uncertain and probably smaller than 1.8 points.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **1. Study Overview**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Total branches analyzed | 709 |
| Branches using new procedure | 425 (60%) |
| Branches using old procedure | 284 (40%) |
| Observation period | Weeks 6–22 |
| Switch implemented | Week 15 |

### **2. Service Score Changes**

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|----------------|---------------|--------|
| New procedure branches | 50.14 | 54.68 | +4.54 |
| Old procedure branches | 50.18 | 52.91 | +2.72 |
| **Observed difference** | | | **+1.82** |

### **3. Statistical Results**
- **Estimated effect:** +1.85 points
- **95% confidence interval:** +1.31 to +2.38 points
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001

### **4. Critical Limitation: Pre-Existing Trends**

| Period | Treatment Group Trend | Control Group Trend |
|--------|----------------------|---------------------|
| Before switch | +0.66 points/week | +0.38 points/week |
| **Differential** | **Treatment was already improving faster (p < 0.001)** |

This violates the key assumption needed for causal inference. Some portion of the observed 1.8-point gain likely reflects pre-existing momentum, not the procedure itself.

### **5. Effect Over Time**

| Weeks After Switch | Treatment Advantage |
|--------------------|---------------------|
| 1–2 | +0.5 to +0.9 points |
| 3–4 | +1.3 to +2.2 points |
| 5–8 | +2.1 to +2.6 points |

The growing advantage is encouraging but could also reflect continued divergence in underlying trajectories.

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **Did service scores improve after the switch?** | Yes — decisively |
| **Did switching branches improve more than non-switching branches?** | Yes — by about 1.8 points |
| **Can we attribute this difference to the new procedure?** | Partially — but not with full confidence |
| **Is the new procedure likely beneficial?** | Probably yes |
| **Should we expand based on this evidence alone?** | Proceed with caution |

**Overall Assessment:** *Suggestive but not conclusive.* The data is consistent with a real positive effect, but the magnitude is uncertain. A reasonable estimate is that the new procedure contributed **0.5 to 1.5 points** of the observed 1.8-point difference.

---

## **RECOMMENDED NEXT STEPS**

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| **1** | **Run a randomized rollout** for remaining 284 branches | Random assignment eliminates selection bias and provides definitive evidence |
| **2** | **Gather qualitative feedback** from branch staff | Understand whether the procedure feels like an improvement on the ground |
| **3** | **Continue monitoring** service scores for 6–12 months | Assess whether the effect persists, grows, or diminishes |
| **4** | **Conduct cost-benefit analysis** | Determine if even a modest improvement justifies implementation costs |
| **5** | **Consider phased expansion** while evidence is gathered | Balance potential benefits against uncertainty |

---

## **CONCLUSION**

The new procedure is associated with meaningful service score improvements, and the evidence points toward a genuine positive effect. However, because branches selected for early adoption were already improving faster, we cannot claim the full 1.8-point difference as the procedure's impact. 

**Recommendation:** Proceed with expansion, but use randomization for remaining branches to generate definitive evidence. This allows the cooperative to capture potential benefits while building a stronger evidence base for future decisions.
