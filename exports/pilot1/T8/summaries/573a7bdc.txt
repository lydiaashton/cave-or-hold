# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** January 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Impact of New Shelving Procedure on Service Scores

---

## **Bottom Line**

We cannot confidently conclude the new procedure improved service scores. While switching branches showed higher scores after the change, **they were already improving before the switch**, which undermines causal claims. The evidence is **inconclusive** — the procedure may help, may do nothing, or the benefit may take months to materialize. We recommend **pausing further rollout decisions** until we have more data.

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did service scores improve after the switch? | **Yes** — in branches that adopted the new procedure |
| Can we attribute this to the new procedure? | **No** — pre-existing trends confound the result |
| Should we expand the rollout systemwide? | **Not yet** — evidence is insufficient |
| Should we abandon the new procedure? | **No** — potential benefit remains plausible |
| What should we do? | **Monitor longer, analyze deeper, decide later** |

**Confidence Level: LOW** — The parallel trends assumption required for causal inference is violated. The estimated 1.5-point effect is likely overstated; the true effect could be zero, modestly positive, or (less likely) negative.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Initial Analysis Showed +1.5 Points — But This Is Likely Biased**
A standard difference-in-differences model estimated a 1.5-point improvement (p < 0.001). **However**, this estimate relies on an assumption that was violated: switching and non-switching branches were not on parallel trajectories before the intervention.

### **2. Switching Branches Were Already Improving**
Event study analysis reveals switching branches had **significantly higher growth** in service scores even before adopting the new procedure:
- 5 of 8 pre-switch weeks showed statistically significant upward trends
- Average pre-treatment trajectory: **+1.3 points** relative to non-switching branches
- This suggests the "treatment effect" captures pre-existing momentum, not the procedure itself

### **3. Post-Switch Evidence Is Weak**
When examining week-by-week effects after the switch:
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Average post-switch effect | 0.7 points |
| Statistically significant weeks | 1 of 8 (week 22 only) |
| Week 22 effect | 2.0 points (p < 0.001) |

The single significant week could reflect random variation, a delayed effect, or external factors.

### **4. Study Scope**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Branches studied | 709 |
| Switched to new procedure | 425 (60%) |
| Kept old procedure | 284 (40%) |
| Observation period | 17 weeks |

---

## **Recommendations**

### **Immediate Actions**
1. **Do not expand rollout** based on current evidence — the case is not yet proven
2. **Continue monitoring** both groups for at least 8-12 additional weeks
3. **Investigate selection** — understand why certain branches chose to switch and whether their characteristics explain the pre-trends

### **Further Analysis**
4. **Extend the time horizon** — if benefits accumulate slowly, we need more weeks to detect them
5. **Consider matched comparison** — pair switching branches with similar non-switching branches on baseline characteristics
6. **Plan randomized evaluation** — for future changes, randomize rollout order to enable valid causal inference

---

## **Conclusion**

The data do not currently support a confident claim that the new shelving procedure improves service scores. The apparent benefit is confounded by pre-existing differences between branches that did and did not switch. **More time and more rigorous analysis are needed** before making systemwide decisions.
