# **Board Memorandum (Final)**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2025  
**Subject:** Impact Assessment of New Shelving and Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and rotation procedure is associated with a **1.7-point improvement** in service scores (approximately 3.5% gain). However, this effect **does not reach conventional statistical significance** when accounting for the structure of the data. The evidence suggests a positive effect, but we cannot rule out the possibility of no effect. We recommend **cautious expansion** with continued monitoring.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Point Estimate Is Positive**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Service score improvement | **+1.69 points** |
| Percentage gain | **+3.5%** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | **-0.76 to +4.15 points** |

The estimated effect is consistently positive across all analytical approaches, but the confidence interval includes zero.

### **2. Service Score Performance**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 49.0 | 47.8 | –1.2 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 53.5 | 54.0 | +0.5 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+1.7** |

Treatment branches maintained service quality while control branches declined slightly.

### **3. Why the Uncertainty?**
- Only **34 branches** were studied, limiting statistical power
- Service scores have natural week-to-week variation (SD ≈ 10 points)
- The effect size (1.7 points) is modest relative to this variation
- **8 weeks of post-intervention data** may be insufficient to detect smaller effects with confidence

### **4. No Adverse Effects Detected**
- Staffing levels unchanged between groups
- Visitor volume stable
- No branches showed service score declines attributable to the new procedure

---

## **Final Verdict**

> **Proceed, but verify.**
>
> The data shows a **positive signal without definitive proof**. The 1.7-point gain is operationally meaningful if real, and there is no evidence of harm. However, the statistical uncertainty means we should treat this as a **promising pilot** rather than a proven success.
>
> **Our recommendation:** Continue the rollout to remaining branches, but commit to a formal re-evaluation after 6–12 months with the full system implemented. If the effect persists and strengthens with more data, the procedure can be confirmed as best practice. If the effect diminishes or disappears, the cooperative can reconsider without having invested heavily in a failed initiative.
>
> **Bottom line for decision-makers:** The risk of proceeding is low; the risk of stopping is potentially leaving service improvements on the table.

---

## **Recommendations**

| Priority | Action | Timeline |
|----------|--------|----------|
| **1** | Continue rollout to remaining 14 branches | Q1 2026 |
| **2** | Extend data collection for full re-evaluation | Ongoing |
| **3** | Track implementation fidelity at each branch | Monthly |
| **4** | Formal re-assessment with 12 months of data | Q4 2026 |

---

## **Conclusion**

The new procedure shows **promising but not definitive** evidence of service improvement. Given the positive point estimate, absence of negative effects, and operational benefits reported by staff, we recommend proceeding with expansion while committing to rigorous ongoing evaluation.
