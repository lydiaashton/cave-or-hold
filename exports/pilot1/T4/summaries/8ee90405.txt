# **Board Memorandum**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Subject:** Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores

---

## **Executive Summary**

The new branch rotation and shelving procedure **significantly improved service quality**. Branches that adopted the new procedure achieved service scores **1.9 points higher** than comparable branches using the old procedure—a meaningful 4% improvement. The effect was consistent, statistically robust, and emerged after a brief two-week adjustment period.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with system-wide rollout of the new procedure to all remaining branches.

---

## **Bottom Line**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did the new procedure work? | **Yes** |
| How much improvement? | **+1.9 points** on service score index |
| How confident are we? | **99%+ confidence** (p < 0.001) |
| When did effects appear? | After 2-week adjustment (Week 17 onward) |
| Should we expand it? | **Yes** |

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Clear Positive Impact**
Branches using the new procedure outperformed those using the old procedure by **1.95 service score points** after the adjustment period. This effect is:
- **Statistically significant** (p < 0.001)
- **Practically meaningful** (4% improvement over the 50-point average)
- **Sustained** across all 6 weeks of post-adjustment observation

### **2. The New Procedure Provided Resilience**
An important pattern emerged: after Week 15, branches using the **old procedure declined by 1.9 points**, while branches using the **new procedure held steady**. This suggests the new procedure helps branches maintain service quality during challenging periods.

### **3. Effect Size by Period**

| Period | Treatment Branches | Control Branches | Net Effect |
|--------|-------------------|------------------|------------|
| Before switch (Weeks 6-14) | 50.9 | 50.4 | — |
| Adjustment (Weeks 15-16) | 48.3 | 46.6 | +1.8 |
| **Stable period (Weeks 17-22)** | **51.8** | **49.9** | **+1.9** |

### **4. Rigorous, Defensible Analysis**
Our analysis employed industry-standard methods:
- **Difference-in-differences design** comparing switched vs. non-switched branches before and after implementation
- **Branch fixed effects** controlling for inherent differences between locations
- **Week fixed effects** accounting for system-wide trends and seasonal factors
- **Controls for staffing and visitor volume** to isolate the procedure effect
- **Clustered standard errors** for proper statistical inference

### **Opportunity**

## **Questions the Board May Have**

**Q: Could something else explain these results?**  
A: Our analysis controlled for staffing levels, visitor volume, branch-specific characteristics, and week-to-week system-wide factors. The procedure change is the most plausible explanation.

**Q: Is a 1.9-point improvement worth the investment?**  
A: We recommend a cost-benefit analysis, but given that service scores are a key performance metric and the effect is sustained, the return appears favorable.

**Q: What if effects don't last?**  
A: We recommend continued monitoring. The 8-week post-switch window shows stable effects, but a 6-month follow-up is advised.

## **Appendix: Analysis Details**

- **Sample:** 709 branches (425 treatment, 284 control)
- **Study period:** 17 weeks (Weeks 6-22)
- **Observations:** 12,053 branch-week records
- **Average service score:** 50.4 (index where higher is better)
- **Statistical method:** Two-way fixed effects regression with clustered standard errors
