# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** [Current Date]  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Executive Summary**

After analyzing 17 weeks of service score data from 34 branches, we find **suggestive but inconclusive evidence** that the new stock rotation and shelving procedure improves service quality. Branches that adopted the new procedure showed modest score gains relative to those that did not, but the evidence is not strong enough to rule out random variation. We recommend continued monitoring before making a systemwide decision.

---

## **Bottom Line**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did service scores improve? | **Possibly, by about 2 points (4%)** |
| Are we confident this is real? | **No—effect is not statistically significant** |
| Should we expand the new procedure? | **Not yet—gather more data first** |
| Should we abandon the new procedure? | **Not necessarily—data are consistent with a modest benefit** |

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. The Numbers**
- **Estimated improvement:** +2.1 points on the service score index (baseline average: ~50)
- **Confidence range:** The true effect could reasonably be anywhere from -1 to +5 points
- **Statistical certainty:** 18% probability this result could occur by chance even if the procedure has no real effect (conventional threshold is 5%)

### **2. What the Data Show**
- **Before the switch** (weeks 6–14): Treatment and control branches followed similar trends, validating our comparison
- **After the switch** (weeks 15–22): Treatment branches pulled ahead modestly, with hints of growing benefits in later weeks
- **Staffing matters:** Higher staffing levels are significantly associated with better service scores, independent of the procedure change

### **3. Study Strengths and Limitations**

| Strengths | Limitations |
|-----------|-------------|
| ✓ 34 branches across 17 weeks | ✗ Only 8 weeks of post-switch data |
| ✓ Valid comparison group (control branches) | ✗ Effect not statistically distinguishable from zero |
| ✓ No missing data; clean records | ✗ Cannot rule out unmeasured factors |
| ✓ Pre-trends support causal interpretation | ✗ May take longer for full effect to emerge |

---

## **Recommendations**

### **Immediate Actions (Next 30 Days)**
1. **Continue the pilot** with current 20 branches—do not expand or contract
2. **Collect 8–12 additional weeks** of data to improve statistical precision
3. **Document implementation quality** at each branch (training received, staff feedback, operational challenges)

### **Medium-Term Actions (60–90 Days)**
4. **Re-analyze with expanded data** and present updated findings to the Board
5. **Conduct branch-level interviews** to identify why some branches may benefit more than others
6. **Assess operational costs** of the new procedure versus estimated service benefits

### **Decision Framework**

| If... | Then... |
|-------|---------|
| New procedure is **low-cost and low-burden** | Proceed with cautious expansion; benefits likely outweigh costs |
| New procedure is **costly or disruptive** | Pause expansion; consider alternative interventions |
| Follow-up analysis shows **stronger, significant effects** | Recommend systemwide adoption |
| Follow-up analysis shows **no effect or negative effects** | Recommend reverting to old procedure or redesigning the new one |

---

## **Questions for Board Discussion**

1. What level of service improvement would justify the operational costs of the new procedure?
2. Are there branches where implementation challenges suggest the need for additional support or training?
3. Should we consider a hybrid approach (e.g., new procedure for high-volume branches only)?
4. What timeline is acceptable for gathering more evidence before making a final decision?
