# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 13, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure shows a **positive but statistically inconclusive effect** on branch service scores. Switched branches experienced an estimated **1.8-point increase** in service scores relative to non-switched branches. While the direction is favorable, the evidence is not strong enough to confirm the procedure caused this improvement with confidence.

**Recommendation:** Continue the pilot and collect more data before making a systemwide rollout decision.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Overview**
| | |
|---|---|
| **Branches studied** | 34 total (20 switched, 14 did not) |
| **Study period** | 17 weeks (Weeks 6–22, 2026) |
| **Switch date** | Week 15 |
| **Method** | Difference-in-differences regression with branch fixed effects |

### **Service Score Results**
*(Scale: higher is better; cooperative average ≈ 50)*

| Period | Control Branches | Switched Branches | Difference |
|--------|-----------------|-------------------|------------|
| Before switch (Weeks 6–14) | 48.1 | 54.4 | 6.2 |
| After switch (Weeks 15–22) | 46.6 | 54.6 | 8.0 |
| **Change attributable to new procedure** | | | **+1.8 points** |

### **Statistical Confidence**
- **Estimated effect:** +1.8 points
- **95% Confidence interval:** -0.8 to +4.4 points
- **Statistical significance:** Not significant at conventional levels (p = 0.17)

**Interpretation:** The true effect could reasonably be slightly negative, zero, or moderately positive. More data is needed for a definitive conclusion.

### **Supporting Evidence**
✓ **Pre-trend validity confirmed:** Switched and non-switched branches followed parallel trends before the intervention (p = 0.18)  
✓ **Controls accounted for:** Analysis adjusts for weekly staffing and visit levels, both of which significantly affect service scores  
✓ **Consistent pattern:** Switched branches maintained higher scores throughout the study period

---

## **Next Steps**

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| **1** | **Extend data collection by 8–12 weeks** | Increases statistical power to detect true effect |
| **2** | **Analyze branch-level heterogeneity** | Identify whether certain branch types benefit more |
| **3** | **Document implementation costs** | Enable formal cost-benefit analysis |
| **4** | **Review staffing interactions** | Higher staffing correlates with better scores; may amplify procedure benefits |

### **Decision Framework**

| If the new procedure is... | Then consider... |
|---------------------------|------------------|
| **Low-cost, low-disruption** | Gradual rollout while continuing evaluation |
| **High-cost or disruptive** | Maintain pilot status until evidence strengthens |
| **Showing branch-specific patterns** | Target rollout to branches most likely to benefit |

---

## **Conclusion**

The new procedure is **promising but not yet proven**. The positive direction of the effect supports continued use in pilot branches, but the board should await additional data before committing to a cooperative-wide mandate. A decision revisited in 2–3 months with expanded data will rest on firmer ground.
