# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure: Impact on Service Scores**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative
**Date:** November 2026
**Subject:** Impact Assessment of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by **1.85 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant (p < 0.001) and represents approximately a **3.5% improvement** relative to the typical branch score of 50. The improvement grew over time, suggesting branches continued to realize benefits as they gained experience with the new system.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with expanding the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
| Component | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Analysis method** | Difference-in-Differences (rigorous quasi-experimental approach) |
| **Treatment group** | 425 branches that switched to the new procedure |
| **Control group** | 284 branches that continued with the old procedure |
| **Observation period** | 17 weeks (Weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at Week 15 |
| **Total observations** | 12,053 weekly branch records |

### **Results**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Treatment effect** | +1.85 service score points |
| **95% Confidence Interval** | [1.31, 2.38] |
| **Statistical significance** | p < 0.001 |
| **Effect size** | 0.18 standard deviations (moderate) |

### **Supporting Evidence**

1. **Parallel trends validated:** Before the switch (Weeks 6–14), treatment and control branches had nearly identical service score trends (average difference: -0.04 points), confirming the groups were comparable and the analysis is valid.

2. **Effects strengthened over time:** The treatment effect grew from approximately 0.9 points in Week 15 to 2.3–2.6 points by Weeks 20–22, suggesting learning effects or cumulative benefits as staff became more proficient with the new procedure.

3. **Robust to controls:** The result holds after accounting for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume, indicating the effect is attributable to the procedure change itself, not other operational factors.

4. **Both groups improved post-switch:** Service scores rose for all branches after Week 15 (likely due to seasonal factors), but switching branches improved **1.8 points more** than non-switching branches.
