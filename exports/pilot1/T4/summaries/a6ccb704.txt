# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new stock rotation and shelving procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.0 points** more than branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant (p < 0.001) and represents a meaningful improvement in branch service quality.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Study Overview
- **709 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **425 branches** (60%) adopted the new procedure starting week 15
- **284 branches** (40%) continued with the old procedure as a control group
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, the standard method for evaluating policy changes with treatment and control groups

### 2. Impact on Service Scores

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 49.34 | 51.01 | +1.67 |
| Treated (new procedure) | 50.17 | 53.81 | +3.64 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+1.98** |

- The **1.98-point improvement** is the estimated causal effect of the new procedure
- 95% confidence interval: **1.39 to 2.54 points**
- Effect remains significant after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### 3. Confidence in Results

✓ **Parallel pre-trends confirmed**: Before week 15, both groups showed similar score trajectories, validating the comparison

✓ **Effect timing matches intervention**: The gap between groups widened only after week 15, consistent with a causal effect

✓ **Robust to controls**: Results hold when accounting for weekly staffing and visit fluctuations

✓ **Consistent across branches**: The benefit did not vary by staffing levels or visitor volume

### 4. Additional Observations

- Both higher staffing and higher visitor volume are associated with better service scores
- The effect appears to **grow over time** in the post-switch period, suggesting branches may improve further as they gain experience
- Control branches also improved slightly (+1.67 points), possibly due to seasonal factors or system-wide initiatives
