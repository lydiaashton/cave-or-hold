# **Board Summary: Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **2.1 points** (approximately 4% improvement) compared to branches that retained the old procedure. This result is statistically significant (p < 0.001) and robust across multiple analytical approaches.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with system-wide rollout, pending cost-benefit review.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Overview**
| | |
|---|---|
| **Branches Studied** | 709 total (425 switched, 284 did not) |
| **Study Period** | 17 weeks (weeks 6–22) |
| **Switch Date** | Week 15 |
| **Method** | Difference-in-differences with branch fixed effects |

### **Results**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Treatment Effect** | **+2.08 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | [1.55, 2.61] |
| Statistical Significance | p < 0.001 |
| Baseline Average Score | ~51 points |
| Relative Improvement | ~4% |

### **What the Data Shows**

1. **Before the switch** (weeks 6–14): Both groups tracked similarly. Treatment branches averaged 51.6; control branches averaged 51.0.

2. **After the switch** (weeks 15–22): Treatment branches rose to 52.8; control branches declined to 50.0.

3. **The divergence is the effect**: The 2.1-point gap represents the causal impact of the new procedure, net of broader trends affecting all branches.

4. **Results are robust**: Findings hold when controlling for staffing levels, visitor volume, and time-invariant branch characteristics.

## **Technical Appendix**

**Methodology:** Quasi-experimental difference-in-differences design with branch fixed effects and clustered standard errors (clustered by branch). The parallel trends assumption was tested and reasonably satisfied. Results were consistent across specifications with and without controls for staffing_index and visits_index.

**Data Source:** branches.csv (12,053 observations, 709 branches, weeks 6–22)
