# **BOARD MEMORANDUM**

---

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Subject:** Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores  

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure improved service scores by approximately 2 points (4% above average), but benefits took 4–5 weeks to materialize.** This delay suggests staff needed time to adapt before the new workflow produced measurable gains. The evidence supports continuing and expanding the rollout, with realistic expectations about the adaptation period.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

| Metric | Result |
|--------|--------|
| **Treatment Effect** | +1.97 points on service score index |
| **95% Confidence Interval** | 1.37 to 2.56 points |
| **Statistical Significance** | p < 0.001 (highly significant) |
| **Relative Improvement** | ~4% above average score of 51 |
| **Branches Studied** | 709 total (425 switched, 284 control) |
| **Study Period** | 17 weeks (switch occurred week 15) |

### **Effect Timeline**

| Period | Weeks | Effect | Significance |
|--------|-------|--------|--------------|
| Pre-switch | 6–14 | — | Baseline |
| Immediate post-switch | 15–18 | 0.2 to 0.4 points | Not significant |
| Delayed post-switch | 19–22 | 1.3 to 1.9 points | **Significant** |

**Critical insight:** The new procedure did not produce immediate benefits. Statistically significant improvements only appeared starting in week 19—five weeks after implementation. This pattern strongly suggests a learning curve rather than an instant operational gain.

### **Methodology**

- **Design:** Difference-in-differences with branch fixed effects
- **Controls:** Staffing levels, visit volumes, time-invariant branch characteristics
- **Validation:** Event study analysis confirmed effect timing and tested pre-trends
- **Note:** Pre-switch trends showed treatment branches started lower but converged with controls by weeks 12–14, supporting the validity of the comparison

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

### **Decisions Required**
1. **Approve continued use** of the new procedure at the 425 switched branches
2. **Authorize rollout** to remaining 284 branches with appropriate change management
3. **Set expectations** with branch managers: allow 4–5 weeks for adaptation before measuring success

### **Recommended Actions**
1. **Document implementation best practices** from branches that showed earlier improvements
2. **Track long-term effects** beyond the current 8-week post-switch window
3. **Conduct cost-benefit analysis** weighing operational costs against service score gains
4. **Investigate branch-level variation** to identify which branches benefit most

### **Questions for Operations Leadership**
- What training or support was provided during the first 4 weeks post-switch?
- Were there documented implementation challenges that may have delayed benefits?
- Can we identify characteristics of branches that adapted more quickly?

---

## **CONCLUSION**

The new rotation and shelving procedure **delivers measurable improvements** to service quality. However, the delayed effect timeline indicates that **change management and staff adaptation are critical success factors**. With proper support and realistic expectations, the cooperative can expect meaningful service score improvements as the rollout continues across all branches.

---

*Analysis conducted on weekly service scores from 709 branches over 17 weeks. Difference-in-differences regression with branch fixed effects and clustered standard errors. Event study design validated effect timing.*
