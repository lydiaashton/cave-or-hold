# **Board Memorandum**

---

## **Evaluation of New Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** Based on data through Week 22  
**Subject:** Impact Assessment of New Branch Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure maintained their performance while non-adopting branches experienced a meaningful decline. The estimated benefit is **+1.9 points** on the service score index—a **4% improvement** that is both statistically significant (p < 0.001) and practically meaningful.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Branches analyzed | 709 |
| Switched to new procedure | 425 (60%) |
| Kept old procedure | 284 (40%) |
| Study period | 17 weeks (Weeks 6–22) |
| Switch implemented | Week 15 |

### Service Score Outcomes

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| **Switched branches** | 50.9 | 51.0 | +0.1 |
| **Non-switched branches** | 50.4 | 48.5 | −1.9 |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | | **+1.9** |

### Statistical Evidence
- **Point estimate:** +1.92 service score points
- **95% Confidence Interval:** [1.35, 2.49]
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001
- **Effect size:** Cohen's d = 0.51 (medium effect)
- **Robustness:** Results hold after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### What This Means
The new procedure's primary benefit appears **protective**: adopting branches maintained their service quality while non-adopting branches declined. This suggests the new procedure helped branches resist system-wide pressures that negatively affected performance during this period.

---

## **Important Considerations**

1. **Selection bias:** Branches that opted into the new procedure showed slightly stronger pre-existing trends. More proactive or better-resourced branches may have been more likely to switch.

2. **Observational evidence:** While our analytical approach controls for many confounding factors, this was not a randomized trial.

3. **Time horizon:** We have 8 weeks of post-switch data. Continued monitoring will confirm whether benefits persist.

---

## **Recommendations**

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| **1** | Expand adoption to remaining branches | Evidence supports positive impact |
| **2** | Continue monitoring for 6–12 months | Confirm long-term sustainability |
| **3** | Document implementation best practices | Capture why the procedure works |
| **4** | Conduct cost-benefit analysis | Ensure operational costs are justified |
| **5** | Support struggling non-switched branches | Address the decline in this group |

---

## **Conclusion**

The data support continued investment in the new stock rotation and shelving procedure. The effect is statistically robust, practically meaningful, and aligns with our expectation that procedural improvements would enhance service quality. We recommend proceeding with broader implementation while maintaining ongoing evaluation.
