# **Board Memorandum**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors
**From:** Data Analysis Office
**Date:** November 13, 2026
**Subject:** Impact Assessment of New Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Executive Summary**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure has **successfully improved branch service scores**. Branches that adopted the new procedure achieved service scores approximately **1.8 points higher** than comparable branches that retained the old procedure. This represents a **3.7% improvement** and is statistically significant (p < 0.001). We recommend proceeding with system-wide implementation.

---

## **Bottom Line**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did the new procedure work? | **Yes** |
| How much improvement? | **+1.8 points** on service score index |
| How confident are we? | **95% certainty** the true effect is between 1.3 and 2.4 points |
| Should we expand it? | **Recommended** |

---

## **Key Findings**

### **The Evidence**

Our analysis tracked **709 branches** over **17 weeks**, comparing 425 branches that switched to the new procedure against 284 that did not. The switch occurred in week 15, providing clear before-and-after comparison.

**Service Score Changes After Week 15:**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched Branches | 49.05 | 51.10 | **+2.05** |
| Non-Switched Branches | 47.99 | 48.28 | +0.28 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+1.76** |

After controlling for branch characteristics, staffing levels, and visitor volume, the estimated treatment effect is **+1.82 points** (95% CI: 1.27 to 2.37).

### **What This Means**

- The average branch scores approximately 50 points on our service index
- The new procedure adds nearly **2 points** to this score
- This improvement is **sustained** across all 8 weeks of post-switch observation
- The effect is **independent of staffing and visit volume** (both of which also positively affect scores)

### **Visual Evidence**

Two charts accompany this memo:
1. **Trends Plot**: Shows service score trajectories for both groups, with a clear divergence after week 15
2. **Event Study Plot**: Demonstrates the treatment-control gap widening post-switch while remaining stable pre-switch

---

## **Recommendations**

### **Immediate Actions**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to remaining 284 branches
2. **Allocate implementation resources** for training and transition support
3. **Establish monitoring protocol** to track service scores during rollout

### **Follow-Up Analysis**

1. **12-month impact assessment**: Confirm effects persist long-term
2. **Cost-benefit analysis**: Quantify implementation costs versus service improvements
3. **Mechanism study**: Identify which elements of the new procedure drive improvements (e.g., faster item location, reduced staff time, better organization)

### **Considerations**

- Branches that did not switch had lower baseline service scores. These locations may benefit from **additional support** alongside the procedure change.
- Staffing and visitor volume independently affect service scores. Continue monitoring these factors during rollout.

---

## **Methodology Note**

Analysis used difference-in-differences and fixed effects regression with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered at branch level). This approach accounts for:
- Pre-existing differences between branches
- Time-invariant branch characteristics
- Weekly variations in staffing and visitor volume
- Correlation of observations within the same branch over time
