# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 13, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **EXECUTIVE SUMMARY**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure shows a **positive but statistically inconclusive** effect on service scores. Switched branches improved by 2.15 points more than non-switched branches after implementation, but this difference could plausibly be due to random variation. We recommend **continued monitoring with additional data collection** before making a systemwide rollout decision.

---

## **BACKGROUND**

Beginning in week 15 of this year, 20 of our 34 branches adopted a new procedure for rotating and shelving stock. The remaining 14 branches continued with the standard procedure. Service scores have been tracked weekly for all branches from weeks 6 through 22, providing an opportunity to evaluate the procedure's impact.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### Service Score Performance

| Group | Before (Weeks 6-14) | After (Weeks 15-22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------|---------------------|--------|
| **Switched Branches** (n=20) | 47.97 | 53.25 | +5.28 |
| **Non-Switched Branches** (n=14) | 48.35 | 51.49 | +3.13 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+2.15** |

### Statistical Assessment

- **Estimated effect:** +2.15 service score points (approximately 4.5% improvement)
- **Statistical significance:** Not significant (p = 0.16)
- **95% confidence interval:** -0.84 to +5.13 points
- **Interpretation:** The data is compatible with effects ranging from slightly negative to moderately positive

### Validity Checks

- ✓ **Parallel trends confirmed:** No significant difference in pre-switch trends between groups (p = 0.25)
- ✓ **Balanced groups:** Treatment and control branches had similar baseline scores
- ✓ **Robust to controls:** Effect unchanged when accounting for staffing and visit levels

---

## **WHAT THIS MEANS FOR DECISION-MAKING**

1. **The procedure is not harmful.** There is no evidence of negative impact on service quality.

2. **A modest benefit is plausible.** The point estimate suggests real improvement, but we lack statistical confidence.

3. **More data is needed.** With 17 weeks and 34 branches, we can only detect fairly large effects. Additional weeks of observation would sharpen our estimates.

4. **Implementation quality matters.** If some branches only partially adopted the new procedure, the measured effect would be diluted.

---

## **RECOMMENDATIONS**

| Priority | Action | Timeline |
|----------|--------|----------|
| **1** | Continue weekly data collection for all branches | Ongoing |
| **2** | Conduct implementation audit at switched branches | 4 weeks |
| **3** | Reanalyze data after 10 additional weeks | Q1 2027 |
| **4** | Evaluate cost-benefit if effect persists | Q2 2027 |

---

## **CONCLUSION**

The new procedure shows promise but does not yet demonstrate statistically reliable improvement. We advise **continuing the current pilot** while gathering more evidence. A definitive go/no-go decision should be deferred until we have sufficient data to detect the expected effect size with confidence.
