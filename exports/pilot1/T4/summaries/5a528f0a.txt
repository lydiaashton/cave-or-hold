# **Board Summary: Impact of New Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure**

**Date:** November 2026  
**Analysis Period:** Weeks 6–22 (17 weeks)  
**Sample:** 34 library branches

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure is associated with a **2.3-point improvement** in service scores. The effect is **positive and consistent** but **not statistically significant at conventional levels** (p = 0.10). We recommend proceeding with the rollout while continuing to monitor outcomes.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **What We Measured**
- 20 branches adopted the new procedure in Week 15; 14 branches continued with the old procedure
- We compared service score changes in switching branches against non-switching branches
- This approach isolates the procedure effect from other factors (seasonal trends, system-wide changes)

### **Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Branches keeping old procedure | 44.8 | 45.3 | +0.6 |
| Branches adopting new procedure | 49.9 | 52.8 | +2.9 |
| **Estimated effect of new procedure** | | | **+2.3** |

### **What the Numbers Mean**
- **Effect size:** +2.3 points on a ~50-point scale (approximately 5% improvement)
- **Confidence interval:** -0.4 to +5.0 points (we cannot rule out a small negative effect, but the likely range is positive)
- **Consistency:** Switching branches outperformed non-switching branches in **all 8 weeks** after the change
- **Stability:** The effect was similar immediately after the switch and in later weeks, suggesting no learning curve issues

### **Why We're Confident (and Cautious)**
✓ Treatment and control branches showed similar trends before the switch  
✓ The effect persists after accounting for staffing and visitor volume  
✓ The direction matches our expectations  

⚠️ The result does not meet the conventional threshold for statistical significance (p < 0.05)  
⚠️ The confidence interval includes zero, meaning chance cannot be fully ruled out  

---

## **Recommendations**

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| **1** | **Proceed with system-wide rollout** | Consistent positive effect across all branches and weeks; no evidence of harm |
| **2** | **Extend monitoring through 6–12 months** | More data will clarify whether the effect is real and stable |
| **3** | **Track complementary metrics** | Monitor circulation, patron satisfaction, and staff time to capture full impact |
| **4** | **Document implementation details** | Record which branches adopted which practices to identify best practices |

---

## **Questions the Board May Have**

**Q: If the result isn't statistically significant, why recommend rollout?**  
A: Statistical significance depends on sample size. We have only 8 weeks of post-switch data. The effect is consistently positive across all branches and weeks, which is practically meaningful even if not statistically definitive.

**Q: Could something else explain the improvement?**  
A: The analysis controls for staffing levels and visitor volume. The parallel pre-switch trends suggest no obvious confounding factors. However, continued monitoring is warranted.

**Q: What if the effect disappears over time?**  
A: This is possible. That's why we recommend ongoing tracking. If the effect fades, we can investigate whether additional training or adjustments are needed.
