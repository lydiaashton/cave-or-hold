# **REVISED MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 13, 2026  
**Subject:** Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence is mixed.** Branches that adopted the new procedure maintained stable service scores while non-adopting branches declined. However, the two groups were on different trajectories before the switch, which limits our confidence in attributing this difference to the procedure itself. We recommend **cautious expansion with close monitoring** rather than full rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. What the Data Shows**
| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|---------------|---------------|--------|
| New procedure branches | 50.9 | 51.0 | **Stable** |
| Old procedure branches | 50.4 | 48.5 | **▼ 1.9 points** |

Branches using the new procedure did not decline, while others did.

### **2. Critical Limitation: Pre-Existing Differences**
The two groups were **not on parallel trends** before the switch:
- Treatment branches were trending **upward** (+0.22 points/week)
- Control branches were trending **downward** (−0.12 points/week)

This means we cannot confidently attribute the post-switch divergence to the procedure itself. The treatment branches may have simply continued their upward trajectory regardless of the intervention.

### **3. Two Different Statistical Answers**
| Model | Estimated Effect | Significance |
|-------|-----------------|--------------|
| Branch-only comparison | 0.08 points | Not significant (p=0.68) |
| With week controls | 1.92 points | Significant (p<0.001) |

The discrepancy exists because the "effect" depends entirely on whether we attribute the control group's decline to system-wide factors (which the new procedure protected against) or to pre-existing differences between the groups.

---

## **Recommendations**

### **1. Proceed Cautiously**
The new procedure shows **no negative effects** and may provide resilience during challenging periods. However, the evidence does not support a strong claim of improvement.

### **2. Pilot Expansion with Evaluation**
- Extend the procedure to a subset of remaining branches (not all 284)
- Use **randomized assignment** for this phase to enable cleaner causal inference
- Monitor for 8-12 weeks before deciding on full rollout

### **3. Investigate the Control Group Decline**
The 1.9-point drop in control branches after week 15 warrants investigation:
- Were there staffing changes, budget cuts, or external factors?
- Did the switch itself divert resources from non-switching branches?
- Understanding this may be as valuable as evaluating the new procedure

### **4. Establish Better Baseline Measures**
Future procedure changes should include:
- Randomized or phased rollout design
- Longer pre-period baseline (more than 9 weeks)
- Clear success metrics defined in advance

---

## **Bottom Line (Restated)**

The new procedure **did not harm** service scores and may help branches weather system-wide challenges. However, we cannot confidently claim it **improved** scores. A cautious, evaluated expansion is warranted—not an immediate full rollout.

---

*This analysis used difference-in-differences methods with 709 branches over 17 weeks. Pre-trend differences limit causal confidence. Full technical appendix available upon request.*
