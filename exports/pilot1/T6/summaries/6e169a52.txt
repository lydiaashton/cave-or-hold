### **The Core Problem: Parallel Trends Violation**

The difference-in-differences method requires that treatment and control branches would have followed similar trends absent the intervention. **This assumption is not met:**

- **Before the switch** (weeks 6-14): The gap between treatment and control branches was already widening by 0.28 points per week
- **After the switch** (weeks 15-22): The gap continued widening at 0.17 points per week — actually **slower** than before

This suggests the observed improvement may reflect **ongoing differences between branch types** rather than an effect of the new procedure.

### **Possible Explanations**

1. **Selection bias**: Branches chosen to switch may have been on an improvement trajectory for other reasons (e.g., more motivated staff, better management, recent investments)

2. **Unobserved factors**: Something else changing at treatment branches (not the procedure) may explain the scores

3. **The procedure may still help**: We cannot rule out a positive effect, but we also cannot confirm it with this data
