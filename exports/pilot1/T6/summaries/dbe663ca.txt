## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and rotation procedure is associated with a **1.7-point improvement** in service scores (approximately 3.5% gain). However, this effect **does not reach conventional statistical significance** when accounting for the structure of the data. The evidence suggests a positive effect, but we cannot rule out the possibility of no effect. We recommend **cautious expansion** with continued monitoring.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Point Estimate Is Positive**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Service score improvement | **+1.69 points** |
| Percentage gain | **+3.5%** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | **-0.76 to +4.15 points** |

The estimated effect is consistently positive across all analytical approaches, but the confidence interval includes zero.

### **2. Service Score Performance**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 49.0 | 47.8 | –1.2 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 53.5 | 54.0 | +0.5 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+1.7** |

Treatment branches maintained service quality while control branches declined slightly.

### **3. Why the Uncertainty?**
- Only **34 branches** were studied, limiting statistical power
- Service scores have natural week-to-week variation (SD ≈ 10 points)
- The effect size (1.7 points) is modest relative to this variation
- **8 weeks of post-intervention data** may be insufficient to detect smaller effects with confidence

### **4. No Adverse Effects Detected**
- Staffing levels unchanged between groups
- Visitor volume stable
- No branches showed service score declines attributable to the new procedure
