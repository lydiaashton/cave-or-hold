# **Library Branch Stock Rotation: Impact of New Procedure on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence is inconclusive but suggestive.** The new procedure is associated with a +2.3 point improvement in service scores, but this result is **not statistically significant** (p=0.10). We cannot confidently conclude the new procedure caused better scores—though the data lean toward a modest positive effect rather than no effect or harm. **Recommendation: Continue monitoring before deciding on system-wide rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** over 17 weeks (Weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure at Week 15; **14 branches** kept the old procedure
- Difference-in-differences analysis isolates the procedure's effect from other factors

### **Results**

| Metric | Control (Old) | Treatment (New) |
|--------|---------------|-----------------|
| Pre-switch (Weeks 6–14) | 44.8 | 49.9 |
| Post-switch (Weeks 15–22) | 45.3 | 52.8 |
| **Change** | **+0.6** | **+2.9** |

**Estimated Effect: +2.3 points**  
**95% Confidence Interval: -0.5 to +5.1**  
**p-value: 0.10**

### **What This Means**
| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did scores improve in treatment branches? | Yes, by 2.9 points |
| Can we attribute this to the new procedure? | **Not with statistical confidence** |
| Could the true effect be zero or negative? | Yes (but unlikely) |
| Could the true effect be meaningfully positive? | Yes (up to ~5 points) |
| Is the new procedure harmful? | Very unlikely |

### **Validity Check**
✓ **Parallel trends confirmed**: Both groups had similar trends before the switch (p=0.89), supporting our comparison method.
