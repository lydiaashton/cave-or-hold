## **Key Findings (Revised)**

### **1. Critical Model Discrepancy**

| Model Specification | Treatment Effect | P-value | Interpretation |
|--------------------|------------------|---------|----------------|
| Branch FE only | +0.08 points | 0.68 | **No effect** |
| Branch FE + Week FE | +1.92 points | <0.001 | Appears significant |

**What this means:** The "effect" only appears when we control for week-specific factors. Without week controls, there is no detectable treatment effect. This indicates the result is driven by **control branch decline**, not treatment branch improvement.

### **2. Parallel Trends Assumption Violated**

A valid difference-in-differences analysis requires treatment and control groups to follow similar trends before the intervention. Our data show:

| Pre-Period Week | Difference (Treatment − Control) | Significance |
|-----------------|----------------------------------|--------------|
| Week 6 | -2.11 points | p < 0.01 ** |
| Week 7 | -2.10 points | p < 0.01 ** |
| Week 8 | -1.31 points | p < 0.05 * |
| Weeks 10-14 | -0.5 to +0.4 points | Not significant |

**Implication:** Treatment and control branches were on **different trajectories** early in the study period. While trends converged by weeks 10-14, this violation of the parallel trends assumption means the DiD estimate may be **biased**.

### **3. What Actually Happened**

| Period | Treatment Branches | Control Branches |
|--------|-------------------|------------------|
| Before Week 15 | 50.92 | 50.36 |
| After Week 15 | 51.00 | 48.50 |
| **Change** | **+0.08** | **-1.86** |

The new procedure did not boost scores—it appears to have **protected** branches from a decline that affected non-adopting branches.

### **4. Alternative Explanations**

The control branch decline could reflect:
- **Selection bias**: Branches that chose to switch may differ systematically (better management, more resources, etc.)
- **External factors**: Something affected control branches negatively after Week 15 (budget cuts, staffing issues, seasonal patterns)
- **Regression to the mean**: If treatment branches were selected because they were underperforming early, their later stability may reflect natural variation
