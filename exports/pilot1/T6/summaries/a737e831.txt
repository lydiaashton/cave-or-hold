## **Critical Caveat: Parallel Trends Assumption**

The difference-in-differences method assumes treatment and control groups would have followed similar trends absent the intervention. **This assumption is violated in our data:**

| Pre-Period | Treatment-Control Gap |
|------------|----------------------|
| Weeks 6-10 (early) | 0.56 points |
| Weeks 11-14 (late) | 1.68 points |
| **Pre-switch divergence** | **+1.12 points** |

The gap was already widening **before** the switch occurred (p < 0.001 for pre-trend). This means our 1.8-point estimate likely **overstates** the true treatment effect—some of that divergence was already happening.

## **What This Means for the Board**

1. **The direction is clear**: Service scores improved at switched branches relative to non-switched branches
2. **The magnitude is uncertain**: The true effect is probably **smaller than 1.8 points** because treatment branches were already improving faster before the switch
3. **Recommendation stands, but tempered**: System-wide rollout is still reasonable given the positive signal, but we should:
   - Set **conservative expectations** for improvement
   - Implement **rigorous tracking** at each new branch
   - Be prepared to **adjust or halt** if effects don't materialize

---

## **Revised Recommendation**

**Proceed with phased rollout** rather than system-wide immediate implementation. Start with 50-75 branches, measure actual effects over 8-12 weeks, then decide on full expansion. This protects against over-investment if the true effect is smaller than our estimates suggest.
