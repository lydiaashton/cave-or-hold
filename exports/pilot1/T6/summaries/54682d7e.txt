**The new procedure is associated with higher service scores, but we cannot confidently isolate its causal effect.** Branches that adopted the new procedure had service scores approximately **2 points higher** than control branches after the switch. However, these branches were already improving relative to controls before the intervention, making it difficult to determine how much of the improvement is attributable to the new procedure versus pre-existing differences.

- The treated-control gap grew by **0.29 points per week** during weeks 6-14 (p < 0.001)
- By week 14, the gap was already **1.97 points** — nearly identical to the post-switch average
- This pattern suggests the two groups were on different trajectories **before** the intervention

## **Revised Recommendation**

**Proceed with measured expansion while gathering more evidence.** The new procedure is associated with higher service scores and shows no negative effects. However, due to pre-existing differences between branches that did and did not adopt the procedure, we cannot confidently attribute the full 2-point gap to the intervention itself. 

**Before system-wide rollout:**
- Investigate whether treated branches differed systematically from controls (e.g., volunteered, were higher-performing, had different management)
- Consider a phased or randomized expansion to generate cleaner evidence
- Continue monitoring to see if the gap stabilizes, grows, or narrows over time
