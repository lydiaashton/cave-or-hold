## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure shows a positive effect on service scores, but the evidence is not definitive.** Switched branches improved by an estimated **1.8 points** relative to control branches (p = 0.06). This is **marginally significant** – suggestive of a real benefit, but not conclusive at the conventional 95% confidence level. The data are consistent with either a modest positive effect or no effect at all.

## **Interpretation**

### **What the Data Show**

✓ The effect is **positive** (in the expected direction)  
✓ The effect is **nearly statistically significant** (p = 0.06)  
✓ At 90% confidence, the effect is **significantly positive** (CI: 0.2 to 3.4)  
✓ Pre-trends were parallel, supporting the validity of the analysis  

⚠️ At 95% confidence, we **cannot rule out zero effect**  
⚠️ The study may be **underpowered** to detect effects smaller than ~2.5 points  
⚠️ Switched branches started with higher scores, though trends were parallel  

### **What This Means**

The evidence suggests the new procedure **likely provides a modest benefit**, but we lack the statistical precision to state this with 95% confidence. The true effect could plausibly range from essentially zero to approximately 3–4 points (a 6–8% improvement).

### **For Board Decision**

| Consideration | Guidance |

2. **Extend data collection** – An additional 2–3 months would substantially improve statistical precision
3. **Monitor implementation** – Ensure switched branches are fully adopting the new procedure
4. **Consider a 90% confidence standard** – For operational decisions where the cost of being wrong is low, the current evidence may be sufficient

## **Conclusion**

The new procedure shows a **positive, marginally significant effect** on service scores. While not definitive at the 95% confidence level, the evidence is suggestive enough to warrant continuation of the pilot and further monitoring. A decision to expand implementation should weigh the uncertain but positive benefit against the costs of rollout.
