# **Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure **significantly improved service scores**. Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **1.8 points** more than branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant (p < 0.001) and represents roughly a **3.6% improvement** relative to the baseline average score of 50.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **709 branches** analyzed over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **425 branches** switched to the new procedure (treatment group)
- **284 branches** kept the old procedure (control group)
- The switch occurred at **week 15**

### **Service Score Changes**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 50.14 | 54.68 | +4.54 |
| Non-switched branches | 50.18 | 52.91 | +2.72 |
| **Difference (New Procedure Effect)** | | | **+1.82** |

### **Statistical Results**
- **Estimated effect:** +1.85 points (95% confidence interval: 1.31 to 2.38)
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001 (highly significant)
- Results hold after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### **Effect Over Time**
The benefit of the new procedure **grew over the 8-week post-switch period**:
- Week 15: +0.9 points
- Week 18: +2.2 points
- Week 22: +2.3 points

This suggests branches may need time to fully realize the benefits of the new procedure.

### **Additional Insights**
- Both staffing levels and visitor volume are positively associated with service scores
- The parallel trends assumption appears valid—both groups had similar trajectories before the switch
- Service scores improved across all branches after week 15, but the improvement was substantially larger for branches using the new procedure

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Expand rollout:** Consider extending the new procedure to remaining branches, given the clear positive impact.

2. **Monitor long-term effects:** Continue tracking service scores to determine if the effect stabilizes, continues growing, or diminishes over time.

3. **Investigate mechanisms:** Analyze which aspects of the new procedure (rotation vs. shelving) drive the improvement to optimize implementation.

4. **Support transitioning branches:** Since benefits appear to grow over time, provide additional support to branches during their first 4–6 weeks after switching.

5. **Cost-benefit analysis:** Evaluate whether the service score improvement justifies any additional costs associated with the new procedure.
