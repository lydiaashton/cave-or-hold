### **Title: New Shelving Procedure Improves Service Scores by 4%**

---

### **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure had a significant positive effect.** Branches that switched to the new rotation and shelving procedure experienced a statistically significant improvement in service scores of **2.1 points** (approximately **4% above baseline**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is highly reliable (p < 0.001) and emerged immediately after implementation.

---

### **Key Findings**

1. **Treatment Effect Size**: The difference-in-differences analysis estimates the new procedure increased service scores by **2.08 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.57 to 2.60 points).

2. **Statistical Significance**: The result is highly statistically significant (p < 0.001), meaning there is less than a 0.1% probability this finding occurred by chance.

3. **Study Design**: 
   - **425 branches** adopted the new procedure (treatment group)
   - **284 branches** kept the old procedure (control group)
   - **17 weeks** of data (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
   - **12,053 total observations**

4. **Parallel Trends Validated**: Before the switch, treatment and control branches showed similar trajectories with no systematic differences. After week 15, treatment branches consistently outperformed control branches by 2–4 points each week.

5. **Control Variables**: The effect holds even after accounting for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume, confirming the improvement is attributable to the procedure change itself.

6. **Control Group Decline**: Interestingly, control group scores declined slightly (-0.95 points) after week 15, while treatment group scores increased (+1.18 points). This suggests the new procedure not only improved scores but may have protected branches from broader downward pressures.

---

### **Next Steps**

1. **Expand Implementation**: Given the clear positive impact, consider rolling out the new procedure to remaining branches currently using the old system.

2. **Monitor Long-Term Effects**: Continue tracking service scores to determine whether the 2-point improvement is sustained, grows, or diminishes over time.

3. **Investigate Mechanisms**: Analyze which specific elements of the new procedure (rotation schedule, shelving method, or their combination) drive the improvement to optimize further.

4. **Cost-Benefit Analysis**: Compare the service score gains against any additional operational costs of the new procedure to calculate return on investment.

5. **Share Best Practices**: Document the implementation process from high-performing switched branches to support training at other locations.
