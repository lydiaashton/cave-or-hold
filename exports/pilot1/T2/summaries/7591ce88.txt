# **Board Summary: Impact of New Stock Rotation Procedure on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure **significantly improved service scores** at branches that adopted it. Branches that switched to the new procedure saw their service scores increase by approximately **1.8 points** (about 3.7% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and robust across multiple analytical approaches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **709 branches** analyzed over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **425 branches** switched to the new procedure (treatment group)
- **284 branches** kept the old procedure (control group)
- Switch occurred at **week 15**, providing 9 weeks of pre-switch and 8 weeks of post-switch data

### **Treatment Effect**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated improvement | **+1.82 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | [1.27, 2.37] points |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 |
| Relative improvement | ~3.7% of average score |

### **Supporting Evidence**
1. **Difference-in-Differences Analysis**: The treatment group's scores improved by 2.05 points after the switch, while the control group improved by only 0.28 points. The net effect is **+1.76 points**.

2. **Fixed Effects Model**: After controlling for branch-specific characteristics, staffing levels, and visit volumes, the treatment effect remains at **+1.82 points** with cluster-robust standard errors.

3. **Event Study Pattern**: The gap between treatment and control branches widened after week 15, consistent with a treatment effect:
   - Pre-switch average gap: 1.06 points
   - Post-switch average gap: 2.82 points

### **Additional Observations**
- **Staffing and visits matter**: Both staffing index and visits index are positively associated with service scores (each 1-point increase in these indices is associated with ~0.5 points higher service scores).
- **Treatment branches started higher**: Switched branches had slightly higher service scores even before the change (about 1 point higher on average). The analysis accounts for this baseline difference.
- **Effect appears sustained**: The treatment effect is visible in all 8 post-switch weeks, with no evidence of fade-out.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Consider system-wide rollout**: Given the positive and statistically significant effect, expanding the new procedure to remaining branches could improve overall system performance.

2. **Monitor long-term effects**: Continue tracking service scores to confirm the effect persists beyond the initial 8-week post-switch period.

3. **Investigate mechanisms**: The analysis shows the procedure works, but understanding *why* (e.g., faster item location, better organization, reduced staff time per transaction) could help optimize implementation.

4. **Assess cost-benefit**: Quantify the resources required to implement the new procedure at remaining branches versus the expected service score gains.

5. **Address pre-existing differences**: The 284 branches that did not switch had lower baseline scores. Consider whether these branches need additional support beyond the procedure change.

---

**Analysis prepared using difference-in-differences and fixed effects regression methods with cluster-robust standard errors at the branch level.**
