# **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure **improved service scores by approximately 1.9 points** (about 4% relative to the average score of 50.4). This effect is statistically significant and became evident after a brief two-week adjustment period. Branches that adopted the new procedure maintained their service levels while non-adopting branches experienced a decline during the same period.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Positive Treatment Effect**
- Branches that switched to the new procedure saw service scores **1.92 points higher** than they would have otherwise (95% CI: 1.33 to 2.51; p < 0.001)
- After a two-week adjustment period (weeks 15-16), the effect stabilized at **1.95 points** (95% CI: 1.34 to 2.56)

### **2. Protection Against Decline**
The data reveal an important pattern: after week 15, **control branches (old procedure) experienced a 1.86-point decline** in service scores, while **treatment branches (new procedure) remained stable**. This suggests the new procedure helped branches resist system-wide pressures that negatively affected service quality.

### **3. Timeline of Effects**
| Period | Treatment Branches | Control Branches | Difference |
|--------|-------------------|------------------|------------|
| Before Week 15 | 50.92 | 50.36 | +0.56 |
| Weeks 15-16 (adjustment) | 48.34 | 46.57 | +1.77 |
| Weeks 17-22 (stable) | 51.81 | 49.86 | **+1.95** |

### **4. Rigorous Analysis**
The analysis used a **difference-in-differences design with two-way fixed effects**, which:
- Compares each branch to itself before and after the switch
- Controls for week-specific factors affecting all branches (seasonal trends, system-wide events)
- Accounts for staffing levels and visitor volume
- Uses clustered standard errors to properly handle repeated observations per branch

### **5. Supporting Factors**
Both staffing levels and visitor volume are positively associated with service scores (each 1-point increase in these indices corresponds to ~0.5 points higher service scores), but the procedure effect holds even after controlling for these factors.

## **Next Steps**

### **Immediate Actions**
1. **Expand rollout**: Consider extending the new procedure to remaining 284 branches, given the clear positive effect
2. **Document best practices**: Capture what makes the new procedure effective for training materials

### **Further Analysis**
1. **Cost-benefit analysis**: Quantify implementation costs vs. the value of a 1.9-point service score improvement
2. **Heterogeneity analysis**: Identify whether certain branch types (size, location, demographics) benefit more or less from the new procedure
3. **Long-term monitoring**: Continue tracking to ensure effects persist beyond the 8-week post-switch window

### **Caveats to Consider**
- The study covers only 8 weeks post-implementation; longer-term effects should be monitored
- Some pre-period differences existed between treatment and control branches (addressed by the fixed effects model, but worth noting)
- The control group's decline may reflect external factors; the new procedure appears to provide resilience rather than dramatic improvement
