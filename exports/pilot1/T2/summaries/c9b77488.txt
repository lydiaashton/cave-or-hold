# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by **1.85 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant (p < 0.001) and represents approximately a **3.5% improvement** relative to the typical branch score of 50. The improvement grew over time, suggesting branches continued to realize benefits as they gained experience with the new system.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **Analysis method:** Difference-in-Differences (DiD) — a rigorous quasi-experimental approach
- **Treatment group:** 425 branches that switched to the new procedure
- **Control group:** 284 branches that continued with the old procedure
- **Observation period:** 17 weeks (Weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at Week 15

### **Results**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Treatment effect** | +1.85 service score points |
| **95% Confidence Interval** | [1.31, 2.38] |
| **Statistical significance** | p < 0.001 |
| **Effect size** | 0.18 standard deviations (moderate) |

### **Supporting Evidence**

1. **Parallel trends validated:** Before the switch (Weeks 6–14), treatment and control branches had nearly identical service score trends (average difference: -0.04 points), confirming the groups were comparable.

2. **Effects strengthened over time:** The treatment effect grew from approximately 0.9 points in Week 15 to 2.3–2.6 points by Weeks 20–22, suggesting learning effects or cumulative benefits.

3. **Robust to controls:** The result holds after accounting for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume, indicating the effect is attributable to the procedure change itself.

4. **Both groups improved post-switch:** Service scores rose for all branches after Week 15 (likely due to seasonal factors), but switching branches improved **1.8 points more** than non-switching branches.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Expand rollout:** Given the clear positive impact, consider accelerating adoption of the new procedure across remaining branches.

2. **Monitor long-term effects:** Continue tracking service scores to determine whether the effect stabilizes, continues growing, or diminishes over time.

3. **Investigate mechanisms:** Conduct qualitative interviews with branch staff to understand *why* the new procedure works (e.g., faster restocking, better organization, reduced errors) to refine implementation.

4. **Cost-benefit analysis:** Quantify the operational costs of the new procedure against the service score gains to assess overall value.

5. **Share best practices:** Document and disseminate implementation lessons from high-performing switched branches to support branches still transitioning.

*Analysis prepared using 12,053 weekly observations from 709 branches. Standard errors clustered at the branch level to account for repeated measures.*
