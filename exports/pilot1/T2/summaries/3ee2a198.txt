# **Analysis of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**
## *Impact on Library Service Scores*

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

The new procedure is associated with a **2.15-point improvement** in service scores, but this effect is **not statistically significant** (p=0.16). While the direction of the effect is positive as expected, we cannot confidently conclude that the new procedure caused a meaningful change in service quality. The data is consistent with anything from a small negative effect to a moderate positive effect.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### 1. Study Design
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6-22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure (treatment group)
- **14 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Switch implemented at **week 15**

### 2. Service Score Changes

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 47.97 | 53.25 | **+5.28** |
| Non-switched branches | 48.35 | 51.49 | **+3.13** |
| **Difference (DiD Effect)** | | | **+2.15** |

### 3. Statistical Results
- **Point estimate:** +2.15 service score points (≈4.5% improvement)
- **95% Confidence Interval:** -0.84 to +5.13 points
- **P-value:** 0.158 (not significant at conventional levels)
- **Pre-trend test:** No significant difference in trends before the switch (p=0.25), supporting the validity of the analysis

### 4. What This Means
- Both groups improved over time, but **switched branches improved slightly more**
- However, the difference is **not large enough to rule out chance** as an explanation
- The confidence interval suggests the true effect could reasonably be:
  - As low as **-0.84 points** (slight harm)
  - As high as **+5.13 points** (meaningful improvement)

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

1. **Continue monitoring** — Collect more weeks of data to increase statistical power. With the current sample size, we can only detect fairly large effects.

2. **Consider implementation fidelity** — Investigate whether all switched branches fully adopted the new procedure. Inconsistent implementation could dilute the measured effect.

3. **Examine heterogeneous effects** — Analyze whether the procedure worked better at certain types of branches (e.g., by size, location, or staffing levels).

4. **Review complementary metrics** — The analysis controlled for staffing and visit indices, but other operational measures may provide additional insight.

5. **Cost-benefit assessment** — Even if the effect is real at ~2 points, evaluate whether the operational costs of the new procedure justify this level of improvement.
