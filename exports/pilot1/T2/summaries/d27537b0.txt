# **Board Summary: Impact of New Shelving and Rotation Procedure on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and rotation procedure **improved service scores by approximately 1.7 points** (about 3.5% relative to baseline). This effect is **statistically significant** (p=0.034), meaning we can be confident the improvement is real and not due to random variation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. The Intervention Worked**
- Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by **1.69 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure
- The 95% confidence interval is **0.13 to 3.26 points**, indicating the true effect is very likely positive
- This represents roughly a **3.5% improvement** over the baseline service score of ~49 points

### **2. Study Design Was Strong**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** switched to the new procedure at week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure as a control group
- Pre-intervention trends were **parallel** between groups (p=0.75), validating our comparison approach
- The analysis controlled for branch-specific characteristics and week-to-week variation

### **3. No Major Confounding Factors**
- Staffing levels did not change differently between treatment and control branches (p=0.83)
- Customer visits showed a slight (non-significant) decline in treatment branches post-switch (p=0.09), which if anything would *understate* the true service score improvement

### **4. Service Score Trends**

| Period | Control Branches | Treatment Branches |
|--------|-----------------|-------------------|
| Before (weeks 6–14) | 49.0 | 53.5 |
| After (weeks 15–22) | 47.8 | 54.0 |
| **Change** | **–1.2** | **+0.5** |

The treatment branches maintained their scores while control branches declined, resulting in a net gain of 1.7 points for the new procedure.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Consider system-wide rollout**: Given the positive and statistically significant effect, expanding the new procedure to remaining branches is warranted.

2. **Monitor implementation quality**: Track whether the effect size holds as more branches adopt the procedure. Some branches may need additional training or support.

3. **Investigate mechanisms**: The improvement could stem from faster item location, better stock organization, or reduced staff time spent shelving. Understanding the "why" could help optimize the procedure further.

4. **Track long-term effects**: Continue monitoring service scores to ensure the benefit persists beyond the initial adoption period.

5. **Cost-benefit analysis**: Compare the 1.7-point service score gain against any implementation costs (training, temporary disruption, etc.) to confirm the return on investment.
