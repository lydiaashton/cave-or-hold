# **Board Memo: Impact of New Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure is associated with a **statistically significant increase of approximately 1.5 points** in service scores for branches that adopted it, relative to branches that continued with the old procedure. However, **important caveats** apply: branches that switched had systematically lower service scores before the change, and the treatment effect only became clearly evident in the final weeks of observation. We recommend proceeding with cautious optimism while continuing to monitor outcomes.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Main Result: Positive Treatment Effect**
- Using a difference-in-differences analysis with branch fixed effects, branches that switched to the new procedure saw service scores increase by **1.5 points more** than non-switching branches after the change (p < 0.001)
- This represents approximately **16% of one standard deviation** in service scores, or about **3% of the average score**
- The effect remains significant after controlling for weekly staffing and visitor volume

### **2. Pre-Existing Differences Between Groups**
- **Critical caveat**: Branches that switched to the new procedure had systematically **lower service scores** before the intervention
- In the 8 weeks before the switch, switching branches scored an average of **1.3 points lower** than non-switching branches
- Five of the eight pre-switch weeks showed statistically significant differences
- This violates the "parallel trends" assumption required for causal inference, suggesting the estimated effect may be partially driven by **mean reversion** (lower-scoring branches naturally improving over time)

### **3. Timing of the Effect**
- The event study analysis shows the treatment effect was **not immediate**
- Weeks 15-21 (the first 7 weeks after switching): effects ranged from 0.2 to 1.1 points, none statistically significant
- **Week 22** (the final week): effect of 2.0 points, statistically significant (p < 0.001)
- This pattern suggests either a delayed effect or that more time is needed to observe the full impact

## **Next Steps**

### **1. Continue Monitoring**
- Extend the observation period to determine if the treatment effect grows over time or stabilizes
- Track whether the week 22 result represents a sustained improvement or temporary fluctuation

### **2. Investigate Pre-Existing Differences**
- Analyze why switching branches had lower baseline scores
- Consider whether branch characteristics (size, location, demographics) explain both the selection into switching and baseline performance
- If possible, match switching and non-switching branches on observable characteristics for a more apples-to-apples comparison

### **3. Mechanism Analysis**
- Examine whether the effect varies by branch type, size, or other characteristics
- Survey branch staff to understand implementation challenges and benefits
- Analyze whether staffing and visitor patterns mediate the effect

### **4. Consider Phased Rollout Evaluation**
- If the procedure continues to show positive results, document best practices from early adopters
- Consider randomizing the rollout order for future procedural changes to enable cleaner causal inference
