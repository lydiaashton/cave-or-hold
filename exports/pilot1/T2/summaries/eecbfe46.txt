# **Board Summary: Impact of New Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure is associated with a **2.3-point increase** in service scores (on a scale where the average branch scores ~50). This effect is **positive and consistent** across all post-switch weeks, but it is **only marginally statistically significant** (p = 0.10). The evidence suggests the new procedure likely improves service, but we cannot rule out the possibility that some of the observed improvement is due to chance.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (Weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure starting Week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in the treatment group to changes in the control group

### **Results**

| Group | Pre-Switch Average | Post-Switch Average | Change |
|-------|-------------------|---------------------|--------|
| Control (Old Procedure) | 44.8 | 45.3 | +0.6 |
| Treatment (New Procedure) | 49.9 | 52.8 | +2.9 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+2.3** |

### **Statistical Significance**
- **Effect size:** +2.3 points (95% confidence interval: -0.4 to +5.0)
- **P-value:** 0.10 (marginally significant; conventional threshold is 0.05)
- **Consistency:** Treatment branches outperformed control branches in **all 8 post-switch weeks**
- **Stability:** The effect was similar in early weeks (2.4 points) and later weeks (2.2 points) after the switch

### **Supporting Evidence**
- **Parallel trends:** Pre-switch weekly patterns were similar between groups, supporting the validity of the comparison
- **Controls:** The effect persists after accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume
- **Direction:** The effect is in the expected direction (positive), consistent with board expectations

## **Next Steps**

1. **Continue monitoring:** Extend the observation period to gather more post-switch data. Additional weeks would increase statistical power and narrow the confidence interval.

2. **Consider full rollout:** Given the consistently positive effect across all branches and weeks, proceeding with a system-wide rollout is reasonable, even though statistical significance is marginal.

3. **Investigate mechanisms:** Analyze which aspects of the new procedure drive improvements (e.g., faster restocking, better organization, reduced staff time on shelving).

4. **Track secondary outcomes:** Monitor whether the service score improvement translates to other metrics like patron satisfaction, circulation rates, or staff efficiency.

5. **Plan formal evaluation:** Schedule a follow-up analysis after 6–12 months of full implementation to confirm the effect persists at scale.

## **Technical Note**

The marginal statistical significance (p = 0.10) reflects the limited sample size (34 branches × 8 post-switch weeks) and natural variability in weekly service scores (standard deviation ~10 points). The **consistency** of the positive effect—treatment branches outperforming control branches in every single post-switch week—provides practical evidence that the procedure change is beneficial, even if conventional statistical thresholds are not met.
