# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure produced a **modest positive effect** on service scores (+1.8 points on average), but this effect is **not statistically significant** at conventional levels (p=0.16). We cannot confidently conclude that the new procedure improved service quality based on this analysis. The expected significant effect was not observed.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15 (treatment group)
- **14 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses difference-in-differences methodology with branch fixed effects and clustered standard errors

### **Main Results**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Treatment effect estimate | **+1.79 points** |
| Standard error | 1.26 |
| p-value | 0.156 |
| 95% Confidence Interval | [-0.68, +4.27] |

### **Supporting Evidence**
1. **Parallel trends assumption validated**: Pre-switch trends show no significant differential patterns between groups (p=0.48), supporting the validity of our comparison.

2. **Baseline differences**: Treatment branches had higher service scores before the switch (54.4 vs. 48.1), but this is accounted for in the analysis.

3. **Post-switch patterns**: The event study shows variable week-to-week effects after the switch, with some weeks showing positive effects (weeks 1, 3, 4, 5, 7) and others showing no discernible impact.

4. **Control group decline**: Control branch scores decreased slightly after week 15 (48.1 → 46.6), while treatment branch scores remained stable (54.4 → 54.6).

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Continue monitoring**: Extend the observation period to determine if effects emerge with longer exposure to the new procedure.

2. **Investigate heterogeneity**: Analyze whether the procedure works better for certain branch types, sizes, or staffing levels.

3. **Qualitative feedback**: Gather staff and patron input on the new procedure to identify implementation challenges or unintended consequences.

4. **Consider power**: With only 8 post-switch weeks and 34 branches, the study may be underpowered to detect modest effects. A longer evaluation period would improve precision.

5. **Cost-benefit assessment**: Even if the effect is real (~+2 points), evaluate whether the operational costs of the new procedure justify the benefit.

---

## **Technical Notes**

- Analysis controls for weekly staffing and visit volume indices
- Standard errors clustered at the branch level to account for within-branch correlation
- Branch fixed effects control for time-invariant branch characteristics
- Service score average across all branches: ~51.6 (scale where 50 is average)
