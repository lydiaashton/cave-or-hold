# **Library Branch Stock Rotation: Impact of New Procedure on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure is associated with a **modest improvement in service scores** (+2.3 points on average), but the evidence is not statistically conclusive. The effect represents approximately a **5% improvement** relative to typical branch performance. While results are promising, we recommend continued monitoring before making a system-wide rollout decision.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** participated in the study over 17 weeks (Weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure starting Week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure
- This quasi-experimental design allows us to isolate the effect of the new procedure by comparing changes in the treatment group against the control group

### **Results**

| Metric | Control Group (Old) | Treatment Group (New) |
|--------|---------------------|----------------------|
| Pre-switch average (Weeks 6–14) | 44.8 | 49.9 |
| Post-switch average (Weeks 15–22) | 45.3 | 52.8 |
| Change | +0.6 | +2.9 |

**Estimated Effect of New Procedure: +2.3 points** (95% confidence interval: -0.5 to +5.1)

### **Statistical Interpretation**
- The p-value of 0.10 indicates **marginal statistical significance**—there is suggestive but not definitive evidence of a positive effect
- The confidence interval includes zero, meaning we cannot rule out the possibility of no effect
- However, the interval also excludes large negative effects, suggesting the new procedure is unlikely to be harmful

### **Validity Check**
- **Parallel trends assumption is satisfied**: Before the switch, both groups showed similar weekly trends in service scores (p=0.89 for trend difference). This supports the validity of our comparison.
- **Visits are a strong predictor**: Higher visitor volume is significantly associated with better service scores, which we accounted for in our analysis.

## **Next Steps**

1. **Extend the observation period**: Collect 8–12 additional weeks of data to increase statistical power and determine whether the effect becomes more precisely estimated.

2. **Investigate heterogeneity**: Analyze whether the new procedure works better for certain types of branches (e.g., by size, location, or baseline performance) to identify best-fit use cases.

3. **Monitor implementation fidelity**: Ensure branches adopting the new procedure are following it consistently; variation in implementation could be masking the true effect.

4. **Consider cost-benefit analysis**: Even a modest 5% improvement may be worthwhile if the new procedure has low implementation costs. Compare the magnitude of improvement against training time, staffing changes, and other resource requirements.

5. **Pilot expansion**: Consider expanding the new procedure to additional branches on a trial basis while continuing to collect rigorous data.

---

## **Technical Notes**

- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch)
- Results are robust to controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume
- Service scores have a typical range of ~30–70 with an overall mean of 48.7 and standard deviation of 10.4
