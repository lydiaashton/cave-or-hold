# **MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative

**Subject:** Impact Assessment of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores—but the effect took time to materialize.** Branches that switched to the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2 points** (about 4% relative to the average score of 51) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. However, this improvement was not immediate; significant gains only appeared 4+ weeks after implementation, suggesting staff needed time to adapt to the new workflow.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **1. Overall Treatment Effect**
- **Estimated impact:** +1.97 points on the service score index (95% CI: 1.37 to 2.56)
- **Statistical significance:** Highly significant (p < 0.001)
- **Practical significance:** Represents a ~4% improvement over the average branch score of 51

### **2. Effect Timeline (Critical Finding)**
The new procedure did **not** produce immediate benefits:
- **Weeks 15–18 (first 4 weeks post-switch):** No statistically significant improvement
- **Weeks 19–22 (weeks 5–8 post-switch):** Clear positive effects emerged, ranging from +1.3 to +1.9 points

This pattern suggests a **learning/adaptation period** where staff became proficient with the new procedures before benefits materialized.

### **3. Study Design & Data**
- **709 branches** total: 425 switched to new procedure, 284 kept old procedure
- **17 weeks** of data (weeks 6–22), with switch occurring at week 15
- Analysis controlled for branch-specific characteristics, staffing levels, and visit volumes
- Standard errors clustered at the branch level to account for repeated measures

### **4. Methodological Note**
Pre-switch trends showed that treatment branches started with lower scores early in the study period but converged with control branches by weeks 12–14. This means the simple before/after comparison may overstate the effect slightly. The event study analysis (tracking effects week-by-week) provides more reliable evidence that the **true effect is positive and emerged gradually** after implementation.

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

### **Immediate Actions**
1. **Continue the new procedure** at switched branches—the data supports its effectiveness
2. **Plan rollout to remaining 284 branches**, but budget for a 4–5 week adaptation period before expecting improvements
3. **Communicate expectations** to branch managers: improvements should not be expected immediately; allow staff time to master the new workflow

### **Further Analysis Recommended**
1. **Cost-benefit analysis:** Quantify the operational costs of the new procedure against the service score gains
2. **Longer-term tracking:** Monitor whether effects persist, grow, or diminish beyond the 8-week post-switch window
3. **Branch-level heterogeneity:** Investigate whether certain branch types (size, location, staffing) benefit more or less from the new procedure
4. **Implementation quality:** Assess whether variation in how branches adopted the procedure explains differences in outcomes

### **Questions for Operations Team**
- What training or support was provided during weeks 15–18?
- Were there implementation challenges that delayed benefits?
- Can we identify best practices from branches that showed earlier improvements?

---

## **CONCLUSION**

The new rotation and shelving procedure **does improve service quality**, but leadership should anticipate a **one-month ramp-up period** before benefits become visible. With proper change management and realistic expectations, the cooperative can expect meaningful service score improvements across all branches as the rollout continues.
