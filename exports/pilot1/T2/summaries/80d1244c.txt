# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure shows a **positive but statistically inconclusive effect** on service scores. Branches that switched to the new procedure experienced an estimated **1.8-point increase** in service scores compared to branches that kept the old procedure. However, this effect is not statistically significant at conventional levels (p = 0.17), meaning we cannot confidently rule out that the observed difference is due to random variation rather than the procedure change itself.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** total: 20 switched to the new procedure, 14 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks** of data (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring in week 15
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in switched branches against changes in non-switched branches

### **Service Score Results**

| Period | Control Branches | Switched Branches | Gap |
|--------|-----------------|-------------------|-----|
| Before switch (weeks 6–14) | 48.1 | 54.4 | 6.2 |
| After switch (weeks 15–22) | 46.6 | 54.6 | 8.0 |
| **Change** | **-1.5** | **+0.2** | **+1.8** |

- The **1.8-point estimated effect** represents the additional improvement in switched branches relative to control branches
- 95% confidence interval: approximately **-0.8 to +4.4 points**
- Switched branches consistently outperformed control branches throughout the study (by about 6 points even before the switch)

### **Validity Checks**
- **Pre-trend test passed**: Treatment and control branches showed parallel trends before the switch (p = 0.18), supporting the validity of the comparison
- **Control variables matter**: Higher staffing and visit levels are significantly associated with better service scores, and the analysis accounts for these factors

### **What This Means**
- The new procedure **may provide a modest benefit** (roughly 1.8 points on a ~50-point scale, or about 3–4%)
- However, the evidence is **not strong enough** to conclude the procedure caused the improvement with confidence
- The effect could plausibly range from slightly negative to moderately positive

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Continue monitoring**: Collect additional weeks of data to increase statistical power and obtain a more precise estimate of the effect

2. **Consider branch-level variation**: Investigate whether certain types of branches (by size, location, or staffing) benefit more from the new procedure

3. **Evaluate cost-benefit**: If the new procedure requires additional resources, weigh the uncertain 1.8-point benefit against implementation costs

4. **Explore complementary factors**: Since staffing and visit levels significantly affect service scores, consider whether the new procedure works better when combined with adequate staffing

5. **Decision guidance**: 
   - If the new procedure is **low-cost and easy to implement**, the positive (though uncertain) effect may justify broader rollout
   - If the new procedure is **costly or disruptive**, consider waiting for more conclusive evidence before expanding
