# **Board Summary: Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure **significantly improved service scores**. Branches that switched to the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **2.1 points** (approximately 4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This result is statistically significant and robust across multiple analytical approaches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **709 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **425 branches** adopted the new procedure; **284 branches** continued with the old procedure
- The switch occurred in **week 15**, creating a natural experiment for comparison
- Analysis used a **difference-in-differences** approach with branch fixed effects to control for time-invariant branch characteristics

### **Main Results**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Treatment Effect** | **+2.08 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | [1.55, 2.61] |
| Statistical Significance | p < 0.001 |
| Baseline Average Score | ~51 points |
| Relative Improvement | ~4% |

### **Supporting Evidence**

1. **Before the switch** (weeks 6–14): Treatment and control branches had similar trends, with treatment branches averaging 51.6 and control branches averaging 51.0 (a small 0.6-point difference).

2. **After the switch** (weeks 15–22): Treatment branches improved to 52.8, while control branches declined slightly to 50.0.

3. **The gap widened**: The treatment effect represents the additional improvement in switched branches beyond what would have occurred naturally.

4. **Robustness**: Results hold when controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume, and when accounting for individual branch characteristics.

### **Additional Insights**

- **Staffing and visits matter**: Both staffing index and visits index positively correlate with service scores (each 1-point increase associates with ~0.5 point higher service score).
- **Control group decline**: Non-switching branches saw a slight decline (-0.95 points) after week 15, suggesting external factors may have negatively affected all branches, making the treatment effect even more notable.

## **Next Steps**

### **Immediate Actions**
1. **Consider system-wide rollout**: Given the positive and statistically significant impact, evaluate expanding the new procedure to remaining branches.

2. **Investigate implementation factors**: Analyze whether certain branch characteristics (size, location, staffing) moderate the treatment effect to optimize rollout strategy.

3. **Monitor sustainability**: Continue tracking service scores to ensure the improvement persists beyond the initial 8-week post-switch period.

### **Further Analysis Recommended**
1. **Cost-benefit analysis**: Quantify the resources required to implement the new procedure versus the value of improved service scores.

2. **Mechanism analysis**: Understand *why* the new procedure works—whether through faster service, better organization, or improved staff efficiency.

3. **Longer-term evaluation**: Extend the study period to assess whether effects accumulate, plateau, or diminish over time.

---

## **Methodology Note**

This analysis used a quasi-experimental difference-in-differences design with branch fixed effects and clustered standard errors. The parallel trends assumption was reasonably satisfied, with only minor pre-period trend differences between groups. Results were consistent across specifications with and without controls for staffing and visitor volume.
