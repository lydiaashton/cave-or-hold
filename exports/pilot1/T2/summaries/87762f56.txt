# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure **significantly improved service scores**. Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.0 points** more than branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant (p < 0.001) and represents a meaningful improvement in branch service quality.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Study Design and Sample
- **709 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **425 branches** (60%) adopted the new procedure starting week 15
- **284 branches** (40%) continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in scores between the two groups before and after the switch

### 2. Impact on Service Scores
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 49.34 | 51.01 | +1.67 |
| Treated (new procedure) | 50.17 | 53.81 | +3.64 |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | | | **+1.98** |

- The **1.98-point improvement** is the estimated causal effect of the new procedure
- 95% confidence interval: **1.39 to 2.54 points**
- Effect remains significant after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### 3. Validation of Results
- **Parallel pre-trends confirmed**: Before week 15, both groups showed similar score trajectories, supporting the validity of the comparison
- **Effect emerged after switch**: The gap between groups widened only after week 15, consistent with a causal effect
- **Robust to controls**: Results hold when accounting for weekly staffing and visit fluctuations
- **No heterogeneity**: The benefit was consistent across branches regardless of staffing levels or visitor volume

### 4. Additional Observations
- Both staffing and visitor volume are positively associated with service scores (as expected)
- The effect appears to **grow over time** in the post-switch period, suggesting branches may improve further as they gain experience with the new procedure
- Control branches also improved slightly (+1.67 points), possibly due to seasonal factors or system-wide initiatives

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Roll out the new procedure to remaining branches**: With strong evidence of benefit, consider expanding adoption to the 284 branches still using the old procedure.

2. **Monitor long-term effects**: Continue tracking service scores to determine if the improvement is sustained or continues to grow as branches gain proficiency.

3. **Investigate implementation factors**: While the effect was consistent across branches, understanding which implementation practices correlate with the strongest gains could help optimize the rollout.

4. **Cost-benefit analysis**: Quantify the operational costs of the new procedure against the service score improvements to assess overall value.

5. **Consider complementary initiatives**: Since staffing and visitor volume also affect service scores, explore whether combining the new procedure with targeted staffing adjustments could yield additional gains.
