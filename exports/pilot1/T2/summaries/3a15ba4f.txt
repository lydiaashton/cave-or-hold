# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and rotation procedure is associated with a **2.2-point improvement** in service scores (approximately 4.5% above baseline). However, this result is **not statistically significant** at conventional levels, meaning we cannot confidently conclude the procedure change caused the improvement. The data is consistent with both a modest positive effect and no effect at all.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **What We Analyzed**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Used a difference-in-differences approach to isolate the effect of the procedure change from other factors (like general time trends and branch-specific characteristics)

### **Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 48.4 | 51.5 | +3.1 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 48.0 | 53.2 | +5.3 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+2.2** |

- **Estimated effect:** +2.16 points on the service score index
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.17 (not significant at the 5% level)
- **95% confidence interval:** -0.9 to +5.2 points

### **What This Means**
The confidence interval tells us the true effect could reasonably be:
- As high as a **5-point improvement** (meaningful gain)
- As low as a **1-point decline** (small negative effect)
- Or anywhere in between, including **no effect**

### **Supporting Evidence**
- **Parallel trends check:** Before the switch, treatment and control branches showed similar trends (p = 0.11), supporting the validity of our comparison
- **Control variables:** Higher staffing and visit levels are associated with better service scores, and our model accounts for these factors
- **Time effect:** All branches saw score improvements after week 15 (+3.1 points for control branches), suggesting broader factors (seasonal, systemic) also influenced scores

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Continue monitoring.** Collect more weeks of data to increase statistical power. With additional observations, we may be able to detect a significant effect if one exists.

2. **Consider a phased rollout evaluation.** If resources allow, randomly assign remaining branches to treatment vs. control to strengthen causal inference.

3. **Investigate heterogeneity.** Some branches may benefit more than others. Analyzing effects by branch size, location, or baseline performance could reveal where the procedure works best.

4. **Qualitative feedback.** Supplement quantitative scores with staff and patron feedback to understand *how* the procedure affects service quality.

5. **Cost-benefit analysis.** Even if the effect is modest, assess whether the operational benefits (efficiency, staff satisfaction) justify continued implementation.

---

## **Technical Notes**

- Analysis uses branch fixed effects to control for time-invariant branch characteristics
- Standard errors are clustered at the branch level to account for repeated observations
- Model controls for weekly staffing and visit levels
- Service score baseline average: ~48 points (higher is better)
