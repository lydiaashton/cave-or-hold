# **Board Memorandum (Final)**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**We cannot confidently conclude the new procedure caused the improvement.** While branches that switched show higher service scores after the change, **these branches were already improving relative to others before the switch**. The gap between switching and non-switching branches was widening by 0.32 points per week *before* week 15, but only 0.16 points per week *after*. This suggests the observed difference may reflect pre-existing factors (e.g., better management, more resources) rather than the procedure itself. **We recommend pausing the system-wide rollout until we can obtain clearer evidence.**

---

## **Final Verdict**

### **DO NOT APPROVE SYSTEM-WIDE ROLLOUT AT THIS TIME**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did service scores improve in switching branches? | **Yes** — but they were already improving before the switch |
| Can we attribute this to the new procedure? | **No** — pre-existing trends explain the pattern |
| Is the evidence sufficient for cooperative-wide investment? | **No** — risk of spending resources on an ineffective change |
| Should we abandon the new procedure entirely? | **No** — it may still help; we simply lack proof |

**Rationale:** The fundamental requirement for causal inference — that treatment and control groups were on parallel trajectories before the intervention — is not met. Rolling out the procedure system-wide based on current evidence risks investing in a change that may deliver no benefit.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **The Critical Issue: Pre-Existing Trends**

| Period | Gap Trend (Treatment vs. Control) |
|--------|-----------------------------------|
| Weeks 6–14 (Before switch) | **+0.32 points/week** |
| Weeks 15–22 (After switch) | **+0.16 points/week** |

**The gap was widening faster BEFORE the switch than after.** This violates the "parallel trends" assumption required for valid causal inference.

### **Weekly Gap Between Treatment and Control Branches**

| Week | Gap (Treatment − Control) |
|------|---------------------------|
| 6 | −0.26 |
| 10 | +1.47 |
| 14 (last pre-switch) | +2.55 |
| 15 (first post-switch) | +2.62 |
| 19 | +3.81 |
| 22 | +3.28 |

**The immediate jump at week 15 was only 0.07 points** (from 2.55 to 2.62), not the 1.87 points the initial model suggested.

### **What the 1.87-Point Estimate Actually Captures**

The statistical model attributed ~1.87 points to the new procedure, but this likely reflects:
- Pre-existing differences between branches that chose to switch vs. those that didn't
- Ongoing trends that began before week 15
- Possible confounding factors (management quality, staffing stability, community demographics)

---

## **Alternative Explanations**

Branches that adopted the new procedure may differ systematically:
- **More proactive leadership** (willing to try new approaches)
- **Better baseline resources** (enabling both adoption and higher scores)
- **Different community characteristics** (affecting both adoption decisions and service scores)

Without random assignment, we cannot separate these factors from the procedure's actual effect.

---

## **Recommendations**

### **1. Do NOT Proceed with System-Wide Rollout (Yet)**

The current evidence is insufficient to justify the cost and disruption of cooperative-wide implementation.

### **2. Pursue Cleaner Evidence**

Options for stronger causal inference:
- **Randomized rollout**: Randomly assign remaining 284 branches to switch now vs. later
- **Staggered implementation**: Track branches as they switch at different times
- **Qualitative investigation**: Interview high-performing switching branches to understand what else may be driving results

### **3. Continue Monitoring Existing Adopters**

- Track whether the 425 switching branches maintain their advantage
- Investigate whether any switching branches saw scores *decline* after adoption
- Gather staff feedback on the new procedure's practical impact

### **4. Revisit Decision in 8–12 Weeks**

With additional data and potentially a randomized design, we can provide a more definitive recommendation.
