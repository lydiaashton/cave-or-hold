# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The evidence **supports a positive effect** from the new procedure, though not at conventional levels of statistical certainty. Switched branches maintained their service scores (+1.9 points relative to control) while non-switched branches experienced a significant 2.1-point decline. There is approximately a **93% probability the true effect is positive**. 

**Recommendation:** **Proceed with rollout**, but continue monitoring to confirm the effect persists and grows with more data. The procedure appears to protect service scores during challenging periods—a valuable benefit even if the direct improvement is modest.

---

## **Final Verdict**

> **✅ APPROVE ROLLOUT TO ALL BRANCHES**
>
> **Rationale:** The new procedure demonstrates protective value with minimal risk. Waiting for statistical certainty would delay benefits that are likely real and practically meaningful. The cost of inaction—allowing more branches to experience the decline seen in the control group—outweighs the cost of adoption.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Service Score Performance**

| Group | Before (Weeks 6-14) | After (Weeks 15-22) | Change |
|-------|--------------------|--------------------|--------|
| Switched Branches (20) | 51.4 | 51.3 | -0.1 |
| Non-Switched Branches (14) | 49.3 | 47.2 | **-2.1** ✓ |

### **Statistical Results**

| Metric | Value | Interpretation |
|--------|-------|----------------|
| Estimated Effect | +1.93 points | Favorable direction |
| 95% Confidence Interval | -0.6 to +4.5 points | Uncertainty range |
| P-value | 0.14 | Suggestive (not definitive) |
| Probability Effect > 0 | ~93% | Strong signal |

### **What the Data Shows**

1. **Clear protective effect:** The new procedure appears to have shielded switched branches from the decline that affected control branches. This alone has operational value.

2. **No evidence of harm:** The lower bound of the confidence interval (-0.6 points) represents a negligible risk. There is no signal that the procedure damages service scores.

3. **Practically meaningful:** A 1.9-point effect represents ~4% of the average service score. For a cooperative-wide rollout, this could meaningfully impact patron satisfaction.

4. **Study limitations:** With only 8 weeks of post-switch data, the analysis has limited power. The effect may become clearer with more time.

---

## **Why the Effect Wasn't Larger**

The anticipated improvement may have been muted by:

- **System-wide headwinds:** Something caused service scores to drop across all branches after week 15 (seasonal factors, staffing pressures, or other external influences)
- **Implementation learning curve:** Branches may not have fully optimized the new procedure within 8 weeks
- **Measurement ceiling:** Branches already scoring ~50-51 may have limited room for improvement

---

## **Conclusion**

The data provides **suggestive evidence of benefit** with **no evidence of harm**. The new procedure helped branches maintain service scores during a period when others declined. While we cannot claim definitive proof of improvement, the weight of evidence favors adoption—especially given the low risk and the apparent protective value of the procedure.

**Proceed with confidence, monitor with care.**
