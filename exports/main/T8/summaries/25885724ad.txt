# **MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Impact of New Branch Shelving and Rotation Procedure on Service Scores

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure **protected service scores from a system-wide decline** that affected branches using the old procedure. Switched branches maintained their scores (average change: +0.15 points) while non-switched branches dropped 2.2 points. The net benefit is **2.3 points** (p < 0.001). 

**However, the new procedure did not actually improve scores in absolute terms.** Given our expectation of no difference, this protective effect is noteworthy but modest. We recommend **cautious rollout** with continued monitoring to understand what caused the system-wide decline and whether the new procedure's protective effect persists.

---

## **Key Findings**

### What Actually Happened

| Group | Pre-Switch | Post-Switch | Change |
|-------|-----------|-------------|--------|
| Switched branches (new procedure) | 52.15 | 52.30 | +0.15 |
| Non-switched branches (old procedure) | 52.72 | 50.56 | −2.17 |
| **Net Difference** | | | **+2.31** |

### Critical Context

1. **No absolute improvement**: Switched branches essentially held steady; they did not get better
2. **System-wide decline**: Something caused service scores to drop after week 14 across the cooperative
3. **Protective effect**: The new procedure appears to have shielded branches from this decline
4. **Mixed results**: Only 52% of switched branches saw any improvement; 48% still declined

### Statistical Summary
- Effect size: +2.31 points (95% CI: 1.0 to 3.5)
- Statistical significance: p < 0.001
- Pre-switch trends were parallel between groups (validating the comparison)

---

## **Final Verdict**

**RECOMMENDATION: PROCEED WITH CAUTIOUS ROLLOUT**

The new procedure provides real, statistically significant value—but it is **defensive, not transformative**. It protects against score declines rather than driving improvement. 

**Reasons to proceed:**
- The protective effect is real and meaningful (2.3 points)
- No evidence of harm from the new procedure
- In a period of system-wide stress, any protective measure has value

**Reasons for caution:**
- We do not understand what caused the system-wide decline
- Only about half of switched branches actually improved
- The effect may not persist long-term

**Suggested approach:** Approve rollout to remaining branches, but pair it with (1) investigation into the cause of the system-wide decline, and (2) a commitment to continue tracking scores for at least 6 more months to confirm the effect holds.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Investigate the decline**: Determine what caused system-wide scores to drop after week 14 (seasonal? staffing? external factors?)
2. **Pilot expansion**: Roll out to additional branches while monitoring closely
3. **Set realistic expectations**: Communicate that the new procedure maintains scores rather than improves them
4. **Longer-term tracking**: Continue monitoring for 6+ months to confirm the protective effect persists

---

## **Technical Notes**

- Difference-in-differences analysis with standard errors clustered by branch
- 150 branches, 17 weeks of data (weeks 6–22)
- Parallel trends assumption verified (p = 0.72 for pre-period trend differences)
- Results robust to controls for staffing and visitor volume
