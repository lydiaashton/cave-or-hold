# **Memorandum to the Board of Directors**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** [Current Date]  
**Subject:** Results of New Branch Procedure Pilot — **Do Not Proceed with Rollout**

---

## **Executive Summary**

**We cannot conclude the new procedure improved service scores.** While branches using the new procedure showed higher scores after the switch, **this difference was already emerging before the procedure changed**. The apparent 2-point improvement is almost entirely explained by pre-existing trends, not the procedure itself.

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

> ### ❌ **Do Not Proceed with System-Wide Rollout**
>
> **The data does not demonstrate that the new procedure causes improved service scores.** The observed difference between pilot and control branches existed before the intervention and reflects selection bias, not treatment effect. Rolling out based on current evidence would invest resources without proven benefit.

---

## **Why We Reached This Conclusion**

### The Critical Problem: Pre-Existing Trends

| Metric | Finding |
|--------|---------|
| **Pre-period gap trend** | Widening by 0.24 points per week (p = 0.001) |
| **Expected gap increase** (from pre-trend alone) | +1.91 points over 8 weeks |
| **Actual gap increase** (post-period) | +2.00 points over 8 weeks |
| **Effect attributable to procedure** | **~0.09 points** (statistically indistinguishable from zero) |

**Translation:** Of the apparent 2-point "improvement," **95% was already happening before the switch**. The procedure itself contributed essentially nothing.

### Visual Evidence

The gap between treated and control branches widened steadily from week 6 through week 22—with no noticeable acceleration at week 15 when the procedure changed. If the procedure caused improvement, we would expect to see the gap widen *after* week 15, not before.

---

## **What Went Wrong with the Initial Analysis**

The standard difference-in-differences method assumes treatment and control groups would have followed **parallel trends** absent the intervention. **This assumption is violated in our data.**

| Analysis Approach | Estimated Effect | Valid? |
|-------------------|------------------|--------|
| Simple DiD (ignores pre-trends) | +2.07 points | ❌ No |
| DiD accounting for pre-trends | +0.09 points | ✅ Yes |

The initial +2.07 point estimate was **misleading**. It captured pre-existing differences between branches, not the procedure's effect.

---

## **Why the Pilot Branches Were Different**

Branches selected for the pilot may have had:
- Better management or more experienced staff
- More resources or funding
- Higher baseline performance trajectory
- Different patron demographics

These factors—not the procedure—explain why their scores were rising faster than control branches both before and after week 15.

---

## **Recommendations**

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| **1** | **Halt rollout plans** | No proven benefit to justify investment |
| **2** | **Document selection criteria** | Understand why pilot branches differed from control |
| **3** | **Design randomized pilot** | Future tests should use random assignment for valid inference |
| **4** | **Extend data collection** | Continue tracking to see if any delayed effect emerges |
| **5** | **Consider alternative metrics** | Staff time, costs, or patron satisfaction may show different results |

---

## **Questions for the Board**

1. **How were pilot branches selected?** If they were chosen for being high-performing, this explains the pre-trend.
2. **What is the cost of the new procedure?** Without proven benefit, costs may outweigh any unmeasured advantages.
3. **Should we run a proper randomized trial?** This would give definitive evidence before any system-wide commitment.
4. **Are there qualitative benefits?** Staff or patron feedback might reveal advantages not captured in service scores.

---

## **Bottom Line**

**We wanted this to work. The data says it didn't.**

The new procedure shows no detectable effect on service scores beyond what was already happening. Proceeding with rollout would commit cooperative resources without evidence of return. We recommend pausing and designing a more rigorous evaluation before any further investment.
