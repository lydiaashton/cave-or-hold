# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores—contrary to initial expectations.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.2 points** (about **4.4%**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and consistent across most branches. 

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **60 branches** continued with the old procedure as a control group
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, isolating the procedure's effect from other factors

### Impact on Service Scores

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|---------------|---------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 50.65 | 52.54 | +1.89 |
| Control branches | 50.84 | 50.51 | -0.34 |
| **Net effect** | | | **+2.23** |

- The **2.23-point improvement** is statistically significant (p < 0.01)
- 95% confidence interval: **1.4 to 3.0 points**
- This represents a **4.4% improvement** relative to the average service score of 51.2

### Why We Can Trust This Result

1. **Parallel trends confirmed**: Before week 15, both groups followed nearly identical trajectories—validating the comparison
2. **Not driven by staffing or visits**: Neither measure changed differently for switched branches, ruling out these alternative explanations
3. **Robust across methods**: The effect holds with branch fixed effects, week fixed effects, and controls for staffing/visits
4. **Broadly consistent**: 70% of switched branches saw improvement; only 30% saw no change or decline

### Unexpected Finding

The cooperative initially expected no measurable impact on service scores from this operational change. The positive effect suggests the new procedure may improve patron experience in ways not anticipated—possibly through faster item location, better shelf organization, or reduced staff time spent on restocking.

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Criterion | Assessment |
|-----------|------------|
| **Statistical significance** | ✓ Confirmed (p < 0.01, even with clustered errors) |
| **Research design validity** | ✓ Confirmed (parallel pre-trends hold) |
| **Effect magnitude** | ✓ Meaningful (4.4% improvement, ~0.2 SD) |
| **Consistency** | ✓ Most branches benefited (70%) |
| **Alternative explanations** | ✓ Ruled out (staffing, visits unchanged) |

**VERDICT: The evidence supports adoption.**

The data provides credible, statistically significant evidence that the new procedure improves service scores. While the effect is modest, it is real, consistent, and achieved without additional staffing or resources. The initial expectation of "no difference" was reasonable—but the evidence now points the other way.

**Confidence level: High**
