# **BOARD MEMORANDUM**

## **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

**Date:** November 2025  
**To:** Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Subject:** **Caution Recommended on System-Wide Rollout**

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**We cannot confidently conclude the new procedure caused the improvement.** While switched branches did see higher scores after adoption, the gap between switched and non-switched branches was **already widening before the switch occurred**. This undermines our ability to attribute the improvement to the new procedure. **We recommend pausing the rollout decision pending further evidence.**

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

| Question | Verdict |
|----------|---------|
| Did service scores improve in switched branches? | **YES** — clear improvement observed |
| Can we credit the new procedure? | **NO** — evidence is insufficient |
| Should we roll out system-wide now? | **NO** — risk of costly mistake |
| Is the procedure likely harmful? | **NO** — no negative effects detected |
| Recommended action | **PAUSE and TEST** with randomized trial |

**Bottom line:** The new procedure may work, but this study does not prove it. Rolling out based on current evidence would be acting on faith, not data.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **1. The Critical Problem: Pre-Existing Trends**

The treatment and control groups were **not on parallel trajectories** before the switch:

| Week | Gap (Treatment − Control) |
|------|---------------------------|
| Week 6 | 1.07 points |
| Week 10 | 2.44 points |
| Week 14 (last pre-switch) | 3.74 points |

**The gap widened by 2.67 points BEFORE the switch.** This means switched branches were already improving relative to control branches for reasons unrelated to the new procedure.

### **2. Revised Effect Estimates**

| Method | Estimated Effect |
|--------|------------------|
| Original DiD (weeks 6-14 avg vs 15-22 avg) | +1.94 points |
| **Conservative DiD (Week 14 baseline only)** | **+0.57 points** |

The more defensible estimate—using the immediate pre-switch week as baseline—suggests an effect **less than one-third** of our initial claim.

### **3. What We Actually Know**

| Fact | Interpretation |
|------|----------------|
| Switched branches improved after week 15 | ✓ Confirmed |
| Control branches also improved (just less) | ✓ Confirmed |
| The gap was widening BEFORE the switch | ⚠️ **Confounds attribution** |
| True causal effect could be small or zero | ⚠️ **Cannot rule out** |

---

## **WHY THIS MATTERS**

Our initial analysis assumed switched and non-switched branches would have followed similar trends absent the intervention. **This assumption does not hold.** The switched branches may have been:
- Better-resourced branches selected for early adoption
- Branches already on an improvement trajectory
- Branches with management more receptive to change

Any of these factors could explain the observed improvement without the procedure itself being the cause.

---

## **RECOMMENDATION**

**Do not approve system-wide rollout at this time.** Instead:

| Action | Rationale |
|--------|-----------|
| **Pause rollout decision** | Evidence is insufficient for causal claim |
| **Run a randomized trial** | Randomly assign remaining branches to switch or not |
| **Collect 8+ more weeks of data** | Allow clearer separation of trend vs. treatment effect |
| **Investigate selection criteria** | Understand why certain branches switched first |

---

## **LESSONS LEARNED**

- Difference-in-differences requires parallel pre-trends—this was not verified before the initial analysis
- Selection bias in which branches switched first confounds the results
- Future policy tests should use random assignment where feasible

---

**We apologize for the initial overconfident conclusion. The data does not support a definitive claim that the new procedure caused the improvement.**
