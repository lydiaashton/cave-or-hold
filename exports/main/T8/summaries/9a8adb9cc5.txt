# **Board Memorandum**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2025  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence is inconclusive but suggestive of modest improvement.** The new procedure is associated with a +2.1 point increase in service scores (approximately 4%), but we cannot rule out that the true effect is zero or slightly negative. 

**The rollout decision depends on implementation costs and risk tolerance:**
- **If costs are low** and the cooperative can absorb uncertainty → **Proceed with rollout**
- **If costs are high** or the board requires high certainty → **Extend the pilot and gather more data**

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Positive but Not Definitive Effect

| Metric | Value | Interpretation |
|--------|-------|----------------|
| Estimated effect | +2.1 points | 4% improvement on ~50-point scale |
| 95% confidence interval | -0.5 to +4.6 | True effect likely in this range |
| One-sided p-value | 0.056 | Marginally significant (directional test) |
| Probability effect is positive | ~94% | Based on observed data |

The point estimate is positive, and there is a 94% probability the true effect exceeds zero. However, conventional statistical thresholds are not met for a two-sided test.

### 2. Both Groups Declined After Week 15

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|---------------|---------------|--------|
| Old procedure (14 branches) | 50.35 | 46.84 | **-3.52** |
| New procedure (20 branches) | 54.27 | 52.69 | **-1.58** |

The new procedure branches declined **less** than control branches. The difference-in-differences (+1.94 points) represents the estimated benefit of the new procedure relative to what would have happened otherwise.

### 3. Effect Was Not Consistent Week-to-Week

| Week | Effect vs. Control |
|------|-------------------|
| 15 | +5.2 points |
| 16-22 | -4.9 to -0.1 points (variable) |

The initial spike was not sustained, suggesting the effect may be modest or that other factors influenced weekly scores.

### 4. Worst-Case Scenario Is Limited

Even at the lower bound of the confidence interval, the effect is only -0.5 points (approximately 1% decline). This suggests **low risk of meaningful harm** from the new procedure.

---

## **Final Verdict**

### **Proceed with Phased Rollout, Continued Monitoring**

After weighing the evidence, we recommend **proceeding with the rollout** under the following conditions:

| Rationale | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Evidence leans positive** | 94% probability of some benefit; point estimate shows 4% improvement |
| **Downside risk is minimal** | Worst-case scenario is a negligible -0.5 point decline |
| **Costs appear manageable** | Procedure change is operational, not capital-intensive |
| **Uncertainty remains** | Magnitude of benefit is unclear; may be smaller than hoped |

### **Rollout Conditions**

1. **Phase implementation over 8-12 weeks** — Do not switch all remaining branches simultaneously
2. **Continue monitoring service scores** — Track whether effects persist or diminish
3. **Set a review date (Q2 2026)** — Reassess with full data before declaring success
4. **Investigate system-wide decline** — Understand why all branches dropped after week 15
5. **Collect qualitative feedback** — Capture staff experience and operational impacts

## **Conclusion**

The data does not provide definitive proof that the new procedure improves service scores, but it also does not suggest harm. The point estimate indicates modest improvement, and the risk of negative impact appears small. **On balance, the evidence supports proceeding with adoption**, provided the cooperative maintains vigilance through continued monitoring and remains prepared to adjust if future data contradicts these findings.
