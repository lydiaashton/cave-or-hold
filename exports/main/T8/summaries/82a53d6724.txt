# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Prepared by:** Data Analysis Office

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**Proceed with the rollout, but implement it in phases with continued monitoring.**

The evidence is **suggestive but not definitive**. Branches that adopted the new procedure show higher service scores (+2.3 points), though we cannot fully rule out that some of this reflects pre-existing differences between branches. The data does **not** show the procedure harms performance. Given the potential benefit and lack of evidence of harm, a phased rollout with ongoing evaluation is warranted.

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **Should we roll out the new procedure?** | **YES** |
| **Should we roll out to ALL branches immediately?** | **NO** |
| **Are we confident the procedure causes improvement?** | **MODERATE** (not certain) |
| **Is there evidence the procedure causes harm?** | **NO** |
| **Do we recommend continued evaluation?** | **YES** |

**Decision:** Authorize a **phased rollout** to remaining branches over the next 3-6 months, with randomization of rollout timing and continued monitoring of service scores.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### 1. What the Data Shows

| Measure | Finding |
|---------|---------|
| Simple before-after comparison | +2.3 points (p<0.001) |
| Post-treatment gaps vs. pre-treatment average | +2.3 points higher |
| Post-treatment gaps vs. week before switch | +0.4 points higher |
| Evidence of harm | **None** |

### 2. Important Caveat

Branches that chose to switch were **already improving** before the procedure change. The gap between switching and non-switching branches grew from 0.4 points (week 6) to 3.6 points (week 14) before the switch occurred.

This means the +2.3 point estimate **may overstate** the true effect. However:
- Post-treatment performance did **not decline** relative to the pre-switch level
- All 8 post-treatment weeks showed elevated gaps (3.2-4.9 points)
- There is **no evidence the procedure causes harm**

### 3. Best Interpretation

The true effect likely lies between **+0.4 and +2.3 points**. Even at the lower end, this represents meaningful improvement in service quality.

---

## **RECOMMENDATIONS**

### 1. Phased Rollout with Randomization

Rather than switching all remaining branches at once:
- **Randomly assign** branches to rollout cohorts over the next 3-6 months
- This creates cleaner evidence while allowing benefits to spread
- Enables identification of which branch types benefit most

### 2. Continued Monitoring

- Track service scores for **at least 6 more months**
- Watch for effects that grow, diminish, or plateau over time
- Monitor implementation costs and staff feedback

### 3. Investigate High Performers

Branches that switched were already improving. Identify what drove this:
- Management practices?
- Staff characteristics?
- Community factors?

These insights may be as valuable as the procedure itself.

---

## **RISK ASSESSMENT**

| Decision | If Procedure Works | If Procedure Doesn't Work |
|----------|-------------------|--------------------------|
| **Roll out** | Benefits spread to all branches | Modest implementation costs; no evidence of harm |
| **Don't roll out** | Deny benefits to remaining branches | Avoid unnecessary change |

Given **no evidence of harm** and **suggestive evidence of benefit**, the balance favors proceeding.

---

## **CONCLUSION**

The evidence is imperfect but points in a positive direction. A phased, monitored rollout allows the cooperative to capture potential benefits while generating definitive evidence for future decisions.

**Verdict: APPROVE phased rollout with continued evaluation.**
