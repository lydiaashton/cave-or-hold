# **MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** [Current Date]  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure  

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new shelving and stock rotation procedure improved service scores by approximately 2 points.** This finding is statistically significant and contradicts the initial expectation that the procedure change would make no difference.

**Recommendation:** Approve system-wide rollout, with continued monitoring to confirm results replicate across all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Impact on Service Scores

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Branches keeping old procedure | 47.6 | 48.4 | +0.8 |
| Branches with new procedure | 50.8 | 53.7 | +2.9 |
| **Effect of new procedure** | | | **+2.1** |

### Statistical Confidence

- **Confidence level:** 99% (p < 0.001)
- **Confidence interval:** 1.2 to 3.1 points
- **Effect size:** 0.22 standard deviations (small-to-moderate)

### Study Quality

- **150 branches** participated (90 switched, 60 did not)
- **17 weeks** of data per branch (9 weeks before, 8 weeks after)
- **Parallel trends confirmed:** Treatment and control groups had similar trends before the switch (p=0.48), supporting causal interpretation
- **Complete data:** No branches dropped out

---

## **Important Context**

**Baseline differences:** Branches selected to switch had higher service scores at the start (51.6 vs. 48.5). This suggests non-random assignment—possibly better-performing branches were chosen to pilot the new procedure. The analysis accounts for this through branch-level controls, but it warrants caution in interpreting results.

**Modest effect:** The 2-point improvement represents 4% of the average service score. Whether this is operationally meaningful depends on your performance targets and the costs of implementation.

**Limited follow-up:** We have only 8 weeks of post-switch data. Long-term sustainability is unknown.

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Criterion | Assessment |
|-----------|------------|
| **Statistical evidence** | ✓ Strong—effect is real, not random noise |
| **Causal validity** | ✓ Supported—parallel trends assumption holds |
| **Effect magnitude** | ⚠ Modest—meaningful but not transformative |
| **Generalizability** | ⚠ Uncertain—switching branches were higher-performing at baseline |
| **Long-term sustainability** | ? Unknown—only 8 weeks of post-switch data |

**Overall: Proceed with rollout, but monitor closely.**

The evidence supports that the new procedure improves service scores. However, given the non-random selection of switching branches and modest effect size, the board should:

1. Approve rollout to remaining branches
2. Track service scores for 8-10 weeks after each branch switches
3. Be prepared to adjust or pause if effects don't replicate in lower-performing branches

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure.

2. **Set up monitoring dashboard:** Track service scores by branch for the first 10 weeks after switching.

3. **Define success criteria:** Decide in advance what effect size would justify continuing vs. reconsidering the rollout (e.g., "If average effect falls below 1 point, pause and investigate").

4. **Document implementation:** Capture what switching branches did to succeed; share with branches yet to switch.

5. **Re-evaluate in 6 months:** Assess whether effects persist and whether rollout costs were justified.

---

## **Conclusion**

The data provides credible evidence that the new procedure improves service quality. While we expected no difference, the procedure delivered a measurable gain. The effect is modest but real. Rolling out system-wide is warranted, provided the board commits to monitoring and remains willing to adjust course if results don't replicate.
