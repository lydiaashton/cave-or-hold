# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Prepared by:** Data Analysis Team

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure improved service scores, supporting a system-wide rollout—with modest expectations.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by **2.3 points on average** (approximately 5% improvement). The effect is statistically significant and consistent across all post-implementation weeks. However, this is an incremental gain, not a transformation. The board should weigh this benefit against implementation costs before approving full rollout.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### Measured Improvement in Service Scores

| Measure | Result |
|---------|--------|
| Average effect | **+2.28 points** |
| 95% Confidence interval | **1.00 to 3.56 points** |
| Statistical significance | **p = 0.0005** |
| Effect size | **~5% of average score** (0.23 standard deviations) |

### What the Data Show

- **Treatment branches** (90 branches): Scores rose from 49.06 to 52.24 after switching (+3.18 points)
- **Control branches** (60 branches): Scores rose from 49.05 to 49.95 without switching (+0.90 points)
- **Attributable to new procedure**: 2.28 points (the difference-in-differences)

### Post-Switch Pattern

After week 15, treatment branches outperformed control branches **every single week**, with gaps ranging from +1.1 to +3.9 points. This consistency strengthens confidence in the finding.

### Validity Assessment

✓ **Parallel trends supported**: Pre-switch trends were statistically similar (joint test p = 0.45)  
⚠️ **One caveat**: Week 9 showed a marginally significant pre-period difference, though overall pre-trends appear balanced

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did the new procedure change service scores? | **Yes** |
| Direction of change? | **Positive** (scores increased) |
| Statistically significant? | **Yes** (p = 0.0005) |
| Practically meaningful? | **Modestly** (~5% improvement) |
| **Recommendation** | **Proceed with rollout** |

**Rationale:** The evidence is clear and robust—the new procedure improved service quality. While the effect is not transformative, a consistent 5% gain across 150 branches represents meaningful cumulative benefit to library patrons. The initial hypothesis (no effect) is rejected. Implementation should proceed with realistic expectations and continued monitoring.

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

1. **Quantify implementation costs** (training, temporary disruption, monitoring)

2. **Compare costs to benefits** (2.3-point service score gain across 60 remaining branches)

3. **If proceeding**: Develop phased rollout plan with clear success metrics

4. **Monitor post-rollout**: Track whether effects persist at scale

5. **Investigate mechanisms**: Understand what drove the improvement to reinforce best practices

---

## **TECHNICAL NOTES**

*Analysis uses difference-in-differences with branch and week fixed effects. Standard errors clustered by branch. The event study design confirms effects emerged after (not before) the policy change. Full technical documentation available upon request.*
