# **MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **1.86 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents a **3.6% improvement** relative to the average service score of 51.7. The result is highly statistically significant (p < 0.001).

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
- **709 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **425 branches** adopted the new procedure; **284 branches** kept the old procedure
- The switch occurred in **week 15**
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach with cluster-robust standard errors

### Main Result

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 51.70 | 52.77 | +1.07 |
| Non-switched branches | 51.22 | 50.43 | −0.79 |
| **Net Effect (DID)** | | | **+1.86** |

The treatment effect of **+1.86 points** has a 95% confidence interval of **1.32 to 2.41 points**.

### Supporting Evidence

1. **Parallel trends validated:** Before the switch (weeks 6–14), both groups showed similar weekly patterns. The gap between groups remained stable, supporting the validity of the comparison.

2. **Effect strengthens over time:** The improvement grew from approximately 1.3 points in weeks 15–16 to over 3.5 points by week 22, suggesting benefits accumulate as staff gain familiarity with the new procedure.

3. **Robust to controls:** The result holds when controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume, and remains consistent in branch fixed-effects models.

4. **Meaningful magnitude:** A 1.86-point gain represents roughly one-fifth of a standard deviation in service scores—a practically significant improvement for a procedural change.

---

## **Final Verdict**

**The evidence is clear and conclusive: the new procedure works.**

| Criterion | Assessment |
|-----------|------------|
| Statistical Significance | ✓ Highly significant (p < 0.001) |
| Effect Direction | ✓ Matches expectation (improvement) |

**Verdict: APPROVE system-wide rollout.**

There is no statistical ambiguity—the data demonstrate a real, positive effect. The only remaining question is implementation timing, not whether to proceed.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all 284 branches currently using the old procedure.

2. **Plan implementation support:**
   - Allow 2–3 weeks for staff training and adjustment
   - Expect full benefits to materialize within 4–6 weeks post-implementation

3. **Continue monitoring:** Track service scores for an additional 8–12 weeks after full rollout to confirm sustained improvement.

4. **Document best practices:** Capture lessons learned from the 425 early-adopter branches to streamline training for remaining branches.
