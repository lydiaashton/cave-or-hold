# **Memorandum to the Board**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Prepared by:** Data Analysis Team

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure **protected service scores from declining** during the post-switch period. Switched branches maintained their performance (+0.15 points) while control branches dropped significantly (−2.17 points). The net benefit of **2.3 points is statistically significant**, but this represents **stability rather than improvement**. We recommend **cautious expansion with close monitoring**, not an immediate full rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. What Actually Happened to Service Scores

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|----------------|---------------|--------|
| Branches with new procedure | 52.15 | 52.30 | **+0.15** |
| Branches with old procedure | 52.72 | 50.56 | **−2.17** |
| **Net difference** | | | **+2.31** |

**Critical insight:** The new procedure did not boost scores—it **prevented a decline** that affected branches using the old procedure. Something caused service scores to drop system-wide after week 14 (possibly seasonal, operational, or external factors), and the new procedure appears to have buffered switched branches from this effect.

### 2. The Effect Is Statistically Reliable

- **P-value < 0.001**: The difference is unlikely to be random chance
- **95% confidence interval**: 1.1 to 3.6 points of protection
- **Effect size**: 0.24 standard deviations (small-to-moderate)

### 3. Important Caveats

| Concern | Implication |
|---------|-------------|
| **We don't know why control branches declined** | The benefit may be context-specific; if whatever caused the decline doesn't recur, the advantage may disappear |
| **Only 8 weeks of post-switch data** | We cannot assess whether the protective effect persists long-term |
| **Effect varies by week** | Weeks 20-21 showed smaller protective effects, suggesting possible fade or instability |
| **Original hypothesis was "no difference"** | We rejected this, but the effect is protective, not enhancing |

### 4. Weekly Pattern After Switch

| Week | Control Avg | Switched Avg | Difference |
|------|-------------|--------------|------------|
| 15 | 53.1 | 55.1 | +2.0 |
| 16 | 51.0 | 53.4 | +2.4 |
| 17 | 48.0 | 50.6 | +2.6 |
| 18 | 49.8 | 52.0 | +2.2 |
| 19 | 51.6 | 53.3 | +1.8 |
| 20 | 50.8 | 50.9 | +0.1 |
| 21 | 50.8 | 51.9 | +1.1 |
| 22 | 49.3 | 51.1 | +1.8 |

The protective effect was strongest in weeks 15-19, weaker in weeks 20-21, and recovered somewhat in week 22. This variability warrants caution.

---

## **Recommendations**

| Priority | Action |
|----------|--------|
| **1** | **Do not yet approve full rollout**—investigate why control branches declined after week 14 |
| **2** | **Expand to additional branches** (e.g., 20-30 more) to test whether the protective effect replicates |
| **3** | **Monitor closely** for at least 12 weeks to assess stability of the effect |
| **4** | **Document the procedure** and identify what specific aspects may provide resilience |
| **5** | **Reconvene in 3 months** with additional data to make a final rollout decision |

---

## **Data and Methods (Summary)**

- **Dataset**: 150 branches, 17 weeks (weeks 6-22), 2,550 branch-week records
- **Treatment**: 90 branches adopted the new procedure starting week 15
- **Control**: 60 branches continued with the existing procedure
- **Analysis**: Difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **Did the new procedure change service scores?** | **Yes** — statistically significant effect (p < 0.001) |
| **Did scores improve?** | **No** — switched branches stayed flat; control branches declined |
| **Does the procedure have value?** | **Yes** — it provided resilience against an unexplained decline |
| **Should we roll out to all branches now?** | **Not yet** — expand cautiously while investigating the underlying cause |

**Decision:** **Approve phased expansion, not full rollout.** The procedure demonstrates real value as a protective measure, but we lack sufficient understanding of why the control group declined and whether the effect will persist. A measured approach—expanding to additional branches while monitoring outcomes and investigating the root cause of the system-wide decline—minimizes risk while allowing the cooperative to capture the demonstrated benefit.

---

## **Conclusion**

The new procedure demonstrably **protected branches from a service score decline** that affected the control group. However, because (a) switched branches did not actually improve, (b) we cannot explain why control branches declined, and (c) the effect shows some week-to-week variability, we recommend a **measured expansion with continued evaluation** rather than immediate system-wide rollout.

The procedure appears valuable as a **risk mitigation tool**, but more evidence is needed before committing all branches to the change.
