# **Board Summary: New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure produced a statistically significant improvement in service scores**, with switching branches gaining approximately **2.1 points** (about **4%**) relative to branches that kept the old procedure. The effect was consistent across all post-switch weeks and robust to controls for staffing and visitor volume.

**However, the effect size is modest, and one-quarter of switching branches saw little to no benefit.** Before approving system-wide rollout, the board should consider implementation costs, branch heterogeneity, and long-term persistence.

**Verdict: Approve rollout, but proceed conditionally.** Recommend a phased implementation with cost-benefit analysis and continued monitoring to confirm the effect holds and to identify which branches benefit most.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure at week 15; **60 branches** continued with the old procedure as a control group
- Analysis compares changes in switching branches against changes in non-switching branches (difference-in-differences method)

### **Results**

| Group | Pre-Switch Average | Post-Switch Average | Change |
|-------|-------------------|---------------------|--------|
| Branches that switched | 50.75 | 53.14 | **+2.39** |
| Branches that did not switch | 49.83 | 50.02 | +0.19 |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | | **+2.20** |

- **Estimated treatment effect: +2.1 points** on the service score index
- **Statistical significance: p < 0.001** (highly significant; less than 1 in 1,000 chance this result occurred by random variation)
- Effect represents a **4.2% improvement** relative to baseline service scores
- **75% of switching branches** showed positive improvements; median branch improvement was +2.4 points
- **25% of switching branches** saw minimal or negative effects (range: -5.7 to +10.0 points)

### **Confidence in Results**

✓ **Parallel trends validated:** Before the switch (weeks 6–14), both groups followed similar trends with no significant divergence, confirming the groups were comparable before the intervention

✓ **Effect persisted:** The improvement was consistent across all 8 post-switch weeks, ranging from +2.4 to +4.4 points each week

✓ **Robust to other factors:** Results hold when accounting for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume

✓ **Statistically rigorous:** Analysis used branch-level fixed effects and standard errors clustered by branch to account for repeated observations

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve phased rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure, starting with branches most similar to high-performing switchers

2. **Conduct cost-benefit analysis** — quantify implementation costs (training, labor, disruption) against the 4% service improvement demonstrated

3. **Investigate branch heterogeneity** — analyze which branch characteristics predict stronger effects to target rollout effectively

4. **Develop implementation support** — document practices from early-adopting branches to create training materials for remaining locations

5. **Monitor during and after rollout** — track service scores at each branch as they transition and continue monitoring early adopters to confirm long-term persistence

---

## **Technical Note**

*Analysis used difference-in-differences regression with branch fixed effects and cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch). The parallel trends assumption—the key requirement for causal inference in this design—was validated by examining pre-switch trends, which showed no significant divergence between treatment and control groups before the intervention (p = 0.24).*
