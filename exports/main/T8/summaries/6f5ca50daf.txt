# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence is inconclusive.** While branches that adopted the new procedure showed higher service scores after the switch (+1.96 points, p=0.003), there are concerning signs that this may reflect pre-existing trends rather than a true causal effect. The treatment group was already falling behind control branches before the switch (from +0.33 in Week 6 to -2.31 in Week 14), suggesting possible mean reversion. **We do not recommend a full rollout based on this evidence alone.** Additional data collection is warranted.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Statistical Result vs. Causal Confidence
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect | +1.96 points |
| Statistical significance | p = 0.003 |
| 95% Confidence Interval | [0.67, 3.25] |
| Pre-trend test | p = 0.076 (borderline) |

The result is statistically significant, but the borderline pre-trend test reduces confidence in a causal interpretation.

### 2. Pre-Trend Pattern Raises Concerns

**Treatment vs. Control Gap Before the Switch:**
| Week | Difference (Treatment - Control) |
|------|----------------------------------|
| 6 | +0.33 |
| 9 | -0.62 |
| 12 | -0.81 |
| 14 | **-2.31** |

The treatment group was deteriorating relative to control before the switch. The post-switch "improvement" may simply reflect a return to baseline rather than a procedure effect.

### 3. Limited Branch-Level Impact
- Only **41% of treatment branches** showed improvement after the switch
- **59% showed no improvement or decline**
- Effect size (2 points) is modest relative to normal weekly variation (5.5 points standard deviation)

### 4. Study Limitations
- Only **8 weeks of post-switch data** – too short to assess sustainability
- **150 branches** with substantial heterogeneity in response
- Cannot rule out mean reversion or unobserved confounders

---

## **Final Verdict**

### **DO NOT PROCEED WITH SYSTEM-WIDE ROLLOUT**

**Rationale:**

| Consideration | Assessment |
|---------------|------------|
| Statistical evidence | ✓ Significant (p=0.003) |
| Causal validity | ⚠️ Uncertain (pre-trend concern) |
| Effect size | ⚠️ Modest (4% improvement) |
| Consistency across branches | ✗ Weak (only 41% improved) |
| Alignment with expectations | ✗ Contradicts prior belief |
| Risk of wrong decision | ⚠️ Medium (costly if ineffective) |

**The statistically significant result is outweighed by concerns about causal validity.** The deteriorating pre-trend in the treatment group suggests the observed "effect" may be mean reversion rather than a true benefit from the new procedure. Given that the procedure was expected to have no service impact, acting on this evidence alone risks implementing an ineffective change across 60 additional branches.

---

## **Recommendation**

**Do not proceed with system-wide rollout at this time.** Instead:

1. **Collect more data** – Extend observation to 16–24 weeks post-switch to assess whether the effect persists or fades

2. **Investigate the pre-trend** – Work with operations to understand why treatment branches were falling behind before the switch

3. **Consider a randomized rollout** – If proceeding, randomly assign remaining branches to treatment/control to strengthen causal inference

4. **Revisit the expectation** – If the procedure was truly expected to have no service impact, investigate whether the observed effect is real or artifactual before acting on it

---

## **Technical Notes**

Analysis used a difference-in-differences regression with branch and week fixed effects, with standard errors clustered at the branch level to account for within-branch correlation. The model controls for time-invariant branch characteristics and common weekly shocks affecting all branches. The pre-trend test (p=0.076) is borderline, reducing confidence in the parallel trends assumption required for causal inference.
