# **New Shelving Procedure: Impact on Service Scores**
### *Analysis for Board Decision on System-Wide Rollout*

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence suggests the new procedure improved service scores, but with important limitations.** We estimate a **2.1 point improvement** (4%), but pre-existing trends in the switched branches mean the true effect could be smaller. The result is statistically significant but modest in practical terms.

**Recommendation: Proceed with cautious expansion, not full rollout.**

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **Did service scores improve?** | Yes — by approximately 2.1 points |
| **Is the improvement statistically reliable?** | Yes — p < 0.001 |
| **Can we confidently attribute this to the new procedure?** | **Partially** — pre-existing trends suggest some effect may be unrelated |
| **Is the effect large enough to justify system-wide costs?** | **Uncertain** — 4% improvement is modest; cost-benefit analysis needed |
| **Should we roll out to all branches now?** | **No** — expand to additional branches first, then reassess |

**Verdict: CONDITIONAL PROCEED**

The new procedure shows promise, but the evidence is not strong enough to justify immediate system-wide rollout. Expand to 50–100 additional branches with enhanced monitoring, then return to the board with 16+ weeks of data before approving full implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **What the Data Shows**
| Finding | Implication |
|---------|-------------|
| +2.1 point average improvement after switch | Positive signal |
| p < 0.001 (statistically significant) | Unlikely due to random chance |
| Treatment branches were already trending up before switch | **Some effect may be pre-existing momentum** |
| Effect size = 0.22 SD (small by convention) | Modest practical impact |
| Effect sustained across 8 weeks | Encouraging, but limited observation window |

### **What We Cannot Rule Out**
- The true effect may be **smaller than 2.1 points** due to pre-trend differences
- The improvement could **fade over time** as the novelty wears off
- **Unobserved factors** (e.g., management attention, seasonal effects) could explain part of the gain
