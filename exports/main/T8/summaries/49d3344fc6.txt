# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

## **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

### **BOTTOM LINE**

**The evidence is suggestive but not conclusive.** Branches that adopted the new procedure showed stronger service score improvements than non-switching branches. However, **the switching branches were already outperforming non-switchers before the change**, which limits our ability to attribute the improvement solely to the new procedure. We recommend a **cautious, monitored expansion** rather than immediate full rollout.

---

### **KEY FINDINGS**

**What the Data Shows**

| Metric | Finding |
|--------|---------|
| **Observed Difference** | +1.94 points higher for switched branches |
| **Statistical Significance** | p < 0.001 |
| **Critical Limitation** | Parallel trends assumption **not satisfied** |

**The Problem: Pre-Existing Differences**

The branches that switched were already on a stronger trajectory **before** adopting the new procedure:

| Week | Treatment - Control Gap |
|------|------------------------|
| Week 6 | +1.07 points |
| Week 10 | +2.44 points |
| Week 14 (last pre-switch) | +3.74 points |

**The gap was widening by ~0.35 points/week even before the switch** (p < 0.001). This means we cannot confidently separate the procedure's effect from the pre-existing trend.

---

### **INTERPRETATION**

**Two Possible Explanations:**

1. **Optimistic:** The new procedure added ~1.9 points on top of an already-positive trajectory
2. **Conservative:** The observed improvement is largely continuation of pre-existing momentum; the procedure itself may have little or no effect

**We cannot distinguish between these with the current data.**

---

### **RECOMMENDATIONS**

| Priority | Action |
|----------|--------|
| **1** | **Pause full rollout** pending stronger evidence |
| **2** | **Expand pilot** to a randomized subset of remaining branches |
| **3** | **Collect 3–6 more months of data** to assess sustained effects |
| **4** | **Re-analyze** with randomized design for causal clarity |

---

### **FINAL VERDICT**

**Do not proceed with system-wide rollout at this time.**

The data show a positive association between the new procedure and service scores, but the study design does not support a causal claim. Branches that opted into the new procedure were already improving faster than those that did not—suggesting they may have had other advantages (management quality, staff engagement, resources) that drove both their decision to switch and their performance gains.

**To move forward with confidence:**
- Randomly assign remaining branches to treatment vs. control
- This eliminates selection bias and isolates the procedure's true effect
- Cost of rigorous evaluation is small compared to risk of system-wide implementation of an ineffective change

**If the board chooses to proceed despite uncertainty:**
- Implement in phases with clear success metrics
- Set a threshold (e.g., +1.5 points sustained over 6 months) for continuation
- Be prepared to halt or modify if effects do not materialize

---

### **CONCLUSION**

The new procedure is associated with improved service scores, but **selection bias** prevents a definitive causal conclusion. A randomized expansion would provide the evidence needed for a confident system-wide decision. The board should weigh the cost of additional evaluation against the risk of scaling an unproven intervention.
