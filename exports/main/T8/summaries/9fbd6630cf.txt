# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure

**Date:** [Current Date]

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Proceed with rollout, but expect more modest gains than observed.** The new procedure is associated with a **2.0-point improvement** in service scores (4% gain, p = 0.003). However, because branches self-selected into adoption—and switching branches already scored 1.3 points higher before the change—the effect when rolling out to the remaining 60 branches may be smaller. A **phased rollout with monitoring** is recommended.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
- **150 branches** participated: 90 adopted the new procedure, 60 continued with the old
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Analysis compares service score changes between adopting and non-adopting branches

### Results Summary

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Branches that switched | 48.55 | 51.72 | +3.17 |
| Branches that did not switch | 47.24 | 48.35 | +1.12 |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | | **+2.04** |

### Statistical Evidence
- **Estimated impact: +2.04 points** on the service score index
- **95% confidence interval: 0.72 to 3.37 points**
- **Statistical significance: p = 0.003**
- **Effect size: 4.2% improvement** relative to baseline

### Why We Can Trust This Result
- Pre-switch trends were **parallel** between groups (p = 0.31), supporting the validity of the comparison
- Results hold when controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume
- The gap between groups widened consistently after the switch

### Important Limitation
**Branches self-selected into adoption.** Those that switched already had significantly higher service scores before the change (+1.3 points, p = 0.017). This suggests switching branches may be systematically different (e.g., more motivated staff, better management). The remaining 60 branches—which initially chose not to switch—may see smaller improvements than the 2.0 points observed here.

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did the new procedure improve service scores? | **Yes** – statistically significant positive effect |
| Should we roll it out to all branches? | **Yes, with caution** – expect more modest gains |
| How confident are we? | **Moderate** – strong statistics, but non-random adoption |

**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **phased rollout** to the remaining 60 branches over 8–12 weeks, monitoring service scores closely. If gains are smaller than expected, investigate implementation barriers before full expansion.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve phased rollout** to remaining branches, starting with 15–20 branches per month.

2. **Document implementation practices** from high-performing switched branches to share with adopting branches.

3. **Track service scores weekly** during rollout; report to the board if gains fall below 1.0 point.

4. **Conduct follow-up analysis** after full rollout to assess system-wide impact.

5. **Consider incentives or support** for branches that may need additional help implementing the new procedure.
