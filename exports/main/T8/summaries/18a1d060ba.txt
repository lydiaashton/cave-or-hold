# Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## Bottom Line

**The new procedure produced a statistically significant improvement in service scores** (+1.8 points, p = 0.001), contrary to initial expectations that it would have no effect. The finding is robust across multiple analytical specifications. **We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout, with continued monitoring.**

---

## Key Findings

### Study Overview
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure; **60 branches** maintained the old procedure
- The switch occurred in **week 15**
- Analysis compares service score changes between the two groups before and after the switch

### Results

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Treatment effect on service score | **+1.84 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | [0.76, 2.93] |
| P-value | 0.001 |
| Effect size (vs. baseline) | +3.7% |

### Interpretation
- Branches using the new procedure improved their service scores by **1.8 points more** than branches using the old procedure
- Both groups saw some improvement after week 15, but the treatment group improved significantly more
- The result holds after accounting for staffing levels and visit volumes
- Pre-switch trends were similar between groups (p = 0.36), supporting the validity of the comparison

---

## Final Verdict

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did the new procedure change service scores? | **Yes** |
| Is the effect statistically reliable? | **Yes** (p = 0.001) |
| Is the effect large enough to matter? | **Modestly** (+3.7% over baseline) |
| Should we roll out to all branches? | **Yes, with monitoring** |

**Rationale:** The evidence is strong enough to act on, but the unexpected nature of the finding warrants careful implementation tracking. The cost of rollout appears justified by the measurable improvement in patron service.

---

## Next Steps

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches
2. **Communicate findings** to branch managers, emphasizing the unexpected positive outcome
3. **Monitor post-rollout scores** for 8–12 weeks to confirm the effect holds at scale
4. **Document the new procedure** in detail to ensure consistent implementation
5. **Consider follow-up research** to understand *why* the new procedure works—this could reveal additional opportunities for improvement

---

## Technical Appendix (for reference)

- **Method:** Difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch)
- **Controls:** Staffing index and visits index included in the model
- **Data quality:** 2,550 observations; no missing values; parallel pre-trends confirmed (p = 0.36)
- **Statistical significance:** The treatment effect is significant at the 1% level (p = 0.001)
- **Robustness:** Effect size unchanged with or without control variables
