# **Library Cooperative Procedure Switch: Impact on Service Scores**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence suggests the new procedure improved service scores, but we cannot be certain.** Branches that switched saw scores rise 2.1 points more than non-switched branches. However, switched branches were already improving faster before the change, which clouds the conclusion. The result is **suggestive but not definitive**. We recommend a **phased rollout with careful monitoring** rather than immediate system-wide adoption.

---

## **Key Findings**

### What the Data Shows

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 51.2 | 55.1 | +3.9 |
| Non-switched branches | 49.1 | 50.8 | +1.8 |
| **Observed difference** | | | **+2.1** |

- **Statistical precision:** The 2.1-point difference is precisely estimated (95% CI: 1.8 to 2.5)
- **Broad-based:** 85% of switched branches improved vs. 68% of non-switched branches
- **Effect size:** ~0.2 standard deviations (small-to-moderate)

### Important Limitation

**Switched branches were already on a stronger trajectory before the procedure change.** This pre-existing difference means the 2.1-point gap may overstate the true causal effect. Because branches self-selected into switching (this was not randomized), we cannot rule out that motivated or better-managed branches both chose to switch *and* would have improved regardless.

---

## **Final Verdict**

**Proceed with cautious optimism.** The data provides meaningful evidence that the new procedure helps, but the study design cannot prove it definitively. The potential upside (improved service scores across 284 additional branches) outweighs the risk, provided implementation is monitored closely. **Recommendation: Approve phased rollout with explicit success metrics and a stop/go review point.**

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Phased rollout:** Expand to additional branches in stages (e.g., 50–75 branches per month), monitoring results before each phase

2. **Set success thresholds:** Define minimum acceptable improvement (e.g., 1.0 point average gain) for continuing the rollout

3. **Track implementation fidelity:** Document how thoroughly each branch adopts the new procedure to distinguish implementation issues from procedure effectiveness

4. **Investigate non-responders:** 15% of switched branches did not improve—understand why before scaling

5. **Extend observation period:** Collect more post-switch data to see if effects persist, grow, or fade

6. **Consider randomized evaluation:** For future policy changes, randomly assign branches to treatment/control to enable causal conclusions
