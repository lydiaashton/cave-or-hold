# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure **had a measurable positive effect** on service scores, contrary to the expectation of no difference. However, the benefit is modest: the new procedure appears to help branches **maintain service levels** rather than dramatically improve them. Branches that kept the old procedure saw scores decline, while switched branches held steady with a slight gain.

**Net effect:** +1.8 points (statistically significant, p < 0.001)

---

## **Final Verdict**

**Proceed with the rollout.** The evidence supports adopting the new procedure system-wide. While the improvement is modest, it is real, statistically robust, and comes at what appears to be minimal risk. The new procedure helps branches maintain service quality in conditions where the old procedure shows decline.

---

## **Key Findings**

### What Changed

| Group | Before | After | Change |
|-------|--------|-------|--------|
| Switched branches | 50.77 | 51.52 | +0.74 |
| Control branches | 51.24 | 50.19 | −1.05 |
| **Net effect** | | | **+1.79** |

### Statistical Evidence
- **Effect size:** +1.79 points (95% CI: 1.02 to 2.57)
- **Significance:** p < 0.001
- **Magnitude:** ~3.5% of average score, or 0.18 standard deviations

### Validity
- Pre-switch trends were parallel between groups (p = 0.33), supporting the analysis
- Effect persists after controlling for staffing and visitor volumes
- No evidence of confounding factors

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure

2. **Set realistic expectations** — communicate that this helps maintain service quality rather than transform it

3. **Monitor during implementation** — confirm the effect holds as more branches switch

4. **Investigate the control group decline** — understand why scores dropped in branches keeping the old procedure; this may reveal additional improvement opportunities

5. **Track costs** — ensure the modest benefit justifies any implementation or operating cost differences

---

*Analysis based on weekly service score data from 150 branches over 17 weeks, using difference-in-differences regression with branch and week fixed effects.*
