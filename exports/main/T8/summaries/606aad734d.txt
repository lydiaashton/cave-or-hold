# **Board Summary: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure shows a statistically significant positive effect on service scores, but the magnitude is modest.** Branches using the new procedure scored approximately **1.9 points (3.8%) higher** than expected based on control branch trends. We recommend a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than immediate systemwide implementation. This approach allows the cooperative to verify benefits at scale before full commitment, particularly since implementation costs and operational requirements are not yet quantified.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Positive but Modest Treatment Effect
- Branches using the new procedure saw service scores **1.9 points higher** than expected based on control branch trends
- This effect is **statistically significant** (p = 0.002), meaning we can be highly confident it's not due to random chance
- However, the effect size is **0.18 standard deviations** — meaningful but not transformative
- The 95% confidence interval ranges from **+0.7 to +3.1 points**

### 2. The New Procedure Protected Against Decline
The benefit appears to come from preventing deterioration rather than driving improvement:
- **Control branches** (old procedure): Scores **declined by 1.7 points** after week 15
- **Treatment branches** (new procedure): Scores **remained stable** (+0.2 points) after week 15

This suggests the new procedure helps branches maintain service quality during periods when scores would otherwise drop.

### 3. Valid Comparison with Some Caveats
- **Parallel trends confirmed**: Before the switch, both groups showed similar week-to-week patterns, validating our comparison method
- **Baseline differences exist**: Treatment branches scored ~1.6 points higher than control branches even before the switch, suggesting branches may not have been randomly selected to switch
- **Sample size**: 150 branches over 17 weeks (2,550 observations), with 102 branches using the new procedure

### 4. Additional Insights
- **Staffing matters**: Higher staffing index is strongly associated with better service scores (each 1-point increase correlates with ~0.94 point increase in service score)
- **Seasonal patterns**: Service scores fluctuate considerably by week across all branches, suggesting external factors (seasonality, holidays, etc.) influence performance
- The treatment effect persists even after accounting for staffing and visit variations

---

## **Final Verdict**

**Proceed with cautious optimism.** The evidence supports that the new procedure has a real, positive effect on service scores. However, given the modest effect size and unanswered questions about implementation costs, we recommend:

1. **Phased rollout** to remaining branches over 6-12 months rather than immediate systemwide implementation
2. **Cost-benefit analysis** before full commitment — quantify training time, operational changes, and any resource requirements
3. **Continue monitoring** service scores during rollout to confirm the effect replicates at scale
4. **Consider staffing levels** when implementing — the new procedure may work best when adequate staffing is in place

The new procedure is unlikely to harm performance and may provide meaningful protection against service score declines. Full systemwide adoption is warranted if implementation costs are low to moderate.

---

## **Next Steps**

2. **Quantify implementation costs** — training, materials, staff time, any equipment needs

3. **Monitor during rollout** — track service scores monthly to verify effect persists

4. **Investigate the staffing relationship** — determine if the new procedure requires minimum staffing levels to be effective

---

## **Technical Notes**

Analysis used a difference-in-differences approach comparing branches that switched procedures (treatment) to those that did not (control), before and after week 15. Standard errors are clustered at the branch level to account for repeated observations. The parallel trends assumption was tested and supported (pre-treatment interaction p = 0.875).
