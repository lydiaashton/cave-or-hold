# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Prepared by:** Data Analysis Team

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The evidence indicates the new procedure improves service scores, but the Board should consider implementation costs before approving system-wide rollout.**

Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2 points** (about 4%) relative to branches that kept the old procedure. The effect is statistically significant and unlikely to be due to chance. However, this analysis does not include cost data, and the study has some limitations the Board should weigh.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### What the Data Shows

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| New Procedure (90 branches) | 51.7 | 53.5 | +1.8 |
| Old Procedure (60 branches) | 50.2 | 50.1 | −0.1 |
| **Estimated Effect** | | | **+2.0 points** |

- **Statistical confidence:** p = 0.001; 95% CI = 0.8 to 3.1 points
- **Effect size:** Moderate (Cohen's d = 0.56)
- **Pre-switch trends:** Parallel between groups, supporting causal interpretation

### Important Limitations

1. **Not a randomized trial:** Branches were not randomly assigned to switch. If higher-performing or better-resourced branches were more likely to switch, the effect could be overstated.

2. **Short follow-up period:** Only 8 weeks of post-switch data. We cannot confirm whether improvements persist long-term or represent a temporary "novelty effect."

3. **No cost data:** This analysis measures service score impact only. If the new procedure requires additional staff time, training, or resources, the net benefit is unknown.

4. **Modest effect size:** A 2-point gain is meaningful but not transformative. The Board should consider whether this improvement justifies system-wide implementation costs.

5. **Unknown mechanism:** We do not know *why* the new procedure works. Understanding the mechanism could help optimize implementation or identify which branches would benefit most.

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did the new procedure change service scores? | **Yes** — statistically significant improvement |
| Is the effect large enough to matter? | **Moderately** — ~4% gain, meaningful but not transformative |
| Can we trust the causal interpretation? | **Reasonably** — design and validity checks support it |
| Should the Board approve rollout? | **Yes, with conditions** — proceed pending cost analysis and phased implementation |

---

## **RECOMMENDATION**

The data supports proceeding with rollout, **conditional on the following**:

1. **Complete a cost-benefit analysis** comparing implementation costs against the value of a 4% service score improvement

2. **Consider a phased rollout** rather than immediate system-wide implementation, allowing for monitoring and adjustment

3. **Establish success metrics** for the rollout (e.g., minimum service score gain, timeline for expected improvement)

4. **Plan for ongoing evaluation** to confirm effects persist beyond the initial months
