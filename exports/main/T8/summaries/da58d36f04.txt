# **MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores and warrants system-wide rollout.** Branches that adopted the new procedure achieved service scores approximately **1.8 points higher** than they would have under the old procedure (p = 0.003). This is a real, statistically reliable effect. However, the improvement is modest and we have only 8 weeks of post-implementation data. We recommend proceeding with the rollout while continuing to monitor outcomes.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Measurable Improvement**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Treatment Effect | +1.84 points |
| 95% Confidence Interval | [0.64, 3.04] |
| P-value | 0.003 |
| Percentage Improvement | ~3.7% |

- The effect persists after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume.
- The 95% confidence interval does not include zero, indicating the result is statistically reliable.

### **2. What Changed**
| Group | Pre-Switch | Post-Switch | Change |
|-------|------------|-------------|--------|
| **Switched Branches** (n=90) | 48.88 | 50.12 | **+1.24** |
| **Control Branches** (n=60) | 50.41 | 49.81 | **-0.60** |

- Switched branches improved; control branches declined slightly.
- The net treatment effect is the difference: 1.24 − (−0.60) = **1.84 points**.

### **3. Valid Comparison**
- Pre-switch trends were parallel (p = 0.67), meaning treatment and control branches moved together before the intervention.
- This validates the difference-in-differences approach and supports causal interpretation.

### **4. Weekly Variation Post-Switch**
| Weeks | Treatment-Control Gap |
|-------|----------------------|
| 15-17 | +0.70 to +0.81 |
| 18-20 | -0.52 to -0.20 |
| 21-22 | +0.26 to +1.04 |

- The effect fluctuates week-to-week, suggesting possible seasonal factors or implementation variation.
- The average across all 8 weeks remains positive and significant.

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **Did the new procedure cause an improvement?** | **Yes** — statistically significant (p = 0.003) |
| **Is the effect large enough to matter?** | **Modest** — ~3.7% improvement, meaningful but not transformative |
| **Should we roll out to all branches?** | **Yes** — low-cost intervention with reliable positive effect |
| **Are there risks or caveats?** | **Yes** — effect varies week-to-week; ~33% of switched branches showed no improvement; only 8 weeks of post-data |

**Verdict: Proceed with rollout, with continued monitoring.**

---

## **Recommendations**

| Priority | Action | Timeline |
|----------|--------|----------|
| **1** | Approve system-wide rollout to remaining 60 branches | Immediate |
| **2** | Develop phased implementation schedule | 30 days |
| **3** | Track service scores for 8–12 weeks post-switch per branch | Ongoing |
| **4** | Investigate branches without improvement; provide targeted support | 60 days |
| **5** | Re-evaluate after 6 months with expanded data | 6 months |

---

## **Conclusion**

The new shelving and rotation procedure produces a **real, positive effect** on service scores. While the magnitude is modest and week-to-week variation exists, the statistical evidence is clear: branches that switched outperformed those that did not. The procedure requires no additional staffing or budget, making it a cost-effective improvement.

**We recommend approval for system-wide rollout**, with the understanding that ongoing data collection will help identify branches needing additional support and confirm whether gains persist long-term.

---

*Analysis based on 2,550 weekly observations from 150 branches. Methods: Difference-in-Differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors. Technical appendix available upon request.*
