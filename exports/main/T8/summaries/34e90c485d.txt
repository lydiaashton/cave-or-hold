# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 2025  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure **likely improved service scores by approximately 2 points (4%)**. While the result doesn't meet conventional statistical significance thresholds, there is a **95% probability the true effect is positive** and an **81% probability of at least a 1-point gain**. We recommend **proceeding with rollout** to all branches, with continued monitoring to confirm the effect at full scale.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Overview**
- **34 branches** tracked over 17 weeks (Weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure starting Week 15; **14 branches** maintained the old procedure as a control group
- Analysis compares service score changes between the two groups, isolating the effect of the new procedure

### **Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 50.89 | 50.90 | +0.01 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 49.69 | 51.72 | +2.03 |
| **Estimated Effect of New Procedure** | | | **+2.02 points** |

### **Statistical Evidence**

| Measure | Value | Interpretation |
|---------|-------|----------------|
| Point estimate | +2.02 points | Observed improvement |
| p-value | 0.11 | Does not meet 0.05 threshold (conservative test) |
| 95% confidence interval | -0.5 to +4.8 | Range of plausible true effects |
| **Probability effect is positive** | **95%** | **Strong evidence of benefit** |
| **Probability effect > 1 point** | **81%** | **Likely meaningful improvement** |
| Probability effect > 2 points | 55% | Roughly even odds of full observed gain |

**Why the p-value is conservative:** The analysis uses cluster-robust standard errors to account for repeated weekly observations from the same branches. This is methodologically correct but makes significance harder to achieve with only 34 branches. Without this adjustment, p=0.027 would be statistically significant.

### **Weekly Pattern**

Before Week 15, treatment branches scored 1–2 points below control branches on average. After switching to the new procedure, treatment branches closed this gap and began outperforming control branches in most weeks. Pre-trends testing confirms the groups were on parallel trajectories before the switch, validating our comparison.

---

## **Final Verdict**

### **Recommendation: PROCEED WITH ROLLOUT**

**Rationale:**

| Consideration | Assessment |
|---------------|------------|
| **Direction of effect** | ✓ Positive (95% probability) |
| **Magnitude of effect** | ✓ Meaningful (~4% improvement on service index) |
| **Statistical certainty** | ⚠ Moderate (not definitive, but favorable odds) |
| **Implementation risk** | ✓ Low (procedural change, reversible if needed) |
| **Cost-benefit** | ✓ Favorable (operational change with potential service gain) |

**Decision Framework:**

For a low-risk operational change with no major capital investment, a 95% probability of positive impact represents sufficient evidence to proceed. The Board is not being asked to bet on a 2-point gain, but rather to accept that the **preponderance of evidence favors adoption**.

**If the Board requires higher certainty:**
- A phased rollout (5–10 branches per month) allows for course correction
- Continued monitoring can confirm whether effects persist at full scale
- The procedure can be modified or reversed if subsequent data contradicts these findings

---

## **Next Steps**

### **Immediate Actions**

1. **Authorize rollout** to remaining 14 branches over the next 2–3 months
2. **Document implementation learnings** from the 20 early-adopter branches to support training
3. **Establish weekly monitoring** of service scores during rollout

### **Review Checkpoint (8–12 weeks post-rollout)**

Reconvene to assess:
- Whether the 2-point improvement is sustained at full scale
- Whether any branches require additional support
- Whether operational costs align with expected benefits

## **Technical Notes**

Analysis uses a difference-in-differences design with branch fixed effects and cluster-robust standard errors. Full statistical output and data are available upon request.
