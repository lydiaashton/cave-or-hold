# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact Analysis**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure is associated with higher service scores — an unexpected but statistically robust finding.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.0 points** relative to branches that kept the old procedure (95% CI: 1.1 to 2.7 points; p < 0.001).

This represents about a **4% improvement** over the baseline. While the effect is clear in the data, it was not anticipated, and the board should weigh whether this magnitude of improvement justifies system-wide implementation.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rollout, but monitor closely to confirm the effect persists at scale.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **150 branches**: 90 adopted the new procedure, 60 kept the old procedure (control)
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22); switch occurred in week 15
- **Method:** Difference-in-differences with branch and week fixed effects

### Results

| Group | Before | After | Change |
|-------|--------|-------|--------|
| Control | 50.59 | 51.02 | +0.43 |
| Treatment | 50.26 | 52.62 | +2.36 |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | | | **+1.93** |

### Statistical Confidence
- **Effect estimate:** +1.93 to +1.98 points (across specifications)
- **95% confidence interval:** 1.1 to 2.8 points (excludes zero)
- **p-value:** < 0.001
- **Robust to:** branch fixed effects, week fixed effects, staffing/visits controls

### Design Validity
- **Pre-treatment balance:** Treatment and control branches had statistically equivalent scores before the switch (p = 0.56)
- **Parallel trends:** Weekly gaps between groups hovered around zero before week 15, then turned consistently positive afterward

---

## **Final Verdict**

**The evidence supports adopting the new procedure system-wide.**

The analysis demonstrates a **causal improvement** in service scores attributable to the new procedure. The effect is:
- ✓ Statistically significant (p < 0.001)
- ✓ Precisely estimated (tight confidence interval)
- ✓ Robust across multiple analytical approaches
- ✓ Consistent with a valid quasi-experimental design

While the 4% improvement was unexpected and is modest in magnitude, it represents a **real gain with no identified downside** in the data. The new procedure delivers measurable value and should be rolled out to all branches.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** to the remaining 60 branches

2. **Implement monitoring:**
   - Track service scores for 8–12 weeks post-implementation at each branch
   - Flag branches where scores do not improve as expected

3. **Investigate the mechanism:** Since this effect was unexpected:
   - Survey staff on how the new procedure affects their work
   - Document any indirect benefits to patron experience

4. **Follow-up evaluation:** Re-assess in 6 months to confirm sustained effects
