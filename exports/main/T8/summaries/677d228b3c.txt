# **Board Summary: New Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure shows a **positive but statistically uncertain** effect on service scores. The best estimate is a **2.5-point improvement** (5% of baseline), but the 95% confidence interval ranges from **-0.4 to +5.3 points**. This means we cannot rule out no effect—or even a small negative effect—with standard statistical confidence. The evidence is **suggestive but not conclusive**.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **phased rollout** while collecting additional data to confirm the effect before committing to system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

| Metric | Result | What It Means |
|--------|--------|---------------|
| **Estimated effect** | +2.5 points | Best estimate of improvement |
| **95% Confidence interval** | -0.4 to +5.3 | True effect could be zero or slightly negative |
| **p-value** | 0.087 | 8.7% chance this result occurred by random variation |
| **Statistical significance** | Not significant at 5% level | Does not meet conventional evidence threshold |
| **Direction of effect** | Positive in all 8 post-switch weeks | Consistent with expected benefit |
| **Parallel trends** | ✓ Confirmed | Validates the analysis approach |

### **The Evidence in Context**

- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure; **14 branches** kept the old procedure
- Switched branches improved by **+2.2 points**; non-switched branches were flat (**+0.0 points**)
- The effect is **economically meaningful** if real (5% improvement on a ~50-point scale)
- But the **statistical uncertainty is substantial**—we need more data to be confident

| If the true effect is... | Rollout impact | Probability |

| **-0.4 points** (lower bound) | Slight degradation | Possible but unlikely |

---

## **Recommendation: Phased Rollout with Monitoring**

Given the **uncertain but promising** evidence:

1. **Roll out to 5–7 additional branches** over the next 4 weeks (not all 14 at once)
2. **Collect 8–12 more weeks of data** to reduce statistical uncertainty
3. **Re-evaluate** with the expanded dataset before completing system-wide rollout
4. **Set a decision threshold**: Proceed with full rollout if the effect remains positive with p < 0.10

### **Why This Approach?**

- **Limits risk**: If the effect is zero or negative, fewer branches are affected
- **Builds evidence**: More data will narrow the confidence interval
- **Preserves option value**: Can stop or adjust if results disappoint
- **Aligns with evidence strength**: Matches the level of confidence the data supports

---

| Effect shrinks toward zero or turns negative | Pause and investigate; likely do not proceed |
| Effect remains positive but uncertain (p ≈ 0.05–0.10) | Continue phased rollout with ongoing monitoring |

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

### **✓ APPROVE PHASED ROLLOUT**

**Rationale:**

1. **The evidence points in the right direction.** All 8 post-implementation weeks show positive effects. The point estimate (+2.5 points) is meaningful for a service metric that averages ~50.

2. **There is no evidence of harm.** Staffing levels and branch visits did not change differentially, and no branch experienced a dramatic decline. The lower bound of the confidence interval (-0.4) suggests minimal downside risk.

3. **The cost of being wrong is asymmetric.** If we roll out and the effect is zero, we incur implementation costs but no service degradation. If we don't roll out and the effect is real, we deny patrons better service across 14 branches.

4. **More data will resolve the uncertainty.** A phased rollout with continued monitoring is a low-risk way to convert "suggestive" evidence into "conclusive" evidence within 2–3 months.

5. **This matches the strength of the evidence.** A definitive system-wide rollout would overstate our confidence. A phased approach respects the statistical uncertainty while acting on the best available information.
