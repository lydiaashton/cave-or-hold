# **Memorandum to the Board**

**Subject:** Analysis of New Branch Shelving and Rotation Procedure

**Date:** November 2026

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Verdict: Approve rollout, with monitoring.**

The evidence is clear and statistically robust: the new procedure improved service scores by approximately 2 points. While this outcome was unexpected, the data quality is strong and the effect is real. The board should approve system-wide adoption, conditional on tracking implementation costs and confirming the effect persists.

---

## **Key Findings**

### What the Data Shows

| Finding | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Estimated effect** | +2.03 service score points |
| **Statistical confidence** | 95% CI: 1.15 to 2.90 (does not include zero) |
| **P-value** | < 0.001 |
| **Effect size** | 0.2 standard deviations (~4% of baseline) |
| **Post-switch observation period** | 8 weeks |

### What Changed When

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Difference |
|-------|---------------|---------------|------------|
| Switched branches (n=90) | 51.1 | 53.6 | +2.5 |
| Non-switched branches (n=60) | 52.3 | 52.8 | +0.5 |
| **Net effect** | | | **+2.0** |

### Why This Analysis Is Credible

- **Parallel pre-trends**: Both groups moved together before the switch (p = 0.84)
- **Controls applied**: Results account for staffing, visitor volume, and branch-specific factors
- **Consistent signal**: Effect persists across the 8-week post-switch period
- **Tight confidence interval**: We can rule out both zero effect and trivially small effects

---

## **Important Considerations**

### Reasons for Caution

1. **Unexpected finding**: This procedure change was not designed to affect service scores. The mechanism is unclear and warrants investigation.

2. **Limited follow-up**: Only 8 weeks of post-implementation data. Effects could fade, grow, or prove unstable.

3. **Small-to-moderate effect**: 2 points on a ~50-point scale is meaningful but not transformative.

4. **Unknown costs**: This analysis does not address implementation costs, staff training time, or operational burden.

5. **No control group post-rollout**: If all branches switch, we lose the ability to monitor ongoing effects.

### Reasons for Confidence

1. **Statistically robust**: Multiple model specifications yield consistent results.

2. **Valid comparison**: Pre-treatment trends were parallel between groups.

3. **Practical significance**: A 4% improvement in service quality may matter to patrons.

4. **No evidence of harm**: Scores improved; no negative effects detected.

## **Conclusion**

This analysis began with the expectation that the procedure change would have no effect on service scores. The data proved otherwise. A 2-point improvement is real, statistically secure, and beneficial to patrons. 

The board should approve rollout while remaining attentive to implementation costs and long-term sustainability. This is a rare case where an operational change delivered unexpected value.
