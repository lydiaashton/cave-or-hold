# **Board Memorandum**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**We cannot confidently conclude the new procedure caused the improvement.** While branches using the new procedure show higher service scores, they were already outperforming control branches *before* the switch. The observed +2 point difference likely reflects **pre-existing differences between branches**, not the effect of the procedure itself. **We recommend against a full rollout until more rigorous evidence is gathered.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### The Critical Problem: Parallel Trends Violated

| Period | Gap (Switched − Control) |
|--------|--------------------------|
| Week 6–10 (early) | −0.2 to 0.0 (switched branches *behind*) |
| Week 11–12 | +0.2 to +0.4 (gap opens) |
| **Week 13–14 (just before switch)** | **+1.4 to +1.9** (large gap already exists) |
| Week 15 (first week after switch) | +1.6 (gap actually *shrinks*) |
| Week 16–22 | +2.2 to +3.1 (gap continues widening) |

**The gap was already +1.92 points in Week 14—the week BEFORE the switch.** In Week 15, when the new procedure began, the gap actually *dropped* to +1.61.

### What the Statistical Model Shows

| Metric | Result | Interpretation |
|--------|--------|----------------|
| DiD estimate | +2.07 points | Mathematically correct but **causally unreliable** |
| Pre-trend test | p = 0.010 | **Significant**—groups were diverging before intervention |
| Actual "jump" at switch | −0.30 points | No immediate effect visible |

---

## **What This Means**

The branches selected to switch to the new procedure were **systematically different** from those that kept the old procedure. They were already on an upward trajectory before the switch. The standard difference-in-differences analysis assumes both groups would have followed parallel trends without the intervention—**this assumption is violated.**

Possible explanations:
1. **Selection bias:** Better-performing branches were chosen to pilot the new procedure
2. **Anticipation effects:** Branches prepared for the switch in advance, improving scores before Week 15
3. **The procedure may still help,** but we cannot isolate its effect from these confounding factors

---

## **Recommendation**

| Option | Recommendation |
|--------|----------------|
| **Immediate full rollout** | ❌ **Not supported**—evidence is inconclusive |
| **Phased expansion** | ⚠️ **Only with randomized assignment** |
| **Gather better evidence first** | ✅ **Recommended** |

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Do not proceed with system-wide rollout** based on current evidence

2. **Design a randomized trial:** Randomly assign 100–150 branches to new vs. old procedure to eliminate selection bias

3. **Run the trial for 12–16 weeks** with weekly service score tracking

4. **Reconvene the Board** with results from the randomized evaluation before making a rollout decision

5. **If randomization is not feasible,** use a matched comparison design pairing similar branches on pre-switch performance trends

---

## **Final Verdict**

> **DO NOT ROLL OUT SYSTEM-WIDE AT THIS TIME.**
>
> The evidence does not support a causal conclusion that the new procedure improves service scores. The observed improvement is confounded by pre-existing differences between branches. Approving a full rollout based on this analysis would risk implementing an ineffective procedure across all 709 branches at unnecessary cost and operational disruption.
>
> **The Board should:** (1) halt the current expansion plan, (2) commission a properly randomized evaluation, and (3) revisit the rollout decision only after rigorous evidence demonstrates a causal benefit.

---

## **Conclusion**

The current analysis **cannot distinguish** whether the new procedure improves service scores or whether better-performing branches were simply selected to adopt it. The +2 point association is real, but the **causal effect is unknown**—it could be zero, modest, or even negative. 

**Prudent course:** Pause the rollout, gather rigorous evidence through randomization or careful matching, then decide.

---

*Analysis based on 12,053 weekly observations from 709 branches. The difference-in-differences identification assumption (parallel trends) is violated, limiting causal interpretation.*
