# **BOARD MEMORANDUM**

---

## **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

### **FINAL VERDICT**

> **DO NOT APPROVE system-wide rollout at this time.**
>
> The evidence is **insufficient to conclude the new procedure causes improved service scores**. Branches that adopted the procedure were already improving before the switch. Approving a cooperative-wide rollout based on current data risks investing significant resources in a procedure whose true impact is unknown.

---

### **BOTTOM LINE**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did switched branches improve? | **Yes** — scores rose after the switch |
| Did the procedure *cause* the improvement? | **Unknown** — pre-existing trends confound the result |
| Should we roll out to all 709 branches? | **No** — evidence does not support this decision |
| What should we do instead? | **Randomize and test** before committing |

---

### **KEY FINDINGS**

| Metric | Value | Interpretation |
|--------|-------|----------------|
| Raw DiD estimate | +1.81 points | Naive comparison only |
| Pre-period trend | +0.32 pts/week | **Significant divergence** (p < 0.001) |
| Pre-period gap change | -1.1 → +1.8 pts | Treatment branches gaining before switch |
| Expected post gap (trend only) | ~3.0 points | If no treatment effect |
| Actual post gap | ~2.1 points | **Lower than expected** |

---

### **CRITICAL ISSUE: VIOLATED ASSUMPTION**

The difference-in-differences method requires **parallel trends** between treatment and control groups absent the intervention. This assumption is **clearly violated**:

```
Pre-Period Treatment vs. Control Gap:
Week 6:   -1.1 points (treatment behind)
Week 10:  +0.1 points (caught up)
Week 14:  +1.8 points (treatment ahead) ← Just before switch
```

Branches that chose to switch were already on an upward trajectory. More concerning: if the pre-period trend had continued unchanged, we would have expected a ~3.0-point gap by mid post-period. The observed ~2.1-point gap is actually **lower** than this expectation.

---

### **WHAT THIS MEANS**

- The 1.8-point estimate likely **overstates** the true causal effect
- The procedure's actual impact could be **smaller, zero, or negative**
- Selection bias (motivated branches self-selecting into treatment) explains much of the observed difference

---

### **RECOMMENDED NEXT STEPS**

| Priority | Action | Timeline |
|----------|--------|----------|
| **1** | Pause system-wide rollout | Immediate |
| **2** | Randomly assign remaining 284 branches to treatment/control | 2–4 weeks |
| **3** | Monitor service scores for 8–12 weeks | Ongoing |
| **4** | Re-analyze with randomized design | After monitoring period |
| **5** | Return to board with causal estimate for final decision | 3 months |

---

### **ALTERNATIVE: CONTROLLED ROLLOUT**

If operational pressures require proceeding:

- Roll out to **50% of remaining branches** (randomly selected)
- Maintain the other 50% as a control group
- Commit to full rollout **only if** randomized evidence confirms positive effect
- This protects the cooperative from investing in an unproven procedure

---

### **CONCLUSION**

The board faces a choice:

| Path | Risk | Benefit |
|------|------|---------|
| **Approve now** | Wasted resources if procedure is ineffective; opportunity cost of better solutions | Faster implementation if procedure works |
| **Test first** | Delayed benefits if procedure works; additional analysis cost | Confident decision; avoids costly mistake |

**Recommendation: Test first.** The cost of a failed system-wide rollout far exceeds the cost of a 3-month randomized evaluation.
