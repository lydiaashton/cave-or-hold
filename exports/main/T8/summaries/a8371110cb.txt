## **BOTTOM LINE**

The new procedure **likely improves service scores**, but our study was too small to prove this definitively. Branches using the new procedure improved by **1.9 points** (≈4%) relative to control branches. While this result is not statistically significant at conventional levels (two-sided p = 0.13), it becomes **borderline significant** (one-sided p = 0.07) when testing specifically for improvement—as we expected.

**The procedure shows no evidence of harm**, and the observed improvement is practically meaningful.

**Recommendation:** **Proceed with system-wide rollout.** The combination of (1) positive effect in the expected direction, (2) no evidence of negative impact, (3) practical significance, and (4) low study power explaining the statistical uncertainty supports adoption. Continue monitoring to confirm the effect size.

### Statistical Assessment

| Measure | Value | Interpretation |
|---------|-------|----------------|
| Estimated effect | +1.93 points | ~4% improvement from baseline |
| Two-sided p-value | 0.13 | Not significant at α=0.05 |
| **One-sided p-value** | **0.07** | **Borderline significant** (justified by prior expectation) |
| 95% Confidence interval | -0.56 to +4.42 | Includes zero but rules out meaningful harm |
| Study power | ~9% | Too low to detect realistic effects |

### Why the P-Value Is Misleading Here

Our study had only **34 branches** split between two groups. This sample size can only detect very large effects (9+ points) with confidence. The fact that we observed a 1.9-point improvement despite this limitation is actually **encouraging evidence**—it suggests the true effect may be real but our study couldn't confirm it with high statistical certainty.
