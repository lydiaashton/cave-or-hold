# **BOARD MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Executive Summary**

We **cannot confidently conclude** that the new procedure improved service scores. While branches using the new procedure showed higher scores after implementation, those same branches were already improving faster than others *before* the switch. This pre-existing difference makes it impossible to isolate the procedure's true effect. We recommend **pausing the rollout** and conducting a proper randomized evaluation.

---

## **Bottom Line**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **Did scores improve at treatment branches?** | Yes (+3.82 points) |
| **Can we attribute this to the new procedure?** | **No** — pre-existing trends explain the improvement |
| **Estimated true treatment effect** | **Uncertain** (could be zero or negative) |
| **Recommendation** | **Do not proceed with systemwide rollout** |

---

## **The Critical Problem: Violated Assumption**

Our analysis method (difference-in-differences) requires that treatment and control branches were on **parallel trends** before the switch. They were not.

### Pre-Treatment Trends (Weeks 6-14)

| Metric | Control Branches | Treatment Branches |
|--------|-----------------|-------------------|
| Weekly improvement rate | +0.52 points/week | +0.77 points/week |
| **Difference** | — | **+0.25 points/week faster** |

This differential trend is **statistically significant** (p < 0.001). Treatment branches were already outperforming control branches before the procedure changed.

### What This Means

Over the 8-week post-switch period, the pre-existing trend alone could explain approximately **2.0 points** of the observed gap. Our estimated "treatment effect" is only **1.8 points**.

**In other words: the entire observed improvement could be due to pre-existing differences, not the new procedure.**

---

## **Raw Data (For Context)**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Control Branches | 49.16 | 51.14 | +1.98 |
| Treatment Branches | 49.67 | 53.49 | +3.82 |
| **Observed Difference** | — | — | **+1.84** |

These numbers initially looked promising. However, the pre-trend analysis shows we cannot interpret this as a causal effect.

---

## **Why This Happened**

Branches were **not randomly assigned** to adopt the new procedure. Early adopters may have:
- More motivated staff
- Better management
- More resources
- Different patron demographics

These unmeasured factors likely drove both the adoption decision AND the improving scores.

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

> **DO NOT ROLL OUT THE NEW PROCEDURE SYSTEMWIDE AT THIS TIME.**

**Rationale:**
1. The data does **not demonstrate** that the new procedure causes improved service scores
2. The observed improvement is fully explainable by pre-existing branch differences
3. Systemwide rollout would commit resources to an unproven intervention
4. A proper randomized evaluation is feasible and would provide definitive answers

**What We Need Before Proceeding:**
- Random assignment of branches to treatment vs. control
- Minimum 8-12 weeks of post-implementation data
- Demonstrated treatment effect with valid causal identification

---

## **Next Steps**

| Action | Timeline | Priority |
|--------|----------|----------|
| **Pause systemwide rollout** | Immediate | **High** |
| **Design randomized pilot** | 4 weeks | High |
| **Randomly assign 50-100 branches** to treatment vs. control | 2 weeks | High |
| **Run for 8-12 weeks** with weekly monitoring | 8-12 weeks | High |
| **Re-evaluate with valid causal design** | After pilot | High |

---

## **Conclusion**

The initial analysis was **inconclusive**, not confirmatory. The new procedure may work, but this data cannot prove it. Rolling out systemwide based on these results risks:
- Implementing a procedure that provides no benefit
- Disrupting operations without measurable gain
- Missing the opportunity to test properly

**We recommend investing in a rigorous evaluation before making a systemwide commitment.**

---

*Analysis based on 12,053 weekly observations from 709 branches. The difference-in-differences identification assumption (parallel pre-trends) is violated (p < 0.001), invalidating causal interpretation.*
