# Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores

## Bottom Line

**The new shelving and rotation procedure improved service scores by approximately 2.3 points** (about 5% above the baseline average of 50). This contradicts the initial expectation that the change would make no difference, but the evidence is statistically strong and consistent across multiple analytical approaches.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## Final Verdict

**Approve the systemwide rollout.** The data provides strong evidence that the new procedure improves service quality. With 150 branches in the cooperative, a 2.3-point average improvement represents meaningful gains for patrons across the entire system. The statistical evidence is robust, the study design is sound, and there is no indication of negative unintended consequences.

---

## Key Findings

### 1. Measurable Improvement in Service Scores
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated impact | +2.34 points |
| 95% Confidence interval | 1.0 to 3.6 points |
| Statistical significance | p = 0.0004 |
| Improvement relative to baseline | 4.7% |

### 2. Rigorous Analysis
- **Method:** Difference-in-differences with branch and week fixed effects
- **Sample:** 150 branches over 17 weeks (90 switched, 60 did not)
- **Robustness:** Result holds when controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### 3. Valid Comparison
Pre-switch trends (weeks 6–14) were parallel between switched and non-switched branches, confirming that the post-switch improvement is attributable to the procedure change rather than pre-existing differences.

### 4. Summary Statistics

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Non-switched branches | 48.83 | 50.99 | +2.16 |
| Switched branches | 48.13 | 52.63 | +4.50 |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | | **+2.34** |

## Technical Note

Analysis uses difference-in-differences with branch and week fixed effects, clustered standard errors at the branch level. Pre-trend tests confirm parallel trends before the intervention. The estimated effect is robust to alternative specifications.
