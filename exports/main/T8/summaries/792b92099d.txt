# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Rotation Procedure  
**Date:** Analysis of Weeks 6–22 Service Score Data  
**Prepared by:** Data Analysis Team

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The evidence is promising but not definitive.** Branches that adopted the new procedure showed service score improvements of approximately **2 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. However, branches that switched were already improving faster *before* the procedure change, making it difficult to isolate how much of the improvement is due to the new procedure itself. 

**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **controlled expansion** (not full rollout) while continuing to monitor results. This allows the cooperative to gather more evidence before committing systemwide.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### Study Overview
| | |
|---|---|
| **Branches studied** | 709 total (425 switched, 284 did not) |
| **Study period** | 17 weeks (Weeks 6–22) |
| **Switch date** | Week 15 |

### What the Data Shows
| Measure | Result |
|---|---|
| Raw improvement (switching branches) | +2.5 points (from 50.7 to 53.3) |
| Comparison group improvement | +0.4 points (from 48.9 to 49.3) |
| **Difference-in-differences estimate** | **+2.1 points** |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 |

### The Critical Limitation

**The parallel trends assumption is violated.** For a clean causal estimate, switching and non-switching branches should have been on similar trajectories before the switch. They were not:

| Period | Gap Between Groups |
|---|---|
| Weeks 6–10 (early pre-period) | 1.4 points |
| Weeks 11–14 (late pre-period) | 2.4 points |
| Weeks 15–22 (post-period) | 4.0 points |

**The gap was already widening before the procedure changed.** This means:
- Some (perhaps much) of the 2.1-point estimate reflects pre-existing differences, not the procedure
- We cannot confidently say how much improvement the procedure itself caused
- The true effect could be smaller than estimated—or possibly zero

### What Supports a Positive Effect
- The gap **accelerated** after Week 15 (from 2.4 to 4.0 points)
- When controlling for the pre-existing trend statistically, the effect remains positive (~2.4 points)
- No evidence of harm; scores improved, not declined

### What Raises Concern
- Immediate pre-post comparison (Week 14 vs. Week 15 only) shows **no significant effect** (0.26 points, p = 0.52)
- Selection bias: Branches chosen to switch early may have been better managed or more motivated
- Only 8 weeks of post-switch data—effects could fade or reverse

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

| Action | Rationale |
|---|---|
| **1. Do NOT approve full systemwide rollout yet** | Evidence is insufficient to justify systemwide change |
| **2. Expand to additional branches in controlled batches** | Gather more data with better comparison groups |
| **3. Randomize future rollout where feasible** | Random assignment would eliminate selection bias |
| **4. Extend monitoring period to 6+ months** | Assess whether effects persist over time |
| **5. Collect qualitative data from staff** | Understand implementation factors that drive success |
| **6. Re-evaluate at next quarter's board meeting** | Decide on full rollout with more robust evidence |

---

## **CONCLUSION**

The new procedure is associated with improved service scores, but we cannot confidently attribute the improvement to the procedure itself. Branches that switched early were already outperforming and improving faster than those that did not. 

A **cautious, evidence-based approach** is warranted: continue expanding while treating the rollout as an ongoing experiment. If effects persist and strengthen with more data, full systemwide adoption can be justified. If effects diminish or disappear, the cooperative can avoid a costly systemwide change that may not deliver expected benefits.

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| **Did service scores improve at switching branches?** | Yes, clearly |
| **Can we attribute this to the new procedure?** | Partially—selection bias clouds the estimate |
| **Is the procedure likely harmful?** | No evidence of harm |
| **Should we roll out to all branches now?** | **No**—evidence is not yet strong enough |
| **Should we continue the program?** | **Yes**—with controlled expansion and monitoring |

---

**Recommendation: APPROVE CONTROLLED EXPANSION WITH CONTINUED EVALUATION**

**Vote Required:** Board approval to expand to additional branches in batches over the next quarter, with mandatory reporting on service score outcomes before any systemwide decision.
