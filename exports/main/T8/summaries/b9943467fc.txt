# **Board Memorandum**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure is associated with a **2.6-point improvement in service scores**, but this result is **not statistically conclusive** (p=0.11). The data suggest a likely positive effect with minimal risk of harm. We recommend a **phased rollout to remaining branches over 8–12 weeks**, with a formal board review before deciding on permanent systemwide adoption.

---

## **Final Verdict**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Recommendation** | ✅ **Proceed with phased rollout** |
| **Confidence Level** | ⚠️ **Moderate** (suggestive evidence, not definitive) |
| **Risk Assessment** | 🟢 **Low** (no evidence of negative effects) |

**Rationale:** The balance of evidence favors adoption. The estimated effect is meaningful (+2.6 points, ~5% improvement), the probability of a positive effect is high (~95%), and the potential downside is minimal. A phased approach protects the cooperative while allowing us to gather more conclusive evidence.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure in week 15; **14 branches** served as controls
- Service scores average ~50; higher is better

### Results

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|----------------|---------------|--------|
| New procedure | 46.9 | 49.4 | **+2.5** |
| Old procedure | 44.9 | 44.9 | **0.0** |
| **Net effect** | | | **+2.6 points** |

### What the Statistics Tell Us

| Metric | Value | Interpretation |
|--------|-------|----------------|
| Estimated effect | +2.6 points | Most likely outcome |
| 95% confidence interval | -0.6 to +5.7 | Range of plausible values |
| P-value | 0.11 | Not significant at 5% level |
| Probability effect is positive | ~95% | Bayesian estimate |

**Bottom line on uncertainty:** We cannot be statistically certain the effect is real, but the evidence leans toward a modest improvement with little downside risk.

### Supporting Observations
- **Pre-trends were parallel** – switched and control branches moved similarly before week 15, validating the comparison
- **65% of switched branches improved** vs. 50% of control branches
- **No branch showed substantial decline** attributable to the new procedure

## **Recommendations**

| Priority | Action | Timeline |
|----------|--------|----------|
| 1 | Extend new procedure to 7–8 additional branches | Weeks 1–4 |
| 2 | Extend to remaining branches | Weeks 5–8 |
| 3 | Continue weekly score tracking; flag any declines | Ongoing |
| 4 | Board review with full data | 90 days post-rollout |
| 5 | Decide on permanent adoption based on accumulated evidence | Q1 2027 |

---

## **Why a Phased Approach?**

| Factor | Consideration |
|--------|---------------|
| **Statistical uncertainty** | Effect is suggestive, not definitive |
| **Downside risk** | Confidence interval lower bound is near zero (-0.6) |
| **Upside potential** | Could be as high as +5.7 points |
| **Operational cost** | Procedure change requires staff training and adjustment |
| **Reversibility** | Easier to adjust mid-rollout than after full implementation |

---

## **Limitations**

- Only **8 weeks of post-implementation data** – longer-term effects unknown
- **34 branches** – moderate sample size limits statistical power
- **Unmeasured factors** – seasonal patterns, staffing changes, or local events could influence scores

---

## **Conclusion**

The evidence is **promising but not definitive**. A phased rollout allows the cooperative to capture potential benefits while gathering more data to confirm the effect before committing to permanent systemwide change. We will provide the board with an updated analysis after 90 days of expanded implementation.
