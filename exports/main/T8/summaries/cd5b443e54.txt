# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure  

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **2.1 points** (95% CI: 1.6 to 2.7) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents approximately a **4% improvement** and is statistically significant (p < 0.001).

**Recommendation:** Proceed with system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated treatment effect | +2.14 service score points |
| 95% Confidence interval | 1.58 to 2.71 points |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 |
| Effect size | ~4.2% improvement over baseline |
| Treatment branches | 425 |
| Control branches | 284 |
| Study period | Weeks 6–22 (17 weeks) |

### What the Analysis Shows

1. **Clear positive effect:** Treatment branches improved by 2.4 points on average after switching, while control branches showed virtually no change (0.2 points).

2. **Robust result:** The effect remains significant after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume, and after accounting for branch-level variation.

3. **Consistent across most branches:** 74% of treatment branches showed improvement; 26% saw no benefit or declined.

4. **Valid comparison:** Pre-switch trends were reasonably parallel between groups, supporting the credibility of the estimated effect.

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **Did the new procedure work?** | **Yes** — statistically significant improvement |
| **Is the effect large?** | **Modest** — 4% improvement, 0.22 standard deviations |
| **Did it work everywhere?** | **No** — 26% of branches saw no benefit |
| **Should we roll it out?** | **Yes** — net positive impact across the system |

**Rationale:** The evidence supports rollout because:
- The average effect is positive and statistically robust
- Even a 4% improvement in service scores benefits patrons system-wide
- The procedure can be refined for underperforming branches post-rollout
- The cost of implementation appears low relative to the benefit

**Caveat:** Monitor branch-level outcomes during rollout and investigate characteristics of the 26% of branches that did not improve to inform future implementation support.

## **Technical Notes**

- **Method:** Difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors
- **Data:** 12,053 weekly observations from 709 branches
- **Controls:** Staffing index, visits index, branch fixed effects
- **Assumptions:** Parallel pre-treatment trends (validated); no spillover effects between branches
