# **Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**We cannot determine whether the new procedure improved service scores.** While branches that switched show higher scores than those that didn't, this difference existed *before* the switch and reflects pre-existing differences between the groups—not a causal effect of the procedure. **We do not recommend system-wide rollout based on this evidence.**

---

## **Final Verdict**

> **The new procedure has NOT been proven to improve service scores.**
>
> **Recommendation: DO NOT ROLL OUT system-wide at this time.**
>
> **Required action: Conduct a randomized controlled trial before any expansion.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. The Apparent Effect Disappears When Accounting for Pre-Existing Trends**

| Model Specification | Estimated Effect | 95% CI | Statistically Significant? |
|---------------------|------------------|--------|---------------------------|
| Standard analysis (ignores pre-trends) | +1.86 points | [+1.51, +2.21] | Yes |
| **Adjusted for pre-existing trends** | **-0.53 points** | **[-1.23, +0.17]** | **No** |

The standard analysis suggests a positive effect, but this is **misleading**. When we account for the fact that switched branches were already on a different trajectory, the estimated effect:
- **Drops by over 2 points**
- **Becomes statistically insignificant**
- **Reverses direction** (slightly negative)

### **2. Why the Standard Analysis Is Misleading**

The event study reveals the core problem:

| Period | Treatment-Control Gap |
|--------|----------------------|
| Week 6 (earliest) | +0.80 points |
| Week 14 (just before switch) | +3.37 points |
| Week 22 (latest) | +5.05 points |

**The gap was widening steadily BEFORE the switch occurred.** This means branches that chose to adopt the new procedure were already different—likely more motivated, better-managed, or facing different conditions. The procedure itself did not create a discontinuous change.

### **3. What We Can Conclude**

- ✅ Switched branches have higher service scores than non-switched branches
- ❌ We **cannot** attribute this difference to the new procedure
- ❌ There is **no evidence** the procedure caused improvement
- ⚠️ The procedure may have **no effect** or even a **slight negative effect**

---

## **Recommendation**

### **Do Not Proceed with System-Wide Rollout**

The current evidence does not support the conclusion that the new procedure improves service scores. Rolling it out system-wide would:

1. **Incur implementation costs** without proven benefit
2. **Disrupt operations** at all 709 branches
3. **Risk negative effects** if the procedure is actually detrimental

### **Instead: Conduct a Proper Randomized Trial**

To get a definitive answer:

| Step | Action |
|------|--------|
| 1 | Randomly assign 100-150 branches to adopt the procedure |
| 2 | Keep remaining branches on the old procedure (control) |
| 3 | Measure service scores for 12+ weeks |
| 4 | Compare outcomes between randomized groups |

Randomization eliminates selection bias and provides credible evidence of causality.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Pause rollout plans** pending stronger evidence
2. **Design a randomized controlled trial** with the research team
3. **Budget for proper evaluation** before any future expansion
4. **Consider qualitative research** to understand why some branches chose to switch
