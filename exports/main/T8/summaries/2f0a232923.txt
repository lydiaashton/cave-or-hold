# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

## **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

**Date:** November 2025  
**Prepared by:** Data Analysis Team

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**We cannot conclude the new procedure improved service scores.** 

While branches that switched to the new procedure did show higher score increases than control branches, **this difference existed before the switch occurred**. Treatment branches were already improving faster than control branches prior to adoption. The observed 2.0-point "effect" is statistically indistinguishable from pre-existing trends.

**Recommendation:** Do **not** proceed with system-wide rollout until further evidence is gathered.

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

> **❌ DO NOT APPROVE ROLLOUT**
>
> **The evidence does not demonstrate that the new procedure causes improvement in service scores.** The apparent benefit is explained by pre-existing differences between branches that chose to adopt early versus those that did not. Proceeding with system-wide implementation would commit resources to an unproven intervention with no demonstrated causal benefit.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### The Critical Issue: Pre-Existing Differences

| Period | Treatment-Control Gap |
|--------|----------------------|
| Week 6 (start) | 0.25 points |
| Week 14 (pre-switch) | 3.05 points |
| Week 15 (post-switch) | 2.96 points |
| Week 22 (end) | 4.43 points |

**The gap was widening BEFORE the switch.** Treatment branches gained 0.25 points/week more than control branches during the pre-period (p < 0.001). This pre-existing trend fully explains the observed post-switch difference.

### What the Data Actually Shows

1. **No discontinuity at the switch:** The gap slightly decreased (3.05 → 2.96) in the first week after adoption
2. **Post-switch performance is below pre-trend projection:** If the pre-existing trend had continued, we'd expect a 5.1-point gap by week 22; actual gap was 4.4 points
3. **Possible interpretations:**
   - The new procedure had no effect (most likely)
   - The new procedure had a small negative effect
   - Unobserved factors favoring treatment branches continued post-switch

### Why the Initial Analysis Was Misleading

The standard difference-in-differences approach assumes parallel pre-trends. When this assumption is violated, the estimator captures pre-existing differences rather than causal effects. This is what occurred here.

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| 1 | **Pause rollout decision** | Evidence does not support effectiveness |
| 2 | **Investigate why treatment branches were improving faster** | May reveal confounding factors (e.g., motivated branches self-selected into early adoption) |
| 3 | **Consider randomized rollout** | Future evaluation should randomly assign branches to ensure valid comparison |
| 4 | **Gather qualitative data** | Interview branch staff about implementation experience and perceived effects |
| 5 | **Extend observation period** | Monitor for 6+ more months to detect any delayed effects |

---

## **LESSONS FOR FUTURE EVALUATIONS**

1. **Always test the parallel trends assumption** before relying on DiD estimates
2. **Random assignment** of treatment would have avoided this selection bias
3. **Event-study designs** (examining effects week-by-week) help detect pre-trend violations
4. **Consider self-selection bias** — branches that volunteered for early adoption may differ systematically

---

## **DECISION REQUESTED**

**Do not approve system-wide rollout at this time.** Request additional analysis to:
- Identify what distinguished early-adopter branches
- Design a randomized evaluation for future procedure changes
- Determine whether the new procedure has any merit worth further testing

---

*This analysis uses difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors. The parallel trends assumption was tested and rejected (p < 0.001), invalidating causal interpretation of the treatment effect.*
