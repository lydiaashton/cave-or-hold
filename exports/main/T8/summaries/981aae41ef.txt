# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The data provide **statistically significant evidence** that the new procedure improved service scores by approximately **2.1 points** (95% confidence interval: 0.9 to 3.3 points). However, because branches were not randomly assigned to switch—and treatment branches had slightly higher scores even before the change—we cannot rule out that some portion of this gain reflects pre-existing differences. The evidence favors a rollout, but expectations should be modest.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
- **150 branches** tracked over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **60 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach to estimate the effect

### Service Score Impact

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|----------------|---------------|--------|
| Branches that switched | 50.29 | 53.50 | +3.20 |
| Branches that did not switch | 49.48 | 50.55 | +1.08 |
| **Net effect (DiD estimate)** | | | **+2.12** |

- **Estimated effect: +2.1 points** (95% CI: 0.9 to 3.3)
- **Statistically significant** (p = 0.0004)
- Effect persists after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### Important Caveats

1. **Pre-existing differences**: Before the switch, treatment branches already averaged 0.8 points higher than control branches, with week-to-week differences ranging from -0.1 to +2.4 points. The parallel trends assumption is plausible but not perfectly satisfied.

2. **Non-random assignment**: Branches were not randomly assigned to switch. Unobserved factors (management quality, local conditions) could explain part of the observed effect.

3. **Modest effect size**: The 2.1-point gain equals about 0.2 standard deviations—meaningful but not transformative.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with rollout**, but communicate that improvements are likely to be modest (1–3 points on average)

2. **Monitor implementation**: Track service scores for 8–12 weeks after full rollout; if effects are smaller than 1 point, reassess

3. **Document best practices**: Capture what distinguishes the new procedure for training purposes

4. **Consider a randomized evaluation** for future policy changes to establish cleaner causal evidence

5. **Leverage early adopters**: The 90 branches that already switched may serve as mentors during rollout

---

## **Final Verdict**

**Proceed with rollout, but with measured expectations.**

The statistical evidence indicates a real positive effect, and there is no evidence of harm. Even if part of the 2-point gain reflects pre-existing branch differences, the remaining effect likely justifies system-wide adoption. However, this is not a breakthrough improvement—the board should anticipate modest gains and continue monitoring to ensure the effect holds at scale.
