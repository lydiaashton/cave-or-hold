# **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative
**Date:** Analysis of weeks 6–22 service score data
**Subject:** Impact of new rotation and shelving procedure on service quality

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **2.4 points** (approximately 5%) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and consistent across the treatment group.

**Recommendation: Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **60 branches** retained the old procedure
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in service scores between groups before and after the switch

### Service Score Impact

| Group | Pre-Switch Average | Post-Switch Average | Change |
|-------|-------------------|---------------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 46.6 | 50.8 | **+4.2** |
| Control branches | 47.6 | 49.4 | +1.8 |
| **Net effect** | | | **+2.4** |

- The **2.4-point improvement** is statistically significant (p < 0.001)
- 95% confidence interval: **1.1 to 3.7 points**
- This represents approximately a **5% improvement** relative to baseline scores

### Supporting Evidence
- **Parallel trends validated**: Before the switch, there was no statistically significant difference in trends between the two groups, supporting the validity of the comparison
- **Consistent effect**: The improvement persisted across all 8 post-switch weeks
- **Widespread benefit**: 89% of switched branches improved, compared to 68% of control branches
- **Robust to controls**: The effect holds after accounting for weekly staffing and visit volume variations

### Expectations vs. Reality
The original expectation was that the new procedure would make **no difference** to service scores. The data clearly contradict this—the new procedure produces a **meaningful, measurable improvement** in service quality.

---

## **Final Verdict**

**The evidence supports adopting the new procedure system-wide.**

| Criterion | Assessment |
|-----------|------------|
| Statistical significance | ✓ Passed (p < 0.001) |
| Practical significance | ✓ Passed (~5% improvement) |
| Methodological validity | ✓ Passed (parallel trends confirmed) |
| Consistency across branches | ✓ Passed (89% of switched branches improved) |
| Original hypothesis | ✗ Rejected (expected no difference; found clear improvement) |

**Verdict: The new rotation and shelving procedure delivers a real, measurable improvement in service quality. The board should approve immediate rollout to all remaining branches.**

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all remaining branches

2. **Monitor implementation**: Track service scores during rollout to ensure the effect replicates across all branches

3. **Document best practices**: Capture implementation lessons from switched branches to support training for remaining branches

4. **Consider cost-benefit analysis**: If the new procedure has associated costs (training, equipment, time), weigh them against the 5% service score improvement

5. **Continue evaluation**: After full rollout, compare system-wide service scores to this study's baseline to confirm the expected improvement materializes

---

*Analysis based on weekly service scores from weeks 6–22, using difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors by branch.*
