# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

---

## **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

### **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure had a modest positive effect, but the case for systemwide rollout is not clear-cut.** 

Branches that switched to the new procedure saw service scores improve by approximately **1.8 points relative to what would have been expected** based on non-switched branches. However, this effect is modest (0.18 standard deviations), and switched branches **still score lower than non-switched branches** even after adoption (51.7 vs. 52.9 post-switch).

The benefit appears to be that the new procedure **helped branches resist a broader decline** in service scores that affected non-switched branches after week 15, rather than producing absolute improvement.

---

### **FINAL VERDICT**

**Conditional Recommendation: Proceed with Cautious Rollout**

| Consideration | Assessment |
|---------------|------------|
| **Statistical evidence** | ✅ Significant (p = 0.001) |
| **Effect size** | ⚠️ Modest (1.8 points, 3.5% of average) |
| **Absolute performance** | ⚠️ Switched branches still underperform |
| **Mechanism clarity** | ⚠️ Unclear why control group declined |
| **Implementation risk** | ✅ Low (procedure change, not structural) |

**Verdict:** The data supports a **phased rollout with monitoring**, not an immediate systemwide mandate. The procedure provides a real but modest benefit—enough to justify broader adoption if implementation costs are low, but not enough to ignore other factors affecting service scores.

**Specific conditions for approval:**
1. Roll out to 20–30 branches first, not all 60 remaining
2. Set a 3-month review checkpoint with explicit success criteria
3. Investigate the post-week-15 decline affecting non-switched branches in parallel
4. Budget for training and support during transition

---

### **KEY FINDINGS**

**1. What Actually Happened to Scores**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Absolute Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|-----------------|
| Switched branches | 51.2 | 51.7 | **+0.5** |
| Non-switched branches | 54.2 | 52.9 | **−1.3** |
| **Relative Effect** | | | **+1.8** |

The switched branches barely improved in absolute terms (+0.5 points). The statistical "effect" comes mainly from the fact that non-switched branches declined (−1.3 points) while switched branches held steady.

**2. Switched Branches Still Underperform**

Even after adopting the new procedure, switched branches scored **lower than non-switched branches in every single post-switch week** (gap ranged from −0.8 to −2.3 points).

**3. Statistical Summary**
- **Relative effect:** +1.76 points (p = 0.001)
- **95% confidence interval:** 0.69 to 2.84 points
- **Effect size:** 0.18 standard deviations (small-to-moderate)
- Results are robust to controlling for staffing and visitor volume

**4. Critical Unanswered Question**

**Why did non-switched branches decline after week 15?** Something affected all branches during the post-switch period (seasonal factors? staffing changes? increased demand?). The new procedure appears to have helped switched branches resist this decline, but we don't know the root cause.

---

### **NEXT STEPS**

1. **Approve phased rollout** to 20–30 branches (not all 60 remaining)

2. **Set review checkpoint** at 3 months with explicit success criteria (e.g., maintain 1.5+ point effect)

3. **Investigate the post-week-15 decline** in parallel—this may reveal larger opportunities for improvement

4. **Interview branch managers** at switched and non-switched locations to understand operational differences

5. **Prepare contingency plan** if effects diminish during broader rollout

---

### **METHODOLOGY NOTE**

This analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach. The key finding is a relative effect: switched branches performed better than they would have otherwise, not that they achieved high absolute scores. The parallel trends assumption is supported by stable pre-period gaps. However, the modest effect size and continued underperformance of switched branches suggest other factors beyond the shelving procedure influence service scores.
