# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Subject:** Recommendation on System-Wide Rollout of New Shelving and Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence is inconclusive.** While branches that switched procedures showed higher service scores after the change, **those same branches were already improving relative to other branches BEFORE the switch**. The gap between switching and non-switching branches grew by 2.5 points in the 9 weeks before the change, then only 0.8 points in the 8 weeks after. This pre-existing trend undermines our ability to attribute the improvement to the procedure change itself.

**Recommendation: Do not proceed with system-wide rollout yet. Conduct additional analysis or a randomized pilot.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **The Critical Issue: Pre-Existing Trends**

| Period | Gap Growth (Treatment vs. Control) |
|--------|-----------------------------------|
| **Pre-switch (Weeks 6–14)** | +2.47 points |
| **Post-switch (Weeks 15–22)** | +0.77 points |

The treatment branches were **already on an upward trajectory** before adopting the new procedure. This violates the key assumption needed to claim the procedure caused the improvement.

### **What the Data Actually Shows**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Raw difference-in-differences estimate | +2.05 points |
| **Estimated effect after accounting for pre-trend** | **~0.8 points or less** |
| Statistical uncertainty | High (pre-trend confounds interpretation) |

### **Alternative Explanations**

The switching branches may differ systematically from non-switching branches in ways we cannot observe:
- Better management or leadership
- More resources or staffing flexibility
- Different community demographics
- Self-selection (branches expecting improvement may have volunteered to switch)

Any of these factors could explain both the pre-trend and the post-switch results.

---

## **Next Steps**

| Priority | Action |
|----------|--------|
| **1** | **Pause system-wide rollout** until causal impact is clearer |
| **2** | **Investigate why certain branches were selected** to switch first—was it random or based on characteristics? |
| **3** | **Conduct a randomized controlled trial** with remaining branches to isolate the true effect |
| **4** | **Gather qualitative feedback** from switched branches—do staff perceive benefits? |
| **5** | **Re-analyze with 12+ more weeks of data** to see if trends stabilize or diverge further |

---

## **What Would Change Our Conclusion?**

We could recommend rollout if future analysis shows:
- A **sharp discontinuity** at Week 15 (not a continuation of prior trends)
- **Randomized assignment** of the procedure change
- **Qualitative evidence** of operational improvements
- **Replication** of effects when rolled out to a new set of branches

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **Did service scores improve in switching branches?** | Yes |
| **Can we attribute this to the new procedure?** | **No—not with confidence** |
| **Should we roll out system-wide now?** | **No** |
| **Is the new procedure worth further investigation?** | **Yes** |

**The procedure may still be beneficial, but this data cannot prove it.** A hasty system-wide rollout risks investing resources in a change that may not deliver the expected returns. A careful, randomized pilot would protect the cooperative's investment while generating definitive evidence.

---

*Analysis based on branches.csv (12,053 observations, 709 branches, Weeks 6–22). The pre-trend violation significantly limits causal inference.*
