# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Contrary to expectations, the data suggests a modest improvement in service scores.** Branches that switched showed a **2.5-point increase** relative to non-switched branches (statistically significant, p < 0.001). Given the evidence of benefit and no evidence of harm, we recommend proceeding with system-wide rollout while continuing to monitor outcomes.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. **Statistically Significant but Modest Effect**
- Difference-in-differences estimate: **+2.48 points** (95% CI: 1.6 to 3.5)
- This finding contradicts our prior expectation of no impact
- Effect size represents approximately **5% improvement** relative to the average service score (~51 points)

### 2. **Important Context on the Comparison**
- Before the switch, treatment branches scored **1.2 points LOWER** than control branches on average
- After the switch, treatment branches scored **1.3 points HIGHER**
- The 2.5-point "effect" represents treatment branches moving from slightly worse to slightly better relative to control

### 3. **High Volatility in Service Scores**
- Week-to-week fluctuations are large (3–9 point swings are common)
- This makes it harder to isolate the procedure effect from normal variation
- The signal is detectable but operates against considerable noise

### 4. **Limited Post-Switch Observation Period**
- Only 8 weeks of data after the switch
- Cannot assess whether the effect persists, grows, or diminishes over longer periods
- Cannot assess seasonal effects (data spans weeks 6–22 only)

---

## **Final Verdict**

**Proceed with system-wide rollout.**

**Rationale:**
- The statistical evidence is strong (p < 0.001, narrow confidence interval entirely above zero)
- There is no signal of harm to any branches
- A 5% service score improvement is meaningful for patron experience
- Delaying rollout to collect more data has opportunity costs — branches not yet switched are missing out on potential benefits
- Continued monitoring can be built into the rollout process itself

**Caveats:**
- Effect size is modest; manage expectations accordingly
- Build in monitoring checkpoints at 8 and 16 weeks post-rollout
- Be prepared to investigate if effects diminish over time

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** — begin scheduling remaining 60 branches for conversion

2. **Develop implementation timeline** — phase conversions over 8–12 weeks to manage training capacity

3. **Build monitoring into the rollout** — track service scores for each cohort at 8 and 16 weeks post-switch

4. **Document implementation** — capture training hours, staff feedback, and any operational challenges

5. **Investigate the mechanism** — the unexpected service score improvement deserves understanding. Consider brief staff surveys to identify what changed (e.g., faster item retrieval, more patron interaction time)

6. **Schedule follow-up review** — reconvene in 6 months to assess whether effects persisted across the full system

---

## **Technical Note**

Analysis uses difference-in-differences regression with standard errors clustered at the branch level (150 branches, 2,550 observations). The parallel trends assumption is satisfied (no differential pre-trends detected, p = 0.87). Bootstrap validation (1,000 iterations) confirms the DiD estimate is stable with 95% CI of [1.55, 3.52]. The low R-squared (~0.01) reflects high inherent volatility in weekly service scores, not model failure.
