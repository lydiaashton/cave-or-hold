# **Memorandum to the Board of Directors**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure  
**Date:** November 2025  
**Prepared by:** Data Analysis Team

---

## **Bottom Line**

**We cannot confidently conclude the new procedure caused the improvement.** While branches that adopted the new procedure showed higher service scores afterward, **those same branches were already improving faster than others before the switch**. This suggests the observed gains may reflect pre-existing differences rather than the procedure itself. **We do not recommend a full rollout based on this evidence alone.**

---

## **Final Verdict**

> **INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE FOR ROLLOUT**
>
> The analysis does not demonstrate that the new procedure causes improved service scores. The apparent 1.9-point benefit disappears when accounting for pre-existing trends. **A randomized controlled trial is needed before making a system-wide investment.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### What the Data Shows

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Branches with new procedure | 49.99 | 53.01 | +3.02 |
| Branches with old procedure | 49.09 | 50.19 | +1.10 |
| **Raw difference** | | | **+1.92** |

### Why We Cannot Claim Causation

**The parallel trends assumption is violated.** For a valid comparison, treatment and control branches should have been on similar trajectories before the switch. They were not:

| Period | Gap Growth (per week) | Statistical Significance |
|--------|----------------------|-------------------------|
| **Before switch** (weeks 6–14) | +0.25 points/week | p = 0.0005 |
| **After switch** (weeks 15–22) | +0.18 points/week | p = 0.02 |

The gap between treatment and control branches was **already widening before the procedure changed**. This indicates:
- Treatment branches may have been systematically different (e.g., better managed, more motivated)
- Branches may have **self-selected** into the new procedure based on expected improvement
- The observed 1.9-point "effect" likely overstates the true impact

### Adjusted Estimate

When accounting for pre-existing trends, the estimated effect of the new procedure is:
- **0.4 points** (not 1.9 points)
- **95% confidence interval:** -1.1 to +1.9 points
- **Not statistically significant** (p = 0.62)

In other words, the data is consistent with anything from a small negative effect to a modest positive effect.

---

## **Recommendation**

**Do not proceed with system-wide rollout based on current evidence.** The observed association between the new procedure and higher scores cannot be distinguished from selection bias.

---

## **Next Steps**

| Action | Rationale | Timeline |
|--------|-----------|----------|
| **Pause full rollout** | Current evidence does not support a causal claim | Immediate |
| **Design randomized trial** | Randomly assign branches to eliminate selection bias | 30 days |
| **Gather qualitative data** | Interview managers about switch decisions; identify confounders | 60 days |
| **Run pilot with randomization** | Test procedure on randomly selected branches | 90–180 days |
| **Re-evaluate with rigorous evidence** | Make rollout decision based on causal estimates | 180+ days |

---

## **What Would Change Our Verdict?**

A properly randomized evaluation could provide defensible evidence. If randomly assigned treatment branches show sustained improvement over controls—with no pre-trend differences—we could confidently recommend rollout.
