# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2025  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Executive Summary**

After analyzing 17 weeks of service score data from 34 branches, we find that the new shelving and stock rotation procedure is associated with a **modest improvement** in service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure improved relative to those that did not, but the evidence **does not reach conventional levels of statistical certainty**.

We recommend **proceeding with a phased rollout while continuing to monitor outcomes**. This balances the positive signal in the data with appropriate caution given the uncertainty.

---

## **Bottom Line**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did the new procedure help? | **Yes, modestly** — approximately +2 points on the service score index |
| Are we confident? | **Not fully** — results could reflect random variation |
| Should we roll out system-wide? | **Proceed cautiously** — phased implementation with continued monitoring |

---

## **What We Studied**

- **34 branches** total (20 adopted new procedure, 14 kept old procedure)
- **17 weeks** of data (weeks 6–22 of the year)
- **Week 15** marked the switch to the new procedure
- **Service score** is our key metric (average branch scores ~50; higher is better)

---

## **What We Found**

### Service Score Changes

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Branches with **new procedure** | 47.83 | 49.14 | **+1.31** |
| Branches with **old procedure** | 52.45 | 51.90 | **-0.55** |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | | **+1.85** |

### Key Observations

1. **Positive direction:** Branches using the new procedure improved while control branches declined slightly, resulting in a net gain of about 2 points.

2. **Uncertainty remains:** The statistical analysis indicates a 11–17% probability that an effect this size could occur by chance alone. By conventional standards (5% threshold), this does not constitute definitive evidence.

3. **Baseline differences:** Branches that adopted the new procedure started with lower service scores (about 4.6 points below control branches). This suggests the switch may have been targeted to struggling branches, which complicates interpretation.

4. **No fading or growth:** The effect appears relatively stable across the 8-week post-switch period—neither diminishing nor strengthening over time.

---

## **What This Means for the Cooperative**

A 2-point improvement on a ~50-point scale represents roughly a **4% gain** in service quality. If real, this would be a meaningful improvement across the cooperative. However, given the statistical uncertainty, we cannot guarantee this effect would replicate in a full rollout.

**Risk assessment:**
- **Upside:** If the effect is real, system-wide adoption would raise service scores across all branches
- **Downside:** If the effect reflects chance, resources invested in training and implementation may not yield expected returns

---

## **Recommendations**

### 1. **Approve a Phased Rollout**
Proceed with extending the new procedure to additional branches in stages rather than all at once. This allows for:
- Continued data collection with larger sample sizes
- Identification of which branch types benefit most
- Course correction if results differ from initial findings

### 2. **Extend the Evaluation Period**
Commit to **6 additional months** of monitoring. Eight weeks of post-implementation data is insufficient for high-confidence conclusions.

### 3. **Establish Clear Success Criteria**
Before the next phase, define what constitutes success:
- Minimum effect size (e.g., 2+ points improvement)
- Statistical confidence level (e.g., p < 0.05)
- Timeline for re-evaluation (e.g., quarterly board review)

### 4. **Investigate Selection Patterns**
Understand why 20 branches switched and 14 did not. If the new procedure was directed toward lower-performing branches, future analysis should account for this to isolate the true procedure effect.

### 5. **Document Implementation Fidelity**
Ensure all branches implement the procedure consistently. Variation in execution could mask or dilute true effects.

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

> **Based on the evidence, we find that the new procedure likely improves service scores, but the data does not support a definitive conclusion.**
>
> **The point estimate (+2 points) is meaningful and in the expected direction. The statistical uncertainty (p = 0.11) reflects limited sample size rather than evidence of no effect. There is no indication of harm.**
>
> **For a business decision of this nature, this constitutes sufficient evidence to proceed—with the understanding that continued monitoring is essential to confirm the effect at scale.**

---

## **Conclusion**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure shows **promise but not proof**. The data leans positive, warranting continued investment and monitoring, but does not justify unconditional system-wide adoption at this time. A measured, evidence-based rollout protects the cooperative's resources while allowing us to capture potential benefits.

We stand ready to provide additional analysis as more data becomes available.
