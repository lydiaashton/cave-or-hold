# **BOARD MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**SUBJECT:** New Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure — Impact Evaluation

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**We cannot confidently conclude the new procedure caused the improvement.** While service scores rose more at switching branches, those branches were already improving faster *before* they switched. This selection bias means the observed 1.8-point gain likely overstates the true effect—which could be much smaller or even zero.

**Recommendation: Do not proceed with system-wide rollout yet. Conduct a randomized trial instead.**

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

> **The evidence is insufficient to support a system-wide rollout.**
>
> The analysis reveals a **fundamental identification problem**: branches that adopted the new procedure were already outperforming the control group before the switch. This pre-existing trend is statistically significant (p = 0.001) and large enough to explain the entire observed "effect."
>
> **We cannot distinguish between:**
> 1. A real procedure improvement of ~1.8 points, OR
> 2. A continuation of pre-existing momentum with **zero causal impact** from the procedure itself
>
> Given the costs of system-wide implementation, acting on this evidence would be premature. **A randomized controlled trial is the appropriate next step.**

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

| Finding | Result |
|---------|--------|
| **Observed service score gain** | +1.81 points (statistically significant) |
| **BUT: Pre-switch trend** | Treatment branches were already gaining 0.32 points/week relative to control |
| **Implication** | Pre-existing trend alone predicts ~2.5 points of "gain" over 8 weeks |
| **Adjusted estimate** | True effect could be **near zero** |
| **Core problem** | Branches self-selected into the new procedure; they were not randomly assigned |

### The Critical Issue: Parallel Trends Violated

The difference-in-differences method requires that treatment and control branches would have followed similar trajectories absent the intervention. **This assumption is violated:**

```
Pre-period gap (Treatment − Control):
Week 6:  -1.08 points
Week 10: +0.12 points  
Week 14: +1.79 points  ← Just before switch
```

Branches that chose to switch were already on an upward path. We cannot separate the procedure's effect from this pre-existing momentum.

---

## **WHAT THIS MEANS**

The data shows a **positive association** between switching and service scores, but **not credible evidence of causation**. Rolling out system-wide based on this evidence risks:
- Implementing a procedure that may not deliver expected benefits
- Incurring rollout costs without corresponding gains
- Missing the opportunity to test more rigorously

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

| Action | Rationale |
|--------|-----------|
| **Pause system-wide rollout** | Evidence does not support confident investment |
| **Randomize remaining branches** | Assign 50% of non-switched branches to new procedure, 50% to status quo |
| **Run 8–12 week trial** | Randomization eliminates selection bias |
| **Re-evaluate with clean evidence** | Make rollout decision based on randomized results |
