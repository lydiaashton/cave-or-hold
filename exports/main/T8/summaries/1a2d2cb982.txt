# **Board Summary: Impact of New Branch Rotation Procedure on Service Scores**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**We cannot confidently conclude the new procedure caused the improvement.** While branches that switched show higher service scores after the change, switched branches were already improving relative to non-switched branches BEFORE the procedure changed. This undermines our ability to isolate the causal effect. The observed 1.8-point improvement may reflect pre-existing trends rather than the intervention itself.

---

## **Key Findings**

| Metric | Value | Interpretation |
|--------|-------|----------------|
| Raw DiD estimate | +1.82 points | Apparent improvement |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 | Precise estimate |
| Pre-period gap growth | +2.35 points | **Problem: trends diverging before switch** |
| Pre-trend test | **Failed** (p < 0.001) | **Parallel trends assumption violated** |

### The Critical Problem

The gap between switched and non-switched branches was already widening **before** the new procedure:

| Week | Treated-Control Gap |
|------|---------------------|
| Week 6 (earliest) | 0.28 points |
| Week 14 (before switch) | 2.63 points |
| **Divergence during pre-period** | **+2.35 points** |

This means the 1.8-point DiD estimate is likely **upwardly biased**. The true causal effect could be substantially smaller—or even zero.

---

## **What We Can Say With Confidence**

1. ✓ Switched branches have higher scores than non-switched branches post-switch
2. ✓ The new procedure is **not harmful** (scores didn't drop)
3. ✓ The effect is precisely estimated (narrow confidence interval)
4. ✗ We **cannot** confidently attribute the improvement to the procedure itself

---

## **Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **Did service scores improve in switched branches?** | Yes |
| **Did the new procedure cause the improvement?** | Uncertain |
| **Is the evidence strong enough for system-wide rollout?** | No |
| **Should the cooperative abandon the new procedure?** | No |

**Bottom-line verdict: The evidence is suggestive but not conclusive. A cautious, evidence-generating approach is warranted.**
