# **Memo to the Board: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores—an unexpected but welcome result.** While we anticipated the procedural change would have no material effect on service quality, branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure achieved service scores approximately **2 points higher** (about 4% improvement) than comparable branches keeping the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant. We recommend proceeding with the system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **102 branches** switched to the new procedure at week 15; **48 branches** maintained the old procedure as a control group
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach to isolate the procedure's effect from other factors

### **Results**

| Group | Pre-Switch Avg | Post-Switch Avg | Change |
|-------|---------------|-----------------|--------|
| New Procedure | 53.07 | 52.57 | -0.50 |
| Old Procedure | 51.58 | 49.08 | -2.50 |

**Estimated Effect: +2.03 points** (p < 0.01)

### **Interpretation**
- Service scores declined system-wide after week 15 (likely seasonal or external factors)
- Branches with the **new procedure experienced a much smaller decline** than those keeping the old procedure
- The new procedure effectively **delivered a 2-point buffer** against this decline
- **This exceeds our expectation of neutral impact**—the change appears to have genuine service benefits, not just operational ones

### **Validity Checks**
✓ **Parallel trends confirmed**: Both groups tracked similarly before the switch (p = 0.58)
✓ **Robust to controls**: Effect holds when accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume
✓ **Statistically significant**: Result confirmed with cluster-robust standard errors (p = 0.002)

---

## **Final Verdict**

**The evidence supports full adoption.** The new procedure delivers a statistically significant improvement in service scores with no identified drawbacks. Given that:

1. The effect is **real and measurable** (not due to chance)
2. The benefit is **practically meaningful** (~4% improvement)
3. The finding is **robust** across multiple analytical approaches
4. There are **no countervailing risks** identified in the data

**We recommend the board approve immediate system-wide rollout of the new procedure.**

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** — The business case is now stronger than anticipated, with both operational and service benefits

2. **Communicate the upside** — Share these results with branch managers to build enthusiasm for adoption

3. **Monitor during implementation** — Track scores as remaining branches transition to ensure consistent results

4. **Document the transition** — Capture lessons learned from the early-adopter branches to smooth the rollout

5. **Re-evaluate in 6 months** — Confirm the effect persists at scale and identify any branches needing additional support

---

**Conclusion:** The new procedure delivers more value than expected. Rather than a neutral operational change, it appears to genuinely improve how branches serve patrons. Full rollout is recommended.
