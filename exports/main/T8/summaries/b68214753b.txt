# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Final Verdict**

### **DO NOT PROCEED WITH SYSTEM-WIDE ROLLOUT**

The evidence does not support a causal conclusion that the new procedure improves service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure were already outperforming control branches before the switch occurred. Approving a full rollout would commit significant resources based on inconclusive evidence.

---

## **Bottom Line**

**We cannot confidently conclude the new procedure improved service scores.** While branches using the new procedure showed higher scores after the switch, those same branches were **already improving faster than control branches before the change occurred**. This pre-existing trend makes it impossible to separate the procedure's effect from other factors.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **The Core Problem: Violated Assumption**

Our analysis relies on the "parallel trends" assumption—that treatment and control branches would have followed similar trajectories absent the intervention. **This assumption is not met.**

| Period | Treatment vs. Control Gap | Gap Change |
|--------|--------------------------|------------|
| Week 6 (start) | Treatment **0.26 points lower** | — |
| Week 14 (pre-switch) | Treatment **2.55 points higher** | +2.81 points |
| Week 22 (end) | Treatment **3.28 points higher** | +0.73 points |

**The gap widened by 2.81 points BEFORE the switch, but only 0.73 points AFTER.**

### **Trend Analysis**

| Metric | Pre-Period (Weeks 6-14) | Post-Period (Weeks 15-22) |
|--------|------------------------|---------------------------|
| Gap widening rate | **0.32 points/week** | **0.16 points/week** |
| Treatment group trend | +0.79 points/week | (continued) |
| Control group trend | +0.44 points/week | (continued) |

**The treatment branches were already pulling ahead before adopting the new procedure.** The rate of gap widening actually **slowed** after the switch.

### **What the 1.85-Point Estimate Really Means**

The standard difference-in-differences calculation yields +1.85 points, but this likely reflects:
- **Pre-existing momentum** in treatment branches (branches that chose to switch may have been more motivated, better managed, or facing different conditions)
- **Not the procedure itself**

---

## **Why This Matters**

Branches self-selected into the new procedure (it wasn't randomly assigned). Branches that volunteered to switch may differ systematically from those that didn't:
- More innovative leadership
- Better baseline management practices
- Different community demographics
- Greater staff engagement

These unobserved factors—not the shelving procedure—may explain the score differences.

---

## **Recommendation**

| Action | Recommendation |
|--------|---------------|
| System-wide rollout | **DO NOT APPROVE** |
| Continue monitoring current adopters | **YES** |
| Conduct randomized trial | **RECOMMENDED** |
| Gather additional evidence | **REQUIRED before rollout** |

---

## **Next Steps**

### **Immediate Actions**
1. **Pause rollout plans** pending further analysis
2. **Document this finding** for transparency with stakeholders who expected a positive recommendation

### **Gather Stronger Evidence**
1. **Randomized trial**: Randomly assign remaining 284 branches to new vs. old procedure to eliminate selection bias
2. **Extended observation**: Continue monitoring current treatment branches for 6-12 more weeks to see if gains persist or revert
3. **Qualitative research**: Interview branch managers to understand what else may have changed alongside the procedure

### **Alternative Analysis**
1. **Matched comparison**: Pair treatment branches with similar control branches on observable characteristics (size, location, baseline scores)
2. **Interrupted time series**: Analyze whether the rate of improvement changed at week 15 for treatment branches specifically
