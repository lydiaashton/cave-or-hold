# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Do not proceed with system-wide rollout based on this evidence alone.** While the average treatment effect is statistically significant (+1.8 points), the underlying data shows troubling instability: several post-switch weeks had **negative effects**, and the pre-period shows **significant differences between groups** in some weeks. Given that this change was expected to have no effect on service scores, these findings warrant deeper investigation before committing resources to a full rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. The Average Effect Masks Volatility
| Week | Effect vs. Control | Significance |
|------|-------------------|--------------|
| 15 | +1.41 | Significant |
| 16 | **−2.61** | Significant (negative!) |
| 17 | +4.31 | Significant |
| 18 | +3.24 | Significant |
| 19 | **−4.13** | Significant (negative!) |
| 20 | +0.56 | Not significant |
| 21 | +0.28 | Not significant |
| 22 | +6.27 | Significant |

**Concern:** Two of eight post-switch weeks showed **statistically significant negative effects**. A genuine procedural improvement should not produce harmful weeks interspersed with beneficial ones.

### 2. Pre-Period Differences Exist
Despite the treatment and control groups having parallel *trends* on average, individual pre-period weeks show significant differences:
- Week 8: +2.18 points (p < 0.001)
- Week 9: −1.48 points (p = 0.014)
- Week 11: −2.53 points (p < 0.001)

This suggests unmeasured factors may differ between groups, casting doubt on the causal interpretation.

### 3. Effect Size Is Modest
- Average effect: **1.8 points** on a ~50-point scale (3.6%)
- This is statistically detectable but operationally small
- Given the expectation of zero effect, even a small positive effect could reflect residual confounding rather than a true causal impact

### 4. Why This Is Surprising
The stock rotation procedure was not designed to affect patron-facing service quality. A measurable effect—positive or negative—suggests either:
- The procedure affects staff workflow in ways that indirectly influence service
- Unmeasured confounders correlate with both switch status and service scores
- Random variation produced a false positive

---

## **Recommendations**

| Priority | Action |
|----------|--------|
| **1** | **Pause rollout** until the mechanism and stability of the effect are understood |
| **2** | **Interview branch staff** at switched vs. non-switched locations to identify what actually changed in daily operations |
| **3** | **Collect additional weeks of data** to determine if the effect stabilizes or reverts |
| **4** | **Investigate the negative weeks** (16 and 19)—were there external factors (holidays, staffing issues, system outages)? |
| **5** | **Consider a randomized rollout** with tighter monitoring if proceeding after investigation |

---

## **Final Verdict**

**NOT RECOMMENDED FOR SYSTEM-WIDE ROLLOUT AT THIS TIME**

| Criterion | Assessment |
|-----------|------------|
| Statistical significance | ✓ Pass (p < 0.01) |
| Effect stability | ✗ Fail (volatile week-to-week) |
| Causal credibility | ⚠ Uncertain (pre-period differences exist) |
| Practical significance | ⚠ Marginal (3.6% of mean) |
| Plausible mechanism | ✗ Unclear (unexpected effect) |

**Rationale:** A procedure change expected to have no effect on service scores should not produce a pattern of alternating positive and negative weekly impacts. This instability, combined with pre-existing differences between groups, suggests the observed effect may not be causal or reliable. The board should **require additional evidence** before approving system-wide implementation.

---

*Analysis based on branches.csv: 150 branches, 17 weeks of data (weeks 6–22), 2,550 observations. Statistical models use cluster-robust standard errors clustered by branch.*
