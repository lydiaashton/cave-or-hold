# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

## **Title: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

**Date:** November 2025

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores, though the effect is modest.** Branches that adopted the new procedure achieved service scores **1.4 to 1.6 points higher** than expected based on pre-existing trends. This represents approximately 3% improvement. We recommend **proceeding with rollout**, but with tempered expectations and close monitoring.

---

## **Final Verdict**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Recommendation** | ✅ **APPROVE ROLLOUT** |
| **Confidence Level** | Moderate (pre-treatment trends were not perfectly parallel) |
| **Expected Impact** | +1.4 to +1.6 points on service score (~3% improvement) |
| **Risk Level** | Low (no negative effects observed) |
| **Implementation Priority** | Standard (not urgent, but worthwhile) |

**Rationale:** The evidence supports expansion despite some methodological limitations. The improvement is real, sustained, and achieved without additional resources. However, we should monitor outcomes to confirm benefits match expectations.

---

## **Key Findings**

### The Evidence

| Metric | Switched Branches | Non-Switched Branches |
|--------|------------------|----------------------|
| Average score before week 15 | 51.9 | 51.2 |
| Average score after week 15 | 53.3 | 50.5 |
| **Change** | **+1.3** | **-0.6** |

### Important Caveat

Treatment branches were already on a slightly better trajectory before the switch. Accounting for this pre-existing trend, the **conservative estimate is +1.4 to +1.6 points** rather than the headline +1.95 points.

### Why We Still Recommend Rollout

1. **Effect is robust:** Even conservative estimates show a positive, meaningful improvement
2. **Sustained over time:** The gap persisted across all 8 post-switch weeks
3. **No downside observed:** Switched branches did not experience any negative effects
4. **Low implementation cost:** The procedure change requires training but no additional resources

**Proceed with rollout, but monitor carefully.** The evidence supports expansion, though the benefit is smaller than initially apparent. The improvement is real and meaningful for patrons, but we should track outcomes to ensure expected benefits materialize.
