# **MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure is associated with a **2-point improvement** in service scores (approximately 4%), which would be operationally meaningful if real. However, the result is **not statistically significant** (p = 0.11), primarily because the study lacks sufficient power to detect effects of this magnitude with confidence.

**VERDICT: Do not proceed with immediate system-wide mandatory rollout.** The evidence is promising but inconclusive. We recommend a **phased voluntary expansion** to interested branches while continuing to monitor outcomes, rather than requiring all branches to adopt the new procedure.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Service scores average around 50 points; higher scores indicate better service

### Results Summary

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|----------------|---------------|--------|
| Control (Old Procedure) | 47.09 | 45.96 | −1.13 |
| Treatment (New Procedure) | 49.36 | 50.25 | +0.89 |

**Estimated Effect of New Procedure: +2.02 points** (approximately 4% improvement from baseline)

### Statistical Confidence
- **95% Confidence Interval:** −0.5 to +4.7 points
- **p-value:** 0.11 (above the 0.05 threshold for statistical significance)
- **Study Limitation:** With 34 branches, we can only reliably detect effects larger than ~3.5 points. The observed 2-point effect is meaningful but too small for this sample to confirm with confidence.

### What We Can Conclude
1. **No evidence of harm.** Treatment branches improved while control branches declined slightly.
2. **Positive signal, not proof.** The 2-point improvement is consistent with a real benefit, but also consistent with random variation.
3. **Results hold after adjusting for staffing and visitor volume.** These factors affect service scores but do not explain the observed difference between groups.

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did the new procedure improve service scores? | **Possibly, but not confirmed** |
| Is there evidence of harm? | **No** |
| Is the evidence strong enough for mandatory system-wide rollout? | **No** |
| Is continued exploration warranted? | **Yes** |

**Recommended Path Forward:**
1. **Allow voluntary adoption** by additional branches that wish to try the new procedure
2. **Continue data collection** for at least 8–12 more weeks
3. **Re-evaluate** with expanded data in Q2 2027
4. **Defer mandatory rollout** until evidence is conclusive

---

## **Next Steps**

### Immediate Actions

| Action | Timeline | Responsible Party |
|--------|----------|-------------------|
| Continue pilot with current 20 treatment branches | Ongoing | Operations Team |
| Open voluntary adoption to interested branches | Q1 2027 | Operations Team |
| Collect 8–12 additional weeks of data | Next 3 months | Data Team |
| Survey branch staff on operational impacts | Next 4 weeks | HR/Operations |

### Decision Framework

**Proceed with mandatory rollout if:**
- Extended data shows statistically significant improvement (p < 0.05)
- Effect size is sustained at 1.5+ points
- Staff feedback indicates operational benefits outweigh costs

**Discontinue or modify if:**
- Extended data shows no effect or negative effect
- Staff report significant operational burdens
- Adoption costs exceed benefits

---

## **Conclusion**

The new procedure shows a positive signal but lacks definitive evidence of effectiveness. A phased voluntary expansion balances the potential benefits against the risk of mandating a procedure that may not deliver meaningful improvements. We will return to the Board in Q2 2027 with updated findings and a final recommendation on system-wide adoption.
