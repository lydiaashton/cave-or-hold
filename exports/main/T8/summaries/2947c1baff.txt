# **Board Memo: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**The data show an unexpected improvement in service scores.** Branches using the new procedure scored **2.4 points higher** (approximately **5%**) than those keeping the old procedure. This contradicts the expectation that the procedural change would have no effect on service quality. While the statistical evidence is solid, the unexpected nature of this finding warrants a **phased rollout with close monitoring** rather than immediate system-wide adoption.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measured Effect
- **Difference-in-differences estimate:** +2.37 points (95% CI: 1.07 to 3.68)
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001
- **Pre-switch:** Treatment branches averaged 1.0 points *below* control branches
- **Post-switch:** Treatment branches averaged 1.4 points *above* control branches

### 2. Why This Is Surprising
The cooperative expected this operational change to have **no measurable impact** on service scores. The positive effect suggests either:
- An unanticipated benefit (e.g., staff can find items faster, patrons experience shorter wait times)
- A confounding factor we haven't identified
- A temporary novelty effect that may fade

### 3. Analytical Validity
- **Parallel trends:** Pre-treatment differences between groups were not statistically significant (joint test p = 0.35)
- **Consistency:** All 8 post-switch weeks showed positive effects
- **Robustness:** Result holds with controls for staffing and visitor volume

### 4. Study Scope
- 150 branches (90 treatment, 60 control)
- 17 weeks of data (weeks 6-22)
- Switch occurred at week 15

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Pause full rollout** — expand the new procedure to an additional 20-30 branches as a validation pilot

2. **Gather qualitative data** — interview staff and patrons at treatment branches to understand *why* scores improved

3. **Monitor for effect decay** — track whether the 2.4-point gain persists beyond the initial 8-week observation window

4. **Reconvene in 8-12 weeks** — review validation pilot results before deciding on system-wide adoption

5. **Document the mechanism** — if a clear explanation emerges, this could inform future operational decisions

---

## **Final Verdict**

**Proceed with cautious expansion.** The statistical evidence is strong enough to justify continuing the new procedure, but unexpected findings deserve verification before committing the entire cooperative. Approve a limited pilot expansion (20-30 additional branches) with explicit evaluation criteria. If the effect holds steady over the next 8-12 weeks and a plausible mechanism is identified, authorize full rollout. If the effect diminishes or remains unexplained, reconsider the investment required for system-wide implementation.

---

## **Technical Notes**

Analysis used difference-in-differences with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch). The parallel trends assumption appears reasonable, though the unexpected direction of the effect suggests caution in interpretation.
