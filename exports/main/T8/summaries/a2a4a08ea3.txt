# **Memorandum to the Board**

**Subject:** Impact of New Branch Procedure on Service Scores  
**Date:** November 15, 2026

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure **did not improve service scores in absolute terms**—branches that switched remained essentially flat (+0.15 points). However, branches that kept the old procedure **declined by 2.2 points** after week 15. The new procedure appears to have **protected against deterioration** rather than driving improvement.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rollout, but investigate the control group decline first to understand the risk being mitigated.

---

## **Key Findings**

### What the Data Actually Shows

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|----------------|---------------|--------|
| Branches with new procedure | 52.15 | 52.30 | **+0.15** (negligible) |
| Branches with old procedure | 52.72 | 50.56 | **−2.17** (notable decline) |
| **Difference** | | | **+2.31** |

### Statistical Summary
- **Effect estimate:** +2.30 points (95% CI: 0.76 to 3.86)
- **P-value:** 0.0035 (statistically significant)
- **But:** The treatment group itself showed **no meaningful improvement**

### What This Means

The new procedure's benefit is **relative, not absolute**. Something caused service scores to drop across the system after week 15 (seasonal factors? staffing changes? increased demand?). Branches using the new procedure were **resilient to this decline**; branches using the old procedure were **vulnerable**.

---

## **Recommendations**

| Priority | Action |
|----------|--------|
| **1** | **Investigate the control group decline** before proceeding—what caused scores to drop after week 15? |
| **2** | **If the decline reflects an ongoing risk**, proceed with rollout as a protective measure |
| **3** | **If the decline was a one-time anomaly**, the business case for rollout is weaker |
| **4** | **Set realistic expectations**: The new procedure maintains service quality; it does not enhance it |

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **Did the new procedure improve service scores?** | **No** — treatment branches stayed flat |
| **Is there a statistically significant effect?** | **Yes** — but it reflects protection against decline |
| **Should we roll out to all branches?** | **Yes, with conditions** — if the factors that caused the control decline persist, the new procedure provides valuable stability |
| **What should the board expect?** | **Stability, not improvement** — the new procedure prevents scores from falling; it does not raise them |

### Bottom Line on Rollout

**Approve the rollout**, but frame it honestly: this is a **risk mitigation measure**, not a quality enhancement. The new procedure shields branches from whatever caused the post-week-15 decline. Before full implementation, we recommend a brief investigation into that decline to confirm the risk is ongoing and not a one-time anomaly.
