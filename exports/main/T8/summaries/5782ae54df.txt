# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Rotation Procedure  

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores and should be rolled out system-wide.**

Branches that adopted the new shelving and rotation procedure experienced service score increases of approximately **2.0 points** compared to branches that retained the old procedure. This represents a **4% improvement** over baseline performance. The finding is statistically significant and holds across multiple analytical approaches.

**Recommendation:** Approve immediate rollout of the new procedure to all 150 branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
| | |
|---|---|
| **Branches studied** | 150 (90 treatment, 60 control) |
| **Observation period** | 17 weeks (weeks 6–22) |
| **Procedure switch** | Week 15 |
| **Analysis method** | Difference-in-Differences with fixed effects |

### Service Score Results

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 50.59 | 51.02 | +0.43 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 50.26 | 52.62 | +2.36 |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | | | **+1.98** |

### Statistical Confidence
- **Effect size:** +1.98 service score points
- **95% confidence interval:** 1.19 to 2.77 points
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001
- **Robustness:** Effect persists after controlling for branch characteristics, seasonal variation, staffing levels, and visitor volume

### Validity Assurance
✓ **Comparable groups:** Treatment and control branches had statistically equivalent service scores before the switch (p = 0.56)  
✓ **Clean pre-trends:** Week-by-week gaps between groups fluctuated around zero before week 15, then turned consistently positive afterward  
✓ **No confounding:** Results hold when accounting for staffing and visitation changes

---

## **Final Verdict**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Did the new procedure change service scores?** | **Yes** |
| **Direction of change** | Positive (improvement) |
| **Confidence in finding** | High (p < 0.001) |
| **Rollout recommendation** | **Proceed** |

**Rationale:** The analysis provides strong causal evidence that the new procedure caused service score improvements. The effect is statistically significant, consistent across post-treatment weeks, and robust to alternative model specifications. While the magnitude (~4% improvement) is modest, it represents a meaningful gain in service quality with no identified negative consequences.
