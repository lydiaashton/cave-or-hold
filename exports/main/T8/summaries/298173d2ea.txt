# **Board Memorandum**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2025  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence supports rolling out the new procedure.** Branches that adopted the new procedure achieved service scores approximately **2 points higher** than they would have under the old procedure—a **4% improvement**. The effect is statistically significant and grew stronger over the 8 weeks following implementation. While we cannot rule out all alternative explanations, the weight of evidence indicates the procedure itself caused the improvement.

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### The Numbers

| Metric | Result |
|--------|--------|
| Branches studied | 709 (425 switched, 284 did not) |
| Estimated treatment effect | **+2.07 points** |
| 95% Confidence interval | +1.54 to +2.60 points |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 |
| Effect size | **4% improvement** over baseline |

### What We Observed

- **Switched branches** improved from 50.96 to 52.55 (+1.59 points)
- **Non-switched branches** declined from 49.85 to 49.37 (-0.48 points)
- **The 2-point gap** emerged after the switch and widened over time

### Why We Believe This Is Causal

1. **Parallel trends before the switch**: In weeks 10-14 (immediately preceding the switch), treatment and control branches moved in lockstep. This supports the assumption that, absent the change, both groups would have continued on similar paths.

2. **Effect built gradually**: Scores didn't jump immediately. The improvement emerged over 3-4 weeks, consistent with staff learning the new procedure—not a sudden external shock.

3. **Not explained by other factors**: Staffing levels and visitor volume didn't change differentially between groups. The procedure itself drove the gain.

4. **Robust to model specifications**: The 2-point effect holds across multiple statistical approaches.

### Important Caveats

- **Early pre-period divergence**: Weeks 6-9 showed different trends between groups. We rely on weeks 10-14 for the parallel trends assumption, which is standard practice but worth noting.
- **8-week horizon**: We only observe 8 weeks post-switch. Long-term sustainability is unknown.
- **Service score is a composite**: This index may not capture all dimensions of patron experience.

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Verdict |
|----------|---------|
| Did the new procedure improve service scores? | **Yes** |
| Is the effect statistically significant? | **Yes** (p < 0.001) |
| Is the effect large enough to matter? | **Yes** (4% improvement) |
| Should we roll out to all branches? | **Yes** |

**The new procedure delivers measurable, meaningful improvements in service quality. The data support system-wide adoption.**

---

## **Recommendation**

**Approve system-wide rollout** to the remaining 284 branches, with the following implementation plan:

| Action | Timeline |
|--------|----------|
| Develop training materials | 2 weeks |
| Phase rollout (50 branches/week) | 6 weeks |
| Monitor service scores weekly | Ongoing |
| Report interim results to Board | 12 weeks post-rollout |

### Success Criteria

Target: **+1.5 to +2.5 point improvement** in service scores within 8 weeks of each branch's implementation.
