# MEMORANDUM

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## BOTTOM LINE

The data show a positive association between the new procedure and service scores (+2.2 points), but we cannot confidently attribute this improvement to the procedure itself. The result is not statistically significant (p=0.10), and branches that switched were systematically different from those that did not. The board faces a decision under uncertainty: proceed based on the encouraging but inconclusive signal, or gather more evidence before committing to a system-wide rollout.

---

## KEY FINDINGS

### 1. Observed Impact on Service Scores

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| 20 branches that switched | 46.7 | 48.5 | +1.8 |
| 14 branches that did not switch | 50.3 | 49.8 | -0.4 |
| **Net estimated effect** | | | **+2.2 points** |

The new procedure is associated with a **2.2-point increase** in service scores, or approximately **4% improvement** relative to the system average of 48.6.

### 2. Confidence in the Result

| Measure | Value | Interpretation |
|---------|-------|----------------|
| Point estimate | +2.16 points | Best estimate of the effect |
| 95% confidence interval | -0.4 to +4.7 | Range of plausible values |
| P-value | 0.10 | 10% chance of this result if no real effect exists |

**What this means:** The data are consistent with a modest positive effect, but also consistent with no effect at all. Conventional statistical standards (p < 0.05) are not met.

### 3. Study Limitations

**Non-comparable groups:** Branches that switched to the new procedure had systematically lower service scores than those that did not, both before and after the switch. This suggests branches may have been selected for the new procedure precisely because they were underperforming.

**Limited post-switch data:** Only 8 weeks of data are available after the change. Effects may take longer to stabilize or may vary seasonally.

**Weekly variation:** The estimated effect fluctuates across post-switch weeks. Only week 22 shows a statistically significant improvement on its own.

### 4. Consistency Across Analyses

All model specifications produced the same point estimate (+2.16 points), which strengthens confidence in the direction of the effect:

- Basic difference-in-differences: +2.16 (p = 0.10)
- With branch fixed effects: +2.16 (p = 0.10)
- Controlling for staffing and visits: +2.16 (p = 0.11)

---

## FINAL VERDICT

**Do not proceed with a full system-wide rollout at this time.**

**Rationale:**

1. **The evidence is too weak to justify system-wide change.** A p-value of 0.10 means there is substantial uncertainty about whether the procedure actually causes improvement. Rolling out to all 34 branches based on this evidence risks investing resources in a change that may have no real effect.

2. **The study design limits causal inference.** Branches were not randomly assigned to switch. The fact that switching branches had lower baseline scores suggests selection bias, which undermines confidence in the estimated effect.

3. **More data would substantially improve decision quality.** Extending observation by 3–6 months would narrow the confidence interval and allow the board to make a more informed decision.

**Recommended Path Forward:**

| Action | Timeline | Purpose |
|--------|----------|---------|
| Continue monitoring current branches | Ongoing | Track whether effects persist or grow |
| Collect 12+ additional weeks of data | 3 months | Improve statistical precision |
| Re-analyze with extended dataset | Month 4 | Make final rollout decision with greater confidence |
| If proceeding, use phased rollout | Months 5–8 | Manage risk and allow course correction |

**Exception:** If the board determines that the potential upside (a 2-point improvement at 20 underperforming branches) outweighs the implementation costs and risk of no effect, a **phased rollout to similar underperforming branches** would be more defensible than system-wide adoption.

---

## CONCLUSION

The new procedure shows promise, but promise alone does not justify a system-wide investment. The responsible course is to gather more evidence before committing all 34 branches to the new procedure. We recommend reconvening this analysis in 3 months with an extended dataset.
