# **To: Library Cooperative Board**
# **From: Data Analysis Team**
# **Subject: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores—contrary to our expectations.** We anticipated no measurable effect, but branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure achieved service scores **2.1 points higher** than comparable branches using the old procedure. This represents a **4% improvement** in service quality. 

**Recommendation: Approve system-wide rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **What We Tested**
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure in week 15; **60 branches** continued with the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** tracked service scores before and after the change
- Analysis isolates the procedure's effect by comparing switching branches against non-switching branches over the same time period

### **What We Found**

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|---------------|---------------|--------|
| Switched to new procedure | 49.0 | 51.3 | **+2.3** |
| Kept old procedure | 49.3 | 49.5 | +0.2 |
| **Attributable to new procedure** | | | **+2.1** |

### **Why We're Confident**

✓ **Statistically significant:** The likelihood this result occurred by chance is less than 1% (p < 0.01)

✓ **Validated methodology:** Pre-switch trends were parallel between groups, confirming the comparison is sound

✓ **Sustained effect:** Improvement held steady across all 8 post-switch weeks with no decay

✓ **Robust finding:** Result persists after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### **What This Means**
- Average service score across all branches: **50**
- Improvement from new procedure: **+2.1 points**
- This is a **0.21 standard deviation** effect—meaningful for a procedural change with no additional cost

---

## **Final Verdict**

> **The evidence is clear and conclusive: the new stock rotation and shelving procedure improves service quality.** 
>
> Despite initial skepticism that this change would matter, the data shows a **statistically significant, sustained improvement** in service scores. The effect is large enough to be meaningful for patrons, the analysis is methodologically sound, and there is no credible alternative explanation for the observed gains.
>
> **Verdict: Proceed with full rollout.**

- **Data:** 2,550 observations (150 branches × 17 weeks)
