# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The data show a statistically significant but modest improvement in service scores associated with the new procedure (+1.7 points, or 3.4% above average). Since the change was expected to have no effect on service quality, this represents an unexpected benefit with no identified downside. The board may wish to proceed with rollout, weighing this modest gain against any implementation costs.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Statistically Significant Positive Effect**
- Switched branches improved by **+1.7 points** relative to non-switched branches
- **95% confidence interval: +0.6 to +2.8 points** (entirely positive)
- **P-value: 0.002** (significant at the 1% level)
- Result is robust to controls for staffing and visit volume

### **2. Modest Effect Size**
- Average service score across all branches: **50.7**
- Improvement represents **3.4% of average** or **0.18 standard deviations**
- In practical terms: a branch scoring 50 would be expected to score approximately **51.7** after adopting the new procedure

### **3. Valid Comparison**
- **90 branches** switched; **60 branches** did not
- Pre-switch scores were nearly identical between groups (49.85 vs. 50.34)
- This supports the conclusion that the procedure change, not pre-existing differences, drove the improvement

### **4. Effect Persisted Over Time**
- Positive difference observed in all 8 post-switch weeks
- No evidence of the effect diminishing during the observation period

---

## **Final Verdict**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Statistical Evidence** | ✅ Clear and significant (p = 0.002) |
| **Direction of Effect** | ✅ Positive (unexpected benefit) |
| **Effect Size** | ⚠️ Modest (+1.7 points on 50-point scale) |
| **Identified Downsides** | ✅ None in the data |
| **Validity of Design** | ✅ Strong (balanced pre-treatment groups) |

### **Recommendation: Proceed with Rollout**

**Rationale:**
1. The effect is **real, not a statistical artifact**—the confidence interval excludes zero
2. The effect is **positive, not negative**—no risk of harming service quality
3. **No downside identified** in the available data
4. Since the procedure was expected to have **zero effect**, any positive gain is a net benefit
5. The modest effect size suggests **low-cost implementation** would be most appropriate

**Caveat:** If rollout requires substantial investment (training, labor, disruption), the board should conduct a cost-benefit analysis. At ~3% improvement, the benefit may not justify major expenses.
