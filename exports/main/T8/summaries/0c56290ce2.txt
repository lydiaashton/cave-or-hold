# **Memorandum to the Board**

**Subject:** Impact of New Branch Shelving and Rotation Procedure on Service Scores  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Analysis Period:** Weeks 6–22 (17 weeks of data from 150 branches)

---

## **Bottom Line**

The data show a **statistically significant but modest improvement** in service scores at branches that adopted the new procedure. The estimated effect is **+1.9 points** (95% CI: +0.3 to +3.6), contradicting the prior expectation of no effect. This represents approximately a 4% improvement relative to the baseline score of 50.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with the rollout, but with tempered expectations. The improvement is real but small.

---

## **Key Findings**

### What We Measured

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|---------------|---------------|--------|
| Branches keeping old procedure | 50.59 | 51.02 | +0.43 |
| Branches using new procedure | 50.26 | 52.62 | +2.36 |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | | **+1.93** |

### Statistical Confidence

- **Effect size:** +1.93 service score points
- **95% confidence interval:** +0.29 to +3.57 points
- **Significance level:** p = 0.021 (significant at 5% level)
- **Relative improvement:** ~4% above baseline

### Why We Trust This Result

1. **Comparable groups:** Before the switch, treatment and control branches had similar service scores (difference not statistically significant, p = 0.56)
2. **Controls for confounders:** The analysis accounts for branch-specific factors, week-to-week variation, staffing levels, and visitor volume
3. **Consistent pattern:** Weekly gaps between treatment and control branches turned positive after week 15 and remained positive

---

## **Context**

The board's prior expectation—that this operational change would not affect service scores—was reasonable. Shelving and rotation procedures are back-office functions that might not obviously connect to patron-facing service quality. However, the data indicate a small positive effect. Possible explanations worth exploring:

- Faster item retrieval by staff
- Improved shelf organization noticed by patrons
- Staff morale or efficiency gains translating to better service interactions

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did the new procedure change service scores? | **Yes** |
| Is the effect statistically significant? | **Yes** (p = 0.02) |
| Is the effect large? | **No** (~4% improvement) |
| Should we roll it out system-wide? | **Yes, with caveats** |

**Reasoning:** The effect is genuine and unlikely to be random noise. However, it is modest. The rollout decision should weigh this small benefit against any additional operational costs or training burdens the new procedure imposes. If the new procedure is cost-neutral or low-cost, it is worth implementing. If it requires significant investment, the board may wish to reconsider.

This analysis overturns the prior assumption that the procedure change would have no effect. The new procedure does improve service scores, but the gain is modest. The board should approve the rollout if operational costs are low, while maintaining realistic expectations about the magnitude of improvement.
