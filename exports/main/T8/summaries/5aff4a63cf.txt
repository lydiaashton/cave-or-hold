# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence indicates the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched saw scores increase by an average of **2.1 points** more than branches that kept the old procedure. This difference is statistically significant and unlikely to be due to chance. **We recommend proceeding with system-wide rollout, with continued monitoring to confirm the effect holds at scale.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measurable Improvement in Service Quality

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Treatment (switched) | 50.1 | 53.8 | +3.7 |
| Control (no switch) | 48.2 | 49.7 | +1.5 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+2.1** |

- The **net effect of +2.1 points** is highly statistically significant (p < 0.001)
- 95% confidence interval: **0.9 to 3.4 points**
- On a scale where the average branch scores about 50, this represents approximately **4% improvement**

### 2. Evidence Supports Causal Interpretation

- **Parallel pre-trends:** Before the switch (weeks 6–14), the gap between treatment and control branches remained stable
- **Clear divergence:** After the switch (weeks 15–22), treatment branches consistently pulled ahead
- **Alternative explanations tested:** Staffing and visitation changes do not account for the improvement

### 3. Study Design

- **150 branches** total: 90 adopted the new procedure, 60 kept the old
- **17 weeks of data:** weeks 6–22, with the switch occurring at week 15
- **2,550 observations** analyzed using difference-in-differences regression

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Criterion | Assessment |
|-----------|------------|
| **Statistical significance** | ✓ Passed (p = 0.0008) |
| **Confidence interval** | ✓ Passed (entirely positive: 0.9 to 3.4) |
| **Parallel trends assumption** | ✓ Passed (no pre-trend divergence detected) |
| **Alternative explanations** | ✓ Ruled out (staffing, visits controlled) |
| **Practical significance** | ✓ Modest but meaningful (~4% improvement) |
| **Study limitations** | ⚠ Observational data (not randomized) |

**VERDICT: Proceed with rollout.**

The evidence meets the threshold for action. While this is observational data rather than a randomized trial, the difference-in-differences design, parallel pre-trends, and robustness checks provide sufficient confidence to recommend system-wide adoption. The risk of proceeding is low; the procedure can be adjusted or reversed if post-rollout monitoring reveals unexpected issues.

---

## **Recommendations**

| Priority | Action |
|----------|--------|
| **1** | Approve system-wide rollout of the new procedure to all 150 branches |
| **2** | Develop implementation support for the 60 branches still using the old procedure (training, materials, phased rollout) |
| **3** | Continue monitoring service scores post-rollout to confirm sustained improvement |
| **4** | Document the procedure in detail for institutional knowledge |
| **5** | Conduct follow-up analysis in 6–12 months to assess long-term effects |

---

## **Conclusion**

The data provide strong evidence that the new rotation and shelving procedure improves service quality. The effect is statistically significant, practically meaningful, and robust to alternative explanations. Rolling out this procedure system-wide is expected to raise the cooperative's overall service performance.
