# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact Analysis**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores by about 2.1 points—contrary to expectations.** This represents a meaningful, unexpected benefit. However, since the original decision framework did not anticipate service score changes, the board should weigh this new evidence against whatever operational considerations (cost, staff time, logistics) originally guided the rollout plan. If those factors remain favorable, the service score improvement strengthens the case for system-wide adoption.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Unexpected Improvement in Service Scores**

| Group | Before Switch (Weeks 6-14) | After Switch (Weeks 15-22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------------|---------------------------|--------|
| New Procedure Branches (90 branches) | 50.11 | 52.35 | **+2.23** |
| Old Procedure Branches (60 branches) | 50.61 | 50.73 | +0.12 |

**Net effect of the new procedure: +2.11 points**

### **2. Statistical Confidence**

- The improvement is **statistically significant** (p < 0.01)
- 95% confidence interval: **+0.92 to +3.38 points**
- The analysis controlled for staffing levels and visitor volume

### **3. Broad-Based Benefits**

- **76% of switched branches** saw their service scores improve
- Only **53% of control branches** improved during the same period
- The effect represents about **0.23 standard deviations**—a meaningful improvement

### **4. Valid Comparison**

- Before the switch, both groups followed nearly identical trends (correlation of 0.99)
- This confirms the improvement is attributable to the procedure change, not pre-existing differences

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Revisit the original business case** for the procedure change with this new information in hand.

2. **Confirm there are no offsetting costs**—the service score gain is valuable only if the new procedure doesn't create burdens elsewhere (staff time, training, compliance).

3. **If operational factors remain neutral or positive**, proceed with rollout to all 60 remaining branches.

4. **If there are concerns**, consider a cost-benefit analysis that quantifies the value of a 2-point service score improvement against any additional operational costs.

5. **Gather qualitative feedback** from switched branches to understand why service scores improved—this was unexpected and worth understanding.

---

## **Final Verdict**

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout.**

The evidence is robust: the new procedure delivers a statistically significant, meaningful improvement in service scores with no apparent downside in the data we examined. While this benefit was not anticipated, it represents genuine value to patrons and strengthens the business case for adoption. Barring any undisclosed operational costs or constraints, rolling out the procedure to all 60 remaining branches is the sound decision.
