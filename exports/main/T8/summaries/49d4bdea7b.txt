# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD OF DIRECTORS**

**TO:** Board of Directors  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Rotation Procedure

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The evidence is inconclusive.** While branches that adopted the new procedure show higher service scores, **we cannot confidently attribute this improvement to the procedure itself.** The branches that switched were already improving relative to other branches *before* the change was implemented.

**Recommendation:** Do **not** approve system-wide rollout based on current evidence.

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

> **❌ DO NOT PROCEED WITH SYSTEM-WIDE ROLLOUT**
>
> **Reason:** The analysis cannot distinguish whether the observed improvement is caused by the new procedure or by pre-existing differences between branches that switched versus those that did not.
>
> **What we need before proceeding:** A properly randomized evaluation that assigns branches to treatment and control groups by chance, not by selection.
>
> **Risk of proceeding now:** The cooperative could invest significant resources in a procedure that provides no real benefit—or worse, one that is costlier than the old procedure without delivering improved service.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### What the Data Shows

| Period | Control Group | Treated Group | Gap Between Them |
|--------|--------------|---------------|------------------|
| Week 6 (earliest) | 49.96 | 50.04 | +0.08 |
| Week 14 (just before switch) | 46.52 | 48.83 | +2.31 |
| Week 15 (first week after) | 53.10 | 55.87 | +2.77 |
| Week 22 (latest) | 54.78 | 58.05 | +3.27 |

### The Critical Problem

**The gap between groups was already widening BEFORE the switch:**
- Week 6: Treated branches led by only **0.08 points**
- Week 14: Treated branches led by **2.31 points**
- This represents a **2.23-point divergence** before the procedure even changed

**The actual "jump" at implementation was small:**
- Week 14 to Week 15: Only **+0.46 points** additional gap
- This is much smaller than the 1.92-point effect my initial analysis claimed

### Why This Matters

The standard difference-in-differences method assumes that treated and control groups would have followed **parallel trends** without the intervention. **This assumption is violated here.** The treated branches were already on a different trajectory before the switch.

### Possible Explanations

1. **Selection bias**: Better-performing or more motivated branches may have been chosen (or volunteered) to switch first
2. **Anticipation effects**: Branches may have improved in preparation for the switch
3. **Unobserved factors**: Something else may be driving both the selection and the improvement

---

## **LESSONS LEARNED**

1. **Natural experiments require careful validation**: We assumed the switch timing created a clean comparison, but pre-existing trends undermined this.

2. **Event study analysis is essential**: Our event study showed diverging pre-trends, which should have been highlighted upfront.

3. **Randomization is the gold standard**: For future procedure changes, randomly assigning branches to treatment would provide much stronger evidence.
