# **Memorandum to the Board**

## **Title: Impact of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure on Service Scores**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure had a statistically significant positive effect, contrary to expectations.** On average, switched branches scored **1.9 points higher** than they would have under the old procedure (p = 0.002). However, the effect varied considerably: roughly **6 out of 10 branches improved**, while **4 out of 10 saw no benefit or declined**. We recommend proceeding with the rollout, but with realistic expectations and branch-level monitoring.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Statistically Significant, Modest Effect
- **Average treatment effect:** +1.9 points (95% CI: 0.7 to 3.1)
- **Relative to baseline:** ~4% improvement on a ~51-point scale
- **Statistical confidence:** p = 0.002; this is unlikely to be random noise

### 2. Substantial Variation Across Branches
| Outcome | Treated Branches | Control Branches |
|---------|------------------|------------------|
| Improved | 56 (62%) | 22 (37%) |
| Declined | 34 (38%) | 38 (63%) |
| Average change | +0.74 | -1.05 |

The new procedure appears to both **raise scores for some branches** and **protect against decline** that affected most control branches.

### 3. Why We Trust This Result
- **Parallel pre-trends verified:** No systematic differences before the switch (p = 0.42)
- **Effect persisted:** Observed across most post-switch weeks
- **No obvious confounders:** Staffing and visit patterns didn't differ between groups

### 4. Effect Size in Context
- **Small-to-moderate** (Cohen's d = 0.19)
- Meaningful for a no-cost procedural change
- Not transformative—should not be oversold

---

## **Final Verdict**

**Recommendation: PROCEED WITH ROLLOUT**

**Rationale:**
| Consideration | Assessment |
|---------------|------------|
| **Statistical evidence** | ✓ Significant (p = 0.002) |
| **Direction of effect** | ✓ Positive (unexpected benefit) |
| **Cost of implementation** | ✓ Low (procedural change only) |
| **Risk of harm** | ✓ None detected (no negative spillovers) |
| **Consistency** | ⚠ Moderate (60% of branches benefited) |
| **Effect size** | ⚠ Modest (~4% improvement) |

**Bottom line:** The evidence supports expansion. This is a low-risk, low-cost change with a positive average effect. Even if only 60% of branches see meaningful gains, the system-wide benefit outweighs the cost of implementation. Branches that don't improve are no worse off than they would have been under the old procedure.

---

## **Next Steps**

### Recommended Actions

1. **Proceed with rollout, but calibrate expectations**
   - Expect improvement at ~60% of branches, not all
   - Average gain of ~2 points where it works

2. **Identify which branches benefit most**
   - Analyze characteristics of the 56 treated branches that improved
   - Use this to prioritize rollout sequence or target support

3. **Monitor closely during expansion**
   - Track each branch for 8–12 weeks post-switch
   - Branches showing no improvement after 4–6 weeks may need additional support

4. **Investigate the mechanism**
   - We expected no effect—understanding why there IS one could inform future decisions
   - Interview staff at high-performing switched branches

5. **Consider complementary interventions**
   - For the ~40% of branches unlikely to benefit, explore what else might help

---

## **Caveats**

- **Not a universal solution:** A meaningful minority of branches did not benefit
- **Short observation window:** Only 8 weeks of post-implementation data
- **Unknown long-term effects:** Sustainability of the improvement is untested
- **Context-specific:** Effect may differ when applied to branches with different characteristics

---

## **Analysis Notes**

- **Data:** 2,550 weekly observations from 150 branches, weeks 6–22
- **Method:** Difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors
- **Controls:** Staffing index and visits index
