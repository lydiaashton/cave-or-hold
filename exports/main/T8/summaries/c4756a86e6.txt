# **Library Cooperative Board Memo**

## **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

### **BOTTOM LINE**

The analysis does **not show a statistically significant effect** at conventional standards (p=0.11). However, the evidence **leans positive**: the point estimate suggests a 2-point improvement, 94% of the probability distribution favors a positive effect, and 80% of treatment branches improved. Given the low apparent risk and potential benefit, we recommend a **cautious phased expansion** with a decision checkpoint in 3 months.

---

### **KEY FINDINGS**

| Finding | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Estimated improvement** | +2.1 service score points (≈4% gain) |
| **Statistical confidence** | Not conclusive (p=0.11); 95% CI ranges from -0.5 to +4.7 points |
| **Probability of positive effect** | ~94% |
| **Study scope** | 34 branches, 17 weeks, 578 observations |
| **Branch-level pattern** | 80% of treatment branches improved vs. 64% of control branches |

**What the data show:**
- Treatment branches improved by **3.2 points** on average after switching (from 50.8 to 53.9)
- Control branches improved by **1.1 points** over the same period (from 52.2 to 53.2)
- The **extra 2.1-point gain** for treatment branches is the estimated effect of the new procedure
- This pattern holds even after accounting for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume

**What we cannot rule out:**
- There is an 11% probability that the observed improvement is due to random chance
- The true effect could be zero or slightly negative (down to -0.5 points)

---

### **FINAL VERDICT**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Statistical significance** | ❌ Not achieved (p=0.11) |
| **Practical significance** | ⚠️ Modest (+2 points, ~4%) |
| **Direction of evidence** | ✓ Favors new procedure |
| **Risk of harm** | ✓ Appears low |
| **Recommendation** | **Proceed with phased rollout** |

**Rationale:** While we lack definitive proof, the weight of evidence favors the new procedure. The cost of waiting for certainty (additional months of data collection) likely exceeds the risk of gradual expansion. A full rollout is not yet warranted, but neither is abandoning the procedure.
