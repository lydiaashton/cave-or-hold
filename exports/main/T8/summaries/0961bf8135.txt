# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

**Prepared for the Library Cooperative Board**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure helped protect service scores during a period of system-wide decline.** While service scores fell across all branches after week 15, branches using the new procedure experienced a much smaller decline (−0.4 points) compared to branches using the old procedure (−2.4 points). The new procedure provided a **2-point buffer** against this decline. This protective effect supports rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **150 branches** tracked over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** switched to the new procedure at week 15 (treatment group)
- **60 branches** kept the old procedure (control group)

### **Results**

| Period | Treatment Branches | Control Branches |
|--------|-------------------|------------------|
| **Before switch** (weeks 6–14) | 53.31 | 52.28 |
| **After switch** (weeks 15–22) | 52.92 | 49.91 |
| **Change** | **−0.39** | **−2.37** |

- **Treatment effect: +1.98 points** relative to control (95% CI: 1.22 to 2.74)
- **Statistically significant** (p < 0.001)
- **Pre-trends were parallel**, validating the comparison

### **Important Context**
- Service scores declined system-wide after week 15 (possibly seasonal or due to external factors)
- The new procedure did not increase scores but **protected against decline**
- No detectable effects on staffing levels or visit volumes

---

## **Final Verdict**

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout.**

The evidence indicates the new procedure provides meaningful protection for service quality during challenging periods. While the original expectation was that the change would make no difference, the data show it delivers a **statistically significant benefit** of approximately 2 service score points. 

Given that:
- The effect is statistically robust (p < 0.001)
- There are no detected negative side effects on staffing or visits
- The procedure helps maintain service quality when external pressures arise

**The board should approve implementing the new procedure across all 60 branches still using the old method.**

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all 60 branches currently using the old method

2. **Investigate the system-wide decline** in weeks 15–22 to understand what drove scores down across all branches

3. **Monitor implementation** to confirm the protective effect persists

4. **Document the new procedure** to ensure consistent adoption

5. **Continue tracking** all metrics to detect any longer-term effects

---

## **Technical Note**

Analysis uses difference-in-differences with branch and week fixed effects, controlling for branch-specific characteristics and common time trends. The parallel pre-trends test (p = 0.27) supports the validity of the causal interpretation.
