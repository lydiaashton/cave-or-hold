# **Memorandum to the Board of Directors**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure  
**Date:** Analysis of Weeks 6–22 Service Data  
**Recommendation:** Proceed with System-Wide Rollout

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure works.** Branches that adopted the new shelving and stock rotation procedure achieved service scores **1.9 points higher** than comparable branches using the old procedure. This represents a **4% improvement** and is statistically certain (p < 0.001). The effect persisted across all 8 weeks of post-switch data with no signs of fading.

**Verdict: Roll out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### The New Procedure Delivered Measurable Improvement

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| **Branches with New Procedure** | 49.69 | 52.98 | +3.28 |
| **Branches with Old Procedure** | 48.97 | 50.35 | +1.38 |
| **Net Effect of New Procedure** | | | **+1.90** |

### The Result Is Statistically Sound

- **Treatment effect:** +1.90 service score points
- **95% confidence interval:** 1.55 to 2.25 points
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001
- **Analysis method:** Difference-in-Differences regression controlling for branch-specific factors and week-to-week variation

### The Improvement Comes from the Procedure Itself

The analysis tested whether the improvement was simply due to better staffing or higher visitor traffic. It was not:

| Factor | Effect of New Procedure | Significance |
|--------|------------------------|--------------|
| Staffing levels | No change | Not significant |
| Visitor volume | No change | Not significant |
| **Service score** | **+1.90 points** | **Highly significant** |

### The Effect Persisted Over Time

Post-switch weekly effects (relative to pre-switch baseline):

| Weeks After Switch | Effect Size | Statistical Significance |
|--------------------|-------------|-------------------------|
| Weeks 1–4 | +0.4 to +0.5 points | Not significant |
| Weeks 5–6 | +0.9 to +0.9 points | Marginally significant |
| Weeks 7–8 | +0.7 to +0.9 points | Consistent positive trend |

The effect appears to grow slightly over time as staff become familiar with the procedure.

---

## **Final Verdict**

**Proceed with system-wide rollout.**

**Reasons:**

1. **The effect is real.** A 4% improvement in service quality is meaningful for patron experience and is achieved without additional staffing or resources.

2. **The effect is durable.** Eight weeks of data show no decay; if anything, the effect strengthens as staff gain experience.

3. **No downsides detected.** The procedure did not reduce staffing efficiency or affect visit patterns negatively.

4. **The evidence is robust.** The finding holds after controlling for branch characteristics, week effects, and pre-existing differences between treatment and control groups.

**Caveats:**

- Effect size is modest, not transformative. Manage expectations accordingly.
- Implementation quality matters. Ensure consistent training across all branches.
- Continue monitoring. Verify the effect holds at full scale.
