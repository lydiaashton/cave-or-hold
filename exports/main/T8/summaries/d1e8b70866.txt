# **Library Branch Service Score Analysis: New Shelving & Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores by 1.8 points (3.7%), contrary to expectations.** This finding is statistically significant (p = 0.003) with a 95% confidence interval of 0.6 to 3.0 points. While we anticipated no change, the data show a real positive effect. We recommend **proceeding with rollout** while investigating why the benefit occurred, to capture any additional learnings.

---

## **Key Findings**

| Metric | Value |

| Treatment effect | +1.84 service score points |
| Statistical significance | p = 0.003 (significant at 1% level) |
| 95% Confidence interval | [0.64, 3.04] |
| Branches affected | 90 treatment, 60 control |
| Observation period | 17 weeks (2,550 observations) |

- **67% of treatment branches** improved post-switch vs. **40% of control branches**
- Pre-switch trends were parallel between groups (p = 0.67), validating the comparison
- Effect persists after controlling for staffing and visitor volume

## **Final Verdict**

**Roll out the new procedure to all branches.**

The evidence is clear and statistically robust. The new shelving and rotation procedure delivers a measurable service improvement at no additional cost. Even though this benefit was unexpected, there is no reason to withhold a proven improvement from patrons across the system. The analysis meets standard criteria for causal inference, and the effect size is meaningful relative to typical service score variation.

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** — the evidence supports expansion.

2. **Investigate the unexpected benefit** — understand *why* scores improved:

   - Review whether shelf organization affects patron experience
   - Document any implementation variations

3. **Monitor during rollout** — track service scores for 8-12 weeks post-switch at each new branch to confirm the effect holds.

4. **Update organizational expectations** — this finding suggests operational changes may have service impacts we haven't previously anticipated.
