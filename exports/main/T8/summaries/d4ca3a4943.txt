# Library Cooperative Branch Procedure Change: Impact Analysis

---

## Bottom Line

**The new shelving and stock rotation procedure produced a statistically significant improvement in service scores.** Branches that switched saw scores increase by approximately **2.1 points** (4.3% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure (p = 0.002).

**Recommendation: Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

## Key Findings

### Study Design
- **150 branches** tracked over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15 (treatment group)
- **60 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis compares changes in service scores between groups before and after the switch

### Results

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 48.55 | 51.72 | +3.17 |
| Control branches | 47.24 | 48.35 | +1.11 |
| **Net effect (treatment)** | | | **+2.08** |

### Statistical Evidence
- **Treatment effect: +2.08 points**
- **95% Confidence Interval: 0.75 to 3.40 points**
- **P-value: 0.002** (highly significant)
- Effect remains significant after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volumes
- Consistent across multiple analytical approaches

### Validity Check
Before the switch (weeks 6–14), both groups showed similar patterns. In week 14 (the last pre-switch week), the groups nearly converged (gap of only 0.16 points). After week 15, the gap widened to 2–4 points and remained elevated across all 8 post-switch weeks.

---

## Interpretation

The board expected the procedure change would make no difference to service scores. The data show otherwise: **the new procedure produced a meaningful and statistically significant improvement.**

The effect is:
- **Reliable**: The confidence interval excludes zero; significant at the 1% level
- **Practically meaningful**: A 2-point increase on a ~50-point scale represents noticeable improvement
- **Robust**: The effect holds after accounting for staffing and visitor volume variations
- **Consistent**: The widened gap persisted across all post-switch weeks

---

## Final Verdict

**The evidence supports system-wide adoption.** While the improvement is modest rather than transformative, it is real, reliable, and comes at no apparent cost to service quality. The new procedure should be rolled out to all remaining branches.

**Confidence level: High** — Multiple analytical approaches yield consistent results, and the pre/post patterns align with a genuine treatment effect.

---

## Next Steps

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all 60 branches currently using the old method

2. **Monitor implementation**: Track service scores at newly switched branches to confirm the effect replicates

3. **Document best practices**: Capture what the 90 early-adopter branches did during transition to support smooth rollout

4. **Report back in 8–12 weeks**: After full rollout, conduct follow-up analysis to confirm sustained improvement

---

*Analysis based on weekly service score data from 150 branches, weeks 6–22. Difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch).*
