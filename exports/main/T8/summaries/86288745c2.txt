# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence is mixed.** Branches that adopted the new procedure showed a **2.1-point improvement** in service scores on average, which is statistically significant overall. However, the effect appears strongest immediately after the switch and is less consistent in subsequent weeks. Given that this procedure change was not expected to affect service scores, we recommend a **cautious, phased rollout** with continued monitoring rather than immediate system-wide adoption.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Overall Positive Effect, But With Caveats
- **Average treatment effect: +2.1 points** (p < 0.001)
- However, week-by-week analysis shows the effect is **driven primarily by week 15** (the first week after switch)
- Weeks 17–22 show positive but **not statistically significant** effects individually

### 2. Pre-Trends Support Causal Interpretation
- No significant differences between treatment and control branches before the switch
- This strengthens confidence that the week-15 jump is related to the procedure change

### 3. Effect Size Context
- **4% improvement** over baseline scores
- **0.22 standard deviations**—small-to-moderate
- **73% of switched branches** improved vs. 48% of control branches

### 4. Why the Unexpected Result?
The procedure change was not designed to affect service scores. Possible explanations:
- Staff morale or attention increased during implementation
- The new procedure indirectly improved patron experience
- Something else coincided with week 15 that affected switched branches

---

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with a monitored pilot expansion rather than full rollout:**

1. **Expand to 20–30 additional branches** over the next quarter
2. **Track service scores weekly** for 8+ weeks at each new branch
3. **Gather qualitative feedback** from staff about what changed
4. **Re-evaluate after 3 months** with more data before deciding on system-wide adoption

## **Final Verdict**

**Proceed with caution—approve limited expansion, not full rollout.**

The statistical evidence shows a real effect, but three factors warrant restraint:

1. **Unexpected finding:** This procedure was not designed to affect service scores, so we should verify the result before scaling
2. **Fading effect:** The impact is strongest at implementation and less clear in later weeks
3. **Modest magnitude:** A 2-point gain is meaningful but not transformative

**Board decision:** Authorize expansion to 20–30 additional branches with mandatory 8-week monitoring. Return to the board in Q3 with updated data for a full rollout decision.
