# **Board Memorandum**

## **New Branch Stock Rotation & Shelving Procedure: Impact Evaluation**

**Date:** November 2026  
**Analysis Period:** Weeks 6-22 (17 weeks of data)  
**Branches Studied:** 709 (425 switched, 284 control)

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence is inconclusive.** While switched branches show higher service scores after implementation, we cannot confidently attribute this to the new procedure. The switched branches were already improving faster than control branches *before* the change, which undermines our ability to isolate the procedure's effect.

**Recommendation: Do not proceed with system-wide rollout yet.** Conduct additional testing with randomized assignment to obtain credible causal evidence.

---

## **Key Findings**

| Metric | Switched Branches | Control Branches |
|--------|------------------|------------------|
| Average score before Week 15 | 49.5 | 48.5 |
| Average score after Week 15 | 52.3 | 49.2 |
| **Observed change** | **+2.8** | **+0.8** |

**Naive estimate: +2.0 points** — but this is likely overstated.

### **Why We Cannot Trust This Estimate**

| Week | Gap (Switched − Control) |
|------|-------------------------|
| 6 | 0.38 points |
| 8 | 0.89 points |
| 10 | 0.70 points |
| 12 | 1.52 points |
| 14 (last pre-switch) | 2.31 points |
| **15 (switch implemented)** | **2.53 points** |
| 17 | 2.72 points |
| 22 | 3.39 points |

**Critical observation:** The gap widened by **1.93 points** during the pre-switch period alone. The immediate jump at implementation was only **0.22 points**.

---

## **The Core Problem: Selection Bias**

Branches selected for early adoption were not randomly chosen. The data suggests:

1. **Higher-performing branches may have been selected** — switched branches scored ~1 point higher even at baseline

2. **Branches already improving may have been prioritized** — the gap was widening steadily before the switch

3. **Standard statistical methods assume parallel trends** — this assumption is violated in our data

**Result:** The 2.0-point "effect" likely captures pre-existing differences, not the procedure's true impact.

---

## **What the Data Does Show**

- Switched branches have consistently higher service scores post-implementation
- The gap has continued to widen through Week 22
- There is a positive *association* between the procedure and scores

**What we cannot determine:** How much of this is caused by the procedure versus branch selection.

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did service scores improve in switched branches? | **Yes** |
| Can we attribute this to the new procedure? | **No — not with confidence** |
| Should we roll out system-wide now? | **No** |
| Is the procedure worth further testing? | **Yes** |

## **Next Steps**

| Action | Timeline | Rationale |
