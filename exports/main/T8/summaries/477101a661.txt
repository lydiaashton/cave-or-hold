# **Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Final Verdict**

**The new procedure is associated with improved service scores, but we cannot confidently claim it caused the full improvement observed.** 

Branches that adopted the new procedure showed better outcomes than those that did not. However, these branches were already outperforming and pulling away from non-adopters *before* the switch occurred. This suggests that branches predisposed to better performance may have been more likely to adopt early.

**True causal effect is likely smaller than the 1.9-point estimate**—possibly substantially so. The procedure may still be beneficial, but the evidence does not support an immediate system-wide mandate.

---

## **Recommendation**

**Approve a randomized, phased rollout rather than immediate system-wide implementation.**

| Approach | Why |
|----------|-----|
| ❌ Immediate full rollout | Evidence too weak to justify system-wide mandate |
| ✅ Randomized phased rollout | Enables clean causal inference; identifies true effect size |
| ✅ Monitor and evaluate | Set clear success thresholds before expanding further |

## **Key Findings**

**Raw difference-in-differences: +1.9 points**

⚠️ **Critical limitation:** The gap between groups was already widening by **0.32 points per week before the switch** (p < 0.001). This violates a core assumption of the analysis method and means the 1.9-point estimate likely overstates the true effect.

---

## **What We Know**

- ✓ Switched branches improved slightly after adoption
- ✓ Non-switched branches declined during the same period
- ✓ The procedure is **associated with** better outcomes
- ✓ Both staffing levels and visit volumes positively affect service scores

## **What We Don't Know**

- ✗ The true causal impact of the procedure
- ✗ Whether average branches (not early adopters) will see the same benefit
- ✗ Whether the effect persists beyond 8 weeks post-adoption

This is a **promising but unproven** intervention. The early adopters did well, but they may have been destined to do well regardless. A randomized rollout will tell us definitively whether the procedure works for typical branches—and is worth the investment of system-wide implementation.
