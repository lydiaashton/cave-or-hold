# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

## **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

### **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure likely improves service scores, but we need more data to be confident about the size of the benefit.** The evidence points to a positive effect of approximately **1 to 2 points** (2–4% improvement), with stronger results appearing 5–8 weeks after implementation. We recommend **continuing the pilot for 8–12 more weeks** before deciding on systemwide rollout.

---

### **FINAL VERDICT**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **Does the new procedure improve service scores?** | **Probably yes** — but we cannot yet rule out that some of the observed gain reflects pre-existing differences between branches |
| **Should we roll out to all branches now?** | **No** — the evidence is promising but not yet definitive |
| **Should we abandon the new procedure?** | **No** — there is no evidence of harm, and the late-post results are encouraging |
| **What should we do?** | **Continue the pilot and reconvene in 2–3 months** with additional data |

---

### **KEY FINDINGS**

**1. Observed Improvement**

| Group | Before Switch (Weeks 6–14) | After Switch (Weeks 15–22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------------|---------------------------|--------|
| Control branches (old procedure) | 48.97 | 50.35 | +1.38 |
| Treatment branches (new procedure) | 49.69 | 52.98 | +3.29 |

The gap between treatment and control branches **widened by 1.9 points** after the switch.

**2. Timing Matters**

The effect was not immediate:
- **Weeks 15–18:** Little to no detectable effect
- **Weeks 19–22:** Clear improvement (+2.2 points over expected trends, p = 0.014)

This pattern suggests the new procedure may require **4–5 weeks to show full benefits** as staff adapt.

**3. Important Caveat**

Branches selected for the new procedure were already outperforming control branches before the switch. This makes it difficult to isolate the procedure's effect from other factors. However:
- There is **no evidence the procedure harms** service scores
- The late-post improvement is **statistically significant**
- A realistic effect size is **1 to 2 points** (2–4% improvement)

**4. Study Scope**

- **425 branches** adopted the new procedure; **284 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Data cover **17 weeks** with the switch at week 15

---

### **NEXT STEPS**

| Priority | Action | Timeline |
|----------|--------|----------|
| 1 | **Continue the pilot** — do not expand or terminate yet | Immediate |
| 2 | Collect 8–12 more weeks of data to confirm the late-post effect | Next 2–3 months |
| 3 | Survey branch staff about implementation challenges and timelines | 4–6 weeks |
| 4 | Plan for randomized evaluation of future procedural changes | Next cycle |
| 5 | **Revisit rollout decision** with additional data | 2–3 months |

---

### **RECOMMENDATION**

**Continue the pilot and gather more data before deciding on systemwide rollout.** The evidence is promising but not yet definitive. If the late-post effect persists over additional weeks, a phased rollout would be justified. If it fades or reverses, the procedure should not be expanded.
