# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure produced a modest but statistically significant improvement in service scores.** Contrary to our expectation of no change, switched branches gained approximately **2.3 points** (5% improvement) relative to non-switched branches. The effect is real and sustained.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **150 branches**: 90 switched to new procedure, 60 kept the old
- **17 weeks of data**: Weeks 6–14 (before), Weeks 15–22 (after)
- Difference-in-differences analysis comparing changes between groups

### **Results**

| Group | Before | After | Change |
|-------|--------|-------|--------|
| Switched branches | 49.75 | 53.60 | +3.85 |
| Non-switched branches | 49.73 | 51.08 | +1.35 |
| **Net effect** | | | **+2.50** |

Regression estimate (controlling for staffing and visits): **+2.33 points**  
95% confidence interval: **1.0 to 3.7 points**  
p-value: **0.001**

### **Evidence Quality**

| Strength | Limitation |
|----------|------------|
| Statistically significant (p < 0.01) | Observational — branches not randomly assigned |
| Effect sustained across all 8 post-switch weeks | Only 9 pre-switch weeks for trend comparison |
| Parallel pre-trends confirmed | Unobserved factors could influence results |
| Robust to controls (staffing, visits) | Effect size is modest (0.23 standard deviations) |

---

## **Final Verdict**

**Proceed with system-wide rollout.**

The evidence supports adoption:

1. **The effect is real** — less than 0.1% probability this result occurred by chance
2. **The effect is positive** — no branches showed harm; all post-switch weeks showed gains
3. **The effect is sustained** — no decay over the 8-week observation period
4. **The downside is limited** — branches already using the procedure report no operational problems

A 5% improvement in service quality is meaningful for a procedural change we expected to be neutral. Unless implementation costs are prohibitive, the data supports expanding the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve rollout** to remaining 60 branches
2. **Track scores** for 8–12 weeks post-rollout to confirm similar gains
3. **Document implementation costs** for future procedural changes
4. **Investigate the mechanism** — understanding why this helped could inform future improvements

---

*Analysis based on weekly service scores from 150 branches, weeks 6–22. Statistical model controls for branch-level variation, staffing index, and visits index. Standard errors clustered by branch.*
