# **Board Memorandum (Final)**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2025  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The analysis does not provide reliable evidence that the new procedure improved service scores.** While the point estimate suggests a modest positive effect (+2 points), the result is **not statistically significant**, and the confidence interval includes zero. Additionally, branches that adopted the new procedure were underperforming before the switch, which complicates interpretation. We recommend **against a system-wide rollout at this time** and suggest either extending the pilot with a redesigned evaluation or reconsidering the procedure itself.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Statistical Evidence Is Weak

| Model Specification | Estimated Effect | P-value | Statistically Significant? |
|--------------------|-----------------|---------|---------------------------|
| Basic analysis | +1.7 points | 0.14 | No |
| With staffing/visitor controls | +2.0 points | 0.07 | No |

- **Interpretation:** There is a 7-14% probability of observing these results even if the new procedure has no real effect
- **95% confidence interval:** The true effect could plausibly range from slightly negative (-0.2) to moderately positive (+4.2)

### 2. Pre-Existing Differences Complicate Interpretation

Before the procedure change (weeks 6-14), branches that would later adopt the new procedure **consistently scored lower** than control branches in most weeks. This means:

- The groups were not equivalent at baseline
- Some of the apparent "improvement" may reflect normal variation rather than the procedure change
- The analysis may overstate the true effect

### 3. Effect Timing Was Inconsistent

| Period | Estimated Effect |
|--------|-----------------|
| Weeks 15-18 (immediate post-switch) | Near zero |
| Weeks 19-20 | +4 to +5 points (significant) |
| Weeks 21-22 (most recent) | Near zero |

If the procedure genuinely improves service, we would expect a more sustained effect rather than a two-week spike.

### 4. Both Groups Improved After Week 15

| Group | Before | After | Change |
|-------|--------|-------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 50.1 | 53.3 | +3.2 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 50.6 | 55.5 | +4.9 |

The control group's improvement suggests external factors (seasonal patterns, cooperative-wide initiatives, etc.) affected all branches. The new procedure's *additional* benefit is the small difference between these changes.

---

## **Final Verdict**

### **Do Not Proceed with System-Wide Rollout**

Based on the evidence presented, the Data Analysis Team's formal recommendation is:

> **The board should NOT approve rolling out the new stock rotation procedure to all branches at this time.**

**Rationale:**

| Criterion | Assessment |
|-----------|------------|
| Statistical significance | ❌ Not met (p > 0.05) |
| Effect consistency | ❌ Not met (effect faded in weeks 21-22) |
| Baseline equivalence | ❌ Not met (treatment branches underperformed pre-switch) |
| Practical significance | ⚠️ Uncertain (effect size modest even if real) |
| Cost-benefit confidence | ❌ Not met (cannot confirm benefit exists) |

**What Would Change Our Recommendation:**

- Statistically significant effect (p < 0.05) sustained over 8+ weeks
- Evidence of parallel pre-trends between treatment and control groups
- Clear mechanism explaining why weeks 19-20 differed from weeks 21-22
- Cost analysis showing benefits exceed implementation costs

---

## **Recommendations**

### Primary Recommendation: **Do Not Roll Out System-Wide**

The evidence does not support the cost and disruption of implementing the new procedure across all 34 branches. The potential benefit is uncertain, and the analysis has notable limitations.

### Alternative Paths Forward

| Option | Description | Pros | Cons |
|--------|-------------|------|------|
| **Extend pilot** | Continue current design for 8-12 more weeks | More data may clarify the effect | May not resolve fundamental design issues |
| **Redesign evaluation** | Randomize procedure assignment; ensure balanced groups | Stronger causal evidence | Requires time and cooperation |
| **Investigate weeks 19-20** | Understand what drove the temporary spike | May reveal actionable insights | Effect may not be replicable |
| **Discontinue pilot** | Return all branches to old procedure | Avoids ongoing uncertainty | Loses potential (if any) benefit |

### If the Board Wishes to Proceed Anyway

Should the board decide the potential upside justifies the risk:

1. **Phase the rollout** over 6-12 months with clear evaluation checkpoints
2. **Set explicit success criteria** (e.g., sustained +3 point improvement over 8 weeks)
3. **Plan for reversal** if targets are not met

---

## **Conclusion**

The new stock rotation procedure has not demonstrated a clear, reliable benefit. The board should not approve a system-wide rollout based on the current evidence. We recommend pausing to either strengthen the evaluation design or reconsider whether this procedure change addresses the right problem.
