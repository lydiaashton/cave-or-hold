# **Board Memorandum (FINAL)**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Executive Summary**

Branches using the new procedure showed higher service scores, but **we cannot attribute this improvement to the procedure itself**. Analysis reveals these branches were already improving faster before they switched. The apparent gains likely reflect pre-existing differences between branches—not the procedure's effect.

---

## **Final Verdict**

> **❌ DO NOT APPROVE system-wide rollout at this time.**
>
> The evidence does not establish that the new procedure causes improved service scores. The observed +1.9 point advantage is attributable to **selection bias**, not the intervention. Approving rollout based on this analysis would risk investing resources in a procedure with unproven benefits.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **The Numbers**

| Measure | Result |
|---------|--------|
| Raw difference (switched vs. control) | +1.9 points |
| **After accounting for pre-trends** | **−0.26 points (n.s.)** |
| Statistical significance | **p = 0.56** |
| Pre-existing trend advantage | +0.27 points/week |

### **The Critical Issue: Selection Bias**

Branches that adopted the new procedure were **already improving before the switch**:

```
Week:     6      8      10     12     14     15     22
          |------|------|------|------|------|------|
Diff:   -0.88  -0.04  -0.25  +0.80  +1.50  +1.35  +3.22
          ^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^
          Already widening BEFORE week 15 switch
```

When we account for this pre-existing trend, the procedure's effect disappears entirely.

### **What Likely Happened**

Branches that volunteered to switch were probably:
- Better managed overall
- More motivated to improve service
- Already implementing other positive changes
- Located in areas with improving conditions

The new procedure may still be beneficial, but **this study cannot prove it**.

---

## **Recommendation**

| Decision | Rationale |
|----------|-----------|
| **❌ Reject system-wide rollout** | Evidence does not support causal claim |
| **✓ Continue monitoring** | Track switched branches to see if gains persist |
| **✓ Gather qualitative feedback** | Interview staff about their experience |
| **✓ Consider randomized trial** | Future evaluation should randomly assign branches |

---

## **If the Board Wishes to Proceed Anyway**

Should the board decide to rollout despite inconclusive evidence (e.g., if the procedure is low-cost and low-risk), we recommend:

1. **Phased implementation** with continued monitoring
2. **Pre-specified success metrics** to evaluate true impact
3. **Exit criteria** if scores do not improve after 6 months
4. **Documentation of implementation costs** for cost-benefit analysis

---

## **Methodology Note**

This analysis used a **difference-in-differences** approach with:
- 709 branches (425 switched, 284 control)
- 17 weeks of data (weeks 6–22)
- Branch fixed effects and clustered standard errors
- Controls for staffing levels and visitor volume

The key limitation is **non-random assignment**: branches self-selected into the new procedure, introducing selection bias that cannot be fully corrected statistically.
