# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Evidence suggests the new procedure improved service scores, contrary to expectations.** Branches that switched saw scores increase by approximately **2.2 points** (95% CI: 0.9 to 3.5) relative to control branches. The effect is statistically significant but modest in magnitude, and about 30% of switched branches saw little or no improvement. 

**Recommendation**: Proceed with rollout, but implement in phases with close monitoring to confirm benefits persist at scale and to identify which branches benefit most.

---

## **Key Findings**

### The Numbers
| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|---------------|---------------|--------|
| Switched branches (n=90) | 50.65 | 52.54 | +1.89 |
| Control branches (n=60) | 50.84 | 50.51 | -0.34 |
| **Net effect** | | | **+2.23** |

- **Statistical confidence**: p=0.001 (cluster-robust); 95% CI: 0.9 to 3.5 points
- **Practical magnitude**: ~4% improvement; about 0.2 standard deviations
- **Consistency**: 70% of switched branches improved vs. 43% of control branches

### Important Caveats
- **Not universal**: 27 of 90 switched branches (30%) saw no improvement or decline
- **Most variation unexplained**: Service scores vary widely week-to-week; the procedure explains only a small portion
- **Unexpected finding**: We hypothesized no effect; this result should be validated during broader rollout

### What We Can Rule Out
The improvement is not explained by changes in staffing levels or visitor volumes—neither changed systematically for switched branches.

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **Did the new procedure change service scores?** | **Yes** — statistically significant improvement |
| **Is the effect large enough to matter?** | **Moderately** — 4% improvement is meaningful for a process change |
| **Can we be confident in the result?** | **Reasonably** — rigorous design, but unexpected finding warrants caution |
| **Should we roll out to all branches?** | **Yes, with conditions** — phased implementation with monitoring |

**Decision**: **Approve rollout with safeguards.** The evidence supports adoption, but the unexpected nature of the finding and variation across branches justify a measured approach rather than immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Phased rollout**: Expand to additional branches in stages rather than all at once, allowing for course correction

2. **Set success criteria**: Define minimum acceptable improvement (e.g., 1.5 points) to evaluate rollout progress

3. **Identify conditions for success**: Investigate what distinguishes the 70% of branches that improved from the 30% that didn't (e.g., branch size, baseline scores, management practices)

4. **Monitor closely**: Track service scores monthly during rollout; be prepared to pause or adjust if effects don't replicate

5. **Document the procedure**: Ensure the specific practices are clearly defined so implementation is consistent

---

*Analysis based on weekly service score data from weeks 6–22. Statistical methods include difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch, n=150 clusters).*
