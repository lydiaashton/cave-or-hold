# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Recommendation: Approve limited rollout while investigating system-wide decline.**

The new procedure shows a **statistically significant protective effect** of 2.0 points (p < 0.001), but it did **not produce absolute improvement** in service scores. Treatment branches still declined from 53.0 to 51.9 after adoption. The procedure reduced the magnitude of decline but did not reverse it.

---

## **Key Findings**

### What the Data Actually Shows

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|----------------|---------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 52.0 | 48.9 | **−3.1** |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 53.0 | 51.9 | **−1.1** |
| **Difference** | | | **+2.0** ✓ |

### Critical Context
- **Neither group improved** — both experienced absolute declines after week 15
- The new procedure **mitigated** the decline but did not **reverse** it
- Only **39% of treatment branches** saw any improvement; **61% still declined**
- Effect size is modest: 2.0 points = 4% of average score, 20% of one standard deviation

### What We Don't Know
- **What caused the system-wide decline?** Something happened at week 15 that affected all branches (seasonal pattern? staffing changes? external factors?)
- **Is the new procedure worth the rollout cost** for a 2-point mitigation rather than actual improvement?
- **Would a different intervention** actually improve scores rather than just slowing the decline?

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Criterion | Assessment |
|-----------|------------|
| **Statistical significance** | ✓ Strong (p < 0.001) |
| **Absolute improvement** | ✗ No — scores still declined |
| **Practical significance** | △ Modest — 4% of average score |
| **Consistency across branches** | ✗ Poor — 61% still declined |
| **System-wide context** | ⚠ Concerning — unexplained decline at week 15 |

**Verdict: CONDITIONAL APPROVAL**

The procedure has demonstrable value as a **protective measure**, but it is not a solution to the underlying performance decline. Approve rollout **only if**:

1. Leadership accepts that this will **slow decline, not improve scores**
2. Resources are allocated to **investigate the week-15 system-wide decline**
3. Implementation costs are **proportionate to the modest 2-point benefit**
4. The procedure is positioned as **one component** of a broader improvement strategy

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Investigate the week-15 decline** — this may represent a larger opportunity than the new procedure itself
2. **Proceed with phased rollout** rather than immediate system-wide adoption
3. **Calculate implementation costs** to assess cost-effectiveness of the 2-point benefit
4. **Explore complementary interventions** that could produce actual score improvements
5. **Set realistic expectations** with stakeholders: this procedure protects against decline but does not improve absolute performance

---

*Analysis based on 12,053 weekly observations from 709 branches. The system-wide decline after week 15 warrants investigation before major procedural commitments.*
