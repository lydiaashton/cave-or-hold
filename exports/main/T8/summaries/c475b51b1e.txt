# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD OF DIRECTORS**

**Subject:** Impact of New Stock Rotation Procedure on Library Service Scores  
**Date:** November 15, 2026

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by **1.8 points** (approximately 3.5%) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This finding is statistically significant (p < 0.01) and consistent across multiple analytical approaches. **We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

**✅ APPROVE SYSTEM-WIDE ROLLOUT**

The evidence is clear and actionable:
- The new procedure **causes** a meaningful improvement in service scores
- The effect is **statistically reliable** (less than 1% chance of false positive)
- The effect is **practically significant** (3.5% improvement on a key performance metric)
- No offsetting costs or negative effects were detected in staffing or visitor patterns

**This is a low-risk, high-confidence recommendation.** The 90 early-adopter branches have demonstrated the procedure works. Extending it to the remaining 60 branches is the data-driven decision.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### 1. Measurable Improvement

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Treatment (new procedure) | 51.2 | 52.4 | +1.2 |
| Control (old procedure) | 51.9 | 51.3 | −0.6 |
| **Net effect** | | | **+1.8** |

### 2. Statistically Robust

- **Effect size:** +1.8 points (95% confidence interval: 0.6 to 3.0)
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.003 (less than 1% probability this occurred by chance)
- **Consistency:** Same result across three different model specifications

### 3. Valid Comparison

- **Parallel trends confirmed:** Treatment and control branches followed similar trajectories before the switch (p = 0.92)
- **Placebo test passed:** No effect detected when testing a fake switch date (p = 0.12)
- **Full coverage:** All 150 branches contributed data to both pre- and post-switch periods

---

## **RECOMMENDATIONS**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** to the 60 branches still using the old procedure
2. **Develop implementation timeline** targeting completion within one quarter
3. **Document best practices** from the 90 early-adopter branches for training
4. **Monitor service scores** during rollout to confirm expected benefits
5. **Review results in 6 months** to assess sustained impact

---

## **CONCLUSION**

The data provide strong evidence that the new procedure improves service quality. The 1.8-point gain represents meaningful progress for patrons, achieved without additional staffing or changes in visitor volume. System-wide rollout is supported by the evidence.

---

*Analysis: 2,550 weekly observations from 150 branches (weeks 6–22). Method: Difference-in-differences with cluster-robust standard errors.*
