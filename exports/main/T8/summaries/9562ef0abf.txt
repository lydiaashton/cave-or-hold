# **Library Branch Stock Rotation Procedure: Impact Analysis**

**Prepared for the Board of Directors**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**We cannot conclude the new procedure improved service scores.** The observed 2.1-point difference between switched and non-switched branches appears to be driven by **pre-existing trends**, not the procedure change itself. Branches that switched were already improving relative to other branches before the switch occurred.

**Recommendation: Do not proceed with system-wide rollout until further evidence is gathered.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Critical Issue: Pre-Existing Trends**

The fundamental assumption for this analysis—that switched and non-switched branches would have followed similar trends without the intervention—is **violated**.

| Period | Trend in Gap (points/week) |
|--------|---------------------------|
| Before switch (weeks 6–14) | +0.29 |
| After switch (weeks 15–22) | +0.22 |

**The gap was already widening before the switch.** In fact, the rate of widening **slowed slightly** after the new procedure was introduced.

At week 6, switched branches were scoring **1 point lower** than non-switched branches. By week 14 (one week before the switch), they were already **1 point higher**. The entire 2.1-point "effect" is consistent with this pre-existing trend.

### **What This Means**

- The 2.1-point DiD estimate likely reflects **selection bias**, not treatment effect
- Branches that switched may have been systematically different (e.g., more motivated management, better resources, prior improvement initiatives)
- The new procedure may have **no effect**, or the effect may be **much smaller** than 2.1 points
- We cannot distinguish the procedure's impact from pre-existing differences between branch groups

| 4 | **Collect additional data** on branch characteristics (size, location, management, prior performance) to control for selection |

## **Technical Notes**

This analysis uses a difference-in-differences design, which requires the "parallel trends" assumption: treatment and control groups must have similar outcome trends before the intervention. A statistical test strongly rejects this assumption (pre-trend interaction coefficient = 0.286, p < 0.001). The observed post-switch difference is consistent with extrapolating the pre-switch trend, suggesting the estimated treatment effect is biased.

---

## **Final Verdict**

**The evidence does not support rolling out the new procedure system-wide at this time.**

The analysis cannot demonstrate that the new stock rotation and shelving procedure caused any improvement in service scores. The apparent benefit is indistinguishable from pre-existing differences between branches that were selected to switch and those that were not. Proceeding with a full rollout based on this evidence would risk investing resources in a procedure that may provide no benefit.

The Board should **withhold approval** for system-wide implementation until a more rigorous evaluation—ideally a randomized controlled trial—provides credible evidence of the procedure's true effect.
