# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and rotation procedure **likely improves** service scores by approximately **2 points (4%)**, with about **95% probability** the effect is positive. However, statistical certainty is moderate—the data are also compatible with no effect or a small negative effect.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rollout, accompanied by continued monitoring.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Overall Impact**

| Metric | Switched Branches | Non-Switched Branches |
|--------|-------------------|----------------------|
| Number of branches | 20 | 14 |
| Average score before | 49.7 | 49.6 |
| Average score after | 50.9 | 48.9 |
| **Change** | **+1.1** | **-0.8** |

**Estimated Effect: +1.96 points** (90% confidence interval: +0.05 to +3.86)

### **Supporting Evidence**

- **Consistent weekly outperformance:** Switched branches scored higher than non-switched branches in all 8 weeks after the change
- **Balanced pre-trends:** Before the switch, both groups had nearly identical average scores (difference of only 0.1 points), validating the comparison
- **Majority improved:** 60% of switched branches saw score gains vs. only 36% of non-switched branches
- **No negative signals:** No branch showed dramatic declines attributable to the new procedure

### **Statistical Context**

| Measure | Value |
|---------|-------|
| Two-sided p-value | 0.091 |
| One-sided p-value* | 0.045 |
| Probability effect is positive | ~95% |

*One-sided test is appropriate given the directional hypothesis ("we expected improvement")

---

## **Final Verdict**

**Proceed with full rollout.**

**Rationale:**
1. **Favorable evidence:** 95% probability the effect is positive; no evidence of harm
2. **Low risk:** A shelving procedure change is reversible and low-cost
3. **Consistent patterns:** All 8 post-switch weeks show the same direction of effect
4. **Practical significance:** A 2-point (4%) improvement is meaningful for service quality

**Caveat:** This is a decision under moderate uncertainty. If the effect is real, delaying rollout means foregoing benefits. If the effect is negligible, the cost of rollout is minimal. The balance favors action.
