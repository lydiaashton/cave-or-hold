# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure had a statistically significant positive effect on service scores.** The evidence rejects the original hypothesis that the procedure would make no difference (p = 0.001). 

However, the effect is **modest in magnitude**: approximately **+2.2 points** on a ~50-point scale. Importantly, the new procedure appears to have **protected branches from decline** rather than actively improving scores—treatment branches held steady while control branches dropped about 2 points after week 15.

---

## **Final Verdict**

**Proceed with rollout, but treat this as a moderate improvement rather than a transformation.**

The data supports adoption because:
- The effect is **real and statistically reliable** (p = 0.001; 95% CI excludes zero)
- The benefit is **consistent across 90 branches** over 8 weeks
- The procedure **prevents service degradation**, which has compounding value over time

However, the board should have **measured expectations**: this is not a breakthrough change. A 2-point gain is meaningful but not dramatic. Rollout should be paced to manage costs and operational disruption.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **150 branches** total: 90 adopted the new procedure, 60 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach with cluster-robust standard errors

### Results

| Period | Control Branches (Old Procedure) | Treatment Branches (New Procedure) |
|--------|----------------------------------|------------------------------------|
| Pre-switch (weeks 6–14) | 48.88 | 50.30 |
| Post-switch (weeks 15–22) | 46.88 | 50.56 |
| **Change** | **−2.00** | **+0.26** |

- **Treatment effect: +2.2 points** (95% CI: 0.9 to 3.5)
- **Statistical significance: p = 0.001**
- **Effect size: 0.22 standard deviations** (small-to-moderate)

### What This Means
The new procedure did not boost scores above baseline—it **prevented a decline** that affected branches keeping the old procedure. This could indicate:
- The new procedure buffers against factors that otherwise reduce service quality
- External factors (seasonal, operational) affected control branches more than treatment branches

### Validity Checks
- **Parallel trends confirmed**: Pre-switch trends were statistically similar between groups
- Results hold after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve phased rollout** to remaining 60 branches over the next 2–3 quarters

2. **Cost-benefit analysis**: Quantify implementation costs per branch to confirm the 2-point gain justifies expenditure

3. **Investigate the control group decline**: Understand why control branch scores dropped after week 15—this may reveal operational issues unrelated to the procedure

4. **Monitor long-term effects**: Current data covers only 8 weeks post-switch; effects may diminish or grow over time

5. **Set success metrics**: Define what service score improvement means for patron satisfaction and circulation to contextualize the 2-point gain

---

*Analysis based on weekly service score data from 150 branches over 17 weeks. Statistical model uses cluster-robust standard errors to account for repeated observations within branches.*
