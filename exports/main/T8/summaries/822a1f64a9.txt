# **Library Cooperative Procedure Change: Impact Analysis**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure prevented a decline in service scores but did not improve them.** Branches using the old procedure experienced a 2.4-point drop after week 15; branches with the new procedure remained stable. Whether to roll this out system-wide depends on two unresolved questions: (1) What caused the control group's decline, and (2) Is preventing this decline worth the implementation costs?

---

## **Key Findings**

### **What Actually Happened to Scores**

| Group | Before (Weeks 6–14) | After (Weeks 15–22) | Actual Change |
|-------|---------------------|---------------------|---------------|
| **New Procedure** | 51.24 | 51.19 | −0.05 (stable) |
| **Old Procedure** | 51.81 | 49.45 | −2.36 (declined) |

### **Statistical Evidence**
- **Treatment effect: +2.23 points** (95% CI: 0.93 to 3.53; p < 0.001)
- This means new-procedure branches scored 2.23 points **higher than they would have** with the old procedure
- Effect size: 0.23 standard deviations (4.4% of baseline)

### **Critical Uncertainties**

1. **Why did control branches decline after week 15?**
   - Seasonal factors? (Weeks 15–22 are late summer/early fall)
   - System-wide operational changes?
   - Random variation?

2. **Will the benefit persist?**
   - If the decline was temporary (e.g., seasonal), the new procedure's advantage may disappear
   - If the old procedure has structural weaknesses, the benefit would continue

3. **Is 2.2 points practically meaningful?**
   - Statistically significant: yes
   - Noticeable to patrons: unknown

---

## **Final Verdict**

**Do not proceed with full system-wide rollout at this time.**

**Rationale:**
- The new procedure shows a **statistically significant protective effect**, but absolute scores did not improve
- The **cause of the control group's decline is unknown** — if it was temporary or seasonal, the benefit may not persist
- **Implementation costs are unquantified** — without knowing training, time, and operational requirements, we cannot assess net value
- The original hypothesis was "no difference" — while we rejected this, the effect is **prevention of decline, not improvement**

**However:** If the board is risk-averse and concerned about future score declines, a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** would be defensible. The procedure does no harm and may provide insurance against future deterioration.
