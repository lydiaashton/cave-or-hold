# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 2025  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence is inconclusive.** While branches using the new procedure showed higher service scores after the switch, the gap between treatment and control branches was **already widening before the procedure changed**. There was no detectable break in the trend at the switch point. 

**Recommendation: Do not proceed with system-wide rollout yet. Conduct additional evaluation.**

---

## **Final Verdict**

> **The new procedure cannot be credited with the observed improvement in service scores.**
>
> The data show that treatment branches were already outperforming control branches before the switch, and this gap continued growing afterward with no discernible change at the intervention point. Without evidence of a causal effect, **we cannot recommend rolling out the new procedure system-wide.**
>
> **Decision Required:** Either (a) invest in a properly randomized evaluation to establish causality, or (b) deprioritize this initiative and focus resources on interventions with proven effectiveness.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **The Critical Issue: Pre-Existing Trends**

| Period | Treatment-Control Gap | Trend |
|--------|----------------------|-------|
| Week 6 | 1.07 points | |
| Week 14 (last pre-switch week) | 3.74 points | **+0.33 pts/week** |
| Week 15 (first post-switch week) | 3.61 points | **−0.14 pts (no jump)** |
| Week 22 | 5.19 points | +0.23 pts/week |

**The gap increased by 2.67 points during the 9 weeks BEFORE the switch.** The post-switch increase continues this same pattern—there is no evidence of a discontinuity caused by the new procedure.

### **Visual Evidence**

![Trend Check](trend_check.png)

*The treatment-control gap (blue line) was widening steadily before week 15. The post-switch trend (orange dashed) actually shows a slower rate of increase than the pre-switch trend (green dashed).*

### **What This Means**

The standard difference-in-differences analysis assumes **parallel trends**—that without the intervention, both groups would have continued on similar paths. **This assumption is violated here.** Treatment branches were already improving relative to control branches before the new procedure was implemented.

Possible explanations:
- Branches that volunteered/were selected for the new procedure may have been more motivated or better-managed
- Unobserved factors (leadership changes, local initiatives) may have affected treatment branches differently
- The new procedure may have no effect, a smaller effect than estimated, or even a negative effect masked by the pre-existing trend

---

## **Recommended Next Steps**

1. **Pause the rollout decision** pending further analysis.

2. **Investigate selection bias**—understand why treatment branches were already diverging from control branches before the intervention.

3. **Consider a randomized trial**—if feasible, randomly assign remaining branches to treatment/control to establish causality.

4. **Extend the observation period**—collect more post-switch data to see if trends diverge more clearly over time.

5. **Gather qualitative evidence**—interview branch managers about implementation quality, staff feedback, and observed changes.

---

## **What We Can Say with Confidence**

- Branches using the new procedure currently have higher service scores (53.6 vs. 49.2)
- However, these branches also had higher scores before the switch (50.5 vs. 48.1)
- The **causal effect of the procedure itself remains unproven**
