# **Board Summary: New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**The data show a positive association between the new procedure and service scores, but we cannot confirm causation.** The +2.1 point difference is statistically significant and consistent, yet the non-random assignment of branches and lack of a clear mechanism mean we cannot rule out alternative explanations. **We recommend a cautious, evidence-based expansion rather than immediate systemwide rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. The Observed Effect
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Difference-in-Differences estimate | +2.1 points |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.01 |
| Effect size (vs. mean of 50) | +4.2% |
| Treatment branches that improved | 61% |
| Control branches that improved | 43% |

### 2. What Supports a Causal Interpretation
- **Parallel pre-trends**: Before week 15, treatment and control branches moved together (p = 0.54)
- **Consistent post-switch effect**: All 8 weeks show positive treatment-control differences (+1.5 to +2.8 points)
- **Robust to controls**: Effect persists after adjusting for branch characteristics, staffing, and visit volumes

### 3. What Undermines Confidence
- **Non-random assignment**: Branches were not randomly selected for the new procedure; unobserved factors (management quality, staff motivation, concurrent initiatives) could explain the result
- **No clear mechanism**: Back-office shelving/rotation changes should not directly affect patron-facing service scores
- **Control branch decline**: Half the "effect" reflects control branches getting worse, not just treatment branches improving
- **Placebo test concern**: A fake treatment date shows a similar (though non-significant) pattern

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Finding | Confidence Level |
|---------|------------------|
| Service scores differed between groups after the switch | **High** |
| The difference is statistically distinguishable from zero | **High** |
| The new procedure *caused* the improvement | **Low to Moderate** |
| Systemwide rollout will replicate this effect | **Uncertain** |

**Verdict: Proceed with monitored expansion, not full rollout.**

The evidence is promising but not definitive. The cooperative should:
1. Expand to an additional 20–30 branches (ideally with random selection)
2. Monitor for 12+ weeks
3. Confirm the effect replicates before committing all 150 branches

This balances the opportunity to capture potential benefits against the risk of scaling a procedure that may not deliver results systemwide.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Select 20–30 additional branches** for phase 2 implementation (random selection preferred)
2. **Extend monitoring window** to 12+ weeks post-switch for all treatment branches
3. **Conduct qualitative investigation** at high-performing treatment branches to identify the mechanism
4. **Review service score methodology** to ensure it captures patron experience, not back-office metrics
5. **Reconvene in 3 months** with phase 2 results to decide on full rollout
