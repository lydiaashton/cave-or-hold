# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

**Prepared for the Board of Directors**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence is suggestive but not definitive.** Service scores at switched branches increased more than at non-switched branches after the procedure change. However, switched branches were already improving faster *before* the change, making it difficult to isolate the procedure's true effect.

**Estimated effect ranges from 0.2 to 2.4 points**, depending on the analytical approach. We cannot confidently conclude the new procedure caused the improvement.

**Recommendation: Expand monitoring before full rollout.**

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **Did service scores improve at switched branches?** | Yes |
| **Did they improve more than at non-switched branches?** | Yes |
| **Can we attribute this to the new procedure?** | Not with confidence |
| **Should we roll out to all branches now?** | No |
| **Should we abandon the new procedure?** | No |

**Rationale:** The branches that switched first were already on a stronger improvement trajectory before the change. This "selection bias" means we cannot separate the procedure's effect from the underlying characteristics of early-adopting branches. The true effect could be anywhere from negligible (~0.2 points) to meaningful (~1.1 points). Given the uncertainty, a **phased, randomized rollout** would generate cleaner evidence before committing system-wide resources.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Observed Improvement, Uncertain Cause

| Estimation Method | Estimated Effect | Interpretation |
|-------------------|------------------|----------------|
| Simple comparison | +2.4 points | Ignores pre-existing differences |
| Short-window comparison | +1.1 points | Compares only weeks near the switch |
| Pre-trend adjusted | +0.2 points | Accounts for prior improvement trajectory |

The wide range reflects genuine uncertainty about what caused the improvement.

### 2. Critical Issue: Pre-Existing Trends

Switched branches were improving **0.27 points per week faster** than non-switched branches even before the procedure change (p < 0.001). This suggests:

- Switched branches may differ systematically (e.g., better management, more engaged staff)
- Some or all of the observed improvement might have occurred without the procedure change
- The simple analysis likely **overstates** the procedure's effect

### 3. What We Can Say With Confidence

- Service scores at switched branches **did increase** after the change
- The increase was **larger** than at non-switched branches
- There is **no evidence of harm** from the new procedure
- The true effect is **likely positive but smaller than 2.4 points**

---

## **Recommendations**

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| **1** | **Do not proceed with full rollout yet** | Evidence is insufficient to justify system-wide change |
| **2** | **Expand the pilot with randomization** | Randomly assign remaining branches to switch now vs. later to get cleaner evidence |
| **3** | **Continue monitoring all branches** | More data will improve estimate precision |
| **4** | **Investigate why switched branches were already improving** | Understanding selection factors can inform implementation strategy |

---

## **About This Analysis**

- **Data:** 12,053 weekly observations from 709 branches over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- **Switch Date:** Week 15 (419 branches adopted the new procedure; 290 retained the old procedure)
- **Limitation:** The non-random selection of which branches switched first introduces uncertainty that cannot be fully resolved with this data
