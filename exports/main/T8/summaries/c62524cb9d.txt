# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD OF DIRECTORS**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure  
**Date:** Analysis Complete  
**Data Period:** Weeks 6–22 (17 weeks of observation)

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The evidence suggests the new procedure improved service quality, but with important caveats.** 

Branches that adopted the new procedure showed a **2.2-point improvement** in service scores relative to control branches. However, treatment branches were already trending upward before the switch began, suggesting some improvement would have occurred regardless. After accounting for this pre-existing trend, the **procedure itself likely contributed 1.5–1.7 points** of improvement (approximately 3% above baseline). The effect remains positive and statistically significant, supporting a rollout recommendation — but expected benefits should be tempered accordingly.

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

### **✓ PROCEED WITH ROLLOUT — WITH CONDITIONS**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **Did the new procedure improve service scores?** | Yes — with moderate confidence |
| **Is the effect large enough to warrant system-wide adoption?** | Yes — 3% improvement is meaningful at scale |
| **Are we certain of the causal effect?** | No — pre-trend violations reduce certainty |
| **Recommendation** | **Approve rollout, but monitor closely and budget for ~1.5 point gains (not 2.2)** |

**Rationale:** The pre-trend issue means we cannot claim the full 2.2-point effect is causal. However, the post-switch acceleration beyond the pre-trend trajectory, combined with stable control group performance, indicates a genuine positive effect. A conservative 1.5–1.7 point estimate still justifies adoption.

**Risk Level:** **Moderate** — The procedure is unlikely to harm service quality, but expected benefits should be set at ~70% of the initial estimate to avoid disappointment.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **Study Overview**
| Component | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| Branches Analyzed | 709 total (425 switched, 284 control) |
| Observation Period | 17 weeks (Weeks 6–22) |
| Switch Date | Week 15 |
| Methodology | Difference-in-Differences with clustered standard errors |

### **Impact Estimates**
| Metric | Estimate |
|--------|----------|
| **Raw DiD Estimate** | +2.21 service score points |
| **95% Confidence Interval** | 1.65 to 2.78 points |
| **Adjusted for Pre-Trend** | ~1.68 points |
| **Statistical Significance** | p < 0.001 |
| **Relative Improvement** | ~3–4% above baseline |

### **Critical Limitation: Pre-Trend Concern**

| Week | Treatment-Control Gap |
|------|----------------------|
| 6 | -0.05 |
| 10 | +0.47 |
| 14 | +1.78 |
| 15 (switch) | +1.96 |
| 22 | +3.63 |

**Interpretation:** Treatment branches were already pulling ahead before the switch (slope = 0.22 points/week, p=0.001). This suggests:
- Branches selected for the pilot may have been more motivated or better-managed
- Some preparation effects may have begun before week 15
- The raw 2.2-point estimate likely overstates the causal effect by ~25%

### **Supporting Evidence**

1. **No Baseline Difference:** At week 6, treatment and control branches had identical scores (52.15 vs 52.20, p=0.94), confirming groups started equivalently.

2. **Effect Persists After Adjustment:** Even accounting for the pre-trend, approximately 1.7 points of improvement remains unexplained by prior trends.

3. **Control Group Stable:** Branches keeping the old procedure showed no meaningful change (-0.2 points), ruling out system-wide factors.

4. **Effect Grows Over Time:** Post-switch gains accelerated beyond the pre-trend trajectory, consistent with a genuine treatment effect.
