# **Board Memorandum**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

Contrary to our expectations, the new procedure **produced a measurable improvement in service scores**. Branches that adopted the change saw scores rise by **2.3 points** (approximately 5%) relative to branches that kept the old procedure. The result is statistically robust and consistent across all post-implementation weeks. **We recommend proceeding with system-wide rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **The Evidence**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Branches keeping old procedure | 48.8 | 51.0 | +2.2 |
| Branches adopting new procedure | 48.1 | 52.6 | +4.5 |
| **Effect attributable to new procedure** | | | **+2.3** |

- **Statistical confidence:** p = 0.0002; 95% confidence interval is 1.1 to 3.7 points
- **150 branches** studied over 17 weeks (9 weeks before, 8 weeks after)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure; **60 branches** maintained the old procedure

### **Why This Result Is Credible**

1. **Valid comparison:** Both groups followed nearly identical trends before the switch. The divergence appeared only after implementation.

2. **Consistent effect:** Treatment branches outperformed control branches in all 8 post-switch weeks.

3. **Robust to controls:** The effect holds after accounting for staffing levels, visitor volume, and branch-specific characteristics.

4. **Sound methodology:** Difference-in-differences design is the standard approach for evaluating policy changes with non-random assignment.

---

## **Final Verdict**

**The new rotation and shelving procedure should be adopted system-wide.**

The evidence is clear and actionable:
- ✅ **The effect is real** — statistically significant at p < 0.001
- ✅ **The effect is meaningful** — a 5% improvement in service quality
- ✅ **The effect is reliable** — consistent across all post-implementation weeks
- ✅ **The risk is low** — branches already using the procedure show sustained gains with no adverse effects

**We would not recommend rollout if the data were ambiguous. They are not.** The analysis demonstrates that this operational change delivers tangible benefits to patrons. Delaying implementation would mean withholding a proven improvement from the 60 branches still using the old procedure.

**Motion for the Board:** Approve immediate rollout of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all remaining branches, with implementation support and monitoring to ensure consistent adoption.
