# **Memorandum to the Board**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving & Rotation Procedure  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Analysis Period:** Weeks 6-22 (17 weeks of data from 150 branches)

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Contrary to initial expectations, branches that adopted the new shelving and rotation procedure achieved service scores **1.8 points higher** (approximately **4% improvement**) than branches that retained the old procedure. The effect is statistically significant (p = 0.003) and robust across multiple analytical approaches.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with system-wide rollout of the new procedure.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Measurable Improvement in Service Scores**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Average treatment effect | +1.84 service score points |
| 95% Confidence Interval | +0.64 to +3.04 points |
| Statistical significance | p = 0.003 |
| Relative improvement | ~3.7% above baseline |

The effect remains consistent (+1.82 points, p = 0.002) after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume, indicating the improvement is attributable to the procedure change itself.

### **2. Treatment vs. Control Comparison**
| Group | Branches | Avg. Score Change | % with Improvement |
|-------|----------|-------------------|-------------------|
| Switched (New Procedure) | 90 | +1.24 | 67% |
| Control (Old Procedure) | 60 | -0.60 | 40% |

Branches using the new procedure were nearly twice as likely to show improvement compared to those using the old procedure.

### **3. Valid Comparison**
- Pre-switch trends were parallel between groups (p = 0.67), confirming the control group provides a valid counterfactual.
- Treatment branches consistently scored ~1.5 points lower before the switch; after implementation, this gap closed and reversed.
- The improvement emerged immediately in week 15 and persisted through week 22.

### **4. Study Strengths**
- **150 branches** across the cooperative (90 treatment, 60 control)
- **2,550 weekly observations** with complete data
- Cluster-robust statistical methods accounting for repeated measures per branch

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did the new procedure change service scores? | **Yes** |
| Was the change positive or negative? | **Positive** |
| Is the result statistically reliable? | **Yes** (p = 0.003) |
| Should we roll out to all branches? | **Yes** |

**Verdict: APPROVE system-wide implementation.**

The data provide strong evidence that the new shelving and rotation procedure improves service scores. This finding contradicts prior expectations but is supported by rigorous analysis with a valid control group. There is no statistical basis for delaying or limiting the rollout.

## **Caveats & Considerations**

- **Unexpected result:** This analysis was commissioned to verify no negative impact; the positive effect was unanticipated. We recommend confirming findings with a follow-up review after full rollout.
- **Variation exists:** One-third of switched branches did not show improvement. Branch-specific factors (layout, staffing stability, training quality) may influence outcomes.
- **Service score methodology:** The service score is an internal index (average ~50 points). The 1.8-point gain represents meaningful improvement within this established metric.
