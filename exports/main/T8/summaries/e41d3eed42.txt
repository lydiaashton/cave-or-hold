**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure  

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure shows a **promising positive effect** on service scores, but the evidence is **not quite statistically conclusive**. Branches that adopted the new procedure improved by approximately **2 points (4%)** more than comparable branches. The statistical evidence leans in favor of improvement (one-tailed p=0.06), but falls just short of conventional confidence thresholds.

**Recommendation: The decision depends on implementation costs.** If rollout costs are modest, proceeding is reasonable given the positive signal. If costs are substantial, collecting more data first would reduce risk.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect Summary

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches (20 branches) | 47.5 | 49.0 | **+1.4** |
| Non-switched branches (14 branches) | 49.3 | 48.8 | **-0.5** |
| **Net Difference (Treatment Effect)** | | | **+1.95 points** |

### 2. Statistical Assessment

| Metric | Value | Interpretation |
|--------|-------|----------------|
| Estimated effect | +1.95 points | ~4% improvement relative to baseline |
| Two-tailed p-value | 0.117 | Not significant at 0.05 level |
| One-tailed p-value | 0.059 | Borderline (tests expected direction only) |
| 95% Confidence Interval | -0.5 to +4.4 points | True effect likely positive, but uncertainty remains |

**What this means:** There's roughly a 6% probability we'd see this result if the procedure had no effect. This is close to but not below the conventional 5% threshold. The confidence interval suggests the true effect is most likely between 0 and 4 points.

### 3. Methodology Validation ✓

Our analysis passes a critical validity check: before the switch (weeks 6-14), both groups followed parallel trends (p=0.82). This confirms the comparison is appropriate.

### 4. Important Context

- **Direction aligns with expectations:** The effect is positive, as anticipated
- **High variation across branches:** Individual branch changes ranged from -5.7 to +7.2 points, suggesting context matters
- **Limited post-switch data:** Only 8 weeks of observations after the change
- **Sample size:** 34 branches is moderate; more data would sharpen conclusions

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

### **Proceed with Cautious, Phased Rollout**

**Rationale:**

| Factor | Assessment | Weight |
|--------|------------|--------|
| Effect direction | ✓ Positive (as expected) | Favorable |
| Effect magnitude | ✓ ~4% improvement (meaningful) | Favorable |
| Statistical confidence | ⚠ Borderline (p=0.06 one-tailed) | Caution |
| Methodology quality | ✓ Sound (parallel pre-trends confirmed) | Favorable |
| Risk of harm | ✓ No evidence of negative effects | Favorable |
| Implementation risk | ⚠ Unknown (depends on costs) | Case-specific |

**Verdict:** The evidence is **strong enough to justify continued implementation** but **not strong enough for immediate system-wide mandate**. We recommend:

1. **Allow branches to adopt** the new procedure voluntarily
2. **Require monitoring** — all adopting branches must report scores for 12 weeks
3. **Set a review date** — Board re-evaluates in 3 months with expanded data
4. **No mandate yet** — do not require non-switched branches to convert until evidence strengthens

This approach balances the promising signal against statistical uncertainty, allowing benefits to accrue while preserving the option to adjust if additional data contradicts early results.
