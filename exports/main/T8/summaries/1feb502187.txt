# **MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure shows a **positive but statistically inconclusive effect** on service scores (+1.9 points, p = 0.15). While the direction aligns with expectations, **we cannot confirm the improvement is real rather than random variation**. We recommend **extending the evaluation period before any system-wide rollout** to avoid committing resources to an unproven intervention.

---

## **Final Verdict**

> **DO NOT PROCEED WITH SYSTEM-WIDE ROLLOUT AT THIS TIME**

**Rationale:**
- The evidence does not meet the standard required for a cooperative-wide investment decision
- A p-value of 0.15 means there is a 15% probability the observed improvement is due to chance—unacceptably high for a major operational change
- The confidence interval (-0.7 to +4.5) includes zero effect, meaning "no impact" is a plausible outcome
- Additional data collection is low-cost and would substantially improve decision quality

**This is not a rejection of the procedure.** It is a recommendation to **verify before committing**.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Impact on Service Scores**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched Branches (n=20) | 49.9 | 52.0 | **+2.1** |
| Control Branches (n=14) | 49.2 | 49.3 | +0.2 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+1.9** |

- **Estimated improvement:** +1.9 points on the service score index
- **Relative improvement:** ~4% above baseline
- **95% confidence interval:** -0.7 to +4.5 points
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.15

### **2. What This Means**

The data shows switched branches improved more than control branches after the procedure change. The **point estimate aligns with our expectations** of improvement. However, the statistical uncertainty means we cannot yet rule out that some or all of this improvement could be due to normal variation.

**In practical terms:** The most likely scenario is a real but modest improvement. We need more data to be confident.

### **3. Why Results Are Less Conclusive Than Expected**

| Factor | Impact |
|--------|--------|
| Natural score variability | Service scores fluctuate ~10 points week-to-week |
| Sample size | 34 branches provides moderate statistical power |
| Post-switch observation window | Only 8 weeks of data since implementation |
| Implementation variation | Branches may adopt procedures at different rates |

### **4. Analysis Validity**

✓ **Parallel trends confirmed:** Switched and control branches followed similar trajectories before the switch (p = 0.87)  
✓ **Complete data:** No missing observations across 578 branch-week records  
✓ **Robust to controls:** Effect persists when adjusting for staffing and visitor volume  

---

## **Recommendations**

### **Immediate Actions**

1. **Pause system-wide rollout** pending additional evidence

2. **Continue pilot monitoring** through week 30 (8 additional weeks). This will approximately double post-switch observations and narrow the confidence interval by roughly 30%

3. **Expand pilot to 5–10 additional branches** on a voluntary basis to increase treatment group size

4. **Document implementation fidelity** at each branch to identify whether full adoption correlates with larger improvements

### **Decision Timeline**

| Date | Action |
|------|--------|
| January 2027 | Interim review with expanded pilot data |
| March 2027 | Final evaluation with full dataset |
| April 2027 | Board decision on system-wide rollout |

### **Success Criteria for Full Rollout**

We recommend proceeding to system-wide implementation if updated analysis shows:
- **Effect size ≥ +1.5 points** (practically meaningful improvement)
- **p-value < 0.10** (reasonable statistical confidence)
- **Consistent effects** across different branch sizes and locations

---

## **Conclusion**

The new procedure is **performing as hoped**—service scores at switched branches improved relative to controls. While we lack definitive statistical proof with current data, there is **no evidence of harm** and **suggestive evidence of benefit**. 

**The prudent path:** Verify the benefit with additional data before committing the cooperative's resources to a full rollout.
