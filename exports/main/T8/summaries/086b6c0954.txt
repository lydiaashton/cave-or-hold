# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact Analysis**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new shelving and rotation procedure improved service scores.** Despite expectations that the operational change would not affect patron-facing metrics, branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by **2.1 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This result is statistically significant (p < 0.001) and represents approximately a **4% improvement** in service quality.

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout.**

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **Did the new procedure change service scores?** | **Yes** — statistically significant improvement |
| **Is the effect meaningful?** | **Yes** — ~4% gain without additional resources |
| **Can we trust this result?** | **Yes** — rigorous design, validated assumptions |
| **Should we roll out to all branches?** | **Yes** — clear evidence supports expansion |

**Verdict: The new procedure delivers measurable value. Approve system-wide implementation.**

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **150 branches** analyzed over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure at week 15
- **60 branches** maintained the old procedure (control group)
- Rigorous difference-in-differences analysis isolates the procedure's effect

### Impact on Service Scores

| Group | Before (Weeks 6–14) | After (Weeks 15–22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------|---------------------|--------|
| Control (Old Procedure) | 47.7 | 48.2 | +0.5 |
| Treatment (New Procedure) | 49.1 | 51.7 | +2.6 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+2.1** |

### Why This Result Is Reliable

| Validation Check | Result |
|------------------|--------|
| Statistical significance | p = 0.0005 |
| 95% confidence interval | 0.93 to 3.23 (excludes zero) |
| Parallel pre-trends | ✓ Confirmed (p = 0.25) |
| Robust to controls | ✓ Effect persists adjusting for staffing and visits |
| Cluster-robust SE | ✓ Accounts for repeated measures per branch |

### Practical Significance
- Service scores average ~50 points system-wide
- A 2.1-point gain equals roughly **4% improvement**
- Improvement achieved **without additional resources**
- Effect appeared immediately and sustained through week 22

## **Why This Matters**

We expected no change to service scores — this was an operational efficiency initiative, not a patron experience intervention. The positive effect suggests:

1. **Staff maintain service quality** while adopting new workflows
2. **Patrons may benefit indirectly** (faster item location, better organization)
3. **Unexpected upside** from an operational change is rare and valuable

This finding strengthens the business case for rollout and warrants investigation into what specifically drove the improvement.

---

## **Technical Notes**

**Method:** Difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch). This is the standard approach for policy evaluation when treatment is staggered or partial.

**Model:** `service_score ~ switched_branch + after_switch + (switched × after) + staffing_index + visits_index`

**Key result:** Interaction coefficient = 2.08, SE = 0.59, p = 0.0005

**Data:** 2,550 observations (150 branches × 17 weeks), no missing values.
