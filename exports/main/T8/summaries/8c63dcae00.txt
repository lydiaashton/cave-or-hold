# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence is suggestive but not definitive.** Branches that adopted the new procedure showed higher service score gains than control branches, but the result is not statistically significant in our simplest analysis. The effect appears strongest in the first 3 weeks after implementation but fades in later weeks. **We recommend a cautious, phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than an immediate cooperative-wide mandate.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Positive Signal, But Uncertain
- **Estimated effect:** +2.35 points on the service score index
- **Statistical significance:** Not significant in basic analysis (p=0.115); significant only when controlling for branch and week characteristics (p<0.05)
- **95% confidence interval:** -0.57 to +5.27 points in the basic model—meaning we cannot rule out no effect

### 2. Effect Appears to Fade Over Time
| Weeks After Switch | Effect Size |
|---|---|
| Weeks 1-3 | +2.0 to +2.4 points |
| Week 4 | -0.9 points (negative) |
| Weeks 5-6 | +1.3 to +1.5 points |
| Weeks 7-8 | +0.1 to +0.4 points (near zero) |

The diminishing effect in later weeks raises questions about sustainability.

### 3. Important Caveat: Regression to the Mean
- Treatment branches started with **significantly lower baseline scores** (44.89 vs. 48.93)
- Some improvement was expected even without intervention
- This makes it harder to isolate the true effect of the new procedure

### 4. Branch-Level Results
- **16 of 20 switched branches (80%)** improved after adoption
- **8 of 14 control branches (57%)** improved during the same period
- The difference is meaningful but not overwhelming

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with a phased rollout** rather than immediate mandate—convert 5-10 additional branches and monitor for 8-12 weeks
2. **Extend the evaluation period** to determine whether effects are sustained beyond 8 weeks
3. **Investigate the 4 switched branches that did not improve**—understanding their circumstances could inform implementation
4. **Document implementation fidelity**—ensure branches are actually following the new procedure as intended
5. **Re-evaluate after Phase 2** with more data before making a final cooperative-wide decision

---

## **Final Verdict**

**PROCEED WITH CAUTION.** The new procedure shows promise, but the evidence does not yet justify a mandatory cooperative-wide rollout. The board should authorize a **Phase 2 pilot** converting additional branches with rigorous monitoring. If the effect proves sustained and statistically significant in Phase 2, proceed with full rollout. If the effect continues to fade or remains inconclusive, reconsider the procedure or investigate implementation barriers before further expansion.

**Risk of full rollout now:** Potential disruption to 14 control branches without confident expectation of benefit.

**Risk of waiting:** Delayed benefits if the procedure does work as intended.

**Recommended balance:** Expand carefully, learn more, then decide.

---

*Analysis based on 578 weekly observations from 34 branches over 17 weeks. Full technical documentation available upon request.*
