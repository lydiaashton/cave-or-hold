# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure  

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence is suggestive but not definitive.** Branches that adopted the new procedure maintained their service scores while others declined, but we cannot rule out that pre-existing differences between branches—not the procedure itself—explain this result. We recommend **a cautious, monitored expansion** rather than immediate system-wide rollout.

---

## **Final Verdict**

**PROCEED WITH CAUTION — DO NOT YET APPROVE FULL ROLLOUT**

The new procedure shows promise, but the data do not yet meet the standard of evidence we should require before committing cooperative-wide resources. The apparent benefit may reflect which branches were selected for the pilot rather than the procedure's actual effect. Approve a **structured expansion with built-in evaluation**, and return to this question with stronger evidence before committing to full implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### What the Data Show

| | **New Procedure** (425 branches) | **Old Procedure** (284 branches) |
|---|---|---|
| Before switch (weeks 6–14) | 50.92 | 50.66 |
| After switch (weeks 15–22) | 50.90 | 48.62 |
| **Change** | **essentially flat** | **−2.04 points** |

### Critical Caveats

1. **Treatment branches were already improving before the switch.** In early weeks (6–9), they scored *below* control branches. By weeks 12–14, they were already 1.5 points *ahead*. This upward trajectory began before the procedure changed.

2. **When we compare only the weeks immediately before and after the switch** (weeks 12–14 vs. 15–22), the estimated effect shrinks from 2.0 points to **0.76 points** (p=0.027).

3. **The new procedure did not improve scores in absolute terms.** Treatment branches went from 50.89 to 50.90—no meaningful change. The "benefit" is entirely that they declined less than control branches.

4. **Branches were not randomly assigned** to the new procedure. Those selected may differ in unmeasured ways (management quality, community factors, etc.) that also affect service scores.

### What We Can Say with Confidence

- Branches using the new procedure **did maintain** their service scores during a period when others declined
- The post-switch gap between groups (averaging 2.3 points) is **consistent and persistent** across all 8 post-switch weeks
- The result is **statistically significant**, but the causal interpretation is uncertain

---

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with expansion, but treat it as a pilot with rigorous monitoring.** The new procedure shows promise, but the evidence does not yet support unconditional system-wide adoption.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Expand to 50–100 additional branches** selected through a more randomized process to strengthen causal inference.

2. **Establish clear success metrics** before expansion (e.g., "maintain scores within 1 point of pre-switch levels").

3. **Investigate why control branches declined.** If this reflects a correctable system-wide issue, addressing it may be more impactful than the procedure change itself.

4. **Reconvene in 8–12 weeks** with fresh data to make a final rollout decision.

---

*This analysis used difference-in-differences methodology. The parallel trends assumption is violated, limiting causal confidence. Full technical appendix available upon request.*
