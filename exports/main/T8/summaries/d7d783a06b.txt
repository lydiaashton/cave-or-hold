## **Board Memorandum**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** [Current Date]  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure  

---

### **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure did not demonstrably improve service scores.** Our initial expectation—that the change would have no measurable impact—appears correct. While a simple statistical comparison suggested a positive effect, more rigorous analysis reveals this was an artifact of pre-existing differences between branches, not a genuine improvement. We recommend **proceeding cautiously** with any rollout decision, as the data do not support a clear benefit.

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. Initial Result Was Misleading**
| Analysis Method | Estimated Effect | Interpretation |
|-----------------|------------------|----------------|
| Simple comparison | +1.86 points (p=0.001) | Appears positive |
| Event study (week-by-week) | -1.46 points (average) | No consistent benefit |
| **Correct interpretation** | **~0 points** | **No detectable effect** |

The discrepancy arises because branches that switched procedures had systematically lower service scores throughout the pre-period. The "improvement" largely reflects these branches returning toward average levels, not a benefit from the new procedure.

**2. Week-by-Week Results Show No Pattern**
- Only **2 of 8 weeks** showed positive effects after the switch
- **3 of 8 weeks** showed significantly *negative* effects (weeks 17, 19, 21)
- No sustained improvement trend emerged

**3. Pre-Existing Differences Between Groups**
| Period | Control Group | Treatment Group | Difference |
|--------|---------------|-----------------|------------|
| Pre-switch (weeks 6-14) | 50.56 | 49.63 | -0.93 |
| Post-switch (weeks 15-22) | 47.98 | 48.93 | +0.95 |

The treatment group started lower and ended slightly higher, but this pattern is inconsistent across individual weeks and likely reflects natural variation rather than procedure effects.

**4. Both Groups Declined Post-Switch**
Service scores fell for all branches after week 15 (possibly seasonal factors). The new procedure did not prevent this decline.

---

### **Final Verdict**

| Criterion | Finding |
|-----------|---------|
| **Statistical evidence of improvement** | ❌ Not found |
| **Consistent week-by-week effect** | ❌ Not found |
| **Valid causal identification** | ❌ Compromised by pre-period imbalances |
| **Practical significance** | ❌ No meaningful effect detected |

**Verdict: The analysis provides no credible evidence that the new procedure improves service scores.**

---

### **Recommendations**

| Action | Priority | Rationale |
|--------|----------|-----------|
| Do not base rollout decision on service score data | High | No evidence of benefit |
| Consider other factors for rollout decision | Medium | Cost, staff feedback, operational efficiency |
| If proceeding, monitor closely | High | Detect any negative effects early |
| Extend evaluation period if possible | Medium | 8 weeks may be insufficient |

---

### **Bottom Line for Decision-Makers**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did the new procedure improve service scores? | **No detectable improvement** |
| Did it harm service scores? | **No clear evidence of harm** |
| Should we roll it out based on this data? | **This analysis provides no support either way** |

If there are other reasons to adopt the new procedure (cost savings, staff preference, operational efficiency), those should drive the decision—not service score improvements, which were not observed.

---

### **Technical Notes**

- Analysis covers 150 branches over 17 weeks (weeks 6-22)
- 90 branches switched; 60 maintained old procedure
- Event study methodology reveals pre-period imbalances that invalidate simple difference-in-differences
- Standard errors clustered by branch
