# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Analysis Period:** Weeks 6–22 (17 weeks of data from 150 branches)

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure helped branches maintain service scores while other branches declined.** The board expected no difference from the change. What we found is more nuanced: branches using the new procedure held steady (essentially no change), while branches keeping the old procedure experienced a meaningful decline. The net result is a **1.9-point advantage** for the new procedure—but this reflects **preventing deterioration** rather than generating improvement.

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did the new procedure change service scores? | **Yes** — statistically significant effect (p < 0.001) |
| Did it improve scores as typically hoped? | **No** — treatment branches stayed flat (+0.2 points) |
| Did it make no difference as expected? | **No** — the board's expectation was incorrect |
| Is there value in the new procedure? | **Yes** — it protects against service score declines |
| Should we roll it out system-wide? | **Yes** — but with calibrated expectations |

**Bottom-line verdict:** The new procedure is **worth adopting**, but it should be framed as a **protective measure** that maintains service quality rather than an **improvement initiative** that enhances it. The board's expectation of "no difference" was technically wrong—there is a real difference—but the nature of that difference is about avoiding decline rather than creating gains.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. What Actually Happened

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|----------------|---------------|--------|
| Branches keeping old procedure | 49.6 | 48.0 | **−1.7 points** |
| Branches using new procedure | 51.3 | 51.5 | **+0.2 points** |

The treatment effect of +1.9 points comes from the treatment group staying flat while the control group declined.

### 2. Statistical Confidence

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect | **+1.91 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | 1.16 to 2.66 points |
| Statistical Significance | p < 0.001 |

The effect is statistically reliable, but the practical interpretation matters.

### 3. Important Context

- **Week 14 anomaly:** The week immediately before the switch (week 14) had unusually low scores for all branches. This may affect the before/after comparison.
- **Parallel trends held:** Before the switch, both groups moved together (p = 0.77 for differential pre-trends), supporting the validity of the comparison.
- **Baseline difference:** Treatment branches started with slightly higher scores (+1.65 points at baseline).

---

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with rollout, but with calibrated expectations:**

1. **The new procedure is beneficial** — it protects branches from the service score declines observed in the control group.

2. **Don't expect score improvements** — branches should anticipate maintaining current performance, not gaining ground.

3. **Investigate the control group decline** — Understanding why non-switching branches declined may be as important as understanding the new procedure's benefits. There may be external factors affecting all branches that the new procedure helps buffer against.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** — The procedure provides a protective effect worth having.

2. **Monitor for the same decline pattern** — As other branches adopt the procedure, verify they avoid the decline seen in the control group.

3. **Investigate root causes** — Why did control branches decline after week 14? Seasonal factors? Staffing changes? Visitor patterns? Understanding this could inform additional improvements.

4. **Set realistic expectations** — Communicate to branch managers that the new procedure helps maintain service quality, not necessarily improve it.
