# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

## **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

**Date:** November 2025  
**Prepared by:** Data Analysis Team

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The data show a positive association, but causal claims are uncertain.** Branches that adopted the new procedure had higher service score improvements than those that didn't (+1.9 points by standard analysis). However, **branches that chose to switch were already different and on a recovering trajectory before the change**, which makes it difficult to isolate the true effect of the procedure itself.

**Recommendation: Proceed with caution.** Consider a controlled pilot expansion with rigorous monitoring rather than immediate system-wide rollout.

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

> **DO NOT APPROVE SYSTEM-WIDE ROLLOUT AT THIS TIME.**
>
> The evidence is **suggestive but not conclusive**. The observed improvement cannot be confidently attributed to the new procedure due to pre-existing differences between branches. Approving a full rollout based on this analysis alone risks investing in a change that may not deliver the expected benefits.
>
> **Instead:** Authorize a **randomized controlled pilot** with 50–100 additional branches. This would cost little extra but would produce definitive evidence for a future rollout decision.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **1. What the Data Show**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 50.48 | 53.56 | +3.08 |
| Non-switched branches | 48.10 | 49.25 | +1.14 |
| **Observed Difference** | | | **+1.94** |

### **2. Critical Limitation: Non-Comparable Groups**

The branches that switched to the new procedure were **systematically different** from those that didn't:

| Issue | Evidence | Implication |
|-------|----------|-------------|
| **Different baseline scores** | Treatment: 50.48 vs. Control: 48.10 before switch | Groups were not equivalent at start |
| **Different pre-switch trends** | Treatment group was recovering from lower scores; control was stable | Cannot attribute post-switch improvement to the procedure alone |
| **Possible selection bias** | Branches may have self-selected into switching based on motivation, management quality, or other factors | Observed effect may reflect branch characteristics, not procedure effectiveness |

### **3. Event Study Reveals the Problem**

The "treatment effect" in the weeks *before* the switch shows the treatment group was already on a different trajectory:

- 8 weeks before switch: Treatment group scored 2.35 points below their eventual trajectory
- 1 week before switch: Treatment group scored only 0.01 points below
- **This recovery pattern predates the procedure change**

### **4. What We Can and Cannot Conclude**

| We CAN say | We CANNOT say |
|------------|---------------|
| Branches that switched saw larger score improvements | The new procedure **caused** the improvement |
| The association is statistically significant | The effect size is exactly +1.94 points |
| No negative effects were observed | All branches would see similar benefits |

---

## **CAVEATS**

1. **Selection bias**: Branches that volunteered (or were selected) to switch may differ in unobservable ways (management quality, staff motivation, local conditions)

2. **Violated parallel trends**: The key assumption for causal inference does not hold in this data

3. **Short observation window**: Only 8 weeks of post-switch data; long-term effects unknown

4. **No randomization**: Without random assignment, we cannot rule out confounding factors

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| **1** | **Do NOT proceed with full rollout yet** | Evidence is insufficient for system-wide change |
| **2** | **Design a randomized pilot** | Randomly assign 50–100 additional branches to switch; this would enable causal inference |
| **3** | **Gather qualitative data** | Interview staff at switched branches about what changed and why scores may have improved |
| **4** | **Extend monitoring period** | Track current switched branches for 6–12 more months to assess sustainability |
| **5** | **Re-analyze with better identification** | Once randomized data is available, produce a more definitive estimate |

---

## **CONCLUSION**

The observed association between the new procedure and service scores is encouraging, but **we cannot confidently attribute the improvement to the procedure itself**. The branches that switched were already different and on a different trajectory. A randomized pilot would provide the evidence needed for a confident rollout decision.

**Recommended decision: Pause full rollout. Invest in a properly designed evaluation.**
