# **Board Memorandum**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure  

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure shows a **small but statistically reliable positive effect** on service scores (+1.9 points, p=0.002). However, the effect is modest relative to normal week-to-week variation, and the finding was unexpected given prior expectations. We recommend a **cautious, phased rollout** rather than immediate systemwide adoption.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measured Impact on Service Scores

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Old Procedure (Control) | 49.6 | 48.0 | ▼ -1.7 |
| New Procedure (Treatment) | 51.3 | 51.5 | ▲ +0.2 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+1.9** |

### 2. Statistical vs. Practical Significance

| Metric | Value | Interpretation |
|--------|-------|----------------|
| Effect size | +1.93 points | Statistically detectable |
| P-value | 0.002 | Less than 1% chance of random occurrence |
| 95% Confidence Interval | +0.7 to +3.1 | Effect is real but modest |
| As % of average score | 3.8% | Small practical impact |
| Relative to weekly variation | 0.18 SD | Dwarfed by normal fluctuations (~10 points/week) |

### 3. Study Validity

- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (2,550 observations)
- **Parallel trends confirmed**: Both groups moved similarly before the switch
- **No pre-existing differences** in staffing or visit patterns between groups
- **Standard errors clustered** by branch to account for repeated observations

### 4. Important Context

- **Control group decline**: Old-procedure branches dropped 1.7 points after week 15. This may reflect seasonal factors or external pressures.
- **Treatment effect may be protective**: The new procedure appears to buffer against decline rather than drive substantial improvement.
- **Unexpected finding**: Prior expectation was no difference. This warrants careful validation before full commitment.

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did the procedure change service scores? | **Yes** — statistically detectable improvement |
| Is the effect large enough to justify immediate full rollout? | **No** — too modest relative to natural variation |
| Should we abandon the new procedure? | **No** — it shows benefit, particularly in protecting against decline |
| What should we do? | **Phased expansion with monitoring** |

**Verdict: Proceed with Caution**

The evidence supports continuing the new procedure, but not rushing to mandate it systemwide. A measured approach allows us to confirm the effect replicates and understand *why* it works before full commitment.

---

## **Recommendation**

| Action | Timeline | Success Metric |
|--------|----------|----------------|
| Approve expansion to 10-15 additional branches | 2 weeks | Implementation complete |
| Monitor service scores during expansion | 8-12 weeks | Effect size remains +1.5 or higher |
| Investigate why control branches declined | Ongoing | Identify external factors |
| Decide on full rollout based on expansion results | Week 12+ | Replicated effect in new branches |

---

## **Risks of Each Path**

| Path | Risk |
|------|------|
| **Immediate full rollout** | Disruption cost if effect doesn't replicate; opportunity cost if resources could be better deployed |
| **Phased expansion (recommended)** | Slower benefit realization; some branches wait longer for improvement |
| **Abandon procedure** | Lose protective effect against service score decline; waste of implementation effort to date |

---

## **Technical Notes**

Analysis used difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch). The parallel trends assumption was tested and supported (pre-treatment interaction p = 0.875). Full regression output and diagnostic tests available on request.
