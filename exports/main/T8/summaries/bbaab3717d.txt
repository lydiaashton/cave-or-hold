# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

---

## **Subject: Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

**Date:** November 2026  
**Prepared by:** Data Analysis Team

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure experienced an average increase of **1.76 points** in their service scores compared to branches that retained the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant (p = 0.003) and represents approximately a **3.5% improvement** relative to the typical branch score of 50.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with system-wide rollout of the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **1. Measurable Improvement in Service Quality**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect on service score | **+1.76 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | 0.68 to 2.85 points |
| Statistical significance | p = 0.003 |

The analysis compared 20 branches that switched to the new procedure against 14 branches that continued with the old procedure, using data from weeks 6–22 with the switch occurring at week 15.

### **2. Valid Comparison Between Groups**

A critical requirement for this analysis is that treatment and control branches were on similar trajectories before the change. Our testing confirms:
- **Pre-switch trends were parallel** (p = 0.73 for difference in trends)
- Both groups showed similar week-to-week variation before week 15
- This supports attributing the observed improvement to the new procedure itself

### **3. Improvement Reflects the Procedure, Not Other Factors**

We tested whether changes in staffing or branch visits could explain the results:
- **Staffing levels:** No significant difference between groups after the switch
- **Branch visits:** No significant difference between groups after the switch

The service score gain appears to stem from the procedural change itself, not from changes in resources or demand.

### **4. Treatment Branches Improved; Control Branches Did Not**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 51.1 | 52.8 | +1.7 |
| Non-switched branches | 52.6 | 52.4 | -0.2 |

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

**The evidence supports system-wide adoption.**

| Criterion | Assessment |
|-----------|------------|
| **Statistical rigor** | ✓ Passed—robust difference-in-differences design with clustered standard errors |
| **Causal identification** | ✓ Passed—parallel pre-trends confirmed; no confounding factors detected |
| **Effect magnitude** | ✓ Meaningful—1.76-point gain represents ~3.5% improvement |
| **Consistency** | ✓ Passed—treatment branches improved on average; control branches did not |
| **Practical significance** | ✓ Passed—service scores are a key performance metric for the cooperative |

**Confidence Level: HIGH**

The analysis meets standard criteria for causal inference. The probability that this result is due to random chance is less than 1%. While not every individual branch showed improvement (reflecting normal operational variation), the aggregate effect is clear and reliable.

**Risk Assessment: LOW**

- No evidence of negative side effects on staffing efficiency or visit patterns
- Control branches showed slight decline, suggesting the new procedure may also protect against seasonal deterioration
- Implementation risk is limited to training and transition logistics, not procedural effectiveness

## **NEXT STEPS**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all 34 branches

2. **Monitor implementation** during rollout:
   - Track service scores for newly converted branches to confirm similar gains
   - Document branch-specific challenges during transition

3. **Plan phased implementation** if operational constraints require it:
   - The evidence supports the procedure's effectiveness; timing can accommodate staffing and training capacity

4. **Continue data collection:**
   - Extend monitoring to assess whether effects persist or grow over time
   - Capture secondary benefits (staff satisfaction, operational efficiency) not reflected in service scores

5. **Document best practices** from the 20 early-adopter branches to support training at remaining locations

---

## **ANALYSIS DETAILS**

- **Data:** 578 observations (34 branches × 17 weeks)
- **Study period:** Weeks 6–22; procedure switch at week 15
- **Method:** Difference-in-differences regression with branch fixed effects and clustered standard errors
- **Robustness:** Results unchanged when controlling for staffing and visits indices
