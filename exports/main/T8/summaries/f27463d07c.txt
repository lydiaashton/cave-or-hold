# Board Summary: New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## Bottom Line

**The evidence is inconclusive.** While branches that adopted the new procedure showed higher service scores afterward, these branches were already outperforming control branches before the switch. We cannot confidently attribute the improvement to the procedure itself. **We do not recommend a full rollout at this time.** Instead, we recommend a randomized controlled trial to establish causal evidence.

---

## Final Verdict

### **DO NOT PROCEED WITH FULL ROLLOUT**

**Reason:** The analysis fails to establish that the new procedure *caused* the observed improvement. The branches that switched were already improving relative to control branches before the switch occurred. This selection bias means the 2.1-point "effect" likely reflects pre-existing differences between branches, not the procedure's impact.

**What would change our recommendation:**
- A randomized trial showing similar effects
- Evidence that the procedure works across diverse branch types
- Demonstration that effects persist beyond the initial observation period

**Risk of proceeding:** Rolling out an unproven procedure across all branches could waste resources on an ineffective change, or worse, mask the real drivers of service score differences that should be addressed instead.

---

## Key Findings

### 1. What the Data Shows

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Raw DiD estimate** | +2.12 points |
| **Statistical significance** | p < 0.001 |
| **BUT: Pre-trend test** | p = 0.0002 (significant) |

The statistical significance is misleading. The treatment and control groups were on different trajectories before the intervention, which invalidates the causal interpretation.

### 2. The Core Problem: Pre-Existing Trends

| Week | Treatment − Control Gap |
|------|------------------------|
| Week 6 | −0.37 points |
| Week 10 | +0.69 points |
| Week 14 (last pre-week) | +1.84 points |
| Week 22 (last post-week) | +4.05 points |

**The gap was widening before the switch ever happened.** Treatment branches gained 2.20 points on control branches during the pre-period alone. This suggests:
- Branches that chose to switch were systematically different (selection bias)
- The observed "effect" may reflect pre-existing momentum, not the procedure

### 3. Alternative Explanations

The data is consistent with several interpretations:
1. **Selection bias**: Better-performing branches opted into the new procedure
2. **Confounding factors**: Treatment branches may have had other improvements (staffing, management, local conditions)
3. **True treatment effect**: The procedure may help, but we cannot isolate its contribution from the above factors

### 4. What We Can Say With Confidence

- Branches using the new procedure have higher service scores than those that did not
- The gap between the two groups continued growing after the switch
- We **cannot** determine whether the procedure caused this difference

---

## Next Steps

### Recommended Actions

1. **Do NOT proceed with full rollout** — the evidence does not support a causal claim

2. **Conduct a randomized controlled trial**:
   - Randomly assign remaining branches to treatment or control
   - This eliminates selection bias and enables valid causal inference
   - Monitor for 12+ weeks to capture full effect

3. **Investigate selection factors**:
   - What characteristics distinguished branches that chose to switch?
   - Were there concurrent changes (staffing, management, local conditions)?

4. **Continue monitoring** both groups to gather more data

### If Board Wishes to Proceed Anyway

If the board decides to rollout despite inconclusive evidence:
- Implement in phases with careful monitoring
- Set clear success metrics and stopping rules
- Be prepared to reverse course if results do not materialize

## Technical Notes

- **Data:** 12,053 observations (709 branches × 17 weeks)
- **Analysis period:** Weeks 6-22 (switch occurred at week 15)
- **Method:** Difference-in-differences with branch fixed effects
- **Critical limitation:** Parallel trends assumption violated (pre-trend p = 0.0002)
- **Sample:** 425 treatment branches (self-selected), 284 control branches
