# **Memorandum to the Board**

## **Title: Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

### **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure produced a modest but reliable improvement in service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by **2.1 points** (about 4%) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and was not expected. While the improvement is real, it is modest in practical terms. The board should weigh this benefit against implementation costs when deciding on system-wide rollout.

---

### **Key Findings**

1. **Statistically Significant Effect**: Switched branches improved by **2.13 points** more than non-switched branches (p<0.001). The 95% confidence interval [0.97, 3.30] excludes zero, confirming the effect is reliable.

2. **Valid Comparison**: Treatment and control groups were nearly identical before the switch (48.8 vs. 48.9, p=0.91) and followed parallel trends in the pre-period (p=0.80). This supports a causal interpretation.

3. **Modest Effect Size**: The 2.1-point improvement equals 0.21 standard deviations—detectable and consistent, but not large. Service scores naturally vary by ~10 points across branches and weeks.

4. **Consistent Across Time**: The treatment group outperformed the control group in all 8 post-switch weeks, with no evidence of fading.

5. **Unexpected Result**: This analysis was intended to confirm the procedure change had no negative impact. The positive effect was unanticipated and may reflect secondary benefits (e.g., staff efficiency, easier navigation) worth investigating.

---

### **Final Verdict**

**Recommendation: Proceed with rollout, with measured expectations.**

The evidence supports adopting the new procedure system-wide. The improvement is statistically robust, consistent across time, and comes with no identified downside. However, the board should expect modest gains—not a transformation. A 2-point service score improvement is meaningful for patron experience but should not be oversold.

If implementation costs are low (minimal training, brief disruption), the rollout is clearly worthwhile. If costs are substantial, the board may wish to conduct a formal cost-benefit analysis before proceeding.

---

### **Next Steps**

1. **Cost-Benefit Assessment**: Compare the service score gain against implementation costs (training, temporary disruption, staff time).

2. **Pilot Expansion**: Consider expanding to 20-30 additional branches to confirm the effect replicates before committing to system-wide adoption.

3. **Understand the Mechanism**: Gather feedback from switched branches to identify *why* scores improved. This could reveal additional benefits not captured in the service score.

4. **Monitor Other Outcomes**: Track operational metrics (circulation, staff hours, patron complaints) to capture the full impact of the procedure change.

5. **Decision Timeline**: Allow 4-6 weeks for cost-benefit analysis and stakeholder input before making a rollout decision.

---

*Analysis prepared using difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors. The study design supports causal inference, though the effect size is modest.*
