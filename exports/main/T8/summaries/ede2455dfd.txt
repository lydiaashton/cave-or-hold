# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD OF DIRECTORS**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure  
**Analysis Period:** Weeks 6–22 (17 weeks of data)  
**Data:** 709 branches, 12,053 weekly observations

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

### ❌ **DO NOT PROCEED WITH SYSTEM-WIDE ROLLOUT**

**The evidence does not support a causal benefit of the new procedure.** The observed improvement in service scores at treatment branches reflects pre-existing differences between branches—not an effect of the intervention itself.

**Confidence Level:** High  
**Risk of Rollout:** Wasted resources on an unproven procedure; opportunity cost of not testing alternatives

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

While branches using the new procedure showed a 1.9-point higher service score gain compared to control branches, **this difference cannot be attributed to the procedure.** Treatment branches were already improving relative to control branches before the switch occurred, violating the fundamental assumption required for causal inference.

**Recommendation: Halt expansion plans and conduct a properly randomized trial before any system-wide decision.**

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### 1. Parallel Trends Assumption Violated

The Difference-in-Differences method requires treatment and control groups to follow similar trajectories before intervention. **This condition is not met:**

| Period | Treatment-Control Gap | Weekly Trend |
|--------|----------------------|--------------|
| Weeks 6-11 | -0.76 to +0.70 | Treatment catching up |
| Weeks 12-14 (immediately pre-switch) | +1.71 to +2.23 | **+0.37 points/week** |
| Weeks 15-22 (post-switch) | +1.89 to +3.14 | +0.15 points/week |

The gap was already widening **before** the intervention. By week 14, treatment branches were already ahead by 2.23 points.

### 2. No Evidence of Intervention Effect

If the new procedure caused improvement, we would observe a discontinuous jump at week 15:

| Week | Gap | Observation |
|------|-----|-------------|
| Week 14 (last pre) | 2.23 | — |
| Week 15 (first post) | 2.08 | **Gap decreased** |
| Week 16 | 2.62 | — |

There is **no level shift** at the intervention point.

### 3. Counterfactual Analysis Confirms No Effect

Projecting the pre-intervention trend forward:

| Week | Actual Gap | Predicted Gap | Difference |
|------|------------|---------------|------------|
| 15 | 2.08 | 2.55 | -0.48 |
| 18 | 2.55 | 3.65 | -1.10 |
| 22 | 2.93 | 5.12 | -2.19 |

**Average:** Actual gaps were 1.21 points **lower** than the pre-trend would have predicted. The intervention shows no positive effect—and may have slightly dampened the existing improvement trajectory.

### 4. What the +1.90 Estimate Actually Represents

The original Difference-in-Differences estimate captures:
- ✅ Pre-existing branch characteristics (management quality, staff motivation, resources)
- ✅ Secular trends unrelated to the procedure
- ❌ **NOT** the causal effect of the new procedure

---

## **ALTERNATIVE EXPLANATIONS FOR OBSERVED DIFFERENCES**

1. **Selection bias:** Branches adopting the new procedure may have had more motivated staff or better management
2. **Regression to the mean:** Some treatment branches had unusually low early scores and were naturally recovering
3. **Concurrent improvements:** Treatment branches may have implemented other changes simultaneously
4. **No true effect:** The procedure itself may have zero impact on service quality

---

## **RECOMMENDED NEXT STEPS**

### Immediate Actions

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| **1** | **Halt system-wide rollout** | No evidence of causal benefit |
| **2** | **Design randomized controlled trial** | Random assignment eliminates selection bias |
| **3** | **Extend observation period** | 17 weeks may be insufficient to detect effects |
| **4** | **Add qualitative measures** | Staff interviews, patron surveys, operational metrics |

### If Board Wishes to Proceed Anyway

Should the Board decide to move forward despite weak evidence:

- Implement as a **limited pilot** (not system-wide)
- Use **random assignment** of branches to treatment/control
- **Pre-register success criteria** before expansion
- Budget for **rigorous evaluation** and potential reversal
- Set a **review date** (e.g., 6 months) with clear go/no-go thresholds

---

## **LESSONS FOR FUTURE EVALUATIONS**

1. **Randomize when possible:** Voluntary adoption creates selection bias that is difficult to correct statistically
2. **Verify parallel trends:** Always examine pre-intervention trajectories before trusting DiD estimates
3. **Look for level shifts:** A true intervention effect should show a discontinuous change at the intervention point
4. **Use multiple methods:** Triangulate with event studies, counterfactual projections, and qualitative data

---

## **CONCLUSION**

This analysis began with a promising finding—branches using the new procedure improved more than those that didn't. However, deeper examination reveals that **the procedure itself likely had no causal effect.** The observed difference reflects which branches chose to adopt, not what the adoption accomplished.

**The Board should not approve system-wide rollout based on this evidence.** Resources would be better spent on a properly designed evaluation or on alternative improvements with clearer evidence of effectiveness.

---

**Final Verdict: REJECT system-wide rollout. Require stronger evidence before expansion.**
