# **Board Summary: Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure **produced a modest but statistically significant improvement** in service scores of approximately **1.8 points** (3.6% increase). While this contradicts the expectation of no difference, the effect is relatively small and based on only 8 weeks of post-implementation data. We recommend **a measured rollout** with careful attention to implementation costs and continued monitoring to confirm the effect persists.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **150 branches** participated (90 switched to the new procedure, 60 kept the old procedure)
- Data covers **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach with branch and week fixed effects

### **Results**

| Period | Treatment Branches (New Procedure) | Control Branches (Old Procedure) |
|--------|-----------------------------------|----------------------------------|
| Before switch (weeks 6–14) | 50.03 | 48.77 |
| After switch (weeks 15–22) | 51.47 | 48.43 |
| **Change** | **+1.44** | **-0.34** |

**Estimated effect of new procedure: +1.77 points**  
95% confidence interval: 0.54 to 3.00 points | p-value: 0.005

### **Important Caveats**
- **Short observation window**: Only 8 weeks of post-switch data; long-term effects are unknown
- **Modest effect size**: The improvement, while statistically significant, represents a 3.6% increase
- **Costs not assessed**: This analysis does not account for implementation or operating costs of the new procedure

---

## **Final Verdict**

**Proceed with rollout, but conditionally.**

The data provides credible evidence that the new procedure improves service scores. The statistical analysis is sound, and the effect is unlikely to be due to chance. However, given the modest effect size and limited post-implementation data, we recommend:

| If... | Then... |
|-------|---------|
| Implementation costs are **low or negligible** | Proceed with full rollout |
| Implementation costs are **moderate** | Proceed with phased rollout and continued monitoring |
| Implementation costs are **substantial** | Delay rollout pending cost-benefit analysis |

**Minimum requirements before full rollout:**
1. Complete a cost assessment of the new procedure
2. Commit to at least 6 months of continued outcome monitoring
3. Establish a review point to reassess after one full year of data

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Assess implementation costs**: Determine whether the new procedure requires additional staff time, training, or resources.

2. **Pilot expansion**: Consider rolling out to additional branches in phases while monitoring outcomes.

3. **Extend monitoring period**: Continue collecting data for at least 6–12 more months.

4. **Investigate mechanisms**: Understand *why* scores improved to optimize the procedure further.

5. **Schedule follow-up review**: Bring this matter back to the board after 6 months with updated data and cost information.

---

*Analysis prepared using weekly service score data from 150 branches over 17 weeks. Statistical models include branch and week fixed effects with standard errors clustered by branch.*
