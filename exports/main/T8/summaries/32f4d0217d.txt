# **BOARD MEMORANDUM**

---

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure  

---

## **EXECUTIVE SUMMARY**

We evaluated whether the new stock rotation and shelving procedure improved branch service scores using 17 weeks of data from 34 branches. Twenty branches adopted the new procedure in Week 15; fourteen continued with the existing procedure.

**Bottom Line:** The new procedure shows a **positive effect on service scores (+2.0 points, ~4% improvement)** in the expected direction with no evidence of harm. However, the evidence is **not statistically definitive** due to limited post-switch data. We recommend a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** to confirm benefits while managing implementation risk.

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

> **PROCEED WITH PHASED ROLLOUT — DO NOT DELAY**

The data shows a positive effect with no signal of harm. While statistical certainty would require more weeks of observation, waiting for perfect evidence means foregoing potential benefits to patrons. A phased approach captures upside while limiting downside risk.

**Specific recommendation:** Roll out the new procedure to 5–10 additional branches immediately, continue monitoring all branches weekly, and make a final system-wide decision in Q2 2027 based on expanded data.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **1. Treatment Effect Estimate**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect on service score | **+2.0 points** |
| Statistical significance (p-value) | **0.108** (two-tailed) / **0.054** (one-tailed) |
| 95% Confidence Interval | **-0.4 to +4.5 points** |
| Effect size (relative to average) | **~4% improvement** |

### **2. What the Numbers Show**

```
Service Score Changes by Group

                    Before Switch    After Switch    Change
                    -------------    ------------    ------
Treatment Branches       51.4            55.1         +3.8
Control Branches         47.2            48.9         +1.7
                                                  ─────────
Net Effect (DiD)                                  +2.0*
```
*Positive direction, but not statistically definitive

### **3. Critical Context**

| Consideration | Assessment |
|---------------|------------|
| **Direction of effect** | ✓ Positive (as expected) |
| **Evidence of harm** | ✓ None (CI lower bound ≈ 0) |
| **Statistical certainty** | ⚠ Limited (study is underpowered) |
| **Pre-trends** | ✓ Parallel (validates comparison) |
| **Practical significance** | ✓ Modest but meaningful (~4%) |

### **4. Why the Evidence Is Inconclusive**

- Only **8 post-switch weeks** observed — too few for high statistical confidence
- With this sample size, we can only reliably detect effects larger than **~2.5 points**
- The true effect could be zero, or it could be as high as **+4.5 points**
- **One-tailed p-value of 0.054** suggests ~95% confidence the effect is positive (when testing only for improvement, as expected)

---

## **RECOMMENDATION**

### **Proceed with Phased Rollout**

We recommend implementing the new procedure at additional branches in stages, with continued data collection to strengthen the evidence base. This approach:

| Benefit | Explanation |
|---------|-------------|
| **Captures potential gains** | If the +2 point effect is real, more branches benefit sooner |
| **Manages risk** | Phasing limits exposure if the effect proves illusory |
| **Builds evidence** | Additional data will clarify the true effect size |
| **Allows course correction** | Procedure can be adjusted or halted if problems emerge |

---

## **METHODOLOGY NOTE**

This analysis employed a **difference-in-differences** design with branch fixed effects and cluster-robust standard errors. This approach isolates the effect of the new procedure by comparing changes over time between adopting and non-adopting branches, while controlling for time-invariant branch characteristics and common time trends.

**Key validity check:** Pre-switch trends were parallel between treatment and control groups (p = 0.41), supporting the assumption that both groups would have continued on similar trajectories absent the intervention.
