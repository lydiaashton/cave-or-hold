# Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact Analysis

**To:** Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## Bottom Line

The evidence is **inconclusive**. While the new procedure shows a positive point estimate (+1.6 points), the result is **not statistically significant** (p = 0.11), and the 95% confidence interval includes zero. There are also concerns about whether the switched branches were comparable to control branches before the change. **We recommend extending the pilot to gather more data before making a system-wide rollout decision.**

---

## Final Verdict

**DO NOT ROLLOUT — EXTEND PILOT**

| Criterion | Status | Threshold |
|-----------|--------|-----------|
| Statistical significance | ❌ Not met | p < 0.05 |
| Confidence interval excludes zero | ❌ Not met | 95% CI > 0 |
| Parallel trends assumption | ⚠️ Questionable | p > 0.10 |
| Consistent post-period effect | ❌ Not met | Majority of weeks positive |

**Verdict:** The data do not currently support a system-wide rollout. Extend the pilot for 12-16 weeks and re-evaluate.

---

## Key Findings

### The Evidence

| Metric | Value | What It Means |
|--------|-------|---------------|
| **Estimated effect** | +1.6 points | Positive direction, as expected |
| **Statistical significance** | p = 0.11 | Not significant at 0.05 threshold |
| **95% confidence interval** | -0.4 to +3.6 | Cannot rule out zero effect |
| **Pre-trend test** | p = 0.09 | Concerning — groups may not have been comparable |
| **Baseline gap** | -1.9 points | Treatment branches started below control |

### What the Data Actually Shows

- **Before the switch:** Treatment branches averaged 1.9 points **below** control branches
- **After the switch:** Treatment branches narrowed the gap but still trailed by 0.3 points
- **Week-to-week:** In the 8 weeks post-switch, treatment branches outperformed control in only **3 of 8 weeks**

### The Core Problem

The switched branches were already underperforming before the change. This creates two competing explanations:

1. **Optimistic:** The new procedure helped them catch up (+1.6 point improvement)
2. **Skeptical:** The apparent improvement is regression to the mean, not a real treatment effect

Our analysis cannot definitively distinguish between these.

---

## Recommendation

**Do NOT proceed with full rollout yet.** Instead:

1. **Extend the pilot by 12-16 weeks** to gather more post-switch data
2. **Consider adding more branches** to the treatment group to improve statistical power
3. **Re-analyze** with additional data before making a rollout decision
4. **Document implementation fidelity** — ensure switched branches are actually following the new procedure

### Decision Criteria for Future Review

| If the updated analysis shows... | Then... |
|----------------------------------|---------|
| Effect > 1.5 points, p < 0.05 | Proceed with rollout |
| Effect > 1.0 points, p < 0.10 | Consider phased rollout |
| Effect < 1.0 points or p > 0.10 | Reconsider the procedure |

---

## Why This Matters

A system-wide rollout requires staff training, potential workflow disruption, and opportunity cost. Given the current uncertainty, the prudent course is to **wait for stronger evidence** before committing resources. The fact that the point estimate is positive is encouraging — but not yet actionable.

---

**Bottom line for decision-makers:** The results are suggestive but not definitive. More data is needed before a rollout decision can be made with confidence.

---

*Analysis based on 578 weekly observations from 34 branches (20 switched, 14 control) over weeks 6-22. Standard errors clustered at the branch level.*
