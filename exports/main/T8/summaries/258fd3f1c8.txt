# **Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure likely improved service scores, but the exact magnitude is uncertain.** The best estimate is a **1.5 to 2.5 point improvement** (3-5% relative to baseline), though pre-existing trends in the switching branches make precise estimation difficult. The evidence supports proceeding with rollout, but with realistic expectations and continued monitoring.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Positive Effect, But Identification Challenges
- **Main estimate:** +2.05 points (95% CI: 1.5 to 2.6)
- **Conservative estimate** (excluding weeks 13-14): +2.43 points (95% CI: 1.8 to 3.0)
- **Important caveat:** Switching branches began outperforming non-switching branches 1-2 weeks *before* the official switch date, which complicates causal interpretation

### 2. Pre-Trend Pattern (Critical for Interpretation)
| Period | Treatment-Control Difference |
|--------|------------------------------|
| Weeks 6-12 | -0.05 (essentially equal) |
| Weeks 13-14 | +1.65 (divergence begins) |
| Weeks 15-22 | +2.38 (post-switch) |

The divergence starting in weeks 13-14 could reflect:
- **Early adoption** (some branches started early) → our estimate is *conservative*
- **Selection bias** (improving branches chose to switch) → our estimate may be *optimistic*

### 3. Supportive Evidence
- **54.6%** of switched branches improved vs. **36.6%** of non-switched
- Non-switched branches **declined** by 1.3 points; switched branches **gained** 0.7 points
- **71%** of switched branches outperformed the average non-switched branch
- Effect remains statistically significant (p < 0.001) even with conservative pre-period

### 4. What We Can Confidently Say
- The new procedure did **not harm** service scores
- Switched branches performed **at least as well as** non-switched branches
- The **most plausible interpretation** is a modest positive effect (1.5-2.5 points)
- System-wide factors negatively affected non-switched branches; the new procedure provided resilience

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with rollout** — the weight of evidence supports a positive effect, and there's no evidence of harm

2. **Set realistic expectations** — communicate that 1.5-2.5 point improvement is the likely range, not a guaranteed outcome

3. **Monitor implementation closely** — track service scores for 8-12 weeks post-rollout to confirm effects persist

4. **Support struggling branches** — 29% of switched branches did not outperform controls; identify and address barriers

5. **Document implementation practices** — capture what high-performing branches did differently to inform training

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Criterion | Assessment |
|-----------|------------|
| **Did the procedure help?** | ✓ Yes — evidence points to positive effect |
| **Is the effect size certain?** | ✗ No — pre-trends create uncertainty |
| **Is there risk of harm?** | ✓ No — no negative effects detected |
| **Recommendation** | **PROCEED WITH ROLLOUT** |

**Rationale:** While we cannot pinpoint the exact effect size, the balance of evidence favors adoption: (1) switched branches demonstrably outperformed controls, (2) there is no evidence of harm, (3) the procedure provides resilience against system-wide score declines, and (4) the risk of inaction (continued decline in non-switched branches) exceeds the risk of rollout.
