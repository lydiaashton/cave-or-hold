# **Library Cooperative Stock Rotation Procedure: Impact Analysis**

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure **produced a statistically significant improvement** in service scores (+2.2 points, p < 0.001), contrary to the expectation of no difference. While the effect is modest, it is consistent across most branches and comes with no identified downsides in the available data. **We recommend proceeding with rollout**, while monitoring costs, long-term sustainability, and providing support to branches that may struggle with the transition.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure (treatment group)
- **60 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Data covers **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in service scores before and after the switch between the two groups
- **Pre-trends were parallel** (p = 0.26), validating the comparison

### **Results**

| Metric | Control Branches (Old Procedure) | Treatment Branches (New Procedure) |
|--------|----------------------------------|------------------------------------|
| Average score before switch | 49.1 | 49.1 |
| Average score after switch | 50.0 | 52.2 |
| **Change** | **+0.9** | **+3.2** |

**Estimated effect of new procedure: +2.2 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.0 to 3.5 points)

### **Statistical Significance**
- The improvement is **highly statistically significant** (p < 0.001)
- The effect remains significant after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume
- The parallel trends assumption is satisfied—both groups showed similar patterns before the switch

### **Branch-Level Impact**
- **83% of branches** using the new procedure improved their service scores
- Only **60% of branches** using the old procedure improved
- The benefit was consistent across branches of different sizes and baseline performance levels

### **Effect Size Context**
- The 2.2-point improvement represents **4.5% of the average service score**
- In statistical terms, this is **0.23 standard deviations**—a small-to-moderate effect
- The effect equals about **18% of the interquartile range** of scores across all observations

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did the new procedure change service scores? | **Yes** — statistically significant improvement |
| Was the effect large? | **Modest** — meaningful but not transformative |
| Was the effect consistent? | **Yes** — 83% of treatment branches improved |
| Are there identified risks? | **None in the data** — though costs and long-term effects unknown |
| **Recommendation** | **Proceed with rollout** |

**Rationale:** The board expected no difference from the new procedure. The data clearly shows a positive difference. While the effect is not large, it is real, consistent, and comes without identified downsides. In the absence of countervailing costs or operational concerns, the evidence supports system-wide adoption.

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new stock rotation and shelving procedure to all 150 branches

2. **Develop implementation timeline**—consider phasing the rollout to manage training and transition logistics

3. **Monitor service scores** during rollout to confirm the effect holds at scale and identify any branches needing additional support

4. **Document best practices** from high-performing branches to support training materials

5. **Track implementation costs** alongside service scores to ensure the benefit justifies any additional resources required

6. **Re-evaluate after 6 months** to assess long-term sustainability of the effect

*Analysis prepared using 17 weeks of data from 150 branches. Statistical methods account for repeated measurements and control for staffing and visitor volume variations.*
