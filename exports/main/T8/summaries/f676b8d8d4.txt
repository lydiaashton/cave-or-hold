# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

**To:** Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores, contrary to initial expectations.** Branches that adopted the new shelving and rotation procedure saw service scores increase by **2.1 points** (approximately 4%) relative to branches that kept the old procedure. The evidence is statistically strong and supports proceeding with a system-wide rollout.

---

## **Final Verdict**

### **✅ APPROVE SYSTEM-WIDE ROLLOUT**

**Confidence Level: High**

| Criterion | Assessment |
|-----------|------------|
| **Statistical Evidence** | Strong (p < 0.001) |
| **Effect Size** | Meaningful (+4.2%) |
| **Risk** | Low (already tested on 90 branches) |
| **Cost-Benefit** | Favorable (no additional costs identified) |

**Rationale:** The data provides clear, robust evidence that the new procedure improves service quality. While this result was unexpected, the analysis is methodologically sound and the effect is large enough to matter for patron experience. The 90 branches that switched serve as a successful pilot. Delaying rollout would deny patrons at the remaining 60 branches a demonstrably better service experience.

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measurable Improvement
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Treatment Effect** | +2.13 service score points |
| **95% Confidence Interval** | +0.98 to +3.28 points |
| **Statistical Significance** | p = 0.0003 (highly significant) |
| **Relative Improvement** | +4.2% vs. baseline |

### 2. What the Data Shows

| Group | Branches | Pre-Switch Avg | Post-Switch Avg | Net Change |
|-------|----------|----------------|-----------------|------------|
| **Switched to new procedure** | 90 | 50.5 | 51.0 | +0.5 |
| **Kept old procedure** | 60 | 51.3 | 49.7 | −1.6 |

**Interpretation:** All branches experienced some downward pressure on scores after week 15 (likely seasonal). However, switched branches not only resisted this decline but improved slightly. The **2.1-point difference** represents the procedure's net benefit.

### 3. Why We Trust This Result

- **Clean comparison:** 90 treatment branches vs. 60 control branches, tracked over 17 weeks (2,550 observations)
- **Parallel trends confirmed:** Before the switch, both groups moved together (no pre-existing divergence, p = 0.20)
- **Robust to alternative explanations:** Effect persists after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume
- **Statistically rigorous:** Analysis uses branch fixed effects and clustered standard errors

## **Caveats & Considerations**

1. **Unexpected result:** We anticipated no measurable effect. The positive outcome is welcome but warrants careful monitoring during rollout to confirm it replicates at scale.

2. **Mechanism unclear:** The analysis shows *that* scores improved, not *why*. We recommend gathering qualitative feedback from switched branches to understand what drove the improvement.

3. **Seasonal factors:** The post-switch period (weeks 15-22) may have unique characteristics. A 6-month follow-up will confirm whether benefits persist.

4. **Implementation quality:** Effects may vary by branch. Provide additional support to branches that historically struggle with procedural changes.

---

## **Appendix: Methodology**

- **Design:** Difference-in-differences quasi-experiment
- **Data:** 150 branches × 17 weeks (weeks 6-22)
- **Treatment:** 90 branches switched procedures at week 15; 60 branches did not
- **Model:** Branch fixed effects with clustered standard errors
- **Outcome:** Service score index (average ~50, higher is better)
