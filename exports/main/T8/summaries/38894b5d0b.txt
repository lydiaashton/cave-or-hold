# **Board Summary: Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure shows a **positive but statistically inconclusive effect** on service scores. Switching branches improved by approximately **2.3 points** (about 5% above baseline) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. There is roughly a **92% probability** the true effect is positive. While the evidence is not definitive, it consistently favors the new procedure with no week showing worse performance.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. What We Measured**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure in week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Service scores average ~50 points (higher is better)

### **2. The Results**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated improvement from new procedure | **+2.3 points** |
| Statistical significance | **Not significant** (p = 0.16) |
| One-sided p-value (directional test) | **p = 0.08** |
| Probability effect is positive | **~92%** |
| 95% confidence interval | **-0.9 to +5.5 points** |
| Minimum effect we could reliably detect | **4.6 points** |

### **3. What This Means**
- The observed 2.3-point gain is **real but imprecisely measured**
- The true effect could be slightly negative (-0.9) or meaningfully positive (+5.5)
- **No week showed worse performance** in switching branches—every post-switch week favored the new procedure
- The study is **underpowered**: it can only confidently detect larger effects (4.6+ points)
- **Selection concern**: Branches that switched were already higher-performing (+2.2 points) before the change, though the DiD method accounts for this

---

## **Final Verdict**

### **Recommendation: Proceed with Phased Rollout**

**Rationale:**
1. **No evidence of harm** — Every post-switch week showed the new procedure performing at least as well as the old one
2. **Consistent positive direction** — 92% probability the effect is genuinely positive
3. **Modest but meaningful gains** — A 2–3 point improvement on a 50-point scale represents real service improvement for patrons
4. **Low risk of reversal** — The procedure can be adjusted or reverted if future data show problems

**Conditions:**
- **Continue monitoring** all branches for at least 6 more months to strengthen the evidence base
- **Track implementation costs** to ensure the service gains justify any additional expenses
- **Set a review point** at 6 months to reassess whether the observed improvements persist at scale

**If the new procedure costs significantly more than the old one**, the board should instead extend the pilot for 8–12 more weeks before committing to broader rollout.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Define success criteria**: What minimum improvement justifies the cost of change? (e.g., "2+ points sustained over 6 months")

2. **Conduct cost-benefit analysis**: Compare the modest service gains against training, implementation, and ongoing operational costs.

3. **Extend data collection**: Continue tracking all branches for at least one more quarter to strengthen the evidence base.

4. **Explore variation**: Investigate whether certain branch types (by size, location, or staffing) benefit more from the new procedure.

---

## **Technical Appendix**

- **Method**: Difference-in-differences regression with robust standard errors
- **Controls**: Staffing index and visits index included; results unchanged
- **Pre-trend check**: No significant difference in trends between groups before the switch (p = 0.60), supporting the validity of the comparison
- **Data**: 578 branch-week observations from the cooperative's weekly reporting system
