# **Board Summary: Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The data show an unexpected improvement in service scores.** Our analysis finds that branches using the new procedure scored approximately **2 points higher** than those using the old procedure—a statistically significant difference we did not anticipate. However, because this was not a randomized trial, we recommend proceeding with the rollout while continuing to monitor outcomes to confirm the effect holds at scale.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. What the Data Show**
- **Estimated effect**: +2.1 points (95% confidence interval: 1.0 to 3.3 points)
- **Statistical significance**: p < 0.001
- Switched branches averaged 53.9 after the change vs. 52.6 before
- Non-switched branches averaged 51.2 after vs. 52.0 before
- **This contradicts our prior expectation** that the procedural change would not affect service scores

### **2. Why We Can Be Reasonably Confident**
- **Comparable groups**: Pre-switch trends were statistically indistinguishable between switched and non-switched branches (p = 0.93)
- **Large sample**: 150 branches, 2,550 weekly observations
- **Consistent pattern**: All 8 post-switch weeks showed positive effects; none showed negative effects
- **Robust to controls**: The effect persists after accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume

### **3. Important Caveats**
- **Not a randomized trial**: Branches were not randomly assigned to switch. Unobserved differences could explain part of the effect.
- **Effect size**: A 2-point gain on a ~50-point scale (~4%) is statistically significant but modest in practical terms.
- **Limited follow-up**: We have only 8 weeks of post-switch data; longer-term effects are unknown.
- **Unexpected finding**: Since we expected no effect, we should verify this result before assuming it will replicate everywhere.

---

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended Actions**
1. **Proceed with rollout, but monitor closely**—the evidence supports moving forward, but we should verify the effect persists
2. **Set up ongoing tracking**: Compare service scores before and after each batch of branches switches
3. **Investigate the mechanism**: Understand *why* scores improved (faster service? cleaner shelves? staff morale?) to reinforce what's working
4. **Consider a formal evaluation**: If resources allow, track additional metrics (patron satisfaction, staff time, operational costs)

### **Decision Framework**
| If the board prioritizes... | Recommendation |
|----------------------------|----------------|
| Capturing potential upside | Proceed with rollout; 2-point gain is "free" value |
| Minimizing risk of false positive | Phase the rollout and verify effects at each stage |
| Understanding the why | Pause to investigate mechanism before full rollout |

---

## **Final Verdict**

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout.**

**Rationale:**
- The statistical evidence is strong (p < 0.001, consistent across all post-period weeks)
- The parallel trends test supports the validity of the comparison
- The potential upside (2-point service score gain) comes at no additional cost
- The risk of proceeding is low—if the effect doesn't replicate, we can pause; if it does, we capture value we weren't expecting

**Conditions:**
1. Implement in phases with score tracking at each stage
2. Set a review point after 8–12 weeks to confirm effects persist
3. Document any branches where scores do not improve for follow-up investigation
