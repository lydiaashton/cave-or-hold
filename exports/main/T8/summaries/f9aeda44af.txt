# **BOARD MEMORANDUM**

---

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 2025  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure  

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The evidence suggests the new procedure improved service scores, but with important caveats.** We estimate a **2.2-point improvement** (4.3%), and we can be 95% confident the true effect is at least **1.8 points**. However, the analysis has limitations that warrant a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **1. Estimated Effect**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Point Estimate | **+2.2 points** on service score index |
| 95% Confidence Interval | 1.83 to 2.54 points |
| Statistical Significance | p < 0.001 |
| Effect Size | 23% of one standard deviation (moderate) |

### **2. What the Data Shows**

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|----------------|---------------|--------|
| Branches with **New Procedure** | 51.0 | 52.0 | +1.0 |
| Branches with **Old Procedure** | 51.0 | 49.9 | −1.2 |

### **3. Strengths of the Analysis**

✓ **No selection bias**: Branches that switched were not systematically different from those that didn't (pre-period scores were identical: 51.00 vs. 51.03, p=0.96)

✓ **Parallel pre-trends held** for the clean pre-period (weeks 6-12): No statistically significant divergence between groups before the switch

✓ **Robust to controls**: Effect persists when accounting for staffing levels, visit volumes, branch characteristics, and week-specific factors

### **4. Important Limitations**

⚠️ **Pre-trend divergence in weeks 13-14**: Treatment and control groups began diverging 1-2 weeks *before* the switch occurred. This violates the key assumption underlying our causal estimate and could mean the true effect is smaller than estimated.

⚠️ **Control group decline**: Branches keeping the old procedure saw scores drop after week 15. We cannot fully explain why. If this reflects system-wide factors that disproportionately affected control branches, our estimate may be inflated.

⚠️ **Unknown implementation costs**: This analysis measures service score impact only. The board must weigh this against training, staffing, and operational costs of rollout.

---

## **RECOMMENDATIONS**

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| **1** | **Proceed with phased rollout** to remaining branches | Evidence supports a positive effect; delaying denies branches potential improvement |
| **2** | **Implement rigorous monitoring** during rollout | Track weekly service scores for each branch for 8 weeks post-switch; establish early warning thresholds |
| **3** | **Investigate control branch decline** | Understanding why old-procedure branches deteriorated could reveal additional improvements |
| **4** | **Set realistic expectations** | Communicate that branches should expect ~1.5–2.5 point improvement, not guaranteed |
| **5** | **Re-evaluate after 50% rollout** | Pause and reassess whether observed effects match projections before completing rollout |

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Recommendation** | **PROCEED WITH ROLLOUT** |
| **Confidence** | **Moderate-High** |
| **Implementation Approach** | **Phased, with monitoring** |

**Rationale for this verdict:**

1. **The lower bound of uncertainty is still meaningful.** Even if the true effect is at the bottom of our 95% confidence interval (1.8 points), this represents a 3.6% improvement—likely worth the implementation cost.

2. **No evidence of systematic bias.** Treatment and control branches were comparable before the switch. This strengthens causal inference.

3. **The risk of inaction is real.** Control branches declined after week 15. If the old procedure is becoming less effective over time, delaying rollout has its own costs.

4. **Phased implementation mitigates risk.** By rolling out in stages with monitoring, the cooperative can verify effects in real-time and adjust course if needed.

**This verdict would change if:**
- Phased rollout shows effects below 1.0 point
- Implementation proves more disruptive or costly than anticipated
- Further analysis explains the control group decline in a way that undermines our estimate

---

## **CONCLUSION**

The statistical evidence supports a **cautious "yes."** The new procedure likely improves service scores by a meaningful amount, and there's no evidence of harmful selection or systematic pre-existing differences between branches. However, the pre-trend divergence in weeks 13-14 introduces uncertainty about the exact magnitude. A phased rollout with monitoring balances the opportunity for improvement against the risk of over-committing to an effect that may be smaller than estimated.
