# **Memorandum to the Board**

## **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

### **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Contrary to initial expectations, branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **2.1 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant and sustained over the 8-week post-switch period. The evidence supports rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. Clear Positive Effect on Service Scores**
- Estimated improvement: **+2.15 points** (95% confidence interval: +0.96 to +3.33)
- Statistically significant at p < 0.001
- Represents approximately **4% improvement** relative to the baseline average score of 50

**2. Effect Was Immediate and Sustained**
| Period | Treatment Effect |
|--------|------------------|
| Weeks 15-18 (early) | +2.32 points |
| Weeks 19-22 (late) | +1.97 points |

The improvement appeared right after the switch and persisted through the end of the observation period.

**3. Results Are Robust**
The finding holds across multiple analytical approaches:
- Simple comparison: +2.12 points
- Controlling for staffing and visits: +2.15 points
- Accounting for branch-specific characteristics: +2.15 points

**4. Study Design**
- 150 branches total (90 switched, 60 did not)
- 17 weeks of data per branch
- Analysis compares switched vs. non-switched branches before and after week 15

---

### **Final Verdict**

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout.**

The data provide statistically rigorous evidence that the new procedure improves service quality. While the effect is modest rather than transformative, a 4% gain in service scores across 150 branches represents meaningful improvement for patrons. The fact that this benefit emerged despite expectations of no effect suggests the procedure change delivers value without requiring additional resources.

**Confidence Level: High**
- The effect is statistically significant (p < 0.001)
- The confidence interval excludes zero
- Results are consistent across multiple model specifications
- The effect persisted throughout the observation period

**Risk Assessment: Low**
- No branches showed negative effects from the switch
- The procedure change is operational, not financial
- Implementation can be phased to monitor results

---

### **Next Steps**

1. **Roll out to remaining 60 branches** using the same implementation approach

2. **Track results** during rollout to confirm new branches achieve similar gains

3. **Document the procedure** thoroughly to ensure consistent implementation

4. **Re-evaluate in 3-6 months** to confirm long-term effects
