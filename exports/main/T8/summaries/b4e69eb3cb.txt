# **Board Summary: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Proceed with rollout based on operational merits.** The new procedure was designed to improve stock rotation efficiency, not service quality. Our analysis found a modest, statistically significant improvement in service scores (+1.9 points), but this was unexpected and should be considered a potential bonus rather than a primary justification. The service score finding alone does not strongly compel adoption, but it does suggest no negative consequences.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Modest Service Score Effect
- Treatment branches showed **1.9 points higher** service scores than expected (p = 0.002)
- However, this is a **small effect** (0.18 standard deviations) by conventional standards
- For context: the average service score is 50 with a standard deviation of 10.6 points

### 2. Important Context: Treatment Branches Were Already Ahead
- **Before the switch**, treatment branches already scored **1.7 points higher** than control branches
- The 1.9-point "effect" represents the gap *widening*, not treatment branches suddenly improving
- This suggests treatment branches may have had other advantages (management, location, etc.)

### 3. What Actually Happened After Week 15
- **Control branches**: Scores **declined 1.7 points** (possibly seasonal or external factors)
- **Treatment branches**: Scores **held steady** (+0.2 points)
- The "benefit" appears to be resilience against decline rather than active improvement

### 4. One Week Drove Much of the Result
- Week 15 (the first week after switch) showed an unusually large 5.6-point gap
- Subsequent weeks averaged a 2.8-point gap
- This raises questions about whether week 15 was an anomaly

### 5. No Negative Effects Detected
- Service scores did not decline for branches adopting the new procedure
- This is reassuring given the operational changes involved

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did the procedure improve service scores? | **Yes, modestly** (+1.9 points) |
| Is the effect large enough to matter? | **Borderline** (small effect size) |
| Could the result be due to other factors? | **Possibly** (pre-existing differences, week 15 anomaly) |
| Did the procedure cause any harm? | **No evidence of negative effects** |
| Should service scores drive the rollout decision? | **No** — decide on operational grounds |

**Recommendation:** Approve rollout if operational metrics (efficiency, cost, labor) support it. The service score data removes concern about negative side effects and suggests a modest upside, but this finding should not be the primary rationale. Frame the service score result internally as "no harm, possible benefit" rather than a proven improvement.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Base the rollout decision on operational analysis** — Stock rotation efficiency, labor hours, and cost metrics should drive the decision, not service scores

2. **If proceeding, frame service scores as a neutral-to-positive finding** — The procedure does not harm service quality and may offer modest protection against score declines

3. **Continue monitoring** — Track service scores during rollout to confirm the effect persists

4. **Investigate the week 15 spike** — Understand whether the large initial gap reflects a real transition effect or temporary anomaly

5. **Consider branch characteristics** — Since treatment branches were already outperforming, examine whether the procedure works better in certain types of branches

---

*Analysis based on branches.csv (150 branches, weeks 6-22, 2,550 observations). Difference-in-differences estimation with cluster-robust standard errors.*
