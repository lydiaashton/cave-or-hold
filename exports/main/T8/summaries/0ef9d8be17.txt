# **Memorandum to the Board**

## **Subject: Impact of New Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure on Service Scores**

---

### **Bottom Line**

The evidence suggests the new procedure **may have improved** service scores, but the finding is less certain than a standard analysis would indicate. The estimated effect ranges from **1 to 3 points** depending on how we account for pre-existing trends. We recommend a **cautious, phased rollout** with continued monitoring rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.

---

### **Key Findings**

**What We Found**
- Standard analysis: **+2.0 points** improvement (p=0.003)
- Using only the most comparable pre-period weeks: **+2.7 points** (p=0.001)
- 95% confidence interval: **0.7 to 3.4 points**

**Important Caveat**
The branches that switched had higher scores at the start of the study period (~2 points higher in weeks 6-9). This gap narrowed substantially in the weeks immediately before the switch, then widened again afterward. While our statistical controls account for this, it introduces uncertainty about how much of the observed improvement is due to the new procedure versus natural variation.

**What We Can Say with Confidence**
- Service scores at switched branches did improve relative to non-switched branches
- The effect is statistically significant under multiple analytical approaches
- There is no evidence of harm from the new procedure

**What Remains Uncertain**
- The precise magnitude of the effect (could be as low as 1 point or as high as 3 points)
- Whether the effect will persist long-term
- Whether the same effect would occur at branches that were not early adopters

---

### **Recommendation**

**Proceed with a phased rollout** rather than immediate system-wide implementation:

1. **Expand to 20-30 additional branches** over the next 4-6 weeks
2. **Monitor scores closely** to confirm the effect replicates
3. **Proceed to full rollout** if the effect is confirmed in the expansion phase

This approach balances the opportunity for improvement against the uncertainty in the evidence.

---

### **Next Steps**

1. Select 20-30 branches for Phase 2 implementation (mix of high and low performers)
2. Establish weekly score tracking for Phase 2 branches
3. Reconvene in 6 weeks to review Phase 2 results before deciding on full rollout

---

### **Final Verdict**

**The new procedure likely provides a modest benefit, but the evidence is not definitive.**

Given that the board expected no effect, the observed improvement—even with uncertainty—represents a favorable surprise. The statistical evidence leans positive (significant under multiple specifications), there is no risk of harm, and the procedure is already operational at 90 branches.

**Verdict: Proceed with rollout, but verify through phased implementation.**

The cost of a cautious, monitored expansion is low. The cost of forgoing a genuine improvement across 150 branches is higher. We recommend moving forward while building in checkpoints to confirm the effect holds at scale.
