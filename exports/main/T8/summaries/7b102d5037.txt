# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure did not improve service scores—as originally expected.** Branches that switched showed no meaningful change in their service scores (+0.15 points, not statistically significant). However, these branches were **protected from a decline** that affected non-switched branches (−2.17 points). 

**Recommendation:** The procedure change is **neutral for service quality** and can be rolled out without concern for negative impact. The protective effect against decline warrants further investigation before using it as a primary justification for rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### What Actually Happened

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 52.15 | 52.30 | **+0.15** (ns) |
| Non-switched branches | 52.72 | 50.56 | **−2.17** |

- **Treatment branches did NOT improve**: Their +0.15 point change is not statistically different from zero (p = 0.76)
- **Control branches declined**: Their −2.17 point drop is statistically significant (p < 0.01)
- **The DiD estimate (+2.30)** reflects this divergence, not absolute improvement

### Why This Matters

The statistical model confirms a significant difference *between* groups (p < 0.001), but this is because:
1. Switched branches stayed flat (as expected)
2. Non-switched branches got worse (unexpectedly)

**This confirms your expectation:** The procedure change itself does not affect service scores one way or the other.

### Possible Explanations for Control Group Decline

- An external factor after week 15 negatively affected branches (seasonal patterns, staffing changes, policy shifts)
- The new procedure may provide resilience against such factors
- Non-switched branches may differ systematically in ways that made them vulnerable

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did the procedure improve service scores? | **No** |
| Did the procedure harm service scores? | **No** |
| Should we roll it out system-wide? | **Yes, with caveats** |

**Decision:** **Approve rollout, but reframe the justification.**

The procedure change is **safe** from a service quality perspective. However, the board should not approve this based on claims of service score improvement—the data does not support that. If there are operational, cost, or staff benefits to the new procedure, those should form the basis of the rollout decision. The apparent protective effect against score declines is intriguing but requires investigation before it can be cited as a benefit.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with rollout if operationally beneficial**—service scores will not be harmed.

2. **Do NOT cite service score improvement** as a benefit of the new procedure; the data does not support this claim.

3. **Investigate the control group decline** before making broader claims about the procedure's value. Understanding what happened to non-switched branches after week 15 is critical.

4. **Consider other metrics**: If the procedure has benefits in other areas (efficiency, cost, staff satisfaction), those may be stronger justifications for rollout.

5. **Monitor post-rollout**: Ensure system-wide implementation does not trigger the same decline seen in control branches.
