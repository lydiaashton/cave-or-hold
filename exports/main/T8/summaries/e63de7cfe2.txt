## **Final Verdict**

### **DO NOT PROCEED WITH SYSTEM-WIDE ROLLOUT AT THIS TIME**

**Reasoning:**

1. **The evidence does not support a causal claim.** The branches that switched to the new procedure were already performing differently from control branches before the switch. This selection bias means we cannot confidently attribute the observed improvement to the procedure itself.

2. **When we control for this bias** by examining only the weeks immediately surrounding the switch, the effect drops from +1.89 points to +0.60 points and becomes statistically indistinguishable from zero (p=0.10).

3. **The cost of a wrong decision is real.** Rolling out an ineffective procedure system-wide would waste resources on training, implementation, and potential disruption—without delivering the expected service improvement.
