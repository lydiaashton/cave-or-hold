# **Evaluation of New Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

**To:** Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Subject:** Findings on New Branch Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure does improve service scores, but the effect is modest and may be fading.** We recommend **proceeding with caution**—continue the rollout but monitor closely, and be prepared to adjust if the effect diminishes further.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **The Good News**
| Metric | Result |
|--------|--------|
| Average improvement | **+2.2 points** (on a ~50 point scale) |
| Statistical significance | **p = 0.0003** (highly reliable) |
| Confidence interval | 1.0 to 3.4 points |

The effect is **real**—not a statistical fluke. Branches using the new procedure consistently outperformed comparable branches using the old procedure.

### **The Caveats**

**1. The effect is small in practical terms**
- 2.2 points represents about **4% improvement** or **0.24 standard deviations**
- By conventional standards, this is a **small effect size**
- Whether this justifies rollout costs depends on implementation expenses we haven't measured

**2. The effect appears to be fading over time**

| Period | Average Effect |
|--------|----------------|
| Weeks 15–19 (first 5 weeks) | **+2.76 points** |
| Weeks 20–22 (last 3 weeks) | **+1.26 points** |

The improvement in recent weeks is **less than half** of what we saw initially. This could reflect:
- Initial enthusiasm that wore off
- Branches reverting to old habits
- A temporary adjustment effect rather than lasting improvement

**3. We only have 8 weeks of post-switch data**
- This is too short to know if the effect will stabilize, continue fading, or disappear entirely

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **Does the new procedure work?** | **Yes** — the effect is statistically significant and positive |
| **Is it transformative?** | **No** — the effect is modest (~4% improvement) |
| **Will it last?** | **Uncertain** — early signs of fading warrant monitoring |
| **Should we roll it out?** | **Yes, with conditions** — proceed but track closely |

**Decision:** **Approve conditional rollout.** The procedure delivers a real but modest benefit. This justifies system-wide adoption, provided we:
1. Monitor for further fading
2. Measure implementation costs
3. Revisit the decision if the effect drops below 1 point

---

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with rollout, but with conditions:**

1. **Continue the rollout** to remaining branches—the evidence shows a real positive effect, even if modest.

2. **Set up active monitoring**: Track service scores monthly for all converted branches. If the average effect drops below 1.0 point, pause and investigate.

3. **Document implementation**: Capture what high-performing branches are doing differently. The variation in effects suggests execution matters.

4. **Revisit in 90 days**: Return to the board with updated data showing whether the effect has stabilized or continued to fade.

5. **Measure costs**: We haven't analyzed whether the new procedure requires additional staff time or resources. A small benefit may not justify significant costs.

---

## **Why We're More Cautious Than Initially Expected**

Your team expected no difference. The data shows there **is** a difference—but it's smaller than a transformative change and shows signs of weakening over time. This warrants a measured approach rather than enthusiastic adoption.

The new procedure is **worth implementing**, but it's not a game-changer. Manage expectations accordingly.
