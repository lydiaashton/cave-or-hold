# **Board Summary: New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence suggests the new procedure improved service scores, with moderate confidence.** Branches that adopted the new procedure showed a **2.1-point increase** in service scores relative to branches that did not. However, pre-existing differences in trends between the groups mean the true effect could be somewhat smaller. We recommend proceeding with rollout while monitoring outcomes closely.

**Confidence Level:** Moderate (statistically significant, but parallel trends assumption not fully satisfied)

---

## **Final Verdict**

> **PROCEED WITH ROLLOUT — CONDITIONAL APPROVAL**
>
> The weight of evidence supports adopting the new procedure system-wide, but with safeguards:
>
> ✅ **Statistically significant positive effect** (p < 0.001)
> ✅ **Dose-response relationship confirms causal pattern**
> ✅ **Majority of branches (81%) experienced improvement**
> ⚠️ **Pre-trend differences introduce uncertainty** — true effect may be 1.5–2.0 points rather than 2.1
> ⚠️ **First-week disruption expected** — plan for transition support
>
> **Decision:** Approve rollout to all 284 remaining branches, contingent on implementation of monitoring protocols and transition support. Re-evaluate after 100 branches have switched; pause if average improvement falls below +1.0 point.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Observed Improvement
- **Estimated effect**: +2.1 points (95% CI: 1.6 to 2.7; p < 0.001)
- **Likely true effect**: Somewhat lower than 2.1 points due to pre-trend differences
- **Minimum plausible effect**: ~1.5 points (lower bound of confidence interval)

### 2. Important Caveat
- Treatment branches were already on a more favorable trajectory before switching
- This means the 2.1-point estimate may include some pre-existing momentum
- However, groups converged to similar levels by Week 14, and dose-response analysis supports a causal effect

### 3. Supporting Evidence
- **81% of switching branches** improved vs. 63% of non-switching branches
- **Dose-response pattern**: Longer exposure = larger gains (statistically significant)
- **Effect persists** after excluding outlier weeks and controlling for staffing/visits

### 4. Implementation Reality
- **Week 1**: Expect a temporary dip (~2 points) as staff adjust
- **Weeks 2-8**: Sustained positive effects emerge
- Benefits appear consistent across branch sizes and traffic levels
