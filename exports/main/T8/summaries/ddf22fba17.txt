# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

## **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

### **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores—contrary to initial expectations.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **1.9 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant and consistent across most switching branches. We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. Clear Positive Effect on Service Scores**
- Treatment branches (90 branches that switched) improved by an average of **3.4 points** from before to after the switch
- Control branches (60 branches that did not switch) improved by only **1.5 points** over the same period
- The **net effect attributable to the new procedure is +1.9 points** on the service score index

**2. Statistically Robust Results**
- The effect is highly statistically significant (p < 0.01)
- 95% confidence interval: **+0.8 to +3.1 points**
- Results hold across multiple analytical specifications, including controls for staffing levels and visit volumes

**3. Consistent Across Branches**
- **83% of treatment branches** saw their service scores improve after switching
- Only 68% of control branches improved during the same period
- 72% of switching branches outperformed the average control branch

**4. Valid Comparison Confirmed**
- Treatment and control branches had nearly identical service scores before the switch (48.0 vs. 47.3; difference not statistically significant)
- Both groups showed parallel trends in the 9 weeks before the intervention
- The improvement appeared immediately after week 15 and persisted throughout the 8-week post-period

**5. Meaningful Impact**
- The average branch service score is approximately 50
- A 1.9-point improvement represents roughly a **4% increase** in service quality

---

### **Why This Matters**

The library cooperative implemented this procedural change primarily for operational reasons, with no expectation that it would affect patron-facing service quality. The positive effect on service scores is therefore an **unexpected co-benefit**. This suggests that the new rotation and shelving procedure may:

- Improve staff efficiency, allowing more time for patron assistance
- Make materials easier to locate, reducing patron wait times
- Signal organizational attentiveness that patrons perceive positively

---

### **Analysis Approach**

This evaluation used a **difference-in-differences** design comparing:
- **Treatment group:** 90 branches that adopted the new procedure at week 15
- **Control group:** 60 branches that continued with the old procedure

The analysis controlled for branch-specific characteristics, week-to-week variations, staffing levels, and visit volumes. The parallel trends assumption was verified—treatment and control branches followed similar trajectories before the switch.

---

### **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did the new procedure change service scores? | **Yes** |
| Was the change positive or negative? | **Positive** |
| Is the result statistically reliable? | **Yes (p < 0.01)** |
| Should the cooperative roll out the new procedure to all branches? | **Yes** |

**Decision:** Proceed with system-wide implementation. The evidence demonstrates a real, positive effect on service quality that was not anticipated. This represents a low-risk, high-reward opportunity to improve patron experience across all 150 branches.

---

### **Next Steps**

1. **Approve full rollout** to remaining 60 branches
2. **Document implementation practices** from high-performing treatment branches
3. **Monitor service scores** during expansion to verify effects replicate at scale
4. **Investigate the mechanism** behind the improvement (staff surveys, patron feedback)
