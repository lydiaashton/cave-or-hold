# **Board Summary: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure helped branches maintain service scores during a period of system-wide decline.** Switched branches held relatively steady while control branches dropped approximately 2.4 points. The evidence supports proceeding with the rollout, though continued monitoring is warranted.

---

## **Final Verdict**

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout.**

The new procedure demonstrates a **statistically significant protective effect** (p = 0.003) on service scores. While switched branches did not improve in absolute terms, they avoided the notable decline experienced by control branches. This represents a meaningful operational benefit.

**Confidence Level: Moderate to High**
- ✓ Rigorous methodology (difference-in-differences with fixed effects)
- ✓ Parallel pre-trends validated
- ✓ Effect persists with controls for staffing and visitor volume
- ⚠ Only 8 weeks of post-switch data available
- ⚠ System-wide decline after week 15 requires investigation

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Effect Size and Direction

Using a rigorous difference-in-differences analysis, we estimate the new procedure **increased service scores by 1.98 points relative to the counterfactual** (p = 0.003).

| Metric | Switched Branches | Control Branches |
|--------|-------------------|------------------|
| Average score BEFORE switch (weeks 6-14) | 53.31 | 52.28 |
| Average score AFTER switch (weeks 15-22) | 52.92 | 49.91 |
| **Change** | **-0.39** | **-2.37** |

**Critical observation:** Neither group improved. The new procedure's benefit was **protective**—it shielded branches from a broader decline that affected the control group.

### 2. The Analysis Is Methodologically Sound

- **Parallel pre-trends confirmed:** Before the switch, both groups followed statistically similar trends (interaction p = 0.53), validating our comparison approach.
- **Results hold with controls:** The effect persists after accounting for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume.
- **Fixed effects model confirms:** When controlling for branch-specific and week-specific factors, the effect remains significant at 1.98 points (p = 0.003).

### 3. Practical Significance

Given that the average branch scores about 50 on the service index, a **2-point relative improvement represents approximately a 4% gain**. More importantly, avoiding a 2.4-point decline may prevent downstream effects on patron satisfaction and branch performance metrics.

### 4. Unanswered Questions

- **Why did control branches decline after week 15?** This system-wide trend warrants investigation—it may reflect seasonal patterns, staffing changes, or other external factors.
- **Will the effect persist?** We have only 8 weeks of post-switch data. Long-term sustainability is unproven.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all remaining branches.

2. **Continue monitoring** service scores monthly during and after rollout to confirm the effect persists at scale.

3. **Document the new procedure** in detail, including training materials and implementation timelines that supported switched branches.

4. **Investigate the system-wide decline** observed after week 15—understanding this trend may reveal additional opportunities for improvement beyond the rotation procedure.

5. **Schedule a follow-up evaluation** in 6 months to assess long-term sustainability of the effect.

---

*Analysis based on 2,550 weekly observations from 150 branches over 17 weeks (weeks 6-22). 102 branches switched to the new procedure; 48 served as controls. The switch occurred at week 15. Statistical models include difference-in-differences regression and panel fixed effects with clustered standard errors.*
