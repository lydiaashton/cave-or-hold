# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**
### *Analysis for Board Decision on System-Wide Rollout*

---

## **Bottom Line**

Branches that adopted the new procedure showed **higher service scores (+2.7 points on average)** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. However, this result **does not reach conventional statistical significance** (p=0.10), meaning we cannot confirm with high confidence that the procedure change caused the improvement. The positive trend and growing effect over time are encouraging, but the evidence is **suggestive rather than conclusive.** The Board must weigh the potential benefit against rollout costs and implementation effort.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **What We Measured**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks**
- **20 branches** implemented the new procedure starting week 15; **14 branches** maintained the old procedure
- Service scores average ~50 points; higher scores indicate better service

### **Results**

| Group | Before | After | Change |
|-------|--------|-------|--------|
| Branches keeping old procedure | 44.9 | 44.9 | — |
| Branches adopting new procedure | 46.9 | 49.4 | **+2.5** |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | | **+2.7 points** |

### **Statistical Confidence**

| Metric | Value | Interpretation |
|--------|-------|----------------|
| Point estimate | +2.65 points | Average improvement |
| p-value | 0.10 | Above 0.05 threshold; not statistically significant |
| 95% Confidence Interval | -0.4 to +5.7 | Includes zero; cannot rule out no effect |

### **Encouraging Signals**

1. **Effect grows over time:** +3.9 points (weeks 15–18) → +5.1 points (weeks 19–22)
2. **Parallel trends confirmed:** Both groups tracked similarly before the switch
3. **No negative effects observed:** Service scores did not decline in any subgroup

### **Limitations**

- Only 8 weeks of post-switch data
- 34 branches limits statistical power
- Observational design (not randomized)

---

## **Final Verdict**

**Proceed with rollout, but phase implementation and continue monitoring.**

**Rationale:**
- The point estimate is positive and meaningful (~6% improvement)
- No evidence of harm or service degradation
- Effect strengthens over time, suggesting genuine benefit as staff gain proficiency
- Statistical uncertainty reflects limited data, not evidence of no effect
- Service improvement aligns with core library mission

**Conditions:**
- Roll out in phases to allow for course correction
- Track service scores monthly for 6 months post-rollout
- Be prepared to adjust or pause if results diverge from pilot findings
