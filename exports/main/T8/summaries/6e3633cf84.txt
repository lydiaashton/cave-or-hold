# **Analysis of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The data does not provide reliable evidence that the new procedure improved service scores.** While a simple before-after comparison suggests a 2-point gain, this result is not trustworthy because the treatment and control branches showed different weekly patterns even before the switch occurred. Our original expectation—that this change would not affect service scores—appears correct.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. **Statistical Limitations**
- The standard analysis shows a 2.04-point increase (p=0.002), **but this relies on an assumption that the data violates**
- Treatment and control branches had significantly different weekly patterns *before* the switch (p<0.0001)
- This means we cannot confidently attribute any difference to the new procedure

### 2. **Volatile Weekly Patterns**
- Post-switch weeks show inconsistent effects: some weeks improved (+3.6 points), others declined (-2.9 points)
- The average post-period effect is only 0.56 points—much smaller than the headline estimate
- This volatility suggests random variation rather than a systematic improvement

### 3. **Baseline Differences**
- Before the switch, treatment branches already scored 0.94 points lower than control branches
- Treatment branches may have been systematically different (e.g., larger branches, different neighborhoods, different staffing patterns)

### 4. **What We Can Confidently Say**
- The new procedure did not *harm* service scores
- There is no clear evidence of meaningful improvement
- The procedure appears neutral with respect to service scores

---

## **Final Verdict**

**The new stock rotation procedure has no demonstrable effect on service scores.**

The analysis confirms the board's original expectation. The apparent 2-point improvement in simpler analyses disappears when we account for pre-existing differences between branches. The event study reveals that treatment and control branches followed different trajectories throughout the study period—not just after the switch—making it impossible to isolate the procedure's true effect.

**This is not a failure.** A neutral effect on service scores means the cooperative can adopt the new procedure for other operational reasons (staff efficiency, inventory management, cost reduction) without concern about harming patron experience.

---

## **Recommendations**

| Action | Rationale |
|--------|-----------|
| **Do not cite service score improvements as justification for rollout** | The evidence does not support this claim |
| **Evaluate the procedure on operational grounds** | Staff time, inventory accuracy, and cost may still favor adoption |
| **If proceeding, continue monitoring** | Track service scores during rollout as a safeguard |
| **Document this evaluation** | Demonstrates evidence-based decision-making to stakeholders |

---

## **Conclusion**

The board's original expectation was correct: the new stock rotation procedure does not meaningfully change service scores. This finding should neither block nor drive the rollout decision. Instead, the board should base its decision on operational considerations while maintaining confidence that patron service will not be affected.
