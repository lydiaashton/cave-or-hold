# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure produces a real, measurable improvement in service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure showed a statistically significant gain of approximately **2.1 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. However, the effect is modest in magnitude and varies by branch type—high-visit branches benefit roughly three times more than low-visit branches.

---

## **Final Verdict**

**Proceed with rollout, but with measured expectations and targeted implementation.**

| Consideration | Assessment |
|--------------|------------|
| **Statistical evidence** | ✅ Strong (p < 0.01) |
| **Effect magnitude** | ⚠️ Modest (+2.1 points, ~4% improvement) |
| **Consistency across branches** | ⚠️ Variable (high-visit branches benefit 3× more) |
| **Durability** | ⚠️ Unknown (only 8 weeks of post-switch data) |
| **Cost-benefit** | ❓ Unknown (implementation costs not analyzed) |

**Recommendation:** Approve system-wide rollout with the following conditions:
1. Set realistic expectations (~2-point average improvement, not transformative)
2. Prioritize high-visit branches where impact is greatest
3. Budget for implementation costs and training
4. Continue monitoring to confirm effects persist beyond initial rollout

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **150 branches** participated in the evaluation (90 switched to new procedure, 60 kept old procedure as control)
- **17 weeks** of data collected (Weeks 6-22)
- New procedure implemented at **Week 15**
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in the treatment group to changes in the control group

### **Service Score Impact**

| Group | Pre-Switch Average | Post-Switch Average | Change |
|-------|-------------------|---------------------|--------|
| Control (Old Procedure) | 48.92 | 48.59 | -0.34 |
| Treatment (New Procedure) | 48.64 | 50.40 | +1.76 |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | | | **+2.10** |

### **Statistical Confidence**
- **Effect size:** +2.10 points on service score index
- **95% Confidence Interval:** +0.84 to +3.36 points
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.01 (highly significant)
- **Relative improvement:** ~4% increase from baseline (~50 points)
- **Percentile gain:** Treatment branches moved from ~47th to ~55th percentile

### **Effect Heterogeneity**

| Branch Type | Estimated Effect |
|-------------|------------------|
| High-visit branches | +3.10 points |
| Low-visit branches | +1.06 points |

The interaction is statistically significant (p = 0.009), indicating the new procedure works substantially better in busier branches.

### **Validation Checks**
1. **Pre-trend analysis:** Before Week 15, treatment and control branches followed parallel trends with no significant differences (p = 0.76). This supports the causal interpretation that the procedure change—not pre-existing branch characteristics—drove the improvement.

2. **Covariate controls:** The effect remains significant (+1.99 points, p < 0.01) after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume.

3. **Effect stability:** No evidence of decay over the 8-week post-switch period; effects remained consistent (p = 0.92 for trend).

4. **Event study pattern:** The treatment-control gap was near zero before Week 15, then widened consistently after implementation.

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** to all 60 branches currently using the old procedure.

2. **Prioritize implementation** at high-visit branches first, where the expected benefit is largest (+3 points vs. +1 point).

3. **Develop implementation plan** with consideration for:
   - Staff training requirements
   - Phased rollout to manage resource constraints
   - Communication plan for branch managers

4. **Continue monitoring** service scores during and after rollout to:
   - Confirm the effect persists beyond 8 weeks
   - Identify any branches that may need additional support
   - Detect any unintended consequences

5. **Conduct cost-benefit analysis** to quantify the operational costs of the new procedure against the service quality improvements documented here.

6. **Document best practices** from the 90 early-adopter branches to support training and implementation at remaining branches.

## **Analytical Notes**

- All statistical models use cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch) to account for repeated observations within branches
- The difference-in-differences estimator isolates the causal effect of the procedure change by netting out common time trends affecting all branches
- Service score index has a system average of approximately 50; higher scores indicate better service
- Effect heterogeneity analysis suggests branch characteristics (particularly visitor volume) moderate the treatment effect
