# **Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure Evaluation**

---

## **Bottom Line & Final Verdict**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure **produced a statistically significant improvement** in service scores of approximately **1.8 points** (on a scale where the average branch scores about 50). However, the effect varied considerably week-to-week, and we lack cost data to assess value.

**VERDICT: Proceed conditionally.** If the new procedure is low-cost or cost-neutral, the modest but real improvement justifies rollout. If significant resources are required, the board should weigh whether a ~3.5% service improvement warrants the investment.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect
- Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by **1.8 points** relative to branches that kept the old procedure
- This effect is statistically significant (p = 0.003)
- The 95% confidence interval ranges from **0.6 to 3.0 points**

### 2. Effect Size
- The improvement represents about **18% of a standard deviation** in service scores
- Given that the average branch scores about 50, this is roughly a **3.5% improvement** in service quality

### 3. Week-to-Week Variability
- The effect was **not consistent** across the 8 post-switch weeks:
  - Strongest weeks: 16, 20, 21 (+3 to +4 points)
  - Weakest week: 17 (−2.1 points, statistically significant decline)
  - Some weeks showed no discernible effect
- This variability suggests the improvement may depend on other factors (staffing, implementation fidelity, seasonal effects)

### 4. Parallel Trends Validation
- Before the switch (weeks 6–14), both groups showed nearly identical trends in service scores
- This validates our analysis approach and increases confidence that the observed improvement is attributable to the new procedure

### 5. Not Driven by Other Factors
- The improvement is **not explained by changes in staffing or visit volumes**—both remained stable across groups
- This suggests the effect is genuinely due to the procedural change itself

**Method:** Difference-in-differences regression with branch fixed effects and clustered standard errors

## **Next Steps**

1. **Obtain cost estimates** for system-wide rollout (training, materials, staff time)

2. **Make rollout decision** based on cost-benefit:
   - **Low/neutral cost** → Proceed with rollout
   - **Significant cost** → Consider whether 3-4% improvement justifies investment

3. **If proceeding, monitor closely:**
   - Track service scores weekly during rollout
   - Document implementation challenges by branch
   - Be prepared to provide additional support where effects are weak

4. **Plan follow-up evaluation** after 6–12 months to:
   - Confirm effects persist long-term
   - Assess whether variability decreases as staff gain experience
   - Calculate return on investment

---

## **Caveats**

- Only 8 weeks of post-implementation data; longer-term effects unknown
- Significant week-to-week variability suggests the effect may depend on contextual factors
- Service scores are an index measure; patron experience impact is not directly measured
- No cost data available for this analysis
