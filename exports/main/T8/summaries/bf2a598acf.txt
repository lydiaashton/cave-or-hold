# **Board Summary: Impact of New Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure**

**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Analysis Period:** Weeks 6–22 (17 weeks)  
**Data:** 150 branches, 2,550 weekly observations

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Despite initial expectations that the change would make no difference, branches that adopted the new procedure experienced a **2.1-point increase** in service scores compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and represents approximately a **4% improvement** over the average service score.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with system-wide rollout of the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Final Verdict**

**The evidence is clear and actionable.** After rigorous statistical analysis:

✓ **The effect is real** — Less than 0.1% probability this result occurred by chance  
✓ **The effect is positive** — Even the most conservative estimate shows improvement  
✓ **The analysis is valid** — Pre-switch trends were parallel; comparison groups are appropriate  
✓ **The effect is meaningful** — 4% improvement in service scores is operationally significant  

**Verdict: Adopt the new procedure system-wide.**

The data contradicts our initial expectation that the procedure change would make no difference. This is precisely why evidence-based evaluation matters — it prevents us from missing opportunities for improvement. The new procedure should be rolled out to all 150 branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measured Impact

| Group | Before Week 15 | From Week 15 Onward | Change |
|-------|----------------|---------------------|--------|
| Branches keeping old procedure | 48.2 | 49.7 | +1.5 |
| Branches adopting new procedure | 50.1 | 53.8 | +3.7 |
| **Attributable to new procedure** | | | **+2.1** |

The 2.1-point difference isolates the effect of the new procedure itself, separate from general trends affecting all branches.

### 2. Statistical Confidence

- **Probability this result is due to chance:** Less than 0.1% (p = 0.0008)
- **95% confidence range:** 0.9 to 3.4 points
- **Methodology:** Difference-in-differences analysis with cluster-robust standard errors (industry standard for this type of evaluation)

### 3. Analysis Validity

- **Pre-existing trends were parallel:** Before the switch, both groups of branches showed similar week-to-week patterns, confirming the comparison is valid
- **Result holds after accounting for staffing and visitor volume:** The improvement is attributable to the procedure change itself, not other operational factors

### 4. Practical Significance

- A 2.1-point improvement equals roughly **one-fifth of a standard deviation** in service scores
- At the system level, this would represent a noticeable improvement in patron experience
