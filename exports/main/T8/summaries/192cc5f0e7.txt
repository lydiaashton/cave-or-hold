# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

---

## **Subject: Impact of New Branch Shelving and Rotation Procedure on Service Scores**

**Date:** November 2025  
**Prepared by:** Data Analysis Team

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**We cannot confidently conclude the new procedure caused the improvement.** While branches using the new procedure showed higher service scores after the switch, the treatment and control branches were already on **different trajectories before the change**. This undermines our ability to attribute the difference to the procedure itself. **We recommend against a full rollout until additional evidence is gathered.**

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### What the Data Shows

| Observation | Detail |
|-------------|--------|
| **Raw difference** | Treatment branches scored ~2 points higher post-switch |
| **Statistical association** | p = 0.001 in simple models |
| **BUT—Critical issue** | Groups differed systematically *before* the switch |

### The Parallel Trends Problem

For a valid comparison, treatment and control branches should have followed similar trends before the intervention. **They did not:**

| Pre-Switch Week | Treatment vs. Control Difference |
|-----------------|----------------------------------|
| Week 7 | -2.26 points (significant) |
| Week 10 | -2.91 points (significant) |
| Week 11 | +5.37 points (significant) |
| Week 12 | +9.90 points (significant) |
| Week 13 | +1.23 points (significant) |

**5 of 8 pre-period differences are statistically significant** (we'd expect less than 1 by chance). A joint statistical test strongly rejects the assumption that the groups were comparable (F-test p < 0.0001).

### What This Means

- The apparent "treatment effect" could simply reflect **pre-existing differences** between branches that chose to switch vs. those that didn't
- Some pre-switch differences (+9.90 points in week 12) are **larger** than the average post-switch effect (+3.43 points)
- We cannot distinguish the procedure's impact from underlying branch characteristics

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **Did the new procedure improve service scores?** | **Cannot determine** — evidence is inconclusive |
| **Should we roll out to all branches now?** | **No** — methodological limitations preclude confident recommendation |
| **Is the new procedure harmful?** | **No evidence of harm** — scores did not decline |
| **Confidence in this assessment** | **High** — standard causal inference assumptions are violated |

### Bottom-Line Recommendation

**DO NOT APPROVE SYSTEM-WIDE ROLLOUT AT THIS TIME**

The data neither confirms nor refutes the value of the new procedure. Approving a full rollout based on this analysis would be acting on evidence that cannot support the causal claim being made.

---

## **RECOMMENDED NEXT STEPS**

### Immediate Actions

1. **Pause rollout decision** until stronger evidence is available

2. **Investigate branch selection** — Why did 90 branches switch and 60 not? What systematic differences exist?

3. **Extend data collection** — Continue monitoring for 3-6 more months

### For Future Evaluation

4. **Randomize remaining branches** — If rolling out further, randomly assign timing to enable cleaner causal inference

5. **Conduct qualitative review** — Interview staff to understand implementation fidelity and contextual factors

6. **Re-convene in Q2 2026** — Review extended data and improved analysis before making final decision

---

## **CONCLUSION**

The initial expectation—that the procedure change would make no difference—may yet prove correct. The observed score differences are **confounded by pre-existing branch differences** and cannot be confidently attributed to the new procedure. 

**This is not a finding that the new procedure doesn't work.** It is a finding that **this analysis cannot tell us whether it works.** The Board should pause the rollout decision pending stronger evidence.
