# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative
**FROM:** Data Analysis Office
**DATE:** November 15, 2026
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

Branches that adopted the new procedure showed **higher service scores** after implementation. However, because these branches were already improving faster than others *before* the switch, we **cannot confidently attribute the full improvement to the new procedure**. 

**Verdict: Proceed with a phased rollout, not systemwide expansion.** Gather more evidence before committing all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Observed Improvement**

| Metric | Result |
|--------|--------|
| **Estimated effect** | +1.8 points on service score index |
| **Confidence interval** | 1.3 to 2.4 points |
| **Statistical significance** | p < 0.001 |
| **Relative improvement** | ~3.6% above baseline |

### **2. Critical Limitation: Pre-Existing Trends**

The analysis method assumes treatment and control branches were on similar trajectories before the switch. **This assumption is violated:**

| Week | Gap (Treatment − Control) |
|------|---------------------------|
| Week 6 | −0.75 points |
| Week 10 | +0.22 points |
| Week 14 | +1.44 points |

Treatment branches gained **2.2 points relative to control** during the pre-switch period alone. This suggests underlying differences between branches that adopted the procedure and those that did not—unrelated to the procedure itself.

### **3. What This Means**

- The **1.8-point estimate likely overstates** the true causal effect
- The new procedure **may still be beneficial**, but the magnitude is uncertain
- Some branches that adopted the procedure may have been **more motivated, better staffed, or otherwise predisposed to improve**

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **Did scores improve at treatment branches?** | Yes |
| **Can we credit the new procedure?** | Partially—evidence is suggestive but not conclusive |
| **Should we roll out to all branches now?** | **No—not yet** |
| **Recommended path forward?** | **Phased expansion with evaluation** |

---

## **Recommendation**

### **Do Not Approve Immediate Systemwide Rollout**

Instead, adopt a **three-phase approach**:

| Phase | Action | Purpose |
|-------|--------|---------|
| **1. Controlled Expansion** | Roll out to 50–100 additional branches selected to resemble original treatment group | Test whether effect replicates |
| **2. Randomized Pilot** | Randomly assign 100+ branches to treatment vs. control | Generate causal evidence free of selection bias |
| **3. Decision Point** | Review results after 8–12 weeks; decide on full rollout | Base systemwide decision on stronger evidence |

---

## **Risk Mitigation**

If the Board chooses to proceed with broader rollout despite uncertainty:

1. **Monitor monthly:** Track service scores for all branches; flag any declines
2. **Set thresholds:** Define minimum acceptable scores; intervene if branches fall below
3. **Collect qualitative data:** Interview staff to understand what drives improvement
4. **Budget for reversal:** Be prepared to revert branches if results disappoint

---

## **Conclusion**

The new procedure is **associated with improved service scores**, and there is reason to believe it contributes positively. However, the pre-existing differences between adopting and non-adopting branches mean we **cannot confirm the procedure caused the full observed improvement**. 

A **cautious, evidence-building approach** protects the cooperative from investing in a procedure that may not deliver expected benefits systemwide. We recommend phased expansion with rigorous evaluation before committing to full rollout.
