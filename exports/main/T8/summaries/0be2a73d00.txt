# **Board Memo: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence suggests the new procedure improved service scores, but with important caveats.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.4 points (5%)** relative to control branches. The effect was consistent across all 8 post-implementation weeks. However, this was not a randomized trial, and we have only short-term data. **We recommend a phased rollout with continued evaluation** rather than immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Criterion | Assessment |
|-----------|------------|
| **Statistical Evidence** | ✅ Strong (p < 0.001) |
| **Effect Consistency** | ✅ All 8 post-weeks positive |
| **Study Design** | ⚠️ Quasi-experimental (not randomized) |
| **Follow-up Duration** | ⚠️ Short-term only (8 weeks) |
| **Cost Data** | ❌ Not available |
| **Overall Confidence** | **Moderate-High** |

**Verdict: Proceed with caution.** The data supports moving forward, but a phased approach with continued monitoring is warranted given the non-randomized design and limited follow-up period.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measured Effect
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Average score increase | +2.4 points |
| 95% Confidence Interval | 1.1 to 3.7 points |
| Percentage improvement | ~5% |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 |

### 2. Effect Pattern
- **All 8 post-treatment weeks** showed positive, statistically significant effects
- **No evidence of fading** — effects remained stable throughout the observation period
- **Pre-treatment trends** were generally parallel (joint test p = 0.35), though one early week showed some difference

### 3. Study Limitations
- **Not randomized**: Branches were not randomly assigned to treatment vs. control
- **Short follow-up**: Only 8 weeks of post-implementation data
- **Unknown costs**: We cannot assess cost-effectiveness without implementation cost data
- **Possible selection bias**: Branches that switched may differ systematically from those that didn't

---

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with a phased rollout** rather than immediate system-wide implementation:

1. **Expand to 20-30 control branches** in the next quarter
2. **Monitor for 12+ weeks** to confirm effects persist
3. **Track implementation costs** to assess cost-effectiveness
4. **Document implementation practices** to ensure consistency

This approach balances the positive evidence with appropriate caution given the study's limitations.
