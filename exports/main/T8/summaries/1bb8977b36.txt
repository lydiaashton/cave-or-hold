# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** [Current Date]  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure  

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence is inconclusive. We cannot confidently attribute score improvements to the new procedure.**

While branches that switched show higher score gains than those that didn't, **the analysis has a fundamental limitation**: the switching and non-switching branches were already on different trajectories *before* the change. This makes it impossible to isolate the procedure's true effect from other factors.

---

## **Final Verdict**

> **DO NOT APPROVE system-wide rollout at this time.**
>
> **Reason:** The data does not support a causal claim that the new procedure improves service scores. The observed improvement is confounded by pre-existing differences between branches.
>
> **Required before reconsideration:** Rigorous evidence from a randomized controlled trial or quasi-experimental design with valid parallel trends.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **What the Data Shows**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Raw DiD estimate** | +2.0 points |
| **Statistical significance** | p < 0.001 |
| **Branches studied** | 709 total (425 switched, 284 did not) |

### **The Critical Problem: Parallel Trends Violation**

Difference-in-differences analysis requires that treated and control branches follow similar trends *before* the intervention. **This assumption is violated in our data:**

| Pre-Switch Week | Difference (Treated − Control) | Statistical Significance |
|-----------------|-------------------------------|-------------------------|
| Week 6 | −2.76 points | p < 0.01 *** |
| Week 7 | −1.80 points | p < 0.01 *** |
| Week 8 | −1.45 points | p < 0.01 *** |
| Week 9 | −1.94 points | p < 0.01 *** |
| Week 10 | −1.95 points | p < 0.01 *** |
| Week 11 | −1.53 points | p < 0.01 *** |
| Week 12 | −1.19 points | p < 0.01 *** |
| Week 13 | −0.73 points | p < 0.10 * |

**Seven of eight pre-switch weeks show statistically significant differences.** This indicates that branches selected for the new procedure were systematically different from those that kept the old procedure.

### **Additional Concerns**

1. **Delayed effect**: The treatment effect only becomes statistically significant in weeks 20–22, not immediately after the week-15 switch. Weeks 15–19 show no significant difference.

2. **Non-random assignment**: Branches were not randomly assigned to switch. Higher-performing or better-resourced branches may have been selected (or volunteered) first.

3. **Alternative explanations**: The observed improvement could reflect:
   - Pre-existing momentum at switching branches
   - Unobserved factors (e.g., management quality, community engagement)
   - External events affecting branches differently

---

## **What We Can and Cannot Conclude**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did switching branches improve more than non-switching branches? | **Yes** — by about 2 points on average |
| Was this improvement *caused* by the new procedure? | **Cannot determine** — pre-existing differences confound the result |
| Should we roll out to all branches? | **No** — not based on this evidence |

---

## **Recommended Next Steps**

| Priority | Action | Timeline |
|----------|--------|----------|
| **1** | Pause system-wide rollout | Immediate |
| **2** | Design randomized controlled trial for remaining branches | 4–6 weeks |
| **3** | Gather qualitative feedback from switching branches | 4 weeks |
| **4** | Extend data collection period | Ongoing |
| **5** | Reconvene Board after rigorous evidence is available | 3–6 months |

### **If Board Chooses to Proceed Despite This Recommendation**

Should the Board decide to proceed with rollout notwithstanding the inconclusive evidence:

- Implement **phased rollout** with clear milestones
- Establish **success metrics** in advance (e.g., minimum +1.5 point improvement)
- Build in **evaluation checkpoints** at 4, 8, and 12 weeks post-implementation per branch
- Be prepared to **pause or reverse** if expected benefits do not materialize
- Document this decision and the analytical concerns for accountability

---

## **Conclusion**

The new procedure *may* improve service scores, but this analysis cannot confirm it. The branches that adopted the procedure were already different from those that did not, making causal inference unreliable. 

**Investing in proper evaluation now will prevent costly rollout of an unproven procedure.**
