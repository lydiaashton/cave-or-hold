### **Key Findings**

**1. Statistically Significant Improvement**
- **Treatment effect: +2.20 points** (95% CI: 0.89 to 3.51)
- **p-value: 0.001** (highly unlikely to be due to chance)
- Control branches declined by 2.0 points; treatment branches maintained their scores
- The analysis satisfies standard validity checks (parallel trends p=0.24)

**2. Effect Is Modest but Meaningful**
- Represents approximately **4.4% improvement** relative to baseline scores (~50)
- Roughly **22% of one standard deviation** in service scores
- Not transformative, but a clear positive signal

**3. Benefits Are Not Universal**
- **51% of treatment branches** saw their scores improve after switching
- **49% saw no improvement or decline** (though still outperformed control branches on average)
- This suggests the procedure works well in some contexts but may need adaptation for others

**4. Unexpected Finding**
- The procedure was designed for operational efficiency, not service quality
- We did not anticipate this downstream benefit
- This warrants both celebration and caution—we should verify the effect persists

### **Next Steps**

2. **Implement phased adoption** to allow for course correction if issues emerge at specific branches
