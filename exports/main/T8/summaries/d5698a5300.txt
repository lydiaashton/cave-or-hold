# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Office  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**We cannot confidently conclude the new procedure caused the improvement.** While branches that adopted the new procedure did show higher service scores after the switch, our analysis reveals that **these branches were already improving relative to others before the change occurred**. The observed gap likely reflects pre-existing differences rather than a causal effect of the procedure itself.

**Recommendation: Do not proceed with system-wide rollout based on this evidence alone.** Additional evaluation is needed.

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

> **NOT READY FOR ROLLOUT**
>
> The data does not support a causal claim that the new procedure improves service scores. Branches that switched were already outperforming control branches before the change, and there was no detectable jump in scores at the moment of switch. **Proceeding with a full rollout would be premature.** A randomized trial or additional evidence is required before committing cooperative-wide resources to this change.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. The Gap Existed Before the Switch**

| Week | Control Avg | Treatment Avg | Gap (T-C) |
|------|-------------|---------------|-----------|
| 6 | 49.28 | 49.02 | -0.26 |
| 9 | 51.86 | 53.04 | +1.18 |
| 12 | 42.90 | 44.77 | +1.87 |
| **14 (last pre-switch)** | **52.78** | **55.33** | **+2.55** |
| 15 (first post-switch) | 49.03 | 51.65 | +2.62 |
| 19 | 42.44 | 46.26 | +3.81 |
| 22 | 50.96 | 54.24 | +3.28 |

**Critical observation:** The treatment-control gap grew from -0.26 to +2.55 points *before* the switch even happened (weeks 6–14). This represents a pre-existing trend of approximately **0.32 points per week** (p < 0.001).

### **2. No Immediate Effect at Switch Point**

When we isolate the immediate change at week 15 (the switch point):
- **Estimated jump:** +0.08 points
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.89 (not significant)

If the procedure caused the improvement, we would expect to see a discontinuous jump at week 15. We do not.

### **3. Why the Simple Analysis Was Misleading**

The initial difference-in-differences estimate of +1.87 points assumed treatment and control branches would have followed parallel trends absent the intervention. **This assumption is violated.** Treatment branches were already on an upward trajectory relative to control branches before adopting the new procedure.

---

## **Possible Explanations**

1. **Selection bias:** Branches that chose to switch may have been more motivated, better managed, or already improving
2. **Confounding factors:** Something else affecting treatment branches (regional factors, management changes, etc.)
3. **The procedure may still help,** but this dataset cannot isolate its effect from pre-existing trends

---

## **Recommendation**

### **Do NOT proceed with system-wide rollout based on current evidence.**

Instead:

| Action | Rationale |
|--------|-----------|
| **Pause rollout** | Current evidence does not support causal claims |
| **Conduct a randomized trial** | Randomly assign remaining branches to treatment/control to ensure valid comparison |
| **Gather qualitative data** | Interview branch staff about perceived benefits/challenges |
| **Re-analyze after 8+ more weeks** | More post-switch data may clarify the pattern |

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Design a randomized rollout** for the remaining 284 branches (if operationally feasible)
2. **Collect branch-level feedback** on implementation experience
3. **Continue monitoring** service scores for early adopters
4. **Return to Board in Q1 2027** with stronger evidence

---

## **Technical Note**

The parallel trends assumption required for valid difference-in-differences estimation is violated in this dataset (pre-trend coefficient = 0.32, p < 0.001). The initial +1.87 point estimate likely captures pre-existing differential trends rather than a causal treatment effect.

---

*We apologize for the initial overconfident conclusion. Rigorous causal inference requires testing assumptions, and the parallel trends assumption does not hold here.*
