# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure: Impact on Service Scores**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure had a positive effect, but did not increase scores in absolute terms.** Branches that switched saw their service scores decline slightly (−0.5 points), but this was significantly less than the decline at branches keeping the old procedure (−2.5 points). The new procedure's net benefit was approximately **+2.0 points relative to what would have happened otherwise** (p = 0.002).

**Important context:** Something affected all branches negatively after week 14 (possibly seasonal factors). The new procedure appears to have **protected branches from the full impact** of this decline.

**Recommendation:** The evidence supports rolling out the new procedure, as it helps maintain service scores better than the old procedure. However, the board should also investigate what caused the system-wide decline after week 14.

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did the new procedure change service scores? | **Yes** — statistically significant effect (p = 0.002) |
| Did scores improve in absolute terms? | **No** — both groups declined after week 14 |
| Did the new procedure help relative to the alternative? | **Yes** — treatment branches declined 80% less than control |
| Should we roll out to all branches? | **Yes** — the procedure provides meaningful protection against score declines |
| How confident are we? | **High** — 95% confidence interval is entirely positive [0.77, 3.28] |

**Verdict: Proceed with rollout, but investigate the system-wide decline.**

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** — the new procedure demonstrably helps branches maintain higher scores than they would otherwise achieve.

2. **Investigate the system-wide decline** — all branches scored lower after week 14. This could be seasonal, operational, or external. Understanding this matters for setting realistic expectations.

3. **Set appropriate expectations** — communicate that the new procedure helps **protect against declines** rather than boost scores outright.

4. **Monitor during rollout** — track service scores for the first 8–12 weeks after each branch switches to verify similar protective effects.

5. **Review in 6 months** — assess whether the protective effect persists and whether the system-wide decline was temporary.
