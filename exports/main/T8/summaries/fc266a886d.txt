# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD OF DIRECTORS**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new shelving procedure significantly improved service scores and should be rolled out to all branches.**

Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by **2.0 points** (approximately 4%) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This result is statistically significant and contradicts the initial expectation that the change would make no difference.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measurable Improvement in Service Scores

| Group | Before (Weeks 6-14) | After (Weeks 15-22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------|---------------------|--------|
| Branches keeping old procedure | 51.0 | 51.3 | +0.2 |
| Branches with new procedure | 50.0 | 52.3 | +2.3 |

**Net effect of new procedure: +2.05 points** (95% confidence interval: 0.95 to 3.16)

### 2. Statistical Confidence

- **P-value: 0.0003** — The result is highly unlikely to occur by chance
- The effect remained significant after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume
- Pre-switch trends were similar between groups, validating the comparison

### 3. Consistent Performance

- The improvement was positive in **every week** after the switch
- Effects ranged from +0.6 to +1.7 points weekly, showing stable benefits

### 4. Analysis Scope

- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6-22)
- **102 branches** adopted the new procedure; **48 branches** served as controls
- **2,550 weekly observations** analyzed using difference-in-differences regression

---

## **Final Verdict**

**The analysis provides strong evidence that the new shelving procedure causes a real, positive improvement in service scores.**

| Criterion | Assessment |
|-----------|------------|
| Statistical significance | ✓ Passed (p = 0.0003) |
| Effect direction | ✓ Positive (higher scores are better) |
| Consistency over time | ✓ Positive in all 8 post-switch weeks |
| Robustness to other factors | ✓ Holds after controlling for staffing and visits |
| Parallel trends assumption | ✓ Verified (groups were comparable pre-switch) |

**Verdict: Proceed with system-wide rollout.**

The data answers the core question definitively: the new procedure *does* improve service scores. The initial expectation of "no difference" is not supported by the evidence. While the 4% improvement is modest rather than transformative, it represents meaningful progress on a key performance metric with no identified negative consequences.

---

## **Recommendation**

**Approve system-wide rollout of the new shelving procedure.**

## **Important Caveats**

1. **Cost-benefit analysis recommended** — This analysis measures service impact only; implementation costs should be weighed against the 4% improvement.

2. **Long-term effects unknown** — Only 8 weeks of post-switch data are available. Continued monitoring will confirm whether gains persist.

3. **Branch-level variation expected** — Some branches may see larger or smaller effects than the 2-point average.
