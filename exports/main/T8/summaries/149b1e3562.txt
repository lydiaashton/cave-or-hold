# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Analysis Period:** Weeks 6–22 (17 weeks of data)

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Contrary to the expectation that there would be no difference, branches that adopted the new procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **2.4 points** (approximately 5% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This result is statistically significant and robust.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

**✅ APPROVE SYSTEM-WIDE ROLLOUT**

The evidence is clear and actionable:

| Criterion | Finding | Verdict |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| **Effect detected?** | +2.38 points | ✓ Yes |
| **Statistically significant?** | p < 0.001 | ✓ Yes |
| **Parallel trends validated?** | p = 0.91 (pre-trends stable) | ✓ Yes |
| **Confidence interval excludes zero?** | [1.61, 3.15] | ✓ Yes |
| **Effect direction?** | Positive (improvement) | ✓ Favorable |

**This analysis provides strong evidence that the new procedure improves service quality.** The board's prior expectation of "no difference" is not supported by the data. There is no statistical basis to delay or limit the rollout.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### 1. Measurable Improvement in Service Quality
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated improvement | **+2.38 points** |
| 95% Confidence interval | 1.6 to 3.2 points |
| Improvement vs. baseline | **~5%** |
| Statistical significance | **Yes** (p < 0.001) |

### 2. Study Design Was Strong
- **150 branches** total: 90 adopted the new procedure, 60 kept the old
- **17 weeks** of data, with the switch occurring at week 15
- **2,550 observations** (one per branch per week)
- Analysis controlled for branch-specific characteristics and week-to-week variations

### 3. Groups Were Comparable Before the Switch
- Before week 15, both groups followed parallel trends
- The treatment group consistently scored about 2–3 points lower than the control group, but this gap remained stable over time
- After the switch, the treatment group closed this gap and slightly exceeded the control group's performance

### 4. Effect Reflects Genuine Service Improvement
- No significant changes were detected in staffing levels or visit volumes
- This suggests the improvement reflects genuine service quality gains rather than changes in resources or demand

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches

2. **Monitor implementation** to ensure consistent application across all branches

3. **Continue tracking service scores** quarterly to confirm the improvement is sustained

4. **Document lessons learned** — consider gathering staff feedback to understand *why* the new procedure works, which may reveal additional best practices

---

## **ABOUT THIS ANALYSIS**

This analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, which compares the change in outcomes for branches that adopted the new procedure against the change for branches that did not. This method isolates the effect of the procedure change from other factors that might affect all branches simultaneously (such as seasonal patterns or system-wide initiatives).
