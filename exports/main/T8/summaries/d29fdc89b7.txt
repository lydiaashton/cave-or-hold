# **Board Summary: New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence suggests the new procedure improved service scores, but we cannot precisely quantify the effect.** Branches that switched showed stronger score gains than non-switched branches, but those branches were already on a more positive trajectory before the switch. The likely improvement is **positive but smaller than the raw 2.1-point difference**. We recommend a **cautious, phased rollout with continued monitoring**.

---

## **Key Findings**

| Metric | Control Branches | Switched Branches |
|--------|-----------------|-------------------|
| Before switch | 48.9 | 50.7 |
| After switch | 49.3 | 53.3 |
| **Change** | **+0.4** | **+2.6** |

**Raw difference: +2.1 points** — but this likely overstates the true effect.

### Important Caveat
Switched branches were already improving faster than control branches before the intervention (p = 0.003). This pre-existing difference means we cannot confidently attribute the full 2.1-point gap to the new procedure alone.

### What Supports a Positive Effect
- Post-switch improvements are **larger and more sustained** than pre-switch trends
- The effect **grows over time** (weeks 19-22 show the strongest gains), consistent with staff gaining proficiency
- Results hold when controlling for staffing and visitor volume

### What Limits Our Confidence
- **Parallel trends assumption violated**: Treatment and control groups had different pre-intervention trajectories
- We cannot rule out that some unmeasured factor drove both the switch decision and the score improvements

---

## **Final Verdict**

**PROCEED WITH CAUTION**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did service scores improve in switched branches? | **Yes** — clearly |
| Can we attribute all of it to the new procedure? | **No** — pre-existing trends confound the result |
| Is there still evidence of a real benefit? | **Yes** — timing and growth pattern support causality |
| Should we roll out to all branches immediately? | **No** — phased approach recommended |
| Is the new procedure worth pursuing? | **Yes** — likely net positive with low downside risk |

**Confidence Level: Moderate** — The data supports a beneficial effect, but the magnitude is uncertain.

---

## **Recommendation**

**Approve a phased rollout with explicit success criteria:**

1. **Phase 1**: 25% of remaining branches (Months 1-2)
2. **Evaluation**: Require minimum +1.0 point improvement to proceed
3. **Phase 2**: 50% of remaining branches (Months 3-4)
4. **Full rollout**: Only if Phase 1 and 2 meet targets (Months 5-6)

This approach balances the likely benefit against the analytical uncertainty.
