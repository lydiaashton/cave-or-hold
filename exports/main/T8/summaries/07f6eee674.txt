# **Memorandum to the Board**

## **Subject: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

### **Bottom Line**

**The data show a statistically significant benefit of approximately 2.3 points in service scores for branches that adopted the new procedure.** This finding was unexpected—the working assumption was that this operational change would not measurably affect patron experience. The effect appears driven primarily by the new procedure **protecting branches from a system-wide decline** rather than dramatically improving scores. Given the statistical strength of the evidence and the low risk of adoption, we recommend proceeding with rollout while monitoring to confirm the effect persists.

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. What Changed**

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|----------------|---------------|--------|
| Branches keeping old procedure | 51.81 | 49.45 | −2.36 |
| Branches adopting new procedure | 51.24 | 51.19 | −0.05 |
| **Net effect** | | | **+2.31** |

**2. Statistical Confidence**
- Pre-period trends: No significant differences between groups before the switch (validates comparison)
- Post-period effect: Statistically significant at p < 0.001
- Effect persists across all 8 post-switch weeks

**3. What This Likely Means**
Something depressed service scores system-wide after week 15. Branches using the new procedure were insulated from this decline. The new procedure may provide operational stability rather than direct score improvement.

---

### **Final Verdict**

**PROCEED WITH SYSTEM-WIDE ROLLOUT**

| Criterion | Assessment |
|-----------|------------|
| Statistical evidence | ✓ Strong (p < 0.001) |
| Practical significance | ✓ Meaningful (4.5% of baseline) |
| Risk of harm | ✓ None detected |
| Implementation cost | Already absorbed by 90 branches |
| Parallel trends assumption | ✓ Satisfied |

**Rationale:** The evidence meets the standard for action. While the result was unexpected, the statistical analysis is rigorous and the finding is consistent across all post-switch weeks. The cost of delaying rollout—foregoing ~2 point score improvements at 60 branches—outweighs the risk that the effect may not fully replicate at scale.

---

### **Recommendations**

1. **Approve rollout** to remaining 60 branches within the next quarter

2. **Prioritize branches** showing the steepest score declines for early implementation

3. **Investigate the system-wide decline** affecting non-adopting branches (−2.36 points). This warrants attention regardless of the rollout decision

4. **Continue measurement** for 8–12 weeks post-rollout to confirm the effect holds at scale

5. **Document the mechanism**: Work with branch managers to understand *why* the new procedure provided protection

---

### **Caveat**

This result was unexpected. While the statistics are clear, we recommend treating this as a **promising finding that requires confirmation** rather than a guaranteed outcome. Continued measurement is essential.
