# **Memorandum to the Board of Directors**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure  

---

## **Final Verdict**

# ⚠️ DO NOT PROCEED WITH FULL ROLLOUT AT THIS TIME

**The evidence is insufficient to conclude the new procedure improves service scores.** While initial analysis suggested a 2-point gain, deeper examination reveals fundamental flaws in the comparison that prevent confident conclusions.

---

## **Bottom Line**

**We cannot recommend system-wide implementation based on current evidence.** The treatment and control branches differed systematically before the switch, and post-switch effects oscillate between gains and losses rather than showing consistent improvement. **More rigorous evaluation is needed before committing resources to a full rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Initial Results Look Promising—but Are Misleading

The basic difference-in-differences estimate shows a **2.04-point improvement** (p = 0.002). However, this masks important problems revealed by week-by-week analysis.

### 2. Critical Problem: Parallel Trends Assumption Violated

For the analysis to be valid, treatment and control branches should have followed similar trends before the switch. **They did not.**

| Pre-Switch Week | Difference (Treatment − Control) | Statistically Significant? |
|-----------------|----------------------------------|---------------------------|
| Week 6 | −2.41 points | Yes |
| Week 7 | +1.61 points | Yes |
| Week 8 | −8.15 points | Yes |
| Week 10 | +2.91 points | Yes |
| Week 11 | −4.97 points | Yes |

**Five of eight pre-switch weeks show significant differences** between groups. This means the treatment and control branches were already different in ways we cannot fully account for.

### 3. Post-Switch Effects Are Unstable

If the new procedure genuinely improved service, we'd expect consistently positive effects. Instead:

| Post-Switch Week | Effect | Significant? |
|------------------|--------|--------------|
| Week 15 | +3.56 | Yes |
| Week 16 | −0.19 | No |
| Week 17 | +3.35 | Yes |
| Week 18 | −1.90 | Yes |
| Week 19 | +3.00 | Yes |
| Week 20 | −2.89 | Yes |
| Week 21 | +1.87 | Marginal |
| Week 22 | −2.35 | Yes |

**Three weeks show significant gains; three weeks show significant losses.** This oscillating pattern is inconsistent with a genuine, stable treatment effect.

### 4. What Might Explain This?

- **Selection bias**: Branches that chose to switch may differ systematically from those that didn't (e.g., more motivated staff, different patron demographics)
- **External factors**: Something else may have affected branches differently over time
- **Measurement noise**: Service scores may be too volatile to detect a real effect with only 8 weeks of post-data

---

### ❌ Do NOT Proceed With Full Rollout

### ✅ Do Proceed With Further Investigation

## **Summary of Evidence**

| Question | Answer | Confidence |
|----------|--------|------------|
| Did service scores change after the switch? | Yes, but inconsistently | High |
| Can we attribute this to the new procedure? | **Not confidently** | Low |
| Is the effect stable over time? | **No** | High |
| Should we roll out to all branches now? | **No** | High |

---

## **What Would Change Our Verdict?**

We would recommend proceeding with rollout if future analysis shows:

1. **Stable positive effects** across multiple consecutive weeks
2. **Evidence of parallel pre-trends** in a better-matched control group
3. **Qualitative support** from staff indicating the procedure is working as intended
4. **Consistent effects** across different types of branches (urban/rural, large/small)
