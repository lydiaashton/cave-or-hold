# Library Branch Stock Rotation Procedure: Impact Analysis

## Bottom Line

**The new procedure produced a modest but statistically significant improvement in service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure gained approximately **1.9 points** over those that kept the old procedure (95% CI: 1.0 to 2.8 points). While this contradicts the expectation of no difference, the effect is relatively small (~4% of average score) and shows signs of **declining over time**. The board may wish to proceed with rollout, but should do so with realistic expectations and continued monitoring.

---

## Key Findings

### 1. Study Design
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6-22)
- **102 branches** adopted the new procedure; **48 branches** kept the old procedure
- Switch occurred in **week 15**
- Analysis uses **difference-in-differences** to isolate the procedure's effect

### 2. Main Result: Small Positive Effect

| Period | Control (Old) | Treatment (New) | Gap |
|--------|--------------|-----------------|-----|
| Before (weeks 6-14) | 47.27 | 48.02 | +0.76 |
| After (weeks 15-22) | 48.73 | 51.43 | +2.70 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+1.95** |

Both groups improved after week 15. The new procedure added roughly **1.9 points** beyond the general improvement.

### 3. Statistical Confidence
- **Statistically significant** (p < 0.001)
- 95% confidence interval: **1.0 to 2.8 points**
- However, effect size is **small** (0.18 standard deviations)

### 4. Important Concern: Effect May Be Fading

| Week | Treatment Advantage |
|------|---------------------|
| 15 | +3.35 |
| 16 | +3.32 |
| 17 | +3.26 |
| 18 | +2.59 |
| 19 | +1.97 |
| 20 | +3.08 |
| 21 | +1.93 |
| 22 | +2.12 |

The treatment advantage **declined 37%** from week 15 to week 22. If this trend continues, the long-term benefit could be substantially smaller than the initial estimate.

### 5. Validity Checks
- **Parallel trends confirmed**: Pre-switch patterns were similar between groups (p = 0.56)
- **Robust to controls**: Result holds accounting for staffing and visitor volume
- **Branch differences controlled**: Result persists with branch fixed effects

---

## Verdict

**Recommendation: Proceed with rollout, but cautiously.**

| Consideration | Assessment |
|--------------|------------|
| **Direction of effect** | ✅ Positive (contrary to expectation of no change) |
| **Statistical confidence** | ✅ High (p < 0.001) |
| **Practical significance** | ⚠️ Modest (~2 points, ~4% of average) |
| **Sustainability** | ⚠️ Uncertain (effect declining over 8 weeks) |
| **Risk of harm** | ✅ None detected (no negative effects observed) |

**Rationale:** The new procedure delivers a real, measurable benefit with no apparent downsides. However, the effect is smaller than a dramatic improvement and may not persist at current levels. A full rollout is justified, but the board should budget for ongoing evaluation and avoid overselling the expected gains to stakeholders.

---

## Next Steps

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** — The evidence supports expansion, but frame expectations realistically (~2 point gain, not a transformation).

2. **Monitor closely for effect decay** — The declining trend warrants attention. Track whether the benefit stabilizes or continues to fade.

3. **Assess implementation costs** — Weigh the ~2-point service score gain against the costs of training and transition.

4. **Investigate why it works** — Understanding the mechanism could help sustain or amplify the effect.

5. **Extend the evaluation period** — Eight weeks post-switch is short-term. Continue tracking for at least 6-12 more months to determine if effects persist, fade, or grow.

---

*Analysis based on 2,550 branch-week observations. Statistical models include branch fixed effects and robust standard errors.*
