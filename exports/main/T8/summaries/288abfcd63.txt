# **Library Branch Shelving Procedure: Impact Analysis**

**Prepared for the Board of Directors**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence is inconclusive.** While branches using the new procedure showed higher service scores after the switch, we cannot confidently attribute this to the procedure itself. Treatment branches were already on an improving trajectory before adopting the new procedure, which undermines our ability to isolate the procedure's effect. **We do not recommend a system-wide rollout based on this evidence alone.**

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did service scores improve at treatment branches? | **Yes** — scores increased after the switch |
| Did the new procedure *cause* the improvement? | **Cannot determine** — pre-existing trends confound the result |
| Should we roll out to all branches? | **No** — not without stronger evidence |
| What would change this verdict? | A randomized trial or additional data showing the effect persists and is not explained by selection |

**Confidence Level: LOW**

The core problem is that branches **chose** whether to adopt the new procedure; they were not randomly assigned. This self-selection means we cannot distinguish the procedure's effect from other factors (e.g., motivated management, recovery from prior struggles, or unobserved branch characteristics).

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Simple Comparison Shows Improvement, But...**

| Group | Before | After | Change |
|-------|--------|-------|--------|
| New Procedure | 49.72 | 50.39 | +0.67 |
| Old Procedure | 49.42 | 47.95 | -1.47 |
| **Simple Difference** | | | **+2.10** |

This 2.10-point difference **appears** significant, but it does not account for pre-existing differences between groups.

### **2. Critical Problem: Treatment Branches Were Already Improving**

An event study analysis reveals that branches choosing the new procedure were **already converging upward** relative to control branches in the weeks before the switch:

- Pre-period differences are **jointly significant** (p=0.001)
- Treatment branches scored 1-2 points below their own baseline in early weeks, then naturally recovered
- This pattern suggests **selection bias** or **mean reversion**, not a treatment effect

### **3. More Conservative Estimate: ~1.1 Points (With Low Confidence)**

When accounting for week-by-week variation, the average post-period effect is only **1.1 points**, and:
- Effects in weeks 15-17 are **not statistically significant**
- Only weeks 18-22 show significant improvements
- This delayed effect could indicate a learning curve—or it could reflect the continuation of pre-existing trends

### **4. Non-Random Assignment Is the Core Issue**

Branches **chose** whether to adopt the new procedure; they were not randomly assigned. This means:
- Motivated or struggling branches may have self-selected into treatment
- We cannot rule out that these branches would have improved anyway

---

## **Recommendations**

1. **Do NOT proceed with system-wide rollout** based on current evidence

2. **Conduct a randomized controlled trial**:
   - Randomly assign remaining branches to treatment or control
   - This would eliminate selection bias and provide credible causal evidence

3. **If proceeding despite uncertainty, implement with rigorous monitoring**:
   - Track service scores for 12+ weeks post-implementation at each branch
   - Be prepared to discontinue if expected improvements do not materialize

4. **Investigate why control branches declined**:
   - Understanding this could reveal system-wide issues unrelated to the shelving procedure

---

## **Technical Note**

*Analysis uses difference-in-differences and event study methods with cluster-robust standard errors. The parallel trends assumption required for valid DiD inference is violated (pre-period coefficients jointly significant, p=0.001), limiting causal interpretation. Data includes 12,053 weekly observations from 709 branches.*
