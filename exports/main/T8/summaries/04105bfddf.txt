# **Memorandum to the Board**

## **Subject: Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

### **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure produced a small but statistically reliable improvement in service scores.** Switched branches improved by **2.1 points** (approximately **4%**) relative to control branches. While this contradicts the initial expectation of no effect, the magnitude is modest. We recommend proceeding with rollout, but with tempered expectations about the operational impact.

---

### **Key Findings**

| Metric | Value | Interpretation |
|--------|-------|----------------|
| Treatment Effect | +2.13 points | Statistically significant (p < 0.001) |
| 95% Confidence Interval | [0.97, 3.30] | Effect is reliably positive |
| Effect Size (Cohen's d) | 0.21 | Small effect (~20% of one SD) |
| Pre-Treatment Difference | 0.16 points | Groups were well-balanced |
| Placebo Test | 0.46 points (p = 0.52) | No spurious pre-trends detected |

**What this means:**
- The improvement is **real**, not random noise
- The improvement is **small**—about 2 points on a ~50-point scale
- The result is **robust** to alternative specifications and controls
- There is **no evidence** the effect was present before the switch

---

### **Context**

The board's expectation that this change would have no measurable impact was reasonable—rotation and shelving procedures are operational details that often don't translate into patron-facing service improvements. The fact that we detected an effect suggests either:

1. Staff can execute the new procedure more efficiently, freeing time for patron service
2. The new arrangement makes materials easier to locate
3. There is a Hawthorne effect (branches performed better because they knew they were part of a change)

---

### **Recommendation**

**Proceed with rollout, but:**

| Action | Rationale |
|--------|-----------|
| **Approve system-wide implementation** | The effect is real and positive; no downside detected |
| **Set modest expectations** | Expect ~2 point improvements, not transformational change |
| **Track implementation fidelity** | Ensure branches actually adopt the new procedure as designed |
| **Monitor for 6 months post-rollout** | Verify the effect persists beyond the study period |
| **Consider complementary improvements** | This change alone won't drive major service gains; pair with other initiatives |

---

### **Final Verdict**

**✓ APPROVE ROLLOUT — with modest expectations**

The evidence supports system-wide adoption, but this is a marginal improvement, not a breakthrough. The board's original skepticism was well-founded: the effect is real but small. Approve the rollout as a low-risk, low-reward operational refinement—not as a major service enhancement.
