# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

---

## **Subject: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

**Date:** November 2025  
**Prepared by:** Data Analysis Team

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure achieved gains of **approximately 2.0 points (4%)** attributable to the change itself. The effect is statistically significant and consistent across analytical methods.

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout.**

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

### **✓ APPROVE ROLLOUT**

**Confidence Level: High**

The evidence supports proceeding with system-wide implementation. The new procedure delivers real, measurable improvements to service quality. While treatment branches showed some improvement relative to controls even before the switch, the procedure itself produced an additional **2.0-point gain** that is:

- **Statistically reliable** (p < 0.001; effect would occur by chance less than 1 in 1,000 times)
- **Practically meaningful** (4% improvement on a ~50-point scale)
- **Broadly distributed** (73% of branches improved)
- **Equity-enhancing** (largest gains at lowest-performing branches)

**Risk Assessment: Low** — The procedure helped most branches and harmed few. No branches experienced severe declines attributable to the change.

**Expected System-Wide Impact:** If all 709 branches adopt the procedure, we project an average service score increase of **2.0 points**, with the greatest benefits accruing to our lowest-performing locations.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

| Finding | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Estimated Treatment Effect** | +2.0 points (95% CI: +1.5 to +2.6) |
| **Statistical Significance** | p < 0.001 |
| **Branch Success Rate** | 73% of switched branches improved |
| **Greatest Impact** | Lowest-performing branches: +3.8 points |
| **Caveat** | Treatment branches were improving relative to control even before the switch |

## **EVIDENCE SUMMARY**

**Study Design:** Difference-in-differences analysis comparing 425 switched branches against 284 control branches over 17 weeks.

| Group | Pre-Switch | Post-Switch | Change |
|-------|-----------|-------------|--------|
| Control | 51.0 | 51.2 | +0.2 |
| Treatment | 51.3 | 53.5 | +2.3 |
| **Attributable Effect** | | | **+2.0** |

**Note:** Treatment branches were closing the gap with control branches before the switch. The 2.0-point estimate represents the *additional* improvement beyond this pre-existing trend.
