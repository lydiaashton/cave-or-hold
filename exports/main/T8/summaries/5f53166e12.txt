# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure  
**Date:** November 2025  
**Analysis Period:** Weeks 6–22 (17 weeks of data)

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Proceed with caution.** The new procedure is associated with higher service scores, but **the evidence for causality is weaker than initially apparent.** Branches that adopted the new procedure were already improving relative to other branches *before* the switch. A conservative estimate suggests the procedure may have added **1.0–1.2 points** to service scores, not the 1.9 points from the standard analysis.

**Recommendation:** Expand to additional branches with careful monitoring before committing to system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. The Pre-Trend Problem

| Period | Treatment–Control Gap |
|--------|----------------------|
| Week 6 (start) | 0.08 points |
| Week 14 (before switch) | 2.31 points |
| Week 22 (end) | 3.27 points |

**Critical issue:** The gap between treatment and control branches was *already widening* before the switch occurred (p = 0.0005). This suggests branches selected for the new procedure were already on an improving trajectory.

### 2. Revised Effect Estimates

| Method | Estimated Effect |
|--------|-----------------|
| Standard DiD (weeks 6–14 baseline) | +1.9 points |
| Conservative DiD (weeks 13–14 baseline) | +1.2 points |
| Week 14 to Week 22 comparison | +1.0 points |

### 3. What We Can Say With Confidence

- The new procedure is **associated with** higher service scores
- The association is **statistically significant** (p < 0.001)
- The **true causal effect is uncertain** due to pre-existing trends
- A reasonable range is **+1.0 to +1.9 points** (2–4% improvement)

### 4. Possible Explanations

1. **Selection bias:** Branches chosen to switch first may have been better-managed or had more motivated staff
2. **Anticipation effects:** Branches preparing for the switch may have improved before formal implementation
3. **True treatment effect:** The procedure itself does improve scores (our hoped-for explanation)

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **Did service scores improve?** | Yes — treatment branches gained ~3 points |
| **Was the new procedure the cause?** | Uncertain — pre-trends suggest partial selection bias |
| **Is the procedure harmful?** | No evidence of harm |
| **Should we roll out everywhere now?** | **No** — evidence insufficient for full commitment |
| **Should we abandon the procedure?** | **No** — signals are positive, worth further testing |

**Verdict: CONDITIONAL APPROVAL**

The procedure shows promise but requires more rigorous validation before system-wide investment. Approve a **randomized pilot expansion** to establish causal evidence, then reconvene for full rollout decision.

---

## **Recommendation**

| Action | Rationale |
|--------|-----------|
| **Expand to 50–100 additional branches** | Test whether effect replicates in branches not on pre-existing improvement trajectories |
| **Use randomized assignment** for next phase | Eliminate selection bias and establish causal evidence |
| **Monitor for 8–12 weeks** | Allow time to observe true treatment effects |
| **Reconvene board after pilot** | Make full rollout decision with stronger evidence |

---

## **Next Steps**

| Timeline | Action |
|----------|--------|
| Immediate | Pause full rollout decision |
| 30 days | Design randomized pilot expansion |

---

**Bottom line for the board:** The new procedure shows promise, but we cannot confidently attribute the observed improvement to the procedure itself. A more rigorous test is warranted before committing cooperative-wide resources.
