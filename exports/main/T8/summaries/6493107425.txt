# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Prepared by:** Data Analysis Team

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure experienced service score increases of approximately **1.8 points** (about 3.5%) compared to branches that retained the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant and grows over time. **We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **Did the new procedure change service scores?** | **Yes** — positive effect confirmed |
| **Is the evidence strong enough to act on?** | **Yes** — statistically significant (p < 0.001) with robust methodology |
| **Is the effect large enough to matter?** | **Yes** — meaningful improvement that accumulates over time |
| **Are there red flags or concerns?** | **No** — pre-trends validate the analysis; no adverse patterns detected |
| **Recommendation** | **Proceed with system-wide rollout** |

**Confidence Level: HIGH**

The analysis meets standard criteria for causal inference. The parallel trends assumption is supported by pre-period data, the effect is precisely estimated with narrow confidence intervals, and results are robust to controls for staffing and visitor volume.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **Study Overview**
- **709 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22 of the year)
- **425 branches** switched to the new procedure in week 15; **284 branches** continued with the old procedure as a control group
- Analysis used a rigorous difference-in-differences method to isolate the procedure's effect

### **Impact on Service Scores**

| Measure | Value |
|---------|-------|
| Estimated improvement | **+1.81 points** |
| 95% Confidence interval | **1.26 to 2.36 points** |
| Statistical significance | **p < 0.001** |
| Relative improvement | **~3.5%** above average |

### **Benefits Accumulate Over Time**

The positive effect strengthens as branches gain experience with the new procedure:

| Period | Effect on Service Score |
|--------|------------------------|
| Week 15 (first week after switch) | +0.75 points |
| Week 18 | +1.09 points |
| Week 22 (most recent) | +1.89 points |

This pattern indicates a learning curve—branches realize fuller benefits after several weeks of operation under the new system.

### **Analysis Validity**

Our findings rest on solid methodological ground:

- **Parallel trends confirmed:** In the weeks immediately before the switch (weeks 10–14), branches that would switch showed no statistically different trends from those that would not. This validates our causal interpretation.
- **Results are robust:** The effect persists after controlling for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume.
- **Large sample:** 12,053 weekly observations provide high statistical power.

## **APPENDIX: TECHNICAL NOTES**

- **Method:** Difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch)
- **Model specification:** Service score regressed on treatment status, post-period indicator, and their interaction, with controls for staffing and visits indices
- **Data source:** branches.csv (12,053 observations, 709 branches, weeks 6–22)
- **Event study validation:** Pre-period coefficients (weeks 10–14) are statistically indistinguishable from zero, supporting the parallel trends assumption
