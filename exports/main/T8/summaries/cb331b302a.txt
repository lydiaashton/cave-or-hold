# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores—an unexpected but statistically clear result.** Branches that switched saw service scores rise by **1.8 points** (3.6%) relative to branches that kept the old procedure. The effect is statistically significant (p = 0.003) with a 95% confidence interval of 0.6 to 3.0 points. While this change was not expected to affect service scores, the evidence supports a genuine positive impact.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15
- **60 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Difference-in-differences analysis with branch-clustered standard errors

### **Results**

| Group | Before | After | Change |
|-------|--------|-------|--------|
| Switched branches | 50.03 | 51.47 | +1.44 |
| Non-switched branches | 48.77 | 48.43 | −0.34 |
| **Net effect** | | | **+1.78** |

- **Treatment effect: +1.78 points** (95% CI: 0.60 to 2.97)
- **P-value: 0.003** — unlikely to occur by chance
- **Effect size: 3.6%** of baseline, or about 18% of one standard deviation

### **Why We're Confident**
- Pre-switch trends were parallel between groups (no significant difference, p = 0.25)
- Result holds after controlling for staffing and visitor volume
- Simple DiD and controlled models produce nearly identical estimates
- Switched branches improved while non-switched branches declined slightly during the same period

---

## **Final Verdict**

**Recommendation: Proceed with cooperative-wide rollout.**

The data provides strong evidence that the new procedure improves service scores. The effect is statistically significant, practically meaningful, and robust to alternative specifications. There is no statistical basis for caution—the confidence interval excludes zero and even the lower bound (0.6 points) represents a positive gain.

**Risk assessment:** Low. The procedure change has already been tested in 90 branches without negative consequences. The main risk is implementation inconsistency, not the procedure itself.

**Expected outcome:** If rolled out to all remaining branches, the cooperative should see an average service score improvement of approximately 1.5 to 2.0 points per branch.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve cooperative-wide rollout.** The evidence supports expansion, with an expected ~1.8 point service score improvement per branch.

2. **Monitor during implementation.** Track scores as each branch adopts the procedure. Given this result was unexpected, close monitoring will help confirm the effect replicates.

3. **Document implementation details.** Capture what each branch does during rollout to identify any variations that might affect outcomes.

4. **Investigate the mechanism.** Consider a follow-up study to understand *why* service scores improved—this could reveal additional operational opportunities.

5. **Communicate to staff.** Share this outcome with branch teams; the improvement exceeded expectations and reflects well on their adaptation.
