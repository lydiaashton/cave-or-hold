# **Library Cooperative Branch Procedure Change: Impact Analysis**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence is mixed.** While branches that adopted the new procedure showed larger service score improvements than control branches, **the switched branches were already improving faster before the intervention**. After accounting for this pre-existing trend, there is no clear evidence of an immediate effect at the switch point. However, the gap continued widening after the switch, suggesting **possible benefits that are smaller and less certain than initially estimated**.

**Recommendation: Proceed with caution.** Consider a phased rollout with careful monitoring rather than immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Critical Issue: Pre-Existing Trends**

The switched branches were already improving relative to control branches **before** the new procedure was implemented:

| Week | Gap (Switched − Control) |
|------|-------------------------|
| 6 | 1.29 |
| 10 | 1.53 |
| 14 (last pre-switch) | 3.23 |

**The gap was increasing by 0.21 points per week before the switch** (p = 0.004). This violates the key assumption needed for a clean causal estimate.

### **What Happened at the Switch Point?**

| Analysis | Estimate | Statistical Significance |
|----------|----------|-------------------------|
| Simple DiD (unadjusted) | +2.13 points | p < 0.001 |
| DiD controlling for pre-trends | +0.07 points | p = 0.88 (not significant) |
| Within-branch comparison | +3.42 points | p < 0.001 |

**Interpretation:** The simple DiD is confounded by pre-existing trends. When we account for these trends, there is **no statistically significant jump** at the switch point itself.

### **Post-Switch Pattern**

| Period | Switched Branches | Control Branches | Gap |
|--------|------------------|------------------|-----|
| Before (weeks 6–14) | 51.18 | 49.07 | 2.11 |
| After (weeks 15–22) | 55.09 | 50.85 | 4.24 |

The gap continued widening after the switch (from 3.6 to 6.0 points), but we cannot definitively attribute this to the new procedure versus continued pre-existing trends.

---

## **What This Means**

| Possibility | Evidence |
|-------------|----------|
| **New procedure has a real effect** | Gap widened after switch; within-branch comparisons show improvement |
| **Effect is smaller than initial estimate** | No significant jump at switch point when controlling for trends |
| **Switched branches differ systematically** | Pre-existing trend suggests these branches were already on different trajectory |

**Most likely:** The new procedure may have a modest positive effect, but it is **smaller and less certain** than the initial 2.13-point estimate suggested.

---

## **Recommendations**

| Action | Rationale |
|--------|-----------|
| **Phased rollout** to remaining branches | Allows continued monitoring and course correction |
| **Randomize rollout order** for remaining 284 branches | Creates cleaner evidence for future evaluation |
| **Track implementation fidelity** | Ensure branches are actually using the new procedure as designed |
| **Re-evaluate in 3–6 months** | More data will clarify whether effects persist and grow |
| **Document branch characteristics** | Identify which types of branches benefit most |

---

## **Final Verdict**

**Do not proceed with immediate system-wide rollout.**

The data does not support the confidence we initially had in this intervention. While the new procedure may provide benefits, we cannot rule out that the observed improvements reflect pre-existing differences between branches rather than the procedure itself.

**The prudent path forward:**

1. **Pause** the planned full rollout
2. **Randomize** the order in which remaining 284 branches adopt the procedure
3. **Monitor** service scores closely during implementation
4. **Reassess** in 3–6 months with stronger causal evidence

This approach protects the cooperative from investing resources in a procedure that may not deliver the expected returns, while still allowing branches to benefit if the procedure does prove effective. The cost of waiting is low; the cost of a failed system-wide rollout is high.
