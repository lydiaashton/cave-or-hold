## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new rotation and shelving procedure achieved service scores **1.96 points higher** (95% CI: 1.42 to 2.50) than branches that kept the old procedure. This ~4% improvement is statistically significant and confirms our expectation. **We recommend proceeding with the system-wide rollout.**

## **The Evidence**

We analyzed 17 weeks of service data from all 709 branches in the cooperative. Starting in week 15, 425 branches switched to the new procedure while 284 branches continued with the old procedure. This created a natural comparison that isolates the effect of the change.

| | Treatment (New Procedure) | Control (Old Procedure) |
|---|---|---|
| Number of Branches | 425 | 284 |
| Average Score Before | 50.8 | 49.4 |
| Average Score After | 51.9 | 48.5 |
| **Change** | **+1.1** | **-0.8** |

**Net effect of new procedure: +1.96 service score points**

---

## **Why This Matters**

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| **Is the result real?** | Yes. The probability this occurred by chance is less than 1 in 1,000 (p < 0.001). |
| **How confident are we?** | 95% confident the true effect is between 1.42 and 2.50 points. |
| **Is it meaningful?** | Yes. A ~4% improvement on a metric where the average branch scores 50. |
| **Did we control for other factors?** | Yes. Results hold after accounting for staffing levels, visitor volume, and branch differences. |
