# **Board Memo: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board  
**Subject:** Impact of New Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores

---

## **Bottom Line**

**We found evidence that the new procedure improved service scores—unexpectedly.** The data shows a **2.4 point average increase** (approximately 5%) in branches that adopted the new procedure compared to those that did not. While this contradicts our initial expectations, the finding is statistically robust and consistent across the post-implementation period. We recommend **proceeding with rollout while continuing to monitor** to confirm the effect persists.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measured Effect
| Measure | Value |
|---------|-------|
| Average score improvement | **+2.37 points** |
| 95% Confidence interval | **+1.07 to +3.68 points** |
| Statistical significance | **p = 0.0003** |
| Relative to average score (~50) | **~5% improvement** |

### 2. Why We're Confident in the Finding
- **Consistent across time:** All 8 post-switch weeks showed positive effects
- **Robust to specifications:** Effect persists when excluding early weeks or narrowing the analysis window
- **Pre-treatment trends largely parallel:** Joint test shows no systematic pre-existing differences (p = 0.35)
- **Large sample:** 150 branches, 2,550 observations

### 3. Important Caveats
- **This was unexpected:** We anticipated no meaningful change in service scores from a back-office procedure change
- **One pre-treatment week showed a difference:** Week 6 had a statistically significant pre-existing gap, though the overall pre-trend test is not significant
- **Mechanism unclear:** We don't yet understand *why* shelving procedures would affect patron-facing service scores
- **Short observation window:** Only 8 weeks of post-implementation data

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Verdict |
|----------|---------|
| **Did the new procedure change service scores?** | **Yes** — statistically significant improvement |
| **Is the evidence strong enough to act on?** | **Yes** — robust across multiple analytical approaches |
| **Should we roll out to all branches?** | **Yes, with monitoring** — proceed but verify |
| **Confidence level** | **Moderate-to-high** — unexpected finding warrants continued verification |

**Decision:** **Approve system-wide rollout.** The statistical evidence supports a real positive effect. Given the low risk (procedure change is already implemented in 60% of branches with no reported problems) and the potential benefit (5% service score improvement), the expected value of rollout is positive. However, we should treat this as **evidence-based action with built-in verification**, not as a settled conclusion.

---

## **Recommendations**

| Priority | Action |
|----------|--------|
| **High** | Proceed with rollout, but **phase it** over 6-8 weeks |
| **High** | **Monitor service scores closely** during rollout to verify effects replicate |
| **Medium** | **Investigate the mechanism** — survey staff and patrons to understand why this works |
| **Medium** | **Track operational costs** to ensure no hidden burdens offset the service gains |
| **Low** | Plan a **6-month follow-up analysis** to confirm sustained impact |
