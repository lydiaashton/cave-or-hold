# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

**To:** Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure shows **suggestive but not definitive evidence** of improving service scores by approximately **+2.7 points (6%)**. The result is **marginally statistically significant** (p=0.10), with a 95% confidence interval ranging from **no effect to +5.7 points**. The growing benefit over time and absence of negative signals are encouraging.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure as a control group
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in service scores between groups before and after the switch
- This design controls for branch-specific characteristics and overall time trends

### **Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 44.9 | 44.9 | 0.0 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 46.9 | 49.4 | +2.5 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+2.7** |

- **Point estimate:** +2.65 service score points
- **95% confidence interval:** -0.4 to +5.7 points
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.10 (marginally significant)
- **Effect size:** ~6% improvement relative to baseline
- **Probability effect is positive:** ~95%

### **Supporting Evidence**

1. **Parallel trends confirmed:** Before the switch, both groups showed similar trends in service scores (p=0.55), validating the comparison method.

2. **Effect grows over time:** The improvement strengthens as branches gain experience with the new procedure:
   - Weeks 15–18 (early period): +3.9 points
   - Weeks 19–22 (late period): +5.1 points
   
   This pattern suggests a learning curve or cumulative benefit.

3. **Results are robust:** The effect remains similar when accounting for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume.

### **Limitations**

- The result does not reach conventional statistical significance (p<0.05), meaning we cannot rule out no effect with high confidence
- Only 8 weeks of post-switch data are available; longer-term effects are unknown
- Branches were not randomly assigned to switch, though the analysis controls for this

---

## **Final Verdict**

**Recommendation: Proceed with full rollout, with conditions.**

The evidence is **not conclusive**, but it points in a positive direction:

| Consideration | Assessment |
|--------------|------------|
| Statistical certainty | ⚠️ Incomplete (p=0.10) |
| Direction of effect | ✅ Positive |
| Magnitude of effect | ✅ Meaningful (~6%) |
| Trend over time | ✅ Improving |
| Risk of harm | ✅ None detected |
| Implementation cost | [Board to assess] |

**If implementation costs are modest and the change is reversible**, the balance of evidence supports rolling out to all branches. The worst-case scenario (no effect) is acceptable, while the likely outcome (modest improvement) benefits patrons.

**If implementation costs are substantial**, consider a phased rollout with continued monitoring to build stronger evidence before full commitment.

## **Conclusion**

This analysis provides **encouraging but not definitive** evidence that the new procedure improves service. Given the positive direction, growing effect over time, and absence of negative signals, we recommend proceeding with rollout **provided implementation costs are reasonable**. Continued monitoring will confirm whether the benefits are sustained and help identify opportunities for further improvement.

---

*Analysis based on weekly service score data from 34 branches, weeks 6–22. Switch occurred week 15. Statistical analysis used difference-in-differences regression with branch-clustered standard errors.*
