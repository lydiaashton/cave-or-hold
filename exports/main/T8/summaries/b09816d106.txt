# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Analysis Period:** Weeks 6–22 (17 weeks of data)

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**We cannot confidently conclude the new procedure caused the improvement.** While branches using the new procedure do show higher service scores, the data reveals these branches were **already improving relative to others before the switch occurred**. This undermines the causal claim. **We recommend against a full rollout until stronger evidence is obtained.**

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

> **❌ DO NOT APPROVE SYSTEM-WIDE ROLLOUT**
>
> **Reason:** The evidence does not support a causal claim. The observed improvement likely reflects pre-existing differences between branches, not the procedure itself.
>
> **Required before reconsideration:** A properly randomized trial or quasi-experimental design that establishes comparable treatment and control groups.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **The Problem: Pre-Existing Trends**

| Week | Difference (Switched − Control) | Statistically Significant? |
|------|--------------------------------|---------------------------|
| Week 6 | −0.88 | No |
| Week 10 | −0.25 | No |
| Week 13 | +1.20 | No (p=0.078) |
| **Week 14** | **+1.50** | **Yes (p=0.031)** |
| Week 15 | +1.35 | No (p=0.057) |
| Week 22 | +3.22 | Yes |

**Critical finding:** By week 14—one week BEFORE the switch—the switched branches were already scoring 1.5 points higher than control branches, and this difference was statistically significant.

### **Statistical Test of Pre-Trends**

| Test | Result |
|------|--------|
| Pre-trend interaction p-value | **0.000** (highly significant) |
| Interpretation | The gap between groups was widening **before** the intervention |

This violates the **parallel trends assumption** required for valid difference-in-differences analysis. The switched branches were already on a different trajectory.

### **What This Means**

The observed 1.9-point improvement likely reflects **pre-existing differences** between branches, not the procedure itself. Possible explanations:

1. **Selection bias:** Branches that switched may have better management, more resources, or more motivated staff
2. **Anticipation effect:** Branches may have improved practices in preparation for the switch
3. **Regression to the mean:** Switched branches may have been selected because they were due for improvement anyway

### **The Week 14 → 15 Transition is Telling**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Week 14 difference (before switch) | +1.50 points |
| Week 15 difference (after switch) | +1.35 points |
| **Change at switch point** | **−0.15 points** |

The gap actually **shrank slightly** in the first week after the switch, which is inconsistent with an immediate treatment effect.

### **Recommended Study Design**

To obtain credible evidence, we recommend:

1. **Randomized Controlled Trial:** Randomly assign remaining branches to treatment vs. control, ensuring comparable groups
2. **Longer pre-period:** Collect more baseline data to verify parallel trends
3. **Process documentation:** Understand what specific practices changed and whether they were implemented consistently
4. **Qualitative interviews:** Talk to branch managers about what actually changed and what else may have influenced scores

---

## **WHAT WE CAN SAY WITH CONFIDENCE**

- ✅ Branches using the new procedure **do** have higher service scores (by ~2 points on average)
- ❌ We **cannot attribute this to the procedure itself** given the pre-existing trends
- ⚠️ The 425 switched branches may have characteristics that would have led to improvement regardless of the procedure
