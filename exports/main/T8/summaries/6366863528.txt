# **Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure shows a modest but statistically significant benefit, primarily by protecting branches from service score declines.** Whether to roll it out system-wide depends on implementation costs. If the procedure is low-cost and easy to adopt, the evidence supports rollout. If it requires significant resources, the board may wish to pilot it further or investigate the causes of the broader service score decline observed in weeks 15-22.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Measured Effect**

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|---------------|---------------|--------|
| **New procedure** (90 branches) | 50.30 | 50.56 | +0.26 |
| **Old procedure** (60 branches) | 48.88 | 46.88 | −2.00 |

**Estimated effect: +2.26 points** (95% CI: 0.95 to 3.56; p=0.0009)

### **2. Important Context**

- **Treated branches started higher**: Even before the switch, they averaged 1.4 points above control branches. The analysis accounts for this, but it means the groups weren't identical.

- **System-wide decline in weeks 15-22**: All branches experienced lower scores after week 15 (approximately -8 points overall), suggesting an external factor such as seasonal changes or back-to-school demand. The new procedure appears to have **protected** branches from this decline rather than actively improving scores.

- **Effect size**: The 2.26-point improvement represents about 4-5% of baseline scores. This is statistically reliable but modest in practical terms.

### **3. Analysis Validity**

| Check | Result |
|-------|--------|
| Parallel trends before switch | ✓ No significant difference (p=0.46) |
| Results hold controlling for staffing & visits | ✓ Yes |
| Complete data | ✓ All 150 branches, all 17 weeks |

---

## **Recommendation**

The board should consider:

1. **Implementation costs**: If the new procedure requires minimal additional resources, rollout is supported by the evidence.

2. **Alternative investigation**: The sharp decline in control branch scores after week 15 warrants investigation. Understanding this decline may be as important as the procedure change itself.

3. **Phased approach**: Consider rolling out to additional branches while monitoring results, rather than immediate system-wide adoption.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Quantify implementation costs** of the new procedure (staff time, training, materials)

2. **Investigate the week 15+ decline** - what external factors affected service scores across the cooperative?

3. **If costs are low**: Proceed with rollout, monitoring service scores for 8-12 weeks

4. **If costs are significant**: Consider a cost-benefit analysis before committing to system-wide adoption

---

## **Final Verdict**

**Proceed with cautionary rollout.**

The evidence demonstrates a real, statistically significant benefit from the new procedure. While the effect is modest (~4-5% improvement), it contradicts the prior expectation that the procedure change would make no difference. The procedure appears to provide resilience against external pressures that negatively affect service scores.

**Conditions for rollout:**
- Confirm implementation costs are reasonable relative to the benefit
- Monitor outcomes for 8-12 weeks post-rollout
- Investigate the system-wide score decline observed in weeks 15-22

**If costs are low:** Recommend system-wide adoption within the next quarter.

**If costs are uncertain:** Recommend a 3-month pilot with 20-30 additional branches before full commitment.

---

## **Appendix: Analysis Details**

- **Data:** 2,550 observations (150 branches × 17 weeks)
- **Study period:** Weeks 6–22; switch occurred week 15
- **Method:** Difference-in-differences regression with branch-clustered standard errors
- **Software:** Python (pandas, statsmodels)
