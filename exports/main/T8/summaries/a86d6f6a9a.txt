# **Board Memorandum (Final)**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure does improve service scores—clearly and significantly for the first 5 weeks after implementation.** Branches saw gains of 5–10 points during this period. However, **the effect fades after week 5** and may turn negative by week 8. 

**Recommendation: Proceed with rollout, but pair it with investigation into why effects fade.** The procedure delivers meaningful short-term benefits. Understanding and addressing the fade could unlock sustained improvement.

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Decision** | ✅ **APPROVE system-wide rollout** |
| **Confidence** | Moderate-High (clear short-term benefit established) |
| **Condition** | Must include sustainability measures to address effect fade |
| **Expected Impact** | +5 to +10 points for first 5 weeks; unknown thereafter |
| **Key Risk** | Benefits may not persist without ongoing support |

**Rationale:** A procedure that delivers 5 weeks of significant improvement is worth adopting—even if we must learn how to sustain it. The alternative (no rollout) guarantees zero benefit.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Weekly Effects After Switch**

| Weeks After Switch | Average Effect | Statistical Significance |
|-------------------|----------------|-------------------------|
| Weeks 1–5 | +5 to +10 points | **Highly significant (p < 0.001)** |
| Weeks 6–7 | +1 to +2 points | Not significant |
| Week 8 | -3 points | Significant decline (p = 0.006) |

### **What This Means**

✓ **The procedure works**—there is clear evidence of improvement  
✓ **The effect is meaningful**—5–10 points represents 10–20% of average score  
⚠️ **The effect doesn't last**—benefits fade after approximately 5 weeks  
❓ **Unknown why it fades**—could be staff adaptation, procedural drift, or measurement issues  

### **Overall Estimate**
- **Average treatment effect:** +2.3 points across all post-switch weeks
- **Probability effect is positive:** 93.9%
- **95% confidence interval:** -0.6 to +5.1 points

The modest overall average masks the strong initial effect and subsequent fade.

---

## **Recommendations**

### **Primary Recommendation: Proceed with Rollout**

The evidence supports adoption because:
1. **Clear benefit exists**—5 weeks of significant improvement is valuable
2. **No evidence of lasting harm**—the week 8 decline needs investigation but isn't definitive
3. **Fade is fixable**—unlike a procedure that simply doesn't work, fading effects can often be addressed through training refreshers, monitoring, or procedural adjustments

### **Critical Companion Actions**

1. **Investigate the fade immediately.** Interview staff at switched branches to understand:
   - Are staff reverting to old habits?
   - Are there operational barriers to sustained implementation?
   - Does the novelty effect explain the pattern?

2. **Build sustainability into rollout.** Consider:
   - Refresher training at week 4–5
   - Regular fidelity checks on procedure implementation
   - Peer mentoring between early-adopter and new branches

3. **Continue monitoring.** Track service scores for all branches post-rollout to confirm the pattern and measure whether sustainability interventions work.

4. **Set expectations.** Communicate to staff that initial gains are expected and that maintaining the procedure requires ongoing attention.

- **Key finding:** Weeks 15–19 all show p < 0.001; weeks 20–21 non-significant; week 22 p = 0.006 (negative)
