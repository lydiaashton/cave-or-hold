# **Board Summary: Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence is inconclusive.** While branches that adopted the new procedure showed larger score improvements than those that did not, we cannot confidently attribute this to the procedure itself. The branches that switched were already improving faster *before* the change, which makes it impossible to isolate the true effect. **We do not recommend a full rollout until this is resolved.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **What the Data Shows**
- **709 branches** total: 425 adopted the new procedure, 284 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Switched branches improved by 3.9 points; non-switched branches improved by 2.0 points
- **Raw difference:** 1.9 points favoring the new procedure

### **Critical Limitation: Pre-Existing Differences**
The analysis relies on an assumption that switched and non-switched branches would have followed similar trends absent the intervention. **This assumption is violated:**

| Period | Gap Trend (Treated vs. Control) |
|--------|--------------------------------|
| Weeks 6–14 (before switch) | +0.19 points/week (p=0.004) |
| Weeks 15–22 (after switch) | Continued widening |

The gap between groups was already widening **before** the switch occurred. This means:
- The 1.9-point raw estimate likely overstates the true effect
- A conservative estimate accounting for pre-existing trends suggests an effect of only **~0.4 points**
- The immediate change at the switch point (week 14→15) was only **0.17 points**—smaller than the prior week's change

### **Gap Evolution Over Time**
```
Week  6: -0.35    Week 14: +1.45  (last week before switch)
Week  7: +0.11    Week 15: +1.62  (first week after switch)
Week  8: +0.93    Week 16: +1.97
...               Week 22: +3.37
```

The gap widened steadily throughout the observation period, with no clear break at the switch point.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Do not proceed with full rollout yet.** The current evidence does not support a confident recommendation.

2. **Investigate why branches were selected to switch.** The pre-existing differential trends suggest switched branches may differ systematically from non-switched branches (e.g., more motivated staff, better management, different patron demographics). Understanding this selection process is critical.

3. **Consider alternative evaluation approaches:**
   - **Randomized rollout:** Randomly assign remaining branches to switch now vs. later
   - **Staggered adoption analysis:** Use more advanced methods that account for differential trends
   - **Qualitative investigation:** Interview branch managers to understand implementation quality and contextual factors

4. **Extend the observation period.** More post-switch data may help distinguish temporary fluctuations from sustained effects.

5. **If proceeding cautiously:** Consider a limited pilot with 20–30 additional branches, with careful monitoring and a pre-specified evaluation plan.

---

## **Final Verdict**

> **Do not roll out the new procedure cooperative-wide at this time.**
>
> The data does not provide reliable evidence that the new procedure causes improved service scores. The observed improvement is confounded by pre-existing differences between branches that switched and those that did not. Proceeding with a full rollout based on this evidence would risk:
> - Implementing a procedure that may not deliver the expected benefits
> - Incurring unnecessary costs (training, staff time, operational disruption)
> - Missing the opportunity to design a more rigorous evaluation
>
> **Recommended path forward:** Pause the rollout, investigate the selection process, and design a randomized or quasi-experimental evaluation that can produce credible causal evidence. If there is strong operational pressure to proceed, limit expansion to a small, monitored pilot with clear success criteria and a commitment to evaluate results before further expansion.
