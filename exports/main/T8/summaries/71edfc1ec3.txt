## **Final Verdict**

**INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE FOR SYSTEM-WIDE ROLLOUT**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did service scores improve in switched branches? | **Yes** – clearly |
| Did the new procedure *cause* the improvement? | **Uncertain** – pre-existing trends confound the result |
| Is the effect likely positive, zero, or negative? | **Probably positive, but smaller than 2 points** |
| Should we roll out to all branches now? | **No** – not without stronger evidence |

---

### **Why This Verdict?**

The core problem is **selection bias**. Branches that switched early were already outperforming the control group and improving faster *before* the switch. This pattern is consistent with two explanations:

1. **The procedure works** – and early adopters benefited
2. **Better branches self-selected** – motivated, well-managed branches opted in (or were chosen) first, and would have improved regardless

We cannot distinguish between these with the current data.

---

### **What Would Change This Verdict?**

| Evidence Type | Current Status | What We Need |
|--------------|----------------|--------------|
| Random assignment | ❌ Not done | Randomize remaining branches to switch now vs. later |
| Parallel pre-trends | ❌ Violated | Show treatment/control had similar slopes before week 15 |
| Mechanism evidence | ❌ Not collected | Qualitative data on *how* the procedure affects service |
| Longer post-period | ⚠️ Only 8 weeks | 12+ weeks to see if effect stabilizes or grows |

---

### **Actionable Path Forward**

1. **Do not mandate system-wide rollout** based on current evidence
2. **Randomize the next phase**: Assign 50% of remaining branches to switch immediately, 50% to wait 8 weeks. This creates a valid control group.
3. **Re-analyze with clean identification**: The randomized comparison will yield a credible causal estimate within 2 months.
4. **If the randomized estimate confirms a positive effect** (even 1 point), proceed with full rollout confidently.

---

**The board's decision:** Proceeding now risks spending resources on a procedure that may not deliver the expected benefit. A brief delay for proper testing protects the cooperative from a costly mistake while still allowing eventual adoption if the effect is real.
