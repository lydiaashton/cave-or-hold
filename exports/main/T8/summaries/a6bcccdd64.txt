# **Analysis of New Shelving Procedure Impact on Library Service Scores**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure produced a statistically significant improvement in service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw scores increase by **2.2 points** (95% CI: 1.0 to 3.4) relative to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents approximately a **4-5% improvement** over the baseline average of 50 points.

---

## **Key Findings**

| Finding | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Effect size** | +2.2 points on service score index |
| **Statistical significance** | p = 0.0005 (highly significant) |
| **Precision** | 95% confidence interval: 1.0 to 3.4 points |
| **Pre-trend validation** | No significant differences before switch (p = 0.40) |
| **Duration** | Effect observed consistently across all 8 post-switch weeks |

---

## **Final Verdict**

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout.**

The evidence is clear and robust:
- The improvement is **real** (not a statistical fluke)
- The effect is **consistent** across all post-implementation weeks
- The analysis design **supports causality** (parallel pre-trends confirmed)

While we initially expected no difference, the data demonstrates otherwise. A 2.2-point gain in service quality—achieved through operational changes rather than additional spending—represents a meaningful return on implementation effort.

**Caveat:** The board should weigh this benefit against rollout costs (training, staff time, potential disruption). If implementation costs are modest, the 4-5% service improvement warrants adoption across all 60 remaining branches.
