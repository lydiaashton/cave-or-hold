# **MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2025  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The evidence is inconclusive.** While branches that adopted the new procedure showed higher service score gains than those that did not (+2.3 points vs. +0.1 points), **we cannot confidently attribute this difference to the procedure itself.** The branches that switched had systematically higher scores throughout the study period, and the gap between groups was already widening before the switch occurred. This suggests the observed difference may reflect pre-existing branch characteristics rather than the procedure's effect.

**Recommendation: Do not proceed with system-wide rollout based on this evidence alone. Gather additional data before making a decision.**

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### What the Data Shows

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Branches with new procedure | 50.37 | 52.76 | **+2.39** |
| Branches with old procedure | 48.68 | 48.79 | +0.11 |
| **Observed difference** | | | **+2.27** |

### Why We Cannot Trust This Estimate

**1. The groups were not comparable from the start**
- Treatment branches had higher average scores throughout the entire study period
- This suggests systematic differences (e.g., better staffing, more resources, different communities)

**2. The gap was widening BEFORE the switch**

| Week | Treatment - Control Gap |
|------|------------------------|
| Week 6 | 0.36 |
| Week 10 | 1.87 |
| Week 14 (last pre-switch) | 3.60 |
| Week 22 (last post-switch) | 4.65 |

- The gap grew from 0.36 to 3.60 points **before** the procedure changed
- Post-switch growth (3.60 to 4.65) appears to continue this pre-existing trend
- Statistical test confirms different pre-switch trends (p < 0.001)

**3. This violates a core assumption of the analysis**
- The "difference-in-differences" method requires parallel trends before the intervention
- When this assumption is violated, the estimated effect is likely **biased upward**
- The true effect could be smaller, zero, or even negative

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did service scores improve in switched branches? | **Yes** (+2.39 points) |
| Can we credit the new procedure? | **No** — evidence is insufficient |
| Should we roll out to all branches? | **Not yet** — need better evidence |

**The data does not demonstrate that the new procedure causes improved service scores.** The observed improvement is confounded by pre-existing differences between branches that chose to switch and those that did not. Rolling out the procedure system-wide based on this analysis would be premature and risks investing resources in a change that may not deliver the expected benefits.

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

1. **Pause rollout decision** — Current evidence does not support a confident recommendation

2. **Investigate why branches were selected** — Understand what distinguished switching branches from non-switching branches (size, location, staffing, prior performance)

3. **Consider alternative evaluation approaches:**
   - **Randomized rollout**: Randomly assign remaining branches to switch now vs. later
   - **Extended observation**: Collect more weeks of data to see if patterns stabilize
   - **Matched comparison**: Compare switching branches to similar non-switching branches

4. **Gather qualitative feedback** — Survey branch managers and staff about their experience with the new procedure

5. **Monitor key metrics** — Continue tracking service scores, staffing, and visits for all branches

---

## **METHODOLOGY NOTE**

This analysis used a quasi-experimental "difference-in-differences" design. However, **the parallel trends assumption was violated** (p < 0.001), meaning the treatment and control groups had different trajectories before the intervention. When this assumption fails, the estimated treatment effect cannot be interpreted as causal. The +2.27 point estimate likely reflects pre-existing differences between branches rather than the procedure's impact.
