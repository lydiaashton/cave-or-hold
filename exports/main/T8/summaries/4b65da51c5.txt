# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD OF DIRECTORS**

---

**TO:** Board of Directors

**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure  

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure produced a statistically significant improvement in service scores.** This contradicts the expectation that the change would make no difference. The effect is real but modest: approximately **+2 points** (about **4% improvement**). Whether this justifies system-wide rollout depends on implementation costs and operational priorities not captured in this analysis.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### 1. The New Procedure Has a Measurable Effect

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| **Branches with New Procedure** | 51.11 | 53.62 | +2.51 |
| **Branches with Old Procedure** | 52.27 | 52.77 | +0.51 |
| **Net Effect of New Procedure** | — | — | **+2.00** |

- **Estimated impact:** +2.03 points on the service score index
- **Statistical confidence:** 95% confidence interval: +0.82 to +3.24 points
- **Significance level:** p = 0.001 (highly unlikely to be random chance)

### 2. Effect Size in Context

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Improvement relative to average | ~4% |
| Typical week-to-week score variation | ±6.0 points |
| Effect as fraction of weekly variation | 0.34× |
| Differences between branches | ±8.3 points |
| Effect as fraction of branch differences | 0.25× |

**Interpretation:** The effect is statistically clear but smaller than normal week-to-week fluctuations at individual branches.

### 3. Study Overview

- **150 branches:** 90 adopted new procedure, 60 kept old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22), switch occurred week 15
- **8 weeks of post-switch data** per treatment branch
- Analysis uses difference-in-differences methodology with cluster-robust standard errors

### 4. Analysis Validity

✓ **Parallel trends confirmed:** Groups followed similar trajectories before the switch (p = 0.94)  
✓ **Results are robust:** Finding holds with controls for staffing, visits, and branch characteristics  
✓ **Effect appears to grow:** Benefits increase ~0.4 points per week post-implementation  
⚠ **Observational study:** Branches were not randomly assigned to treatment  
⚠ **Limited follow-up:** Only 8 weeks of post-switch data available

## **FINAL VERDICT**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **Did the procedure change service scores?** | **Yes** — the effect is statistically significant |
| **Is the effect large?** | **Modest** — about 4%, smaller than typical weekly variation |
| **Can we trust the finding?** | **Reasonably** — design is sound, but not a randomized trial |
| **Should we roll out system-wide?** | **Conditional** — depends on costs and priorities |

**Recommendation:**

> **Proceed with caution.** The evidence confirms the procedure improves scores, contrary to expectations. However, the modest effect size means the board should:
>
> 1. **Obtain implementation cost estimates** for the remaining 60 branches
> 2. **Compare benefits to alternatives** — could the same resources achieve larger gains elsewhere?
> 3. **Consider a phased rollout** to gather more data before full commitment
> 4. **Set clear success metrics** — define what improvement level justifies the investment

The board's decision should weigh this ~4% gain against implementation costs, staff time, and opportunity costs of alternative investments.
