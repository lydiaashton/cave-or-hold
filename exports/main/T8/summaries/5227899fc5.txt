# **Board Summary: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure shows a **positive but statistically inconclusive effect** on service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure improved by an average of **2.4 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. However, this difference is not statistically significant (p=0.14), meaning we cannot confidently rule out that the observed improvement occurred by chance. 

**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
| | |
|---|---|
| **Total branches** | 34 |
| **Treatment group** (new procedure) | 20 branches |
| **Control group** (old procedure) | 14 branches |
| **Study period** | 17 weeks (weeks 6–22) |
| **Switch date** | Week 15 |
| **Service score scale** | Average ~50 points (higher is better) |

### Results Summary

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| **New Procedure** | 48.77 | 50.98 | **+2.21** |
| **Old Procedure** | 50.31 | 50.14 | -0.17 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+2.38** |

### Statistical Confidence
- **Estimated effect:** +2.38 points on service score
- **95% Confidence Interval:** -0.79 to +5.54 points
- **P-value:** 0.14

### Interpretation
- The data suggest a **modest positive effect**, but the evidence is not strong enough to be conclusive
- The **true effect could range** from slightly harmful (-0.8 points) to meaningfully beneficial (+5.5 points)
- Pre-switch trends were similar between groups, supporting the validity of the comparison

## **Final Verdict**

| | |
|---|---|

**Rationale:** The procedure shows promise without clear proof. A phased approach captures potential benefits while generating additional evidence. Full rollout would be premature; abandoning the procedure would discard a potentially valuable improvement.
