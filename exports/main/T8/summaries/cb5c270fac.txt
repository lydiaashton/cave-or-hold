# **Board Memorandum**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure  

---

## **Bottom Line**

**We cannot confidently conclude the new procedure improved service scores.** The branches that adopted the new procedure were already outperforming control branches before the switch, and this pre-existing trend explains most (or all) of the observed improvement. **We recommend pausing the cooperative-wide rollout pending further analysis.**

---

## **Final Verdict**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Did service scores improve?** | Yes, but not clearly due to the procedure |
| **Is the evidence sufficient for rollout?** | **No** |
| **Recommendation** | **Pause and gather more evidence** |

**Rationale:** The branches selected for early adoption were already on a stronger performance trajectory before switching. When we account for this pre-existing advantage, the estimated effect of the new procedure becomes statistically indistinguishable from zero. Rolling out cooperative-wide based on current evidence risks investing resources in a change that may not deliver the expected benefits.

---

## **Key Findings**

### The Critical Problem: Pre-Existing Trends

| Period | Gap (Treatment − Control) | Change |
|--------|---------------------------|--------|
| Week 6 (study start) | 0.17 points | — |
| Week 14 (day before switch) | 2.07 points | **+1.90 points** |
| Week 22 (study end) | 3.40 points | +1.33 points |

**The gap grew faster BEFORE the switch than after.** This violates the key assumption needed to attribute the improvement to the new procedure.

### Statistical Analysis

| Model | Estimated Effect | Statistical Significance |
|-------|------------------|--------------------------|
| Standard DiD (ignores pre-trends) | +2.09 points | p < 0.001 |
| DiD controlling for pre-trends | +0.23 points | p = 0.52 (not significant) |

When we account for the fact that treatment branches were already improving faster, **the estimated effect becomes statistically indistinguishable from zero.**

---

## **What This Means**

The 2-point improvement we initially observed is likely **not caused by the new procedure**. Instead, it reflects that:
1. The 425 branches selected to switch early were already on a stronger performance trajectory
2. This pre-existing advantage continued after the switch
3. We cannot separate the procedure's effect from these pre-existing differences

---

## **Recommendations**

### 1. **Pause Cooperative-Wide Rollout**
Do not extend the new procedure to remaining branches until we have clearer evidence of its effectiveness.

### 2. **Conduct Additional Analysis**
- Extend the observation period to see if effects emerge over longer time horizons
- Investigate whether certain types of branches benefit more than others
- Consider a randomized rollout for remaining branches to enable cleaner causal inference

### 3. **Gather Qualitative Evidence**
Survey branch staff about operational impacts (efficiency, workload, patron feedback) that may not be captured in service scores alone.

### 4. **Revisit in 8–12 Weeks**
With additional data, we can reassess whether the procedure shows clearer benefits over time.

---

## **Conclusion**

While we hoped to find evidence supporting the new procedure, the data does not provide confident evidence of improvement. The observed gains appear to reflect pre-existing differences between branches rather than the procedure itself. A cautious approach—pausing rollout and gathering more evidence—is warranted before committing cooperative-wide resources.
