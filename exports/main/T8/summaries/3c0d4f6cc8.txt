# **Memorandum to the Board**

## **Title: Impact of New Shelving and Rotation Procedure on Library Service Scores**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure is associated with higher service scores, but we cannot be certain the procedure itself caused the full improvement.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by **2.2 points** more than control branches. However, these branches were already improving faster before the switch, which may inflate the estimated effect.

**Verdict: Proceed with a phased rollout, not immediate system-wide implementation.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **709 branches** total: 425 switched to the new procedure, 284 kept the old procedure (control group)
- Data covers **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach to isolate the effect of the new procedure

### Main Result
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Estimated effect** | +2.19 service score points |
| **95% Confidence Interval** | [1.62, 2.76] |
| **Statistical significance** | p < 0.001 |
| **Effect size** | 4.4% increase from baseline; 0.21 standard deviations |

### Important Limitation: Pre-Existing Differences
The branches that chose to switch were **already improving faster** than control branches before the procedure changed:

| Period | Treatment Branches | Control Branches | Gap |
|--------|-------------------|------------------|-----|
| Week 6 (start) | 51.25 | 51.75 | -0.50 |
| Week 14 (before switch) | 50.68 | 48.51 | +2.17 |
| Week 22 (end) | 53.57 | 49.38 | +4.19 |

The gap widened by **2.7 points during the pre-period alone**, suggesting treatment branches differed systematically from control branches (e.g., more motivated staff, better management, or different community demographics).

### What This Means
- The **true causal effect is likely smaller than 2.2 points**, possibly substantially so
- However, the post-switch improvement is real and meaningful for patrons at switching branches
- There is **no evidence of harm**—scores did not decline after the switch

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did service scores improve at switching branches? | **Yes** |
| Can we attribute all of the improvement to the new procedure? | **No** |
| Is the new procedure worth rolling out? | **Probably, with caveats** |

**Rationale:** Even if only half of the 2.2-point gain is causal (~1 point, or 2%), this represents a real benefit to patrons with no observed downside. However, the violation of parallel trends means we should not assume every branch will see the full 2.2-point gain.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Phased rollout** rather than immediate system-wide implementation. Switch branches in batches and monitor results.

2. **Randomized assignment** for remaining branches where feasible. This would provide a cleaner causal estimate for future decisions.

3. **Track implementation fidelity.** Branches that implement the procedure more thoroughly may see larger gains.

4. **Set realistic expectations.** Communicate to branch managers that a 1–2 point improvement is a reasonable expectation, not the full 2.2 points observed.

5. **Continue data collection.** Monitor service scores for at least 12 weeks post-switch to assess whether effects persist or fade.

6. **Conduct cost-benefit analysis.** If the new procedure requires additional staff time or resources, weigh the modest service score improvement against those costs.
