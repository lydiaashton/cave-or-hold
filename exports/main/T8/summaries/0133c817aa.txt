# **Board Summary: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure produces a statistically significant but modest improvement in service scores.** Switched branches scored about **2 points higher** (≈4% improvement) than they would have under the old procedure. The evidence supports proceeding with rollout, assuming implementation costs are reasonable.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure; **14 branches** kept the old procedure
- Switch occurred at **week 15**
- Analysis uses a "difference-in-differences" approach to isolate the procedure's effect

### Results

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 49.7 | 50.9 | +1.2 |
| Treated (new procedure) | 53.6 | 56.9 | +3.3 |
| **Net Effect of New Procedure** | | | **+2.0** |

- **Estimated impact: +2.03 points** on the service score index
- **95% confidence interval: 0.5 to 3.6 points** (effect is reliably positive)
- **Statistical significance: p = 0.011**
- **Effect size: 0.20 standard deviations** (small but meaningful)

### Validity Checks
- **Pre-trends were parallel:** Before week 15, both groups followed similar trends, supporting causal interpretation
- **Results are robust:** Effect holds after controlling for branch characteristics, week effects, staffing, and visits

---

## **Considerations for the Board**

| Factor | Evidence |
|--------|----------|
| **Does it work?** | Yes — effect is statistically reliable |
| **How large is the benefit?** | Modest — about 4% improvement |
| **Could it be noise?** | Unlikely — p = 0.011, CI entirely positive |
| **Implementation costs?** | Not analyzed — board must assess |
| **Risk of rollout?** | Low — procedure change is reversible |

---

## **Final Verdict**

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout.**

**Rationale:**
1. **The effect is real** — statistical evidence is clear and robust
2. **The direction is favorable** — no risk of harm detected
3. **Cumulative benefit matters** — a 2-point gain across all 34 branches serves more patrons better
4. **Low reversal cost** — if results disappoint, branches can revert to the old procedure

**Caveat:** This recommendation assumes implementation costs (training, staff time, transition disruption) are not prohibitive. If costs are substantial, the board should conduct a formal cost-benefit analysis before proceeding.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Quantify implementation costs** (training, staff time, temporary disruption)

2. **If proceeding**, develop training materials based on the 20 early-adopter branches' experience

3. **Monitor during rollout** — track service scores to confirm the effect replicates

4. **Evaluate after 6 months** — assess whether improvements are sustained across all branches
