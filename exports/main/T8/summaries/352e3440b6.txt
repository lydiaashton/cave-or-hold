# **Memorandum to the Board**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** [Current Date]  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Do not proceed with systemwide rollout based on current evidence.** The apparent 2-point improvement cannot be confidently attributed to the new procedure.

---

## **Final Verdict**

> **The new procedure has NOT been proven effective.**
>
> The observed improvement reflects pre-existing differences between branches, not the procedure's impact. A randomized controlled trial is required before any systemwide decision.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Critical Issue: Parallel Trends Assumption Violated**

Difference-in-differences analysis requires that treatment and control groups follow similar trends before the intervention. **This assumption is violated in our data:**

| Test | Result |
|------|--------|
| Pre-trend differential | +0.24 points/week (p = 0.0004) |
| Trend over 8 weeks | Would explain 1.91 points of the "effect" |
| Raw DiD estimate | 2.07 points |
| **Adjusted estimate** | **0.16 points** (not statistically significant) |

### **What the Gap Data Actually Shows**

The gap between treated and control branches was widening steadily before week 15:

| Period | Average Gap |
|--------|-------------|
| Week 6 | 1.54 points |
| Week 14 | 3.15 points |
| Week 22 | 4.36 points |

When we extrapolate the pre-treatment trend forward, the **actual post-treatment gaps match what we would have predicted without any intervention**. In 5 of 8 post-treatment weeks, the actual gap was **below** what the pre-trend predicted.

### **Selection Bias Concern**

Branches that switched to the new procedure:
- Had higher service scores throughout the study (average 52.8 vs. 50.9 before switch)
- Were improving faster even before the intervention
- Were likely **not randomly selected** — possibly higher-performing branches were chosen first

This selection bias makes it impossible to isolate the procedure's true effect.

---

## **Recommendations**

| Priority | Action |
|----------|--------|
| **1** | **Pause systemwide rollout** — Current evidence does not support the expected benefit |
| **2** | **Conduct a randomized trial** — Randomly assign remaining branches to treatment/control |
| **3** | **Extend observation period** — Collect 6+ months of data |
| **4** | **Investigate selection criteria** — Understand why certain branches were chosen first |
| **5** | **Gather qualitative feedback** — Survey staff about perceived benefits/challenges |

---

## **What Would Constitute Convincing Evidence?**

A proper evaluation would require:
1. **Random assignment** of branches to treatment and control groups
2. **Demonstrated parallel pre-trends** between groups before intervention
3. **Sustained effect** over multiple months, not just 8 weeks
4. **Replication** across different types of branches (urban/rural, large/small)
