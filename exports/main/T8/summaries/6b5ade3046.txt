# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD OF DIRECTORS**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure  

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure is associated with improved service scores, but we cannot definitively prove causation.** Branches that adopted the new procedure showed a +1.86 point improvement relative to those that didn't. However, because branches were not randomly assigned to switch, and because the two groups showed different trends even before the switch, we cannot rule out that other factors contributed to this difference.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with a phased rollout with careful monitoring, or run a randomized pilot at remaining branches to confirm the effect before full implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Observed Improvement**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Branches using new procedure | 50.69 | 52.45 | +1.76 |
| Branches using old procedure | 48.96 | 48.89 | -0.07 |
| **Observed difference** | | | **+1.86 points** |

- This represents approximately **4% improvement** (0.19 standard deviations)
- The statistical association is strong (p < 0.001)

### **Important Limitations**

1. **Non-random assignment**: Branches that switched started with higher baseline scores (+1.72 points). They may differ in unobserved ways (management quality, staff engagement, community support).

2. **Pre-existing trend differences**: The two groups were already on different trajectories before the switch, which weakens our ability to attribute the post-switch difference to the procedure itself.

3. **Limited follow-up**: Only 8 weeks of post-switch data. We don't know if improvements persist, grow, or fade.

4. **No cost data**: We cannot assess whether the improvement justifies implementation costs.

### **Supporting Evidence**

- Staffing levels and visit volumes did not change differentially, suggesting the switch itself (not concurrent operational changes) drove the result
- The improvement appears to be sustained or growing over the 8-week post-period
- Benefits were observed across branches with different baseline performance levels

---

## **Final Verdict**

**Proceed with phased rollout, but treat this as an ongoing evaluation—not a settled decision.**

The evidence is **suggestive but not conclusive**. Here is our assessment:

| Factor | Assessment |
|--------|------------|
| **Statistical significance** | ✓ Strong (p < 0.001) |
| **Effect size** | △ Modest (4% improvement) |
| **Causal identification** | ⚠ Weakened (parallel trends violated) |
| **Consistency across branches** | ✓ Good (all quartiles benefited) |
| **Durability** | ? Unknown (only 8 weeks of data) |
| **Confounding factors** | ✓ Unlikely (staffing/visits unchanged) |

**Why we lean toward rollout despite uncertainty:**

1. **No evidence of harm**: Scores didn't decline for any group
2. **Low risk**: If the procedure doesn't work, branches can revert
3. **Growing effect**: The benefit appears to increase over time, not fade
4. **Operational plausibility**: Better stock organization logically improves service

**Why we recommend phased rather than full rollout:**

1. **Confirmation bias risk**: Branches that volunteered to switch may be better-run overall
2. **Implementation costs**: Unknown—could outweigh benefits at scale
3. **Opportunity for learning**: A phased approach lets us refine training and identify which branches benefit most

---

## **Recommendation**

**Adopt Option A: Phased Rollout with Monitoring**

- Roll out to 50-100 branches over the next quarter
- Track service scores weekly for each cohort
- Pause and evaluate after each phase before continuing
- Commit to a full re-analysis after 6 months of additional data

This balances potential benefits against implementation risk and builds stronger evidence for the final decision.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Board decision**: Approve phased rollout approach
2. **Cost assessment**: Operations team to estimate implementation costs per branch
3. **Selection criteria**: Determine which branches enter the first phase (consider randomization within phase)
4. **Monitoring plan**: Establish metrics, dashboards, and review schedule
5. **Follow-up analysis**: Re-evaluate after 6 months with additional data
