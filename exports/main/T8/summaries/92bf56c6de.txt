# **BOARD MEMORANDUM**

## **New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure: Impact Evaluation**

---

### **Bottom Line**

The new procedure is associated with **higher service scores**, but the true causal effect is **smaller than initial estimates**. Branches that adopted the procedure were already improving faster before the switch, which inflates the apparent impact. The best evidence suggests a **real effect of 1.0–1.5 points** (2–3% above baseline), not the full 2.2 points. **Proceed with rollout, but with tempered expectations.**

---

### **Key Findings**

**Study Overview**
- 709 branches tracked over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- 425 branches adopted the new procedure in week 15; 284 branches continued with the old procedure
- Service scores average ~50 points (higher is better)

**Results**

| | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Branches with old procedure | 50.5 | 50.9 | +0.4 |
| Branches with new procedure | 51.4 | 54.0 | +2.6 |
| **Naive difference-in-differences** | | | **+2.2** |
| **Adjusted estimate (accounting for pre-trends)** | | | **~1.0–1.5** |

**Critical Limitation**
Branches that chose to switch were already on a stronger improvement trajectory before week 15. This selection bias means the 2.2-point estimate **overstates** the true causal effect. The event study analysis suggests the actual procedure effect emerged in weeks 19–22 at approximately **1.0–1.5 points**.

---

### **Final Verdict**

**The new procedure likely does improve service scores, but the benefit is modest—approximately 1 to 1.5 points, not 2.2 points.** The evidence does not support the level of confidence we initially had. The procedure is worth rolling out, but it is not a transformational change. Expectations should be calibrated accordingly, and we should continue searching for higher-impact improvements to member experience.

---

### **Recommendations**

1. **Approve rollout** – The procedure appears beneficial, just less so than initially believed

2. **Set realistic expectations** – Communicate to branches that improvements of ~1 point are the anticipated outcome

3. **Monitor for 12 weeks post-implementation** – Track whether the effect materializes as expected or diminishes

4. **Investigate what drove early adopters' performance** – Management practices, staffing, or local factors may matter more than the procedure itself

5. **Use randomized assignment for future changes** – To avoid this uncertainty next time

---

### **Decision Requested**

Authorize the operations team to proceed with systemwide rollout, with the understanding that expected service score improvements are **modest (~1–1.5 points)** and should be monitored closely.
