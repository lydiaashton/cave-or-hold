# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Proceed with a phased rollout.** The new procedure shows a **statistically significant but modest improvement** of ~2.5 points on service scores. However, the effect appears to be fading over time, and treatment branches started from a lower baseline. We recommend a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than immediate system-wide adoption.

---

## **Key Findings**

| Metric | Finding |
|--------|---------|
| **Net effect** | +2.5 points (p < 0.001) |
| **Effect size** | 0.27 standard deviations (small-to-medium) |
| **Early post-switch (weeks 15-18)** | +1.55 points |
| **Late post-switch (weeks 19-22)** | +1.06 points |
| **Pre-switch gap** | Treatment branches scored 1.2 points lower |

### What This Means

1. **The effect is real but not large** — Statistically significant, yet modest in practical terms relative to normal score variability.

2. **Possible fading effect** — The advantage decreased in weeks 19-22, with one week showing treatment branches underperforming.

3. **Some catch-up, not just improvement** — Treatment branches were underperforming before the switch; part of the gain may reflect regression to the mean.

4. **No negative consequences detected** — The procedure did not harm service scores, which was a legitimate concern.

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **Did the procedure change service scores?** | Yes — a small but real improvement |
| **Is the effect large enough to matter?** | Marginally — meaningful but not transformative |
| **Should we roll out to all branches?** | Yes, but gradually and with monitoring |
| **Confidence level** | Moderate — effect is clear, persistence is uncertain |

**Verdict: APPROVE PHASED ROLLOUT**

The evidence supports adoption, but not urgency. The board's initial expectation—that this would make no difference—was reasonable given the nature of the change. The data show a **small positive effect**, which is better than no effect and certainly better than a negative one. A cautious, evidence-based rollout minimizes risk while allowing the cooperative to capture the benefit.

---

## **Recommendation**

**Approve a phased rollout, not immediate system-wide adoption.**

| Phase | Action | Success Criteria |
|-------|--------|------------------|
| 1 | Roll out to 20-30 additional branches over 4-6 weeks | Implementation completed |
| 2 | Monitor scores for 8+ weeks | Effect remains ≥1.5 points |
| 3 | If effect holds, proceed to full rollout | Board approval |
| 4 | If effect fades further, reconsider or refine | Board decision |

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Extend the evaluation period** — Collect 8-12 more weeks of data to determine if the effect stabilizes or continues fading.

2. **Investigate why** — Interview switched branches to understand what changed operationally; identify which elements drive the improvement.

3. **Cost assessment** — Quantify implementation costs (training, labor, equipment) to confirm the benefit justifies the expense.

4. **Report back** — Return to the board in one quarter with updated findings and a final rollout recommendation.

---

## **Bottom Line Restated**

The board's initial expectation—that this change would make no difference—was reasonable. The data show a **small positive effect**, not a breakthrough. A measured, evidence-based rollout is warranted rather than either rejecting the procedure or adopting it everywhere immediately.

**Final recommendation: Approve phased rollout with quarterly review.**
