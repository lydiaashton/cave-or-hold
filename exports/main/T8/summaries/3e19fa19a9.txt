# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact Analysis**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure is associated with improved service scores, but causal confidence is limited.** Branches that adopted the new procedure showed a **1.9-point improvement** relative to non-switched branches. However, the switched branches were already improving relative to controls before the change, making it difficult to isolate the procedure's true effect.

**Verdict: Conditionally Recommend Rollout with Monitoring**

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Observed Improvement, but Pre-Trends Are Concerning

| Period | Switched Branches | Non-Switched Branches | Gap |
|--------|------------------|----------------------|-----|
| Before (Weeks 6–14) | 51.64 | 50.18 | 1.46 |
| After (Weeks 15–22) | 52.36 | 49.00 | 3.36 |
| **Change** | **+0.72** | **-1.18** | **+1.90** |

**Important caveat:** The gap between groups was already widening before week 15. Statistical testing confirms a significant pre-existing differential trend (p=0.003). This means the 1.9-point estimate likely **overstates** the true effect of the procedure change.

### 2. The Gap Accelerated After the Switch

| Period | Average Gap (Treatment − Control) |
|--------|----------------------------------|
| Weeks 6–9 (early pre) | 0.84 points |
| Weeks 10–14 (late pre) | 1.76 points |
| Weeks 15–22 (post) | 3.36 points |

The gap roughly doubled after the switch, but it was already growing. A reasonable estimate is that **some portion** of the 1.9-point effect is attributable to the procedure, but the exact magnitude is uncertain.

### 3. Control Group Declined While Treatment Held Steady

Non-switched branches saw service scores drop by 1.18 points after week 15, while switched branches increased by 0.72 points. The new procedure may have **protected branches from decline** even if the absolute improvement is smaller than the raw DiD suggests.

### 4. Analysis Limitations

- **Parallel trends violation:** Treatment and control groups were diverging before the intervention
- **Non-random assignment:** Branches self-selected (or were selected) to switch; they were already higher-performing
- **Short post-period:** Only 8 weeks of data after the change
- **Unobserved confounders:** Other differences between switching and non-switching branches may exist

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did service scores improve at switched branches? | **Yes** – they increased while controls declined |
| Can we confidently attribute this to the new procedure? | **No** – pre-existing trends confound the estimate |
| Is there evidence of harm from the new procedure? | **No** – switched branches performed better on all metrics |
| Should we roll out to all branches immediately? | **No** – evidence is insufficient for full confidence |
| Should we continue exploring this procedure? | **Yes** – the association is positive and worth pursuing |

**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **phased rollout** rather than immediate system-wide adoption. Switch branches in cohorts of 50–100, monitor service scores for 4–6 weeks per cohort, and pause if effects do not materialize. This approach balances the promising signals against the analytical uncertainty.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve phased rollout** to remaining branches in controlled cohorts.

2. **Establish monitoring thresholds:** Define minimum acceptable improvement (e.g., 0.5 points) for each cohort before proceeding.

3. **Investigate why control branches declined:** Understanding this drop may reveal opportunities to improve all branches regardless of procedure.

4. **Plan a randomized evaluation:** For future procedure changes, randomly assign branches to ensure valid causal inference.

5. **Gather qualitative feedback:** Interview staff at switched branches about implementation challenges and perceived benefits.

---

*Analysis prepared using difference-in-differences methodology. The parallel trends assumption was violated (pre-trend p=0.003), limiting causal confidence.*
