# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

---

## **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

**Date:** November 2025  
**Prepared by:** Data Analysis Team  
**Data Period:** Weeks 6–22 (17 weeks of observations)

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The evidence is inconclusive.** The new procedure shows a positive point estimate (+2.0 points, ~4%), but the result is **not statistically significant** (p = 0.09). The 95% confidence interval ranges from **-0.3 to +4.3 points**, meaning we cannot rule out that the procedure has no effect—or even a small negative effect.

**We do not have sufficient evidence to recommend a system-wide rollout.**

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **1. The Numbers**

| Measure | Value |
|---------|-------|
| Branches studied | 34 (20 switched, 14 did not) |
| Observations | 578 branch-week records |
| Estimated effect | **+2.0 points** on service score |
| Statistical significance | p = 0.09 (not significant at p < 0.05) |
| 95% Confidence Interval | **-0.3 to +4.3 points** |
| Probability effect is positive | ~95% (but magnitude uncertain) |

### **2. What Changed**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 52.2 | 52.5 | +0.3 |
| Non-switched branches | 51.9 | 50.3 | **-1.6** |

The apparent benefit comes from switched branches **holding steady** while control branches declined. We do not know why control branches declined (seasonal factors? unrelated operational issues?).

### **3. Why the Evidence Is Weak**

| Issue | Impact |
|-------|--------|
| **Underpowered study** | Our sample can reliably detect effects of 2.3+ points; observed effect is 2.0 |
| **Wide confidence interval** | True effect could be zero or slightly negative |
| **Only 8 weeks post-switch** | Too short to assess sustainability or longer-term impacts |
| **Control group decline** | Unclear if this is normal variation or a separate problem |

### **4. What We Can Say With Confidence**

✓ Treatment and control branches had **parallel trends before the switch** (valid comparison)  
✓ The new procedure did **not harm** service scores in switching branches  
✓ Staffing levels remain an important independent driver of service quality  

### **5. What We Cannot Say**

✗ Whether the procedure **causes** improvement  
✗ Whether the effect would **replicate** at other branches  
✗ Whether the effect would **persist** beyond 8 weeks  

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did the procedure improve service scores? | **Uncertain** — point estimate is positive, but not statistically significant |
| Should we roll out to all branches now? | **No** — evidence is too weak to justify system-wide change |
| Should we abandon the procedure? | **No** — no evidence of harm; potential benefit exists |
| What should we do? | **Extend the study** with more data before deciding |

---

## **RECOMMENDED NEXT STEPS**

| Priority | Action | Timeline |
|----------|--------|----------|
| 1 | **Switch the remaining 14 branches** — treat as extended pilot, not rollout | Within 4 weeks |
| 2 | **Continue weekly tracking** for all 34 branches | 12+ additional weeks |
| 3 | **Investigate control branch decline** — was this procedure-related or external? | Within 6 weeks |
| 4 | **Return to Board** with updated analysis once we have 20+ weeks post-switch data | ~4 months |
| 5 | **Make final rollout decision** based on stronger evidence | After updated analysis |

---

## **WHAT WOULD CHANGE OUR MIND?**

We would recommend full rollout if future data shows:

- **Statistical significance** (p < 0.05) with similar effect size
- **Narrower confidence interval** that excludes zero (e.g., +1.0 to +3.5)
- **Sustained effect** over 20+ weeks, not just 8 weeks
- **Consistent effects** across different branch types and sizes

---

## **RISKS OF ACTING NOW**

| Scenario | If We Roll Out | If We Wait |
|----------|----------------|------------|
| Procedure works (+2 pts) | Gain benefit sooner | Delayed benefit |
| Procedure has no effect | Wasted implementation effort | No loss |
| Procedure has negative effect | System-wide harm | Avoided harm |
| External factors caused control decline | Misattributed success | Better understanding |

**Waiting has low cost and high value.** The procedure can be implemented later if evidence strengthens. Acting now risks system-wide change based on uncertain evidence.

---

## **CONCLUSION**

This analysis demonstrates **responsible stewardship of evidence**. The new procedure shows promise, but promise is not proof. The Board is asked to:

1. **Approve continued monitoring** of all 34 branches
2. **Authorize switching the remaining 14 branches** as part of an extended evaluation
3. **Defer the rollout decision** until we have data sufficient to detect effects of this magnitude

A hasty decision risks implementing a change that may not work. A patient decision protects the cooperative while preserving the option to adopt if evidence strengthens.
