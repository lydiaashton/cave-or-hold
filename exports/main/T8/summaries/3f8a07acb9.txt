# **Board Summary: New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Contrary to expectations, the new procedure did change service scores—and for the better.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.3 points** (about **5% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant. 

However, since this outcome was unexpected, we recommend **a measured rollout with continued monitoring** rather than an immediate system-wide mandate, to confirm the effect holds across different branch contexts.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **150 branches** total: 90 adopted the new procedure, 60 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data**: Weeks 6–14 before the switch, Weeks 15–22 after
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach to isolate the procedure's effect

### **Main Result**
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 48.8 | 51.0 | +2.2 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 48.1 | 52.6 | +4.5 |
| **Effect of new procedure** | | | **+2.3** |

- The **2.3-point improvement** is statistically significant (p < 0.001)
- **95% confidence interval**: 1.1 to 3.7 points
- This represents roughly a **5% improvement** over the baseline service score

### **Why This Was Unexpected**

The service score index was designed to measure patron-facing service quality, not back-office operations like rotation and shelving. We anticipated the procedural change would be neutral—neither helping nor harming the metrics patrons experience. The positive effect suggests either:

1. **Staff efficiency gains** from the new procedure freed up time for patron interaction
2. **Improved material availability** from better shelving practices led to higher patron satisfaction
3. **Unmeasured factors** coincided with the switch at treatment branches

### **Evidence Quality**

**Strengths:**
- Clear control group for comparison
- Similar pre-switch trends between groups (supports valid comparison)
- Effect is consistent across all 8 post-switch weeks
- Holds after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

**Limitations:**
- Only 8 weeks of post-switch data (relatively short-term)
- Cannot pinpoint the exact mechanism driving the improvement
- Some branches may have implemented the procedure differently

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did the new procedure change service scores? | **Yes** |
| Was the change positive or negative? | **Positive** |
| Is the evidence statistically reliable? | **Yes** (p = 0.0002) |
| Should we roll out to all branches? | **Yes, with monitoring** |

**The new procedure improved service scores by a meaningful margin with high statistical confidence. The unexpected nature of this result warrants a phased rollout to verify the effect generalizes, but there is no statistical reason to delay adoption.**

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Phased rollout**: Expand the procedure to 15–20 additional branches over the next 2–3 months, rather than all 60 at once.

2. **Enhanced monitoring**: Track service scores weekly during the phased rollout to confirm the effect replicates.

3. **Investigate the mechanism**: Survey staff at treatment branches to understand *how* the new procedure may have improved patron experience (e.g., more time for assistance, faster item retrieval, better stock availability).

4. **Document best practices**: Capture specific implementation details from high-performing treatment branches to ensure consistent rollout.

5. **Re-evaluate in 6 months**: After broader implementation, conduct a follow-up analysis to confirm sustained improvement and assess whether the effect size holds.

---
