# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Analysis Period:** Weeks 6–22 (17 weeks)

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure is associated with improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure gained approximately **2 points** relative to branches that kept the old procedure. The effect is statistically significant. However, because switching branches were already higher-performing at baseline, some uncertainty remains about whether the procedure alone caused the improvement.

**Recommendation: Proceed with rollout, but monitor closely.**

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **1. Study Overview**
- **150 branches:** 90 adopted the new procedure, 60 retained the old (control)
- **17 weeks:** 9 weeks before switch (weeks 6–14), 8 weeks after (weeks 15–22)
- **Method:** Difference-in-differences comparing score changes between groups

### **2. Service Score Results**

| Group | Before | After | Change |
|-------|--------|-------|--------|
| New Procedure (90 branches) | 51.7 | 53.5 | **+1.8** |
| Old Procedure (60 branches) | 50.2 | 50.1 | **-0.1** |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+2.0 points** |

### **3. Statistical Evidence**
| Metric | Value | Interpretation |
|--------|-------|----------------|
| Estimated effect | +1.97 points | Meaningful improvement |
| p-value | 0.001 | Highly statistically significant |
| 95% Confidence Interval | 0.80 to 3.14 | Effect is almost certainly positive |
| Pre-trend test | p = 0.96 | ✓ Groups tracked similarly before switch |
| Baseline difference | +1.42 points | ⚠ Switched branches started higher |

### **4. What This Means**
- **Good news:** The gap between switched and control branches widened after the switch, consistent with a real treatment effect
- **Caveat:** Switched branches were already outperforming control branches before the procedure change, suggesting non-random selection
- **Bottom line:** The evidence points to a genuine improvement, but it's not as definitive as a randomized trial would be

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **Did service scores improve?** | **Yes** — switched branches gained ~2 points relative to control |
| **Is this statistically reliable?** | **Yes** — p = 0.001, narrow confidence interval |
| **Can we be certain it's causal?** | **Mostly** — parallel pre-trends support causality, but baseline differences introduce some uncertainty |
| **Should we roll out system-wide?** | **Yes, with monitoring** — the likely benefit outweighs the risk |

**Confidence Level: Moderate-to-High**

The statistical evidence is strong, and the pre-trend analysis supports a causal interpretation. The main limitation is that branches were not randomly assigned to switch, so we cannot completely rule out that motivated or better-resourced branches both chose to switch *and* improved for other reasons. However, the fact that control branches showed no improvement while switched branches did suggests the procedure itself contributed to the gain.
