# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence suggests the new procedure improved service scores, but with important caveats.** Branches that adopted the new procedure showed a **2.2-point increase** in service scores. However, because switching branches were underperforming before the change, some improvement may reflect natural recovery rather than the procedure itself. We recommend a **phased rollout with rigorous monitoring** rather than immediate systemwide implementation.

**Confidence level: Moderate** (not high)

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **Did service scores improve after the switch?** | **Yes** — clearly and significantly |
| **Was the new procedure the sole cause?** | **Uncertain** — pre-existing trends complicate attribution |
| **Should we roll out to all branches?** | **Yes, but phased** — proceed with caution and monitoring |
| **Is this a guaranteed success?** | **No** — effects may be smaller in non-struggling branches |

**Decision Recommendation:** **APPROVE PHASED ROLLOUT**

**Rationale:** The timing of improvements (concentrated after week 15), the magnitude of effects (7 of 8 post-switch weeks significant), and the practical importance of service quality improvements outweigh the uncertainty from pre-trend violations. However, the non-random selection of pilot branches means we should verify effects continue as we expand to different types of branches.

**Risk if Wrong:** If the procedure is less effective than estimated, the cost is primarily staff training time—not irreversible harm. This favors proceeding while monitoring.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Estimated Effect: +2.16 Points, But Interpret with Caution

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Raw difference-in-differences | **+2.16 points** |
| 95% Confidence interval | +1.60 to +2.71 |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 |

**Important caveat:** This estimate assumes treatment and control branches would have followed parallel trends without the intervention. The data suggests this assumption may not fully hold.

### 2. Pre-Trend Concern

| Period | Treatment vs. Control | Statistically Significant? |
|--------|----------------------|---------------------------|
| Weeks 6-8 | Treatment **behind** by 1.3–2.4 points | **Yes** |
| Weeks 9-14 | No clear difference | No |
| Weeks 15-22 | Treatment **ahead** by 1.4–2.2 points | **Yes** |

The early underperformance of treatment branches suggests they were **selected for the pilot because they were struggling**. This creates uncertainty about how much improvement is due to the procedure versus natural recovery.

### 3. Timing Supports (But Doesn't Prove) Causal Effect

- Week 15 (first week after switch): Effect is positive but not yet significant (+0.73)
- Weeks 16-22: Effects are consistently positive and significant (+1.39 to +2.22)
- The **step-change pattern** around week 15 is more consistent with a treatment effect than gradual regression to the mean

### 4. Study Limitations

| Issue | Impact |
|-------|--------|
| Non-parallel pre-trends | May overstate treatment effect |
| Non-random assignment | Selection bias possible |
| 8-week post-period | Cannot assess long-term sustainability |
| No randomization | Cannot rule out all confounding |

---

## **Recommendations**

### Proceed, But Cautiously

1. **Approve phased rollout** rather than immediate systemwide implementation
   - Start with 50–100 branches per quarter
   - Prioritize branches similar to pilot participants

2. **Build evaluation into the rollout**
   - Track service scores weekly for each implementing branch
   - Compare against matched control branches that haven't yet switched
   - Establish clear success criteria (e.g., +1.5 points within 6 weeks)

3. **Set a review point**
   - After 100 branches have implemented, pause and evaluate
   - If effects are weaker than pilot, reconsider full rollout

4. **Document implementation conditions**
   - Track staffing levels, training quality, and branch characteristics
   - Identify which branches benefit most (and least)

### Do Not Do

- ❌ Do not assume the 2.16-point effect will replicate exactly
- ❌ Do not skip monitoring—this is not a guaranteed success
- ❌ Do not implement everywhere simultaneously (limits learning)

---

## **Conclusion**

The new procedure is **likely beneficial**, but the evidence is not as definitive as we initially hoped. The pilot branches were struggling before the change, which complicates interpretation. A cautious, monitored rollout allows us to confirm the effect while minimizing risk if the procedure proves less effective in different contexts.

**Recommended decision:** Approve phased rollout with built-in evaluation milestones.
