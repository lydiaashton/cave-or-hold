# **Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative
**Date:** November 2026

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores by approximately 1.8 points (4%), contradicting the expectation of no effect.** The statistical evidence is strong and the effect is positive. Pending reasonable implementation costs, we recommend system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did the procedure change service scores? | **Yes** – increased by ~1.8 points |
| Is this statistically reliable? | **Yes** – p < 0.001, 95% CI [1.1, 2.6] |
| Is the effect large? | **Modest** – about 4% of average score |
| Are there validity concerns? | **No** – pre-trends were parallel |

### **Evidence Summary**
- **150 branches** studied over 17 weeks (90 switched, 60 did not)
- Switched branches improved from 48.9 → 50.1 after adopting the new procedure
- Control branches remained flat (50.4 → 49.8)
- No significant differences in trends before the switch (p=0.61)

---

## **Final Verdict**

**Recommend rollout to all branches.**

**Rationale:**
1. **The effect is real** – The data provides strong evidence (p < 0.001) that the new procedure improves service scores. The prior expectation of "no difference" is not supported.
2. **The effect is positive** – There is no risk of harm; all plausible values in the confidence interval represent improvement.
3. **The evidence is credible** – Parallel pre-trends and consistent results across model specifications support a causal interpretation.
4. **The magnitude is meaningful** – A 4% improvement in service quality is worthwhile if implementation costs are modest.

**Condition:** This recommendation assumes implementation costs (training, labor, equipment) are not prohibitive relative to the service quality benefit.
