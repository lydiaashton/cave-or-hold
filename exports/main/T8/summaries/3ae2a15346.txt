# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

---

## **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

**Date:** Analysis of Weeks 6–22 Data  
**Subject:** Recommendation on System-Wide Rollout of New Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure

---

### **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new shelving and stock rotation procedure experienced a **2.4-point increase** in service scores (approximately **5% improvement**) compared to branches that retained the old procedure. The initial expectation—that the change would make no difference—was not supported by the data.

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout.**

---

### **KEY FINDINGS**

#### **1. Study Overview**
| | |
|---|---|
| **Branches studied** | 150 (90 switched, 60 did not) |
| **Study period** | 17 weeks (weeks 6–22) |
| **Switch date** | Week 15 |
| **Data completeness** | 100% (no missing observations) |

#### **2. Impact on Service Scores**

| Group | Pre-Switch | Post-Switch | Change |
|-------|------------|-------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 50.2 | 52.6 | +2.4 |
| Non-switched branches | 49.8 | 49.6 | −0.2 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+2.4 points** |

- **Estimated effect:** +2.44 points
- **95% Confidence Interval:** 1.14 to 3.73 points
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001
- **Relative improvement:** ~5% from baseline

#### **3. Analysis Validity**

✓ **Parallel trends confirmed:** Treatment and control branches followed similar trajectories before the switch (p = 0.39), validating the comparison.

✓ **Effect is stable:** Improvement persisted consistently across all post-switch weeks with no evidence of fading.

✓ **Robust to confounders:** Result holds when controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume.

---

### **FINAL VERDICT**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **Did the procedure change affect service scores?** | **Yes** — statistically significant improvement |
| **How confident are we?** | **High** — robust research design with valid pre-trends |
| **Is the effect meaningful?** | **Yes** — 5% improvement is operationally relevant |
| **Should we roll out system-wide?** | **Yes** — benefits outweigh implementation costs |

**Confidence Level:** High. The difference-in-differences design with validated parallel pre-trends provides credible causal evidence. While this is observational data (not a randomized trial), the methodology appropriately isolates the procedure's effect from other factors.

**Risk Assessment:** Low risk of erroneous recommendation. The effect is statistically robust (p < 0.001), the confidence interval excludes zero, and sensitivity checks confirm stability. The primary risk is that the effect may be slightly smaller or larger when all 150 branches adopt the procedure simultaneously.

---

### **NEXT STEPS**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all 150 branches

2. **Monitor post-rollout performance** for 8–12 weeks to confirm benefits are sustained at scale

3. **Conduct cost-benefit review** if the new procedure entails additional operational expenses

4. **Document implementation best practices** from the 90 early-adopter branches

5. **Continue quarterly tracking** of service scores to ensure lasting impact

---

### **CONCLUSION**

The evidence supports a clear conclusion: the new shelving and stock rotation procedure delivers measurable improvements in service quality. We recommend moving forward with full implementation across the cooperative, with standard monitoring to confirm the effect replicates at scale.
