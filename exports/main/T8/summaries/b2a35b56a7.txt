# **MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The data suggest the new procedure **likely improves service scores** (95% probability of a positive effect), with a best estimate of **+2.2 points (~4% improvement)**. However, we cannot rule out a negligible effect at conventional statistical standards. 

**Recommendation:** If implementation costs are low, proceed with rollout. If costs are significant, gather more data before deciding.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Overview**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (Weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Switch implemented at **Week 15**

### **Service Score Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| **Treatment (Switched)** | 51.72 | 52.86 | **+1.14** |
| **Control (No Switch)** | 53.35 | 52.40 | **-0.95** |
| **Net Effect** | — | — | **+2.09** |

### **What the Statistics Tell Us**

| Metric | Value | Interpretation |
|--------|-------|----------------|
| Best estimate | +2.21 points | ~4% improvement |
| P-value | 0.094 | Not significant at 5% level |
| 95% Confidence Interval | -0.38 to +4.79 | True effect likely in this range |
| Probability effect is positive | 95.3% | Only 4.7% chance of negative effect |

**Bottom line on evidence:** The data lean positive, but we lack the precision to be fully confident.

### **Validity Checks**
✓ **Pre-trends were parallel:** Treatment and control groups had similar trends before the switch.

✓ **No evidence of harm:** Control branches declined slightly (-0.95 points); treatment branches improved (+1.14 points).

---

## **Final Verdict**

**PROCEED WITH CAUTIOUS ROLLOUT**

The evidence supports adopting the new procedure, with three important caveats:

1. **The effect is likely real but modest** – Expect ~4% service score improvement, not a transformation
2. **Monitor closely** – Track scores for 12+ weeks post-implementation at each new branch
3. **Be prepared to adjust** – If effects don't materialize at new branches, pause and investigate

**Rationale:** 
- 95% probability of positive effect
- No evidence of harm to any branch
- Control branches declined while treatment branches improved
- Low decision risk if implementation costs are modest

**What would change this verdict:**
- If implementation costs exceed the value of a 4% service score improvement
- If early rollout branches show no improvement after 8–12 weeks
- If operational challenges emerge that weren't apparent in the pilot branches

---

## **Recommendations**

### **Implementation Plan**

| Phase | Action | Timeline |
|-------|--------|----------|
| 1 | Roll out to 5–10 additional branches | Next 30 days |
| 2 | Monitor service scores weekly | Ongoing |
| 3 | Evaluate results and decide on full rollout | 90 days |

### **Success Metrics**
- Service score improvement of +1.5 points or more at new branches
- No increase in operational complaints or costs
- Staff feedback indicates procedure is sustainable

---

## **Limitations**

- **Short post-implementation window** (8 weeks)
- **Modest sample size** (34 branches)
- **Cannot rule out small effects** (true effect could be as low as -0.4 points)

---

## **Conclusion**

The new procedure shows **promising but inconclusive** evidence of improvement. The board should weigh the **likely 4% service score gain** against **implementation costs and risks**. If costs are modest, the evidence supports moving forward. If costs are substantial, additional data would reduce decision risk.
