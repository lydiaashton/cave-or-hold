# **Memorandum to the Board of Directors**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure likely improved service scores, but we recommend a phased rollout rather than immediate system-wide implementation.**

Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by **2.0 points** relative to branches that kept the old procedure. However, branches that chose to switch were already improving slightly faster before the change, which introduces uncertainty about how much of the gain is due to the procedure itself versus branch characteristics.

---

## **Key Findings**

### What We Tested
- **709 branches** tracked over 17 weeks (February–June 2026)
- **425 branches** switched to the new procedure in week 15; **284 branches** continued with the old procedure
- We compared how service scores changed before and after the switch

### What We Found

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Branches keeping old procedure | 49.5 | 49.0 | −0.5 |
| Branches using new procedure | 50.7 | 52.2 | +1.5 |
| **Net effect** | | | **+2.0** |

- The **2.0-point improvement** is statistically significant (p < 0.001)
- We are 95% confident the true effect lies between **1.4 and 2.5 points**
- The effect persisted after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### Important Caveat

**Branches self-selected into the new procedure.** Treatment branches were already scoring higher and improving slightly faster than control branches before the switch. This suggests more motivated or better-resourced branches may have opted in first. The 2-point estimate could be partially inflated by this selection bias.

| Week | Treatment-Control Gap |
|------|----------------------|
| Week 6 (start) | 0.55 points |
| Week 14 (pre-switch) | 2.47 points |
| Week 15+ (post-switch) | 3.11 points (average) |

The gap widened gradually before the switch, then jumped and remained elevated after—consistent with a real treatment effect, but not definitive proof.

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did service scores improve in switching branches? | **Yes** — clearly |
| Can we attribute all improvement to the new procedure? | **Uncertain** — selection bias may explain part of the effect |
| Is the procedure worth expanding? | **Yes** — even if half the effect is real, it's beneficial |
| Should we roll out to all branches immediately? | **No** — validate with a controlled expansion first |

**Confidence Level: Moderate**

The evidence is promising but not conclusive. A phased rollout allows us to confirm the effect while minimizing risk.

---

## **Recommendation**

**Approve a phased rollout with evaluation milestones:**

| Phase | Action | Timeline | Success Criterion |
|-------|--------|----------|-------------------|
| 1 | Expand to 50–100 additional branches | Weeks 1–8 | Service scores improve by ≥1.0 point |
| 2 | Evaluate results; adjust training if needed | Weeks 9–12 | Effect confirmed in new branches |
| 3 | Complete system-wide rollout | Weeks 13–20 | — |
| 4 | Continue monitoring | Ongoing | No degradation in scores |

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Board decision:** Approve phased rollout approach
2. **Operations:** Identify 50–100 branches for Phase 1 (consider random selection to reduce bias in future evaluation)
3. **Training:** Prepare materials and schedule for Phase 1 branches
4. **Data Team:** Set up monitoring dashboard to track service scores weekly during rollout
5. **Review:** Return to Board in 12 weeks with Phase 1 results and recommendation for Phase 2

---

## **Appendix: Analysis Details**

- **Data:** 12,053 weekly observations from 709 branches
- **Method:** Difference-in-differences regression with branch fixed effects
- **Standard errors:** Clustered at the branch level
- **Controls:** Weekly staffing index and visits index
- **Limitation:** Non-random assignment to treatment introduces potential selection bias
