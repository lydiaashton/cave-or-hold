# **Impact of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure on Service Scores**

**To:** Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2025  

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure is **associated with modestly higher service scores** (approximately +1.8 points, p=0.003), but the evidence has important limitations. The effect is **not consistent across all post-switch weeks**, and pre-existing differences between branches complicate interpretation. 

**Recommendation:** Consider a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than immediate system-wide adoption.

---

## **Key Findings**

### What the Data Show
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 50.41 | 49.81 | −0.60 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 48.88 | 50.12 | +1.24 |
| **Estimated Effect** | | | **+1.84** |

### Important Caveats
- **Only half the post-switch weeks** (4 of 8) show statistically significant improvements
- **Treatment branches started lower** than control branches and the gap closed somewhat, but not uniformly
- **Pre-existing differences** between the groups make it difficult to isolate the procedure's true effect
- The 1.8-point improvement represents about **3.7% of baseline scores**—modest in practical terms

---

## **Interpretation**

The data suggest the new procedure **may improve** service scores, but we cannot be confident this represents a causal effect. The branches that adopted the new procedure were already different from those that didn't, and the improvement was not sustained consistently across all post-switch weeks.

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Criterion | Assessment |
|-----------|------------|
| **Statistical significance** | ✓ Yes (p=0.003) |
| **Consistent effect across weeks** | ✗ No (only 4 of 8 weeks significant) |
| **Parallel pre-trends** | ✗ No (assumption violated) |
| **Practical significance** | △ Modest (~4% improvement) |
| **Risk of harm** | ✓ None detected |

**Verdict: PROMISING BUT NOT CONCLUSIVE**

The board expected no difference. We found a positive signal, but one that falls short of the evidence standard typically required for system-wide policy changes. The new procedure does not appear harmful, and it may provide modest benefits. However, the analytical limitations mean we cannot confidently attribute the improvement to the procedure itself.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Do not proceed with immediate full rollout**—evidence is promising but not definitive

2. **Expand the pilot**: Invite additional branches to adopt the procedure while continuing to track outcomes

3. **Collect more data**: Extend the observation period to determine if effects stabilize or diminish over time

4. **Investigate heterogeneity**: Identify which types of branches benefit most (size, location, staffing levels)

5. **Reassess in 3 months**: With more data and a longer observation window, revisit the rollout decision

---

**Bottom line for the Board:** The new procedure shows promise, but the evidence does not yet justify a confident system-wide rollout. A cautious, data-driven expansion is recommended.
