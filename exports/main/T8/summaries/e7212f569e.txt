# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** December 2025  
**RE:** Impact of New Branch Shelving and Rotation Procedure on Service Scores

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence suggests the new procedure improved service scores, but we cannot be fully certain.** Branches that adopted the new procedure showed a 1.9-point greater improvement than control branches. However, these branches were already trending upward before the switch, which makes it difficult to isolate the procedure's true effect.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with caution. Consider a controlled pilot expansion to 50-100 additional branches before system-wide rollout, with rigorous monitoring to confirm the effect.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Observed Improvement
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches (425) | 50.4 | 52.4 | +2.1 |
| Control branches (284) | 50.1 | 50.3 | +0.2 |
| **Observed difference** | | | **+1.9** |

### Important Caveat: Pre-Existing Trends
The switched branches were already improving relative to control branches *before* the procedure change:

| Weeks Before Switch | Relative Difference |
|---------------------|---------------------|
| 8-9 weeks before | -0.9 to -0.1 points |
| 4-5 weeks before | -0.2 to +0.3 points |
| 2-3 weeks before | +0.8 to +1.2 points |

This upward trend means **some portion of the 1.9-point effect may reflect pre-existing differences**, not the procedure itself. The true effect could be smaller—or the trend could have continued regardless of the switch.

### Encouraging Signals
- Post-switch improvements (+1.4 to +3.2 points) were larger than pre-switch trends
- Effects grew over time, consistent with staff learning the new procedure
- Results held after controlling for staffing and visit volumes

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Do not proceed with immediate system-wide rollout**

2. **Expand pilot to 50-100 additional branches** selected to match control branch characteristics

3. **Collect 8-12 weeks of post-switch data** from new pilot branches before making rollout decision

4. **Investigate why switched branches were trending upward** before the change—were they systematically different (larger, urban, better-staffed)?

5. **Return to Board in Q2 2026** with expanded pilot results for final rollout decision

---

## **Final Verdict**

**Conditionally Favorable — Proceed with Expanded Pilot**

The data shows a positive association between the new procedure and service scores, but selection bias prevents us from claiming definitive causation. The switched branches appear to have been chosen *because* they were already improving, which inflates our estimate of the procedure's effect.

**Most likely scenario:** The procedure has a real but modest positive effect (approximately 0.5–1.0 points rather than the observed 1.9 points). This would still represent meaningful improvement across 709 branches, warranting continued investment—but not at the cost of system-wide disruption without further validation.

**Decision threshold:** If the expanded pilot confirms effects of 1.0+ points with flat pre-trends, proceed with full rollout. If effects are below 0.5 points or pre-trend concerns persist, reconsider the procedure or investigate which branch types benefit most.

---

*The switched branches may have been selected for the new procedure because they were already improving or had characteristics associated with improvement. This selection bias limits our confidence in the causal effect.*
