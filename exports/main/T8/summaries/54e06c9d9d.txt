# **Board Memorandum**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Office  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Final Verdict**

**Proceed with caution. The evidence is suggestive but not conclusive.**

The new procedure shows promise in the expected direction, but the data does not provide statistical confirmation. This is not a failure of the procedure—it is a limitation of the study design. The board faces a choice between waiting for stronger evidence (delaying potential benefits) or acting on incomplete evidence (accepting some risk).

**Our recommendation:** Proceed with the rollout, but treat it as a **conditional decision** pending verification. Commit to a formal re-evaluation in Q2 2027 with clear success criteria. If the additional data does not confirm a positive effect, be prepared to reconsider.

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The data does not confirm that the new procedure improves service scores.** While branches using the new procedure showed a 2.4-point average improvement, this result is **not statistically distinguishable from chance**. The study lacked sufficient data to detect the modest effect we expected. 

**Recommendation:** Either (a) extend the pilot to gather more evidence before a full rollout, or (b) if proceeding with rollout, acknowledge this decision rests on factors beyond the current service score evidence.

---

## **What the Data Shows**

| Finding | Result |
|---------|--------|
| Observed improvement | +2.38 points |
| 95% Confidence Interval | -0.79 to +5.54 points |
| Statistical significance | p = 0.14 (not significant) |
| Probability effect is truly positive | ~93% |

**Key limitation:** With 578 observations, our study could only reliably detect effects larger than **4.5 points**. The 2.4-point improvement we observed falls below this threshold, meaning we cannot confidently distinguish it from random variation.

---

## **Why We Got an Inconclusive Result**

1. **Small sample size:** 34 branches over 17 weeks provides limited statistical power
2. **Modest effect size:** If the true improvement is around 2 points (as observed), we would need approximately **3.5 times more data** to confirm it
3. **Natural variation:** Service scores fluctuate week-to-week, making modest improvements harder to detect

---

## **Options for the Board**

| Option | Pros | Cons |
|--------|------|------|
| **A. Extend the pilot** (12+ more weeks) | Builds stronger evidence before committing | Delays potential benefits |
| **B. Proceed with rollout** | Captures benefits sooner if effect is real | Risk of implementing an ineffective change |
| **C. Roll out with embedded evaluation** | Balances speed with learning | Requires ongoing monitoring commitment |

---

## **Recommendation**

**Option C: Proceed with rollout while committing to rigorous ongoing evaluation.** 

Given the 93% probability the effect is positive and the operational momentum behind the change, a full rollout is defensible. However, the board should:

1. **Set a review date** (Q2 2027) with a commitment to re-evaluate the evidence
2. **Establish a minimum success threshold** (e.g., sustained 2+ point improvement)
3. **Be prepared to adjust** if additional data does not confirm the expected benefit

---

## **Bottom Line Restated**

We expected this procedure to improve service scores. The data is **consistent with that expectation** but does not **confirm** it. Proceeding now is a reasonable bet, but it remains a bet—one the board should plan to verify with additional evidence.
