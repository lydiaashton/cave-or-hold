# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure  

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure should be rolled out system-wide, but with tempered expectations.**

The procedure did not improve service scores in an absolute sense. Instead, it **protected branches from decline** during a period when scores fell system-wide. Branches using the new procedure maintained their performance (~51 points) while control branches dropped 2.4 points. The net benefit is **2.3 points** (p < 0.001), but this reflects avoided deterioration rather than genuine improvement.

**Implication:** The new procedure is worth adopting as a defensive measure, but the board should not expect it to raise service scores above pre-existing levels.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **1. Study Overview**
- **150 branches** participated: 90 adopted the new procedure, 60 retained the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** design, the standard method for evaluating policy changes with treatment and control groups

### **2. Impact on Service Scores**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 51.81 | 49.45 | −2.36 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 51.24 | 51.19 | −0.05 |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | | | **+2.31** |

- **Treatment effect: +2.31 points** (p < 0.001)
- Effect remains significant (+2.21 points, p = 0.001) when controlling for staffing levels and visitor traffic
- Baseline average service score across all branches: 51.0

### **3. Validation of Results**
- **Pre-trends examined:** Treatment branches showed a slight downward drift relative to control before the switch (−0.24 points/week, p=0.013), but week-by-week differences were not statistically significant
- **Sustained effect:** The positive impact persisted across all 8 post-switch weeks, with treatment branches outperforming control branches by 1.5–3.0 points weekly
- **Key insight:** The new procedure appears to have halted a deteriorating trend rather than producing absolute gains

### **4. System-Wide Context**
- Control branches experienced a 2.4-point decline after week 15, suggesting system-wide pressures (seasonal, operational, or external factors)
- The new procedure buffered switching branches against these pressures

---

## **RECOMMENDATION**

**Approve system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all 60 branches still using the old method.

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

1. **Implement rollout** to remaining 60 branches within the next quarter

2. **Monitor transition** over 8–12 weeks post-implementation to confirm effects persist at scale

3. **Investigate system-wide decline:** The 2.4-point drop at non-switching branches warrants separate investigation. The new procedure is a protective measure, not a solution to underlying issues

4. **Set realistic expectations:** Communicate to stakeholders that the procedure helps maintain service quality during challenging periods, not that it will raise scores above historical levels

5. **Capture best practices** from the 90 early-adopter branches to inform training and support smooth implementation

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did the new procedure change service scores? | **Yes** — by +2.3 points relative to control |
| Did it improve scores in absolute terms? | **No** — switching branches stayed flat (~51 points) |
| Is it worth rolling out? | **Yes** — it protects against decline |
| Will it solve service quality challenges? | **No** — additional interventions needed |

**Decision:** Proceed with rollout as a defensive measure while investigating root causes of system-wide score declines.
