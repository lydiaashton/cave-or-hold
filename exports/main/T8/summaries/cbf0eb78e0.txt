# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

---

## **Evaluation of New Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

**To:** Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Subject:** Recommendation on System-Wide Rollout

---

### **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure helped branches maintain service levels during a period of decline, but did not produce substantial improvement.** We recommend rollout with tempered expectations—the benefit is real but modest, representing protection against decline rather than transformational gains.

---

### **FINAL VERDICT**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **Did the new procedure change service scores?** | **Yes** — statistically significant effect (p = 0.001) |
| **Should we roll out to all branches?** | **Yes** — with realistic expectations |
| **Will this transform our service quality?** | **No** — benefit is defensive, not transformative |
| **Confidence in this recommendation?** | **Moderate-High** — sound methodology, modest effect size |

**Decision:** ✅ **Proceed with system-wide rollout**

**Rationale:** The 2.2-point treatment effect is statistically robust and the 95% confidence interval (0.89 to 3.51) excludes zero. Even a modest protective effect is valuable given the low cost of procedure adoption relative to potential service degradation. However, the board should pursue additional initiatives if substantial service score improvements are desired.

---

### **KEY FINDINGS**

**1. Statistically Significant but Modest Effect**

| Group | Before | After | Change |
|-------|--------|-------|--------|
| Treatment branches (new procedure) | 50.30 | 50.56 | **+0.26** |
| Control branches (old procedure) | 48.88 | 46.88 | **−2.00** |
| **Net treatment effect** | | | **+2.20** |

- **95% confidence interval:** 0.89 to 3.51 points (entirely positive)
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.001
- **Practical significance:** ~4% relative to baseline

**2. Critical Context: Protection, Not Improvement**

The treatment effect is driven primarily by control branches declining while treatment branches held steady. In absolute terms:
- Treatment branches: essentially **flat** (+0.26 points)
- Only **51% of treatment branches** saw any improvement (vs. 33% of control branches)

**3. Why This Still Matters**

During the study period, something caused system-wide service scores to drop. Branches using the new procedure were **resilient** to this pressure. If similar pressures recur, the new procedure may provide valuable protection.

**4. Analysis Is Methodologically Sound**

- ✓ Parallel trends confirmed before the switch (p = 0.24)
- ✓ Results hold after controlling for staffing and visitor volume
- ✓ Cluster-robust standard errors account for repeated observations

---

### **NEXT STEPS**

1. **Approve phased rollout** to remaining 60 branches

2. **Investigate the system-wide decline** — Why did control branches drop 2 points? Understanding this may reveal larger opportunities for improvement

3. **Monitor during implementation** — Track whether the protective effect holds at scale

4. **Consider complementary improvements** — Since this procedure alone won't drive substantial gains, evaluate what else might boost service scores

5. **Document implementation costs** — Ensure the modest benefit justifies any training or operational expenses
