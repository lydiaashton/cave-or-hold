# **Board Memorandum — FINAL**

---

## **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2025  
**Subject:** Recommendation on System-Wide Rollout of New Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**We cannot confidently conclude the new procedure caused the improvement.** While switched branches show higher service scores after adoption, **over half of the performance gap (52%) developed BEFORE the switch occurred.** Branches that chose to switch were already improving faster than other branches. The observed 1.8-point difference likely reflects pre-existing differences between branches, not the effect of the procedure itself.

**Recommendation:** Do NOT proceed with system-wide rollout. Instead, run a properly controlled pilot with random assignment to isolate the true effect.

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

### **❌ DO NOT APPROVE SYSTEM-WIDE ROLLOUT AT THIS TIME**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did service scores improve in switched branches? | **Yes** — but they were already improving before the switch |
| Can we attribute this to the new procedure? | **No** — pre-existing trends explain most of the difference |
| Is the evidence strong enough to justify rollout costs? | **No** — risk of spending resources on an ineffective change |
| What should the Board do? | **Approve a randomized pilot** before any broader implementation |

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. The Gap Was Widening Before the Switch**

| Period | Gap (Treatment − Control) | Trend |
|--------|---------------------------|-------|
| Week 6 (start) | 1.02 points | — |
| Week 14 (before switch) | 2.89 points | **+1.87 points** |
| Week 22 (end) | 4.64 points | +1.75 points |

**Critical insight:** 52% of the total gap growth occurred BEFORE the procedure changed. The gap was already widening at 0.23 points/week before the switch, and only accelerated slightly to 0.27 points/week after.

### **2. What the 1.8-Point "Effect" Really Captures**

The standard difference-in-differences estimate of +1.8 points assumes both groups would have followed parallel trends without the intervention. **This assumption is violated:**

- Switched branches were improving at **+0.16 points/week** before the switch
- Non-switched branches were declining at **−0.07 points/week** before the switch
- This pre-existing trend difference is statistically significant (p<0.001)

The 1.8-point estimate conflates the procedure effect with these pre-existing differences.

### **3. Likely Explanation**

Branches that opted into the new procedure probably differ systematically from those that didn't:
- More engaged management
- Better resources or staffing
- Greater capacity for operational change

These same factors likely drove their improving service scores—independent of the stock rotation procedure.

---

## **Why This Matters**

Rolling out the procedure system-wide based on this evidence carries real risks:

| Risk | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **No benefit** | The procedure may have little or no effect; branches see no improvement |
| **Implementation costs** | Staff training, process changes, potential disruption |
| **Opportunity cost** | Resources spent on this instead of interventions with proven impact |
| **Credibility** | If rollout fails to deliver, trust in data-driven decisions erodes |

---

## **Recommendations**

### **1. Pause System-Wide Rollout**
Do not expand the procedure until there is stronger causal evidence.

### **2. Run a Randomized Pilot**
- Randomly assign 50-100 branches to adopt the procedure now
- Randomly assign 50-100 branches to continue with the old procedure
- Track service scores for 12-16 weeks
- This design isolates the causal effect

### **3. Investigate Selection Factors**
Analyze what distinguishes branches that switched from those that didn't (size, location, budget, management tenure, etc.). This helps understand the confounding.

### **4. Set Clear Success Criteria**
Before any rollout, define:
- Minimum effect size worth pursuing (e.g., ≥1.0 point improvement)
- Acceptable implementation cost per branch
- Timeline for evaluation

---

## **Conclusion**

The data shows correlation, not causation. Branches that adopted the new procedure were already on a better trajectory. A randomized evaluation is the only way to determine whether the procedure itself delivers value. We recommend investing in proper evaluation before committing to system-wide implementation.
