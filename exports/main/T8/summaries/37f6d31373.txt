# **Board Summary: New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence is inconclusive.** While branches using the new procedure performed better than control branches after the switch, **they did not actually improve in absolute terms**—their scores remained flat. The apparent benefit comes entirely from control branches declining. Additionally, treatment and control branches were already on different trajectories *before* the switch, which undermines confidence in the causal interpretation. **We recommend a cautious, phased rollout with continued monitoring rather than immediate system-wide adoption.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### What the Data Shows

| Metric | Switched Branches | Control Branches |
|--------|------------------|------------------|
| Number of branches | 425 | 284 |
| Average score before | 50.9 | 50.7 |
| Average score after | 50.9 | 48.6 |
| **Change** | **0.0 (no change)** | **-2.0 (decline)** |

**Critical observation:** Switched branches did not improve. They held steady while control branches declined.

### Methodological Concern

The standard analysis assumes treatment and control branches would have followed similar trends absent the intervention (**parallel trends assumption**). This assumption is **violated** in our data:

- Treatment branches were already outperforming control branches *before* the switch
- The gap widened from -1.4 points (week 6) to +1.3 points (week 14) **prior to the procedure change**
- Statistical test confirms significant pre-existing differential trends (p < 0.001)

This means the estimated "2-point benefit" may reflect pre-existing trends rather than a true causal effect.

### Two Possible Interpretations

| Interpretation | What It Means |
|---------------|---------------|
| **Optimistic** | The new procedure protected branches from system-wide decline |
| **Concerning** | The apparent benefit is just continuation of pre-existing trends; the procedure may have no real effect |

---

## **Final Verdict**

**DO NOT APPROVE system-wide rollout at this time.**

**Reasoning:**
1. The new procedure did not demonstrably improve service scores
2. The analytical method relies on an assumption (parallel trends) that the data violates
3. We cannot distinguish between a real protective effect and pre-existing trends
4. System-wide implementation costs should not be incurred without clearer evidence of benefit

**However, do not abandon the procedure either.** The possibility that it protects against decline warrants further investigation.

---

## **Recommendation**

**Approve a structured pilot program:**

1. **Expand selectively:** Add 50-100 branches to the treatment group, chosen to match control branch characteristics
2. **Extend timeline:** Collect 8-12 additional weeks of data
3. **Improve analysis:** Use matched comparison groups rather than simple treatment/control split
4. **Return for decision:** Bring updated evidence to the board in 4 months

## **What Would Change Our Verdict**

We would recommend full rollout if follow-up analysis shows:
- Treatment branches actually **improve** (not just hold steady)
- Newly matched treatment/control pairs show similar pre-trends
- The effect persists across multiple time periods
- Benefits outweigh implementation costs
