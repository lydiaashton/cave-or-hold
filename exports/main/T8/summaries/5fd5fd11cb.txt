# **Memorandum to the Board of Directors**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence is inconclusive.** While branches using the new procedure show higher service scores after the switch, we cannot confidently attribute this improvement to the procedure itself. The treatment branches were already improving relative to control branches *before* the switch, which undermines our ability to isolate the procedure's effect.

**Verdict: Do not proceed with system-wide rollout based on this analysis.** Additional evidence is needed before committing cooperative-wide resources.

---

## **Key Findings**

### The Core Problem: Violated Assumption

The difference-in-differences method requires that treatment and control groups follow **parallel trends** before the intervention. **This assumption is not met:**

| Period | Treatment Group | Control Group | Gap (Treatment − Control) |
|--------|-----------------|---------------|---------------------------|
| Weeks 6-9 (early pre) | Behind | Ahead | **−0.81** |
| Weeks 11-14 (late pre) | Catching up | Stable | **+0.46** |
| Weeks 15-22 (post) | Ahead | Behind | **+1.82** |

**The treatment group closed a 1.27-point gap BEFORE the switch even happened.** This suggests they were already on an upward trajectory independent of the new procedure.

### What the Data Actually Shows

1. **Both groups dropped sharply at week 15** (treatment: −3.9 points; control: −3.5 points), suggesting week 14 was anomalously high for all branches — not a treatment effect.

2. **The treatment group's improvement began before the switch.** From weeks 6-14, they gained ground steadily against the control group.

3. **Post-switch, the treatment group maintained their advantage** but did not show a clear "jump" attributable to the procedure change.

4. **Week 16 was unusually low for both groups**, further suggesting external factors (seasonal, operational) affected all branches simultaneously.

### Statistical Summary (With Caveats)

| Metric | Value | Reliability |
|--------|-------|-------------|
| Raw DiD estimate | +1.98 points | **Biased** — does not account for pre-trends |
| Adjusted estimate* | ~0.5 to 1.0 points | **Uncertain** — rough adjustment for pre-trend |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 | **Misleading** — significance doesn't fix biased design |

*If we subtract the pre-period convergence rate from the post-period gap, the remaining "effect" is much smaller and less certain.

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Verdict |
|----------|---------|
| **Did the new procedure improve service scores?** | **Cannot determine** — pre-existing trends confound the analysis |
| **Is the 2-point DiD estimate credible?** | **No** — parallel trends assumption is violated |
| **Should we roll out system-wide now?** | **No** — evidence is insufficient to justify the investment |
| **Should we abandon the new procedure?** | **No** — it may still help; we simply lack proof |
| **What should the Board do?** | **Pause and gather better evidence** before deciding |

### Rationale

1. **Selection bias is likely.** Treatment branches (n=425) were not randomly assigned. They may have been more motivated, better-staffed, or otherwise predisposed to improve.

2. **The pre-period trend alone explains most of the observed "effect."** Treatment branches gained 1.27 points relative to control before the switch; the post-switch gain was only 1.36 additional points.

3. **External factors affected all branches.** The sharp week 14-to-15 drop in both groups suggests seasonal or operational factors unrelated to the procedure change.

4. **The cost of a wrong decision is high.** A system-wide rollout requires training, staffing adjustments, and operational disruption. Acting on flawed evidence risks wasting resources on a procedure that may not deliver the expected benefits.

---

## **Recommendations and Next Steps**

### Immediate Actions (Next 30 Days)

1. **Pause rollout planning.** Do not commit resources to system-wide implementation until better evidence is available.

2. **Document the selection process.** Understand how branches were chosen for the new procedure. Were they volunteers? High-performers? This affects how we interpret the results.

3. **Continue data collection.** Maintain weekly service score tracking for all branches.

### Medium-Term Actions (Next 3-6 Months)

4. **Extend the observation period.** Collect at least 6 more months of data. A longer pre-period would allow better assessment of underlying trends.

5. **Consider a randomized trial.** If feasible, randomly assign remaining branches to treatment vs. control. Randomization ensures parallel trends by design.

6. **Conduct qualitative research.** Interview branch managers in both groups to understand what else changed during this period (staffing, training, local factors).

7. **Re-analyze with better methods.** Consider:
   - Synthetic control methods to construct a better comparison group
   - Interrupted time series for individual branches
   - Matching treatment branches to similar control branches before analysis

### Decision Timeline

| Timeline | Action |
|----------|--------|
| **Now** | Pause rollout; continue monitoring |
| **3 months** | Re-analyze with extended data |
| **6 months** | Make final rollout decision based on improved evidence |

## **Appendix: Technical Notes**

- **Parallel trends violation:** Treatment branches gained 1.27 points relative to control branches during the pre-period (weeks 6-14), indicating non-parallel trajectories.
- **Week 14 anomaly:** Both groups scored unusually high in week 14, then dropped in week 15 — suggesting external factors rather than treatment effects.
- **Selection bias concern:** Treatment branches (n=425) may differ systematically from control branches (n=284) in unobserved ways.
- **Data source:** branches.csv (12,053 observations, 709 branches, 17 weeks)
- **Analysis method:** Difference-in-differences with cluster-robust standard errors at the branch level; controls for staffing index and visits index

---

**We apologize for the initial overconfident conclusion.** Proper causal inference requires careful attention to assumptions, and the parallel trends assumption was not adequately verified before the first draft. We recommend pausing any rollout decision until more robust evidence is available.
