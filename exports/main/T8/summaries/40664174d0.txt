# **Memorandum to the Board**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Rotation Procedure  

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new shelving and rotation procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **1.96 points (4.1% improvement)** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and consistent across the post-switch period. 

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout to all remaining branches.**

---

## **Final Verdict**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Does the new procedure improve service scores?** | **Yes** |
| **Statistical confidence** | High (p = 0.003) |
| **Effect size** | Modest (+4.1%) |
| **Evidence quality** | Strong |
| **Recommendation** | **Roll out with monitoring** |

**Confidence Statement:** We are confident the new procedure causes a real improvement in service scores. The 95% confidence interval (0.67 to 3.25 points) excludes zero, and the result holds after controlling for branch characteristics and weekly trends. However, the effect is modest and based on only 8 weeks of post-switch data. We recommend rollout **conditional on continued monitoring** to verify the improvement persists long-term.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measurable Improvement in Service Quality

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated improvement | +1.96 service score points |
| 95% Confidence Interval | 0.67 to 3.25 points |
| Relative gain | 4.1% above baseline |
| Statistical significance | p = 0.003 |

### 2. Study Overview

- **150 branches total:** 90 adopted the new procedure (treatment), 60 kept the old (control)
- **17 weeks of data:** Weeks 6–22 (9 weeks before switch, 8 weeks after)
- **Switch occurred:** Week 15
- **Analysis method:** Difference-in-differences regression with branch and week controls

### 3. Valid Comparison

Before the switch, treatment and control branches followed nearly identical trends in service scores. This validates our comparison and gives confidence that the observed improvement is due to the new procedure, not other factors.

### 4. Effect Is Robust

- Improvement persisted consistently across 7 of 8 weeks after the switch
- The effect is **not** explained by changes in staffing levels or branch visit volumes
- Treatment branches improved by 2.31 points on average vs. 0.35 points for control branches

## **Important Caveats**

1. **Modest effect size:** ~4% improvement is meaningful but not transformative
2. **Limited timeframe:** Only 8 weeks of post-switch data; long-term sustainability unknown
3. **Some variability:** Week 21 showed minimal difference between groups
4. **Implementation matters:** Effects depend on proper adoption at each branch
