# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

**Prepared for the Board of Directors**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Although we expected no measurable impact, branches that switched to the new shelving and rotation procedure achieved service scores **2.3 points higher** (approximately **5% improvement**) than branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and sustained. We recommend approving system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Overview**
- **150 branches** participated: 90 adopted the new procedure, 60 maintained the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data**: Weeks 6–14 (before), Weeks 15–22 (after)
- Rigorous difference-in-differences analysis controls for branch differences, staffing, and visitor volume

### **Service Score Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 49.75 | 53.60 | +3.85 |
| Non-switched branches | 49.73 | 51.08 | +1.35 |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | | **+2.50** |

**Adjusted estimate (with controls): +2.33 points**
- 95% confidence interval: 1.0 to 3.7 points
- p-value: 0.001 (highly significant)

### **Why This Finding Is Reliable**

1. **Groups were comparable before the switch**: Switched and non-switched branches showed no diverging trends prior to week 15 (p = 0.16)
2. **Result is robust**: The effect persists whether or not we account for staffing levels and visitor volume
3. **Effect is consistent**: Improvement held steady across all 8 post-switch weeks, ranging from 2.1 to 4.0 points weekly
4. **Sample is substantial**: 2,550 observations across 150 branches

### **What This Means**

- The average branch scores approximately 50 on the service index
- A 2.3-point gain represents a **4.7% improvement** in service quality
- The benefit appears independent of staffing or traffic changes—the procedure itself is driving the improvement

---

## **Final Verdict**

**The new procedure should be rolled out to all branches.**

The data provides strong, statistically significant evidence that the procedure change improved service scores. While this finding contradicts our initial expectation that the change would have no effect, the analysis is methodologically sound and the result is clear:

| Criterion | Assessment |
|-----------|------------|
| Statistical significance | ✓ Passed (p < 0.001) |
| Practical significance | ✓ Passed (5% improvement) |
| Research design validity | ✓ Passed (parallel trends confirmed) |
| Result consistency | ✓ Passed (stable across all post-switch weeks) |

**There is no statistical basis for withholding the rollout.** The procedure delivers measurable benefits at no identified cost.
