# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**The data provides evidence that the new procedure improved service scores, though the effect is modest.** Branches that adopted the new procedure showed service score increases of approximately **2 points (about 4%)** relative to branches that kept the old procedure. This difference is statistically significant (p = 0.001) and consistent across the 8-week post-switch period.

**Recommendation: Proceed with rollout, but monitor closely.** The evidence supports adoption, though expectations should be calibrated to a modest improvement rather than a transformational change.

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did the new procedure change service scores? | **Yes** |
| Was the change positive or negative? | **Positive** |
| Is the result statistically reliable? | **Yes (p = 0.001)** |
| Is the effect large or small? | **Modest (~4%)** |
| Should we roll out to all branches? | **Yes** |

**Rationale:** The analysis provides credible evidence that the new procedure improved service scores. While the effect is not large, it is statistically significant, consistent across post-switch weeks, and shows no signs of negative consequences. Given that no harms were detected and some benefit was observed, rolling out the procedure to all branches is justified. However, the board should expect incremental rather than dramatic improvements and should continue monitoring to ensure effects persist at scale.

---

## **Key Findings**

### What the Data Shows
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect | **+2.04 points** on service score |
| Statistical significance | **p = 0.001** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | **[0.81, 3.27]** |
| Relative improvement | **~4%** from baseline |

### Study Strengths
- **Comparable groups**: Treatment and control branches had similar service scores before the switch (51.4 vs. 52.1)
- **Consistent effect**: Positive differences observed in all 8 post-switch weeks
- **Robust to controls**: Effect persists when accounting for staffing and visitor volume

### Limitations to Consider
- **Modest magnitude**: The effect is statistically significant but small in practical terms
- **Observational design**: Branches were not randomly assigned; unobserved factors could influence results
- **Short follow-up**: Only 8 weeks of post-switch data; long-term effects unknown
- **Limited explanatory power**: The procedure explains <1% of score variation (many other factors matter)

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve phased rollout** to remaining branches, with realistic expectations (~4% improvement)

2. **Establish monitoring framework** to track whether effects persist at scale and over time

3. **Set evaluation checkpoints** at 3, 6, and 12 months post-rollout

4. **Document implementation details** from the 90 early-adopter branches to support consistent adoption

5. **Consider complementary improvements**: Since this procedure explains limited variation in scores, additional interventions may be needed for larger gains

---

## **Technical Notes**

Analysis used difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch). The parallel trends assumption appears reasonable based on pre-switch comparisons. Effect size should be interpreted as an average; individual branch experiences may vary.
