# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD OF DIRECTORS**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure  
**Date:** November 2025  
**Data Period:** Weeks 6–22 (17 weeks, 34 branches)

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The evidence is inconclusive.** We cannot determine whether the new procedure improves service scores based on the available data. The estimated effect is positive (+2.2 points) but **not statistically significant**, meaning the observed improvement could reasonably be due to random variation. 

**Recommendation:** **Do not proceed with full rollout.** Either (a) collect more data before deciding, or (b) if expansion is desired for other reasons, treat it as an experiment with continued rigorous evaluation.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **The Numbers**

| Metric | Value | Interpretation |
|--------|-------|----------------|
| Estimated effect | +2.2 points | Positive direction |
| 95% Confidence Interval | -0.6 to +5.0 | Includes zero |
| P-value | 0.12 | Not significant at 5% level |
| Minimum detectable effect | ~3 points | Study underpowered for smaller effects |

### **What This Means**

The data are **consistent with three possibilities**:
1. The procedure has **no effect** (most conservative interpretation)
2. The procedure provides a **modest improvement** of 2-5 points
3. The procedure has a **small negative effect** (less likely but possible)

**We cannot distinguish among these with confidence.**

### **Context**

- Treatment branches started **3-4 points below** control branches
- After the switch, the gap **narrowed** to 1-2 points
- This narrowing drives our positive estimate
- No negative operational impacts detected (staffing, visits unchanged)

---

## **WHY WE CANNOT CONCLUDE THE PROCEDURE WORKS**

1. **Statistical uncertainty**: The confidence interval includes zero
2. **Limited data**: Only 8 weeks post-switch, 34 branches total
3. **Non-random assignment**: Branches self-selected into switching, introducing potential bias
4. **Pre-existing differences**: Treatment and control groups had different baseline scores

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **Did the new procedure improve service scores?** | **Cannot determine** |
| **Is there evidence of harm?** | **No** |
| **Should we roll out to all branches now?** | **No** |
| **Should we abandon the procedure?** | **No** |
| **What should we do?** | **Gather more evidence before deciding** |

### **Rationale**

> *"Absence of evidence is not evidence of absence."*
>
> The data do not prove the procedure works, but they also do not prove it fails. The point estimate is positive, there are no detected negative impacts, and the confidence interval allows for meaningful benefits. However, **statistical rigor requires us to acknowledge uncertainty** rather than act on inconclusive results.

---

## **RECOMMENDED NEXT STEPS**

| Option | Action | Timeline |
|--------|--------|----------|
| **A. Wait for more evidence** | Continue monitoring current branches; re-analyze with 6+ months of post-switch data | Re-evaluate Q2 2026 |
| **B. Controlled pilot expansion** | Roll out to 5-10 additional branches as a formal experiment with pre-registered success criteria | Immediate, with evaluation plan |
| **C. Full rollout** | **Not recommended** without stronger evidence | — |

### **If the Board Chooses Option B:**
- Pre-register success criteria (e.g., "proceed if effect >1.5 points with p<0.10")
- Commit to discontinuing if results prove negative
- Collect qualitative feedback alongside quantitative metrics

---

## **CONCLUSION**

**Honest assessment:** We do not have sufficient evidence to conclude the new procedure improves service. The point estimate is encouraging, but statistical uncertainty is too high for a confident rollout decision.

**Practical guidance:** If the board wishes to proceed despite uncertainty, do so as a **controlled pilot with explicit evaluation criteria**, not as a confirmed improvement.
