# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure  
**Date:** Analysis of weeks 6–22 service score data  
**Analysis Type:** Quasi-experimental impact evaluation (difference-in-differences)

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

# ❌ NOT RECOMMENDED FOR SYSTEM-WIDE ROLLOUT

**The evidence does not support that the new procedure improves service scores.** The apparent improvement is attributable to pre-existing differences between branches, not the procedure itself.

---

## **Bottom Line**

**We cannot conclude the new procedure improved service scores.** While branches that switched show higher scores after the change, this pattern was already emerging *before* they switched. The apparent 2.2-point improvement likely reflects pre-existing differences between branches, not the procedure itself. When we account for this pre-existing trend, the estimated effect drops to essentially **zero** and is statistically insignificant.

**We need more evidence before recommending system-wide adoption.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### The Problem: Violated Assumption

Standard impact analysis assumes treatment and control groups would have followed similar trends without the intervention. **This assumption is violated in our data:**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Pre-switch trend in gap | **+0.32 points per week** (growing) |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 |
| Weeks of pre-trend | 9 weeks (weeks 6-14) |

The gap between switched and non-switched branches was **already widening before the procedure changed**. This suggests branches selected for the new procedure were already on an upward trajectory.

### Two Very Different Estimates

| Method | Estimated Effect | Significance |
|--------|-----------------|--------------|
| Standard analysis (ignores pre-trend) | +2.20 points | p < 0.001 |
| **Trend-adjusted analysis** (accounts for pre-trend) | **-0.19 points** | **p = 0.67** |

The trend-adjusted estimate—which is methodologically appropriate given the data—shows **no detectable effect**.

### What the Weekly Data Shows

```
Week  |  Gap (Switched - Control)
------|---------------------------
  6   |  -0.50
  9   |  +1.13
  12  |  +1.83
  14  |  +2.17  ← Switch happens after this week
  15  |  +2.44
  18  |  +3.05
  22  |  +4.19
```

The gap grows steadily throughout the entire period—before AND after the switch. There is no clear "jump" attributable to the procedure change.

---

## **Why This Happened**

Branches were likely **not randomly assigned** to switch. Possible explanations:

1. **Selection bias**: Better-performing or improving branches may have been chosen to pilot the new procedure
2. **Volunteer bias**: Branches confident in their performance may have volunteered to switch
3. **Phased rollout**: The cooperative may have intentionally started with branches more likely to succeed

Any of these would create the pattern we observe: apparent improvement that actually predates the intervention.

---

## **Next Steps**

| Action | Rationale |
|--------|-----------|
| **Pause system-wide rollout** | Current evidence does not support the expected benefit |
| **Gather more data** | Extend the evaluation period to 6-12 months |
| **Consider randomized rollout** | Randomly assign remaining branches to switch now vs. later to enable cleaner comparison |
| **Investigate selection process** | Understand why switched branches were already improving |
| **Examine other outcomes** | Check if the procedure affected costs, efficiency, or staff satisfaction even if service scores didn't change |

---

## **Decision Requested**

**Do NOT approve system-wide rollout based on current evidence.**

The data does not demonstrate that the new procedure causes improved service scores. Proceeding with a full rollout risks investing resources in a change that may not deliver benefits. We recommend extending the evaluation period and improving the research design before making a system-wide decision.

---

*Analysis based on 12,053 weekly observations from 709 branches. Difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors and pre-trend adjustment.*
