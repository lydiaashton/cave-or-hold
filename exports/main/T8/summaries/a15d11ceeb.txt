# **Memorandum to the Board**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure  

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores by approximately 2 points (4%), a statistically significant but modest gain.** Whether this warrants system-wide rollout depends on implementation costs and strategic priorities.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Measurable Improvement in Service Quality**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Treatment (switched to new procedure) | 50.3 | 52.4 | **+2.1** |
| Control (kept old procedure) | 48.3 | 48.4 | **+0.1** |

**Net effect of new procedure: +2.0 points** (95% CI: 1.2 to 3.0)

### **2. Results Are Statistically Reliable**

- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001
- The finding holds after controlling for branch characteristics, staffing levels, and visitor volume
- Pre-switch trends were parallel between groups, validating the comparison

### **3. Important Limitations**

- **Non-random assignment:** The 90 branches that switched may differ from the 60 that did not in unobserved ways
- **Limited post-period:** Only 8 weeks of data after the switch; long-term effects are unknown
- **No cost data:** We cannot assess whether the improvement justifies implementation expenses
- **Modest effect size:** A 4% improvement is real but not transformative

---

## **Final Verdict**

**Proceed with cautious optimism, but gather more information before full commitment.**

The data contradicts the initial expectation that the procedure would make no difference. There is a genuine, measurable improvement. However, given the modest effect size and study limitations, I recommend:

1. **Do not mandate immediate system-wide rollout.** Allow branches to adopt voluntarily while monitoring outcomes.

2. **Collect cost data.** If the new procedure requires significant staff time, training, or disruption, the 4% gain may not justify the expense.

3. **Extend the evaluation period.** Track the 90 switched branches for another 3–6 months to confirm the effect persists and does not fade.

4. **Investigate heterogeneity.** Some branch types may benefit more than others; targeted rollout could maximize impact.

**If implementation costs are low and the effect proves durable, full rollout is warranted. If costs are substantial or the effect diminishes over time, the procedure may be best suited for branches where it shows the strongest gains.**
