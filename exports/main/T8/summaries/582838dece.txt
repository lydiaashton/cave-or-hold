# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact Analysis**

**Prepared for the Board of Directors**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence is mixed.** Branches that adopted the new procedure showed higher service scores after the change, but our analysis reveals a critical limitation: **treatment branches were already improving relative to control branches before the switch occurred.** This pre-existing trend makes it difficult to confidently attribute the improvement to the new procedure alone.

**Recommendation: Do not proceed with immediate system-wide rollout. Instead, expand the pilot to additional branches with careful monitoring to gather more conclusive evidence.**

---

## **Final Verdict**

> **NOT READY FOR SYSTEM-WIDE ROLLOUT**
>
> The analysis does not provide sufficient evidence that the new procedure *caused* the observed improvement in service scores. While the outcome is promising and no harm was detected, the violation of the parallel trends assumption means we cannot distinguish the procedure's effect from pre-existing differences between branches that chose to switch and those that did not.
>
> **Decision:** Approve a structured pilot expansion with randomized assignment and rigorous evaluation before considering full implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Observed Improvement, But Causality Is Uncertain

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|---------------|---------------|--------|
| Treatment (New Procedure) | 49.7 | 50.4 | +0.7 |
| Control (Old Procedure) | 49.4 | 48.0 | −1.5 |
| **Raw Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+2.1** |

While the raw comparison shows a 2.1-point improvement, this estimate is likely **biased upward** due to pre-existing trends.

### 2. Critical Concern: Pre-Treatment Trend Violation

A core assumption of our analysis is that treatment and control branches would have followed similar trajectories absent the intervention. **This assumption is violated:**

- In weeks 6–10, treatment and control branches performed similarly (differences ranged from −0.59 to +0.01)
- In weeks 11–14, treatment branches began pulling ahead **before the switch** (differences grew from +0.77 to +1.33)
- This pre-trend is statistically significant (p = 0.001)

**Implication:** If the pre-switch trend continued naturally, it could account for approximately **2.0 points** of the observed 2.1-point effect—leaving essentially no detectable impact from the procedure itself.

### 3. Alternative Interpretation

The data are consistent with two scenarios:

| Scenario | Estimated True Effect | Confidence |
|----------|----------------------|------------|
| Optimistic (pre-trend unrelated to treatment) | +1.5 to +2.5 points | Low |
| Conservative (pre-trend reflects selection bias) | 0 to +0.5 points | Moderate |

Branches that volunteered to switch may have been more motivated, better managed, or otherwise positioned to improve regardless of the procedure change.

### 4. What We Can Say With Confidence

- **No harm detected:** Branches using the new procedure did not experience any decline in service scores
- **Positive association exists:** Switched branches ended the period with higher scores than control branches
- **Statistical precision is good:** Confidence intervals are tight, but they reflect precision around a potentially biased estimate

---

## **Next Steps**

### Recommended Approach: Cautious Expansion with Rigorous Evaluation

1. **Pause full rollout** — The current evidence does not support a confident system-wide implementation

2. **Expand the pilot strategically** — Invite 50–100 additional branches to adopt the procedure, with attention to:
   - Including branches with varying baseline performance levels
   - Ensuring participation is not limited to already high-performing or motivated branches

3. **Improve the evaluation design:**
   - **Randomized rollout:** If feasible, randomly assign branches to early vs. late adoption
   - **Longer observation period:** Track scores for 6+ months post-switch
   - **Pre-register analysis plan:** Specify evaluation criteria before expansion begins

4. **Gather qualitative data** — Interview branch managers to understand:
   - Why some branches chose to switch and others did not
   - How the procedure was implemented in practice
   - Any observed operational benefits beyond service scores

5. **Re-evaluate in 3–6 months** — With better data, the board can make a more informed decision about system-wide adoption
