# Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores

**Date:** November 2026  
**Analysis Period:** Weeks 6-22 (17 weeks)  
**Data:** 578 weekly observations from 34 branches

---

## Final Verdict

**DO NOT PROCEED WITH SYSTEMWIDE ROLLOUT**

The evidence is insufficient to justify changing procedures at all 34 branches. While the new procedure is associated with higher service scores, we cannot determine whether the procedure *caused* the improvement or whether better-performing branches simply chose to adopt it. A randomized evaluation is required before making this decision.

---

## Bottom Line

The new procedure is associated with a **2.4-point improvement** in service scores, but **causality cannot be confidently established**. Switched branches were already improving faster than control branches *before* the procedure change, which likely inflates the estimated effect. The board should **not proceed with systemwide rollout** until a randomized pilot provides clearer causal evidence.

---

## Key Findings

### Primary Estimate (with important caveats)

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Point Estimate | +2.36 points |
| 95% Confidence Interval | -0.57 to +5.29 points |
| P-value (two-tailed) | 0.115 |
| P-value (one-tailed, bootstrap) | 0.056 |

### Critical Concern: Pre-Existing Trends

| Period | Difference (Switched − Control) |
|--------|--------------------------------|
| Week 6 | +1.89 |
| Week 10 | +4.97 |
| Week 14 | +7.03 |
| **Pre-trend test** | **p = 0.010** (diverging before switch) |

The switched branches were already pulling ahead before the procedure change. This suggests **selection bias**: better-performing or faster-improving branches may have been more likely to adopt the new procedure.

### Post-Switch Pattern

| Period | Difference (Switched − Control) |
|--------|--------------------------------|
| Weeks 15-18 (early) | +5.42 |
| Weeks 19-22 (late) | +6.97 |

The gap continued widening after the switch, but given the pre-trend, we cannot attribute this to the procedure itself.

---

## Recommendations

### Immediate Decision: Pause systemwide rollout

The evidence is too uncertain to justify the costs and disruption of changing procedures at all 34 branches.

### Required Next Steps:

1. **Randomized rollout**: Randomly assign 10-15 additional branches to the new procedure. This eliminates selection bias and provides causal evidence.

2. **Extend monitoring**: Collect at least 16 weeks of post-switch data to improve statistical power.

3. **Document implementation**: Track whether branches are actually following the new procedure consistently.

4. **Re-analyze in 3-4 months**: With randomized assignment and more data, we can provide a definitive answer.

---

## Decision Threshold

| Evidence Standard | Current Status | Needed for Rollout |
|-------------------|----------------|-------------------|
| Statistically significant effect (p < 0.05) | ❌ Not met | Required |
| Parallel pre-trends verified | ❌ Violated | Required |
| Randomized assignment | ❌ Not done | Strongly recommended |
| Effect size > 2 points | ✓ Met | Maintained |

---

## Conclusion

The new procedure shows **promising but inconclusive** results. The positive association may reflect the procedure's benefit, pre-existing branch differences, or both. A randomized evaluation is needed before committing to systemwide implementation.

---

*Analysis used difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors and bootstrap validation. The pre-trend violation is a significant limitation that undermines causal interpretation.*
