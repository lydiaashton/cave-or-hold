# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD OF DIRECTORS**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

**Date:** November 15, 2026

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure produced a modest but statistically reliable improvement in service scores.** Branches using the new procedure averaged **1.77 points higher** (3.6% improvement) than those keeping the old procedure. This contradicts our initial expectation of no effect. However, the improvement is modest, and **38% of branches saw no benefit or declined**.

**Recommendation:** The board should weigh the **modest benefit** against implementation costs before approving system-wide rollout. If costs are low, proceed. If costs are substantial, consider targeted rollout or further piloting.

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

**Proceed with cautious, phased rollout — conditional on cost.**

| Consideration | Assessment |
|---------------|------------|
| **Statistical evidence** | ✓ Strong (p=0.003) |
| **Effect magnitude** | ⚠ Modest (3.6% improvement) |
| **Consistency across branches** | ⚠ Mixed (62% benefited) |
| **Alternative explanations** | ✓ Ruled out (not staffing/traffic) |
| **Implementation risk** | ⚠ Unknown without cost data |

**Verdict:** The procedure works, but not everywhere and not dramatically. **Approve rollout only if per-branch implementation costs are low relative to the value of a ~2-point service score improvement.** A phased approach (25-50 branches first) allows the cooperative to verify benefits at scale while limiting exposure if results don't replicate.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### 1. Statistically Significant but Modest Effect

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Net effect (DiD estimate) | **+1.77 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | **+0.58 to +2.96 points** |
| P-value | **0.003** |
| Effect size | **0.17 standard deviations** (small-to-moderate) |

### 2. Mixed Results Across Branches

| Group | Mean Change | Median Change | % Improved |
|-------|-------------|---------------|------------|
| Treatment (New Procedure) | +1.44 | +1.58 | 62% |
| Control (Old Procedure) | -0.34 | -0.04 | 50% |

**Notable:** Over one-third of treatment branches (34 of 90) did not benefit from the new procedure.

### 3. Not Driven by Staffing or Traffic

The improvement is not explained by changes in staffing levels (p=0.51) or visitor volume (p=0.75), suggesting the effect reflects genuine operational changes from the procedure itself.

### 4. Pre-Switch Trends Were Parallel

Treatment and control branches showed similar service score trends before the switch (p=0.25), supporting the validity of the comparison.

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

1. **Quantify implementation costs** — The benefit is modest (~1.8 points), so costs must be proportionate.

2. **Investigate why 38% of branches didn't benefit** — Branch characteristics (size, location, staffing) may moderate the effect. Targeted rollout may be more efficient.

3. **Consider phased rollout** — Implement at additional branches while monitoring outcomes before committing system-wide.

4. **Define success thresholds** — Determine what magnitude of service score improvement justifies the investment.

5. **If proceeding:** Capture implementation practices from high-performing branches to support adoption.

---

## **ANALYSIS DETAILS**

- **Data:** 2,550 observations from 150 branches over 17 weeks
- **Method:** Difference-in-differences regression with branch-clustered standard errors
- **Switch point:** Week 15 (9 weeks pre, 8 weeks post)
