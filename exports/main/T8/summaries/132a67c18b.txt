# **Board Summary: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure shows a small positive effect on service scores, though the practical impact is modest.** Switched branches maintained their scores while control branches declined—resulting in a statistically significant 2-point difference. However, switched branches did not actually improve in absolute terms. The board should weigh this modest benefit against implementation costs and operational considerations before deciding on system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. What the Data Shows

| Metric | Switched Branches | Control Branches |
|--------|-------------------|------------------|
| Average score BEFORE switch (weeks 6-14) | 53.31 | 52.28 |
| Average score AFTER switch (weeks 15-22) | 52.92 | 49.91 |
| **Change** | **-0.39** | **-2.37** |

**Difference-in-differences estimate: +1.98 points (p = 0.003)**

The critical nuance: switched branches did not get better—they simply **declined less** than control branches. The 2-point effect represents protection from decline, not improvement.

### 2. Effect Size Context

- **0.20 standard deviations**—conventionally considered a "small" effect
- Service scores naturally vary by ~10 points week-to-week
- The 2-point difference is **statistically detectable but may not be noticeable to patrons**

### 3. Unanswered Questions

- **Why did control branches decline?** Scores dropped 2.4 points after week 15 across all control branches. This could reflect seasonal patterns, external factors, or other changes unrelated to the procedure.
- **Will the effect persist?** We only have 8 weeks of post-switch data.
- **What are the implementation costs?** This analysis does not account for staff training time, operational complexity, or other resource requirements.

---

## **Final Verdict**

**Recommendation: Proceed with rollout, but with tempered expectations.**

**Rationale:**
1. **No harm detected**—the procedure does not negatively affect service quality
2. **Small but real benefit**—the 2-point effect is statistically robust across multiple analyses
3. **Low-risk change**—since the procedure was expected to be neutral, any positive effect is a bonus

**However, the board should:**
- Confirm that implementation costs are modest before approving
- Set realistic expectations—this is not a transformative improvement
- Monitor outcomes during rollout to ensure effects persist at scale

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** if implementation costs are reasonable.

2. **Document implementation learnings** from the early-adopter branches.

3. **Investigate the broader decline** in control branch scores after week 15—this may point to external factors worth addressing.

4. **Re-evaluate in 3-6 months** to confirm the effect is sustained.

---

*Analysis based on 2,550 weekly observations from 150 branches over 17 weeks (weeks 6-22). 102 branches switched to the new procedure; 48 served as controls. The switch occurred at week 15.*
