# Board Summary: New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## Bottom Line

Contrary to the expectation of no effect, the new procedure is associated with a **statistically significant 2.1-point improvement** in service scores (p = 0.001). The 95% confidence interval [0.9 to 3.3] indicates the true effect is very likely positive. This represents approximately a **4% improvement** over baseline scores.

---

## Key Findings

### Study Overview
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** switched to the new procedure in week 15; **60 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Analysis compares service score changes between switched and non-switched branches

### Results

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|----------------|---------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 47.90 | 48.36 | +0.46 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 48.04 | 50.58 | +2.54 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+2.08** |

### Why We Trust These Results

✓ **Valid comparison**: Before the switch, both groups showed similar trends in service scores (p = 0.22), confirming the groups were comparable

✓ **Statistically significant**: The 2.1-point improvement is unlikely to be due to chance (95% confidence interval: 0.9 to 3.3 points)

✓ **Robust finding**: The effect holds after accounting for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume

✓ **Consistent over time**: Improvement was observed across all post-switch weeks (15–22)

### What This Means

The average branch scores about 50 on the service index. The new procedure lifted switched branches by about 2 points above what would have been expected without the change—a meaningful improvement in service quality.

---

## Final Verdict

**The evidence supports rolling out the new procedure.**

The analysis demonstrates a clear, statistically significant improvement in service scores with no detectable negative pre-trends. While this study measured only service scores, the positive effect is large enough (4% improvement) to warrant implementation, assuming no offsetting costs or operational burdens.

**Confidence Level: High** — The research design is sound, the effect is statistically robust, and the finding is consistent across all post-implementation weeks.

**Caveat:** This analysis does not assess implementation costs, staff workload, or other operational factors. The board should weigh these alongside the service score improvement.
