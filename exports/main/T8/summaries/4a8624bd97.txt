# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Recommendation: Do NOT proceed with system-wide rollout based on current evidence.**

---

## **Final Verdict**

> **The evidence is insufficient to support cooperative-wide implementation.**
>
> While branches using the new procedure showed improved service scores, the analysis cannot distinguish whether this improvement was caused by the procedure itself or by pre-existing differences between branches. The statistical methods required to claim causality fail a critical validity test.
>
> **Verdict: NOT PROVEN** — Additional rigorous testing is required before proceeding.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **What the Data Shows**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| New Procedure Branches | 51.83 | 54.86 | +3.02 |
| Old Procedure Branches | 52.53 | 53.21 | +0.68 |
| **Observed Difference** | | | **+2.34** |

On the surface, this looks like a 2.3-point improvement. **However, this estimate is not reliable.**

### **Critical Methodological Issue**

The standard analysis method (difference-in-differences) requires that treatment and control groups follow similar trends before the intervention. **This assumption is violated:**

| Pre-Switch Week | Treatment vs. Control Gap | Statistically Significant? |
|-----------------|---------------------------|---------------------------|
| Week 6 | -2.65 points | Yes |
| Week 7 | -2.11 points | Yes |
| Week 8 | -2.61 points | Yes |
| Week 9 | -2.24 points | Yes |
| Week 10 | -2.34 points | Yes |
| Week 11 | -2.20 points | Yes |
| Week 12 | -1.36 points | Yes |
| Week 13 | -1.31 points | Yes |

**All 8 pre-switch weeks show treatment branches significantly underperforming control branches.** This pattern suggests:

1. **Selection bias:** Branches chosen to switch may have been systematically different (perhaps lower-performing branches were prioritized for the new procedure)
2. **Mean reversion:** Lower-performing branches may have been due to improve regardless of the procedure change
3. **Existing convergence trend:** The gap may have been narrowing anyway

### **Post-Switch Evidence Is Weak**

After the switch, only 1 of 8 weeks shows a statistically significant effect. The improvement is not consistent or robust across the post-switch period.

---

## **What This Means**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did service scores improve at switching branches? | **Yes** |
| Can we attribute this to the new procedure? | **No—not with confidence** |
| Is the evidence strong enough for system-wide rollout? | **No** |
| Could the new procedure still be beneficial? | **Possibly—but unproven** |

---

## **Next Steps**

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| 1 | **Pause system-wide rollout** | Current evidence does not support causal claims |
| 2 | **Investigate branch selection process** | Understand why treatment branches differed from control |
| 3 | **Consider randomized trial** | Randomly assign remaining branches to test causal effect |
| 4 | **Extend monitoring period** | Collect more post-switch data to assess sustainability |
| 5 | **Analyze branch-level heterogeneity** | Identify which branch types benefit most (if any) |
| 6 | **Conduct cost-benefit analysis** | Weigh implementation costs against uncertain benefits |

---

## **Alternative Approaches for Stronger Evidence**

1. **Randomized Controlled Trial:** Randomly assign remaining 284 branches to new vs. old procedure
2. **Staggered Rollout Analysis:** Use the varying switch timing across branches more rigorously
3. **Matching Methods:** Pair similar branches and compare outcomes
4. **Qualitative Assessment:** Interview branch staff about perceived impacts

---

## **Conclusion**

The data shows a positive *association* between the new procedure and service scores, but the methodological limitations prevent us from claiming a causal *effect*. Rolling out the procedure system-wide based on this evidence alone carries risk: we may invest resources in a change that does not actually improve service, or we may miss opportunities to target the procedure where it truly helps.

**Recommendation:** Gather stronger evidence before committing to cooperative-wide implementation.

---

*Analysis based on weekly service score data from Weeks 6–22. Statistical method: Difference-in-Differences with event study validation. Parallel trends assumption: VIOLATED.*
