# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure  
**Recommendation:** Proceed with phased rollout, with managed expectations

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure shows promise, but the evidence is mixed.** After an initial adjustment period, branches that adopted the new procedure achieved service scores approximately **1.2 points higher** than expected. However, pre-existing differences between branches and a 4-week lag before benefits appeared suggest the effect is **modest and takes time to materialize**. We recommend proceeding with rollout, but with realistic expectations and strong implementation support.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **What the Data Shows**

| Period | Effect on Service Scores | Statistical Significance |
|--------|-------------------------|-------------------------|
| Weeks 15-18 (immediate) | +0.15 to +0.85 points | Not significant |
| Weeks 19-22 (after adjustment) | +1.41 to +2.22 points | **Significant** (p < 0.01) |
| **Average post-switch effect** | **~1.2 points** | **Significant** |

### **Important Caveats**

1. **Branches were not comparable at baseline.** Switched branches started the study period performing worse than control branches and were still catching up when the switch occurred. This makes it difficult to isolate the procedure's effect from pre-existing improvement trends.

2. **Benefits take time.** No measurable improvement appeared in the first 4 weeks after switching. Staff likely needed time to learn and adapt to the new procedure.

3. **Effect size is modest.** A 1.2-point improvement represents roughly **2-3%** of the average service score—meaningful but not transformative.

### **What We Can Say with Confidence**

- Branches that switched **did improve** relative to control branches in weeks 19-22
- The improvement is **statistically significant** in the later post-switch period
- There is **no evidence of harm** from the new procedure

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| 1 | **Proceed with phased rollout** | Evidence supports benefit, but magnitude is uncertain |
| 2 | **Build in 4-6 week adjustment period** per branch | Effects don't appear immediately; avoid premature judgment |
| 3 | **Provide enhanced training and support** | Learning curve is evident; support may accelerate benefits |
| 4 | **Track branch-level outcomes** during rollout | Identify which branches benefit most and why |
| 5 | **Re-evaluate after 6 months** | Determine if effects persist and justify full adoption |

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Recommendation** | ✅ **APPROVE** phased system-wide rollout |
| **Confidence Level** | ⚠️ **Moderate** (pre-trend concerns reduce certainty) |
| **Expected Impact** | 📈 **+1 to +2 points** on service scores (after 4-6 week adjustment) |
| **Risk Assessment** | 🟢 **Low risk** — no evidence of negative effects |
| **Implementation Priority** | 🟡 **Medium** — modest gains warrant careful, supported rollout |

**Rationale:** The weight of evidence favors adoption. While methodological limitations prevent a definitive causal claim, the post-switch improvement is statistically significant, sustained across multiple weeks, and accompanied by no detectable harms. A phased rollout with strong training support offers the best balance of potential benefit and implementation risk.

---

## **CONCLUSION**

The new procedure appears to provide a **modest, delayed improvement** in service scores. While the evidence is not as strong as a simple analysis suggests, there is no indication of negative effects and clear benefits emerge after branches have time to adapt. A **cautious, well-supported rollout** is warranted, with continued monitoring to confirm the effect holds at scale.
