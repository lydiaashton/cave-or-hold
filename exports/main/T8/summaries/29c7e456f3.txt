# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**The data shows a positive association between the new procedure and service scores, though this was not expected.** Branches that switched to the new procedure maintained higher service scores than non-switched branches after the change (difference of 2.3 points, statistically significant). While we cannot rule out all alternative explanations, the evidence supports proceeding with the rollout while monitoring outcomes closely.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. What the Data Shows
- Switched branches declined by **0.6 points** after week 15; non-switched branches declined by **2.9 points**
- The **2.3-point difference** between groups is statistically distinguishable from zero (p=0.004)
- The effect is **consistent across all 8 post-switch weeks** (ranging from 1.4 to 3.3 points)
- Pre-switch trends were **parallel** between groups (p=0.90), strengthening confidence in the comparison

### 2. Important Caveats
- **This was unexpected.** The procedure was not designed to affect service scores, so this finding warrants careful interpretation
- **Observational data:** Branches were not randomly assigned to switch; unobserved factors could influence the result
- **Both groups declined** in absolute terms—the new procedure appears to have protected against decline rather than driving improvement
- **Effect size:** 2.3 points represents about 4% of the baseline score, or roughly one-quarter of a standard deviation

### 3. What We Cannot Conclude
- We cannot definitively prove the procedure *caused* the difference (though the design supports this interpretation)
- We cannot explain *why* the procedure affected scores (staff efficiency? patron satisfaction? other mechanisms?)
- We cannot guarantee the effect will persist or replicate at full scale

---

## **Final Verdict**

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout.**

**Confidence level: Moderate to high.**

**Rationale:**
| Consideration | Assessment |
|---------------|------------|
| Statistical evidence | ✓ Significant (p=0.004), tight confidence interval |
| Study design | ✓ Strong (parallel pre-trends, control group, 17 weeks) |
| Effect consistency | ✓ Observed in all 8 post-switch weeks |
| Effect size | △ Modest but meaningful (4% of baseline) |
| Causal certainty | △ Observational data limits definitive claims |
| Expected vs. actual | △ Unexpected finding requires cautious interpretation |

**The benefits outweigh the risks.** Even if the true effect is smaller than estimated, there is no evidence of harm, and the procedure may protect branches from broader service declines. Rolling out while continuing to monitor is the prudent course.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with rollout, but frame it carefully.** Present this as a pilot evaluation showing promising results, not a guaranteed improvement

2. **Build in evaluation from the start.** As remaining branches adopt the procedure, continue tracking service scores to confirm the effect replicates

3. **Investigate the mechanism.** Interview staff at switched branches to understand what, if anything, feels different about their operations

4. **Understand the broader decline.** Non-switched branches dropped nearly 3 points after week 15—this system-wide trend deserves attention regardless of the procedure question

5. **Set realistic expectations.** Tell the board the evidence suggests a modest positive effect, but implementation quality and local conditions may vary

---

## **Methodology Note**

This analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** design with 150 branches over 17 weeks. The approach controls for branch-specific characteristics and common time trends. Pre-intervention trends were statistically indistinguishable between groups. However, because branch assignment to the new procedure was not randomized, unobserved confounders cannot be fully ruled out.
