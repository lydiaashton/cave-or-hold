# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure shows a **promising positive effect** on service scores (+1.9 points, or ~4%), with 18 of 20 pilot branches improving. However, the result is **not statistically conclusive** (p=0.18), and we cannot confidently rule out that some of the improvement reflects broader trends affecting all branches.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **cautious expansion** rather than immediate full rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **What We Analyzed**
- **34 branches** over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure in week 15; **14 branches** kept the old procedure
- Service scores range around 50 on average (higher is better)

### **What We Found**

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|----------------|---------------|--------|
| Branches that switched | 53.0 | 57.9 | +4.9 |
| Branches that didn't switch | 48.5 | 51.5 | +3.1 |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | | **+1.9** |

- The new procedure is associated with a **1.9-point improvement** in service scores
- This effect is **not statistically significant** at conventional levels (p = 0.18)
- **18 of 20 switched branches** (90%) showed improvement after adopting the procedure
- Both groups improved after week 15, suggesting external factors (e.g., seasonal patterns) may have boosted scores system-wide

### **Analysis Quality**
- Pre-switch trends were **parallel** between groups, supporting the validity of our comparison
- Results account for inherent differences between branches and weekly variation
- When controlling for staffing and visit levels, the effect increases to +2.1 points (p=0.11)

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **Did the new procedure improve service scores?** | Probably yes, but we cannot be certain |
| **Is the improvement large enough to matter?** | Yes—if real, a 2-point gain (~4%) is operationally meaningful |
| **Could this result be due to chance?** | Yes—there is roughly a 1 in 5 chance |
| **Could the new procedure actually harm service?** | Unlikely—the data rules out meaningful negative effects |
| **Should we roll out to all branches now?** | **No**—evidence is promising but not yet conclusive |
| **Should we abandon the new procedure?** | **No**—the signal is positive and worth pursuing |

**Verdict:** **Continue with phased expansion.** The evidence favors the new procedure, but the uncertainty warrants additional data before system-wide commitment.

---

## **Recommended Next Steps**

| Priority | Action | Timeline | Success Metric |
|----------|--------|----------|----------------|
| **1** | Continue monitoring current 20 pilot branches | Ongoing | Sustain or improve +1.9 point effect |
| **2** | Expand pilot to 5–10 additional branches | Next quarter | Replicate positive effect in new branches |
| **3** | Track complementary metrics (staff hours, patron feedback, circulation) | Ongoing | No increase in labor costs; improved patron satisfaction |
| **4** | Reconvene board review | 3–6 months | Make final rollout decision with stronger evidence |

---

## **Decision Framework**

| If the true effect is... | System-wide rollout would... | Our confidence |
|--------------------------|------------------------------|----------------|
| ~2 points (our estimate) | Provide modest benefit | Moderate |
| Zero (possible) | Have no impact | Possible |
| Negative (unlikely) | Harm service quality | Very unlikely |

A **phased expansion** minimizes risk while allowing us to convert "promising" into "proven."
