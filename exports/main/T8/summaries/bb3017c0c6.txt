# **Memorandum to the Board**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

**Date:** November 15, 2026

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure likely improves service scores by about 2 points. The evidence points clearly in the expected direction, though we cannot rule out a smaller effect with complete certainty. Given the absence of negative effects and the operational feasibility, **we recommend proceeding with the rollout** while continuing to track outcomes.

---

## **Final Verdict**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Recommendation** | ✅ **APPROVE systemwide rollout** |
| **Confidence** | Moderate (evidence is suggestive, not definitive) |
| **Expected benefit** | +1.5 to +2.5 points on service score index |
| **Risk level** | Low (no negative effects observed) |
| **Condition** | Continue monitoring; reassess in 6 months |

**Rationale:** Three factors support this verdict:
1. **Direction is clear** — 75% of treatment branches improved vs. 43% of control
2. **No harm detected** — no branch showed sustained decline attributable to the procedure
3. **Low downside** — implementation costs are sunk; ongoing costs are minimal

---

## **Key Findings**

| Metric | Result |
|--------|--------|
| **Estimated improvement** | +1.9 points (on ~50-point scale) |
| **Confidence interval** | -0.4 to +4.3 points |
| **Statistical confidence** | 89% (p = 0.11) |
| **Branches improved** | 75% of treatment vs. 43% of control |

**What this means:** The data points in the expected direction—branches that switched to the new procedure improved relative to those that did not. However, we cannot rule out the possibility that the observed improvement reflects normal variation rather than a true procedure effect.

---

## **Analysis Approach**

We used a **difference-in-differences design** comparing 20 treatment branches (new procedure) against 14 control branches (old procedure) over 17 weeks. The switch occurred at week 15. This method isolates the procedure effect from underlying trends affecting all branches.

**Validity confirmed:** Treatment and control branches followed parallel trends before the switch, supporting the credibility of the comparison.
