# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores by 2.1 points — an unexpected but statistically robust finding.** The effect is clear in the data (100% of bootstrap samples show a positive effect), but since this outcome was not anticipated, we should investigate *why* it occurred before full rollout. **Recommendation: proceed with rollout, but pair it with qualitative research to understand the mechanism.**

---

## **Final Verdict**

**✅ APPROVE ROLLOUT — with monitoring**

The evidence supports adopting the new procedure system-wide. The improvement is real, statistically significant, and sustained. However, because the mechanism is unclear, we recommend:

1. **Phased implementation** over 3–6 months rather than immediate full rollout
2. **Continued tracking** of service scores at each branch during implementation
3. **Staff interviews** at 10–15 early-adopter branches to identify what drove the improvement

---

## **Key Findings**

### **The Numbers**

| Group | Before | After | Change |
|-------|--------|-------|--------|
| Switched branches (n=90) | 49.0 | 51.3 | +2.3 |
| Non-switched branches (n=60) | 49.3 | 49.5 | +0.2 |
| **Net effect** | | | **+2.1** |

- **95% Confidence Interval:** +0.9 to +3.4 points
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.01
- **Bootstrap validation:** 100% of 1,000 resamples showed positive effect

### **Why This Is Surprising**

The stock rotation procedure was not designed to affect service quality. Possible explanations:
- Staff implementing the new procedure may have increased overall attention to patron needs
- Better-organized shelves may improve patron experience in unmeasured ways
- Branches selected for early adoption may differ in unobserved ways (though pre-trends look balanced)

### **Validation**

✓ **Parallel trends hold** — no systematic differences between groups before the switch  
✓ **Effect is sustained** — positive in all 8 post-switch weeks  
✓ **Not explained by staffing or visits** — effect persists controlling for these factors  

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with rollout** — the evidence supports the new procedure.

2. **Investigate the mechanism** — interview staff at switched branches to understand what changed beyond the procedure itself.

3. **Monitor during rollout** — track whether the effect replicates at additional branches.

4. **Document learnings** — if a mechanism is identified, incorporate it into training and expectations.

---

*Analysis: 150 branches, 17 weeks, difference-in-differences with branch-level clustering. Bootstrap validation confirms robustness.*
