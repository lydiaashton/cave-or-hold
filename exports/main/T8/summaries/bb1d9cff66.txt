# **Board Summary: New Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure Evaluation**

---

## **Bottom Line**

We **cannot conclude with statistical confidence** that the new procedure improves service scores. The estimated effect is **positive (+2.4 points, ~5%)** but not statistically distinguishable from zero (p = 0.125). The data is compatible with three scenarios: (1) a meaningful improvement of up to 5 points, (2) no effect, or (3) a small negative effect.

---

## **Final Verdict**

**DO NOT PROCEED WITH FULL ROLLOUT BASED ON CURRENT EVIDENCE**

**Rationale:**
- The result is **not statistically significant** (p = 0.125), meaning we cannot rule out that the observed improvement is due to random chance
- The **95% confidence interval includes zero** (-0.7 to +5.4 points), so the true effect could be null or even slightly negative
- The study had **low statistical power (35%)**, making it an unreliable basis for a system-wide decision

**However**, the evidence is **promising enough to warrant further investigation**:
- 80% of treatment branches improved vs. 57% of control branches
- The effect appears to strengthen over time (weeks 18–22 showed larger effects)
- No negative side effects were detected on staffing or visit volumes

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** total: 20 switched to the new procedure (treatment), 14 kept the old procedure (control)
- **17 weeks** of data (weeks 6–22), with the switch implemented in week 15
- **9 weeks before** and **8 weeks after** the switch
- Analysis used a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in treatment branches to changes in control branches

### **Service Score Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (no switch) | 50.3 | 50.1 | -0.2 |
| Treatment (switched) | 48.8 | 51.0 | +2.2 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+2.4** |

- **Estimated effect: +2.38 points** (95% confidence interval: -0.7 to +5.4)
- **P-value: 0.125** (not statistically significant at the 0.05 level)

### **Supporting Evidence**

1. **Branch-level consistency**: 80% of treatment branches (16 of 20) showed improvement after switching, compared to only 57% of control branches (8 of 14)

2. **Effect grows over time**: The treatment effect was modest immediately after the switch but increased in later weeks (weeks 18–22 showed effects of 4.0–5.5 points, with week 22 reaching statistical significance)

3. **Parallel pre-trends**: Before the switch, both groups followed similar trends, supporting the validity of the comparison

4. **Not explained by other factors**: The improvement is not driven by changes in staffing levels or visit volumes

### **Why Isn't This Statistically Significant?**

The study was **underpowered** to detect an effect of this size:
- With 34 branches, we had only **35% statistical power** to detect a 2.4-point effect
- The minimum effect we could reliably detect (80% power) would be **4.2 points**
- Our observed effect (2.4 points) is meaningful but smaller than what this sample size can confirm with high confidence

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended Actions**

1. **Continue the pilot for 8–12 more weeks**: This will:
   - Double the post-switch observations
   - Increase statistical power to ~60–70%
   - Allow us to confirm whether the effect stabilizes, grows, or diminishes

2. **Set clear success criteria for rollout decision**:
   - Effect size ≥ 2.0 points AND p-value < 0.10, OR
   - Effect size ≥ 3.0 points (regardless of p-value, given practical significance)

3. **Monitor implementation fidelity**: Ensure branches are fully implementing the new procedure as designed

4. **Track branch-level variation**: Investigate why some treatment branches showed larger improvements than others (range: -3.1 to +8.6 points) to identify best practices

### **Decision Framework**

| If implementation is... | Recommendation |
|------------------------|----------------|
| Low cost, low disruption | Continue pilot; consider limited expansion to 5–10 additional branches |
| Moderate cost | Continue pilot only; no expansion until results are clearer |
| High cost or disruptive | Pause expansion; reassess after additional data is collected |
