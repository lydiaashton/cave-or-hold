# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

## **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The data do not provide statistically reliable evidence that the new procedure improved service scores.** While the estimated effect is positive (+1.9 points), the result is not statistically significant (p = 0.12), and the confidence interval includes zero. The observed difference could plausibly be due to random variation. **We recommend gathering more evidence before proceeding with a system-wide rollout.**

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

**DO NOT PROCEED WITH SYSTEM-WIDE ROLLOUT AT THIS TIME.**

**Rationale:**
- The evidence is **insufficient to confirm** the procedure works as intended
- Rolling out based on inconclusive data risks implementing an ineffective change across all branches
- The cost of waiting for more data is low; the cost of a failed system-wide rollout is higher

**Path Forward:** Extend the study by 8–12 weeks, then re-evaluate. If the effect persists and becomes statistically significant, proceed with rollout.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **Study Overview**
| | |
|---|---|
| **Branches Analyzed** | 34 total (20 switched to new procedure, 14 kept old) |
| **Study Period** | 17 weeks (Weeks 6–22) |
| **Switch Date** | Week 15 |
| **Method** | Difference-in-differences analysis |

### **Main Result**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| **New Procedure** | 52.2 | 52.5 | +0.3 |
| **Old Procedure** | 51.9 | 50.3 | −1.6 |
| **Estimated Effect** | | | **+1.9 points** |

- **Point Estimate:** +1.9 points on the service score index
- **95% Confidence Interval:** −0.5 to +4.3 points **(includes zero)**
- **Statistical Significance:** p = 0.12 **(not significant at 0.05 level)**

### **What This Means**

1. **We cannot conclude the procedure works.** The data are compatible with no effect, a small negative effect, or a moderate positive effect. We cannot distinguish signal from noise.

2. **The point estimate is in the expected direction.** The +1.9 point estimate aligns with the organization's expectation of improvement, but this alone does not establish the procedure is effective.

3. **The study may be underpowered.** With only 34 branches (the effective independent units), the study may lack sufficient statistical power to detect a modest true effect.

4. **Control branches declined.** Branches keeping the old procedure saw scores drop 1.6 points after Week 15. Understanding why may be as important as evaluating the new procedure.

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

### **Recommended: Gather More Evidence Before Rollout**

1. **Extend the observation period** by 8–12 weeks to increase statistical power
2. **Investigate the control group decline**—was this seasonal, or due to other operational factors?
3. **Consider a randomized rollout** to remaining branches to strengthen causal inference
4. **Re-analyze** with additional data before making a system-wide decision

### **If the Board Prefers to Proceed Despite Uncertainty**

- Implement **phased rollout** with clear success metrics
- Set a **formal evaluation date** (e.g., 12 weeks post-rollout)
- Be prepared to **reverse course** if additional data show no benefit

---

## **CONCLUSION**

The organization expected the new procedure to improve service scores. The data show a positive point estimate but **do not provide sufficient evidence to confirm this expectation**. Given the uncertainty, a cautious approach—gathering more data before committing to system-wide implementation—is warranted.
