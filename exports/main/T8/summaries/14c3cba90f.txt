# **Memorandum to the Board**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure shows a **positive direction of effect but inconclusive evidence**. Branches that adopted the new procedure improved their service scores by approximately **2.2 points** relative to branches that kept the old procedure. However, this result is not statistically significant at conventional levels (p=0.10), and the 95% confidence interval includes zero.

**There is approximately a 94% probability the true effect is positive, but we cannot rule out no effect.**

---

## **Final Verdict**

> **Do not proceed with system-wide rollout at this time.**

The data do not provide sufficient evidence that the new procedure improves service scores. While the estimated effect points in the expected direction, the uncertainty is too large to justify the costs and disruption of cooperative-wide implementation.

**Recommended path forward:**
1. **Continue the evaluation** with additional branches and weeks of data
2. **Reassess in 3 months** with stronger statistical power
3. **Proceed to full rollout only if future evidence reaches statistical significance** (p<0.05)

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Estimated Effect Is Positive But Uncertain**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Branches with New Procedure (20 branches) | 45.0 | 47.8 | +2.7 |
| Branches with Old Procedure (14 branches) | 48.7 | 49.2 | +0.5 |
| **Estimated Effect of New Procedure** | | | **+2.2** |

- **Point estimate:** +2.24 points
- **95% Confidence Interval:** -0.46 to +4.94 points
- **P-value:** 0.104

### **2. Switched Branches Still Lag Behind**

Despite the improvement, branches using the new procedure **underperformed control branches in 6 of 8 post-switch weeks**. The procedure may have helped them narrow the gap, but it did not make them outperform branches that kept the old procedure.

| Week | Control Avg | Switched Avg | Difference |
|------|-------------|--------------|------------|
| 15 | 51.1 | 51.1 | 0.0 |
| 16 | 47.2 | 47.2 | 0.0 |
| 17 | 51.4 | 51.3 | -0.1 |
| 18 | 54.7 | 51.4 | -3.3 |
| 19 | 49.1 | 45.2 | -3.8 |
| 20 | 46.1 | 42.1 | -4.1 |
| 21 | 46.5 | 44.8 | -1.7 |
| 22 | 47.4 | 48.9 | +1.5 |

### **3. Analysis Is Methodologically Sound**

- **Parallel trends verified:** Switched and control branches followed similar trajectories before the switch
- **Robust methods:** Difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors
- **Adequate pre-period:** 9 weeks of baseline data before the switch

### **4. Why the Uncertainty?**

- **Limited sample size:** Only 34 branches (20 switched, 14 control)
- **High week-to-week variability:** Service scores fluctuate substantially
- **Minimum detectable effect:** With this sample, we could reliably detect only effects of ~3.8 points or larger

---

## **What This Means for the Cooperative**

| Possible True Effect | Consistent with Data? | Implication |
|---------------------|----------------------|-------------|
| Large positive (+4 or more) | Yes | Rollout would meaningfully improve service |
| Modest positive (+1 to +3) | Yes | Rollout would provide slight benefit |
| No effect (0) | Yes | Rollout would have no impact |
| Small negative (-1 to -3) | Unlikely but possible | Rollout could slightly harm service |

**The data cannot distinguish among these scenarios with confidence.**
