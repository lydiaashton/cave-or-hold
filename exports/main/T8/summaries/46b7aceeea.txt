# **Board Summary: Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence is mixed.** While switched branches show higher service scores after the change, **the treatment and control groups were already on different trajectories before the switch**. The treatment-control gap was widening even before week 15 (from 0.7 points in week 6 to 2.6 points in week 14). 

After accounting for this pre-existing trend, the **adjusted effect is approximately 0.3 to 1.5 points** rather than the initial 2.1-point estimate. This is still positive but substantially smaller and less certain.

**Recommendation: Proceed with a phased rollout while continuing to monitor, rather than immediate system-wide implementation.**

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **Did service scores improve in switched branches?** | Yes, clearly |
| **Can we attribute this to the new procedure?** | Not with high confidence |
| **Is the new procedure harmful?** | No evidence of harm |
| **Should we roll out to all branches immediately?** | **No** — evidence is insufficient |
| **Should we abandon the new procedure?** | **No** — signals are positive |
| **Recommended path forward** | **Phased expansion with monitoring** |

**Bottom line verdict:** The new procedure shows promise but the current analysis cannot definitively prove causation. A cautious, data-driven expansion is warranted.

### 3. Adjusted Effect Estimate
| Measure | Estimate |

### 4. Effect Appears to Grow Over Time
Later post-treatment weeks (19-22) show larger gaps than early weeks (15-18), which could indicate:
- A genuine learning effect from the new procedure, OR
- Continuation of the pre-existing divergent trend

We cannot confidently distinguish between these explanations.

## **Methodology Notes**

**Design:** Difference-in-differences comparing switched vs. non-switched branches.

**Limitation:** The parallel trends assumption is violated—treatment and control groups were diverging before the intervention. This means the standard DiD estimate likely overstates the true effect.

**Data:** 709 branches, 17 weeks (12,053 observations), weeks 6-22.
