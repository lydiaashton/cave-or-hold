# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure produced an unexpected improvement in service scores.** Branches using the new procedure scored **2.2 points higher** (approximately 4–5%) than expected based on control branch trends. This effect is statistically significant and sustained across all post-switch weeks.

**Recommendation:** The data supports rolling out the new procedure, but the Board should weigh this benefit against implementation costs and operational priorities, since service score improvement was not an anticipated outcome of this change.

---

## **Study Design**

We analyzed weekly service scores from **150 branches** over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22). The new procedure was implemented at week 15:

| Group | Branches | Procedure |
|-------|----------|-----------|
| Treatment | 90 branches | New procedure (weeks 15–22) |
| Control | 60 branches | Old procedure (entire period) |

This design isolates the procedure's effect by comparing treatment branches to control branches, accounting for overall trends.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Service Score Improvement

| Group | Before (Weeks 6–14) | After (Weeks 15–22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------|---------------------|--------|
| New Procedure | 48.2 | 52.2 | +4.0 |
| Old Procedure | 48.2 | 50.0 | +1.8 |
| **Net Effect** | — | — | **+2.2** |

### 2. Statistical Confidence

- **95% confidence interval:** 1.3 to 3.2 points
- **p-value:** < 0.01
- The effect is unlikely to be due to chance

### 3. Pre-Trends Validate the Comparison

Before the switch, treatment and control branches moved in parallel:
- Average pre-switch difference: −0.02 points (essentially zero)
- No systematic differences in any pre-switch week

### 4. Effect Was Sustained

All 8 post-switch weeks showed positive effects (ranging from 2.9 to 4.0 points), with no evidence of fading.

---

## **Context for Decision-Making**

### What This Means

- A branch scoring 48 would be expected to score approximately **50** after adopting the new procedure
- The improvement is modest but real and consistent
- Service scores were not expected to change—this procedure was designed for operational efficiency, not patron-facing service

### Possible Explanations (Speculative)

Since this effect was unexpected, we cannot confirm the mechanism. Possibilities include:
- Staff spend less time locating materials, freeing time for patron assistance
- Better stock rotation keeps high-demand items available more consistently
- Clearer procedures improve staff confidence in patron interactions

---

## **Final Verdict**

**Proceed with system-wide rollout.**

The evidence is clear: the new procedure improves service scores. While this outcome was unexpected, the data is robust:

| Criterion | Assessment |
|-----------|------------|
| **Effect exists?** | Yes—statistically significant (p < 0.01) |
| **Effect is meaningful?** | Yes—4–5% improvement, sustained over 8 weeks |
| **Comparison is valid?** | Yes—parallel pre-trends confirmed |
| **Any harm detected?** | No—no negative effects observed |
| **90 branches already using it?** | Yes—successfully implemented |

**The Board should approve rollout to the remaining 60 branches.** The service score improvement is a beneficial side effect of a procedure change that was already underway. There is no statistical or operational reason to halt or reverse the transition.

**Caveats:**
- Monitor implementation costs and staff feedback during rollout
- Continue tracking service scores to confirm effects persist at full scale
- Consider investigating *why* this operational change improved service scores—understanding the mechanism could inform future improvements

---

## **Methodology Notes**

- Difference-in-differences regression with branch and week fixed effects
- Standard errors clustered by branch (10,000-iteration bootstrap confirms results)
- Controlled for staffing index and visits index
- 95% CI: [1.3, 3.2] points
