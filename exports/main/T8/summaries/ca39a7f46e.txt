## **Final Verdict**

### **DO NOT PROCEED WITH COOPERATIVE-WIDE ROLLOUT**

**The evidence does not support a causal claim that the new procedure improved service scores.** While branches using the new procedure show higher scores after the switch, these same branches were **already improving faster before the switch occurred**. We cannot determine whether the procedure itself caused the improvement or whether better-performing branches were simply selected for the pilot.

---

## **Bottom Line**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did service scores improve in switching branches? | **Yes** (+1.02 points) |
| Did the new procedure *cause* this improvement? | **Cannot determine** |
| Is rollout justified based on this analysis? | **No** |

---

## **Key Findings**

### **The Critical Flaw: Pre-Existing Trends**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Treatment group pre-switch trend | +0.26 points/week faster than control |
| Statistical significance | p = 0.0001 |
| Pre-trend divergence over 9 weeks | **2.33 points** |
| Observed "treatment effect" | **2.21 points** |
| **Explained by pre-trends** | **106%** |

**Interpretation:** The entire observed improvement can be explained by the fact that treatment branches were already on a stronger upward trajectory *before* the procedure change.

### **What the Data Shows**

| Group | Branches | Before | After | Change |
|-------|----------|--------|-------|--------|
| Control (Old Procedure) | 284 | 51.03 | 49.87 | −1.16 |
| Treatment (New Procedure) | 425 | 51.00 | 52.02 | +1.02 |
| **Raw Difference** | | | | **+2.19** |

The raw difference looks positive, but **this comparison is not valid** for causal inference because the groups were not equivalent before the intervention.

### **Why This Matters**

The difference-in-differences method requires **parallel trends**: both groups should have been on similar trajectories before the intervention. This assumption is **violated** in our data. When treatment and control groups differ systematically before the intervention, we cannot attribute post-intervention differences to the intervention itself.

| Better-managed branches were selected for pilot | **Likely** |
| Branches with upward momentum were chosen | **Likely** |

## **Recommendations**

### **Immediate Actions**

| Action | Priority | Timeline |
|--------|----------|----------|
| **Pause cooperative-wide rollout** | High | Immediate |
| Document how switching branches were selected | High | 2 weeks |
| Continue monitoring current adopters | Medium | Ongoing |
| Investigate branch selection criteria | High | 4 weeks |

### **Path Forward**

1. **Gather more information:**
   - How were the 425 switching branches selected?
   - Were there eligibility criteria (performance thresholds, staffing levels, etc.)?
   - What other differences exist between the two groups?

2. **Extend the observation period:**
   - Collect 8-12 additional weeks of data
   - Assess whether the gap widens, narrows, or stabilizes

3. **Consider alternative evaluation methods:**
   - Matched comparison (pair similar branches)
   - Randomized rollout for remaining branches
   - Interrupted time series on treatment branches only

4. **Revisit decision in Q1** with additional data and analysis

## **Conclusion**

> **We cannot recommend the cooperative-wide rollout at this time.** The analysis does not demonstrate that the new shelving procedure caused the observed improvements. Branches that adopted the procedure were already performing differently before the switch, making it impossible to isolate the procedure's effect. We recommend pausing the rollout, investigating the selection process, gathering additional data, and reconsidering with stronger evidence.
