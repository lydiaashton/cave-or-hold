# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Subject:** Recommendation on System-Wide Rollout of New Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure **likely improves service scores** by approximately 2 points (~4% improvement). The statistical evidence is **moderately strong** (95% probability the effect is positive), though not definitive. However, **30% of branches saw scores decline**, indicating the procedure does not work uniformly. We recommend a **supported voluntary rollout** with continued monitoring, rather than either a mandatory system-wide adoption or abandoning the procedure.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Impact on Service Scores**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Treatment (20 branches) | 48.7 | 50.5 | **+1.8** |
| Control (14 branches) | 47.6 | 47.5 | **-0.1** |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+1.9 points** |

- **95% Confidence Interval:** -0.3 to +4.2 points
- **80% Confidence Interval:** +0.5 to +3.4 points (entirely positive)
- **Probability of Positive Effect:** ~95%
- **Effect Size:** Medium (Cohen's d = 0.57)

### **Branch-Level Variation**

The procedure did not affect all branches equally:

- **14 of 20 treatment branches (70%)** improved after switching
- **6 of 20 treatment branches (30%)** saw scores decline
- **Top gainers:** +6 to +8 points
- **Largest declines:** -2 to -3.5 points
- **Control branches:** Only 50% improved during the same period

This variation suggests the procedure's effectiveness depends on branch-specific factors.

### **Data Quality & Validity**

- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **Parallel trends confirmed:** Treatment and control groups showed similar patterns before the switch
- **Controls accounted for:** Staffing levels and visit volumes both correlate with service scores

---

## **Final Verdict**

### **Proceed with Supported Voluntary Rollout**

**The evidence supports offering the new procedure to additional branches, but not mandating it system-wide.**

**Rationale:**
1. **Likely beneficial overall** — 95% probability of positive effect; ~4% average improvement
2. **Not universally effective** — 30% of pilot branches declined; one-size-fits-all mandate risks harming some locations
3. **Practical significance** — A 2-point gain on a 50-point scale is meaningful for patron experience
4. **Low regret** — Branches can revert if scores decline; continued monitoring limits downside risk

**Implementation Approach:**
- Open the procedure to **interested branches** on a voluntary basis
- Provide **enhanced training and implementation support** to all adopters
- Require **participation in ongoing evaluation** (monthly score reporting)
- Flag branches with **declining scores after 8 weeks** for targeted review

**Success Criteria for Future Mandatory Consideration:**
- Sustained +2 point improvement over 6 months
- Reduced branch-level variation (fewer than 20% of branches declining)
- Statistical significance at p < 0.05 with expanded dataset

## **Conclusion**

The new shelving procedure shows **real but uneven benefits**. A supported voluntary rollout balances the likely system-wide gains against the risk that some branches may not benefit. Continued monitoring will clarify which branch characteristics predict success and whether the procedure should eventually become standard practice.

---

*Analysis based on weekly service scores from 34 branches (weeks 6–22). Twenty branches adopted the new procedure in week 15; fourteen branches continued with the existing procedure. Service scores are an internal index where approximately 50 represents average performance.*
