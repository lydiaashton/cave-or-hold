# **Library Cooperative Stock Rotation Procedure: Impact on Service Scores**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new stock rotation and shelving procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **2.25 points** (approximately 4.5%) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant (p < 0.001). Contrary to the expectation that the change would make no difference, the evidence strongly supports rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure; **60 branches** kept the old procedure (control group)
- The switch occurred at **week 15**
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in the treatment group to changes in the control group

### Results

| Group | Pre-Switch Average | Post-Switch Average | Change |
|-------|-------------------|---------------------|--------|
| Control (Old Procedure) | 49.65 | 50.12 | +0.46 |
| Treatment (New Procedure) | 50.45 | 53.11 | +2.66 |
| **Net Effect** | — | — | **+2.25** |

### Statistical Evidence
- **Effect size:** +2.25 points on the service score index
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001 (highly significant)
- **95% confidence interval:** 1.0 to 3.5 points
- **Parallel trends validated:** Pre-switch trends were statistically similar between groups (p = 0.78), supporting the validity of the comparison

### Additional Insights
- The new procedure's effect remained significant after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume
- Both staffing and visits positively correlate with service scores, but the procedure effect is independent of these factors
- The improvement emerged consistently after week 15 and persisted through the observation period

---

## **Final Verdict**

**The new procedure should be adopted system-wide.**

The analysis provides robust evidence that the new stock rotation and shelving procedure improves service quality. The 2.25-point gain is both statistically reliable and practically meaningful — representing a 4.5% improvement over baseline scores. The study design rules out alternative explanations such as seasonal effects, underlying branch differences, or changes in staffing and visitor volume.

There is no statistical basis for withholding the new procedure from remaining branches. Delaying rollout would mean denying 60 branches a proven improvement to their service delivery.

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new stock rotation and shelving procedure to all 150 branches

2. **Monitor implementation** during the rollout to ensure consistent adoption and identify any branches needing additional support

3. **Continue tracking service scores** for at least 8–12 weeks post-rollout to confirm the effect persists at scale

4. **Document best practices** from the 90 early-adopter branches to support training at remaining branches

5. **Consider cost-benefit analysis** if the new procedure involves additional labor or resources, weighing the 4.5% service improvement against any incremental costs
