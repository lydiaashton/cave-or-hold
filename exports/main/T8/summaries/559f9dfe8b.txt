# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

## **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

### **BOTTOM LINE**

The new procedure **significantly improved service scores**. Branches that switched to the new procedure saw their service scores increase by approximately **2.5 points** (about 5% above baseline), while branches that kept the old procedure showed no change. This result is statistically significant and contradicts the initial expectation that the procedure change would have no effect on service quality.

**Recommendation: Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

### **FINAL VERDICT**

**Adopt the new procedure system-wide.**

The evidence is clear and unambiguous:

| Evidence | Result | Supports Rollout? |
|----------|--------|-------------------|
| Statistical significance | p = 0.0002 | ✓ Yes |
| Effect size | +2.5 points (5%) | ✓ Yes |
| Confidence interval | [1.18, 3.82] — entirely positive | ✓ Yes |
| Control group stability | No change (+0.01) | ✓ Yes |
| Pre-period trends | Parallel between groups | ✓ Yes |

**There is no statistical or practical basis for delaying rollout.** The new procedure improved service quality without requiring additional staffing or resources. The only risk is in *not* adopting a proven improvement.

---

### **KEY FINDINGS**

**Study Overview**
- Analyzed 150 branches over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- 90 branches adopted the new procedure at week 15; 60 branches kept the old procedure
- Used a difference-in-differences approach to isolate the causal effect of the procedure change

**Service Score Results**

| Group | Before Switch (Weeks 6–14) | After Switch (Weeks 15–22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------------|---------------------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 50.13 | 50.14 | +0.01 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 50.81 | 53.39 | +2.58 |

**Estimated Impact**
- The new procedure increased service scores by **2.50 points**
- 95% confidence interval: **1.18 to 3.82 points**
- Statistical significance: **p < 0.001** (highly significant)
- Effect size: Approximately **5% improvement** over baseline scores

**Validity Checks**
- Result holds after controlling for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume
- Pre-switch trends were parallel between groups, supporting the validity of the comparison
- Standard errors account for repeated observations at each branch

---

### **NEXT STEPS**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all remaining branches

2. **Monitor implementation** to ensure consistent adoption across all locations

3. **Track service scores** post-rollout to confirm the effect persists at scale

4. **Document best practices** from the 90 early-adopter branches to support training

5. **Investigate mechanisms** behind the improvement (e.g., faster item retrieval, better organization) to further optimize the approach

---

### **ANALYTICAL NOTE**

This analysis used a quasi-experimental difference-in-differences design, which compares the change in outcomes for branches that adopted the new procedure against the change for branches that did not. This approach controls for both time-invariant branch characteristics and common time trends, providing a credible estimate of the causal effect of the procedure change. Standard errors are clustered at the branch level to account for within-branch correlation over time.
