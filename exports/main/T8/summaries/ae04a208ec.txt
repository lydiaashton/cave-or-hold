# **Memorandum to the Board of Directors** *(Final)*

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure produced a small but real improvement in service scores.** Branches using the new procedure gained an average of **2.2 points (4.4%)** relative to branches using the old procedure. This effect is **statistically significant** (p = 0.001) and consistent across all post-switch weeks, but **modest in practical terms** — smaller than typical week-to-week score fluctuations. 

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rollout, but **temper expectations** and **monitor closely** to confirm the effect sustains at scale. The improvement is real but not transformative.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Statistically Significant but Modest Effect
- Treatment branches gained **+2.2 points** relative to control branches
- 95% confidence interval: **0.9 to 3.6 points** — the effect is reliably positive
- However, this is **smaller than typical week-to-week variation** (2.6 points) in service scores

### 2. Valid Comparison
Before the switch, treatment and control branches performed identically (difference: -0.2 points, p = 0.90). The post-switch improvement is attributable to the procedure change.

### 3. Meaningful Distribution Shift
While the average gain is modest, the procedure shifted the distribution:
- Treatment branches scoring above 50 increased from **52.7% to 61.8%** (+9 percentage points)
- This suggests more branches reached acceptable service levels

### 4. Consistent Across Time
The effect was positive in all 8 post-switch weeks, ranging from +1.3 to +3.0 points, indicating the result is stable, not a one-week anomaly.

### 5. Study Overview
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Total branches | 150 |
| Branches using new procedure | 90 |
| Study period | 17 weeks |
| Treatment effect | +2.2 points |
| Statistical significance | p = 0.001 |
| Practical magnitude | Modest |

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve phased rollout** rather than immediate system-wide implementation. Convert branches in batches to monitor effects.

2. **Set realistic expectations.** Communicate that the improvement is real but modest — not a transformational change.

3. **Track service scores for 12 weeks post-conversion** for each batch. If effects diminish at scale, pause and investigate.

4. **Investigate why the procedure works.** Interview staff at treatment branches to understand the mechanism — this may reveal opportunities to amplify the effect.

5. **Re-evaluate in 6 months.** If the effect sustains and no negative consequences emerge, complete the rollout. If not, consider whether the operational costs justify the modest gain.

---

## **Final Verdict**

**Approve the rollout — with conditions.**

The evidence supports adopting the new procedure: the effect is real, consistent, and positive. However, because the magnitude is modest, we recommend:

| Decision | Rationale |
|----------|-----------|
| ✅ **Proceed with rollout** | Statistically significant improvement; no evidence of harm |
| ⚠️ **Phase implementation** | Monitor effects at scale before full commitment |
| ⚠️ **Set modest expectations** | This is an incremental gain, not a breakthrough |
| ⚠️ **Revisit in 6 months** | Confirm sustainability before locking in permanently |

**Bottom line for the Board:** This change makes things slightly better, not dramatically better. If the operational costs of the new procedure are low, it is worth adopting. If costs are substantial, the modest benefit may not justify system-wide implementation.

---

*Analysis based on weekly service score data from 150 branches over 17 weeks, using difference-in-differences methodology with branch fixed effects and cluster-robust standard errors.*
