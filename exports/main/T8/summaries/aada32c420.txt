# **Board Summary: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The data does not provide clear evidence that the new procedure improved service scores.** While switched branches show higher scores after the change, this apparent improvement is explained by pre-existing differences between branches—not the new procedure itself. When we account for the fact that switched branches were already improving faster before the change, there is **no detectable additional benefit** from the new procedure. **We do not recommend system-wide rollout based on this evidence.**

---

## **Final Verdict**

> **Do not proceed with system-wide rollout.**
>
> The observed improvement in service scores at switched branches **cannot be attributed to the new procedure**. The evidence indicates that branches selected for the new procedure were already on a stronger performance trajectory before implementation. After accounting for these pre-existing trends, the estimated effect of the new procedure is **approximately zero**.
>
> Rolling out the procedure system-wide would incur costs without demonstrated benefit. If the board wishes to pursue this initiative further, a **randomized controlled evaluation** should be conducted before any expansion.

## **Key Findings**

### **1. What the Raw Data Shows**
| Group | Pre-Switch Average | Post-Switch Average | Change |
|-------|-------------------|---------------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 50.69 | 52.45 | +1.76 |
| Non-switched branches | 48.96 | 48.89 | -0.07 |

At first glance, switched branches appear to have gained **+1.8 points** relative to non-switched branches.

### **2. The Critical Problem: Pre-Existing Trends**
Branches that switched were **already different** before the procedure change:

| Metric | Finding |
|--------|---------|
| **Baseline gap** (weeks 6-14) | Switched branches scored 1.7 points higher |
| **Pre-switch trend** | The gap was **widening by 0.23 points per week** before the switch (p = 0.001) |
| **Implication** | Switched branches were on an upward trajectory regardless of the new procedure |

### **3. What Happens When We Account for Pre-Trends**
If the pre-switch trend had simply continued (without any effect from the new procedure), we would expect:

| Week | Predicted Gap* | Actual Gap | Difference |
|------|---------------|------------|------------|
| 15 | 2.85 | 2.69 | -0.16 |
| 16 | 3.08 | 2.83 | -0.25 |
| 17 | 3.30 | 2.97 | -0.33 |
| 18 | 3.53 | 3.43 | -0.10 |
| 19 | 3.76 | 4.02 | +0.26 |
| 20 | 3.98 | 3.98 | 0.00 |
| 21 | 4.21 | 3.92 | -0.29 |
| 22 | 4.43 | 4.64 | +0.20 |

*Predicted based on pre-switch trend continuing

**Average excess gap: -0.08 points** (essentially zero)

### **4. Why This Matters**
The standard statistical method (difference-in-differences) assumes both groups would have followed parallel trends without the intervention. **This assumption is violated here.** Switched branches were already improving faster, so the 1.8-point estimate is biased upward.

## **Recommendations & Next Steps**

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| **1** | **Do not proceed with system-wide rollout** | No clear evidence of benefit |
| **2** | **Investigate why switched branches differ** | Were they larger? Better staffed? More resources? |
| **3** | **Consider a randomized pilot** | Randomly assign remaining branches to switch now vs. later |
| **4** | **Collect more data** | Extend observation period to 6+ months post-switch |
| **5** | **Evaluate implementation costs** | Even a small benefit may not justify costs |

---

## **What Would Constitute Stronger Evidence?**

To make a confident recommendation, we would need:

1. **Randomized assignment** of branches to treatment vs. control
2. **Longer observation period** (6-12 months post-implementation)
3. **Evidence of parallel pre-trends** between groups
4. **Consistent effects** across different types of branches

---

## **Decision Guidance**

| Scenario | Recommendation |
|----------|---------------|
| Implementation costs are **low** and board is **risk-tolerant** | Consider limited expansion with careful monitoring |
| Implementation costs are **significant** | Do not proceed; require stronger evidence |
| Board wants **high confidence** | Require randomized evaluation before rollout |
