# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

## **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

### **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores, and we recommend rolling it out to all branches.** Branches that switched saw service scores increase by approximately **1.9 points** relative to branches that did not switch. This effect is statistically significant (p < 0.01), consistent across most branches, and shows no signs of harm. While the improvement was unexpected—the procedure change was not designed to affect service quality—the data is clear that it did.

---

### **Final Verdict**

**ROLL OUT TO ALL BRANCHES**

The evidence supports system-wide adoption. The new procedure delivers a measurable service quality improvement at no detected cost.

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. Measurable Improvement in Service Scores**
- Treatment branches (90 branches that switched) improved by an average of **3.4 points** from before to after the switch
- Control branches (60 branches that did not switch) improved by only **1.5 points** over the same period
- The **net effect of the new procedure is +1.9 points** on the service score index

**2. Strong Statistical Evidence**
- The effect is highly statistically significant (p < 0.01)
- 95% confidence interval: **+0.8 to +3.1 points**
- Results are robust across multiple analytical approaches

**3. Broad-Based Benefits**
- **83% of treatment branches** saw their service scores improve after switching
- Only 68% of control branches improved during the same period
- The effect is consistent across the treatment group

**4. Valid Comparison**
- Treatment and control branches had similar service scores before the switch
- Both groups showed parallel trends before the intervention
- The improvement appeared immediately after week 15 and persisted

**5. Effect Size in Context**
- The average branch service score is approximately 50
- A 1.9-point improvement represents roughly a **4% increase** in service quality

---

### **Important Considerations**

**Why This Matters**

The procedure change was designed for operational efficiency, not service quality. The fact that it improved service scores is a bonus—but one we should understand. Possible explanations:

- Staff freed up by efficient shelving may have more time for patron assistance
- Faster material availability could improve patron experience
- Staff engagement with the new procedure may have had spillover effects

**Study Limitations**

- Only 8 weeks of post-switch data; longer-term effects are unknown
- We cannot definitively explain *why* service scores improved
- Effects during full rollout may differ from the pilot

---

### **Next Steps**

1. **Approve System-Wide Rollout**
   - Extend the new procedure to all remaining branches
   - Target completion within the next 6 months

2. **Monitor During Implementation**
   - Track service scores monthly during rollout
   - Flag any branches where scores decline for follow-up

3. **Investigate the Mechanism**
   - Interview staff at high-performing branches to understand what changed
   - Document practices that correlate with larger gains

4. **Follow-Up Review**
   - Schedule a board update after 6 months of full implementation
   - Confirm that effects persist at scale

---

### **Conclusion**

The new procedure has delivered an unexpected but welcome benefit: improved service scores alongside its intended operational improvements. The evidence is strong, the effect is positive, and there are no detected harms. We recommend proceeding with confidence.
