# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2025  
**Subject:** Recommendation on System-Wide Rollout of New Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Proceed with rollout, but with monitoring and evaluation.** The data show a statistically significant 2-point (4%) improvement in service scores at branches that adopted the new procedure. However, we cannot be fully certain this improvement was caused by the procedure itself, as switched branches were already improving faster before the change. Given the positive signal, low apparent risk, and potential benefit to patrons, we recommend moving forward while committing to continued evaluation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **709 branches** analyzed over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- **425 branches** adopted the new procedure at week 15; **284 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Service scores average approximately 52 points (higher is better)

### What the Data Show

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated improvement | **+2.0 points** |
| Statistical significance | **p < 0.001** (highly significant) |
| 95% Confidence Interval | **+1.3 to +2.7 points** |
| Relative improvement | **~4% of average score** |
| Effect consistency | **7 of 8 post-switch weeks show positive effects** |

### Important Caveat

Branches that switched to the new procedure were **already improving faster** than other branches before the switch occurred. The gap between switched and non-switched branches widened from 0.8 points (week 6) to 2.5 points (week 14, just before the switch).

This means we cannot rule out that switched branches would have continued improving regardless of the procedure change. **The true effect could be smaller than 2 points — possibly zero.**

### Why We Still Recommend Proceeding

| Consideration | Assessment |
|--------------|------------|
| **Direction of evidence** | All indicators point positive; no negative signals |
| **Statistical strength** | Highly significant; stable across analytical approaches |
| **Practical significance** | 4% improvement is meaningful for patron experience |
| **Risk of harm** | No evidence of negative effects on service scores |
| **Cost of waiting** | Delays potential benefits to patrons at 284 branches |

---

## **Final Verdict**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Recommendation** | ✅ **APPROVE ROLLOUT** |
| **Confidence Level** | ⚠️ **Moderate** (not high) |
| **Best-Case Effect** | +2.0 points (4% improvement) |
| **Worst-Case Effect** | No meaningful change |
| **Risk of Harm** | Low (no negative effects detected) |

**Rationale:** The evidence is imperfect but points in a positive direction. In the absence of red flags and given the potential patron benefit, the prudent course is to proceed while maintaining accountability through continued measurement.

---

## **Recommendation**

**Approve system-wide rollout with the following conditions:**

1. **Implement at all remaining branches** over the next 8–12 weeks
2. **Continue weekly service score tracking** for all branches
3. **Re-evaluate in 12 weeks** with expanded data to confirm the effect
4. **Document implementation details** at each branch to identify best practices

## **Risk Mitigation**

If the 12-week follow-up analysis shows:
- **Confirmed improvement** → Continue current procedure; consider additional optimizations
- **No effect detected** → Revert to old procedure at low-performing branches; investigate further
- **Negative effect detected** → Pause and reassess procedure design (no evidence of this risk currently)
