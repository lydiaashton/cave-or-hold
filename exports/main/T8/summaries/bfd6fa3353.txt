# MEMORANDUM

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Impact of New Shelving Procedure on Branch Service Scores

---

## Bottom Line

**The new shelving procedure produced a statistically significant but modest improvement in service scores (+1.8 points, p=0.024). The effect is real but small—approximately 3.5% above the average score.**

---

## Key Findings

### 1. Measurable Improvement in Service Scores

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated improvement | **+1.79 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | 0.24 to 3.34 points |
| Statistical significance | p = 0.024 |
| Relative to average score | **+3.5%** |
| Effect size (Cohen's d) | **0.18 (small)** |

Branches that adopted the new procedure saw their service scores rise relative to branches that kept the old procedure. The effect is unlikely to be due to chance.

### 2. Analysis Is Reliable

- **Fair comparison confirmed**: Before the switch (weeks 6-14), treated and control branches showed parallel trends in service scores (p=0.92). This validates our comparison method.
- **Robust to other factors**: The improvement holds after accounting for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume.
- **Large sample**: Analysis covers 2,550 weekly observations from all 150 branches over 17 weeks.

### 3. Effect Pattern Over Time

The improvement varied week-to-week, which is normal for operational changes:

- **Strongest gains**: Weeks 16, 20, and 21 (+4.0, +3.4, and +4.2 points)
- **Modest gains**: Other weeks showed smaller but generally positive effects
- **No negative effects**: No week showed a statistically significant decline

---

## Final Verdict

**Recommendation: Proceed with cautious, phased rollout.**

| Consideration | Assessment |
|---------------|------------|
| **Statistical evidence** | ✓ Significant (p=0.024) |
| **Practical significance** | ⚠ Small effect (d=0.18) |
| **Confidence in estimate** | ⚠ Wide CI (0.24 to 3.34) |
| **Prior expectation** | ✓ Effect detected (expected none) |
| **Risk of harm** | ✓ No negative effects observed |

**Rationale:**

1. **The effect is real but modest.** We can be confident the procedure improves scores, but the magnitude is small relative to normal week-to-week variation.

2. **No evidence of harm.** The new procedure did not reduce service scores in any measurable way.

3. **Costs matter.** Without knowing implementation costs (training, equipment, staff time), we cannot confirm the benefit justifies full rollout. A phased approach allows cost tracking alongside benefit verification.

4. **Uncertainty remains.** The lower bound of our confidence interval (0.24 points) suggests the true effect could be trivial. Continued monitoring during rollout will refine this estimate.
