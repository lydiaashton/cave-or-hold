# **Board Memorandum (Final)**

## **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

### **Bottom Line**

**Proceed with caution.** The data suggests the new procedure improved service scores, but we cannot precisely measure the effect. Switched branches were already improving faster than control branches *before* the change, which limits our ability to isolate the procedure's true impact. We recommend a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than immediate system-wide implementation.

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. Observed Improvement (With Caveats)**
- Standard analysis suggests a **~2.0 point improvement** in service scores
- However, this estimate rests on an assumption that the data does not support

**2. Critical Limitation: Pre-Existing Trends**
| Period | Control Branch Trend | Switched Branch Trend |
|--------|---------------------|----------------------|
| Before week 15 | Flat (0.06 pts/week, not significant) | Rising (0.27 pts/week, **statistically significant**) |

- Switched branches were already on an upward trajectory before adopting the new procedure
- This violates the key assumption needed to cleanly measure the procedure's effect
- The true effect could be smaller than 2.0 points—or potentially larger

**3. What We Can Say With Confidence**
- Switched branches ended the observation period with notably higher scores than control branches
- The gap between groups widened after week 15
- The new procedure is associated with better outcomes, but causation is uncertain

**4. Analysis Scope**
- 709 branches, 17 weeks of data (weeks 6-22)
- 425 switched branches, 284 control branches
- Over 12,000 branch-week observations

---

### **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **Did service scores improve at switched branches?** | Yes, clearly |
| **Can we attribute this entirely to the new procedure?** | No—pre-existing trends confound the result |
| **Is the new procedure likely harmful?** | No evidence of harm |
| **Is full rollout justified based on this data alone?** | No—evidence is suggestive but not definitive |
| **Recommended action?** | Phased expansion with improved measurement |

**Rationale:** The switched branches appear to have been higher-performing and on a stronger trajectory before the procedure change. This could reflect selection bias (better branches were chosen to pilot the new procedure) rather than procedure effects. However, there is no evidence of negative impact, and the association with improved scores is consistent. A cautious, data-driven expansion is warranted.

---

### **Next Steps**

1. **Approve phased rollout** – Expand to additional branches in stages rather than all at once

2. **Improve measurement** – For the next phase, consider:
   - Random assignment of branches to new vs. old procedure
   - Or staggered rollout with multiple "switch dates" for cleaner analysis

3. **Continue monitoring** – Track service scores for all transitioning branches for at least 8 weeks post-switch

4. **Gather qualitative data** – Interview staff at switched branches to understand operational changes that may explain score improvements

5. **Reassess after next phase** – With better data, determine whether to proceed with full rollout, modify the procedure, or halt expansion
