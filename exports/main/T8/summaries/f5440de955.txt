# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Analysis Period:** Weeks 6–22 (17 weeks of data)

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure provided measurable protection against service score decline.** Contrary to our initial expectation that the change would make no difference, the data shows a clear effect: branches using the new procedure declined only slightly (-0.3 points) while control branches dropped substantially (-2.2 points). The net benefit is **+1.9 points of decline avoided**, which is statistically significant (p = 0.002). However, the procedure did not improve scores in absolute terms—both groups declined. **We recommend rollout, but with tempered expectations: the benefit is protective rather than enhancing.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
- **150 branches** tracked over 17 weeks
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15
- **60 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)

### What Actually Happened to Service Scores

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Branches that switched | 49.86 | 49.56 | **-0.31** |
| Branches that did not switch | 49.14 | 46.92 | **-2.22** |
| **Net Effect (Decline Avoided)** | | | **+1.92** |

### Critical Interpretation
- **Did scores improve?** No—treatment branches also declined, just less severely
- **What was the benefit?** The new procedure shielded branches from approximately 1.9 points of decline that affected the control group
- **Why did everyone decline?** Something affected the entire cooperative after week 15 (possibly seasonal factors, external conditions, or measurement changes)
- **Is the effect real?** Yes—statistically significant (p = 0.002) with a 95% confidence interval of +0.7 to +3.1 points

### Effect Size Context
- **Magnitude:** 1.9 points on a ~50-point scale (approximately 4%)
- **Relative to variability:** About 0.22 standard deviations—a modest but meaningful effect
- **Practical value:** Preventing decline may be as valuable as generating improvement, depending on board priorities

---

## **Final Verdict**

**✅ PROCEED WITH ROLLOUT**

**Rationale:**
1. **The hypothesis was rejected.** We expected no difference; the data shows a real, statistically significant effect.
2. **The effect is beneficial.** Even though absolute scores declined, the new procedure demonstrably protected branches from worse outcomes.
3. **No evidence of harm.** Treatment branches did not underperform; they outperformed control branches relative to the counterfactual.
4. **The benefit is reliable.** The result holds across multiple statistical specifications and is unlikely to be due to chance.

**Caveats:**
- This is a **modest** benefit, not a transformation. Expect ~2 points of decline prevention, not score increases.
- The broader decline affecting all branches warrants investigation—the new procedure mitigates but does not solve this.
- If the board's strategic goal is absolute score improvement above baseline, this procedure alone will not achieve it.

---

## **Recommendation**

**Approve system-wide rollout** to the remaining 60 branches, with clear communication that the procedure protects service quality during challenging periods rather than elevating it above historical levels.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Board vote** on rollout approval at next meeting

2. **Investigate the broader decline** affecting all branches after week 15—this may warrant separate attention

3. **Develop implementation timeline** for remaining 60 branches (target: 8–12 weeks)

4. **Monitor post-rollout** to confirm the protective effect persists at scale

5. **Consider complementary initiatives** if the board seeks absolute score improvement rather than decline prevention

---

*Analysis conducted using difference-in-differences regression with branch and week fixed effects. The initial hypothesis (no effect) was rejected at p < 0.01.*
