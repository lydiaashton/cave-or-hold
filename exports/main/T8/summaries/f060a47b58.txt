# **BOARD SUMMARY: Impact of New Branch Procedure on Service Scores**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure produced a modest but statistically significant improvement in service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure improved by approximately **2.1 points more** than branches that kept the old procedure (p < 0.001). This represents about a 4% gain on the service score index.

**The effect is real but modest.** All 8 post-switch weeks show positive differences, suggesting the result is not a statistical fluke. However, the effect size is small, and we have only 8 weeks of data to assess sustainability.

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **Did the new procedure change service scores?** | **Yes** — statistically significant improvement of +2.1 points |
| **Is the effect large enough to matter?** | **Modestly** — meaningful but not transformative (~4% gain) |
| **Should we roll out to all branches?** | **Yes, with caveats** — proceed while managing expectations |
| **How confident are we?** | **High** on existence of effect; **moderate** on long-term magnitude |

**Bottom-line recommendation:** Approve system-wide rollout, but frame expectations appropriately and commit to ongoing monitoring.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Overview**
- **150 branches** participated: 90 adopted the new procedure, 60 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- **Complete data** for all branches across all weeks (2,550 observations)

### **Main Result**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| New Procedure (90 branches) | 48.95 | 52.00 | +3.05 |
| Old Procedure (60 branches) | 48.79 | 49.71 | +0.92 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+2.13** |

- **Treatment effect: +2.13 points** (95% confidence interval: 0.97 to 3.30)
- **Statistical significance: p = 0.0003** (highly significant)
- **Effect size: Cohen's d = 0.21** (small but meaningful)

### **Why We Trust This Result**

1. **Parallel trends confirmed:** Before the switch, treatment and control branches had nearly identical service score patterns (average difference of only 0.16 points with no meaningful trend)

2. **Effect persists after controls:** The 2.1-point improvement remains unchanged when accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume

3. **Consistent across post-switch weeks:** All 8 weeks after the switch show positive effects (ranging from +1.1 to +3.8 points)

4. **No pre-existing differences:** The two groups were comparable before the procedure change, supporting a causal interpretation

## **Technical Appendix**

**Method:** Difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch)

**Model:** Service Score = β₀ + β₁(Switched Branch) + β₂(Post-Period) + β₃(Switched × Post) + Controls

**Key coefficient (β₃):** 2.13, SE = 0.59, 95% CI [0.97, 3.30], p < 0.001

**Controls tested:** Staffing index, visits index (neither altered the main finding)

**Limitations:**
- Only 8 weeks of post-switch data available
- Effect size is modest (small by conventional standards)
- Long-term sustainability unknown
