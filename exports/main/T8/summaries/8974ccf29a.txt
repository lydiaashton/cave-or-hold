# **MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2025  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure  

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Recommend proceeding with systemwide rollout.** The new shelving and stock rotation procedure produced a statistically significant improvement in service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure saw scores increase by approximately **1.9 points** relative to branches that continued with the old procedure.

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did the new procedure improve service scores? | **Yes** |
| Is the evidence statistically reliable? | **Yes** (p = 0.017) |
| Should we roll out to all branches? | **Yes, with monitoring** |
| How confident are we? | **Moderate to high** — the effect is real, but the true magnitude could range from modest (+0.3) to substantial (+3.5) |

**Rationale:** The 95% confidence interval [0.34, 3.46] does not include zero, confirming a positive effect. However, the lower bound is close to zero, and we have only 8 weeks of post-switch data. The recommendation is to proceed, but with committed tracking to verify benefits persist and accumulate over time.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Analysis Overview**

| Component | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| Branches studied | 34 total (20 switched, 14 did not) |
| Observation period | 17 weeks (weeks 6–22) |
| Switch date | Week 15 |
| Method | Difference-in-differences with branch and week fixed effects |

### **Service Score Results**

| Period | Treatment Branches (New Procedure) | Control Branches (Old Procedure) |
|--------|-----------------------------------|----------------------------------|
| Before switch (weeks 6–14) | 49.7 | 49.6 |
| After switch (weeks 15–22) | 50.9 | 48.9 |
| **Change** | **+1.2 points** | **-0.7 points** |

**Estimated effect of new procedure: +1.9 points**  
**95% Confidence Interval: +0.3 to +3.5 points**  
**Statistical significance: p = 0.017**

### **Why We Trust This Result**

1. **Parallel trends verified:** Treatment and control branches followed similar service score patterns before the switch.

2. **Robust to controls:** The effect persists after accounting for weekly staffing levels and customer visit volumes.

3. **Consistent post-switch:** The treatment-control gap widened in most weeks after the switch.

4. **Adequate sample:** 578 branch-week observations provide reasonable statistical power.

---

## **Recommendations & Next Steps**

### **1. Approve Systemwide Rollout** ✓
Expand the new procedure to all 14 branches currently using the old method.

### **2. Implementation Timeline**
- **Months 1–2:** Train staff at remaining branches
- **Months 3–6:** Monitor service scores during transition
- **Month 6+:** Evaluate long-term sustainability

### **3. Ongoing Monitoring**
Track weekly service scores by branch during rollout. If scores do not improve within 8–12 weeks, investigate implementation fidelity.

### **4. Success Criteria**
- **Target:** +1.5 points average improvement per branch
- **Minimum acceptable:** +0.5 points (lower bound of confidence interval)
- **Review point:** 6 months post-rollout
