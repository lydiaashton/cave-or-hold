# Library Cooperative Branch Procedure Change: Impact Analysis

**Date:** November 2026  
**Prepared for:** Board of Directors  

---

## Bottom Line

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure is associated with a **2-point improvement in service scores** (approximately 4% increase from baseline). However, this result is **not statistically significant** at conventional levels (p=0.105), and the confidence interval includes zero. We **cannot conclude with confidence** that the procedure caused the improvement. We recommend **against immediate system-wide rollout** and instead advise continued evaluation with additional data collection.

---

## Key Findings

### Study Design
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure at week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure as a control group
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in treatment branches against changes in control branches

### Results

| Metric | Control (No Switch) | Treatment (Switched) |
|--------|---------------------|----------------------|
| Service score before week 15 | 50.89 | 49.69 |
| Service score after week 15 | 50.90 | 51.72 |
| **Change** | **+0.01** | **+2.03** |

**Difference-in-Differences Estimate: +2.02 points**

### Statistical Significance
- **p-value: 0.105** (does not meet the conventional 0.05 threshold)
- **95% confidence interval: -0.4 to +4.5 points**
- The interval includes zero, meaning we cannot rule out no effect
- The data is consistent with either no effect or a meaningful positive effect (up to ~4.5 points)

### Branch-Level Patterns
- **70% of treatment branches** showed improvement after switching
- **50% of control branches** showed improvement (expected by chance)
- Treatment branch improvements ranged from -3.5 to +10.4 points
- Pre-switch trends were not significantly different between groups, supporting the validity of the comparison

### Effect Size Context
- A 2-point gain represents about **4% improvement** relative to the typical branch score of ~50
- If real, this is a **modest but meaningful** improvement in service quality

---

## Final Verdict

**Do not proceed with system-wide rollout at this time.**

**Rationale:**
1. **Statistical uncertainty:** The p-value of 0.105 means there is roughly a 10% chance of observing this result even if the procedure has no real effect. This exceeds standard thresholds for decision-making.

2. **Confidence interval includes zero:** The true effect could be anywhere from slightly negative (-0.4) to substantially positive (+4.5). Committing to system-wide rollout risks investing in a procedure that may not work.

3. **Limited post-switch data:** Only 8 weeks of post-implementation data are available. Effects may change over time (e.g., initial enthusiasm could fade, or staff could improve with practice).

4. **Substantial branch variation:** Some branches improved dramatically while others declined. Understanding this variation before expansion would improve targeting and implementation.

**Recommended path forward:**
- Continue monitoring the current 20 treatment branches for an additional 8–12 weeks
- Consider expanding to 5–10 additional branches as a second pilot wave
- Re-evaluate with more data in Q1 2027
- If the effect persists and precision improves (narrower confidence interval), revisit system-wide rollout

---

## Next Steps

### Immediate Actions

1. **Extend data collection:** Continue weekly service score tracking for all branches through at least week 30.

2. **Qualitative follow-up:** Interview branch managers from high-performing and low-performing treatment branches to identify implementation factors.

3. **Cost assessment:** Document training time, staff hours, and any operational costs associated with the new procedure.

4. **Second-wave pilot:** If resources allow, invite 5–10 additional branches to adopt the procedure, maintaining the remaining branches as controls.

### Decision Triggers

| Condition | Action |
|-----------|--------|
| p < 0.05 with positive effect in follow-up analysis | Proceed with system-wide rollout |
| p > 0.20 or effect near zero | Discontinue expansion; explore alternatives |
| p between 0.05–0.20 | Continue phased evaluation with additional branches |

---

## Conclusion

The new procedure shows **promising but inconclusive results**. The point estimate suggests a 4% service score improvement, but statistical uncertainty is too high to justify system-wide investment. A **patient, evidence-based approach**—collecting more data while maintaining experimental controls—will enable a more confident decision in early 2027.
