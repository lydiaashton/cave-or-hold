# **Library Cooperative Service Procedure Change**
## **Impact Analysis & Recommendation**

---

### **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure likely improved service scores, but confidence is moderate.** The estimated effect is **+1.9 points** (3.7% improvement), statistically significant at p < 0.001. However, branches selected for the new procedure had higher baseline scores, suggesting non-random assignment. The true effect is probably positive but may be smaller than estimated.

**Recommendation: Proceed with rollout, with enhanced monitoring.**

---

### **Key Findings**

**Study Overview**
- **709 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **425 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15
- **284 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)

**Results**

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|---------------|---------------|--------|
| **New Procedure** | 51.6 | 52.4 | +0.7 |
| **Old Procedure** | 50.2 | 49.0 | −1.2 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+1.9** |

**Statistical Confidence**
- Effect size: **+1.90 points**
- 95% confidence interval: **1.34 to 2.45 points**
- Statistical significance: **p < 0.001**

**Important Caveats**
| Concern | Finding | Implication |
|---------|---------|-------------|
| **Baseline differences** | Treatment branches scored 1.5 points higher *before* the switch | Better branches may have been selected first |
| **Pre-trend violation** | Groups showed different trajectories before week 15 (p=0.003) | Standard DiD assumptions not fully met |
| **Control group decline** | Control scores dropped 1.2 points after week 15 | Part of the "effect" reflects control deterioration, not just treatment improvement |

**What This Means**
- The new procedure is associated with improved scores
- The +1.9 estimate may be inflated due to selection bias
- The true effect is likely positive but possibly smaller (e.g., +1.0 to +1.5 points)
- No evidence of harm; treatment branches improved while controls declined

---

### **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **Did the new procedure improve scores?** | **Probably yes** |
| **Is the 1.9-point estimate reliable?** | **Uncertain—likely overstated** |
| **Should we roll out to all branches?** | **Yes, with monitoring** |
| **Confidence level** | **Moderate (not definitive)** |

**Rationale:** Despite methodological limitations, three factors support rollout: (1) no evidence of negative effects, (2) 425 branches successfully implemented the change, and (3) the direction of effect aligns with expectations. However, the board should expect a more modest improvement than the headline 1.9 points.
