# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** Impact of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure on Service Scores

**Date:** [Current Date]

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure produced a **statistically significant 2.1-point improvement** in service scores (approximately 4% gain). The effect is real and robust across multiple analytical tests. However, the magnitude is modest, and non-random branch selection introduces some uncertainty about exact replication at scale.

**Verdict: The new procedure works. Recommend rollout with monitored implementation.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### Service Score Impact

| Group | Branches | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|----------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Treatment (switched) | 90 | 50.8 | 53.7 | +3.0 |
| Control (no switch) | 60 | 47.6 | 48.4 | +0.9 |
| **Net Effect** | — | — | — | **+2.1** |

### Statistical Confidence

- **Highly significant** (p < 0.001)
- **95% confidence interval:** 1.3 to 2.8 points
- Effect persists after controlling for branch characteristics, weekly variations, staffing levels, and visitor volume
- Pre-switch trends were parallel between groups (p = 0.28), supporting valid comparison

### Important Caveats

1. **Non-random selection**: Treatment branches started 6.7% higher than control branches (50.8 vs. 47.6), suggesting branches were not randomly assigned to switch. This may reflect volunteer bias or systematic selection.

2. **Modest effect size**: 2.1 points equals 0.22 standard deviations — meaningful but not transformative.

3. **Generalizability**: The exact effect may vary when rolling out to remaining branches, which differed systematically from early adopters.

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did the procedure change service scores? | **Yes** — statistically significant improvement |
| Is the effect large? | **Modest** — 4% gain, about 0.22 SD |
| Can we trust the comparison? | **Reasonably** — parallel pre-trends, but non-random selection limits certainty |
| Should we roll out system-wide? | **Yes** — positive effect with no identified harms, but monitor closely |

**Bottom-line recommendation:** Proceed with rollout, but set realistic expectations. The gain is real but incremental. Implementation quality and branch-level support will affect outcomes.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** to remaining 60 branches

2. **Phase implementation** over 4-6 weeks with training support for each cohort

3. **Track service scores** for 8-12 weeks post-rollout to confirm effect persists

4. **Document lessons learned** from early-adopter branches to support later adopters

5. **Re-evaluate after full rollout** — if effects match current estimates, the procedure delivers meaningful value; if effects are smaller, investigate implementation gaps

---

## **Appendix: Methodology**

**Data:** 2,550 weekly observations from 150 branches over 17 weeks (weeks 6-22). Switch occurred at week 15.

**Method:** Difference-in-differences regression with branch and week fixed effects, controlling for staffing index and visits index.
