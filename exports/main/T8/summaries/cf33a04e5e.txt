# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

## **Subject: Evaluation of New Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure shows a modest but statistically significant improvement in service scores.** Branches using the new procedure averaged **2 points higher** (approximately 4% improvement) than would be expected based on control branch trends. This contradicts our prior expectation of no effect.

**Recommendation:** The evidence supports proceeding with rollout, but expectations should be calibrated—the benefit is real but modest. Continue monitoring to confirm the effect persists.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Results Summary

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect | +1.96 service score points |
| 95% confidence interval | +1.05 to +2.87 points |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 |
| Effect size (relative to mean) | ~4% improvement |
| Effect size (Cohen's d) | 0.20 (small-to-moderate) |

### Important Context

1. **The effect is consistent in direction but varies in magnitude**: Post-switch weekly effects ranged from 0.1 to 2.0 points, suggesting the benefit may fluctuate over time.

2. **Not explained by staffing or visits**: Both groups had similar staffing changes. Visit volume increased slightly more for treated branches, but this does not fully account for the service score difference.

3. **Baseline comparison was valid**: Treated branches started slightly lower than control branches before the switch (-0.73 points), which strengthens confidence that the post-switch improvement reflects the procedure change rather than pre-existing trends.

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **Did the new procedure change service scores?** | **Yes** — the effect is statistically significant and positive |
| **Is the effect large enough to matter?** | **Modestly** — 4% improvement is meaningful but not transformational |
| **Should we roll out to all branches?** | **Yes** — the evidence supports adoption, with monitored implementation |
| **Confidence in this recommendation?** | **Moderate-to-high** — rigorous design, but effect size is modest and should be verified at scale |

**Bottom-line verdict:** The data provides sufficient evidence to recommend system-wide adoption. The procedure change does improve service scores, contrary to our initial expectation. However, the board should expect incremental rather than dramatic gains.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve rollout** to remaining 60 branches
2. **Track service scores** for 12 weeks post-rollout to confirm effect persists at scale
3. **Document operational learnings** from the 90 early-adopter branches
4. **Revisit in 6 months** to assess whether the effect is sustained long-term
