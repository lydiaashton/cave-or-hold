## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure improved service scores, but the effect is modest and may diminish over time. We recommend proceeding with rollout, with realistic expectations and ongoing monitoring.**

Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by **2.1 points** on average (approximately 4% improvement). The effect is statistically significant and credible. However, the impact appears to decline after the first month, and the long-term sustainability is unknown.

## **WHAT WE KNOW**

✓ **The effect is real** — p=0.001, confidence interval excludes zero  
✓ **Causal interpretation is credible** — treatment and control branches followed parallel trends before the switch  
✓ **Effect is robust** — unchanged after controlling for staffing and visit levels  
✓ **All post-switch weeks show positive effects** — no week showed a negative point estimate  

## **WHAT WE DON'T KNOW**

⚠ **Why the effect attenuates** — drops ~50% from weeks 15-18 to weeks 19-22  
⚠ **Long-term sustainability** — we only have 8 weeks of post-switch data  
⚠ **Mechanism** — effect is not explained by staffing or visit changes  
⚠ **Generalizability** — will the remaining 60 branches see the same gains?  

---

## **RECOMMENDATION**

**Proceed with rollout, with conditions:**

| Action | Rationale |
|--------|-----------|
| **1. Approve system-wide rollout** | Evidence supports a real, positive effect |
| **2. Set realistic expectations** | Communicate ~2 point gain, not 3+; effect may diminish |
| **3. Monitor during implementation** | Track scores weekly to detect attenuation early |
| **4. Schedule 6-month follow-up** | Evaluate whether effects persist long-term |
| **5. Investigate mechanism** | Interview staff to understand why the procedure works |
