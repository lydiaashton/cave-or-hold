# **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Subject:** Recommendation on System-Wide Rollout of New Branch Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence is mixed. While switched branches have higher service scores after the change, we cannot confidently attribute this improvement to the new procedure.** Branches that switched were already improving relative to other branches *before* the procedure changed. We recommend a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than immediate system-wide adoption.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Switched Branches Have Higher Scores, But...

| Period | Switched Branches | Control Branches | Gap |
|--------|-------------------|------------------|-----|
| Pre-switch (weeks 6-14) | 51.57 | 50.64 | 0.94 points |
| Post-switch (weeks 15-22) | 53.95 | 50.84 | 3.11 points |

The gap widened by **2.17 points** after the switch. However, this simple comparison is misleading.

### 2. Critical Issue: Groups Were Already Diverging Before the Switch

The gap between switched and control branches was **already growing before the procedure changed**:

| Week | Gap (Switched − Control) |
|------|--------------------------|
| 6    | 0.19 points |
| 8    | -0.32 points |
| 10   | 1.14 points |
| 12   | 1.58 points |
| 14   | 2.13 points |
| **15** (switch) | **2.56 points** |
| 22   | 3.78 points |

**The gap grew by 1.94 points during the pre-switch period alone.** This violates the key assumption needed to attribute the change to the procedure.

### 3. What Is the Actual Effect of the Procedure?

Different estimation methods give very different answers:

| Method | Estimated Effect |
|--------|------------------|
| Standard comparison | +2.17 points |
| Conservative (using week 14 as baseline) | +0.97 points |
| Immediate jump at switch | +0.43 points |
| Adjusted for pre-trend | **Uncertain (possibly zero)** |

### 4. What We Can Say With Confidence

- Switched branches are performing better now (average score ~54 vs ~51)
- The procedure **may** have contributed to this improvement
- But we cannot rule out that switched branches were already on a better trajectory for other reasons

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did service scores improve in switched branches? | **Yes** — clearly |
| Did the new procedure cause the improvement? | **Cannot determine** — evidence is inconclusive |
| Should we roll out to all branches immediately? | **No** — too much uncertainty |
| Should we abandon the new procedure? | **No** — potential benefit exists |

**Our verdict: Proceed with caution.** The new procedure shows promise, but the current evidence does not support a confident system-wide rollout. The most likely scenarios are:

1. The procedure has a **modest positive effect** (0.5–1.0 points), smaller than the initial analysis suggested
2. The procedure has **little to no effect**, and switched branches were already improving for other reasons (e.g., they may have been higher-performing branches selected for the pilot)

Without additional evidence, we cannot distinguish between these scenarios.

---

## **Recommendation**

**Do NOT proceed with immediate system-wide rollout.** Instead:

1. **Continue the current pilot** with both procedures running in parallel
2. **Extend monitoring** for at least 3–6 more months to see if the effect stabilizes
3. **Investigate why** switched branches were already diverging before the change (were they higher-performing branches selected for the pilot?)
4. **Consider a randomized rollout** to remaining branches to get cleaner evidence
5. **If proceeding with rollout**, do so in phases with careful tracking at each stage

---

## **Why This Matters**

Rolling out a procedure system-wide requires confidence that it causes improvement. Our analysis shows an association between the new procedure and higher scores, but we cannot establish causation due to pre-existing differences in trends. A premature rollout risks:

- Implementing a procedure that may not deliver the expected benefits
- Missing the opportunity to identify what actually drives the improvement
- Potential disruption without clear return on investment
