# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **2.2 points (4.1%)** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and the evidence strongly supports rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **60 branches** kept the old procedure
- Service scores average around 50 points (higher is better)

### Results

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 53.6 | 54.4 | +0.8 |
| Non-switched branches | 53.3 | 51.9 | −1.3 |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | | **+2.2** |

### Statistical Confidence
- **95% confidence interval:** 1.0 to 3.4 points
- **P-value:** 0.0004 (highly significant)
- The effect is **unlikely to be due to chance**

### Validity Checks
- **Parallel trends confirmed:** Before the switch, treatment and control branches followed similar trajectories
- **Effect is consistent:** Positive effects observed across all 8 post-switch weeks
- **Robust to controls:** Result holds after accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume

### Important Context
The board expected the procedure change to make no difference to service scores. Instead, we found a clear positive effect. Notably, non-switching branches experienced a slight decline in scores after week 15, while switching branches improved. This suggests the new procedure may help branches maintain service quality despite external pressures.

---

## **Final Verdict**

**✅ Proceed with system-wide rollout.**

The analysis provides strong, credible evidence that the new procedure improves service scores. The research design is sound, the statistical results are robust, and the key assumptions have been verified. There is no statistical reason to delay or limit the rollout.

2. **Monitor implementation** during the transition to ensure consistent adoption
