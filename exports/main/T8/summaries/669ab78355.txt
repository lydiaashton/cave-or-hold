# **Memorandum to the Board**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure  
**Date:** November 2025  
**Prepared by:** Data Analysis Team

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence is inconclusive.** While branches that adopted the new procedure showed higher service scores after the switch (+2.2 points relative to control branches), **this improvement cannot be confidently attributed to the procedure itself.** Switched branches were already improving relative to control branches *before* the treatment began, suggesting the estimated effect may be substantially overstated.

**Recommendation: Do not proceed with system-wide rollout yet.** Continue monitoring and gather additional evidence before making a decision.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
- **709 branches** analyzed over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **425 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **284 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Analysis uses difference-in-differences methodology

### Results Summary

| Group | Before | After | Change |
|-------|--------|-------|--------|
| Switched branches | 51.59 | 52.83 | +1.24 |
| Control branches | 51.31 | 50.37 | -0.95 |
| **Observed Difference** | | | **+2.18** |

### Critical Concern: Pre-Treatment Trends

| Period | Avg. Gap (Switched − Control) |
|--------|------------------------------|
| Weeks 6–11 | -0.14 points |
| Weeks 12–14 | +1.11 points |
| Weeks 15–22 | +2.46 points |

**The gap was already widening before the treatment started.** The pre-treatment trend coefficient is 0.22 points/week (p=0.005), indicating switched branches were on an upward trajectory independent of the new procedure.

### Statistical Summary
- **Raw DiD estimate:** +2.17 points (p < 0.001)
- **Parallel trends assumption:** **Violated** (pre-trend p = 0.005)
- **Interpretation:** The true effect is likely smaller than estimated; may even be zero

### Possible Explanations for Pre-Trend
1. **Selection bias:** Branches chosen to switch may have been better-managed or had more motivated staff
2. **Anticipation effects:** Switched branches may have begun preparing for the change before week 15
3. **Other concurrent changes:** Switched branches may have experienced other improvements simultaneously

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Pause system-wide rollout** until causal evidence is clearer.

2. **Extend the observation period** by 8–12 weeks to see if the gap continues widening or stabilizes.

3. **Investigate selection criteria** used to determine which branches switched—identify any systematic differences between switched and control branches.

4. **Consider a randomized trial** for the remaining 284 branches to isolate the true causal effect.

5. **Collect qualitative data** from switched branches to understand implementation quality and any concurrent changes.

6. **Re-evaluate in Q2 2026** with extended data and additional analysis.

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Criterion | Assessment |
|-----------|------------|
| Statistical significance | ✓ Passed (p < 0.001) |
| Parallel trends assumption | ✗ **Failed** (p = 0.005) |
| Effect size | Uncertain (likely overstated) |
| External validity | Unknown |
| **Overall confidence** | **Low** |

**Decision:** The analysis does not provide sufficient evidence to justify a system-wide rollout. The observed improvement may reflect pre-existing differences between branches rather than the procedure's effect. Additional evidence is needed before committing resources to expand the program.

---

**Conclusion:** Proceed with caution. The new procedure *may* improve service scores, but the current data cannot confirm this. Invest in better evidence before scaling.
