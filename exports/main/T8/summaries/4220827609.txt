# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new rotation and shelving procedure is associated with a **2.3-point improvement in service scores** (approximately 5% relative to baseline). The evidence is **suggestive but not definitive**—statistically significant at the 10% level but not at the conventional 5% threshold. We recommend a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Overview**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15
- **14 branches** maintained the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses difference-in-differences methodology to isolate the procedure's effect

### **Service Score Results**

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|---------------|---------------|--------|
| Branches that switched | 44.9 | 48.7 | +3.8 |
| Branches that didn't switch | 48.9 | 50.3 | +1.4 |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | — | — | **+2.35** |

### **Statistical Confidence**
- **Effect size:** +2.35 points on ~50-point scale (**5.2% improvement**)
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.08–0.09 (90% confidence, not 95%)
- **Confidence interval:** The true effect likely falls between -0.3 and +5.0 points
- **Pre-existing trends:** No evidence that treatment branches were on different trajectories before the switch (p = 0.61)

### **Important Context**
1. **Selection pattern:** Branches that switched had lower baseline scores, suggesting they may have been chosen because they were underperforming.
2. **System-wide improvement:** Both groups improved over time, indicating favorable external factors (e.g., seasonal patterns, cooperative-wide initiatives).
3. **Practical significance:** A 2.35-point gain is operationally meaningful for patron experience, even if statistical certainty is moderate.

---

## **Recommendations**

### **Proceed with Cautious Expansion**

| Action | Rationale |
|--------|-----------|
| **1. Phase in the new procedure** over the next 3–6 months | Allows continued data collection while extending benefits to more branches |
| **2. Prioritize lower-performing branches** for early rollout | These branches showed the largest gains; equity consideration |
| **3. Establish 90-day review checkpoint** | Reassess effect size with additional data before full commitment |
| **4. Monitor implementation fidelity** | Ensure branches are correctly applying the new procedure |
| **5. Track secondary metrics** (staffing, visits) | Understand mechanisms and detect any unintended consequences |

### **Defer Full Rollout Decision Until:**
- At least 12 additional weeks of post-implementation data are collected
- Statistical confidence reaches conventional thresholds (p < 0.05)
- Cost-benefit analysis of implementation is completed

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Criterion | Assessment |
|-----------|------------|
| **Effect Direction** | ✅ Positive (consistent across all analyses) |
| **Statistical Certainty** | ⚠️ Moderate (p ≈ 0.08, below 5% threshold) |
| **Practical Significance** | ✅ Meaningful (~5% improvement) |
| **Risk of Harm** | ✅ None detected |
| **Implementation Cost** | ? Not assessed in this analysis |

**VERDICT: PROCEED WITH PHASED ROLLOUT**

**Rationale:** The evidence points in a favorable direction with no detected risks. While we lack definitive statistical proof, waiting for perfect certainty would delay potential benefits to patrons. A phased approach balances opportunity with prudence—extending the procedure to more branches while building the evidence base needed for a final system-wide decision.

**Success Metric:** If the effect remains positive and grows to statistical significance (p < 0.05) with additional data, recommend full rollout. If the effect diminishes or reverses, pause and reassess.

---

## **Conclusion**

The new procedure demonstrates **promising positive effects** on service quality without evidence of harm. While statistical certainty is not yet at the highest standard, the direction and magnitude of effects support a measured expansion. Continued evaluation will provide the confidence needed for a final system-wide decision.
