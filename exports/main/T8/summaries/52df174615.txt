# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

**Date:** November 2026

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure had a modest protective effect, but did not improve service scores in absolute terms.** Both groups experienced declines after the switch date, but branches using the new procedure declined less (-0.70 points) than those using the old procedure (-2.57 points). The net relative benefit is **+1.87 points** (p = 0.001).

**Recommendation: Do not proceed with system-wide rollout at this time.** The evidence does not support the expectation that this procedure would improve service quality. Instead, it modestly buffered against a decline that affected all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
- **150 branches**: 90 adopted the new procedure, 60 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22), with the switch at week 15
- Analysis compares changes between groups using difference-in-differences

### Results
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 50.56 | 47.98 | **-2.57** |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 49.63 | 48.93 | **-0.70** |

**Critical observation:** Service scores declined for ALL branches after week 15. The new procedure mitigated this decline but did not reverse it.

### Statistical Assessment
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Relative effect (DiD) | +1.87 points |
| 95% Confidence Interval | [0.73, 3.01] |
| p-value | 0.0013 |
| Effect size | 0.19 SD (small) |
| Pre-trend balance | Confirmed (p = 0.81) |

### Practical Significance
- The effect represents **3.8% of the average service score**
- This is a **small effect** by conventional standards (< 0.2 SD)
- The board expected "no difference"—we found a statistically detectable difference, but one that did not produce absolute improvement

---

## **Final Verdict**

**The new procedure should not be rolled out system-wide.**

**Rationale:**
1. **No absolute improvement:** Service scores declined across all branches. The procedure did not deliver the improvement the board anticipated.
2. **Modest effect size:** At 0.19 standard deviations, the protective effect is small and may not justify implementation costs.
3. **Unexplained decline:** Something caused service scores to drop after week 15 for all branches. This should be investigated before making procedural changes.
4. **Opportunity cost:** Resources spent on system-wide rollout could be better directed toward addressing the underlying cause of the decline.

**Alternative path forward:**
- Investigate what caused the overall decline after week 15
- Consider retaining the new procedure only in branches where it is already implemented (no need to reverse)
- Explore other interventions that could produce absolute improvements in service quality

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Investigate the overall decline** affecting all branches after week 15 (seasonal factors? staffing changes? external events?)

2. **Conduct cost-benefit analysis** of the new procedure to determine if the modest protective effect justifies ongoing use

3. **Explore alternative interventions** that could produce absolute improvements in service scores

4. **Continue monitoring** both groups to determine if the protective effect persists or diminishes over time

---

## **Technical Notes**

Analysis uses difference-in-differences with cluster-robust standard errors at the branch level. All 150 branches have complete data. Pre-trends between groups were parallel (p = 0.81), supporting causal interpretation of the relative effect.
