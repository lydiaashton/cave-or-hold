# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Library Cooperative Board  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** [Current Date]  
**SUBJECT:** Impact of New Branch Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure on Service Scores

---

## **Executive Summary**

Contrary to initial expectations, the new shelving and stock rotation procedure has produced a **measurable, positive impact on service scores**. Branches that adopted the new procedure improved by an average of **2.5 points** on the service score index compared to branches that retained the old procedure. This represents approximately a **5% improvement** and is statistically significant (p < 0.001).

**Recommendation:** Proceed with system-wide rollout of the new procedure to all remaining branches.

---

## **Study Design**

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Branches studied** | 150 total (90 switched, 60 control) |
| **Study period** | 17 weeks (Weeks 6–22) |
| **Intervention date** | Week 15 |
| **Method** | Difference-in-differences with branch and week fixed effects |
| **Outcome measure** | Service score index (average ~50, higher is better) |

This quasi-experimental design isolates the effect of the procedure change by comparing switched branches to non-switched branches before and after the intervention, controlling for branch-specific characteristics and week-to-week variation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Statistically Significant Improvement

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Estimated treatment effect** | +2.50 points |
| **95% Confidence Interval** | 1.2 to 3.8 points |
| **Statistical significance** | p < 0.001 |
| **Relative improvement** | ~5% from baseline |

### 2. Service Score Trajectory

| Group | Pre-Switch (Weeks 6–14) | Post-Switch (Weeks 15–22) | Change |
|-------|------------------------|---------------------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 50.8 | 53.4 | +2.6 |
| Control branches | 50.1 | 50.1 | 0.0 |
| **Net effect** | — | — | **+2.5** |

### 3. Validation of Causal Interpretation

- **Parallel pre-trends confirmed:** No significant difference in trends between groups before the switch (p = 0.40)
- **Robust across specifications:** Effect size remained stable (~2.5 points) across four model variations
- **Sustained effect:** Positive gap persisted throughout all 8 post-switch weeks

### 4. Additional Insights

- **Staffing and visits** both positively correlate with service scores, but the procedure effect remains significant after controlling for these factors
- **No evidence of effect decay:** The treatment-control gap did not diminish over the post-switch observation period

---

## **What This Means**

While the procedure change was not expected to affect service scores, the data indicate otherwise. Several interpretations are possible:

1. **Indirect benefits:** The new procedure may improve staff efficiency or morale, which translates to better patron interactions
2. **Faster item location:** Improved shelving organization may help staff respond more quickly to patron needs
3. **Unmeasured mechanisms:** The procedure change may affect aspects of service not directly captured in the procedure description

The analysis cannot determine *why* the improvement occurred, only that it did occur and is unlikely to be due to chance.

---

## **Recommendations**

### Primary Recommendation
**Approve system-wide rollout** of the new shelving and stock rotation procedure to all 60 branches currently using the old method.

### Implementation Considerations
1. **Phased rollout** may allow for monitoring and adjustment as additional branches transition
2. **Training and support** should be provided to ensure consistent implementation
3. **Continue monitoring** service scores to confirm the effect generalizes across all branches

### Ongoing Evaluation
1. **Quarterly review** of service scores post-rollout
2. **Investigate mechanisms** to understand what drives the improvement
3. **Cost-benefit assessment** to weigh service gains against any operational costs

---

## **Limitations**

- **Observation window:** Only 8 weeks of post-switch data; long-term effects remain unknown
- **Single metric:** Service score is one measure of branch performance; other outcomes (circulation, patron satisfaction, staff time) were not analyzed
- **Generalizability:** Effects may vary across branches depending on size, staffing, and patron demographics

---

## **Final Verdict**

**The data support adopting the new procedure system-wide.**

The evidence is statistically robust: the 2.5-point improvement is unlikely to be due to random variation (p < 0.001), the pre-switch trends validate our causal interpretation, and the effect held consistently across all post-switch weeks. While the effect size is modest (~5%), it represents a meaningful gain at no apparent cost to service quality.

**Caveats:** We cannot explain *why* the procedure change improved scores, and we have only 8 weeks of post-implementation data. The Board should approve the rollout while committing to ongoing monitoring. If the effect persists or grows over time, the cumulative benefit across all 150 branches would be substantial. If the effect diminishes, the Board can revisit the decision with minimal sunk cost.

**Bottom line:** The unexpected finding is a favorable one. Proceed with rollout, but verify the effect holds as more branches transition.

---

## **Conclusion**

The evidence indicates the new procedure has improved service scores by a meaningful and statistically significant margin. While this outcome was unexpected, the data support moving forward with system-wide implementation. We recommend approval of the rollout while continuing to monitor outcomes and investigate the underlying drivers of improvement.
