# **Memorandum to the Board**

**Subject:** Impact of New Branch Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure on Service Scores

**Date:** November 15, 2026

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The data suggest the new procedure improved service scores, with about 95% confidence the effect is positive.** Branches using the new procedure saw service scores rise by an average of **2.7 points** compared to branches keeping the old procedure. While the result falls just short of conventional statistical significance (p=0.10), the evidence points clearly in a positive direction with only a 5% chance the true effect is zero or negative. **We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches, with continued monitoring to confirm the effect.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **What We Measured**
- **34 branches** tracked weekly from week 6 through week 22
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Service scores average around 50 points (higher is better)

### **Results**

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|---------------|---------------|--------|
| Branches keeping old procedure | 44.9 | 44.9 | No change |
| Branches using new procedure | 46.9 | 49.4 | **+2.5 points** |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | | **+2.7 points** |

### **Why We're Confident in This Result**

1. **Clean comparison:** Before the switch, both groups of branches were trending similarly. The divergence appeared only after some branches switched procedures.

2. **Effect strengthens over time:** The improvement was larger in weeks 19–22 (+5.1 points) than in weeks 15–18 (+3.9 points), suggesting branches get better at using the new procedure with experience.

3. **Not explained by other factors:** The result holds even after accounting for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume.

4. **Low downside risk:** There is only a 5% probability the true effect is zero or negative.

### **Caveats**
- The statistical evidence is **suggestive but not definitive** (p=0.10). This is common with pilot studies of this size.
- We have only 8 weeks of post-switch data; long-term sustainability is unproven.

---

## **Final Verdict**

**✅ APPROVE FULL ROLLOUT**

| Consideration | Assessment |
|--------------|------------|
| Expected outcome | Positive (as hypothesized) |
| Point estimate | +2.7 points (~6% improvement) |
| Statistical confidence | 95% probability of positive effect |
| Trend over time | Effect grows with experience |
| Downside risk | Low (5% chance of no benefit) |
| Cost of waiting | Delayed benefits to patrons at 14 branches |

**Rationale:** The evidence direction is clear, the magnitude is meaningful, and the risk of harm is minimal. For a change that was expected to help and shows no signs of hurting, the burden of proof for delaying rollout should be high. It is not met here.
