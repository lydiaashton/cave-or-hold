# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure is associated with a small but statistically significant improvement in service scores (+1.7 points, or 3.5% above average). The effect is real and consistent, though modest in magnitude. Rollout is recommended if implementation costs are reasonable.**

---

## **Key Findings**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect | +1.74 points |
| 95% Confidence interval | +0.64 to +2.84 points |
| P-value | 0.002 |
| Effect as % of average score | 3.5% |
| Effect as % of score SD | 18% |

### **What the Data Shows**
- Switched branches improved relative to control branches after week 15
- The effect was **consistent** across all 8 post-switch weeks (ranging from +0.6 to +1.9 points)
- Pre-switch scores were nearly identical between groups, supporting causal interpretation

### **What the Data Does Not Show**
- No evidence of large or transformative improvement
- No information on implementation costs (training, labor, disruption)
- No data on long-term effects beyond 8 weeks
- No insight into *why* the change helped

---

## **Considerations for the Board**

| Factor | Evidence |
|--------|----------|
| **Statistical reliability** | Strong (p = 0.002) |
| **Practical significance** | Modest (3.5% improvement) |
| **Consistency** | Effect persisted across all post-switch weeks |
| **Costs** | Unknown – requires separate assessment |
| **Prior expectation** | Data contradicts expectation of no effect |

---

## **Recommendations**

1. **Request cost estimates** for training and implementing the new procedure at remaining 60 branches
2. **Conduct a cost-benefit analysis** – does a 3.5% service improvement justify the expense?
3. **If costs are low**, proceed with rollout
4. **If costs are substantial**, consider limiting to branches where marginal gains matter most
5. **Investigate mechanisms** – understanding *why* this helped could inform future procedural changes

---

## **Final Verdict**

**Proceed with rollout, with modest expectations.**

The evidence is clear: the new procedure improves service scores. While the effect is smaller than we might have hoped for, it is:

- **Real** – statistically significant at the 1% level
- **Reliable** – consistent across all 8 post-switch weeks
- **Low-risk** – procedural changes are reversible if needed

Given that library service improvements are cumulative and affect thousands of patron interactions weekly, a 3.5% gain across 150 branches represents meaningful value. The burden now shifts to ensuring implementation is efficient and costs are controlled.

**Vote recommendation: Approve system-wide rollout, contingent on reasonable implementation costs.**
