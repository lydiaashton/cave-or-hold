# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD OF DIRECTORS**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure  
**Date:** November 15, 2026

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The evidence suggests the new procedure improved service scores, but we cannot be certain.** Branches that adopted the new procedure showed service score increases of approximately **2.25 points** relative to branches that kept the old procedure. This finding is statistically significant and consistent across all post-implementation weeks.

However, because branches were not randomly assigned to switch (this was an operational decision, not a controlled experiment), we cannot rule out that other factors contributed to the improvement. **We recommend proceeding with caution** — consider a phased rollout with continued monitoring rather than immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **1. Observed Improvement**
| Group | Before | After | Change |
|-------|--------|-------|--------|
| Switched Branches (90) | 50.45 | 53.11 | +2.66 |
| Non-Switched Branches (60) | 49.65 | 50.12 | +0.47 |
| **Estimated Effect** | | | **+2.25** |

- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.0005
- **95% Confidence Interval:** 1.0 to 3.5 points
- **Effect visible in all 8 post-switch weeks**

### **2. What Supports a Causal Interpretation**
- Switched and non-switched branches had **similar pre-switch trends** (parallel trends test: p = 0.78)
- Baseline characteristics (staffing, visits) were **not significantly different** between groups
- Effect is **consistent week-to-week** after implementation
- Result holds after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### **3. Important Limitations**
- **Not a randomized trial**: Branches may have been selected to switch based on unobserved factors (management quality, staff engagement, facility conditions)
- **Short observation window**: Only 7 weeks of post-implementation data
- **Unknown mechanisms**: We don't know *why* scores improved, which makes replication uncertain

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

**PROCEED WITH PHASED ROLLOUT**

We recommend implementing the new procedure at remaining branches, but in **two phases** with monitoring checkpoints:

| Phase | Branches | Timeline | Go/No-Go Criteria |
|-------|----------|----------|-------------------|
| 1 | 30 branches | Weeks 1-8 | Proceed if average effect ≥1.5 points |
| 2 | 30 branches | Weeks 9-16 | Proceed if Phase 1 results hold |
| Complete | All 150 branches | Week 17+ | Full system adoption |

**Rationale:**
- The statistical evidence is strong enough to justify moving forward
- The parallel trends test and consistent week-to-week effects reduce concern about selection bias
- A phased approach protects against wasting resources if the effect doesn't replicate
- This balances the opportunity for improvement against the uncertainty inherent in observational data

**What would change our recommendation:**
- If Phase 1 shows effects below 1.0 point: Pause and investigate
- If Phase 1 shows negative effects: Halt rollout and reassess the procedure
- If Phase 1 confirms effects of 2.0+ points: Accelerate to full rollout
