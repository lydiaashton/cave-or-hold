# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD OF DIRECTORS**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 2025  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Proceed with a phased rollout.** The data show the new procedure is associated with **significantly higher service scores** (+2.3 points, p < 0.001). However, branches that adopted the procedure were underperforming just before the switch, introducing some uncertainty about whether the full effect is causal. The evidence supports expansion, but a **phased approach with continued monitoring** is the prudent path forward.

---

## **Key Findings**

### The Positive Signal
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect | +2.3 points |
| 95% Confidence Interval | [1.0, 3.6] |
| Statistical significance | p = 0.0005 |
| Practical interpretation | Treatment branches maintained scores; control branches declined |

### The Concern
| Period | Treatment − Control |
|--------|---------------------|
| Weeks 6-10 | +0.07 (essentially equal) |
| Weeks 11-14 | **−1.39** (treatment worse) |
| Weeks 15-22 | **+1.73** (treatment better) |

The treatment group was underperforming just before adoption. This pattern could reflect mean reversion, selection effects, or a genuine treatment effect—we cannot definitively distinguish among these with current data.

### What We Know With Confidence
- **No evidence of harm:** The new procedure did not negatively impact service scores
- **Meaningful association:** Branches using the new procedure scored higher after adoption
- **Consistent results:** Effect holds across multiple statistical specifications
- **Practical significance:** ~2.3 points represents 4-5% improvement on a ~50-point scale

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **Did the new procedure change service scores?** | **Yes** — there is a statistically significant association |
| **Is the effect causal?** | **Probably, but not certain** — pre-trend concerns introduce some doubt |
| **Should we roll out to all branches?** | **Yes, but phased** — expand while monitoring to confirm the effect |
| **Is there risk of harm?** | **Low** — no negative effects observed in treatment branches |

**Bottom-line judgment:** The weight of evidence favors the new procedure. The statistical signal is strong, there is no evidence of harm, and the practical benefit is meaningful. The pre-trend concern warrants caution but should not block adoption. A phased rollout allows the cooperative to capture potential benefits while building additional evidence.

---

## **Recommendations**

| Priority | Action | Timeline |
|----------|--------|----------|
| **1** | Approve phased rollout to 20-30 additional branches | Next board meeting |
| **2** | Track service scores weekly for all switching branches | Ongoing |
| **3** | Report interim results to board | 8 weeks |
| **4** | Re-evaluate for full system-wide adoption | 3 months |

---

## **Limitations**

- Analysis covers only 8 weeks post-switch; long-term effects unknown
- Pre-trend violation introduces uncertainty about causal interpretation
- Service score is one metric; operational costs and staff workload should also be considered
