# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout.**

Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure achieved a **2.1-point improvement** in service scores compared to branches that retained the old procedure. This confirms the expected benefit and supports expanding the new procedure to all 709 branches.

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did the new procedure improve service scores? | **Yes** |
| Is the effect statistically reliable? | **Yes** (p < 0.001) |
| Is the effect practically meaningful? | **Yes** (~4% improvement) |
| Has the effect persisted over time? | **Yes** (8 weeks, no fade-out) |
| **Should we roll out to all branches?** | **Yes** |

**Confidence Level: High**

The analysis meets the key standard for causal inference—parallel trends between treatment and control groups in the weeks immediately before the switch. The effect is robust across multiple statistical specifications and shows no signs of diminishing over time.

## **Key Findings**

### Impact on Service Scores

| Group | Branches | Before | After | Change |
|-------|----------|--------|-------|--------|
| New procedure | 425 | 50.7 | 54.0 | +3.3 |
| Old procedure | 284 | 50.7 | 51.9 | +1.2 |
| **Net Effect** | | | | **+2.1** |

### Statistical Confidence
- **Effect size:** 2.1 points (95% CI: 1.6 to 2.7)
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001
- **Relative improvement:** 4% above baseline

### Validation of Results
- **Pre-trends aligned:** Both groups had nearly identical service scores in the weeks before the switch (weeks 12-14), supporting a valid comparison
- **Effect sustained:** Improvement appeared immediately in week 15 and persisted through all eight weeks of observation
- **Robust to controls:** Result holds after accounting for staffing levels and visitor volumes
