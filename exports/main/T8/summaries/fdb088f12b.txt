# **Library Cooperative Branch Procedure Change: Impact Analysis**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure shows a statistically detectable but modest improvement in service scores.** The estimated effect is **+1.7 points** (p=0.002), but this represents only **0.18 standard deviations**—below the conventional threshold for a "small" effect. The effect is smaller than typical week-to-week score variation (9.3 points).

**Important caveats:**
- Branches selected for the new procedure had **lower baseline scores** (51.2 vs 54.2), raising regression-to-the-mean concerns
- The model explains only **1.4%** of score variation—the procedure is not a major driver of service quality
- Control branches' scores declined after week 15; the "effect" partly reflects this drop rather than treatment improvement alone

---

## **Final Verdict**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Statistical significance** | ✓ Yes (p=0.002) |
| **Practical significance** | ✗ No (effect too small) |
| **Recommendation** | **Do not mandate rollout** |

**Rationale:** While the effect is real, it is too small to justify the cost and disruption of forcing all 60 remaining branches to change procedures. The new procedure may benefit some branches, but it is not a solution to systemic service quality challenges.

## **Key Findings**

### **Statistical Evidence**

| Metric | Value | Interpretation |
|--------|-------|----------------|
| **Estimated effect** | +1.73 points | Statistically significant |
| **95% Confidence interval** | [0.64, 2.81] | Does not include zero |
| **P-value** | 0.002 | Significant at 1% level |
| **Effect size (Cohen's d)** | 0.18 SD | Below "small" threshold (0.20) |
| **vs. weekly volatility** | 0.19x | Effect is 1/5 of typical week-to-week variation |

### **Important Context**

⚠️ **Baseline imbalance**: Treatment branches started with significantly lower scores (51.20 vs 54.16). This suggests lower-performing branches were selected to test the new procedure, which may have biased results toward showing improvement.

⚠️ **Limited explanatory power**: The treatment explains only 1.4% of variation in service scores. Other factors (staffing, visits, local conditions) matter far more.

⚠️ **Control group decline**: Non-switching branches' scores dropped 1.28 points after week 15. The measured "effect" partly reflects this decline rather than treatment branches improving substantially.

✓ **Parallel trends satisfied**: Pre-switch trajectories were similar between groups (p=0.69), supporting the validity of the comparison.

✓ **Effect persisted**: Positive impact observed in both early (weeks 15–18) and late (weeks 19–22) post-periods.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Do not mandate immediate system-wide rollout.** The evidence suggests modest benefit that may not justify rollout costs across all 60 remaining branches.

2. **Offer voluntary adoption** to interested branches, with clear communication that expected improvement is modest (~1–2 points).

3. **Investigate the control group decline.** Understand why non-switching branches' scores dropped after week 15—this may reveal external factors affecting service quality.

4. **Identify high-impact drivers.** Since the procedure explains only 1.4% of score variation, prioritize analysis of factors with larger effects (staffing, visits, local conditions).

5. **Reassess in 6 months.** If voluntary adoption proceeds, collect additional data to confirm whether effects persist and whether certain branch types benefit more than others.
