# **Analysis of New Shelving Procedure Impact on Library Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores by approximately 2.3 points**—a statistically significant finding (p < 0.001) that contradicts prior expectations. While the shelving and rotation change was designed for operational efficiency, not service quality, the data shows it delivered a meaningful ~5% improvement in service scores at no additional cost.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with systemwide rollout. This is a rare case where an operational change produces measurable service benefits without extra investment.

---

## **Key Findings**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Treatment effect | +2.28 points |
| 95% Confidence interval | [1.04, 3.52] |
| Statistical significance | p = 0.0003 |
| Branches studied | 150 (90 treated, 60 control) |
| Observation period | 17 weeks (weeks 6-22) |

**Why this finding is credible:**
- Treated and control branches had identical baseline scores (week 6: 44.20 vs 44.22)
- Pre-treatment trends were parallel (statistical test: p = 0.40)
- Effect appeared precisely at the switch point and persisted across all 8 post-switch weeks
- Result holds after controlling for staffing and visitor volume

---

## **Final Verdict**

**ROLL OUT THE NEW PROCEDURE SYSTEMWIDE.**

The evidence is clear and actionable:
- ✅ **Statistically significant** improvement (p < 0.001)
- ✅ **Practically meaningful** gain (~5% above average)
- ✅ **No countervailing costs** identified
- ✅ **Robust to alternative specifications** and controls

The prior expectation—that this change would not affect service scores—has been falsified by the data. This is precisely why evidence-based decision-making matters: we discovered value that would have been overlooked.

**Board Action Required:** Authorize implementation at all remaining 60 branches.
