# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure shows a **modest positive effect** on service scores (+2.9 points, approximately 6% improvement). While the result falls just short of conventional statistical significance (p = 0.10), the evidence points in the expected direction with no indication of harm. 

**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **phased rollout** to all branches, accompanied by continued monitoring. The potential benefit to patrons outweighs the risk that the effect may be smaller than observed.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Overview**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22 of the year)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **14 branches** maintained the old procedure as a comparison group
- Analysis compares service score changes between the two groups using a difference-in-differences approach

### **Service Score Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Branches with new procedure | 48.6 | 50.4 | **+1.8** |
| Branches with old procedure | 53.6 | 52.5 | **–1.1** |
| **Estimated effect of new procedure** | | | **+2.9** |

### **Statistical Assessment**
- **Point estimate:** +2.9 points on the service score index
- **95% confidence interval:** –0.5 to +6.3 points
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.10 (not significant at the standard 5% threshold)
- **Practical significance:** The effect represents approximately 6% of the average service score—a modest but potentially meaningful improvement

### **Additional Observations**
- Branches that switched to the new procedure started with lower baseline service scores (about 5 points lower on average)
- The analysis accounts for this baseline difference
- Service scores across all branches showed a slight declining trend during the study period; switched branches reversed this trend
- Controlling for staffing levels and branch visit volumes did not materially change the results

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Criterion | Assessment |
|-----------|------------|
| **Direction of effect** | ✅ Positive (as expected) |
| **Statistical confidence** | ⚠️ Suggestive, not definitive (90% confidence) |
| **Practical importance** | ✅ Modest but meaningful (~6% improvement) |
| **Risk of harm** | ✅ No negative effects detected |
| **Cost of reversal** | ✅ Low (procedure can be adjusted if needed) |

**VERDICT: Proceed with rollout, with monitoring.**

The evidence supports adoption. While we lack ironclad statistical proof, the balance of evidence favors the new procedure:
- The effect is in the expected direction
- The confidence interval is overwhelmingly positive (only the lower 2.5% tail dips below zero)
- There is no signal of adverse effects
- Service improvements of this magnitude matter to patrons over time

**Implementation approach:** Roll out to all branches over the next quarter while continuing to track service scores. If scores do not improve within 12 weeks post-implementation, reconvene to assess whether adjustments are needed.
