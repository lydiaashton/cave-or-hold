# **REVISED MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 2025  
**SUBJECT:** Impact of New Branch Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**We cannot confidently conclude the new procedure caused the improvement.** While switched branches did show higher service scores after the change, **branches selected to switch first were already improving faster before the procedure change**. The gap between switched and non-switched branches widened at virtually the same rate before and after the switch (0.30 vs. 0.31 points/week), suggesting the observed difference reflects pre-existing trends rather than a treatment effect.

**Recommendation:** **Do not proceed with systemwide rollout based on this evidence alone.** Either (a) extend the pilot with better evaluation design, or (b) gather additional evidence before committing resources to a full rollout.

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did switched branches have higher scores after Week 15? | **Yes** |
| Did the new procedure *cause* the improvement? | **Cannot determine** |
| Is the evidence sufficient to justify systemwide rollout? | **No** |
| Should the Board approve rollout today? | **Not recommended** |

**Bottom line verdict:** The data shows correlation, not causation. The branches that switched were already on a stronger trajectory before the procedure change. Approving a systemwide rollout based on this evidence would be premature and risks investing in a procedure that may not deliver the expected benefits.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. The Critical Problem: Pre-Existing Trends**

The fundamental assumption of our analysis—that switched and non-switched branches would have followed similar trends absent the intervention—is **violated**.

| Metric | Pre-Switch | Post-Switch |
|--------|------------|-------------|
| Gap widening rate | 0.30 points/week | 0.31 points/week |
| Ratio | — | **1.02** (virtually identical) |

If the pre-switch trend had continued, the expected gap at Week 22 would have been **5.06 points**. The actual gap was **4.91 points**—actually slightly *lower* than expected.

### **2. What the Weekly Data Shows**

**Gap between switched and non-switched branches:**

| Week | Gap | Period |
|------|-----|--------|
| 6 | 0.20 | Pre |
| 10 | 1.99 | Pre |
| 14 | 2.63 | Pre (last week before switch) |
| 15 | 2.74 | Post (first week after switch) |
| 18 | 3.77 | Post |
| 22 | 4.91 | Post |

**There is no visible "jump" or acceleration at Week 15.** The gap was already widening steadily before the switch and continued at the same pace afterward.

### **3. What We Can Say with Confidence**

| Observation | Supported by Data? |
|-------------|-------------------|
| Switched branches had higher scores after Week 15 | **Yes** (52.1 vs. 48.3) |
| Switched branches improved from their own pre-switch levels | **Yes** (+1.4 points) |
| The new procedure *caused* this improvement | **No** — pre-existing trends explain the pattern |
| Non-switched branches declined slightly | **Yes** (−0.6 points) |
| Switched branches were different from non-switched from the start | **Yes** — Week 6 gap was already 0.2, growing to 2.6 by Week 14 |

### **4. Why This Matters**

Branches selected to switch first appear to have been **systematically different** from those that didn't—possibly larger, better-staffed, or in more affluent areas. These branches were already on an upward trajectory. The statistical model attributes their continued improvement to the procedure, but the data does not support this causal claim.

---

## **Analysis Overview**

- **Data:** 12,053 weekly observations from 709 branches
- **Method:** Difference-in-differences regression (standard approach for policy evaluation)
- **Critical limitation:** Parallel trends assumption violated
- **Period:** 17 weeks (9 weeks before and 8 weeks after the switch)

---

## **Next Steps**

| Action | Timeline | Owner |
|--------|----------|-------|
| **1. Pause systemwide rollout decision** | Immediate | Board |
| **2. Investigate branch selection criteria** — Why were certain branches chosen to switch first? | 2-4 weeks | Operations |
| **3. Consider a randomized rollout** — Randomly assign remaining branches to switch in different waves to enable cleaner causal inference | Next quarter | Operations + Analytics |
| **4. Extend monitoring period** — Collect more post-switch data to see if patterns change | Ongoing | Analytics |
| **5. Gather qualitative feedback** — Interview branch staff about whether the new procedure feels like an improvement | 4-6 weeks | Operations |
| **6. Report back to Board** with updated evidence and recommendation | 3 months | Director |

---

## **Conclusion**

The initial analysis appeared to show a positive effect, but closer examination reveals the evidence is **not sufficient to support a causal claim**. The branches that switched were already outperforming and improving faster before the procedure change. Without a cleaner evaluation design (such as random assignment), we cannot determine whether the new procedure itself improves service scores or whether we are simply observing pre-existing differences between branches.

We recommend pausing the rollout decision and investing in better evidence before committing cooperative-wide resources.
