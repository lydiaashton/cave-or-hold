# **Memorandum to the Board**

## **Subject: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Contrary to initial expectations, branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure experienced service scores approximately **2 points higher** than branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and represents a meaningful ~4% improvement on the service score index.

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
- **150 branches** analyzed over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** switched to the new procedure at week 15; **60 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Analysis compares service score changes between the two groups before and after the switch

### Results

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 48.55 | 50.30 | +1.75 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 49.46 | 53.24 | +3.78 |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | | | **+2.03** |

- **Estimated effect: +2.03 points** (95% CI: 0.82 to 3.25)
- **Statistical significance: p = 0.001**
- Effect remains significant after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### Why We Can Trust This Result

1. **Parallel pre-trends**: Before week 15, both groups moved together—no systematic differences in trajectories (pre-trend test: p = 0.19)

2. **Clean identification**: The switch timing was uniform (week 15), and the control group provides a valid counterfactual

3. **Robust to controls**: Adding staffing and visits as covariates does not diminish the effect (adjusted estimate: +2.18 points, p = 0.001)

4. **Consistent post-switch pattern**: The treatment-control gap widened after week 15 and stayed elevated across all 8 post-switch weeks

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **Did the new procedure change service scores?** | **Yes** — the evidence is statistically significant and robust |
| **Is the effect large enough to matter?** | **Modest but meaningful** — ~4% improvement on the service index |
| **Should we be concerned about bias?** | **Some caution warranted** — this is observational data, but pre-trend tests support the comparison |
| **Do we recommend rollout?** | **Yes** — the benefit is real, the procedure is already working in 60% of branches, and continued monitoring can catch any issues |

**Bottom line on the bottom line:** We expected no effect. The data say otherwise. The analysis is sound, the result is unlikely to be due to chance, and the improvement—while modest—is genuine. Proceed with rollout, but track outcomes to confirm the effect persists.
