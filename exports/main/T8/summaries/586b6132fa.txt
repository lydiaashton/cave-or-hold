# **Memorandum to the Board**

## **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

### **Bottom Line**

**The evidence suggests the new procedure improved service scores, but with important caveats.** Branches that adopted the new procedure showed a **2.1-point increase** in service scores relative to control branches. This finding is statistically significant but should be interpreted cautiously because treatment and control branches showed different performance patterns even before the switch. 

**Recommendation: Proceed with rollout, but monitor closely.** The potential upside (meaningful service improvement) outweighs the risk, but we should verify the effect holds as more branches adopt the procedure.

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. Estimated Improvement: +2.1 Points**
- Difference-in-differences estimate: **+2.12 points** (p < 0.001)
- 95% confidence interval: **+0.95 to +3.30 points**
- Represents approximately **4% improvement** over baseline scores (~50 points)

**2. Important Caveat: Pre-Existing Differences**
- Treatment branches scored higher than control branches *before* the switch (+0.82 points on average)
- This pre-existing gap introduces uncertainty into the estimate
- However, the week immediately before the switch (week 14) showed minimal difference (+0.28 points)
- Using week 14 as baseline yields a larger estimate: **+2.66 points**

**3. Post-Switch Pattern Is Consistent**
- All 8 post-switch weeks show positive differences (ranging from +2.15 to +3.91)
- Less week-to-week variability after the switch than before
- Effect appears sustained, not fading over time

**4. Why We Expected No Change**
- This procedure change was designed to improve operational efficiency, not directly affect service quality
- The service score improvement may be an indirect benefit (e.g., staff have more time for patrons)
- This unexpected positive is a favorable surprise, but warrants verification

---

### **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **Did the new procedure change service scores?** | Yes — the data shows a positive effect |
| **How confident are we?** | Moderate — statistically significant, but pre-trends introduce uncertainty |
| **Should we roll out to all branches?** | **Yes** — with monitoring |
| **What's the risk?** | Low — the procedure was expected to be neutral; no downside identified |
| **What's the opportunity?** | ~4% service improvement across the entire system |

**Bottom line verdict:** The statistical evidence, combined with the consistency of post-switch results and the absence of any identified downside, supports proceeding with system-wide rollout. However, the pre-existing differences between branches mean we should treat the +2.1 point estimate as an upper bound and verify the effect replicates as additional branches switch.

---

### **Next Steps**

1. **Approve phased rollout** with built-in evaluation checkpoints

2. **Track service scores for each newly switched branch**:
   - Compare pre- and post-switch scores for each branch individually
   - This will confirm whether the effect replicates beyond the original 90 branches

3. **Set a review point after 50% rollout**:
   - Re-run the analysis with expanded data
   - If effect size drops substantially (<1.0 point), reconsider full rollout

4. **Investigate the mechanism** (medium priority):
   - Survey staff at switched branches about perceived changes
   - This could reveal whether the benefit is real or reflects scoring artifacts

5. **No immediate action needed if rollout reveals no benefit**:
   - The procedure change was expected to be neutral
   - If service scores don't improve, there's no harm in proceeding for operational reasons
