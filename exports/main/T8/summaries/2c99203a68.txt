# **Board Summary: New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

**Date:** November 2026  
**Analysis Period:** Weeks 6–22 (17 weeks)  
**Sample:** 150 branches (90 switched, 60 control)

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores by 2.1 points (4%).** This finding contradicts the prior expectation that the procedural change would have no measurable effect on service quality. Nevertheless, the evidence is robust across multiple analytical methods, with less than a 1% probability that this result occurred by chance. **We recommend proceeding with a full rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Service Score Impact**

| Group | Before | After | Change |
|-------|--------|-------|--------|
| New procedure branches | 50.8 | 53.7 | +2.9 |
| Old procedure branches | 47.6 | 48.4 | +0.9 |
| **Net effect (Difference-in-Differences)** | | | **+2.1** |

- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.001
- **95% Confidence Interval:** 0.83 to 3.33 points
- **Effect size:** ~4% of average service score

### **2. Why This Result Is Credible**

| Validation | Result |
|------------|--------|
| Parallel pre-trends | ✓ Groups moved together before week 15 |
| Multiple methods agree | ✓ DiD, fixed effects, and t-tests all show ~2.1 point effect |
| Cluster-robust errors | ✓ Accounts for repeated measures per branch |
| Balanced data | ✓ All 150 branches have complete 17-week records |

### **3. Interpreting the Unexpected Result**

The operational change was not designed to affect service quality. Possible explanations for the improvement:

- Staff time reallocated from stock management to patron assistance
- Improved shelf organization enhancing patron experience
- Morale or workflow benefits from the new procedure

These mechanisms warrant investigation during rollout.
