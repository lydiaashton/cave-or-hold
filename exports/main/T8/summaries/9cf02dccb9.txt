# **Board Memorandum**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure shows **promising but inconclusive evidence** of improved service scores. While switched branches gained about 2 points on average compared to non-switched branches, the result is **not statistically significant**, and **30% of pilot branches actually saw scores decline**. For a decision affecting all branches, this uncertainty warrants caution. We recommend **extending the pilot with targeted improvements** rather than immediate full rollout, while preparing implementation resources for branches that wish to adopt the procedure voluntarily.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. The New Procedure Is Associated with Higher Scores**
| Group | Before | After | Change |
|-------|--------|-------|--------|
| Switched branches (20) | 48.7 | 50.5 | **+1.8** |
| Non-switched branches (14) | 47.6 | 47.5 | **-0.1** |

**Net improvement attributable to the new procedure: +1.9 points** (approximately 4% gain from baseline)

### **2. Most Branches Benefited, But Not All**
- **14 of 20 switched branches (70%)** saw their scores improve
- **6 of 20 switched branches (30%)** saw scores decline
- The best-performing branches gained **6–8 points**; those that struggled lost **2–3.5 points**
- By comparison, only half of the non-switched branches improved during the same period

### **3. The Evidence Is Suggestive, Not Definitive**
- Statistical confidence level: **89%** (p = 0.11)
- The 95% confidence interval ranges from **-0.3 to +4.2 points**—this includes zero, meaning we cannot rule out no effect
- Effect size is **moderate** (Cohen's d = 0.57), indicating a practically meaningful impact if the effect is real

### **4. No Pre-Existing Differences in Trends**
- Before the switch, both groups followed similar patterns
- The improvement in switched branches began after week 15, when the new procedure was implemented
- This supports the conclusion that the procedure change—not other factors—drove the observed differences

---

## **Recommendations**

### **1. Do Not Mandate Immediate System-Wide Rollout**
Given the statistical uncertainty and variation in branch outcomes, a mandatory rollout carries unacceptable risk of disappointing results at multiple branches.

### **2. Extend and Improve the Pilot**
- Collect **8–12 additional weeks of data** to increase statistical confidence
- Work with the **6 underperforming branches** to identify implementation barriers
- Document best practices from the **top-performing switched branches**

### **3. Allow Voluntary Adoption**
- Branches may opt into the new procedure with **enhanced training support**
- Require participating branches to commit to **full implementation fidelity** for at least 8 weeks
- Monitor outcomes and share results transparently

### **4. Return to Board in One Quarter**
- Present updated analysis with expanded data
- Recommend full rollout only if confidence interval excludes zero and at least 80% of branches show improvement

---

## **Final Verdict**

**Do not proceed with mandatory system-wide rollout at this time.**

The data shows a positive signal, but it is not strong enough to justify mandating this change across all 34 branches. The 11% probability that this result could occur by chance, combined with the fact that nearly one-third of pilot branches saw scores decline, means a full rollout risks wasting resources and potentially harming service at multiple locations.

**However, do not abandon the procedure either.** The magnitude of the observed effect (+2 points) is operationally meaningful, and 70% of branches did improve. This warrants continued investment in understanding and refining the implementation.

**The prudent path forward:** Extend the pilot, support voluntary adopters, learn from both successes and struggles, and return to the board with stronger evidence in 90 days. If the effect is real, it will become clearer with more data. If it is not, we will have avoided a costly system-wide mistake.
