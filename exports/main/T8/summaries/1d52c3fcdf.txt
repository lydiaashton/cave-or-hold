# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure produced a small but real improvement in service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw scores increase by approximately **2.4 points** (about 5%) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This contradicts the prior expectation of no difference. The effect is statistically significant and consistent across all post-switch weeks.

---

## **Final Verdict**

**Proceed with rollout, conditional on costs.**

The evidence clearly shows the new procedure improves service scores. The question is whether a 5% improvement justifies the investment:

| If Implementation Costs Are... | Recommendation |
|-------------------------------|----------------|
| **Low** (minimal training, no equipment) | **Roll out immediately** — clear net benefit |
| **Moderate** (some training, minor disruptions) | **Roll out with monitoring** — likely worthwhile |
| **High** (major retraining, new equipment, significant downtime) | **Conduct cost-benefit analysis first** — improvement may not justify expense |

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Overview**
- **150 branches** participated: 90 adopted the new procedure, 60 kept the old procedure
- Data covers **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Analysis used a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes over time between the two groups

### **Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 49.7 | 51.1 | +1.4 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 49.8 | 53.6 | +3.8 |
| **Net Effect of New Procedure** | | | **+2.4** |

- The new procedure produced a **2.4-point improvement** in service scores (95% confidence interval: 1.0 to 3.8 points)
- This effect is **statistically significant** (p = 0.001)
- Results hold after accounting for staffing levels, visitor volume, and inherent differences between branches

### **Why We Trust This Result**
- **Before the switch**, both groups had nearly identical service score trends (average difference of only 0.02 points in weeks 6–14)
- **After the switch**, every single week showed a larger treatment-control gap than any pre-switch week
- This confirms the improvement was caused by the new procedure, not pre-existing differences between branches

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Quantify implementation costs** — estimate training time, labor hours, and any equipment needs per branch

2. **Make rollout decision** — based on cost-benefit assessment using the 5% improvement estimate

3. **Monitor during implementation** — track service scores as remaining branches adopt the procedure

4. **Document best practices** — capture what switching branches did to implement successfully

5. **Optional follow-up** — after full rollout, evaluate whether the improvement is sustained over time

---

## **Appendix: Technical Notes**

*For those interested in the methodology:*

- Analysis used difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch) to account for repeated observations
- Results are robust to inclusion of branch fixed effects and control variables (staffing index, visits index)
- Service score is an index where higher values indicate better service; the cooperative-wide average is approximately 50
- The parallel trends assumption was verified by examining pre-period differences between groups
