# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new shelving and stock rotation procedure significantly improved service scores.** Contrary to the expectation of no difference, branches that adopted the new procedure saw their service scores increase by approximately **2.1 points (4.3%)** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant and the analysis meets standard validity checks. **We recommend proceeding with a full rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measurable Improvement in Service Quality
- Branches using the new procedure achieved service scores **2.13 points higher** than expected based on pre-existing trends
- This represents a **4.3% improvement** relative to the average branch score of ~50
- The 95% confidence interval is **[0.98 to 3.26 points]**, meaning we can be highly confident the true effect is positive

### 2. Strong Statistical Evidence
- The result is **highly statistically significant (p < 0.001)**
- Analysis covered **150 branches** over **17 weeks** (weeks 6-22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure; **60 branches** served as a control group

### 3. Analysis Validity Confirmed
- **Parallel trends assumption is satisfied**: Treatment and control branches showed similar service score patterns before the switch (p = 0.80), validating our comparison approach
- Results hold after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume
- The improvement appeared consistently across the post-switch period

### 4. What the Data Shows

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (Old Procedure) | 48.8 | 49.7 | +0.9 |
| Treatment (New Procedure) | 48.9 | 52.0 | +3.1 |
| **Difference** | | | **+2.1** |

---

## **Technical Notes**

This analysis used a **difference-in-differences** research design, comparing branches that switched procedures to those that did not, before and after week 15. Standard errors were clustered at the branch level to account for repeated observations. The parallel trends assumption—the key validity condition for this approach—was tested and supported (pre-period interaction term p = 0.80).

*Analysis prepared from branches.csv (2,550 observations, 150 branches, weeks 6-22)*
