# **Memorandum to the Board**

**Subject:** New Branch Procedure Impact on Service Scores – Revised Analysis  
**Date:** November 15, 2026

---

## **Bottom Line**

**We cannot conclude the new procedure improved service scores.** While branches that switched showed higher scores after the change, this apparent improvement was likely due to a **pre-existing upward trend** in those branches—not the new procedure itself. When we account for this trend, the estimated effect is statistically indistinguishable from zero.

**Recommendation: Do not proceed with system-wide rollout until we have clearer evidence.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### The Problem: Treatment and Control Groups Were Different Before the Switch

| Period | Treatment Branches | Control Branches |
|--------|-------------------|------------------|
| Pre-switch trend (weeks 6–14) | **+0.22 points/week** | **-0.07 points/week** |

Treatment branches were already improving **before** they adopted the new procedure. Control branches were essentially flat. This violates the key assumption needed to attribute post-switch differences to the procedure change.

### What the Numbers Show

| Analysis Method | Estimated Effect | Statistically Significant? |
|-----------------|-----------------|---------------------------|
| Simple comparison (ignores pre-trend) | +1.98 points | Yes |
| **Controls for pre-existing trend** | **-0.46 points** | **No** |
| Post-switch only (controls for baseline) | +1.96 points | Yes |

The first method is **misleading** because it doesn't account for the fact that treatment branches were already on an upward trajectory. The second method (which properly accounts for pre-trends) shows **no detectable effect**.

### Why This Matters

If treatment branches were already gaining ~0.22 points per week before the switch, we would expect them to gain roughly **1.8 points over 8 weeks** even without any procedure change. This explains nearly all of the observed "improvement."

---

## **What Could Explain the Pre-Existing Trend?**

We don't know for certain, but possibilities include:

1. **Selection bias:** Branches chosen to pilot the new procedure may have been ones already showing improvement or with more motivated staff
2. **Anticipation effects:** Treatment branches may have begun changing behavior before week 15 in anticipation of the switch
3. **Other concurrent changes:** Treatment branches may have received other improvements (staffing, resources) unrelated to the procedure

---

## **Recommendations**

### 1. **Pause the Rollout**
Do not expand the new procedure system-wide until we have stronger evidence of its effectiveness.

### 2. **Gather Better Evidence**
Options for obtaining clearer answers:
- **Randomized rollout:** Randomly assign remaining branches to treatment vs. control to ensure groups are comparable
- **Staggered implementation:** Roll out in waves and use more robust statistical methods
- **Longer observation period:** Collect more post-switch data to see if effects persist or grow

### 3. **Investigate Why Treatment Branches Were Different**
Understand what distinguished the pilot branches. If they had characteristics that made them more likely to improve regardless of procedure, the new approach may still work when applied more broadly—or it may not.

### 4. **Consider Qualitative Feedback**
Survey branch staff about whether the new procedure feels like an improvement. Quantitative scores may not capture all benefits (or costs).

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did service scores improve in treatment branches? | **Yes** – but they were already improving before the switch |
| Can we attribute this to the new procedure? | **No** – the evidence does not support a causal claim |
| Should we roll out the new procedure system-wide? | **No** – not without better evidence |
| Was the pilot a waste? | **No** – it revealed a critical evaluation flaw before costly expansion |

**The responsible decision is to pause, redesign the evaluation, and gather stronger evidence before committing system-wide resources.**
