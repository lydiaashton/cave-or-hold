# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure produced a modest but real improvement in service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.2 points (4.4%)** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. The effect is statistically significant and sustained over the observation period, though **30% of branches saw little or no benefit**.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **60 branches** continued with the old procedure as a control group
- Analysis uses difference-in-differences methodology with branch and week fixed effects

### Impact on Service Scores

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|---------------|---------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 50.65 | 52.54 | +1.89 |
| Control branches | 50.84 | 50.51 | -0.34 |
| **Net effect** | | | **+2.23** |

- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.01
- **95% confidence interval:** 1.4 to 3.0 points
- **Effect size:** 0.21 standard deviations (small-to-moderate)

### Consistency and Reliability
- **70% of switched branches** improved; **30% saw little or no benefit**
- Effect held steady across all 8 post-treatment weeks (no fade-out detected)
- Parallel trends verified before the switch
- Not explained by changes in staffing or visit volumes

### Limitations
- Only **8 weeks of post-treatment data** — long-term effects unknown
- Implementation costs not captured in this analysis
- Substantial variation across branches suggests context matters

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with phased rollout** — implement in waves to monitor effects before full deployment

2. **Identify at-risk branches** — investigate characteristics of the 30% that didn't improve to target support

3. **Define success metrics** — establish what improvement level justifies implementation costs

4. **Plan follow-up evaluation** — reassess after 6–12 months to confirm effects persist

5. **Maintain adequate staffing** — staffing levels positively affect scores and should not be reduced

---

## **Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **Did the new procedure improve service scores?** | **Yes** — average increase of 2.2 points (4.4%) |
| **Is the effect statistically reliable?** | **Yes** — p < 0.01, robust to multiple specifications |
| **Is the effect large enough to matter?** | **Modestly** — meaningful but not transformative |
| **Will every branch benefit?** | **No** — approximately 30% saw little or no improvement |
| **Should we roll out system-wide?** | **Yes, with conditions** — proceed with phased implementation, monitoring, and support for branches that may struggle |

**Recommendation:** **Approve rollout with monitoring.** The evidence supports adoption, but the board should treat this as a measured improvement rather than a breakthrough, and should plan for ongoing evaluation and targeted support during implementation.

---

*Analysis based on weekly service score data from weeks 6–22. The switch occurred at week 15. Statistical methods include difference-in-differences regression with branch and week fixed effects.*
