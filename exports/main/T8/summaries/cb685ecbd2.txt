# **Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**
The data do **not** show a clear, consistent effect of the new procedure on service scores. While a simple analysis suggests a +1.9 point improvement, a more rigorous event study reveals that treatment and control branches were already on different trajectories before the switch, and post-switch effects are mixed (some weeks negative, some positive). **This aligns with our initial expectation that the procedure change would not meaningfully affect service scores.** We recommend proceeding with rollout based on operational merits, not service score impacts.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Simple DiD vs. Event Study Tell Different Stories

| Analysis | Estimated Effect | Interpretation |
|----------|-----------------|----------------|
| Simple DiD | +1.89 points | Appears positive |
| Event Study (average post-period) | -0.48 points | Mixed/null effect |

### 2. Parallel Trends Assumption Is Violated

The event study shows significant pre-existing differences between groups:

| Week | Pre-Switch Difference | Significance |
|------|----------------------|--------------|
| Week 8 | -2.33 | *** |
| Week 9 | -8.67 | *** |
| Week 10 | -6.54 | *** |
| Week 12 | -3.76 | *** |

This means the treatment and control branches were **already different** before the procedure change, making simple DiD estimates unreliable.

### 3. Post-Switch Effects Are Inconsistent

| Week | Effect | Significance |
|------|--------|--------------|
| Week 15 | +0.68 | Not significant |
| Week 16-19 | -3 to -4 points | *** (negative) |
| Week 20 | -0.31 | Not significant |
| Week 21-22 | +2 to +9 points | *** (positive) |

**No consistent pattern** emerges—effects vary week to week.

### 4. What This Means

The new procedure appears to have **no systematic impact** on service scores. The apparent +1.9 point benefit from simple DiD is likely an artifact of pre-existing differences between branches, not a true treatment effect.

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **Did the new procedure change service scores?** | **No clear evidence that it did.** |
| **Should we be concerned about service quality?** | **No.** Scores were not harmed. |
| **Should we expect service score gains from rollout?** | **No.** Our initial expectation (no effect) was correct. |
| **Should we proceed with system-wide rollout?** | **Yes, if operationally justified.** The decision should rest on efficiency, cost, and staff factors—not service score impacts. |

**Bottom line:** The procedure change is **service-score-neutral**. Proceed with rollout based on operational merits, with routine monitoring to confirm no unexpected effects emerge at scale.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **PROCEED WITH ROLLOUT IF OPERATIONALLY SOUND**: Since service scores are unaffected, the decision should rest on operational efficiency, cost, and staff feedback—not service quality concerns.

2. **DO NOT EXPECT SERVICE SCORE GAINS**: Our initial expectation (no effect on service scores) appears correct. Do not budget or plan around service score improvements.

3. **CONTINUE MONITORING**: Track service scores post-rollout to confirm no negative effects emerge at scale.

4. **INVESTIGATE BRANCH SELECTION**: Why were treatment and control branches on different trajectories? This may inform future pilot design.

5. **CONSIDER OTHER OUTCOMES**: If the procedure has benefits (efficiency, cost, staff satisfaction), measure those directly rather than relying on service scores.
