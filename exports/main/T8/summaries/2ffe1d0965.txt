# **Library Cooperative Procedure Change: Impact Analysis**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new shelving and rotation procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw their service scores increase by **+2.34 points** (95% CI: 1.76 to 2.92) more than branches that kept the old procedure. This represents a **4.4% improvement** and is highly statistically significant (p < 0.001). The analysis passes all validity checks, including the parallel pre-trends assumption.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all remaining branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
| | |
|---|---|
| **Branches studied** | 709 (425 treatment, 284 control) |
| **Study period** | 17 weeks (weeks 6–22) |
| **Switch date** | Week 15 |
| **Data completeness** | 100% (no missing observations) |

### Service Score Results

| Group | Before | After | Change |
|-------|--------|-------|--------|
| New Procedure | 51.83 | 54.86 | +3.02 |
| Old Procedure | 52.53 | 53.21 | +0.68 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+2.34** |

### Statistical Evidence
- **Treatment effect:** +2.34 points (95% CI: 1.76 to 2.92)
- **P-value:** < 0.001
- **Effect size:** 0.23 standard deviations (moderate practical significance)
- **Pre-trend test:** p = 0.96 (parallel trends confirmed)

### Why This Is Causal
The difference-in-differences design compares changes over time between switching and non-switching branches. Because both groups followed parallel trends before the switch, the divergence after week 15 can be attributed to the procedure change itself, not to pre-existing differences.
