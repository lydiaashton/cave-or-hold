# **Board Summary: New Branch Procedure Evaluation**

---

## **Bottom Line**

The evidence is **inconclusive**. The new procedure shows a positive association with service scores (+1.9 points), but we **cannot rule out that the true effect is zero**. We recommend **continuing to collect data** before making any rollout decision.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. The Result
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect | +1.9 points |
| 95% Confidence interval | **-0.3 to +4.1 points** |
| Statistical significance | Not significant (p = 0.10) |

**What this means:** The data are consistent with effects ranging from a small negative impact (-0.3 points) to a moderate positive impact (+4.1 points). We do not have sufficient evidence to conclude the procedure works.

### 2. Study Scope
- **34 branches** (20 switched, 14 did not)
- **17 weeks** of data (9 weeks before, 8 weeks after the switch)
- **578 observations** total

### 3. Why the Uncertainty?
- **Short post-switch period**: Only 8 weeks of data after implementation
- **Modest sample size**: 34 branches limits statistical power
- **Natural variation**: Week-to-week score fluctuations are larger than the observed effect
- **No pre-existing differences in trends**: Both groups moved similarly before the switch (supporting validity of the comparison)

### 4. What We Can Say
- ✓ The procedure does **not appear harmful** (lower bound of CI is near zero)
- ✓ The **direction of the effect is positive** across all analytical approaches
- ✗ We **cannot confirm** the procedure improves service scores
- ✗ We **cannot quantify** the likely magnitude of benefit

---

## **Recommendations**

### **Do Not Roll Out Yet — Gather More Evidence**

| Action | Rationale | Timeline |
|--------|-----------|----------|
| **Continue current arrangement** | Evidence is insufficient to justify system-wide change | Immediate |
| **Collect 12+ more weeks of data** | Would substantially improve statistical power | Next 3 months |
| **Re-analyze with extended data** | May achieve statistical significance if effect is real | Q2 2026 |
| **Consider pilot expansion only if** | Additional branches could accelerate learning | Optional |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;• Resources allow monitoring | | |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;• No operational disruption | | |

### **Decision Criteria for Future Rollout**

Proceed to system-wide implementation only when:
- ✓ Effect remains positive (point estimate > +1.0 points)
- ✓ 95% confidence interval excludes zero (p < 0.05)
- ✓ Effect is stable across multiple months
- ✓ Operational feedback from switched branches is favorable

---

## **What Would Change Our Conclusion?**

| Scenario | Implication |
|----------|-------------|
| Effect stays ~+2 points with more data | Becomes statistically significant; recommend rollout |
| Effect shrinks toward zero | Procedure has no meaningful impact; do not rollout |
| Effect grows to +3 or more | Strong benefit; prioritize rollout |
| Effect turns negative | Procedure is harmful; discontinue at switched branches |

---

## **Limitations**

- **8 weeks post-switch** is a short observation window for organizational changes
- **Service score is one metric** — operational efficiency, staff time, and patron satisfaction were not measured
- **Cannot assess costs** — training time and transition disruption not quantified

---

## **Final Verdict**

> ### **DO NOT PROCEED WITH SYSTEM-WIDE ROLLOUT AT THIS TIME**
>
> **Reason:** The evidence does not meet the threshold for a major operational change. The confidence interval includes zero, meaning we cannot be confident the procedure has any real effect.
>
> **Required before reconsideration:**
> 1. At least 12 additional weeks of data collection
> 2. Statistical significance at the 95% confidence level (p < 0.05)
> 3. Stable or growing effect size over time
>
> **Risk of acting now:** Potential waste of resources implementing a procedure that may have no benefit, or disruption to branches for minimal gain (~2 points on a 50-point scale).
>
> **Risk of waiting:** Minimal — branches that switched can continue using the procedure while we gather evidence.

---

## **Bottom Line for Decision-Makers**

> *"The data suggest the new procedure may help, but we don't have enough evidence to be confident. Investing in more data collection now will prevent a costly mistake — either rolling out something that doesn't work, or abandoning something that does."*
