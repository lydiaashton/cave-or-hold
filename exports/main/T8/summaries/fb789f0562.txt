# **BOARD MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors

**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure  

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

# ❌ DO NOT PROCEED WITH SYSTEM-WIDE ROLLOUT

**The evidence is insufficient to conclude the new procedure causes improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the procedure were already outperforming control branches before implementation. The observed 1.8-point improvement cannot be reliably attributed to the procedure itself.

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did treatment branches improve more than control branches? | **Yes** (+1.8 points) |
| Can we attribute this to the new procedure? | **No** — pre-existing trends explain the gap |
| Should we roll out to all branches? | **Not yet** — need stronger evidence |

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### What the Data Shows

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Branches with **new procedure** | 49.7 | 54.8 | **+5.0** |
| Branches with **old procedure** | 49.2 | 52.4 | +3.2 |
| **Raw difference** | | | **+1.8** |

### The Critical Problem: Non-Parallel Pre-Trends

**Difference-in-differences analysis requires parallel trends before the intervention. This assumption is violated:**

| Week | Gap (Treatment − Control) |
|------|---------------------------|
| Week 6 | −0.63 points |
| Week 10 | +0.25 points |
| Week 14 | **+2.51 points** |

**The treatment group was already gaining 3.1 points on the control group BEFORE the switch.** This divergence is statistically significant (p < 0.001).

### What This Means

- Branches selected for the new procedure may have had **better management, more motivated staff, or other advantages**
- The 1.8-point "effect" could be entirely explained by these pre-existing differences
- **We cannot isolate the causal impact of the procedure** from this observational data

---

## **IMPLICATIONS**

| Scenario | True Procedure Effect | Risk of Full Rollout |
|----------|----------------------|---------------------|
| Optimistic | ~1.5–2.0 points | Low |
| Realistic | ~0.5–1.0 points | Moderate |
| Pessimistic | ~0 points | **High** (costs without benefits) |

**We simply do not know which scenario is correct.**

---

## **RECOMMENDED NEXT STEPS**

| Priority | Action | Timeline |
|----------|--------|----------|
| **1** | **Pause full rollout** | Immediate |
| **2** | **Randomize remaining 284 branches** (50% treatment, 50% control) | 2–4 weeks |
| **3** | **Collect 12+ weeks of post-switch data** | Ongoing |
| **4** | **Investigate branch selection criteria** | 4 weeks |
| **5** | **Return to Board with causal evidence** | Quarter 1 |

---

## **CONCLUSION**

> **"Association is not causation."**

The new procedure is **associated with** higher service scores, but we cannot conclude it **causes** higher service scores. The branches that adopted it were already on a stronger trajectory before implementation.

**A randomized evaluation would provide definitive evidence** and allow the Board to make an informed decision about system-wide rollout. Until then, proceeding with full rollout risks investing significant resources in an unproven intervention.
