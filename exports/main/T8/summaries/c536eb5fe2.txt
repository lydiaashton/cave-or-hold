# **FINAL BOARD MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The evidence is inconclusive.** While branches that switched procedures show higher service scores after the change, **we cannot confidently attribute this improvement to the new procedure itself.** Branches that switched were already on a different trajectory before the switch, which undermines our ability to isolate the procedure's true effect. **We recommend against a full rollout until more rigorous evidence is obtained.**

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **Did service scores improve after the switch?** | ✓ Yes |
| **Can we credit the new procedure?** | ⚠️ Uncertain |
| **Is rollout justified by current evidence?** | ✗ No |
| **Recommended action** | **Pause and test** |

**Rationale:** The 2.2-point improvement observed in switched branches is real, but approximately 30-50% of this gain may reflect pre-existing trends rather than the procedure's effect. Rolling out system-wide based on current evidence risks investing resources in a change that may deliver smaller benefits than expected—or none at all.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### 1. What the Raw Data Shows

| Branch Type | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 49.1 | 51.0 | +1.9 |
| Switched (new procedure) | 50.2 | 54.3 | +4.1 |
| **Simple Difference** | — | — | **+2.2 points** |

### 2. ⚠️ Critical Limitation: Pre-Trends Are Not Parallel

The difference-in-differences method requires that treated and control branches were on similar trajectories before the switch. **This assumption is violated:**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Pre-trend test p-value** | 0.0006 |
| **Interpretation** | Treated branches were already improving faster than control branches BEFORE the switch |

**Pre-period trend slopes (weeks 6-14):**
- Control branches: +0.10 points/week (not significant)
- Switched branches: **+0.42 points/week** (highly significant)

This means switched branches were gaining ~0.3 points/week on control branches even before adopting the new procedure.

### 3. Event Study Results

Looking week-by-week relative to the switch:

| Period | Pattern |
|--------|---------|
| **Weeks 6-8** | Switched branches significantly BELOW control (−1.6 to −2.7 points) |
| **Weeks 9-14** | Gap narrows; switched branches catching up |
| **Weeks 15-22** | Switched branches pull ahead (+0.3 to +2.0 points) |

**The problem:** The post-switch improvement could be (a) the procedure's effect, or (b) continuation of the pre-existing convergence trend, or (c) both. We cannot distinguish these.

### 4. What This Means for the Estimated Effect

The 2.2-point "effect" is likely **upwardly biased**. Some portion of this gain would have occurred even without the procedure change, because switched branches were already on an upward trajectory.

**Realistic effect range:** 0.0 to 1.5 points (rather than the observed 2.2 points)

---

## **WHY DID THIS HAPPEN?**

Branches were not randomly assigned to switch procedures. Possible explanations for the pre-trend difference:

1. **Selection bias:** Higher-performing or more motivated branches may have volunteered to switch first
2. **Mean reversion:** Branches with unusually low scores in early weeks may have been selected, and scores naturally regressed toward average
3. **Anticipation effects:** Branches may have prepared for the switch before week 15, improving scores in advance

---

## **RECOMMENDATIONS**

### Priority 1: Conduct a Randomized Evaluation
- Randomly assign remaining branches to switch now vs. delay 8-12 weeks
- This creates valid counterfactual and enables causal inference
- Cost: Minimal (delayed benefit for control group)
- Value: Definitive evidence for cooperative-wide decision

### Priority 2: If Rollout Proceeds Despite Uncertainty
- Implement in phases with delayed-start control groups
- Track service scores weekly for all branches
- Set explicit success criteria (e.g., minimum 1.0 point sustained improvement)
- Be prepared to halt or modify if effects don't materialize

### Priority 3: Gather Complementary Evidence
- Interview branch staff about implementation experience
- Analyze cost implications of the new procedure
- Investigate whether effects vary by branch size, location, or community

---

## **CONCLUSION**

The switched branches show improved service scores, but **we cannot determine whether the new procedure caused this improvement**. The pre-existing differences between switched and control branches create substantial uncertainty about the true effect.

**Our verdict:** The evidence is insufficient to justify system-wide rollout. We recommend a randomized evaluation or, if proceeding, a carefully monitored phased implementation with explicit success criteria and willingness to adjust.
