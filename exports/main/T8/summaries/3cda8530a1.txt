# **Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure Evaluation**

---

## **Bottom Line**

The evidence is **suggestive but not conclusive**. The new procedure is associated with a +2.3 point advantage in service scores, but this result is not statistically significant at conventional levels (p=0.09). The data shows switched branches were more resilient than non-switched branches, yet we cannot definitively attribute this to the procedure. We recommend **continuing the study with expanded monitoring** before committing to a full rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** total: 20 adopted the new procedure (treatment), 14 kept the old procedure (control)
- **17 weeks of data**: Weeks 6–14 (before switch) and Weeks 15–22 (after switch)
- **578 branch-week observations** analyzed using difference-in-differences methodology

### **What the Data Shows**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| **Switched Branches** | 52.74 | 52.29 | -0.45 |
| **Non-Switched Branches** | 53.93 | 51.15 | -2.78 |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | | | **+2.32** |

**Interpretation**: Switched branches declined less than non-switched branches. However, with a p-value of 0.088, there is approximately a **9% probability** this pattern occurred by chance.

### **Statistical Confidence**
- **95% Confidence Interval**: -0.3 to +5.0 points
- **P-value**: 0.088 (**not significant** at α=0.05)
- **Conclusion**: The data is compatible with no effect, a small negative effect, or a moderate positive effect

### **Validity Checks**
- ✅ **Parallel trends confirmed**: Switched and non-switched branches were on similar trajectories before the switch (p=0.70)
- ✅ **Results robust**: Effect size unchanged when controlling for staffing levels and visitor volumes
- ⚠️ **Limited power**: With 34 branches, the study may be underpowered to detect small but real effects

---

## **Final Verdict**

### **Do Not Approve Full Rollout at This Time**

**Rationale:**

| Consideration | Assessment |
|--------------|------------|
| **Statistical evidence** | Insufficient (p=0.088, CI includes zero) |
| **Risk of wasted investment** | Moderate—rollout costs may not yield expected benefits |
| **Risk of missed opportunity** | Low—procedure can be adopted later if evidence strengthens |
| **Current trajectory** | Switched branches holding steady; no urgency to act |

**The Board Should:**

1. **Extend data collection** for 8–12 additional weeks
2. **Request a follow-up analysis** with the expanded dataset
3. **Revisit the rollout decision** at the next quarterly meeting

**Threshold for Approval:**
- P-value < 0.05, **OR**
- Lower bound of 95% CI > 0 (entirely positive), **OR**
- Point estimate > 3.0 points with p < 0.10

---

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended Actions**

1. **Extend the study period**
   - Continue collecting data for 8–12 additional weeks
   - This will improve statistical power and precision

2. **Consider a phased pilot expansion**
   - Roll out to 5–7 additional branches as a pilot
   - Monitor closely before deciding on system-wide implementation

3. **Investigate the control group decline**
   - Non-switched branches dropped 2.8 points—understand why
   - This decline may indicate external pressures affecting all branches

4. **Re-convene the board in 3 months**
   - With additional data, we can provide a more definitive recommendation
   - Decision thresholds should be established in advance (see above)

---

## **Conclusion**

The data is **promising but not definitive**. The new procedure shows a positive association with service scores, but statistical uncertainty remains. Given the stakes of a system-wide rollout, we recommend gathering more evidence before making a final decision. The study design is sound, and additional weeks of data will substantially improve our ability to detect a true effect.

**Bottom line for the board: Wait for stronger evidence before approving full rollout.**
