# **Memorandum to the Board of Directors**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative
**From:** Data Analysis Team
**Date:** November 15, 2026
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure works.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure achieved service scores approximately **2.16 points higher** than comparable branches using the old procedure. This improvement is statistically reliable and represents a **4% gain** in service quality. We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **The Numbers**

| Measure | Result |
|---------|--------|
| Branches studied | 709 (425 switched, 284 did not) |
| Study period | 17 weeks (weeks 6–22 of the year) |
| Estimated improvement from new procedure | **+2.16 service score points** |
| Statistical confidence | Greater than 99.99% (p < 0.0000000001) |
| 95% Confidence Interval | 1.60 to 2.73 points |

### **What the Data Shows**

1. **Before the switch** (weeks 6–14), branches that would later adopt the new procedure had virtually identical service scores to those that would not. The average difference was only 0.03 points (p=0.91), confirming the two groups were comparable at the start.

2. **After the switch** (weeks 15–22), service scores declined across the entire cooperative for reasons unrelated to the procedure change. However:
   - Branches keeping the **old procedure** saw scores drop by about **3.0 points**
   - Branches using the **new procedure** saw scores drop by only **0.8 points**

3. **The new procedure protected service quality.** Rather than producing an absolute increase, it shielded branches from the broader decline affecting the cooperative.

### **Why We Are Confident**

- The 95% confidence interval [1.60, 2.73] does not include zero—the effect is definitively positive
- Results remain consistent after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume
- Pre-trend analysis confirms treatment and control groups were on parallel trajectories before the switch

## **Appendix: Analytical Method**

This analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** design, the standard approach for evaluating policy changes when randomized assignment is not feasible. The method compares changes in outcomes for branches that adopted the new procedure to changes for branches that did not, isolating the effect of the procedure itself from other factors affecting all branches.

Standard errors are clustered at the branch level to account for repeated observations over time. Full technical documentation and data visualizations available upon request.
