# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Library Cooperative Board  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new shelving and rotation system saw their service scores increase by approximately **2.2 points** relative to branches that kept the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant and represents a meaningful gain given that the average branch scores around 50.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with system-wide rollout of the new procedure.

---

## **Final Verdict**

**The evidence supports system-wide adoption of the new procedure.**

Our analysis provides strong, credible evidence that the new shelving and rotation procedure causally improved service scores. The 2.2-point improvement is:

- **Statistically certain** — less than 1 in 1,000 chance this result is due to random variation
- **Practically meaningful** — represents roughly a 4% improvement over the average baseline score of 50
- **Consistent** — observed across branches with different baseline performance levels
- **Growing** — effects increased over the 8-week post-switch period, suggesting branches had not yet reached the full benefit by the end of our observation window

**We recommend the Board approve rolling out the new procedure to all remaining branches.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Measurable Improvement in Service Quality**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Treatment Branches (New Procedure) | 51.6 | 52.8 | +1.2 |
| Control Branches (Old Procedure) | 51.3 | 50.4 | −0.9 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+2.2** |

The 2.2-point improvement is the estimated causal effect of the new procedure, isolated from other factors that may have affected all branches during this period.

### **2. Results Are Statistically Robust**

- **Confidence Interval:** 1.6 to 2.7 points (95% confidence)
- **Statistical Significance:** p < 0.001 (less than 1 in 1,000 chance this result is due to random variation)
- **Controls Applied:** Analysis accounts for staffing levels, visitor volume, and inherent differences between individual branches

### **3. Trends Support Causal Interpretation**

Before the switch (weeks 6–14), treatment and control branches tracked closely together, with an average difference of only 0.3 points. After the switch (weeks 15–22), the gap widened to 2.5 points in favor of treatment branches. This pattern supports the conclusion that the procedure change caused the improvement, not pre-existing differences between branches.

### **4. Benefits Accumulate Over Time**

The treatment effect grew from approximately 1.7 points in the first week after implementation to over 3 points by week 22. This suggests branches continue to realize additional benefits as staff become more proficient with the new system.

### **5. Improvements Across All Branch Types**

The positive effect was observed across branches with different baseline performance levels. Both lower-performing and higher-performing branches benefited from the change.

---

## **Study Design**

- **709 branches** total (425 treatment, 284 control)
- **17 weeks** of data (weeks 6–22, 2026)
- **12,053 observations** (one per branch per week)
- **Switch point:** Week 15
- **Method:** Difference-in-Differences regression with branch fixed effects and clustered standard errors

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches.

2. **Develop implementation support** including training materials and on-site assistance. The growing effect over time suggests a learning curve; proper support will help branches realize benefits faster.

3. **Continue monitoring service scores** post-rollout to confirm effects persist at scale and to identify any branches requiring additional support.

4. **Document best practices** from early-adopter branches to accelerate learning across the cooperative.

5. **Conduct cost-benefit analysis** if the new procedure requires additional resources, weighing those costs against the value of sustained service score improvements.
