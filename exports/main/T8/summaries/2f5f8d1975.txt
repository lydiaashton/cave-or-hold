# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Library Cooperative Board  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure is associated with a **2-point improvement** in service scores. The evidence leans positive (roughly 88% probability of a real benefit), but we cannot rule out no effect. 

**Recommendation depends on implementation cost:**
- **If low-cost/low-risk:** Proceed with rollout—the likely benefit outweighs uncertainty
- **If costly/disruptive:** Continue the pilot and collect more data before deciding

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Overview**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **17 branches** adopted the new procedure at week 15; **17 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Analysis compares changes in switching branches against changes in non-switching branches

### **Service Score Results**

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|----------------|---------------|--------|
| Kept Old Procedure | 47.2 | 48.9 | +1.7 |
| Switched to New Procedure | 51.4 | 55.1 | +3.8 |
| **Net Effect of New Procedure** | | | **+2.0** |

### **Statistical Confidence**
- **Estimated effect:** +2.0 points on the service score index
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.12 (not significant at the 5% level)
- **95% confidence interval:** -0.6 to +4.6 points

**What this means:** We cannot confidently rule out that the new procedure has no effect. However, we also cannot rule out a meaningful improvement of up to 4.6 points (roughly 10% of the average score). The confidence interval is 89% positive, suggesting the evidence leans toward benefit.

### **Important Context**
- Branches that switched already had higher service scores than non-switching branches before the change (51.4 vs. 47.2). This suggests the groups differ in ways beyond the procedure.
- The service score gap between the two groups widened after the switch, but the pattern varies week-to-week.
- Higher staffing levels are associated with higher service scores across all branches.

---

## **Final Verdict**

### **Proceed with Phased Rollout, With Monitoring**

**Rationale:**
1. **The signal is positive.** The 2-point estimate is in the expected direction, and most of the confidence interval suggests benefit rather than harm.
2. **Procedure changes are typically reversible.** Unlike capital investments, shelving procedures can be adjusted or reverted if outcomes disappoint.
3. **Waiting has costs.** Delaying a potentially beneficial change means branches continue operating below their potential.
4. **Uncertainty argues for caution, not inaction.** A phased rollout allows learning while spreading benefits.

**Conditions:**
- Roll out to **half of remaining branches** in the next quarter
- **Monitor service scores monthly** for 12 weeks post-implementation
- **Set a review date** (e.g., February 2027) to evaluate whether effects match expectations
- **Be prepared to pause or adjust** if scores decline or show no improvement

**If the procedure requires significant training or resources,** consider continuing the pilot for 8–12 more weeks before committing.

## **Decision Framework**

| If follow-up analysis shows... | Then... |
|-------------------------------|---------|
| Effect remains ~2 points or higher | Complete full rollout |

| Effect shrinks toward zero | Pause and investigate implementation issues |

---

## **Technical Appendix**

- **Method:** Difference-in-differences regression with branch and week fixed effects
- **Standard errors:** Clustered at the branch level to account for repeated observations
- **Controls:** Staffing index and visits index (neither substantially alters the main result)
- **Data quality:** Complete records for all 34 branches across all 17 weeks; no missing values
- **Parallel trends:** Pre-switch gap between groups was relatively stable (4–5 points), supporting the validity of the comparison
