# MEMORANDUM

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## BOTTOM LINE

**The new procedure shows promising but not definitive evidence of improving service scores.** The estimated +2.2 point improvement is consistent across all analytical approaches, but statistical uncertainty and baseline differences between branches prevent a confident recommendation. The board faces a judgment call: proceed with caution given the positive signal, or wait for stronger evidence.

---

## KEY FINDINGS

### 1. Estimated Effect on Service Scores

| Group | Pre-Switch Average | Post-Switch Average | Change |
|-------|-------------------|---------------------|--------|
| Switched branches (20 branches) | 46.7 | 48.5 | +1.8 |
| Non-switched branches (14 branches) | 50.3 | 49.8 | −0.4 |
| **Estimated effect of new procedure** | | | **+2.2 points** |

### 2. Statistical Confidence

- **P-value: 0.10** — Above the 0.05 threshold for statistical significance
- **95% Confidence Interval: −0.4 to +4.7 points** — The true effect could be zero or slightly negative
- **Interpretation:** We cannot rule out that the observed improvement occurred by chance

### 3. Critical Limitations

**Groups Were Not Comparable:** Branches that switched to the new procedure scored 3–5 points lower than control branches throughout the entire study period, both before and after the switch. This suggests switched branches may have been selected because they were already underperforming, which biases our results.

**Pre-Existing Trends Differed:** Statistical tests confirm that treatment and control branches followed different trajectories before the switch (p < 0.001), violating a key assumption of our analysis method.

**Effects Were Inconsistent:** The estimated benefit varied widely across the eight post-switch weeks, with only the final week showing a statistically significant positive effect.

---

## FINAL VERDICT

**Recommendation: Do not proceed with system-wide rollout at this time.**

**Rationale:**

1. **The evidence is not strong enough to justify system-wide change.** A p-value of 0.10 means we lack the statistical confidence typically required for major operational decisions affecting all 34 branches.

2. **The study design limits causal inference.** Because switched branches were systematically different from control branches from the start, we cannot be certain the improvement was caused by the new procedure rather than other factors (e.g., management attention, regression to the mean, or concurrent initiatives).

3. **The potential upside is modest.** Even if the +2.2 point effect is real, it represents approximately 4% improvement on the service score index. The board should weigh this against implementation costs, staff training time, and operational disruption.

4. **Better evidence is achievable.** Extending the observation period and collecting more post-switch data would narrow the confidence interval and provide greater certainty at relatively low cost.

**However,** if the board is inclined to move forward despite these concerns, we recommend a **phased rollout** starting with branches most similar to the original 20 treatment branches (i.e., those with lower baseline scores), combined with rigorous ongoing monitoring and a predefined threshold for success.
