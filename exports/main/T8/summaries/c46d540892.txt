# **MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

The new procedure shows a **positive but statistically inconclusive effect** on service scores. Branches that switched improved by an estimated **1.5 points** more than branches that kept the old procedure, but this difference could plausibly be due to random variation. We **cannot confidently conclude** the new procedure caused an improvement based on this data alone.

**Recommendation:** Do not mandate system-wide rollout yet. Extend the observation period and gather more evidence before making a cooperative-wide decision.

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

### **DO NOT APPROVE system-wide rollout at this time**

| Criterion | Finding | Verdict |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| **Statistical significance** | p = 0.17 | ❌ Not met |
| **Confidence interval** | [-0.67, +3.73] includes zero | ❌ Not met |
| **Effect size** | Small (d = 0.15) | ⚠️ Marginal |
| **Direction of effect** | Positive (+1.53 points) | ✓ Favorable |
| **Branch adoption** | 90% of switched branches improved | ✓ Favorable |

**Rationale:** Two of five criteria for a confident rollout decision are not met. The evidence suggests a possible benefit, but the risk of implementing an ineffective procedure across all 34 branches outweighs the potential gain at this time.

**Path to approval:** Revisit this decision after:
1. Collecting 8–12 additional weeks of data, AND/OR
2. Expanding the treatment group by 5–10 branches

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### Study Overview
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Total branches | 34 (20 switched, 14 did not) |
| Study period | Weeks 6–22 (17 weeks) |
| Switch date | Week 15 |
| Observations | 578 branch-weeks |

### Service Score Results

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| **Switched branches** | 47.27 | 51.59 | +4.32 |
| **Non-switched branches** | 47.96 | 50.75 | +2.79 |
| **Net effect (DiD)** | | | **+1.53** |

### Statistical Assessment

| Measure | Value |
|---------|-------|
| Estimated effect | +1.53 points |
| 95% Confidence interval | -0.67 to +3.74 |
| P-value | 0.17 |
| Statistically significant? | **No** |
| Effect size (Cohen's d) | 0.15 (small) |

**Interpretation:** The true effect could be slightly negative, zero, or moderately positive. The data are consistent with no real effect.

### Branch-Level Patterns
- **90%** of switched branches (18/20) showed score improvements
- **79%** of non-switched branches (11/14) also improved
- Both groups improved over time; switched branches improved slightly more
- Some switched branches saw declines (range: -5.3 to +9.9 points)

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

### Recommended Actions

1. **Pause system-wide rollout.** The evidence is suggestive but not conclusive. A cooperative-wide change should rest on firmer evidence.

2. **Extend the observation period.** Eight weeks post-switch may be insufficient for branches to fully adapt. Collect 8–12 additional weeks of data.

3. **Consider a phased expansion.** Invite 5–10 additional branches to adopt the procedure now, creating a larger treatment group and stronger statistical power.

4. **Investigate implementation quality.** Variation in branch-level results suggests execution may matter. Survey branches about fidelity to the new procedure and identify best practices.

5. **Assess cost implications.** Even if the effect is real at +1.5 points, weigh this against any additional staffing, training, or operational costs.

### Decision Framework

| If the board prioritizes... | Recommendation |
|----------------------------|----------------|
| **Evidence-based decisions** | Wait for more data before expanding |
| **Innovation and continuous improvement** | Proceed with cautious, monitored expansion |
| **Risk avoidance** | Maintain status quo; the old procedure is not failing |

---

## **TECHNICAL NOTE**

This analysis used a **difference-in-differences** approach with **cluster-robust standard errors** (clustered by branch), the appropriate method for this quasi-experimental design. The parallel trends assumption appears reasonable—both groups followed similar patterns before the switch. Adding controls for staffing and visit levels did not materially change the results.
