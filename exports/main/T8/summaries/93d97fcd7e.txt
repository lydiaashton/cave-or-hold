# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Contrary to prior expectations, branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by **2.2 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. The effect is statistically significant (p < 0.01), sustained across all post-switch weeks, and represents a meaningful improvement in service quality. We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Study Design**

This pilot evaluated whether the new procedure would affect service quality. We analyzed weekly service scores from **150 branches** over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22). The new procedure was implemented at week 15:

| Group | Branches | Procedure |
|-------|----------|-----------|
| Treatment | 90 branches | New procedure (weeks 15–22) |
| Control | 60 branches | Old procedure (entire period) |

The "difference-in-differences" design isolates the procedure's effect by comparing changes in treatment branches to changes in control branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Service Score Improvement

| Group | Before (Weeks 6–14) | After (Weeks 15–22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------|---------------------|--------|
| New Procedure | 48.2 | 52.2 | +4.0 |
| Old Procedure | 48.2 | 50.0 | +1.8 |
| **Net Effect** | — | — | **+2.2** |

### 2. Statistical Confidence

- **P-value: 0.0003** — highly unlikely to occur by chance
- **95% confidence interval: 1.0 to 3.4 points**
- **Pre-trends validated:** No significant difference between groups before the switch (p = 0.19)

### 3. Sustained Effect

The improvement held consistently across all 8 post-switch weeks, with no evidence of fading.

### 4. Practical Significance

- Effect size: **23% of one standard deviation**
- Relative improvement: **4.6%** over baseline scores
- A branch scoring 48 would be expected to score approximately 50 after adoption

---

## **Final Verdict**

**Recommendation: APPROVE system-wide rollout.**

| Criterion | Assessment |
|-----------|------------|
| **Effect Direction** | ✅ Positive (scores increased) |
| **Statistical Significance** | ✅ Strong (p < 0.01) |
| **Practical Significance** | ✅ Meaningful (4.6% improvement) |
| **Study Validity** | ✅ Sound (parallel trends confirmed) |
| **Effect Consistency** ✅ | Sustained across all post-switch weeks |
| **Implementation Risk** | ✅ Low (no additional budget or staffing required) |

**Rationale:** The evidence is clear, robust, and unexpected. The new procedure delivers measurable service quality improvements at no apparent cost. Delaying rollout would mean withholding a proven benefit from patrons at the 60 branches still using the old procedure.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** to the remaining 60 branches
2. **Phase implementation** to manage training and transition workload
3. **Monitor scores** post-rollout to confirm effects persist at scale
4. **Document the procedure** to ensure consistent implementation

---

## **Methodology**

Difference-in-differences regression with branch and week fixed effects; standard errors clustered by branch; controlled for staffing and visits indices.
