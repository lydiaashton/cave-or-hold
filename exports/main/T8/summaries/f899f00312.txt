# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure  
**Date:** November 2025  
**Prepared by:** Data Analysis Team

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**VERDICT: DO NOT PROCEED WITH SYSTEM-WIDE ROLLOUT**

The evidence does **not** support the conclusion that the new procedure improved service scores. The apparent 1.8-point gain reflects pre-existing differences between branches, not the procedure itself. Rolling out system-wide based on this analysis would risk investing resources in a change that may have no effect.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

| Metric | Value | Interpretation |
|--------|-------|----------------|
| Raw DiD estimate | +1.82 points | Appears positive |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 | Precise estimate |
| **Parallel trends test** | **FAILED (p < 0.001)** | **Critical flaw** |
| Immediate effect at switch | **+0.04 points** | **Essentially zero** |
| Pre-switch gap trend | +0.29 pts/week | Already widening |

### The Critical Problem

**The gap between switched and non-switched branches was growing before the procedure changed:**

| Week | Gap (Switched − Non-Switched) |
|------|------------------------------|
| 6 | 0.28 points |
| 10 | 1.66 points |
| 14 (last pre-switch week) | 2.63 points |
| 15 (first post-switch week) | 2.60 points |
| 16 | 2.79 points |

**There is no visible break at week 15.** The gap continued its pre-existing trajectory. This pattern is inconsistent with a genuine treatment effect.

### Why This Happened

Branches that switched likely differed systematically from those that didn't:
- They may have been better-managed overall
- They may have been more motivated to improve
- They may have implemented other changes simultaneously

The analysis cannot separate the procedure's effect from these confounding factors.

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did the new procedure improve service scores? | **Cannot determine from this data** |
| Is the evidence strong enough for rollout? | **No** |
| Should we invest further in evaluation? | **Yes** |

---

## **RECOMMENDED NEXT STEPS**

| Priority | Action | Timeline |
|----------|--------|----------|
| 1 | **Halt rollout planning** | Immediate |
| 2 | **Design randomized trial** for remaining branches | 4 weeks |
| 3 | **Interview switched branches** about other concurrent changes | 2 weeks |
| 4 | **Collect additional data** (3-6 more months) | Ongoing |
| 5 | **Reconvene board** with stronger evidence | 8-12 weeks |

---

## **WHAT WOULD CONVINCE US?**

A valid evaluation would require:
1. **Random assignment** of branches to switch or not (eliminates selection bias)
2. **Stable pre-trends** (parallel trajectories before intervention)
3. **Clear break at intervention** (visible change in trajectory at week 15)
4. **Sustained effect** (not just one or two weeks)

None of these conditions are met in the current data.

---

**Bottom line:** The analysis is inconclusive. The apparent improvement predates the intervention. We cannot recommend rollout without stronger evidence.
