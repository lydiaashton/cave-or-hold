# **Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure Evaluation**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure produced an unexpected improvement in service scores.** Although the procedure change was not expected to affect service quality, branches that adopted it saw service scores increase by an average of **1.9 points** (approximately 4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and robust. We recommend proceeding with the system-wide rollout, with the added confidence that there is a measurable service quality benefit beyond the operational goals.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Unexpected Improvement in Service Quality
- Branches using the new procedure improved their service scores by **1.9 points more** than branches using the old procedure
- 95% confidence interval: **0.7 to 3.1 points** improvement
- This represents approximately a **4% increase** on the service score scale (average branch scores ~50)
- The result is statistically significant (p = 0.002), meaning there's less than a 1% chance this finding is due to random variation

### 2. Strong Evidence the Effect Is Real
The analysis used a rigorous "difference-in-differences" approach:
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure (treatment group)
- **60 branches** kept the old procedure (control group)
- **Before the switch** (weeks 6-14), both groups had nearly identical service scores (47.1 vs 47.0), confirming they were on parallel trajectories
- **After the switch** (weeks 15-22), the treatment group pulled ahead, with the gap widening by 1.9 points

### 3. Effect Is Not Explained by Other Factors
The improvement holds even after controlling for:
- Staffing levels (staffing index)
- Visitor volume (visits index)
- Branch-specific characteristics

This suggests the procedure change itself—not external factors—drove the improvement.

### 4. Why Might This Happen?
While the procedure was designed for stock rotation and shelving efficiency, several mechanisms could explain the service score improvement:
- Staff spending less time searching for items may have more time for patron assistance
- Better-organized shelves may improve the patron experience directly
- The change may have boosted staff morale or engagement

We recommend investigating these mechanisms during rollout to maximize the benefit.

### 5. Most Branches Benefited
- **79% of treatment branches** saw service score improvements after switching
- Only **60% of control branches** improved during the same period
- However, about 21% of treatment branches saw no improvement or decline, suggesting the procedure may work better in some contexts than others

---

## **Final Verdict**

**ROLL OUT THE NEW PROCEDURE SYSTEM-WIDE.**

The evidence is clear and actionable:
| Criterion | Assessment |
|-----------|------------|
| **Statistical significance** | ✓ Passed (p = 0.002) |
| **Effect size** | ✓ Meaningful (1.9 points, ~4%) |
| **Research design** | ✓ Strong (parallel trends confirmed) |
| **Robustness** | ✓ Holds with controls and clustered SE |
| **Consistency** | ✓ 79% of branches improved |

**Confidence level: High.** The combination of a well-designed natural experiment, statistically significant results, and a plausible mechanism gives us strong confidence that this finding is real and will generalize to the full rollout.

**Risk level: Low.** Even if the service score benefit does not fully materialize at scale, the procedure change was already planned for operational reasons. The service improvement is a bonus, not a requirement for proceeding.

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with System-Wide Rollout**
   - The evidence supports expanding the new procedure to all 150 branches
   - Expected average improvement: ~1.9 points on the service score index
   - This is a welcome additional benefit beyond the operational objectives

2. **Investigate the Mechanism**
   - Since this effect was unexpected, gather qualitative feedback from branch staff
   - Understand which aspects of the new procedure may be driving service improvements
   - Use these insights to refine training and implementation

3. **Monitor Implementation Quality**
   - Given that 21% of early-adopter branches did not see improvement, investigate whether implementation fidelity varied
   - Consider providing additional training or support to ensure consistent adoption

4. **Track Outcomes Post-Rollout**
   - Continue monitoring service scores to confirm the effect persists at scale
   - Watch for any branches that underperform and provide targeted support

5. **Document the Finding**
   - This unexpected benefit should be included in the business case for the procedure change
   - Consider sharing this finding with other library cooperatives considering similar changes

## **Analysis Notes**

- **Data**: 2,550 observations (150 branches × 17 weeks)
- **Method**: Difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered at branch level)
- **Switch date**: Week 15 (mid-study period)
- **Service score**: Higher is better; system average is approximately 50
- **Key assumption**: Parallel trends between treatment and control groups before the switch (confirmed by pre-period data)
