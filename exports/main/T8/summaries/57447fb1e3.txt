# **Memorandum to the Board**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Office  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure  

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Do not roll out the new procedure system-wide.** The data do not demonstrate that the new procedure improves service scores. Treatment branches showed virtually no change (+0.2 points), and the apparent 2-point advantage over control branches is driven by the control group's decline rather than treatment group improvement. Additionally, treatment branches were already outperforming control branches before the switch, raising concerns about selection bias.

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did the new procedure improve service scores? | **No** — treatment branches showed no meaningful change |
| Is the statistical result reliable? | **Uncertain** — pre-existing differences between groups undermine causal inference |
| Should we roll out to all branches? | **No** — insufficient evidence of benefit |
| What should we do instead? | **Extend evaluation** and investigate why control branches declined |

---

## **Key Findings**

### Critical Context
- Treatment branches scored **~1.7 points higher** than control branches throughout the pre-period (weeks 6-14)
- This pre-existing gap suggests systematic differences between the two groups (e.g., better-performing branches may have been more likely to adopt the new procedure)
- The parallel trends assumption required for causal inference is questionable

### What Changed After the Switch

| Group | Before | After | Change |
|-------|--------|-------|--------|
| New Procedure (90 branches) | 50.91 | 51.10 | **+0.19** |
| Old Procedure (60 branches) | 49.22 | 47.36 | **−1.87** |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+2.05** |

The treatment group's service scores remained essentially flat. The statistical "effect" comes entirely from the control group's decline.

### Statistical Summary
- **DiD Estimate:** +2.05 points (95% CI: 0.77 to 3.33)
- **P-value:** 0.002
- **Interpretation:** While statistically significant, the effect is not causally attributable to the procedure given pre-existing group differences and lack of treatment group improvement

---

## **Recommendation**

**Do not proceed with system-wide rollout.** Instead:

1. **Extend the evaluation period** by 8-12 weeks to determine if patterns persist
2. **Investigate why control branches declined** during weeks 15-22 (external factors? staffing issues?)
3. **Consider randomized assignment** for future procedure changes to eliminate selection bias
4. **Revisit the rollout decision** after additional data and analysis

---

## **Technical Notes**

Analysis used difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch). The pre-period gap between treatment and control branches (averaging 1.69 points, ranging from 0.99 to 2.59 across weeks) indicates non-comparable groups, limiting causal interpretation.
