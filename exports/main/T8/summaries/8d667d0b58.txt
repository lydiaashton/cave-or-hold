# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Prepared by:** Data Analysis Office

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The evidence is inconclusive.** Branches that adopted the new procedure showed improved service scores relative to non-adopters. However, these branches were already outperforming the control group by an increasing margin *before* the switch, which limits our ability to attribute the improvement solely to the new procedure.

**Recommendation:** Do not proceed with cooperative-wide rollout based on current evidence. Obtain stronger causal evidence before committing resources.

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

**❌ Cannot recommend full rollout at this time.**

The statistical analysis does not support a confident conclusion that the new procedure caused the observed improvement. The branches selected for the new procedure were already on a stronger performance trajectory before implementation. Approving a cooperative-wide rollout based on this evidence risks:

- Implementing a procedure that may not deliver the expected benefits
- Incurring transition costs without guaranteed return
- Disrupting operations at 284 branches without clear justification

**Path forward:** Either (a) run a randomized controlled trial with remaining branches, or (b) extend monitoring of current adopters and re-evaluate with more data.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### What the Data Shows

| Period | Average Gap (Treated − Control) |
|--------|--------------------------------|
| Pre-switch (weeks 6-14) | +0.94 points |
| Post-switch (weeks 15-22) | +3.11 points |
| **Observed increase** | **+2.17 points** |

### Critical Limitation: Parallel Trends Violated

The gap between groups was **already widening before the switch**:

| Week | Gap (Treated − Control) |
|------|------------------------|
| 6 | +0.19 |
| 9 | +0.89 |
| 12 | +1.58 |
| 14 (last pre) | +2.13 |
| 15 (first post) | +2.56 |
| 22 (last post) | +3.78 |

The pre-treatment gap trend increased by 0.30 points per week (p < 0.001). This means the standard difference-in-differences estimate of 2.17 points is likely **biased upward** and does not represent the true causal effect.

## **RECOMMENDED NEXT STEPS**

| Priority | Action | Timeline |
|----------|--------|----------|
| 1 | **Pause full rollout decision** | Immediate |
| 2 | Design randomized controlled trial for remaining 284 branches | 4-6 weeks |
| 3 | Continue monitoring the 425 current adopters | Ongoing |
| 4 | Investigate characteristics that distinguished early adopters | 4-8 weeks |
| 5 | Re-evaluate with stronger evidence | 8-12 weeks |

---

## **CONCLUSION**

The new procedure is *associated* with higher service scores, but we cannot confidently claim it *caused* the improvement. The branches that adopted the procedure were already improving faster than their peers before implementation. 

**The board should not approve cooperative-wide rollout based on this analysis alone.** Stronger evidence is needed before committing resources across 284 additional branches.
