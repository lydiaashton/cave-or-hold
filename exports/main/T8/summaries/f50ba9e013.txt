# MEMORANDUM

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## Final Verdict

**DO NOT PROCEED WITH SYSTEM-WIDE ROLLOUT AT THIS TIME.**

The evidence is **inconclusive**. While the observed effect is positive (+2.3 points), the data do not provide sufficient statistical confidence to confirm the procedure causes real improvement. Approving a full rollout now would be a decision based on **hope rather than evidence**.

**Recommended path:** Extend the pilot for 8–12 weeks, then re-evaluate.

---

## Bottom Line

| Metric | Value | Interpretation |
|--------|-------|----------------|
| Observed improvement | +2.3 points | Positive direction ✓ |
| 95% Confidence Interval | -0.5 to +5.1 points | **Includes zero** ⚠️ |
| P-value | 0.12 | **Not statistically significant** ⚠️ |
| Conclusion | **Inconclusive** | Cannot confirm the procedure works |

---

## Key Findings

### 1. What the Data Show

- **Switched branches** improved by 4.2 points on average
- **Control branches** improved by 2.0 points on average (likely due to seasonal factors or other trends)
- **Net effect attributed to the procedure:** +2.3 points
- **Statistical certainty:** Low—the same data are compatible with effects ranging from slightly harmful (-0.5) to moderately beneficial (+5.1)

### 2. Why This Matters

A system-wide rollout affects all 34+ branches, staff training budgets, and patron experience. Making this decision on inconclusive evidence carries risk:

| Risk | Consequence |
|------|-------------|
| If procedure doesn't work | Wasted implementation costs, staff frustration |
| If procedure has negative effect | Service quality declines across the cooperative |
| If procedure works | Delayed benefits from slower rollout |

The **third risk is acceptable** compared to the first two.

### 3. Branch Variation Suggests Implementation Matters

Switched branches varied widely in outcomes (-2.5 to +8.9 points). This suggests:
- The procedure **may work well in some contexts** and poorly in others
- **Training quality, staffing, or branch characteristics** could be key moderators
- A blanket rollout without understanding these factors could produce uneven results

---

## Recommendations

### Immediate Decision (This Meeting)

□ **Decline full rollout**  
□ **Authorize pilot extension** (8–12 weeks)  
□ **Request follow-up analysis** with expanded data

### Pilot Extension Plan

| Action | Timeline | Responsible Party |
|--------|----------|-------------------|
| Continue monitoring current 20 switched branches | Ongoing | Operations |
| Add 5–10 branches to switched group | Weeks 1–4 | Operations |
| Document implementation variations | Weeks 1–12 | Branch Managers |
| Re-analyze data | Week 13 | Data Team |
| Final rollout decision | Week 14 | Board |

### Success Criteria for Re-evaluation

| Criterion | Threshold |
|-----------|-----------|
| Statistical significance | p < 0.05 |
| Lower bound of 95% CI | Above 0 points |
| Consistent positive effects | ≥70% of switched branches show improvement |

If these criteria are met, proceed with confidence. If not, reconsider the procedure or investigate modifying factors.

---

## What Changed From Our Expectations

We expected improvement, and the **direction is correct**. However:
- The **magnitude is smaller** than would be needed for clear statistical confirmation
- The **sample size is insufficient** to detect modest effects with confidence
- **Patience now prevents costly mistakes later**
