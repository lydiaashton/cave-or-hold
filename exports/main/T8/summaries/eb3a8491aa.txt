# **FINAL BOARD MEMORANDUM**

---

## **New Shelving Procedure Shows Promise, But Evidence Has Limitations**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**Proceed with caution.** The data suggests the new procedure may improve service scores, but we cannot confidently isolate its effect from pre-existing differences between branches. The most defensible estimate is **+0.8 to +1.5 points** (not the +2.0 initially calculated). We recommend a **phased expansion with continued evaluation** rather than immediate full rollout.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **1. What the Data Shows**

| Metric | Estimate | Confidence |
|--------|----------|------------|
| Standard DiD estimate | +2.05 points | ⚠️ Likely overstated |
| Conservative estimate (weeks 10-14 baseline) | +1.46 points | Moderate |
| Most conservative (week 14 baseline only) | +0.79 points | Moderate |
| Simple treatment before/after | +1.30 points | Low (no control) |

### **2. Critical Limitation: Pre-Existing Trends**

**The parallel trends assumption is violated.** Treatment branches were already improving relative to control branches BEFORE the switch:

| Week | Treatment-Control Gap |
|------|----------------------|
| Week 6 | -0.36 points |
| Week 10 | +1.19 points |
| Week 14 | +2.11 points |

This 2.47-point drift over 8 weeks **before** the procedure changed suggests:
- Treatment branches may have been systematically different (more motivated, better managed, etc.)
- The standard DiD estimate (+2.05) likely **overstates** the true treatment effect
- We cannot confidently claim the procedure *caused* the improvement

### **3. What We Can Say With Confidence**

- Treatment branches **did** achieve higher service scores after switching (52.5 vs. 49.6 for control)
- The gap between treatment and control **did widen** after the switch
- Even the most conservative estimate (+0.79 points) represents a **positive direction**
- No evidence of harm from the new procedure

### **4. What We Cannot Say With Confidence**

- The exact magnitude of the procedure's effect
- Whether the improvement would replicate at all branches
- Whether branches that didn't volunteer to switch would see similar gains

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

### **Recommendation: Phased Expansion with Evaluation**

**Vote: Proceed, but conditionally.**

| Consideration | Assessment |
|---------------|------------|
| **Evidence quality** | ⚠️ Moderate (pre-trend violation limits causal claims) |
| **Direction of effect** | ✓ Positive (all estimates show improvement) |
| **Risk of harm** | ✓ Low (no negative effects observed) |
| **Resource requirements** | ✓ Low (process change, not resource-intensive) |
| **Opportunity cost** | ✓ Low (delaying full rollout has minimal downside) |

**Rationale:** The evidence is imperfect but points in a favorable direction. A phased approach allows the cooperative to capture potential benefits while building stronger evidence and managing risk. If the effect is real, branches benefit incrementally. If the effect was driven by pre-existing branch characteristics, the cooperative avoids costly system-wide implementation of an ineffective change.

**Success threshold for full rollout:** Phased expansion should demonstrate **minimum +0.5 point improvement** in service scores across new adopters to justify system-wide implementation.

---

## **METHODOLOGY NOTE**

This analysis uses difference-in-differences regression, which requires the "parallel trends" assumption (treatment and control groups would have followed similar trajectories absent the intervention). **This assumption is violated in our data**, meaning causal claims should be treated as suggestive rather than definitive. The conservative estimates (+0.79 to +1.46 points) are more reliable than the standard DiD estimate (+2.05 points).
