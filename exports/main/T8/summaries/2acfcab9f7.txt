# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure shows a positive effect on service scores, but we cannot rule out that the true effect is zero.** The estimated improvement is +1.6 points (about 3% of the average score), with a 95% confidence interval ranging from -0.5 to +3.7 points. There is no evidence of harm.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** participated (20 adopted the new procedure, 14 maintained the old)
- Data collected weekly from **Week 6 through Week 22**
- The procedure switch occurred at **Week 15**
- Analysis uses a rigorous **difference-in-differences** approach with branch and week fixed effects

### **Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Treatment (switched) | 47.95 | 51.83 | +3.88 |
| Control (not switched) | 49.38 | 51.65 | +2.27 |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | | | **+1.62** |

### **Statistical Significance**
- **Estimated treatment effect:** +1.62 points
- **95% confidence interval:** -0.50 to +3.74 points
- **P-value:** 0.135 (not statistically significant at conventional levels)

### **Validity Check**
Our event study analysis confirms **no evidence of differential pre-trends** between treatment and control branches before the switch (p = 0.54), supporting the validity of our causal inference approach.

---

## **Interpretation**

1. **Both groups improved after Week 15.** Service scores increased across all branches, suggesting broader factors (seasonal patterns, system-wide initiatives) influenced scores during this period.

2. **The treatment group improved slightly more,** but the additional improvement (~1.6 points, or about 3% of the average score of 50) is within the range of normal variation.

3. **Statistical uncertainty is substantial.** The true effect could plausibly be slightly negative, zero, or modestly positive (up to +3.7 points).

4. **There is no evidence of harm.** Even the lower bound of the confidence interval suggests minimal negative impact.

---

## **Final Verdict**

### **Recommendation: CONDITIONAL ROLLOUT WITH CONTINUED EVALUATION**

**We recommend proceeding with a phased rollout, contingent on the following:**

| Consideration | Assessment |
|---------------|------------|
| **Direction of effect** | ✅ Positive (aligns with expectations) |
| **Risk of harm** | ✅ Low (no evidence of negative impact) |
| **Statistical certainty** | ⚠️ Limited (cannot rule out zero effect) |
| **Practical significance** | ⚠️ Modest (~3% improvement if real) |

**Rationale:**
- The point estimate is positive and meaningful
- There is no evidence the procedure causes harm
- Waiting for definitive statistical significance may delay benefits indefinitely
- A phased approach allows continued learning while capturing potential gains

**Conditions for rollout:**
1. **Implement in stages** (e.g., 5-10 branches per quarter) rather than all at once
2. **Maintain 3-5 control branches** for ongoing comparison
3. **Continue data collection** for at least 6 months post-rollout
4. **Set a review date** (Q3 2027) to reassess with expanded data
5. **Be prepared to pause or reverse** if negative effects emerge

## **Conclusion**

The evidence is **suggestive but not definitive**. Given the positive direction of effects, absence of harm, and operational benefits of standardizing procedures across branches, a **cautious phased rollout is warranted**. However, the board should commit to continued evaluation and remain prepared to adjust course based on emerging evidence.
