# **Library Cooperative Branch Procedure Change: Impact Analysis**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure shows a positive effect on service scores, but the evidence is not statistically conclusive.** Branches that adopted the new procedure gained an average of **2.0 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. However, with our current data, we cannot rule out that this improvement could be due to chance.

**Recommendation: Proceed with a phased rollout while continuing to monitor results.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### The Evidence

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| **Switched Branches (20)** | 48.2 | 50.3 | +2.1 |
| **Non-Switched Branches (14)** | 51.6 | 51.6 | 0.0 |

**Net Effect: +2.0 points** (p = 0.125, not statistically significant at conventional levels)

### What This Means

1. **The direction is positive.** All estimates point to improvement—there's no evidence the new procedure harms service scores.

2. **The uncertainty is substantial.** The 95% confidence interval ranges from -0.6 to +4.7 points. The true effect could be zero, modestly negative, or as high as +4.7 points.

3. **More data would help.** With only 8 weeks of post-switch data and 34 branches, we have limited statistical power. Extending the evaluation period would narrow the confidence interval.

4. **No red flags.** Switched branches improved without any detectable negative consequences. Staffing and visit patterns remained stable.

---

## **Final Verdict**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Effect Direction** | ✅ Positive |
| **Statistical Significance** | ⚠️ Not confirmed (p = 0.125) |
| **Evidence of Harm** | ✅ None detected |
| **Implementation Risk** | ✅ Low (20 branches already using) |
| **Recommendation** | **Proceed with rollout** |

**Rationale:** While statistical significance is not achieved, the balance of evidence favors proceeding:
- The point estimate is positive (+2.0 points)
- The confidence interval is mostly positive (75% of the range is above zero)
- No branch experienced measurable harm
- Reversal is feasible if future data contradicts these findings
- 20 branches have already implemented successfully

## **Next Steps**

### Rollout Plan

| Phase | Branches | Timeline | Success Check |
|-------|----------|----------|---------------|
| 1 | Remaining 14 branches | Weeks 1–6 | No score decline >1 point |
| 2 | All branches monitoring | Months 2–6 | Maintain +1.0 point average |
| 3 | Board review | Month 6 | Confirm sustained improvement |

### Monitoring Requirements

1. **Track service scores weekly** for all branches during rollout
2. **Flag any branch** with a 2+ point decline for immediate review
3. **Report to board quarterly** on aggregate score trends
4. **Reassess statistical significance** after 6 months of additional data

### Contingency

If service scores decline by more than 1 point on average during rollout:
- Pause implementation
- Investigate causes at affected branches
- Report findings to board before proceeding

## **Questions the Board May Have**

**Q: If it's not statistically significant, why proceed?**  
A: The effect is positive (+2.0 points), there's no evidence of harm, and 20 branches are already using the procedure successfully. The risk of a negative outcome appears low based on existing data.

**Q: What's the worst-case scenario?**  
A: The true effect is zero or slightly negative. In that case, we would have invested implementation effort without gaining service improvements. Reversal to the old procedure remains an option.

**Q: Why not wait for more data?**  
A: We could extend the evaluation, but 20 branches are already operating under the new procedure. A phased rollout allows us to capture potential benefits while maintaining the option to adjust based on incoming data.

**Q: How will we know if it's working?**  
A: We will track aggregate service scores across all branches. If the average improvement persists or grows over 6 months, confidence in the procedure's effectiveness will increase.

---

## **Conclusion**

The data shows a **positive but inconclusive** effect of the new procedure on service scores. The +2.0 point improvement is encouraging and consistent across all analyses, but our confidence interval includes zero. Given the absence of negative effects and successful implementation at 20 branches, we recommend **proceeding with a phased rollout** while maintaining rigorous monitoring. This approach balances the potential for service improvement against the uncertainty in our current evidence.
