# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Prepared by:** Data Analysis Team

---

## **Executive Summary**

Branches that adopted the new stock rotation procedure showed higher service scores after implementation. However, our analysis reveals these branches were already outperforming before the change. **We cannot conclude the procedure caused the improvement.** We recommend against system-wide rollout until stronger evidence is obtained.

---

## **Bottom Line**

| Finding | Result |
|---------|--------|
| Raw difference-in-differences | +1.86 points (p < 0.001) |
| Adjusting for pre-existing trends | -0.53 points (p = 0.138) |
| Parallel trends assumption | **Violated** |
| **Verdict** | **Effect not established** |

---

## **Final Verdict**

### **DO NOT PROCEED WITH SYSTEM-WIDE ROLLOUT**

The evidence does not support the conclusion that the new procedure improves service scores. The observed association reflects **which branches chose to adopt the procedure**, not the procedure's effect.

**Confidence Level:** LOW

**Rationale:**
1. Branches that switched were already improving faster before implementation
2. No clear discontinuity in outcomes at the point of implementation
3. When accounting for pre-existing trends, the estimated effect is negligible and statistically insignificant
4. Non-random adoption creates selection bias that cannot be fully corrected with observational data

---

## **Analysis Overview**

### **Study Design**

We analyzed weekly service scores from **709 branches** over **17 weeks** (weeks 6-22). The new procedure was implemented at **425 branches** starting in week 15, while **284 branches** continued with the existing procedure.

### **The Critical Problem: Violated Parallel Trends**

Difference-in-differences analysis requires that treatment and control groups would have followed similar trends absent the intervention. **This assumption is not met:**

| Period | Treatment Group Trend | Control Group Trend | Gap Change |
|--------|----------------------|---------------------|------------|
| Pre-switch (weeks 6-14) | -0.57 pts/week | -0.88 pts/week | +0.31 pts/week |
| Post-switch (weeks 15-22) | -0.91 pts/week | -1.14 pts/week | +0.23 pts/week |

The gap was widening **before** the procedure changed. The rate of widening actually **slowed** after implementation.

### **Event Study: No Clear Treatment Effect**

Week-by-week analysis shows the treatment-control gap increased steadily throughout the study period with **no sharp break at week 15**. This pattern is inconsistent with a causal effect of the procedure.

---

## **What We Can and Cannot Conclude**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did switching branches have higher scores after week 15? | **Yes** |
| Did the procedure cause this difference? | **Cannot determine** |
| Is the effect large enough to justify rollout costs? | **Unknown** |
| Should we roll out to all branches now? | **No** |

---

## **Recommendations**

### **1. Halt System-Wide Rollout**

Do not expand the procedure to remaining branches based on current evidence.

### **2. Conduct a Randomized Controlled Trial**

- Randomly assign 50-100 branches to adopt the procedure
- Compare outcomes against randomly assigned control branches
- This eliminates selection bias and provides credible causal evidence

### **3. Investigate Early Adopters**

Understand what distinguished branches that chose to switch:
- Management quality?
- Staff characteristics?
- Local market conditions?
- Prior performance trends?

### **4. If Proceeding Without RCT, Use Phased Rollout with Rigorous Monitoring**

- Implement in 3-4 waves over 6-12 months
- Pre-register success criteria
- Be prepared to stop if targets are not met

## **Conclusion**

While we hoped to recommend proceeding with the new procedure, the data does not support that conclusion. The observed improvements reflect **selection bias** — branches that chose to switch were already on better trajectories. 

**We recommend investing in a randomized trial before committing resources to system-wide implementation.** This approach will provide the evidence needed to make a confident decision.

---

*This analysis used difference-in-differences regression with branch and week fixed effects, cluster-robust standard errors, and event study methodology. The parallel trends assumption was tested and found to be violated.*
