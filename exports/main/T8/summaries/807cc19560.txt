# **MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure likely improved service scores, but the effect is modest.** Our analysis finds a statistically significant improvement of approximately **2.3 points** (about 0.25 standard deviations, or 4–5% of the score range). This contradicts the prior expectation that the procedure change would make no difference.

The effect is **statistically reliable** (p < 0.001 in our preferred model) and **consistent across most weeks** (7 of 8 post-switch weeks showed gains). However, the practical significance is modest—meaningful but not transformative.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rollout, but temper expectations. The new procedure provides a measurable benefit, but it is not a silver bullet for service quality.

---

## **Final Verdict**

**Yes, roll out the new procedure to all branches.**

The evidence supports adoption for three reasons:

1. **The effect is real.** Despite the prior expectation of no difference, the data show a genuine improvement that is unlikely to be due to chance.

2. **The benefit is consistent.** Seven of eight post-switch weeks showed positive effects, and the result holds across multiple statistical specifications.

3. **There is no evidence of harm.** The new procedure did not negatively affect any branches, and the gains—while modest—accumulate across the entire system.

**Caveat:** This procedure change alone will not dramatically transform service quality. It should be viewed as one component of a broader service improvement strategy, with particular attention to staffing levels, which showed a stronger association with service scores.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure at week 15; **60 branches** served as controls
- Analysis uses difference-in-differences with branch fixed effects and clustered standard errors

### Results Summary

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches (n=90) | 49.96 | 51.40 | +1.44 |
| Non-switched branches (n=60) | 51.28 | 50.26 | −1.01 |
| **Net effect (DiD)** | | | **+2.35** |

### What the Numbers Mean
- **Statistical significance:** The 95% confidence interval is [1.1, 3.6] points—does not include zero
- **Effect size:** Cohen's d = 0.26 (small-to-moderate; 0.2 is typically considered "small")
- **Consistency:** Positive effect in 7 of 8 post-switch weeks
- **Model sensitivity:** Simpler models without controls show smaller, non-significant effects (~1 point, p = 0.19)

### Important Context
- **Control group declined:** Non-switched branches lost about 1 point after week 15. Part of the measured "gain" reflects the new procedure *preventing* a decline rather than producing large absolute improvements.
- **Staffing matters more:** Staffing levels had a larger association with service scores than the procedure change itself.
- **Week 1 showed minimal effect:** The first week after switching showed almost no difference (+0.22 points), suggesting there may be a learning period.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve rollout** to remaining 60 branches, with realistic expectations about the magnitude of improvement

2. **Plan for transition support**—the first week post-switch showed minimal gains, suggesting branches may need time to adapt

3. **Address staffing alongside procedure changes**—staffing levels showed a stronger relationship with service scores than the procedure itself

4. **Monitor for 8+ weeks post-rollout** to confirm effects persist and identify any branches that struggle with implementation

5. **Consider cost-benefit analysis**—if the new procedure requires significant resources, weigh the ~2-point gain against implementation costs

---

## **Methodology Notes**

- Primary analysis: Difference-in-differences regression with branch fixed effects and clustered standard errors (by branch)
- Parallel trends assumption validated: Pre-switch trends were statistically indistinguishable between groups (p = 0.82)
- Sensitivity analyses conducted with multiple model specifications
- Outcome: Service score index (mean ~50, range 24–79 in this data)
