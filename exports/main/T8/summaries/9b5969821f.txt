# **BOARD SUMMARY: New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure Evaluation**

---

## **Bottom Line**

We **cannot determine** whether the new procedure improves service scores. The data shows a positive trend (+1.8 points), but with only 34 branches, we lack the statistical power to distinguish this from random variation. **Do not proceed with full rollout** without additional evidence.

---

## **Key Findings**

| What We Measured | Result |
|------------------|--------|
| Estimated effect | **+1.8 points** on service score index |
| Statistical significance | **Not significant** (p = 0.21) |
| 95% Confidence interval | **-1.1 to +4.6 points** |
| Probability effect is positive | **~90%** (one-tailed) |

### What This Means

- **The data is inconclusive**: The true effect could be slightly harmful (-1 point), zero, or moderately beneficial (+4-5 points)
- **The study was underpowered**: With only 34 branches, we could only detect fairly large effects. A 2-point improvement is plausible but unproven
- **No evidence of harm**: The new procedure did not negatively affect staffing or visits

---

## **Final Verdict**

> **DO NOT APPROVE system-wide rollout at this time.**

The evidence is insufficient to justify the cost and disruption of changing all branches. The board should either:

| Option | Action |
|--------|--------|
| **Gather more evidence** | Continue monitoring current branches for 8-12 additional weeks |
| **Expand the evaluation** | Randomly assign additional branches to switch, then re-analyze |
| **Table the initiative** | If resources are limited, maintain the current mixed approach |

---

## **Recommendation**

**Do not roll out system-wide.** Instead:

1. **Extend data collection** — 8-12 more weeks would substantially improve our ability to detect modest effects
2. **Consider a randomized rollout** — If expanding, randomly assign additional branches to switch, creating a larger evaluation sample
3. **Weigh costs against uncertain benefits** — A 2-point improvement (if real) is modest; ensure implementation costs don't exceed potential gains

---

## **Why the Uncertainty?**

With only 34 branches split between treatment and control, random week-to-week fluctuations in service scores make it difficult to isolate the procedure's effect. This is a limitation of the study design, not necessarily evidence that the procedure doesn't work.
