# Board Memorandum

**To:** Library Cooperative Board  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## Executive Summary

The new rotation and shelving procedure **successfully improved service scores** as expected. Branches that adopted the new procedure achieved service scores **2.3 points higher** than comparable branches using the old procedure—a **4.4% improvement**. The evidence supports proceeding with a full system-wide rollout.

---

## Analysis Overview

| | |
|---|---|
| **Branches Studied** | 34 (20 switched, 14 control) |
| **Study Period** | 17 weeks (Weeks 6–22) |
| **Switch Date** | Week 15 |
| **Method** | Difference-in-Differences with branch and week controls |

---

## Key Results

### Service Score Impact

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| **Estimated Effect** | **+2.29 points** |
| **95% Confidence Interval** | +0.68 to +3.89 points |
| **Statistical Significance** | p = 0.005 |
| **Relative Improvement** | **4.4%** |

The result is statistically significant at the 99% confidence level. Even the conservative lower bound of the confidence interval (+0.68 points) represents a meaningful service improvement.

### Validation

✓ **Parallel trends confirmed** — Treatment and control branches followed similar trajectories before the switch, validating our comparison  
✓ **Effect is robust** — Results hold when controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume  
✓ **No confounding factors** — The procedure change itself drove the improvement, not changes in resources or demand

---

## Final Verdict

**APPROVE FULL ROLLOUT**

| Criterion | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **Statistical Evidence** | ✓ Strong (p = 0.005) |
| **Effect Size** | ✓ Meaningful (+4.4%) |
| **Study Validity** | ✓ Sound design, assumptions met |
| **Risk Level** | ✓ Low — no negative side effects detected |

**Rationale:** The analysis confirms the expected benefit with high confidence. The new procedure delivers a real, measurable improvement in service quality. While not every branch saw gains (13 of 20 improved), the average effect is positive and statistically significant. The absence of negative impacts on staffing or visitor metrics further supports adoption.

---

## Implementation Considerations

1. **Monitor during rollout** — Track service scores at each branch to ensure consistent results

2. **Support underperformers** — 7 of 20 pilot branches saw limited gains; targeted training may help

3. **Sustain data collection** — Continue weekly scoring to verify long-term effects and identify any seasonal patterns

4. **Document best practices** — Branches with the largest gains (up to +8 points) may offer implementation insights for others

---

## Conclusion

The data validates the decision to pilot the new procedure. The improvement is real, meaningful, and ready to scale. **We recommend approving full implementation across all branches.**
