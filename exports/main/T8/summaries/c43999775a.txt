# **Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure Evaluation**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores—contrary to our expectations.**

Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure maintained service scores approximately **2 points higher** than branches that kept the old procedure. This represents a **3.9% improvement** relative to baseline performance. The effect is statistically significant (p = 0.001) and consistent across all post-implementation weeks.

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. The Effect Was Unexpected But Real

We entered this evaluation expecting no meaningful change to service scores. The data shows otherwise:

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|----------------|---------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 49.14 | 46.92 | **−2.22** |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 49.86 | 49.56 | **−0.31** |

**Difference-in-differences estimate: +1.95 points** (95% CI: 0.78 to 3.13)

The new procedure did not boost scores so much as it **protected branches from a system-wide decline** that affected all branches using the old procedure.

### 2. Results Are Robust

- **Statistically significant:** p = 0.001, even with conservative clustered standard errors
- **Consistent across time:** All 8 post-switch weeks showed positive effects (range: +1.3 to +2.6 points)
- **Holds with controls:** Effect persists when accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume
- **Holds with branch fixed effects:** Effect persists when accounting for individual branch characteristics

### 3. Analysis Is Valid

- **Parallel trends confirmed:** Treatment and control branches had statistically similar trends before the switch (p = 0.41)
- **Minimal pre-existing differences:** Treatment branches scored only 0.7 points higher before the switch (not statistically significant)
- **Large sample:** 150 branches, 17 weeks, 2,550 observations

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Criterion | Assessment | Evidence |
|-----------|------------|----------|
| **Statistical Significance** | ✅ **PASSED** | p = 0.001; 95% CI [0.78, 3.13] excludes zero |
| **Practical Significance** | ✅ **PASSED** | ~4% improvement on baseline; meaningful for service quality |
| **Consistency** | ✅ **PASSED** | Positive effect in all 8 post-switch weeks |
| **Validity** | ✅ **PASSED** | Parallel trends confirmed; no pre-existing differences |
| **Robustness** | ✅ **PASSED** | Holds across multiple model specifications |

### **Overall Verdict: ROLL OUT THE NEW PROCEDURE**

**Confidence Level: HIGH**

The evidence is strong, consistent, and statistically rigorous. Despite our initial expectation that the procedure change would have no effect, the data clearly demonstrates a meaningful improvement in service scores. The new procedure should be implemented across all branches.

## **Technical Appendix**

- **Method:** Difference-in-differences regression with branch-clustered standard errors
- **Treatment group:** 102 branches (switched at week 15)
- **Control group:** 48 branches (retained old procedure throughout)
- **Data period:** Weeks 6–22 (9 weeks pre-switch, 8 weeks post-switch)
- **Outcome measure:** Service score index (baseline average ≈ 50; higher is better)
- **Software:** Python with statsmodels
