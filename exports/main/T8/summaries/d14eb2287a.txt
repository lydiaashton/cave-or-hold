## **New Shelving Procedure: Impact on Library Service Scores**

### **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure produced an unexpected improvement in service scores.** Branches that switched saw scores increase by approximately **2 points** relative to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and unlikely to be due to chance. While the mechanism is unclear, the data supports proceeding with rollout—there is evidence of benefit and no evidence of harm.

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. Statistically Significant Improvement**
- Treatment branches improved by **3.7 points** after the switch; control branches improved by **1.5 points**
- **Net effect: +2.1 points** (95% confidence interval: 0.9 to 3.4 points)
- Probability this occurred by chance: less than 1 in 1,000 (p = 0.0008)

**2. Analysis Is Methodologically Sound**
- All 150 branches have complete data before and after the switch
- Pre-switch trends were parallel between groups—the divergence began at week 15, exactly when the switch occurred
- Staffing levels and visitor volume did not change differentially, ruling out these confounders

**3. Why This Is Surprising**
- The cooperative expected no effect on service scores
- The mechanism linking shelving/rotation procedures to service quality is not yet understood
- This suggests there may be indirect benefits (e.g., staff efficiency, patron satisfaction, item accessibility) worth investigating

**4. Practical Significance**
- A 2-point gain on a ~50-point scale represents roughly **4% improvement**
- The change required no additional budget or staffing—pure operational adjustment

---

### **Final Verdict**

**Recommendation: APPROVE system-wide rollout.**

The evidence is clear and robust. The new procedure improves service scores with no additional cost. While the result was unexpected, the data quality and statistical analysis support confidence in the finding. Delaying rollout would mean withholding a proven improvement from patrons at the 60 branches still using the old procedure.

**Risk assessment:** Low. The change is operational only, requires no capital investment, and can be adjusted if unforeseen issues arise post-rollout.

**Vote requested:** Authorization to implement the new shelving and rotation procedure across all 150 branches within the next 6 months.

---

### **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with rollout** — the evidence indicates benefit with no identified downside

2. **Investigate the mechanism** — interview staff at switched branches to understand what changed in daily operations and patron interactions

3. **Monitor post-rollout scores** — confirm the effect persists at scale and does not diminish over time

4. **Document the finding** — this unexpected positive outcome should inform future operational decisions; not all procedural changes need to target service scores directly to affect them

5. **Consider a formal evaluation** after 6–12 months to assess long-term sustainability and identify any branches where the effect is weaker or stronger

---

*Analysis based on 2,550 weekly observations from 150 branches over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22). Switch occurred at week 15. Method: difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors.*
