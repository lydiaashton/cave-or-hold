# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure  
**Date:** November 2026

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**We cannot confidently conclude the new procedure improved service scores.** While branches that switched show higher scores after the change, **the same upward trajectory was already happening before they switched**. The observed improvement appears to reflect pre-existing differences between branches rather than a causal effect of the procedure itself. **We recommend against systemwide rollout until further evidence is gathered.**

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

### **DO NOT PROCEED WITH SYSTEMWIDE ROLLOUT**

**Confidence Level:** Low  
**Evidence Quality:** Insufficient for causal inference  
**Risk of Rollout:** Moderate (resources invested in potentially ineffective change)

**Rationale:** The data show correlation, not causation. Branches that adopted the new procedure were already outperforming the control group before the switch occurred. The rate of improvement after the switch matches the rate before it. This pattern is inconsistent with a genuine treatment effect and suggests the observed gains would have happened regardless of the procedure change.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### The Critical Problem: Pre-Existing Trends

| Week | Treatment Avg | Control Avg | Gap Between Them |
|------|---------------|-------------|------------------|
| 6 | 46.9 | 46.7 | **0.2 points** |
| 9 | 42.3 | 40.7 | **1.6 points** |
| 12 | 56.8 | 54.3 | **2.5 points** |
| 14 (last before switch) | 52.8 | 50.1 | **2.6 points** |
| 15 (first after switch) | 51.8 | 49.0 | **2.7 points** |
| 19 | 58.7 | 54.5 | **4.1 points** |
| 22 (final) | 49.1 | 44.2 | **4.9 points** |

**The gap was already widening by ~0.30 points per week BEFORE the switch.** After the switch, it widened by ~0.31 points per week—**essentially the same rate.**

### What This Means

- The standard analysis method (difference-in-differences) assumes treatment and control branches would have followed parallel trends without the intervention. **This assumption is violated.**
- Branches that switched were already on a stronger upward trajectory—possibly due to more motivated management, better staffing, or other unmeasured factors.
- When we account for the pre-existing trend, **the estimated effect of the procedure itself is approximately zero** (0.02 points after adjustment).

### What We Can and Cannot Say

| Claim | Supported? |
|-------|------------|
| Switched branches have higher scores than non-switched branches | ✓ Yes |
| Switched branches improved more than non-switched branches | ✓ Yes |
| The new procedure **caused** the improvement | ✗ **No** |
| Rolling out to all branches will improve systemwide scores | ✗ **Uncertain** |

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

1. **Pause rollout decisions** pending additional evidence
2. **Extend the observation period**—collect more weeks of data to see if the gap continues widening at the same rate or accelerates
3. **Investigate selection factors**—understand why certain branches switched and whether those characteristics (not the procedure) explain the improvement
4. **Consider a randomized trial**—if feasible, randomly assign remaining branches to switch now vs. later to establish causal evidence
5. **Gather qualitative evidence**—interview branch managers about implementation experience and perceived benefits
