# **Board Memorandum (Final)**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** [Current Date]  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Do not proceed with systemwide rollout yet.** The evidence is **inconclusive**. While a simple analysis suggests improvement, a more rigorous examination reveals serious concerns: the branches selected to switch had systematically different performance patterns before the change, making it impossible to confidently attribute outcomes to the new procedure. **Recommend additional evaluation before any decision.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Conflicting Results**
| Analysis Method | Estimated Effect | Interpretation |
|-----------------|------------------|----------------|
| Simple DiD | +1.86 points (p=0.001) | Appears positive |
| Event Study Average | -1.46 points | Suggests negative/null |
| Week-by-Week (post-switch) | 2 positive, 6 negative | Mostly negative |

These methods should align if the analysis is valid. The discrepancy signals a problem.

### **2. Critical Problem: Pre-Existing Differences**
The branches that switched to the new procedure had **systematically lower service scores throughout the entire pre-period**:

| Pre-Switch Week | Treatment vs. Reference |
|-----------------|------------------------|
| Week 6 | -3.88*** |
| Week 7 | -7.38*** |
| Week 8 | -4.98*** |
| Week 9 | -1.38* |
| Week 10 | -5.88*** |
| Week 11 | -2.02*** |
| Week 12 | -4.31*** |

**6 of 7 pre-period weeks show statistically significant differences.** This violates the "parallel trends" assumption required for valid causal inference. The treatment and control groups were not comparable before the switch.

### **3. What Actually Happened Post-Switch**
- **Weeks 15-16**: Small positive effects (+0.97, +1.34)
- **Weeks 17-22**: Mostly negative effects (-2.67, -0.91, -5.53, -0.79, -2.61, -1.45)
- **3 weeks significantly negative**, 1 week marginally negative

The pattern does not show sustained improvement.

### **4. Likely Explanation**
Branches were probably **not randomly selected** to switch. Those with lower baseline performance may have been prioritized for the new procedure (or volunteered). This creates **selection bias** that confounds the analysis. The apparent "benefit" in simple DiD reflects regression toward the mean, not a true treatment effect.

---

## **Recommendations**

### **Immediate Actions**
1. **Pause systemwide rollout** pending further investigation
2. **Document selection criteria** used to determine which branches switched
3. **Investigate why** certain branches were chosen (volunteer? performance-based? geographic?)

### **Additional Analysis Needed**
4. **Propensity score matching** to create more comparable treatment/control groups
5. **Extended evaluation period** to determine if effects stabilize
6. **Qualitative interviews** with branch managers about implementation experience

### **Future Approach**
7. **Randomized rollout** for remaining branches to enable valid causal inference
8. **Pilot with willing volunteers** only, with clear expectations about uncertainty

---

## **Final Verdict**

> **The new procedure cannot be recommended for systemwide rollout based on current evidence.**
>
> The analysis suffers from fundamental selection bias that prevents causal inference. Branches that adopted the new procedure were systematically different from those that did not, and post-switch performance was mostly negative when examined week-by-week.
>
> **Decision:** **HOLD** — Do not approve rollout at this time.
>
> **Path Forward:** Conduct additional investigation into selection mechanisms, extend the evaluation period, and consider a randomized rollout design for remaining branches to generate credible evidence before making a systemwide decision.
