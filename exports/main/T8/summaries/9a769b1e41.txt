# **MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** Analysis based on 17 weeks of branch data (weeks 6-22)  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure  

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure produces a real, measurable improvement in service scores—but whether it warrants system-wide rollout depends on implementation costs.**

Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by **1.8 points** (3.5% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically robust (p < 0.001) and consistent across all analytical checks. However, the practical impact is modest relative to normal week-to-week variation in scores.

**Verdict:** The procedure works. Proceed with rollout **if** implementation costs are low to moderate. If costs are substantial, prioritize lower-performing branches where the impact is largest.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measured Impact

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches (425 branches) | 51.46 | 54.04 | +2.58 |
| Control branches (284 branches) | 51.21 | 51.96 | +0.75 |
| **Net Effect of New Procedure** | | | **+1.81** |

The treatment effect of **+1.81 points** has a 95% confidence interval of **1.26 to 2.36 points**.

### 2. Statistical Confidence

- **p-value:** < 0.001 (less than 0.01% probability this result occurred by chance)
- **Effect size:** 0.18 standard deviations (small-to-moderate practical significance)
- **Robustness:** Result holds across multiple model specifications with different controls

### 3. Context for Decision-Making

| Metric | Value | Interpretation |
|--------|-------|----------------|
| Average service score | 52.2 | Baseline |
| Treatment effect | +1.8 | 3.5% improvement |
| Week-to-week variation (std dev) | ~9.5 | Much larger than treatment effect |
| Effect as % of one std dev | 18.5% | Modest but meaningful |

### 4. Heterogeneous Effects

The procedure benefits all branches, but impact varies by starting performance:

| Branch Type | Pre-Switch Score | Post-Switch Score | Change |
|-------------|------------------|-------------------|--------|
| Lower-performing branches | 44.98 | 48.10 | **+3.12** |
| Higher-performing branches | 57.91 | 59.96 | **+2.05** |

Lower-performing branches show approximately **70% larger gains**, suggesting targeted rollout may maximize return on investment.

### 5. Validity Checks Passed

**Parallel Trends Assumption:** Before the switch (weeks 6-14), treatment and control branches tracked closely together with an average difference of only 0.25 points. This confirms the groups were comparable before the intervention.

**Visual Evidence:** The attached chart shows treatment and control branches following similar trajectories before week 15, then diverging afterward as the new procedure took effect.

### 6. Analysis Scope

- **709 branches** total (425 adopted new procedure, 284 retained old procedure)
- **12,053 branch-week observations**
- **17 weeks** of data (weeks 6-22), with switch occurring at week 15
- Controls included for staffing levels and visitor volume

---

## **Limitations**

1. **Cost data unavailable:** This analysis measures service impact only, not cost-effectiveness.
2. **Limited post-treatment window:** Only 8 weeks of post-switch data; long-term sustainability unknown.
3. **Implementation variation:** We cannot verify all switching branches implemented the procedure identically.

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Does the procedure improve service scores? | **Yes** — statistically significant, consistent effect |
| Is the improvement large? | **Modest** — 3.5% gain, smaller than typical week-to-week variation |
| Should we roll it out everywhere? | **Conditional** — proceed if costs are reasonable |
| Is there a smarter rollout strategy? | **Yes** — prioritize lower-performing branches for maximum impact |

---

## **Conclusion**

The evidence supports the new procedure as a genuine improvement to branch service. The board's decision should hinge on implementation costs:

- **Low-cost implementation:** Approve system-wide rollout
- **High-cost implementation:** Target lower-performing branches first; reassess after 6 months

Either way, the procedure should be adopted—just at a pace and scale appropriate to its cost.
