# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

---

## **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

**Date:** Analysis of Weeks 6–22  
**Data:** 12,053 weekly observations across 709 branches

---

### **BOTTOM LINE**

**We cannot confidently conclude the new procedure improved service scores.** While branches that switched show higher scores after the change, these same branches were already improving faster than others *before* they switched. After accounting for this pre-existing trend, the data does not show clear evidence that the procedure itself caused improvement.

**Recommendation:** Do not proceed with system-wide rollout yet. Instead, conduct a more rigorous evaluation with random assignment or extend the observation period.

---

### **FINAL VERDICT**

> **DO NOT ROLL OUT TO ALL BRANCHES AT THIS TIME**
>
> The evidence is **insufficient to support system-wide adoption**. The apparent improvement is explained by pre-existing differences between branches rather than the procedure itself. Rolling out now would risk:
> - Implementing a procedure with unproven benefits
> - Disrupting operations at 284 branches without clear return
> - Missing the opportunity to test the procedure more rigorously
>
> **What we need before proceeding:** A randomized controlled trial or additional data showing the effect persists after accounting for pre-trends.

---

### **KEY FINDINGS**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Raw Difference-in-Differences** | +1.94 points |
| **Pre-Existing Trend** | +0.30 points/week (before switch) |
| **Expected Gap Growth from Trend Alone** | ~+2.55 points |
| **Actual Gap Growth** | +1.94 points |
| **Adjusted Effect (accounting for pre-trend)** | **−0.61 points** |

**1. Switched Branches Were Already Improving Faster**
- In the 9 weeks before the switch, treatment branches gained ~0.30 points/week more than control branches
- This pre-trend is statistically significant (p < 0.001)
- If this trend had continued without any procedure change, we would have expected the gap to widen by ~2.55 points

**2. Actual Improvement Was Less Than Expected from Trend**
- The gap between switched and non-switched branches grew by only 1.94 points
- This is *less* than the 2.55 points expected from the pre-existing trend alone
- This suggests the procedure may have had no effect—or possibly a small negative effect

**3. Standard DiD Assumption Is Violated**
- Difference-in-differences analysis requires "parallel trends" (groups would have moved similarly without treatment)
- The pre-trend data clearly violates this assumption
- The +1.94 point estimate likely reflects selection bias, not causal effect

---

### **WHAT THIS MEANS**

Branches selected for early adoption appear to have been those already on an upward trajectory—perhaps better-managed, better-staffed, or with more improvement capacity. The procedure change coincided with continued improvement, but the data does not show the procedure *caused* that improvement.

---

### **NEXT STEPS**

| Priority | Action |
|----------|--------|
| **1** | **Pause system-wide rollout** — evidence does not support expansion |
| **2** | **Run a randomized trial** — randomly assign remaining branches to new vs. old procedure |
| **3** | **Extend observation period** — collect more weeks of data to see if effects emerge |
| **4** | **Investigate selection criteria** — understand why certain branches were chosen for early adoption |
| **5** | **Consider qualitative feedback** — survey branch staff about whether the procedure helps their work |

---

### **WHY THE INITIAL ANALYSIS WAS MISLEADING**

Standard before/after comparisons can be deceptive when:
- Treatment groups are not randomly selected
- Groups have different underlying trajectories
- The outcome measure has secular trends

Our initial analysis did not adequately account for the fact that switched branches were already outperforming and improving faster before the procedure change.
