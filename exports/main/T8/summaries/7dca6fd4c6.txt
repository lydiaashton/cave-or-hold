# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure **likely improves service scores by about 2 points (4%)**, but the evidence is not statistically definitive (p = 0.12). The effect is in the expected direction, and the confidence interval suggests the true effect is probably between 0 and 5 points.

---

## **Final Verdict**

**Proceed with system-wide rollout, with monitoring.**

**Rationale:**
1. **The effect is in the expected direction** — branches that switched improved relative to those that didn't
2. **The 90% confidence interval barely includes zero** (-0.1 to +4.3), suggesting the effect is likely positive
3. **A 4% service improvement is operationally meaningful** for library patrons
4. **The procedure has already been tested at 20 branches** without reported problems
5. **Waiting for statistical certainty would delay benefits** — the cost of additional pilot time may exceed the value of reduced uncertainty

**Caveat:** If implementation costs are substantial, the board should conduct a formal cost-benefit analysis before committing.

---

## **Key Findings**

| Metric | Value | Interpretation |
|--------|-------|----------------|
| Estimated effect | **+2.1 points** | Meaningful improvement if real |
| Statistical significance | **p = 0.12** | Suggestive, not definitive |
| 90% Confidence interval | **-0.1 to +4.3 points** | Effect is likely positive |
| 95% Confidence interval | **-0.6 to +4.8 points** | Cannot rule out zero effect |

### **What the Data Shows**

- **34 branches** total: 20 adopted the new procedure, 14 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22), with the switch implemented at week 15
- Treatment branches had higher baseline scores (53.8 vs. 46.5); analysis accounts for this
- Pre-switch trends were parallel between groups, supporting the validity of the comparison
- Staffing and visit patterns did not differ systematically between groups

## **Next Steps**

### **For Rollout**
1. **Implement at remaining 14 branches** over the next 4–8 weeks
2. **Continue weekly service score tracking** to confirm the effect persists at scale
3. **Document implementation procedures** to ensure fidelity across all branches

### **For Monitoring**
1. **Review service scores after 8 weeks** of full implementation
2. **Track secondary outcomes**: staff time, patron satisfaction, inventory accuracy
3. **Establish a threshold for reversal** if scores decline post-rollout

### **If Results Disappoint**
- Revisit procedure design and implementation quality
- Consider reverting branches that show negative effects
- Use the data to refine the procedure before re-testing
