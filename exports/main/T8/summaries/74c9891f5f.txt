## **Bottom Line**

The data shows a **positive but statistically inconclusive** effect. Switched branches maintained service scores while non-switched branches declined, yielding an estimated **+2.3 point advantage**. However, the result is not statistically significant at conventional levels (p=0.09), meaning we cannot rule out that this difference occurred by chance. **We recommend a cautious, monitored rollout** rather than a full immediate deployment.

## **Final Verdict**

**PROCEED WITH PHASED ROLLOUT — DO NOT DELAY, BUT MONITOR CLOSELY**

**Why proceed?**
- The effect is **positive in direction** and **meaningful in magnitude** (~4% improvement)
- Switched branches were **protected from decline** while control branches dropped nearly 3 points
- There is **no evidence of harm** to any branch type
- The **opportunity cost of waiting** is real — every week of delay exposes branches to potential score erosion

**Why phased?**
- Statistical uncertainty remains (p=0.09, not p<0.05)
- We need **more data** to confirm the effect with greater confidence
- A phased approach allows us to **detect problems early** and adjust if needed

**Bottom line verdict**: The procedure **likely helps** but is **not proven**. The balance of evidence favors action over inaction, provided we commit to rigorous follow-up evaluation.
