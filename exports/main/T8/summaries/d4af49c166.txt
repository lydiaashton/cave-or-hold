# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

**To:** Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Shelving and Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure is associated with improved service scores, with strong statistical evidence of a positive effect.** Branches that switched saw an average increase of **2.5 points** (approximately **5% improvement**), while branches keeping the old procedure showed no change. The effect is statistically significant (p < 0.001) and consistent across all 8 weeks of post-switch data.

**However, we have only 8 weeks of post-switch data, and 30% of treatment branches did not improve.** We recommend proceeding with rollout **with continued monitoring** to confirm the effect persists and to identify branches that may need additional support.

---

## **Final Verdict**

> **Proceed with system-wide rollout.**
>
> The statistical evidence is strong enough to justify expanding the new procedure to all branches. The improvement is real, meaningful, and observed consistently across all post-switch weeks. However, this should be done with a formal monitoring plan in place, with a commitment to evaluate results after 6 months and provide targeted support to branches that do not show improvement within the first 8-12 weeks.
>
> **The risk of waiting for more data exceeds the risk of proceeding with monitoring.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Statistically Significant Improvement

| Group | Before | After | Change |
|-------|--------|-------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 50.13 | 50.14 | +0.01 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 50.81 | 53.39 | +2.58 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+2.57** |

- **Treatment effect:** +2.50 points (95% CI: 1.24 to 3.90)
- **P-value:** < 0.001
- **Effect observed in every post-switch week** (weeks 15-22)

### 2. Important Caveats

| Consideration | Finding | Implication |
|--------------|---------|-------------|
| **Time horizon** | Only 8 weeks post-switch | Effect may not persist; needs longer-term monitoring |
| **Branch variation** | 30% of treatment branches declined | Not all branches benefit; some may need support |
| **Pre-existing differences** | Treatment branches started 0.67 points higher | DiD method accounts for this, but worth noting |
| **Mechanism unknown** | We don't know why it works | May help identify which branches benefit most |

### 3. Validation Checks Passed

✓ Parallel pre-trends confirmed (p = 0.40)  
✓ Effect robust to controlling for staffing and visits  
✓ Not driven by outliers (trimmed analysis: +2.52 points)  
✓ Consistent across all 8 post-switch weeks
