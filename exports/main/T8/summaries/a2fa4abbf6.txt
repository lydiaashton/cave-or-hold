# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

---

## **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

**Date:** November 2025  
**Prepared by:** Data Analysis Team

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**We cannot confidently conclude that the new procedure improved service scores.** While branches using the new procedure showed higher scores after implementation, branches selected for the pilot were already improving faster than other branches *before* the switch. This pre-existing difference accounts for nearly all of the observed improvement.

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

### **❌ DO NOT PROCEED WITH FULL ROLLOUT AT THIS TIME**

**Rationale:** The evidence does not establish that the new procedure *caused* the improvement in service scores. Rolling out a system-wide change without clear evidence of benefit risks:
- Wasting resources on an ineffective intervention
- Disrupting operations at branches that may not benefit
- Missing the opportunity to identify what actually drives service score improvements

**Path Forward:** Extend the pilot with randomized assignment to establish causal evidence before making a cooperative-wide commitment.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **1. What the Data Shows**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Raw difference-in-differences estimate | +2.0 points |
| **After accounting for pre-trends** | **~0.1 points (not statistically significant)** |
| Event study average post-switch effect | +0.7 points |
| Event study 95% confidence interval | **-0.3 to +1.8 points** |

### **2. The Critical Problem: Pre-Existing Trends**

Branches selected for the new procedure were already on a stronger upward trajectory before the switch:

| Group | Pre-Switch Trend |
|-------|------------------|
| Control branches (old procedure) | +0.81 points/week |
| Treatment branches (new procedure) | +1.05 points/week |
| **Difference** | **+0.24 points/week** |

**Implication:** Over the 8-week post-switch period, this pre-existing trend alone would produce approximately **1.9 points** of improvement—nearly all of the observed 2.0 point effect.

### **3. Event Study Results**

When comparing each week to the period just before the switch (controlling for branch characteristics):

- Only **3 of 8 post-switch weeks** show statistically significant effects
- The average post-switch effect (**0.7 points**) has a confidence interval that **includes zero**
- This suggests the true effect could be positive, negative, or negligible

### **4. What We Can Conclude**

| Conclusion | Confidence |
|------------|------------|
| Branches using the new procedure have higher service scores | **High** |
| The new procedure *caused* the improvement | **Low** |
| The effect is large enough to justify rollout costs | **Uncertain** |

---

*Technical note: The difference-in-differences identification strategy relies on the "parallel trends" assumption—that treatment and control groups would have followed similar trajectories absent the intervention. This assumption is violated in our data, biasing the standard DiD estimate upward. The event study approach provides more reliable but less precise estimates.*
