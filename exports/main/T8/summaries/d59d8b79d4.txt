# **BOARD MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** January 2025  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**Do not approve system-wide rollout based on current evidence.** While branches using the new procedure show higher service scores, the analysis cannot confidently attribute this improvement to the procedure itself. Branches that adopted the new procedure were already improving faster than other branches **before** the switch occurred.

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

> **INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE FOR SYSTEM-WIDE ROLLOUT**
>
> The data show a correlation between the new procedure and higher service scores, but **causation has not been established**. The fundamental assumption required for causal inference (parallel pre-trends) is violated. Approving full rollout now risks:
>
> - Implementing a procedure that may not deliver expected benefits
> - Wasting staff time and resources on ineffective changes
> - Missing the opportunity to identify what actually drives service improvements
>
> **Vote recommendation: DEFER decision pending stronger evidence**

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### What the Data Shows

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Standard DiD estimate | +1.86 points (statistically significant) |
| **Pre-trend violation** | **Highly significant (p < 0.001)** |
| Treatment group trajectory before switch | Improving 0.32 points/week faster than control |
| Gap at week 6 (treatment − control) | −0.63 points |
| Gap at week 14 (treatment − control) | +2.51 points |

### The Critical Problem

**The treatment and control groups were on different trajectories BEFORE the intervention.** 

- At week 6, treatment branches scored **0.6 points lower** than control branches
- By week 14 (one week before switch), treatment branches scored **2.5 points higher**
- This 3+ point swing occurred **before the new procedure was implemented**

If the pre-switch trend had continued, we would expect a gap of **4.4 points** by week 22. The actual gap was **2.9 points**—suggesting the treatment group's advantage actually grew more *slowly* after the switch.

### What This Means

The standard analysis attributes +1.86 points to the new procedure. However, because branches self-selected into adoption, this likely reflects:
- **Selection bias:** More motivated or better-managed branches may have opted in
- **Pre-existing momentum:** Treatment branches were already improving
- **Unmeasured factors:** Other differences between adopters and non-adopters

**The true causal effect could be zero, positive, or even negative.** The current data cannot distinguish among these possibilities.

---

## **RECOMMENDATION**

**Pause rollout pending stronger evidence.** Three options:

| Option | Description | Timeline |
|--------|-------------|----------|
| **A. Randomized rollout** | Randomly assign remaining 284 branches to treatment/control | 2-4 weeks to design |
| **B. Matched comparison** | Use statistical matching to create more comparable groups | 2-3 weeks analysis |
| **C. Staggered rollout with evaluation** | Roll out in phases, using later phases as controls for earlier ones | Immediate start, 12+ weeks evaluation |

**Recommended: Option C** with rigorous monitoring. This allows branches to benefit while generating credible evidence.
