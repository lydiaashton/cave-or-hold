# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure shows a **positive association** with service scores (+1.9 points), but the evidence is **not statistically conclusive** (p = 0.10). The true effect could reasonably range from slightly negative to moderately positive. Given the uncertainty and the costs of system-wide implementation, we recommend **continuing the pilot and gathering more data** before making a final rollout decision.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure; **14 branches** kept the old procedure
- The switch occurred at **week 15**
- Service scores average around 50 (higher is better)

### **Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 51.1 | 52.8 | **+1.7** |
| Control branches | 52.6 | 52.4 | **-0.2** |
| **Difference (Treatment Effect)** | | | **+1.9 points** |

### **Statistical Assessment**
- **Effect size:** +1.9 points (approximately 4% improvement relative to the ~50-point average)
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.10 (not significant at the conventional 0.05 threshold)
- **95% confidence interval:** -0.5 to +4.3 points
- **Interpretation:** The true effect could range from slightly negative to moderately positive

### **Supporting Evidence**
- **16 of 20 switched branches** (80%) showed individual improvement after the change
- **Pre-trends were parallel:** Both groups showed similar patterns before the switch, supporting the validity of the comparison
- **Effect persists with controls:** Accounting for staffing and visitor volume did not change the estimated effect

### **Limitations**
- **Limited statistical power:** With only 34 branches, the study could only reliably detect effects larger than about 3.3 points. The observed 1.9-point improvement may be real but too small to confirm with this sample size.
- **Short observation window:** Only 8 weeks of post-switch data limits our ability to assess whether effects grow or fade over time.

---

## **Final Verdict**

**DO NOT ROLL OUT SYSTEM-WIDE AT THIS TIME**

**Rationale:**
- The evidence is **promising but inconclusive**. While the direction of the effect is positive and most switched branches improved, we cannot confidently rule out chance as an explanation.
- The **cost of a wrong decision is asymmetric**. Rolling out a procedure that doesn't work wastes resources and disrupts operations across all branches. Waiting for more data only delays potential benefits.
- **More data is attainable.** Extending the pilot by 3–6 months would substantially improve statistical confidence at minimal cost.

**Conditions for Reconsideration:**
- Continue the pilot and re-evaluate after collecting 3–6 additional months of data
- If the effect remains positive and becomes statistically significant (p < 0.05), proceed with system-wide rollout
- If the effect diminishes or becomes negative, discontinue the new procedure

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Continue the pilot:** Maintain the new procedure at the 20 switched branches to gather additional weeks of data.

2. **Expand the evaluation:** Consider switching a few more branches to increase statistical power, or extend the observation period by several months.

3. **Monitor for sustained effects:** Track whether the improvement holds, grows, or diminishes over time.

4. **Assess operational factors:** Investigate whether the new procedure affects staff workload, costs, or other operational metrics beyond service scores.

5. **Decision timeline:** Revisit the rollout decision after 3–6 more months of data, when we will have greater confidence in the true effect size.

---

## **Recommendation**

**Hold on system-wide rollout.** The evidence points in a positive direction, but it is not strong enough to justify the cost and disruption of changing procedures at all branches. Continue monitoring and gather more data before making a final decision.
