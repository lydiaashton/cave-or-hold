# **BOARD MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

> **DO NOT PROCEED WITH SYSTEM-WIDE ROLLOUT**
>
> The evidence does not demonstrate that the new procedure causes improved service scores. The apparent benefit is explained by pre-existing differences between branches that chose to switch and those that did not. Approving a full rollout based on this analysis would risk investing resources in a change that may provide no benefit.

---

## **EXECUTIVE SUMMARY**

Initial analysis suggested the new procedure improved service scores by 2 points. However, deeper examination revealed that **branches selected for the pilot were already improving before the switch**. After accounting for this pre-existing trend, **there is no detectable benefit from the new procedure itself**.

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

| Question | Finding |
|----------|---------|
| Did switched branches show higher scores? | Yes (+2.05 points vs. control) |
| Was this caused by the new procedure? | **No—pre-existing trend explains it** |
| Can we justify a full rollout? | **No—insufficient evidence of causality** |
| Recommended action | **Pause and redesign evaluation** |

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **1. Pre-Trend Violates Analysis Assumptions**

The difference-in-differences method requires treatment and control groups to follow similar trends before the intervention. This was **not the case**:

| Period | Treatment-Control Gap |
|--------|----------------------|
| Week 6 | -0.36 points |
| Week 10 | +1.19 points |
| Week 14 | +2.11 points |

**The gap widened by 0.28 points per week (p < 0.001) before any switch occurred.**

### **2. Post-Switch Results Match Pre-Trend Predictions**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Predicted gap (if pre-trend continued) | 3.27 points |
| Actual observed gap | 2.90 points |
| **Attributable to new procedure** | **-0.37 points** |

The post-switch performance is exactly what we would have predicted from the pre-switch trend alone.

### **3. Likely Explanation: Selection Bias**

Branches that volunteered (or were selected) to switch may have:
- More motivated leadership
- Better underlying management practices
- Greater capacity for improvement independent of the procedure

This created an **upward trend unrelated to the procedure itself**.

---

## **RECOMMENDATIONS**

### **Immediate Action**
- **Do not approve system-wide rollout** at this time
- Continue current operations while evaluation is redesigned

### **Next Steps for Proper Evaluation**

| Option | Description | Timeline |
|--------|-------------|----------|
| **Randomized trial** | Randomly assign branches to switch; eliminates selection bias | 8-12 weeks |
| **Staggered rollout analysis** | Use later-switching branches as controls for earlier ones | Ongoing |
| **Matched comparison** | Pair each switching branch with a similar non-switching branch | 4-6 weeks |

### **If Board Chooses to Proceed Anyway**

Should the board decide to proceed despite the weak evidence:
- Set minimum success threshold: **+1.0 point improvement per branch**
- Monitor each branch for **12 weeks post-switch**
- Commit to **reverting branches** that fail to meet threshold
- Budget for **ongoing evaluation** to confirm results

---

## **CONCLUSION**

We understand the board's expectation that the new procedure should help. Our analysis was designed to test that expectation rigorously. **The test did not confirm the hypothesis.**

Proceeding with a full rollout would mean investing cooperative-wide resources based on an apparent effect that may not exist. We recommend pausing, redesigning the evaluation with proper controls, and making a rollout decision based on credible causal evidence.

| Scenario | If We Roll Out | If We Pause |
|----------|---------------|-------------|

| Procedure has no effect | Wasted resources, disruption | Resources preserved |

**The cost of being wrong about rollout far exceeds the cost of additional evaluation.**
