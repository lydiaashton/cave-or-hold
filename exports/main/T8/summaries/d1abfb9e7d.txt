# **Board Memorandum**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and rotation procedure is associated with a **2.4-point improvement in service scores** compared to branches that retained the old procedure. The evidence is **positive but not statistically definitive** (p ≈ 0.08–0.10). We recommend **proceeding with rollout** while maintaining active monitoring.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Measured Impact on Service Scores**

| Group | Before Switch (Weeks 6–14) | After Switch (Weeks 15–22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------------|---------------------------|--------|
| Switched branches (n=20) | 47.8 | 49.5 | **+1.7** |
| Non-switched branches (n=14) | 48.3 | 47.6 | **–0.7** |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+2.4 points** |

### **2. Statistical Confidence**

- **Effect size:** 2.4 points (approximately ¼ of a standard deviation)
- **95% confidence interval:** –0.5 to +5.3 points
- **Statistical significance:** Marginal (p ≈ 0.08–0.10)
- **Interpretation:** The data favor a positive effect, but we cannot rule out no effect with high confidence

### **3. Consistency Across Branches**

- **75% of switched branches** (15 of 20) improved after the change
- **50% of non-switched branches** (7 of 14) improved during the same period
- The benefit appeared in both early weeks (15–17) and later weeks (18–22) after the switch

### **4. Important Context**

- Service scores were **declining across all branches** over time (approximately 1 point per week)
- Switched branches **reversed this decline**; non-switched branches continued to deteriorate
- The effect holds even after accounting for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume
- Both groups had **similar baseline scores** before the switch, supporting a valid comparison

---

## **Final Verdict**

**Proceed with cooperative-wide rollout.**

### **Rationale**

| Consideration | Assessment |
|--------------|------------|
| **Direction of effect** | ✓ Positive (+2.4 points) |
| **Consistency** | ✓ 75% of branches improved |
| **Durability** | ✓ Effect persisted across 8 weeks |
| **Harms detected** | ✓ None |
| **Statistical certainty** | ⚠ Not definitive, but leans positive |
| **Cost of reversal** | Low (procedure can be adjusted if needed) |

### **Why This Verdict**

1. **The preponderance of evidence favors adoption.** While statistical confidence is not at the conventional 95% threshold, the effect is in the expected direction, meaningful in magnitude, and consistent across branches and time periods.

2. **The risk profile is asymmetric.** The cost of adopting an ineffective procedure is modest and reversible. The cost of delaying a beneficial procedure—continued score declines at 14 branches—is ongoing and measurable.

3. **Waiting for perfect evidence is not optimal.** Extending the trial would narrow confidence intervals, but branches not yet switched would continue experiencing declining scores. The current evidence is sufficient for a reasoned decision.

4. **Monitoring mitigates remaining uncertainty.** A structured follow-up plan ensures we will detect if the effect fails to materialize at scale, allowing for course correction.

---

## **Implementation Plan**

### **Phase 1: Full Rollout (Weeks 1–4)**
- Implement new procedure at all remaining 14 branches
- Provide standardized training materials from high-performing switched branches
- Establish baseline metrics at each location

### **Phase 2: Active Monitoring (Weeks 5–16)**
- Collect weekly service scores across all 34 branches
- Track staffing time and patron feedback
- Flag any branch showing score declines >3 points for investigation

### **Phase 3: Evaluation (Week 17)**
- Re-run difference-in-differences analysis with full cooperative data
- Expected: Narrower confidence intervals, more precise effect estimate
- Board review: Confirm continuation or adjust procedure

## **Conclusion**

The data support moving forward. The new procedure demonstrates a positive effect on service scores with no detected harms. While statistical certainty is not absolute, the evidence is sufficient to justify cooperative-wide adoption with structured monitoring. We recommend the board authorize full rollout effective immediately.
