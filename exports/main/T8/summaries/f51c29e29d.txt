# **Memorandum to the Board**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

**Date:** November 15, 2026

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores and should be rolled out system-wide.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by **2.1 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents a **4% improvement** in service quality and is statistically significant (p < 0.01).

---

## **Key Findings**

### **What We Tested**
- **150 branches** participated: 90 switched to the new procedure, 60 kept the old
- **17 weeks of data** collected (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring in week 15
- We compared score changes in switching branches against non-switching branches to isolate the procedure's effect

### **Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched to new procedure | 49.0 | 51.3 | +2.3 |
| Kept old procedure | 49.3 | 49.5 | +0.2 |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | | **+2.1** |

### **Statistical Evidence**
- **Estimated effect:** +2.13 points
- **95% confidence interval:** +0.9 to +3.4 points
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.01
- **Effect persisted** across all 8 weeks after implementation with no decline

### **Validation**
- **Pre-trends were flat:** Before the switch, differences between groups fluctuated randomly with no systematic pattern
- **Post-trend is consistent:** Every week after the switch showed a positive effect (range: +1.2 to +2.4 points)
- **Result is robust:** The effect holds when accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Criterion | Assessment |
|-----------|------------|
| **Statistical significance** | ✅ Passed (p < 0.01) |
| **Practical significance** | ✅ Passed (4% improvement) |
| **Parallel trends assumption** | ✅ Reasonable (no systematic pre-trend) |
| **Effect consistency** | ✅ Passed (positive in all 8 post-weeks) |
| **Confounding factors** | ✅ Passed (no effect on staffing/visits) |
| **Long-term evidence** | ⚠️ Limited (only 8 weeks post-switch) |

**Overall: PROCEED WITH ROLLOUT**

The evidence is strong enough to recommend system-wide adoption. The single limitation is the relatively short post-implementation window; a follow-up evaluation in 6 months is advised to confirm sustained benefits.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Authorize rollout** to the 60 branches still using the old procedure
2. **Develop training materials** documenting the specific practices that drove improvement
3. **Monitor scores during implementation** to confirm effects replicate across all branches
4. **Schedule 6-month follow-up** to assess long-term sustainability
