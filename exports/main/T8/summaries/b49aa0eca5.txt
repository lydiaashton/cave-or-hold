## **Impact of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure on Service Scores**

---

### **BOTTOM LINE**

**The evidence suggests the new procedure has a positive effect, but with important caveats.** Branches that switched showed service score gains of approximately **+1 to +1.5 points** in weeks 6-8 after implementation. However, there was no detectable benefit in the first 5 weeks, and the analysis has limitations due to pre-existing differences between branches that switched versus those that didn't.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rollout, but implement careful monitoring and allow 6+ weeks before evaluating success at each branch.

---

### **KEY FINDINGS**

**1. Study Overview**
| | |
|---|---|
| **Branches analyzed** | 709 total (425 switched, 284 control) |
| **Time period** | 17 weeks (weeks 6–22) |
| **Switch date** | Week 15 |

**2. Treatment Effects Over Time**
| Period After Switch | Effect on Service Score | Statistical Significance |
|---------------------|------------------------|-------------------------|
| Weeks 1-5 (15-19) | +0.1 to +0.6 points | Not significant |
| Weeks 6-8 (20-22) | +1.0 to +1.5 points | **Statistically significant** |

**3. Important Limitations**
- **Pre-existing differences:** Branches that chose to switch had systematically different service score patterns in the early weeks of the study, raising questions about whether they were also different in unobserved ways
- **Convergence before switch:** The groups' trends did align in the 4 weeks immediately before the switch, which supports (but doesn't prove) the validity of the comparison
- **Modest effect size:** Even the significant effects represent only ~2-3% improvement relative to the baseline score of 50

**4. Simple Difference-in-Differences Estimate**
The pooled estimate of **+1.94 points** (p < 0.001) should be interpreted cautiously—it averages together weeks with no effect and weeks with positive effects, and doesn't fully account for pre-existing differences between groups.

---

### **NEXT STEPS**

1. **Proceed with cautious rollout** - The later-week evidence is encouraging, but manage expectations

2. **Set realistic timelines** - Allow at least 6 weeks before evaluating whether the new procedure is working at each branch

3. **Implement rigorous monitoring** - Track service scores weekly for each newly switched branch to confirm similar patterns emerge

4. **Consider a more controlled rollout** - If feasible, randomly assign remaining branches to switch in different weeks to generate cleaner evidence

5. **Investigate branch characteristics** - Understand why some branches showed gains and whether certain types of branches benefit more

---

### **FINAL VERDICT**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **Did the new procedure change service scores?** | **Yes** — there is evidence of a positive effect |
| **Was the effect immediate?** | **No** — benefits emerged only after 5+ weeks |
| **Is the evidence conclusive?** | **No** — pre-existing differences between groups create uncertainty |
| **Should we roll out to all branches?** | **Yes, with conditions** — proceed but monitor closely and allow adequate time for benefits to materialize |
| **What effect size should we expect?** | **+1 to +1.5 points** after 6+ weeks (not the +1.94 from simple DiD) |

**Bottom-line verdict:** The new procedure appears to improve service scores, but the effect is modest and takes time to develop. The board should approve the rollout while setting realistic expectations and establishing a monitoring framework to verify results as implementation proceeds.

---

### **TECHNICAL NOTES**

- Analysis uses cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch)
- Event study design with week fixed effects reveals the time pattern of effects
- Pre-trend tests: Weeks 6-13 jointly significant (p=0.0015), but weeks 10-13 not significant (p=0.11)
- Post-trend tests: Weeks 15-22 jointly significant (p=0.023); weeks 20-22 jointly significant (p=0.040)
