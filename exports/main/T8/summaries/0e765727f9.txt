# **Board Memorandum (Final)**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors
**From:** Data Analysis Team
**Date:** November 2025
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence suggests the new procedure improved service scores, but with important caveats.** The estimated improvement is **1.2 to 2.1 points** (2–4% on a ~50-point scale). However, branches that adopted the new procedure were already showing stronger service score trends before the switch, which introduces uncertainty about how much of the improvement is due to the procedure itself versus pre-existing differences. A **phased rollout with careful monitoring** is recommended rather than immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Total branches studied | 709 |
| Branches using new procedure | 425 (60%) |
| Branches using old procedure | 284 (40%) |
| Study period | Weeks 6–22 (17 weeks) |
| Switch implemented | Week 15 |

### Service Score Results

| Group | Pre-Switch | Post-Switch | Change |
|-------|------------|-------------|--------|
| **New Procedure** | 50.96 | 52.55 | **+1.59** |
| **Old Procedure** | 49.85 | 49.37 | **-0.48** |

**Raw Difference-in-Differences: +2.07 points**

### Critical Limitation: Pre-Existing Trends

The analysis relies on the assumption that treatment and control branches would have followed similar trends absent the intervention. **This assumption does not fully hold:**

- In weeks 6–9, both groups had nearly identical service scores (~49.5)
- By weeks 10–14, the treatment group had already pulled ahead by ~2 points
- The treatment group was improving at nearly **double the rate** of the control group before the switch

**Adjusted estimate** (using weeks 10–14 as baseline): **+1.22 points** (95% CI: +0.62 to +1.82)

### What This Means

Branches that volunteered to switch may have been systematically different—perhaps more motivated leadership, better staffing, or already prioritizing service improvements. The procedure itself may still have caused improvement, but we cannot isolate the effect with full confidence.

---

## **Recommendations**

### Cautious Rollout Approach

1. **Pilot expansion**: Roll out to 50–100 additional branches first, prioritizing those with characteristics similar to the original control group.

2. **Set clear success criteria**: Define minimum improvement thresholds (e.g., +1.0 points sustained over 8 weeks) before proceeding to full rollout.

3. **Randomize where possible**: For the next phase, consider random assignment among willing branches to strengthen causal inference.

4. **Monitor closely**: Track service scores weekly for 12 weeks post-implementation at each new branch.

5. **Document implementation factors**: Capture branch characteristics (staffing, management tenure, prior performance) to identify what conditions enable success.

### Decision Timeline

- **Weeks 1–4**: Select and prepare pilot branches
- **Weeks 5–16**: Implement and monitor pilot
- **Week 17**: Board review of pilot results
- **Week 18+**: Decision on full system-wide rollout

---

## **Decision Requested**

The Board is asked to:

☐ **Approve** a phased pilot expansion (50–100 branches) rather than immediate system-wide rollout

☐ **Authorize** staff to develop selection criteria and monitoring protocols for the pilot

☐ **Schedule** a follow-up review in 4 months to evaluate pilot results before deciding on full rollout

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **Did service scores improve after the switch?** | Yes—treatment branches improved by ~2 points relative to control branches. |
| **Can we confidently attribute this to the new procedure?** | Partially—pre-existing trends suggest some improvement would have occurred anyway. |
| **Is the procedure likely beneficial?** | Probably yes—the effect persists even with conservative adjustments, and the pattern of gradual post-switch improvement supports a real treatment effect. |
| **Should we roll out immediately to all branches?** | **No**—the uncertainty warrants a phased approach with monitoring before full commitment. |
| **Should we abandon the procedure?** | **No**—the evidence points toward net benefit, and the risks of continued piloting are low. |

### Bottom-Line Recommendation

**Proceed with a phased rollout, not immediate system-wide adoption.** The procedure likely improves service scores, but the magnitude is uncertain. A cautious expansion allows the cooperative to capture potential benefits while protecting against the risk that the observed gains were driven by branch characteristics rather than the procedure itself.
