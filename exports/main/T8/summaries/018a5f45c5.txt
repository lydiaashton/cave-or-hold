# **Board Summary: New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure Evaluation**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores by approximately 2.1 points (4%), and this effect is statistically significant.** The evidence supports proceeding with rollout, though with appropriate monitoring given the quasi-experimental design and modest effect size.

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **Did the new procedure cause an improvement?** | **Yes** — the 2.1-point gain is statistically significant (p=0.001) and robust across multiple analytical specifications |
| **Is the evidence strong enough to act on?** | **Yes, with caveats** — the difference-in-differences design with fixed effects is appropriate for this policy question, and the parallel trends assumption holds |
| **Should we roll out to all branches?** | **Proceed with rollout, but monitor closely** — the benefit is real but modest; track outcomes to confirm the effect persists at scale |
| **What could change this verdict?** | If implementation costs are high, if effects fade after 3–6 months, or if a randomized follow-up study shows different results |

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Overview**
- **150 branches** participated: 90 adopted the new procedure, 60 continued with the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22), with the switch implemented at week 15
- **Analysis approach:** Difference-in-differences design with branch fixed effects and cluster-robust standard errors

### **Service Score Impact**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 50.8 | 53.7 | +2.9 |
| Control branches | 47.6 | 48.4 | +0.9 |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | | **+2.1** |

- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.001
- **Effect size:** 4% increase relative to mean score of 50.5
- **Branch-level consistency:** 77% of switched branches improved vs. 60% of control branches

### **Important Caveats**

| Concern | Evidence | Implication |
|---------|----------|-------------|
| **Baseline differences** | Switched branches scored 3.2 points higher before the switch | Controlled for in the analysis, but indicates non-random selection |
| **Parallel trends** | Pre-switch gap was stable (no significant trend, p=0.115) | Supports validity of the DiD comparison |
| **Post-switch duration** | Only 8 weeks of data after implementation | Monitor for effect persistence |
| **Cost-benefit unknown** | No cost data in this analysis | Board should weigh 4% gain against implementation costs |

---

## **Recommendations**

1. **Approve rollout** to remaining 60 branches using the old procedure

2. **Implement over 4–6 weeks** to allow for training and adjustment

3. **Monitor service scores monthly** for the first 6 months post-rollout

4. **Conduct cost analysis** to assess whether the 4% improvement justifies implementation expenses

5. **Return to the board in 6 months** with full implementation results and cost-benefit assessment
