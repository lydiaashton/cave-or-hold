## **New Branch Procedure: Impact on Service Scores**

---

### **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched saw scores increase by **+2.3 points** (approximately **4-5% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and grew stronger over time.

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout.**

---

### **KEY FINDINGS**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Branches studied | 709 (425 switched, 284 did not) |
| Study period | 17 weeks (weeks 6-22) |
| Switch date | Week 15 |
| **Treatment effect** | **+2.27 points** |
| 95% confidence interval | 1.7 to 2.8 points |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 |
| Effect size | ~4.4% improvement |

**1. Clear Positive Impact**
The difference-in-differences analysis shows the new procedure caused a meaningful increase in service scores. This result holds after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume.

**2. Effect Strengthened Over Time**
- Weeks 15-18 (early): +1.8 points
- Weeks 19-22 (late): +2.8 points

This learning curve suggests branches improved their implementation with experience.

**3. No Confounding Factors**
Staffing and visitor traffic did not change differently between switching and non-switching branches, confirming the improvement is attributable to the procedure itself.

**4. Trends Were Comparable Before the Switch**
Pre-switch data shows treatment and control branches followed similar trajectories in the 6 weeks immediately before switching, supporting the validity of the comparison.

---

### **VISUAL SUMMARY**

The event study chart shows the treatment-control gap by week:

- **Before switch** (weeks 6-14): Groups tracked closely together
- **After switch** (weeks 15-22): Switched branches pulled ahead, with the gap widening from ~1.3 to nearly 3 points

- **Method:** Difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch)
- **Robustness:** Results consistent across model specifications with and without controls
- **Assumption check:** Pre-treatment trends show no systematic divergence between groups in the 6 weeks before switch
