# **Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**
**We cannot conclude the new procedure improved service scores.** The apparent positive effect is sensitive to model specification and disappears when controlling for branch-level differences. Given that no effect was expected and the evidence is inconclusive, **service scores should not be used as justification for rollout**. If the procedure has operational benefits, proceed on those grounds—but do not expect service score improvements.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. The Evidence Is Mixed

| Model Specification | Effect Size | P-value | Significant? |
|---------------------|-------------|---------|--------------|
| Basic DiD | +1.89 | 0.001 | ✓ Yes |
| + Staffing/Visits Controls | +1.89 | 0.001 | ✓ Yes |
| **+ Branch Fixed Effects** | **-0.22** | **0.55** | **✗ No** |
| + Week Fixed Effects | +2.56 | 0.09 | Marginal |
| + Both Fixed Effects | +1.89 | 0.001 | ✓ Yes |

**Critical Issue:** When controlling for branch-level differences alone, the effect **disappears completely**. This suggests the apparent benefit may reflect *which branches switched* rather than the procedure itself.

### 2. Pre-Trend Violations

The difference-in-differences method requires parallel trends before the intervention. Our data shows:

| Pre-Switch Week | Difference (Treatment - Control) | Significant? |
|-----------------|----------------------------------|--------------|
| Week 9 | -8.19 points | ✗ Yes (p<0.001) |
| Week 10 | -6.54 points | ✗ Yes (p<0.001) |
| Week 12 | -2.33 points | ✗ Yes (p<0.001) |

**This violates a core assumption** of the analysis and undermines causal claims.

### 3. What Actually Happened

| Group | Before | After | Change |
|-------|--------|-------|--------|
| Switched Branches | 50.54 | 50.37 | -0.17 (stable) |
| Non-Switched Branches | 49.85 | 47.79 | -2.06 (declined) |

The "effect" is driven entirely by the **control group's decline**, not the treatment group's improvement. Switched branches simply maintained their baseline.

### 4. Why This Matters

- **No effect was expected**: This procedure change was operational, not service-oriented
- **Selection bias likely**: Branches that chose to switch may differ systematically from those that didn't
- **Short observation window**: Only 8 weeks post-switch limits conclusions
- **Model sensitivity**: Results change dramatically based on specification

---

## **What We Cannot Conclude**

❌ That the procedure *caused* any improvement in service scores  
❌ That effects would persist with system-wide rollout  
❌ That service scores should factor into the rollout decision  

---

## **Recommendations**

1. **Do NOT cite service scores as justification** for rolling out the new procedure.

2. **Proceed (or not) based on operational merits**: If the new rotation/shelving procedure has efficiency, cost, or staff satisfaction benefits, use those as the basis for the decision.

3. **Continue monitoring**: Track service scores during any rollout, but treat them as a neutral metric—neither expecting improvement nor decline.

4. **Investigate the control group decline**: The 2-point drop in non-switched branches warrants attention regardless of this procedure change.

5. **Consider a more rigorous evaluation**: If service scores are important to the decision, a randomized rollout with longer follow-up would provide clearer evidence.

## **FINAL VERDICT**

> **On the question of whether the new procedure changed service scores:**
>
> ### **NO RELIABLE EFFECT DETECTED**
>
> The data do not support a conclusion that the new procedure improved (or harmed) service scores. The statistically significant result from basic models is not robust to alternative specifications that account for branch-level differences. Pre-trend violations further undermine causal interpretation.
>
> **The Board should make its rollout decision on operational grounds, not service score expectations.**
