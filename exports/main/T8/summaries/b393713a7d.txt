# **Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure Evaluation**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence is inconclusive.** While branches that switched show higher service scores after the change, we cannot confidently attribute this to the new procedure. Branches that adopted the new procedure were already improving faster than others *before* the switch, suggesting selection bias (better-performing branches may have volunteered to switch).

**Recommendation: Do not proceed with systemwide rollout yet.** Conduct a properly randomized trial or gather more evidence before committing resources.

---

## **Key Findings**

### What the Data Shows
| | |
|---|---|
| Switched branches improved by | +2.8 points |
| Non-switched branches improved by | +0.8 points |
| **Raw difference** | **+2.1 points** |

### Why We Can't Claim Causation
- **Parallel trends violated:** Treatment group was already improving faster before the switch (p = 0.001)
- **Week 14 (pre-switch):** Treatment group already 1.61 points ahead (p = 0.002)
- **Selection bias likely:** Branches that chose to switch may have been more motivated or better-managed

### What This Means
The observed 2.1-point gap likely reflects **both** the procedure's effect **and** pre-existing differences between branches. We cannot separate these with the current data.

---

## **Final Verdict**

**Cannot recommend systemwide rollout based on current evidence.**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did switching branches improve? | **Yes** (+2.8 points) |
| Did the procedure cause the improvement? | **Uncertain** |
| Is the effect large enough to matter? | **Possibly** (if real, ~4% improvement) |
| Should we roll out to all 709 branches? | **Not yet** |

**Bottom line:** The new procedure *may* work, but this analysis cannot prove it. Rolling out systemwide based on this evidence risks investing resources in a procedure that may not deliver the expected benefits. A randomized trial would cost less than a failed systemwide implementation.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Pause systemwide rollout** until we have stronger causal evidence

2. **Run a randomized controlled trial:** Randomly assign branches to new vs. old procedure to eliminate selection bias

3. **Alternatively, use a staggered rollout:** Randomize the timing of implementation across branches for a cleaner analysis

4. **Gather qualitative data:** Interview switching branches to understand implementation challenges and perceived benefits

5. **Re-analyze after 6–12 months** with better identification strategy before making a final decision

---

*The current analysis uses observational data where branches self-selected into the new procedure. This limits our ability to draw causal conclusions. A randomized design would provide the evidence needed for a confident rollout decision.*
