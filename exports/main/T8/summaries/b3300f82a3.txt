# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD OF DIRECTORS**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative

**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Rotation Procedure

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

# **DO NOT PROCEED WITH SYSTEM-WIDE ROLLOUT**

**The evidence does not support the claim that the new procedure causes improved service scores.** The observed improvement is attributable to pre-existing differences between branches that chose to switch versus those that did not—not to the procedure itself.

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

While switched branches did show higher service scores after the change, our analysis reveals these branches were **already improving before they switched**. This indicates **selection bias**, not a causal effect of the procedure. **We recommend against a full rollout until more reliable evidence is obtained.**

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### 1. Initial Results Were Misleading
The simple before/after comparison showed switched branches gaining +2 points. However, this analysis **did not account for pre-existing differences** between branches that chose to switch versus those that didn't.

### 2. Parallel Trends Assumption Is Violated
For a valid causal comparison, treatment and control branches should follow similar trends before the intervention. **This was not the case:**
- Treatment branches were already improving relative to control branches **before** the switch
- 7 out of 9 pre-switch weeks showed statistically significant differences
- Statistical test confirms pre-trends are highly significant (p < 0.0001)

### 3. When Pre-Trends Are Accounted For, the Effect Disappears
| Model | Estimated Effect | Statistical Significance |
|-------|------------------|--------------------------|
| Simple DiD (ignores pre-trends) | +1.98 points | Significant (p < 0.001) |
| DiD **with pre-trend control** | -0.46 points | **Not significant (p = 0.28)** |

### 4. Likely Explanation: Selection Bias
Branches that volunteered to switch may have been:
- Better managed overall
- More motivated to improve
- Already implementing other improvements
- Different in unobserved ways that affect service scores

---

## **RECOMMENDATION**

**Do NOT proceed with system-wide rollout at this time.** The current evidence does not support a causal claim that the new procedure improves service scores.

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

| Action | Rationale | Timeline |
|--------|-----------|----------|
| **Pause full rollout** | Avoid investing resources in a procedure with unproven benefits | Immediate |
| **Run a randomized trial** | Randomly assign some branches to adopt the procedure; this would eliminate selection bias | 3-6 months |
| **Gather more pre-intervention data** | More historical data would help distinguish true effects from pre-existing trends | Ongoing |
| **Interview switched branches** | Qualitative insights may reveal whether branches were already improving for other reasons | 1-2 months |
| **Re-analyze in 6 months** | With more post-switch data, we may be able to better isolate the procedure's effect | 6 months |
