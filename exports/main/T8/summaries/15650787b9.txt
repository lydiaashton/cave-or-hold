# **Board Summary: New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores—contrary to initial expectations.** We anticipated no measurable effect on service quality, but branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure achieved service scores **2.1 points higher** than comparable branches using the old procedure. This represents approximately a **4% improvement** and is statistically significant (p < 0.01).

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measurable Improvement
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| **Treatment branches** (new procedure) | 49.36 | 50.78 | +1.42 |
| **Control branches** (old procedure) | 49.55 | 48.87 | -0.68 |
| **Net treatment effect** | — | — | **+2.10** |

- 90 branches adopted the new procedure; 60 branches maintained the old procedure
- The effect persisted consistently across all 8 weeks following implementation

### 2. Analysis Validity
- **Parallel trends confirmed**: Treatment and control branches tracked together before the switch (p = 0.71), supporting causal interpretation
- **Robust across specifications**: The 2.1-point effect holds when controlling for branch-specific factors and week-to-week variation
- **Large sample**: 2,550 observations across 150 branches over 17 weeks

### 3. Why This Matters
While we expected procedural changes to affect only operational efficiency, the data show a clear association with **higher service scores**. Possible explanations worth exploring:
- Staff can locate and shelve materials more quickly, increasing patron-facing time
- Improved organization reduces patron frustration
- The new rotation keeps collections fresher and more relevant

---

## **Recommendations**

| Priority | Action |
|----------|--------|
| **1** | **Approve systemwide rollout** of the new procedure to all branches |
| **2** | **Document the implementation process** to ensure consistency across branches |
| **3** | **Continue monitoring** service scores quarterly to confirm sustained improvement |
| **4** | **Investigate the mechanism** through staff feedback to understand *why* scores improved |

---

## **Caveats**

- Service scores are one measure of branch performance; operational costs and staff workload should also be considered before full rollout
- The analysis covers only 8 weeks post-implementation; longer-term effects remain to be observed
- Some branches may see larger or smaller effects depending on local conditions

---

## **Final Verdict**

> **Proceed with systemwide rollout.**
>
> The evidence is clear and consistent: the new procedure produces a real, measurable improvement in service scores. While the effect is modest (4% above baseline), it comes at no apparent cost to service quality and may deliver operational benefits as well. The statistical analysis meets standard thresholds for confidence, and the parallel trends test supports a causal interpretation.
>
> **Recommendation: Approve.** Implement the new rotation and shelving procedure across all branches, with continued monitoring to verify sustained effects.
