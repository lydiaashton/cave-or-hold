# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new shelving and rotation procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **1.8 points** (approximately **3.6%** improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant (p = 0.003), with less than a 1% probability the result occurred by chance.

**This finding was unexpected** based on prior assumptions, but the data is clear and the analysis is robust.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **What We Studied**
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15
- **60 branches** continued with the old procedure (serving as a control group)
- Analysis compares how service scores changed for each group before and after week 15

### **What We Found**

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|----------------|---------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 50.03 | 51.47 | +1.44 |
| Non-switched branches | 48.77 | 48.43 | −0.34 |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | | **+1.78** |

- **Treatment effect: +1.78 points** on the service score index
- **95% confidence interval: 0.60 to 2.97 points**
- **Statistical significance: p = 0.003**
- **Practical significance: 3.6%** improvement relative to typical branch scores

### **Why We're Confident in This Result**

1. **Strong research design.** By comparing switched branches to non-switched branches over the same time period, we control for system-wide factors (seasonal changes, budget cycles, etc.) that might affect all branches.

2. **Pre-trends check out.** Before week 15, both groups followed similar patterns—there's no evidence the switched branches were already on an upward trajectory.

3. **Result holds with controls.** The effect persists even after accounting for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume.

4. **Clear timing.** The divergence between groups appears precisely when the procedure change occurred, not before.

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **Did the new procedure affect service scores?** | **Yes** — the evidence is statistically significant and robust. |
| **Should we roll out to all branches?** | **Yes** — the expected benefit is clear and meaningful. |
| **How confident should we be?** | **High confidence** in the direction and existence of the effect; moderate confidence in the exact magnitude (could range from 0.6 to 3.0 points). |
| **What surprised us?** | We expected no effect. The positive result suggests the procedure change has benefits we did not anticipate—worth investigating further. |

### **Questions to Consider**

1. **Why did this work?** The new procedure was not expected to affect service scores. Understanding the mechanism (faster shelving? better stock availability? improved staff morale?) could help us replicate this success elsewhere.

2. **Are there costs we haven't captured?** If the new procedure requires additional training time or equipment, those should be weighed against the service improvement.

3. **Should we set a target?** A 1.8-point improvement is the average effect. Setting a minimum acceptable improvement (e.g., 1.0 point) could help identify branches that need additional support during rollout.
