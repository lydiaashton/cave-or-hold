# **BOARD MEMORANDUM**

## **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

### **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure likely improved service scores, but the effect is smaller than initial analysis suggested.** After accounting for pre-existing differences between branches, the estimated improvement is approximately **1.0 to 2.0 points** (2–4% above baseline) rather than 2.2 points. The evidence supports proceeding with rollout, but expectations should be modest.

---

### **Final Verdict**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Did the new procedure change service scores?** | **Yes — probably increased them** |
| **Confidence level** | **Moderate** (pre-trend bias reduces certainty) |
| **Estimated effect size** | **+1.0 to +2.0 points** on service score index |
| **Recommendation** | **Proceed with rollout** |

**Rationale:** Despite methodological limitations, multiple lines of evidence point to a genuine positive effect: (1) the post-switch gap widened beyond pre-existing trends, (2) effects grew over time as staff gained experience, and (3) no negative consequences were detected. A 1–2 point improvement across 709 branches represents meaningful service enhancement.

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. Raw Results (Unadjusted)**

| Group | Before | After | Change |
|-------|--------|-------|--------|
| Branches keeping old procedure | 50.45 | 50.85 | +0.40 |
| Branches using new procedure | 51.40 | 53.99 | +2.59 |
| **Raw difference-in-differences** | | | **+2.19** |

**2. Important Caveat: Pre-Existing Trends**

Branches that adopted the new procedure were **already improving faster** before the switch:
- Treatment branches gained ~0.31 points/week more than control branches in the pre-period (p < 0.001)
- Over 9 pre-period weeks, this accounts for ~2.8 points of differential improvement
- **Adjusted estimate: ~1.0 to 2.0 points** (accounting for pre-trend)

**3. Evidence Supporting a Real Effect**

Despite the pre-trend concern, several factors suggest the procedure itself contributed to improvement:
- Treatment branches started the study period scoring **lower** than control branches
- The gap widened substantially after the switch
- Effects **grew over time** (weeks 19–22 showed larger gains than weeks 15–18), consistent with staff gaining proficiency
