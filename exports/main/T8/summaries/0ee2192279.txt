# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2025  
**SUBJECT:** Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure likely improved service scores, but the effect is smaller than initial analysis suggested.** After accounting for pre-existing differences between branches, the evidence indicates an improvement of approximately **1.0 to 1.5 points** (about **2-3% improvement**). While the statistical evidence has limitations, the direction of the effect is consistently positive. **We recommend proceeding with rollout, but with tempered expectations and continued monitoring.**

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Does the new procedure improve service scores?** | **Yes** — evidence supports a positive effect |
| **Is the effect statistically significant?** | **Yes** — p < 0.05 even in conservative specifications |
| **Is the effect practically meaningful?** | **Modestly** — approximately 2-3% improvement |
| **Should the cooperative roll out to all branches?** | **Yes, with conditions** — phased implementation recommended |
| **What is the key risk?** | Pre-existing differences between branches may mean the true effect is smaller than estimated |

**Decision Confidence:** Moderate. The data support proceeding, but expectations should be calibrated to a 1.0–1.5 point improvement rather than the 2.0+ points suggested by simpler analyses.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **Study Overview**
- **709 branches**: 425 adopted the new procedure, 284 retained the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22), with the switch implemented in week 15

### **Important Methodological Note**

⚠️ **The treatment and control groups were on different trajectories before the switch.** Branches that adopted the new procedure were already declining more slowly than those that did not. This violates a key assumption of the analysis method and means the initial estimate of 2.05 points is likely **overstated**.

### **Revised Impact Estimates**

| Analysis Approach | Estimated Effect | Confidence |
|-------------------|------------------|------------|
| Standard comparison | 2.05 points | Likely biased high |
| Weeks closest to switch (13-16) | 1.21 points | More reliable |
| Immediately adjacent weeks (14-15) | 0.87 points | Most conservative |
| **Recommended estimate** | **~1.0 to 1.5 points** | **Best judgment** |

### **What the Data Still Shows**

Despite the methodological concern, several patterns support a **positive effect**:

1. **Treatment group improved** after the switch (+1.30 points from their pre-switch average)
2. **Control group declined** after the switch (−0.75 points from their pre-switch average)
3. **Effect is consistent** across all 8 post-switch weeks (treatment group outperformed control in every week)
4. **Statistical significance** holds even in conservative specifications (p < 0.05)

### **Post-Switch Performance (Weeks 15-22)**

| Group | Average Service Score |
|-------|----------------------|
| New procedure branches | 52.5 |
| Old procedure branches | 49.6 |
| **Difference** | **2.9 points** |

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

### **Recommended Actions**

1. **Approve phased rollout** of the new procedure
   - Evidence supports a positive effect, though smaller than initially estimated
   - Expected improvement: **~1.0 to 1.5 points** on service score index (2-3%)

2. **Implement with monitoring**
   - Track service scores during rollout to confirm effects persist
   - Set up early warning triggers if scores do not improve as expected

3. **Consider a staggered rollout**
   - Roll out to additional branches in phases
   - Use each phase as an opportunity to validate the effect

4. **Investigate what drives the improvement**
   - Interview staff at high-performing branches to identify best practices
   - Determine which elements of the new procedure matter most

### **Risk Mitigation**

- If the true effect is at the low end (~0.9 points), the benefit may not justify implementation costs at some branches
- Consider branch-level factors (size, staffing, volume) that might moderate the effect
- Be prepared to adjust or pause rollout if monitoring shows weaker-than-expected results

---

## **CONCLUSION**

The evidence **supports a modest positive effect** of the new procedure on service scores, though the magnitude is uncertain due to pre-existing differences between branches. The most defensible estimate is **1.0 to 1.5 points** (approximately 2-3% improvement).

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rollout, but implement in phases with careful monitoring. Temper expectations to approximately half the initially estimated benefit.

---

*Analysis based on 12,053 weekly observations from 709 branches. Standard errors clustered at the branch level. Pre-trend violation detected (p < 0.001); estimates adjusted accordingly.*
