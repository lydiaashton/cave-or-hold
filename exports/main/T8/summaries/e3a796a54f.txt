# **Memorandum to the Board**

## **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

### **Bottom Line**

**The evidence suggests the new procedure improved service scores, but with important caveats.** Branches that adopted the new procedure showed service score gains of approximately **2 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. However, branches that switched were already on a slightly more positive trajectory before the change, which introduces some uncertainty about the exact magnitude of the effect.

**Recommendation: Proceed with cautious rollout, with close monitoring.**

---

### **Key Findings**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated Treatment Effect | ~2.0 service score points |
| 95% Confidence Interval | 1.5 to 2.7 points (basic model) |
| Statistical Significance | p < 0.001 |
| Branches Studied | 709 total (425 switched, 284 control) |
| Study Period | 17 weeks (weeks 6–22) |

**1. Positive Association, But Causality Has Uncertainty**
The basic analysis shows a 2.1-point improvement associated with the new procedure. However, branches that switched were already trending slightly upward relative to control branches before the switch. This means the true effect could be smaller than 2 points, though the week-by-week pattern suggests a real change occurred at week 15.

**2. Effect Appears to Grow Over Time**
The gap between treatment and control branches widened from about 1.5 points in week 15 to about 4 points by week 22. This pattern is consistent with staff learning and improving with the new procedure over time.

**3. Control Group Declined After Week 15**
Branches that kept the old procedure saw scores drop by about 0.6 points after week 15, while switching branches improved. Some of this may reflect seasonal patterns affecting all branches.

**4. Robust to Controls**
The result holds after accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume, suggesting the effect is not simply due to those factors.

---

### **Final Verdict**

**Proceed with rollout, but temper expectations.**

The weight of evidence supports the new procedure having a positive effect on service scores. The growing gap over time and the statistical significance even after controlling for week-specific patterns suggest something meaningful changed at week 15. However, because the treatment and control groups were not perfectly comparable before the switch, we cannot rule out that some portion of the observed improvement reflects pre-existing differences rather than the procedure itself.

**Expected outcome:** A realistic expectation is a **1.0 to 2.0 point improvement** per branch (rather than the full 2.1 points from the basic model). Even at the lower end, this represents meaningful service quality gains across 709 branches.

**Risk assessment:** Low. The procedure has already been tested at 425 branches without apparent negative consequences. The main risk is over-investing in a rollout that delivers smaller benefits than hoped—not that it will cause harm.

---

### **Next Steps**

1. **Approve phased rollout** – Expand to remaining branches, but implement in stages to allow for monitoring

2. **Establish clear success metrics** – Target 1.5–2.0 point improvement per branch; track weekly during rollout

3. **Continue rigorous evaluation** – Collect at least 8–10 weeks of post-implementation data per branch to confirm effects persist

4. **Document implementation practices** – Capture what successful early-adopter branches did differently to support training

5. **Set a review point** – Reassess after 50% rollout; if effects are smaller than expected, investigate why before full deployment

---

### **Methodology Note**

This analysis used a difference-in-differences approach. A pre-trend test revealed treatment branches had slightly different trajectories before the switch (statistically significant at p=0.002), which introduces some uncertainty about causal attribution. Models controlling for week-specific effects still show a significant positive association. Standard errors were clustered at the branch level.
