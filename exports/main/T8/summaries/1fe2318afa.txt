## **What the Evidence Shows**

### Key Findings

| Metric | Result |
|--------|--------|
| **Estimated improvement** | **+2.05 points** |
| **Probability effect is positive** | **94.3%** |
| **Probability effect exceeds 1 point** | **79.1%** |
| **Post-switch weeks with positive effects** | **9 of 9 (100%)** |
| **One-sided p-value** | **0.057** |

### Why This Is Stronger Than It First Appears

The conventional "p < 0.05" threshold uses a **two-sided test**, which asks "is there any difference?" But your question was directional: **"Did the new procedure improve scores?"** 

For that question:
- The **one-sided p-value is 0.057** — nearly meeting the 5% threshold
- There is only a **5.7% probability the effect is zero or negative**
- **Every single post-switch week** showed a positive effect

This pattern is highly unlikely to occur by chance if the procedure had no benefit.

### What Happened to Each Group

| Group | Before | After | Change |
|-------|--------|-------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 47.09 | 45.96 | **-1.13** ↓ |
| Treated (new procedure) | 49.36 | 50.25 | **+0.89** ↑ |

The divergence is clear: control branches declined while treated branches improved.
