# **Board Summary: New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.0 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant (p < 0.01) and represents a meaningful improvement. Contrary to the initial expectation of no difference, the evidence supports rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. The New Procedure Increased Service Scores

| Group | Pre-Switch Average | Post-Switch Average | Change |
|-------|-------------------|---------------------|--------|
| Switched Branches (90) | 51.1 | 53.6 | +2.51 |
| Control Branches (60) | 52.3 | 52.8 | +0.51 |
| **Net Effect** | — | — | **+2.00** |

The 2-point gain represents a **3.8% improvement** over the average service score and equals **0.20 standard deviations**—a modest but practically meaningful effect.

### 2. Most Switched Branches Benefited

- **76.7% of switched branches** saw their scores improve after adoption
- Only **51.7% of control branches** saw improvements in the same period
- The difference is statistically significant (p = 0.002)

### 3. The Analysis Is Reliable

- **Parallel trends confirmed**: Before the switch (weeks 6–14), switched and control branches showed no statistically significant difference in their score trajectories (p = 0.94). This validates the comparison.
- **Robust to other factors**: The effect remains significant after accounting for weekly staffing levels and visit volumes.
- **Adequate sample**: 150 branches over 17 weeks (2,550 observations).

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did the new procedure change service scores? | **Yes** |
| Direction of change? | **Positive** (scores increased) |
| Statistical confidence? | **High** (p = 0.001) |
| Initial expectation met? | **No** (we expected no difference) |
| **Recommendation** | **Proceed with system-wide rollout** |

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Implement rollout** to the 60 branches still using the old procedure.

2. **Monitor results**: Track service scores at newly switched branches to confirm the effect replicates at scale.

3. **Understand the mechanism**: The analysis shows *that* the procedure works, but not *why*. Consider follow-up with branch staff to identify which aspects of the new procedure drive improvements.

4. **Evaluate costs**: If the new procedure requires additional resources (training, time, equipment), weigh those against the 2-point service score gain.
