# Board Summary: New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

**Date:** November 2025  
**Analysis Period:** Weeks 6–22 (switch implemented Week 15)  
**Data:** 709 branches, 12,053 weekly observations

---

## Bottom Line

**We cannot confidently conclude the new procedure improved service scores.** While branches that switched show higher scores after the change, they were already improving relative to other branches *before* the switch. The evidence is inconclusive.

**Recommendation: Do not proceed with full rollout yet.** Instead, gather more evidence through a controlled pilot.

---

## Key Findings

### 1. The Core Problem: Pre-Existing Differences

| Time Period | Treatment vs. Control Gap |
|-------------|---------------------------|
| Week 6 (start) | -0.37 points |
| Week 14 (before switch) | +1.84 points |
| Week 22 (end) | +4.05 points |

**The gap widened by 2.2 points BEFORE the switch and 2.2 points AFTER.** This suggests the divergence was already underway and may not be caused by the new procedure.

### 2. Statistical Tests Confirm the Concern

- **Pre-trend test:** p = 0.0002 (highly significant)
- This means treatment and control branches were on **different trajectories** before the intervention
- The standard difference-in-differences estimate of +2.1 points likely **overstates** the true effect

### 3. What We Can Say

- Branches that adopted the new procedure have higher service scores than those that did not
- However, these branches were already outperforming before the switch
- We cannot separate the procedure's effect from pre-existing differences

---

## Recommendations

### 1. Pause Full Rollout

Do not expand the procedure to all branches until we have stronger evidence of causality.

### 2. Run a Proper Pilot

- **Randomly assign** 50-100 branches to adopt the procedure
- **Randomly assign** 50-100 branches to continue with the old procedure
- Track scores for 12+ weeks
- Randomization ensures groups are comparable at baseline

### 3. Investigate Why Some Branches Were Selected

The non-random selection of which branches switched first may explain the results:
- Were higher-performing branches chosen to pilot the new procedure?
- Were branches with more resources or better management selected?
- Understanding selection criteria is essential for interpretation

### 4. Continue Monitoring

Keep collecting data on all branches. If the new procedure has a real effect, we should see:
- Acceleration in treatment branch scores after switch (not just continuation of prior trend)
- Similar patterns when other branches eventually adopt the procedure

---

## Final Verdict

> **The board should NOT approve a system-wide rollout at this time.**
>
> The observed improvement in service scores cannot be attributed to the new procedure with confidence. The branches that switched were already on a stronger trajectory before adoption, and our analysis cannot disentangle selection effects from true treatment effects.
>
> **Approving rollout now risks:**
> - Spending resources on a procedure that may not deliver benefits
> - Disrupting operations at branches that may see no improvement
> - Missing the opportunity to identify what actually drives service score gains
>
> **The prudent course:** Invest in a properly randomized evaluation. If the procedure truly works, a well-designed pilot will confirm it within 3-4 months. If it does not, we avoid costly system-wide implementation of an ineffective change.
