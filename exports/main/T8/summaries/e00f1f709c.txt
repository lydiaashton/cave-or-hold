# **Board Summary: New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**
## **REVISED ANALYSIS WITH IMPORTANT CAVEATS**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**We cannot confidently conclude the new procedure improved service scores.** While switched branches did outperform control branches after the switch, our analysis reveals a critical flaw: **switched branches were already improving faster than control branches BEFORE the switch occurred**. This means the apparent 2.2-point gain is likely overstated—the true effect could be much smaller, possibly near zero. **We recommend additional analysis before approving system-wide rollout.**

---

**Reasoning:** The data shows correlation, not causation. Switched branches were already on a stronger upward trajectory before the procedure change. Until we can isolate the procedure's true effect, a costly system-wide rollout is not justified.

**However,** there is no evidence the new procedure is harmful. Continuing the pilot while gathering better evidence is the prudent path.

## **Key Findings**

### ⚠️ Critical Issue: Pre-Existing Trends Violate Our Analysis Assumptions

The standard analysis method (difference-in-differences) requires that treatment and control groups follow similar trends before the intervention. **This assumption is violated in our data:**

| Period | Trend for Switched Branches |
|--------|----------------------------|
| Before switch (weeks 6-14) | Already gaining ~0.30 points/week faster than control |
| Statistical significance | p = 0.0015 (highly significant pre-trend) |

**What this means:** If this pre-existing trend continued naturally, we would expect switched branches to pull ahead by ~2.4 points over 8 weeks—even without any procedure change. This is actually *larger* than the 2.17-point effect we originally estimated.

### Revised Effect Estimates

| Method | Estimated Effect | Reliability |
|--------|-----------------|-------------|
| Original DiD (biased) | 2.17 points | ❌ Violates assumptions |
| Week 14 → Week 15 jump | 0.43 points | ⚠️ Noisy, single-week |
| Last 3 pre-weeks vs. first 3 post-weeks | 1.00 points | ⚠️ Still potentially biased |
| **True effect** | **Unknown** | ❓ Cannot determine from this data |

### What We Can Say With Confidence

- Switched branches ended the study period with higher scores than control branches
- The gap widened after week 15
- **However**, the gap was already widening before week 15
- We cannot separate the procedure's effect from the pre-existing trend

## **What Went Wrong in Our Initial Analysis**

Our first analysis assumed switched and control branches would have followed parallel trends without the intervention. The data shows this was not true—switched branches were already on a stronger upward trajectory. This is a common pitfall in observational studies and highlights why we need either:
1. **Random assignment** of branches to treatment/control, or
2. **Longer pre-period data** to verify parallel trends before trusting the results
