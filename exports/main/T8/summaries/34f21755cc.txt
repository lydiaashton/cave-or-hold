# **Memorandum to the Board**

## **Subject: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence is inconclusive.** While branches that adopted the new procedure showed higher service scores after the switch, the analysis reveals serious methodological concerns that prevent us from confidently attributing this improvement to the new procedure. **We do NOT recommend a system-wide rollout based on this evidence alone.** Additional investigation is needed.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **What the Simple Analysis Shows**
- Switched branches averaged **+1.9 points higher** service scores after the switch compared to non-switched branches
- This difference is statistically significant in a basic model

### **Why We Cannot Trust This Result**

**1. Treatment and Control Groups Were Already Diverging Before the Switch**

| Week | Treatment − Control Gap |
|------|------------------------|
| Week 6 | −0.88 |
| Week 10 | −0.25 |
| Week 14 (last pre-switch week) | +1.50 |

The treatment group was already improving relative to control **before** the procedure changed. This violates the core assumption needed to interpret the result as causal.

**2. Post-Switch Effects Are Inconsistent**

Looking week-by-week after the switch:
- **4 of 8 weeks** showed negative or negligible effects
- **1 week** (Week 20) showed an unusually large spike (+8.80 points) that drives much of the average
- The first 3 weeks after switch actually showed **negative** effects

**3. We Cannot Rule Out Selection Bias**

The 425 branches that switched may differ systematically from the 284 that didn't (e.g., more motivated staff, better management, different patron demographics). These differences—not the procedure—may explain the score gap.

---

## **Recommendations**

1. **Do NOT proceed with system-wide rollout** based on current evidence.

2. **Investigate why branches were selected** to switch. If selection was non-random, the comparison is fundamentally biased.

3. **Consider a randomized trial**: Randomly assign remaining branches to new vs. old procedure to get a clean causal estimate.

4. **Gather qualitative feedback** from switched branches—do staff and patrons perceive improvements?

5. **Extend the observation period** to see if effects stabilize or if Week 20 was an anomaly.

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did service scores improve in switched branches? | **Yes** — but they were already trending up before the switch |
| Can we credit the new procedure? | **No** — we cannot isolate the procedure's effect from other factors |
| Should we roll out to all branches? | **Not yet** — evidence is insufficient to justify the cost and disruption |
| Is the new procedure harmful? | **Unlikely** — no evidence of systematic negative effects |
| What should the Board do? | **Pause and investigate** — do not approve rollout until cleaner evidence is available |

---

## **Methodology Note**

This analysis uses difference-in-differences, which requires that treatment and control groups would have followed parallel trends absent the intervention. The pre-treatment data shows this assumption is violated—the groups were already diverging before the switch. This means the +1.9 point estimate likely reflects pre-existing differences rather than the procedure's effect.
