# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

---

## **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

### **BOTTOM LINE**

**Proceed with caution. The evidence is promising but not definitive.**

Branches that adopted the new procedure showed service scores **2.2 points higher** than expected. However, branches selected for early adoption were already improving before the switch, meaning we cannot confidently attribute the full improvement to the new procedure. We recommend a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than immediate system-wide implementation.

---

### **FINAL VERDICT**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did switched branches improve more than non-switched branches? | **Yes** (+2.2 points) |
| Is this difference statistically significant? | **Yes** (p < 0.001) |
| Can we confidently attribute this to the new procedure? | **No** — pre-existing trends complicate interpretation |
| Should we roll out to all branches immediately? | **No** — more evidence needed |
| Should we abandon the new procedure? | **No** — evidence is promising, just not conclusive |

**Recommendation:** Continue the rollout in phases while collecting additional data to confirm the effect.

---

### **KEY FINDINGS**

**1. The Observed Improvement**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Branches Using New Procedure | 51.40 | 53.99 | +2.59 |
| Branches Using Old Procedure | 50.45 | 50.85 | +0.40 |
| **Observed Difference** | | | **+2.2** |

**2. The Complication: Pre-Existing Trends**

Before the switch, treatment branches were already gaining ground on control branches:

| Week | Treatment − Control Gap |
|------|------------------------|
| 6 | −0.83 |
| 10 | +1.06 |
| 14 | +2.51 |

The gap widened by **3.3 points during the pre-period alone**, before the new procedure was implemented. This suggests treatment branches were selected because they were already improving, or they differed systematically from control branches in ways that affected trends.

**3. What This Means for the Estimate**

| Scenario | Likely True Effect |
|----------|-------------------|
| If pre-trend continued unchanged | Effect could be **smaller than 2.2** (possibly near zero) |
| If pre-trend would have plateaued | Effect could be **close to 2.2** |
| If new procedure accelerated existing improvement | Effect could be **real but smaller than observed** |

**We cannot distinguish between these scenarios with the current data.**

**4. Encouraging Signs**

- The effect **grew over time** (weeks 19–22 showed larger gains than weeks 15–18)
- When excluding transition weeks (15–18), the effect is **larger** (2.9 points), not smaller
- The result is **statistically robust** across multiple model specifications

### **RECOMMENDATIONS**

1. **Do NOT approve immediate system-wide rollout** — the evidence is not strong enough to justify the cost and disruption

2. **Continue phased rollout** — proceed with introducing the procedure to additional branches, but in controlled batches

3. **Extend data collection** — gather 8–12 more weeks of data to see if effects persist and to better separate treatment effects from pre-existing trends

4. **Investigate selection criteria** — understand why certain branches were chosen for early adoption and whether they differed systematically from others

5. **Consider randomized assignment** for the next phase to enable cleaner causal inference

6. **Set a decision checkpoint** — reconvene in 8–12 weeks with additional data to make a final go/no-go decision on system-wide rollout

---

### **ANALYTICAL METHOD**

This analysis used a **difference-in-differences** design, which assumes treatment and control groups would have followed parallel trends absent the intervention. The pre-period data shows this assumption may not hold, which limits confidence in the causal estimate. All models controlled for branch-specific characteristics, week-specific factors, staffing levels, and visitor volume.
