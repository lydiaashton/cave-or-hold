# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure  
**Analysis Period:** Weeks 6–22 (9 weeks before, 8 weeks after switch)  
**Date:** [Current Date]

---

## **Bottom Line**

**We cannot conclude the new procedure improved service scores.** While branches using the new procedure achieved higher scores after the switch, this improvement was already underway before they switched. The data do not support a causal claim that the procedure itself caused the improvement.

---

## **Final Verdict**

> **❌ DO NOT PROCEED WITH COOPERATIVE-WIDE ROLLOUT**
>
> **Reason:** The evidence does not demonstrate that the new procedure causes improved service scores. The observed gains reflect pre-existing differences between branches that chose to switch versus those that did not—not the effect of the procedure itself.
>
> **Required before reconsideration:** A randomized controlled trial or other rigorous evaluation design that can isolate the procedure's effect from branch selection bias.

---

## **Key Findings**

### The Critical Problem: Pre-Existing Trends

| Period | Treatment Avg | Control Avg | Gap |
|--------|---------------|-------------|-----|
| Week 6 (study start) | 51.7 | 51.5 | **0.17** |
| Week 14 (before switch) | 54.8 | 52.8 | **2.07** |
| Week 22 (study end) | 58.6 | 55.2 | **3.40** |

**The gap grew by 1.9 points BEFORE the switch.** It grew by only 1.0 points AFTER. Treatment branches were already improving faster than control branches before adopting the new procedure.

### Two Different Estimates

| Analysis Method | Estimated Effect | Statistical Significance |
|-----------------|------------------|--------------------------|
| Simple comparison (ignores pre-trend) | +2.09 points | p < 0.001 |
| **Proper analysis (accounts for pre-trend)** | **+0.23 points** | **p = 0.52 (not significant)** |

---

## **Why the Initial Analysis Was Misleading**

The standard difference-in-differences method assumes treatment and control groups would have followed **parallel trends** without the intervention. This assumption is **violated** in our data:

- Treatment branches were gaining ~0.24 points/week on control branches **before** the switch
- This pre-existing trend explains most of the observed "effect"
- After accounting for it, the procedure itself shows **no detectable impact**

---

## **What This Means**

Possibilities consistent with the data:

1. The new procedure has **no effect** on service scores
2. The new procedure has a **small positive effect** (too small to detect with this data)
3. Branches that chose to switch were **already better performers** on an upward trajectory (selection bias)

**We cannot distinguish among these with the current data.**

---

## **Recommended Next Steps**

| Action | Timeline | Rationale |
|--------|----------|-----------|
| **Pause rollout** | Immediate | Current evidence does not support the investment |
| **Design randomized trial** | 4–6 weeks | Randomly assign branches to eliminate selection bias |
| **Gather qualitative feedback** | Ongoing | Interview staff about whether the procedure changes their work |
| **Reconvene Board** | After trial results | Make rollout decision based on rigorous evidence |

---

## **Conclusion**

**The data do not support rolling out the new procedure.** The observed improvement in service scores was already happening before branches switched. We cannot attribute the gain to the procedure itself.

Before investing in a cooperative-wide rollout, we need stronger evidence—ideally from a randomized evaluation where branches are randomly assigned to adopt (or not adopt) the new procedure.
