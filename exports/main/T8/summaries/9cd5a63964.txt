## **Final Verdict**

**Proceed with rollout, but temper expectations.**

The evidence supports moving forward with the new procedure for three reasons:

1. **No detected harm** – Service scores at switching branches did not decline relative to controls
2. **Positive association** – A consistent +2.0 point gap favors the new procedure, even if partly attributable to selection
3. **Operational momentum** – 425 branches have already adopted the procedure; reversing course would create confusion

However, the board should understand:

- **The true effect is likely smaller than 2.0 points** due to pre-existing differences between early-adopter and control branches
- **Some branches may see minimal gain** – the benefit may depend on implementation quality and branch characteristics
- **Continued evaluation is essential** – we should not assume the observed effect will replicate exactly

**Verdict: APPROVE with conditions** – Authorize the rollout, but require quarterly reporting on service score trends for newly switched branches to confirm the effect holds at scale.

*This analysis was conducted using standard difference-in-differences methodology. The violation of parallel pre-trends is a common limitation in observational studies of voluntary program adoption. A randomized rollout design would have provided stronger causal identification.*
