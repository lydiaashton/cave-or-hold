# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD OF DIRECTORS**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** [Current Date]  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Rotation Procedure  

---

## **Executive Summary**

The new procedure **likely improved service scores**, with an estimated effect of **+1.8 points** (statistically significant, p < 0.001). However, the effect is **modest in size** (~17% of a standard deviation), and we lack cost data to assess whether system-wide rollout is economically justified. 

**Recommendation: Proceed with a phased rollout while collecting cost data and monitoring outcomes.**

---

## **Bottom Line**

| Question | Answer | Confidence |
|----------|--------|------------|
| Did the new procedure improve service? | **Probably yes** | High |
| How large is the improvement? | **+1.8 points** (modest) | High |
| Is this statistically reliable? | **Yes** (CI: 1.3 to 2.4) | High |
| Should we expand to all branches? | **Conditionally yes** | Medium |

**Key caveat:** This recommendation assumes implementation costs are reasonable. If the new procedure requires significant additional staff time, training, or resources, the modest 1.8-point gain may not justify system-wide adoption.

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

> **The new procedure should be rolled out to remaining branches, BUT with important conditions:**
>
> 1. **Do it in phases** — not all 284 remaining branches at once
> 2. **Track costs** — we cannot complete a cost-benefit analysis without this data
> 3. **Set a review date** — reconvene in 6 months to confirm effects persist and weigh benefits against costs
> 4. **Be prepared to adjust** — if costs exceed expectations or effects diminish, the board should be ready to pause or modify the rollout
>
> **Why not a stronger recommendation?** The effect is real and positive, but modest. A 1.8-point improvement on a ~50-point scale is meaningful but not transformative. Without knowing what the new procedure costs in staff time, training, or materials, we cannot say whether this is the best use of library resources.
>
> **Why not a weaker recommendation?** The statistical evidence is solid. The 95% confidence interval excludes zero, the effect appears to be growing over time, and the analysis controls for branch differences and weekly fluctuations. There is no evidence of harm, and the trajectory suggests potential for larger gains as staff gain familiarity.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measured Impact

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Estimated treatment effect** | **+1.81 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | [1.27, 2.36] |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 |
| Effect size (Cohen's d) | 0.17 (small-to-moderate) |

### 2. Important Context

**The treatment and control groups were not perfectly comparable before the switch:**
- Early in the study (weeks 6-10), treatment branches had lower scores than control branches
- By weeks 12-14 (just before the switch), the groups had converged
- This convergence before the intervention is **encouraging** and supports the validity of our estimates

**The effect appears to be growing:**
| Period | Treatment-Control Gap |
|--------|----------------------|
| Weeks 15-16 | ~2.0 points |
| Weeks 17-18 | ~1.9 points |
| Weeks 19-20 | ~2.5 points |
| Weeks 21-22 | ~3.0 points |

This pattern suggests branches may continue to benefit as staff gain experience with the new procedure.

### 3. What 1.8 Points Means

- A branch averaging 50 points would improve to approximately **52 points**
- This represents about **3.6% improvement** above baseline
- In practical terms: noticeable but not transformative

---

## **Limitations & Uncertainties**

| Issue | Impact |
|-------|--------|
| **Non-random assignment** | Branches self-selected (or were selected) for the new procedure; unobserved differences may exist |
| **Pre-trend differences** | Groups differed in early weeks, though they converged before the switch |
| **Short post-period** | Only 8 weeks of data after the switch; long-term effects unknown |
| **No cost data** | Cannot assess whether benefits outweigh implementation costs |
| **Modest effect size** | 1.8 points is meaningful but not large |

---

## **Recommendations**

### Primary Recommendation: **Phased Rollout with Evaluation**

1. **Expand to additional branches** in phases (not all at once)

2. **Track implementation costs** (staff time, training, materials)

3. **Continue monitoring service scores** to confirm effects persist

4. **Set a decision checkpoint** after 3-6 months to evaluate:
   - Are effects consistent with our estimates?
   - Do benefits outweigh costs?
   - Are there branches struggling with implementation?

### Alternative: **Targeted Rollout**

If resources are limited, consider prioritizing:
- Branches with below-average service scores (most to gain)
- Branches with adequate staffing (best positioned to implement)

### If Costs Are High: **Reconsider**

If the new procedure requires substantial additional resources, the board may wish to:
- Limit adoption to willing branches
- Modify the procedure to reduce costs
- Invest in other service improvements with higher returns
