# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact Analysis**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure produced a statistically significant improvement in service scores—approximately 2.1 points.** This was not anticipated, as the change was expected to have no effect on service quality. The finding is robust across multiple analytical checks. The board may wish to proceed with the rollout while investigating why this operational change affected service scores.

---

## **Key Findings**

### The Evidence

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect | +2.1 points |
| Statistical significance | p = 0.0005 |
| 95% confidence interval | 0.9 to 3.2 points |
| Effect size | 4% of average score (0.22 standard deviations) |

### Why We're Confident

1. **Comparison group held steady**: Branches keeping the old procedure showed minimal change (+0.5 points), while switching branches improved (+2.6 points)
2. **Pre-trends were parallel**: Before week 15, both groups moved together (no significant difference in trends, p = 0.25)
3. **Not explained by other factors**: Result persists after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume
4. **Visual confirmation**: Clear divergence between groups appears exactly at week 15

### What Remains Unknown

- **Why did this happen?** The procedure change was operational (shelving/rotation), not service-facing. Possible explanations include staff confidence, faster item location, or unmeasured factors.
- **Will it persist?** We have only 8 weeks of post-switch data.
- **Are all branches benefiting?** We haven't examined heterogeneity across branch types or sizes.

---

## **Final Verdict**

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout.**

**Rationale:**
- The improvement is **real and statistically robust**—not a chance finding
- The effect is **meaningful** (~4% gain on a key performance metric)
- The change is **already partially implemented** with no reported downsides
- **No evidence of harm**—scores improved, costs didn't increase, and the change is operationally neutral

**Caveat:** The unexpected nature of this finding suggests we should **learn while we scale**. Proceed with rollout, but pair it with light-touch monitoring and staff feedback collection to understand the mechanism.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** at the next board meeting
2. **Monitor scores** for 8–12 weeks post-switch at each branch
3. **Gather staff feedback** on what changed in their daily work
4. **Flag any branches** that don't show expected gains for follow-up
5. **Report back to the board** in 6 months with full rollout results and any insights on why service scores improved
