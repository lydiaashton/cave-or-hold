# **Board Summary: Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

The evidence is **inconclusive**. The data suggests the new procedure may improve service scores by about 2 points (4%), but we cannot rule out that the observed difference is due to random variation. The study had limited statistical power due to the small number of branches (34) and short post-switch period (8 weeks).

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
| | |
|---|---|
| **Branches studied** | 34 total (20 adopted new procedure; 14 kept old) |
| **Study period** | 17 weeks (Weeks 6–22) |
| **Switch date** | Week 15 |
| **Analysis method** | Difference-in-differences with branch fixed effects |

### Results Summary

| Metric | Treatment (New Procedure) | Control (Old Procedure) |
|--------|---------------------------|-------------------------|
| Pre-switch average | 52.8 | 47.9 |
| Post-switch average | 53.5 | 46.7 |
| **Change** | **+0.70** | **-1.20** |

- **Estimated treatment effect: +2.03 points** (95% confidence interval: -0.47 to +4.53)
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.11 (not significant at the 5% level)
- **Branch-level outcomes:** 55% of treatment branches improved vs. 43% of control branches

### Important Context

The control group's decline (-1.20 points) suggests external factors negatively affected service scores during this period. The new procedure appears to have **protected adopting branches from this decline**, resulting in a net positive difference.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Extend the evaluation period** — Eight weeks post-switch may be insufficient. Continue collecting data for 3–6 more months to increase statistical power.

2. **Proceed with phased rollout** — Given the positive direction of results, consider gradual expansion while monitoring outcomes at each stage.

3. **Investigate variation across branches** — Treatment branch outcomes ranged from -7 to +6 points. Understanding which branches benefit most could inform targeted implementation.

4. **Verify implementation quality** — Ensure all adopting branches are correctly following the new procedure. Variation in fidelity could explain mixed results.

5. **Schedule re-evaluation** — Return to the board in 3–6 months with updated analysis and a recommendation on system-wide adoption.

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **Did the new procedure improve service scores?** | We cannot say with confidence. |
| **Is there evidence of harm?** | No — no branches showed dramatic declines attributable to the procedure. |
| **Should we roll out to all branches now?** | **No** — the evidence does not support a definitive system-wide decision. |
| **Should we abandon the procedure?** | **No** — the point estimate is positive, and there is no evidence of harm. |

**Recommended path forward:** Continue the new procedure at current branches, consider a limited expansion to additional willing branches, and collect 3–6 more months of data before making a final system-wide decision. This balances potential benefits against the risk of committing resources to an unproven change.

---

## **Conclusion**

The new procedure shows promise but requires more evidence before full commitment. A cautious, data-driven approach is warranted.
