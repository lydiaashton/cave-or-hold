**Subject:** Impact of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure on Service Scores

## **Executive Summary**

Branches that adopted the new stock rotation and shelving procedure showed higher service scores than branches that kept the old procedure. The difference is statistically significant but modest in magnitude. We recommend a measured rollout with monitoring.

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Service Scores Improved After the Switch

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Net Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|------------|
| Control (old procedure) | 51.2 | 50.2 | **-1.0** ↓ |

### 2. Statistical Evidence

- **Effect size:** +1.88 points (95% CI: 0.69 to 3.06)
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.002
- **Practical significance:** ~4% improvement on a ~50-point scale

### 3. Validation Checks

| Check | Result |
|-------|--------|
| Parallel pre-trends | ✓ Passed |
| Effect persistence | ✓ Consistent across 8 weeks |
| Spillover effects | ✓ None detected |

### 4. Variation Across Branches

- **Top performers:** Up to +9 points
- **Median treated branch:** +0.9 points
- **Some branches:** Minimal or no measurable gain

---

## **Final Verdict**

> **The new procedure should be rolled out, but expectations should be tempered.**
>
> The analysis provides credible evidence that the procedure improved service scores. The statistical signal is clear (p = 0.002), and the research design supports a causal interpretation. However, the effect is modest—less than 2 points on a 50-point scale—and varies substantially across branches.
>
> This is not a transformative change, but it is a real one. For an operational adjustment that requires no additional budget or staffing, a ~4% service improvement is worthwhile. The fact that control branches declined slightly while treated branches improved suggests the new procedure may also provide resilience against service degradation.
>
> **Recommendation:** Proceed with system-wide rollout, but invest in understanding why some branches benefited more than others. This could amplify gains and ensure all branches realize the benefit.
