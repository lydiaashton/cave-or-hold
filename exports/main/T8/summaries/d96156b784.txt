# Board Memorandum (Final)

---

## **Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Analysis Period:** Weeks 6–22  
**Branches Studied:** 709 (425 switched, 284 control)

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure helped branches maintain service quality during a system-wide decline.**

After week 15, service scores fell across the entire library system (average drop: -1.67 points). However, branches using the new procedure declined **much less** than those using the old procedure:

| Group | Score Change |
|-------|--------------|
| New Procedure | -0.80 points |
| Old Procedure | -2.96 points |
| **Difference** | **+2.16 points** |

The new procedure provided a **protective effect** equivalent to 2.16 service score points. This is statistically significant (p < 0.001) and suggests the new procedure makes branches more resilient to external pressures.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **The System-Wide Context**

Something affected all branches negatively after week 15. System-wide averages dropped from 53.08 to 51.41 points. This could reflect:
- Seasonal patterns (late summer/early fall)
- Staffing changes
- Budget constraints
- Other external factors

### **The New Procedure's Protective Effect**

| Measure | Value |
|---------|-------|
| Protective Effect | **+2.16 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | **+1.60 to +2.73 points** |
| Statistical Significance | **p < 0.001** |
| Relative Benefit | **73% less decline** vs. control |

### **Why This Matters**

1. **Resilience**: Branches with the new procedure lost less than one-third the service quality that control branches lost.

2. **Growing Benefit**: The protective effect strengthened over time, from ~1 point in week 15 to ~3.3 points by week 22.

3. **Valid Comparison**: Pre-switch trends were parallel between groups, confirming the effect is attributable to the procedure change.

---

## **Final Verdict**

### **✓ ROLL OUT THE NEW PROCEDURE SYSTEM-WIDE**

**Confidence Level: HIGH**

**Rationale:**
- The protective effect is **statistically robust** (p < 0.001, narrow confidence interval)
- The benefit is **operationally meaningful** (2+ points on a ~50-point scale)
- The effect **grows over time**, suggesting compounding benefits
- There is **no evidence of harm** — switched branches performed as well or better than control branches in all post-switch weeks

**Caveats:**
- This analysis shows the procedure helps during decline periods; we have not observed whether it improves scores during stable or growing periods
- The system-wide decline after week 15 warrants investigation independent of this decision

**Bottom Line:** Even if the new procedure only protects against future service declines, that resilience alone justifies system-wide adoption. The board should approve rollout.
