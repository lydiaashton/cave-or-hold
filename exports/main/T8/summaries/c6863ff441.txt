# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

## **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

**Date:** November 2025  
**To:** Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new shelving and stock rotation procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **2.1 points** (approximately **4% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This difference is statistically significant (p < 0.001).

The analysis found **no evidence** supporting the initial expectation that the change would make no difference. Instead, the data shows a clear, positive effect.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with system-wide rollout, with continued monitoring.

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| **Did the new procedure change service scores?** | **Yes** |
| **Was the change positive or negative?** | **Positive** |
| **Is the effect statistically reliable?** | **Yes** (p < 0.001) |
| **Should the cooperative adopt the new procedure?** | **Yes** |

**Verdict: The new procedure should be rolled out to all branches.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Statistically Significant Improvement
| | Control Branches | Treatment Branches |
|---|---|---|
| Before switch | 50.61 | 50.11 |
| After switch | 50.73 | 52.35 |
| **Change** | **+0.12** | **+2.23** |
| **Net Effect** | | **+2.1 points** |

- **95% Confidence Interval:** 0.9 to 3.4 points
- **P-value:** 0.0006 (highly significant)

### 2. Valid Comparison Confirmed
The analysis compared 90 treatment branches against 60 control branches. Critically, **pre-switch trends were parallel** — there were no systematic differences between the groups before the procedure change. This supports a causal interpretation.

### 3. Effect Is Consistent
All 8 weeks after the switch showed positive effects for treatment branches. Five of the 8 weeks showed individually significant improvements.

### 4. Robust to Other Factors
The result holds after accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume, suggesting the improvement is attributable to the procedure change itself.

---

## **Limitations**

| Limitation | Implication |
|---|---|
| **8-week observation window** | Long-term persistence is unknown; continued monitoring recommended |
| **Implementation assumed uniform** | Actual rollout may vary; training and support will matter |
| **Service score is one metric** | Consider complementary measures (cost, staff time, patron satisfaction) |

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve rollout** to remaining 60 branches
2. **Monitor service scores** for 8-12 weeks post-rollout
3. **Document implementation practices** from early adopters
4. **Review cost implications** if the new procedure has different resource requirements

---

## **Conclusion**

The data provides strong evidence that the new procedure improves service scores. While the initial expectation was no effect, the observed 4% improvement is both statistically significant and operationally meaningful. Proceeding with system-wide adoption is supported by the evidence.
