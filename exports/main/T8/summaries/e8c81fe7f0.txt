# **Board Summary: New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence is inconclusive.** The new procedure is associated with a 2-point improvement in service scores, but we cannot confidently rule out that the true effect is zero. The data do not provide statistically reliable evidence that the procedure caused the improvement.

---

## **Key Findings**

| Finding | Result |
|---------|--------|
| **Point estimate** | +2.3 points |
| **95% Confidence interval** | -0.6 to +5.3 points |
| **Statistical significance** | p = 0.12 (not significant) |
| **Branches in study** | 34 (20 switched, 14 control) |

### **What the Confidence Interval Means**

The data are compatible with **three different realities**:
1. **No effect** (true effect ≈ 0)
2. **Modest benefit** (true effect ≈ 2–3 points)
3. **Moderate benefit** (true effect ≈ 5 points)

We cannot distinguish among these possibilities with the current data.

### **Important Caveat: Pre-Existing Differences**

Treatment and control branches had **different service score trajectories before the switch**. This violates a key assumption of our analysis method and means the 2.3-point estimate may not reflect a true causal effect. Some of the apparent improvement could be due to pre-existing differences between branches that chose (or were chosen) to switch.

---

## **Final Verdict**

**Do not proceed with systemwide rollout at this time. Extend the pilot.**

### **Reasoning**

| Consideration | Assessment |
|--------------|------------|
| **Statistical evidence** | Insufficient to confirm benefit |
| **Risk of harm** | Low (no negative signal detected) |
| **Cost of waiting** | Modest (continued pilot operations) |
| **Cost of wrong decision** | Systemwide change based on uncertain evidence |

### **My Professional Judgment**

As the analyst responsible for this evaluation, I cannot endorse a systemwide rollout based on the current evidence. The parallel trends violation is a serious methodological concern that undermines causal claims. Rolling out now would mean making a cooperative-wide decision on the basis of a pattern that may reflect pre-existing branch differences rather than the procedure itself.

**Extending the pilot by 8–12 weeks is the responsible course.** It costs little and could provide the clarity needed for a confident decision.

---

## **Recommended Action Plan**

| Step | Action | Timeline |
|------|--------|----------|
| 1 | Continue current pilot (no expansion) | Immediate |
| 2 | Collect 8–12 additional weeks of data | Weeks 23–34 |
| 3 | Re-analyze with full dataset | Week 35 |
| 4 | Return to board with definitive recommendation | Week 36 |

### **What We Need to See for Rollout Approval**

- Statistically significant effect (p < 0.05)
- Confidence interval entirely above zero
- No evidence of continued pre-trend divergence

---

## **Why We Can't Be Certain**

1. **Sample size is limited**: 34 branches over 17 weeks provides modest statistical power
2. **Pre-trend differences**: Switching and non-switching branches behaved differently before the intervention
3. **External factors**: Staffing and visitation patterns vary week-to-week and may confound the results

---

## **Summary for Decision-Makers**

> *"The new procedure may improve service scores, but the current data cannot confirm this. Extending the pilot is the most defensible path forward. If operational pressures require immediate action, proceed with rollout only alongside strict monitoring and explicit reversal triggers."*
