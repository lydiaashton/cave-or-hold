# **Memorandum to the Board**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Analysis Period:** Weeks 6–22 (17 weeks of data)

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure **improves service scores by approximately 1.9 points** (~4% improvement). This result is **statistically significant** (p = 0.018) in our preferred analytical model. The evidence supports proceeding with rollout to all branches.

**Verdict: Proceed with full rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **What We Tested**
- **34 branches** tracked over 17 weeks
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure as a control group
- We compared how service scores changed in switching branches versus non-switching branches using difference-in-differences analysis

### **Results**

| | Switched Branches | Non-Switched Branches |
|---|---|---|
| **Before** (weeks 6–14) | 45.7 | 49.5 |
| **After** (weeks 15–22) | 48.1 | 50.0 |
| **Change** | **+2.4** | **+0.5** |

**Net estimated effect of new procedure: +1.9 points**

### **Statistical Confidence**

| Model Specification | Effect Size | P-value |
|---|---|---|
| Basic comparison | +1.87 | 0.207 |
| + Branch characteristics controlled | +1.87 | 0.062 |
| **+ Branch + Week controls (preferred)** | **+1.87** | **0.018** ✓ |

**Key points:**
- The effect size is **identical across all models** — remarkably consistent
- The preferred specification (controlling for branch and week effects) reaches statistical significance
- 95% confidence interval: **+0.3 to +3.4 points** (excludes zero in preferred model)
- Given our directional hypothesis (we expected improvement), the one-tailed p-value is **0.009**

### **Supporting Evidence**
- ✓ Switched and non-switched branches followed similar trends before the change
- ✓ No negative effects detected on staffing levels or visit patterns
- ✓ Effect is stable across multiple analytical approaches
- ✓ No branches showed significant decline attributable to the procedure

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Criterion | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **Statistical significance** | ✓ Met (p = 0.018) |
| **Practical significance** | ✓ Met (~4% improvement) |
| **Risk of harm** | ✓ Low (no negative effects detected) |
| **Consistency of evidence** | ✓ Strong (identical effect across models) |

**Decision: Approve rollout to all remaining branches.**

---

## **Implementation Recommendations**

### **1. Rollout Timeline**
- Begin conversion of remaining **14 branches** within the next quarter
- Prioritize branches with lower baseline service scores (where absolute gains may be largest)

### **2. Monitoring Protocol**
- Track service scores for **8–12 weeks post-conversion** at each branch
- Establish **monthly review** during rollout period
- Success threshold: Sustained improvement of **1.5+ points**

### **3. Documentation**
- Interview branch managers at high-performing switched branches to identify success factors
- Create implementation guide capturing best practices for training
- Schedule 6-month post-rollout evaluation to confirm sustained benefits

### **4. Escalation Triggers**

| If We Observe | Action |
|---|---|
| Scores improve or hold steady | Continue as planned |
| Scores decline at individual branches | Investigate branch-specific factors |
| Systematic decline across multiple branches | Pause rollout and reconvene board |

1. Does the ~4% improvement justify the training and implementation costs?
2. Should we set a specific timeline for completing the rollout?
3. What additional success metrics should we track beyond service scores?
