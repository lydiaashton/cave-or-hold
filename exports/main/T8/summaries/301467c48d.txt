# **Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure Evaluation**

**Date:** November 2026  
**Analysis Period:** Weeks 6–22 (17 weeks)

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure shows **promising but inconclusive evidence** of improvement. The estimated effect is positive (+1.6 points), but the data do not rule out the possibility of no effect or even a small negative effect. There is approximately an **87% probability the true effect is positive**, but this falls short of the conventional 95% confidence threshold. We recommend a **phased rollout with explicit continuation criteria** rather than immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Final Verdict**

> **PROCEED WITH CAUTION: Approve phased rollout, require follow-up review.**
>
> The evidence leans positive but is not definitive. Move forward with the new procedure, but build in checkpoints to verify the benefit materializes before full commitment.

---

## **Key Findings**

### The Evidence

| Metric | Old Procedure | New Procedure |
|--------|---------------|---------------|
| Branches | 14 | 20 |
| Average score before switch | 48.1 | 49.2 |
| Average score after switch | 47.5 | 50.2 |
| **Change** | **-0.6** | **+1.0** |

**Estimated effect: +1.6 service score points**

### What the Statistics Actually Tell Us

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Is the effect positive? | Likely, but not certain |
| Could the true effect be zero? | Yes (within confidence interval) |
| Could the true effect be negative? | Yes, though unlikely (~7% probability) |
| Is this statistically significant? | No (p = 0.13; threshold is typically 0.05) |
| Should we be concerned about harm? | Low concern, but cannot be ruled out |

### 95% Confidence Interval: **-0.5 to +3.6 points**

This means: If we repeated this study many times, 95% of the time the true effect would fall in this range. **Zero is inside this range**, so we cannot confidently exclude "no effect."

---

## **Recommendation**

**Approve a phased rollout to remaining branches with built-in evaluation checkpoints.**

**Rationale:**
- The preponderance of evidence (~87% probability) suggests a positive effect
- No branch showed dramatic deterioration after switching
- The potential upside (+3+ points) outweighs the limited downside risk
- However, statistical uncertainty warrants caution before full commitment

**Phased Rollout Plan:**

| Phase | Branches | Timeline | Decision Point |
|-------|----------|----------|----------------|
| 1 | 5 additional branches | Months 1–3 | Continue if no score decline |
| 2 | Remaining 9 branches | Months 4–6 | Continue if trend remains positive |
| 3 | System-wide review | Month 12 | Full adoption or revision |

**Continuation Criteria:**
- Proceed to next phase if average scores do not decline by more than 1 point
- Halt and reassess if system-wide scores drop by 2+ points
- Revisit board at Month 12 with full-year data for final adoption decision

---

## **Why Not Full Immediate Rollout?**

1. **Statistical uncertainty:** We lack conclusive evidence the procedure works
2. **Implementation costs:** Training and transition require resources
3. **Reversibility:** A phased approach allows course correction if problems emerge
4. **Learning opportunity:** Early adopters can identify best practices to share

---

## **Why Not Wait for More Data?**

1. **Opportunity cost:** 20 branches are already using the new procedure; delaying denies potential benefits to the remaining 14
2. **Direction is clear:** All point estimates favor the new procedure
3. **Low risk of harm:** No evidence of significant negative impacts
4. **Practical significance:** Even a 1–2 point improvement matters for service quality
