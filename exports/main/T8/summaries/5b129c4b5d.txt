# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure  
**Date:** November 15, 2026

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure produced a **modest improvement in service scores** (+1.6 points, approximately 3%), but the effect is **not statistically significant at conventional levels**. The evidence points in a positive direction, but we cannot confidently rule out that the observed improvement occurred by chance.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **phased rollout** to remaining branches while continuing to monitor outcomes. Do not accelerate to full implementation until we have additional data.

---

## **Final Verdict**

**Proceed with caution, but proceed.**

The data shows a **positive signal without definitive proof**. For a library service decision of this nature, this warrants continued implementation with monitoring rather than stopping or accelerating. Specifically:

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did the procedure improve service scores? | **Probably, but we're not certain** |
| Is the improvement large enough to matter? | **Yes, if real** (3% is meaningful for service quality) |
| Should we roll out to all branches immediately? | **No** — wait for more evidence |
| Should we stop the rollout? | **No** — the signal is positive, not negative |
| What's the risk of proceeding? | **Moderate** — we may invest in a procedure with no real effect |
| What's the risk of stopping? | **Moderate** — we may abandon a procedure that does help |

**My professional judgment:** The balance tips toward **continuing the rollout** because (1) the effect is in the expected direction, (2) the magnitude would be worthwhile if real, (3) there's no evidence of harm, and (4) library service improvements typically justify moderate uncertainty. However, this should be treated as a **continuing evaluation**, not a final endorsement.

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22 of the year)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **14 branches** maintained the old procedure as a control group
- Analysis compares service score changes between the two groups before and after the switch

### Impact on Service Scores

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Control branches (no switch) | 54.3 | 53.1 | −1.2 |
| Treatment branches (switched) | 48.9 | 49.3 | +0.4 |
| **Net Effect of New Procedure** | | | **+1.6** |

- The new procedure is associated with a **+1.6 point increase** in service scores (approximately **3% improvement**)
- **Statistical confidence:** p = 0.15 (not significant at the standard 5% threshold)
- **95% confidence interval:** −0.6 to +3.8 points (the true effect could be slightly negative or as high as +3.8)

### Validity Checks Passed
✓ **Parallel trends confirmed:** Before the switch, both groups showed similar weekly trends, supporting a valid comparison  
✓ **No confounding factors:** Staffing levels and visitor volumes did not change differently between groups after the switch  
✓ **Effect persists** after controlling for branch characteristics and other weekly measures

### Important Context
- Branches selected for the new procedure had **lower baseline service scores** (48.9 vs. 54.3), suggesting they were chosen because they had more room for improvement
- The analysis accounts for this difference, but it means results may not generalize equally to higher-performing branches
