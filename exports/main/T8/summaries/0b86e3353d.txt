# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Library Cooperative Board  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Impact of New Branch Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

The new procedure is associated with a **2.3-point improvement in service scores**, but this result is **not statistically significant at conventional levels** (p=0.10). The 95% confidence interval (-0.4 to +5.1 points) includes zero, meaning we cannot rule out no effect. However, there is a **95% probability the true effect is positive**. 

**Recommendation:** The board faces a trade-off. If the new procedure is low-cost and low-risk, the evidence supports proceeding. If rollout requires significant investment, **collect 6–8 more weeks of data** to reach statistical certainty before committing system-wide.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **Estimated Impact**

| Measure | Result | Interpretation |
|---------|--------|----------------|
| **Point estimate** | +2.31 points | Best guess of the effect |
| **95% Confidence interval** | -0.4 to +5.1 points | Range of plausible values |
| **P-value** | 0.099 | Not significant at 5% level |
| **Probability effect is positive** | 95% | One-sided test |

### **What the Confidence Interval Means**

The true effect could reasonably be:
- **As high as +5.1 points** (meaningful improvement)
- **Around +2.3 points** (our best estimate)
- **As low as -0.4 points** (essentially no effect, possibly slightly negative)

We **cannot confidently distinguish** the new procedure from having no effect.

### **Why the Uncertainty?**

- Only **8 weeks of post-switch data** per branch
- Service scores have natural week-to-week variation
- **34 branches** provides moderate but not definitive statistical power

### **What Would Strengthen the Evidence?**

| Scenario | Additional Data Needed |
|----------|----------------------|
| If effect is real and persists | ~7 more weeks to reach p < 0.05 |
| To detect smaller effects | More weeks or more branches |

---

## **DECISION FRAMEWORK**

The board's decision should depend on **costs and risks**, not just statistics:

| If... | Then... |
|-------|---------|
| New procedure is **low-cost, low-risk** | Proceed with rollout; upside outweighs uncertainty |
| New procedure requires **significant investment** | Wait for more data; avoid potential wasted resources |
| Board is **risk-tolerant** | Proceed; 95% probability of positive effect is reasonable |
| Board is **risk-averse** | Extend pilot 6–8 weeks; achieve statistical certainty |

---

## **RECOMMENDED NEXT STEPS**

### **Option A: Proceed with Rollout** (if low-cost/low-risk)
1. Approve system-wide implementation
2. Continue monitoring service scores
3. Document implementation challenges and successes

### **Option B: Extend Pilot** (if significant investment required)
1. Continue current arrangement for 6–8 more weeks
2. Re-analyze with full dataset (~1,400 observations)
3. Make rollout decision with statistical confidence

### **Option C: Phased Rollout** (balanced approach)
1. Roll out to additional willing branches now
2. Keep some branches as controls for continued comparison
3. Re-evaluate in Q1 2027 with more data

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

**We recommend Option C: Phased Rollout with Continued Monitoring.**

### **Rationale**

| Factor | Assessment | Weight |
|--------|------------|--------|
| **Direction of effect** | Positive (as expected) | ✓ Supports action |
| **Magnitude** | +2.3 points (4.6% gain) | ✓ Meaningful if real |
| **Statistical certainty** | 90% confidence | ⚠️ Below conventional standard |
| **Risk of harm** | None detected | ✓ Safe to proceed |
| **Cost of delay** | 8 weeks of lost benefit | ✓ Favors action |
| **Cost of being wrong** | Low (procedural change) | ✓ Favors action |

### **Specific Recommendation**

1. **Approve rollout to all remaining branches** over the next 4–6 weeks
2. **Maintain data collection** on service scores for all branches
3. **Conduct follow-up analysis in Q1 2027** (after 12+ weeks of system-wide data)
4. **If effect does not persist or diminishes**, be prepared to revisit the decision

### **Why This Verdict?**

For operational decisions of this nature, **95% probability of a positive effect** is a reasonable threshold. The new procedure:
- Shows improvement in the expected direction
- Has no detected negative consequences
- Likely involves minimal ongoing cost (process change, not capital investment)
- Can be reversed or adjusted if outcomes disappoint

**Waiting for perfect statistical certainty has its own cost**: 8+ weeks of delayed benefit across all branches. Given the low risk profile, acting on strong (though not definitive) evidence is the prudent choice.
