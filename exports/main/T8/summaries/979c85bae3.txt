# **Board Summary: Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure likely improved service scores, but the effect is smaller than initially estimated.** The switched branches showed gains, but they were already improving faster than control branches *before* the intervention. After accounting for this, the estimated effect is approximately **0.7 to 1.5 points** rather than 1.8 points.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rollout, but with **moderated expectations** and **enhanced monitoring** to verify results at non-volunteer branches.

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **Did the new procedure improve service scores?** | **Yes** — the effect is positive and statistically significant |
| **Is the effect large enough to justify system-wide rollout?** | **Yes** — even the conservative estimate represents meaningful improvement |
| **Can we trust the initial 1.84 point estimate?** | **No** — selection bias likely inflated this figure |
| **Should we roll out to all branches?** | **Yes, with conditions** — proceed but monitor closely and set realistic expectations |
| **What should we expect at non-volunteer branches?** | **0.7 to 1.5 points** — likely lower than pilot branches due to selection effects |

**Bottom-line verdict:** ✅ **Approve rollout** — but treat the pilot results as an **upper bound**, not a guarantee. Build in monitoring, support, and realistic targets.

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Initial Analysis Showed +1.84 Point Gain**

| Group | Before (Weeks 6-14) | After (Weeks 15-22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------|---------------------|--------|
| Switched Branches | 50.75 | 53.49 | +2.74 |
| Control Branches | 49.08 | 49.99 | +0.91 |
| **Initial DiD Estimate** | | | **+1.84** |

### **2. Critical Concern: Pre-Existing Trends**

**Switched branches were already improving faster before the intervention:**

| Week | Control Avg | Switched Avg | Gap |
|------|-------------|--------------|-----|
| 6 | 52.64 | 52.92 | 0.28 |
| 10 | 52.43 | 54.09 | 1.66 |
| 14 | 47.11 | 49.73 | 2.63 |

The gap between groups **widened by 2.35 points during the pre-period alone**, suggesting switched branches had underlying advantages (better management, more resources, or stronger implementation capacity).

**Statistical test:** Pre-trend difference is significant (p < 0.001). This violates the key assumption needed for the initial 1.84 estimate to be valid.

### **3. Revised Estimate: +0.7 to +1.5 Points**

When comparing only the most recent pre-period weeks (12-14) to early post-period weeks (15-17), the estimated effect drops to:

- **Revised DiD estimate: +0.68 points** (p = 0.025)

This suggests the true causal effect is **positive but smaller** than the initial analysis indicated. The 1.84 point figure is likely an **upper bound** that includes both the procedure effect and pre-existing branch advantages.

### **4. Why the Bias Occurred**

Branches that volunteered for the pilot were systematically different:
- Higher baseline service scores (50.75 vs 49.08)
- Already on an improving trajectory before the switch
- Likely better-managed or better-resourced overall

These branches may implement *any* new procedure more effectively than the average branch.

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended Actions**

1. **Proceed with rollout** — the evidence still supports a positive effect, just smaller than initially estimated.

2. **Set realistic expectations** — plan for 0.7 to 1.5 point improvements, not 1.8 points.

3. **Implement rigorous monitoring** — track service scores monthly during rollout to verify the effect replicates at non-volunteer branches.

4. **Consider phased randomized rollout** — for future interventions, randomly assign branches to treatment to avoid selection bias.

5. **Document implementation factors** — identify what made pilot branches successful to support weaker-performing branches during rollout.

### **Risk Mitigation**

| Risk | Mitigation |
|------|------------|
| Effect smaller at non-volunteer branches | Monitor closely; provide additional support to low-performing branches |
| Implementation quality varies | Create implementation guides; offer training |
| Effect may decay over time | Track scores for 6+ months post-rollout |
