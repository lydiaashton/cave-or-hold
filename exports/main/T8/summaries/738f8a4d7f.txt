# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Prepared by:** Data Analysis Office

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The evidence is mixed.** While switched branches show higher scores after the change, **these branches were already improving relative to others BEFORE the switch**. The estimated 2-point benefit is likely overstated. **We recommend a cautious, monitored expansion rather than immediate full rollout.**

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Does the procedure show promise?** | ✓ Yes |
| **Is the evidence conclusive?** | ✗ No |
| **Recommend full rollout now?** | ✗ No |
| **Recommend continued testing?** | ✓ Yes |

**Decision:** **Proceed with phased expansion, not full rollout.** The procedure may be beneficial, but we cannot confidently separate its effect from pre-existing differences between branches. Expand to 50-100 additional branches with rigorous monitoring before committing system-wide.

---

## **WHAT WE FOUND**

### The Concerning Pattern

| Period | Treatment vs. Control Gap |
|--------|--------------------------|
| Weeks 6-8 (early baseline) | Treatment **0.22 points LOWER** |
| Weeks 13-14 (just before switch) | Treatment **1.65 points HIGHER** |
| Weeks 15-22 (after switch) | Treatment **2.05 points HIGHER** |

**Critical issue:** The gap widened by **1.87 points BEFORE the procedure changed**. This suggests switched branches were already on a different trajectory.

### What This Means

| Possible Explanation | Implication |
|---------------------|-------------|
| Branches self-selected to switch | Better-performing or more motivated branches may have opted in |
| Switched branches got extra attention | Staff training or management focus before week 15 could explain early gains |
| The procedure itself works | Some of the post-switch improvement may be real, but we can't isolate how much |

### Revised Estimate

If we account for the pre-existing divergence, the **true effect of the procedure is uncertain**—it could be:
- **Smaller than 2 points** (if pre-trends continued)
- **Near zero** (if the entire gap was pre-existing momentum)
- **Still positive** (if the procedure added to existing gains)

---

## **WHAT WE CAN SAY WITH CONFIDENCE**

✓ Switched branches have higher scores than non-switched branches  
✓ The gap increased after week 15  
✓ The new procedure did not harm service scores  

## **WHAT WE CANNOT SAY WITH CONFIDENCE**

✗ The procedure *caused* the improvement  
✗ The effect size is truly 2 points  
✗ All branches would see the same benefit  

---

## **REVISED RECOMMENDATIONS**

| Priority | Action |
|----------|--------|
| **1** | **Pause full rollout** until we better understand the pre-trend issue |
| **2** | **Investigate why** switched branches were diverging before week 15 (interview branch managers, review implementation timeline) |
| **3** | **Expand to 50-100 additional branches** with careful monitoring as a second-phase pilot |
| **4** | **Consider random assignment** for future procedure changes to enable cleaner evaluation |
| **5** | **Continue tracking** all branches for another 8-12 weeks to see if the gap stabilizes or grows |

---

## **QUESTIONS FOR THE BOARD**

1. Why did some branches switch and others not? Was this voluntary or assigned?
2. Did switched branches receive additional training or resources before week 15?
3. Is a 2-point improvement (if real) worth the cost of system-wide implementation?
4. Should we invest in a more rigorous trial before committing to full rollout?

---

**Bottom line:** The new procedure shows promise, but we cannot confidently attribute the improvement to the procedure itself. A cautious, evidence-building approach is warranted before system-wide commitment.
