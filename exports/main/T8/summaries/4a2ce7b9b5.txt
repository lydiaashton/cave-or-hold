# **Analysis of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure: Impact on Service Scores**

---

## **Bottom Line**

Branches that adopted the new procedure showed **modest but statistically significant improvements** in service scores (+1.8 points, p=0.001). The analysis passes key validity checks, though branches self-selected into the new procedure rather than being randomly assigned.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rollout, but monitor outcomes closely.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Observed Improvement
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect | +1.8 points |
| 95% Confidence interval | 0.8 to 2.9 points |
| Statistical significance | p = 0.001 |
| Relative improvement | 3.6% above average |

### 2. Important Limitation
Branches that switched had **higher service scores even before the switch** (average 3.5 points higher). While our analysis controls for this baseline difference, unobserved factors (e.g., management quality, staff motivation) could partially explain the improvement.

### 3. Parallel Trends Supported
Pre-switch trends were statistically similar between groups (p=0.358), which supports the validity of the comparison method.

### 4. Effect Appears Stable
Improvements persisted across all 8 weeks post-implementation without fading.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with phased rollout** to remaining branches over the next quarter.

2. **Track service scores** for 8–12 weeks post-rollout to confirm effects persist at scale.

3. **Gather staff feedback** on practical benefits and implementation challenges.

4. **Review implementation costs** against the modest but real service improvement.

5. **Consider randomized evaluation** for future procedural changes to strengthen causal evidence.

---

## **Final Verdict**

**Approve the rollout with monitored implementation.**

The evidence indicates the new procedure likely produces a real, positive effect on service scores. While the study design has limitations (non-random assignment), the analysis passes standard validity checks and the effect is consistent across all post-implementation weeks. The improvement is modest but meaningful for a service metric, and there is no evidence of harm. Given the cooperative's investment in developing this procedure and the absence of negative findings, proceeding with rollout is justified—provided the board commits to ongoing monitoring to confirm the effect holds at scale.

---

*Analysis based on 2,550 weekly observations from 150 branches. Key limitation: non-random assignment to treatment.*
