# MEMORANDUM

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## Bottom Line

**Recommendation: Proceed with caution. The new procedure is associated with improved service scores, but we cannot confidently attribute the full effect to the procedure itself.**

Branches that adopted the new procedure showed service score gains of approximately **2 points** after the switch. However, these branches were already improving relative to control branches *before* the switch, which weakens our ability to claim the procedure caused the improvement. We recommend a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than immediate system-wide adoption.

---

## Key Findings

### Study Overview
| | |
|---|---|
| **Branches studied** | 709 total (425 treatment, 284 control) |
| **Time period** | 17 weeks (weeks 6-22) |
| **Switch date** | Week 15 |
| **Method** | Difference-in-differences with branch and week controls |

### Results Summary

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| **Observed service score improvement** | +2.05 points |
| **95% Confidence interval** | +1.70 to +2.40 points |
| **Statistical significance** | p < 0.001 |
| **Effect size** | 0.2 standard deviations (modest) |

### Important Caveats

1. **Pre-existing trends differ.** Treatment branches were already converging toward control branches before the switch (pre-trends test: p < 0.001). This suggests the branches selected for the new procedure may have been on an improvement trajectory regardless.

2. **Possible selection bias.** Treatment branches started about 2.8 points lower than control branches. They may have been chosen for the new procedure *because* they needed improvement, making it difficult to separate the procedure effect from natural regression to the mean.

3. **Modest practical impact.** While statistically significant, the 2-point gain represents less than one-fifth of a standard deviation in service scores.

4. **Effect grew over time.** Post-switch effects ranged from +0.3 points (week 15) to +1.8 points (week 22), suggesting either a learning curve or continued convergence.

### What We Can and Cannot Conclude

| We CAN say | We CANNOT say |
|---|---|
| Treatment branches improved relative to control after the switch | The procedure *caused* the full 2-point improvement |
| The association is statistically robust | The effect would replicate at all 284 remaining branches |
| No negative effects were observed | The investment in rollout will pay for itself |

---

## Next Steps

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Pilot at 50-100 additional branches** | Gather more evidence before full commitment |
| 2 | **Randomize selection for next phase** | Eliminate selection bias for cleaner evaluation |
| 3 | **Track implementation costs** | Compare benefits against training and transition expenses |
| 4 | **Monitor for 8-12 weeks post-switch** | Distinguish learning effects from sustained gains |
| 5 | **Reconvene board in Q1** | Decide on full rollout based on pilot results |

---

## Final Verdict

**The evidence is suggestive but not conclusive.** 

The new procedure shows a positive association with service scores, and there is no indication of harm. However, the failed pre-trends test means we cannot rule out that the observed improvement reflects branch selection or natural convergence rather than the procedure itself.

**Our verdict: Conditionally favorable, with verification required.**

If the board proceeds with rollout, we strongly recommend:
1. **Random assignment** of the next cohort to enable causal inference
2. **Cost tracking** to ensure benefits exceed implementation expenses
3. **A stop-loss checkpoint** after 8 weeks to confirm effects materialize as expected

This approach balances the opportunity to improve member experience against the risk of investing in a procedure whose true effect remains uncertain.
