# **Analysis of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence does not support rolling out the new procedure.** While the basic analysis shows a 2-point improvement, deeper examination reveals the treatment and control branches had systematically different service patterns before the switch. The post-switch effects are volatile and inconsistent. **We recommend pausing the rollout and conducting further investigation.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. **Initial Results Are Misleading**
- Simple difference-in-differences suggests a 2.04-point improvement (p=0.002)
- **However**, this estimate is likely biased due to pre-existing differences between groups

### 2. **Parallel Trends Assumption Violated**
- Treatment branches had significantly different service scores than control branches in 5 of 8 pre-switch weeks
- Week 8 showed an 8-point gap between groups before any switch occurred
- This means we cannot confidently attribute post-switch differences to the procedure change

### 3. **Post-Switch Effects Are Inconsistent**
| Week | Effect | Significant? |
|------|--------|--------------|
| 15 | +3.56 | Yes |
| 16 | -0.19 | No |
| 17 | +3.35 | Yes |
| 18 | -1.90 | Yes (negative) |
| 19 | +3.00 | Yes |
| 20 | -2.89 | Yes (negative) |
| 21 | +1.87 | Marginal |
| 22 | -2.35 | Yes (negative) |

- Effects alternate between positive and negative
- No sustained improvement pattern

### 4. **Possible Explanations**
- Treatment branches may differ systematically from control branches (size, location, staffing, patron demographics)
- Week-to-week volatility may reflect external factors (holidays, local events, staffing changes)
- The procedure may have no real effect on service scores

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Do not proceed with full rollout** until evidence is clearer
2. **Investigate branch characteristics**: Compare treatment and control branches on size, location, staffing, and patron demographics
3. **Consider a randomized rollout**: If feasible, randomly assign remaining branches to treatment/control to ensure valid comparison
4. **Gather qualitative data**: Interview staff at switched branches about their experience with the new procedure
5. **Extend the observation period**: Collect more post-switch data to see if patterns stabilize

---

## **Final Verdict**

**DO NOT APPROVE FULL ROLLOUT**

The current evidence is insufficient to conclude that the new procedure improves service scores. The apparent 2-point gain is confounded by pre-existing differences between treatment and control branches. Post-switch effects show no consistent pattern, alternating between positive and negative week to week.

**To move forward, the board should:**
1. **Halt the rollout** pending further analysis
2. **Request a redesigned evaluation** with proper randomization or better-matched comparison groups
3. **Reconvene in 60–90 days** after additional data collection and analysis

Approving a system-wide rollout based on this evidence would risk implementing a change that may have no benefit—or could even harm service quality at some branches—while incurring unnecessary implementation costs.
