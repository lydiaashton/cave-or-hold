# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

---

## **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

**Date:** November 2026  
**Prepared by:** Data Analysis Team

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**Recommendation: Proceed with phased rollout, with monitoring.**

The new procedure **likely improves service scores**, but the magnitude is uncertain due to methodological limitations in the data. After accounting for pre-existing differences between branches, the **adjusted effect is approximately 0.4 to 2.1 points**. Given that no negative effects were detected and the potential benefit is real, we recommend expansion with careful monitoring rather than waiting for additional evidence.

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **Did the new procedure improve service scores?** | **Yes, probably** — all estimates are positive |
| **How large is the effect?** | **Uncertain** — between 0.4 and 2.1 points |
| **Is there risk of harm?** | **Low** — no negative effects detected in any analysis |
| **Should we roll out to all branches?** | **Yes, but phased** — expand in waves with monitoring |

**Rationale:** The cost of waiting (delayed improvements at 284 branches) likely exceeds the cost of a modest overestimate. A phased rollout allows us to gather better evidence while capturing benefits.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **1. Positive Effect, But Uncertain Magnitude**

| Estimate Method | Effect Size | Notes |
|-----------------|-------------|-------|
| Standard DiD | +2.12 points | Assumes parallel trends |
| Trend-Adjusted | +0.40 points | Accounts for pre-existing trends |
| Matched Comparison | +2.19 points | Controls for baseline levels |

**The true effect likely falls between 0.4 and 2.1 points.**

### **2. Critical Limitation: Pre-Existing Trends**

Branches selected for the new procedure were **already improving faster** than other branches before the switch:

| Group | Pre-Switch Weekly Trend |
|-------|------------------------|
| Treatment branches | −0.22 points/week |
| Control branches | −0.43 points/week |
| **Difference** | **+0.21 points/week** |

This means treatment branches were gaining ~0.21 points per week relative to control **even before the intervention**. Over 8 weeks, this pre-existing trend alone would widen the gap by ~1.7 points—accounting for much of the observed 2.1-point difference.

### **3. Effect Appears to Grow Over Time**

| Weeks Since Switch | Observed Effect |
|-------------------|-----------------|
| Week 1 | +0.94 points |
| Week 4 | +1.76 points |
| Week 8 | +3.33 points |

This pattern is encouraging—it suggests branches may still be learning to maximize benefits. However, it could also reflect continued divergence of pre-existing trends.

### **4. No Negative Effects Detected**

- Service scores did not decline for any group
- Effects were positive across branches with different baseline performance levels
- No adverse patterns in staffing or visitation metrics

---

## **RECOMMENDATION**

**Proceed with phased rollout to remaining branches.**

| Phase | Branches | Timeline | Go/No-Go Criteria |
|-------|----------|----------|-------------------|
| **Phase 1** | 50–75 branches | Weeks 1–8 | Proceed if avg improvement ≥0.5 points |
| **Phase 2** | 100–150 branches | Weeks 9–16 | Proceed if avg improvement ≥0.5 points |
| **Phase 3** | Remaining branches | Weeks 17+ | Full rollout |

**Why phased rather than immediate?**
- Allows detection of any unexpected negative effects
- Provides better data for estimating true effect size
- Enables refinement of training and implementation

**Why not wait for more evidence?**
- 284 branches are currently not using a likely-beneficial procedure
- Opportunity cost of delay is real
- No safety concerns identified

## **ABOUT THIS ANALYSIS**

**Data:** 12,053 weekly observations from 709 branches (weeks 6–22)  
**Method:** Difference-in-differences with cluster-robust standard errors  
**Key Limitation:** The parallel trends assumption is violated—treatment branches were already on a more favorable trajectory before the switch. This means the standard DiD estimate likely **overstates** the causal effect.

**What would strengthen future evaluations:**
- Random assignment of branches to procedure type
- Longer pre-intervention baseline period
- Additional outcome measures beyond service scores
