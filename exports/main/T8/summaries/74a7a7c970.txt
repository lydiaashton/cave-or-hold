# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**The data shows a statistically detectable difference, but the practical significance is uncertain.** Branches using the new procedure showed service scores approximately **2 points higher** (about 4%) than would be expected based on control branch trends. However, given that no effect was anticipated, the board should consider whether this represents a genuine operational improvement or an unexplained anomaly before committing to system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measured Effect
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect | +1.99 points |
| 95% Confidence interval | +0.76 to +3.21 points |
| Statistical significance | p = 0.002 |
| Effect size | ~0.2 standard deviations |

### 2. Pattern in the Data
- **Before switch (weeks 6-14):** Switched branches averaged ~2 points higher than control branches
- **After switch (weeks 15-22):** Switched branches averaged ~4 points higher than control branches
- The **gap widened** by approximately 2 points coincident with the procedure change

### 3. Important Context
- This effect was **not anticipated** based on the nature of the procedural change
- The effect size is **modest** relative to normal week-to-week variation in scores
- Both groups experienced similar weekly fluctuations; the difference is in the average gap
- No obvious confounding factors identified (staffing and visit patterns controlled for)

### 4. What We Cannot Rule Out
- Unobserved differences between the 90 switched and 60 control branches
- Coincidental timing with other unmeasured factors
- The possibility that the effect may not persist or replicate at scale

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Pause before full rollout** - Given the unexpected finding, gather more evidence before committing resources

2. **Investigate the mechanism** - Interview staff at switched branches to understand what, if anything, feels different about service delivery

3. **Consider a randomized rollout** - If proceeding, randomize the remaining 60 branches to treatment/control to strengthen causal inference

4. **Extend the observation period** - Collect 8-12 more weeks of data to see if the effect persists, grows, or fades

5. **Define decision criteria** - Determine in advance what effect size would justify the cost of system-wide implementation

---

## **Final Verdict**

**Recommendation: Proceed with rollout, but with measured expectations and continued evaluation.**

The statistical evidence supports a real, positive effect—the confidence interval excludes zero and the result is robust to controls. A 4% improvement in service scores is meaningful for patron experience, even if modest. The fact that the effect was unexpected should prompt investigation, not inaction.

**Conditions for proceeding:**
- Document the current procedure details from switched branches before rollout begins
- Commit to 12 weeks of post-rollout monitoring with the same metrics
- Set a threshold for success (e.g., effect persists at ≥1 point) and review at that time

The risk of proceeding appears low (no negative effects detected), while the potential benefit to service quality is real. However, the board should treat this as an evidence-building exercise, not a settled conclusion.

---

*Analysis based on weekly service scores from 150 branches over 17 weeks (weeks 6-22). The procedure switch occurred at week 15. Statistical model uses difference-in-differences estimation with branch fixed effects and standard errors clustered at the branch level.*
