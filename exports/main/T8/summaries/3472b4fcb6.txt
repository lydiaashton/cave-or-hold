# **MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Board of Directors

**Date:** [Current Date]  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

### **DO NOT PROCEED WITH SYSTEM-WIDE ROLLOUT**

**Confidence Level: LOW**

The evidence does not support expanding the new procedure to all branches at this time. While the statistical estimate shows a +2.1 point improvement, critical methodological flaws undermine confidence that the procedure *caused* this improvement.

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**Recommendation: DEFER** – Continue monitoring the 425 early-adopter branches for 8-12 additional weeks before making a rollout decision. The current analysis cannot distinguish between genuine treatment effects and alternative explanations (regression to mean, selection bias, or temporary fluctuations).

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### What the Data Shows

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Simple DiD Estimate | +2.09 points |
| Statistical Significance | p < 0.001 |
| Weeks with Clear Positive Effect | **1 of 8** post-switch weeks |
| Weeks with Negative/No Effect | **7 of 8** post-switch weeks |

### Critical Concern: Parallel Trends Violation

The difference-in-differences method requires that treatment and control branches were on similar trajectories before the switch. **This assumption is violated:**

| Issue | Evidence |
|-------|----------|
| Pre-trend test | Significant (p < 0.001) |
| Pre-switch pattern | Treatment branches systematically underperforming |
| Event study (pre-period) | All 8 weeks show negative coefficients (-3.18 to -8.71) |

### Event Study Results (Relative to Week 14)

| Period | Weeks | Pattern |
|--------|-------|---------|
| **Pre-Switch** | 6-13 | All negative, all significant |
| **Post-Switch** | 15-21 | Mostly negative or no effect |
| **Post-Switch** | 22 | First clear positive effect (+3.76) |

---

## **WHY WE CANNOT RECOMMEND ROLLOUT**

| Concern | Implication |
|---------|-------------|
| **Non-parallel pre-trends** | Treatment and control branches were on different trajectories before the switch |
| **Only 1 positive week** | Week 22 is the only post-switch week with a clear positive effect |
| **Possible regression to mean** | Treatment branches may have been in a temporary dip that naturally recovered |
| **Selection bias risk** | Branches that switched may have been those already struggling |
| **Insufficient data** | 8 weeks post-switch may be too short to establish a reliable pattern |

---

## **RECOMMENDED NEXT STEPS**

| Priority | Action | Timeline |
|----------|--------|----------|
| 1 | Continue monitoring all 425 treatment branches | Immediate |
| 2 | Collect 8-12 additional weeks of data | Ongoing |
| 3 | Investigate why treatment branches underperformed pre-switch | 2-4 weeks |
| 4 | Analyze whether week 22 had special circumstances | 2 weeks |
| 5 | Re-evaluate rollout decision with extended data | 10-12 weeks |

---

## **WHAT WOULD CHANGE OUR RECOMMENDATION**

We would support rollout if future analysis shows:

- ✓ Multiple consecutive weeks with positive, significant effects
- ✓ Evidence that pre-trends stabilize or parallel trends hold in extended data
- ✓ Consistent effects across different branch types and regions
- ✓ No credible alternative explanations for the observed pattern

---

## **CONCLUSION**

**The new procedure is promising but unproven.** Week 22's strong result (+3.76 points) is encouraging, but one week does not establish a reliable pattern. The board should request additional data before committing resources to system-wide implementation.

**Rushing to rollout risks:**
- Wasting resources on a procedure that may not deliver expected benefits
- Disrupting operations at 284 branches without clear evidence of gain
- Missing the opportunity to identify and address why treatment branches were underperforming

**Patience now will enable a confident, evidence-based decision later.**
