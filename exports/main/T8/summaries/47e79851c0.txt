# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Impact of New Branch Shelving and Rotation Procedure on Service Scores

---

## **Bottom Line**

**We cannot confidently conclude that the new procedure caused the observed improvement.** While branches that switched procedures showed service scores approximately 2 points higher after the change, the treatment and control groups were on different trajectories before the switch. This undermines our ability to attribute the difference to the procedure itself. **We recommend against a system-wide rollout based on this evidence alone.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **60 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Analysis compares changes in service scores between groups before and after the switch

### **What the Data Shows**

| Period | Treatment Branches | Control Branches | Difference |
|--------|-------------------|------------------|------------|
| **Before switch** (weeks 6–14) | 48.6 | 48.9 | −0.3 |
| **After switch** (weeks 15–22) | 50.4 | 48.6 | +1.8 |

On the surface, this suggests a ~2-point improvement. However, the pre-switch data reveals a problem.

### **Critical Limitation: Groups Were Not Comparable**

For this type of analysis to support causal conclusions, the treatment and control groups must be on similar trajectories before the intervention (the "parallel trends" assumption). **This assumption is violated in our data:**

- In 8 of 9 pre-switch weeks, treatment branches scored **lower** than control branches
- The gap ranged from 0.2 to 1.2 points below control
- Statistical testing confirms these pre-existing differences are significant (p < 0.001)

**What this means:** The apparent "improvement" after the switch could reflect:
- Treatment branches simply returning to their normal performance level after a temporary slump
- Pre-existing differences between the two groups of branches
- Other factors unrelated to the procedure change

### **Statistical Results**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Raw difference-in-differences estimate | +1.99 points |
| 95% confidence interval | +0.75 to +3.23 points |
| Statistical significance | p = 0.002 |
| **Parallel trends test** | **Failed (p < 0.001)** |

The statistically significant coefficient does not overcome the fundamental methodological concern.

---

## **Recommendations**

### **Do Not Proceed with System-Wide Rollout**

The current evidence is insufficient to justify the cost and disruption of changing procedures at all 60 remaining branches.

### **Next Steps**

1. **Gather more data** — Extend the evaluation period to include additional pre- and post-switch weeks

2. **Use random assignment** — When rolling out to additional branches, randomly assign which branches switch and when. This creates a cleaner comparison.

3. **Investigate pre-period differences** — Understand why the 90 treatment branches underperformed relative to control branches before the switch. Were they systematically different (size, location, patron demographics)?

4. **Consider a stepped-wedge design** — Roll out the procedure to remaining branches in randomized phases over time, allowing for ongoing evaluation with better causal identification

5. **Revisit the decision** after 8–12 additional weeks of data with improved methodology

---

## **Questions for Discussion**

- Why were these 90 branches selected for early adoption? Were they chosen because they were underperforming?
- What is the cost of the new procedure (training, staff time, potential disruption)?
- Should we commission a more rigorous evaluation before making a system-wide decision?

---

## **Final Verdict**

**Hold on system-wide implementation.** The observed association between the new procedure and higher service scores is promising but not causally established. A more rigorous evaluation is needed before committing cooperative-wide resources to this change.
