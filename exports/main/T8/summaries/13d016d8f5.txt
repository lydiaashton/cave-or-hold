# MEMORANDUM

**To:** Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Final Recommendation on New Shelving & Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## Final Verdict

**PROCEED WITH ROLLOUT — BUT WITH TEMPERED EXPECTATIONS**

| Question | Verdict |
|----------|---------|
| **Is the procedure harmful?** | No — no evidence of negative effects |
| **Does it improve service scores?** | Likely yes, but effect is uncertain |
| **Will we see 2–4 point gains?** | Probably not — expect 0–2 points |
| **Should we roll out system-wide?** | Yes, but monitor closely |
| **Confidence level** | Moderate (not high) |

---

## Bottom Line

The new procedure is **safe and probably beneficial**, but the evidence does not support confident claims of a 2–4 point improvement. Branches that volunteered to switch were already improving faster than others before adoption. The **true causal effect is likely 0–2 points**, not the 2–4 points suggested by naive analysis.

**Recommendation:** Roll out to remaining branches, but:
- Set expectations at **1 point of improvement** (not 2–4)
- **Monitor service scores weekly** during implementation
- Be prepared to **reassess** if gains don't materialize within 8–10 weeks

---

## Evidence Summary

| Finding | Implication |
|---------|-------------|
| Switched branches improved +5.3 points post-switch | Encouraging, but not causal |
| Control branches also improved +3.3 points | Secular trend affects all branches |
| Pre-switch gap was growing +0.26 pts/week (p=0.0002) | **Selection bias present** |
| DiD estimate: +2.02 points (95% CI: 1.50–2.55) | Statistically significant but potentially biased |
| No branch showed score declines after switching | Procedure is not harmful |

---

## Why We Cannot Be Certain

```
Pre-Switch Trend (Weeks 6–14):
  Treatment branches were already gaining ~0.25 points/week MORE than control
  
This means:
  • Treatment branches were "hot" — already improving
  • They may have adopted the procedure BECAUSE they were improving
  • Some (or much) of the post-switch gap growth would have happened anyway
```

**The parallel trends assumption is violated.** This is a fundamental limitation of the observational design. We cannot cleanly separate the procedure effect from pre-existing branch momentum.

## Risk Assessment

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|------|------------|--------|------------|
| Procedure provides no benefit | Low-Moderate | Wasted implementation effort | Monitor; can revert if needed |
| Procedure provides modest benefit (1–2 pts) | **Most Likely** | Modest ROI | Set realistic expectations |
| Procedure provides full benefit (2–4 pts) | Low | High ROI | Pleasant surprise |
| Procedure harms performance | Very Low | Negative ROI | No evidence of this; monitor anyway |

---

## Implementation Guidance

1. **Communicate conservatively:** Tell branch managers to expect "up to 2 points" of improvement, not "2–4 points."

2. **Track leading indicators:** Monitor service scores weekly for the first 8 weeks post-switch. If no improvement is visible by week 6, investigate.

3. **Document implementation quality:** Ensure branches actually adopt the procedure as designed. Poor implementation could mask real benefits.

4. **Consider a formal evaluation:** For future procedure changes, use randomized rollout to enable cleaner causal inference.

---

## Conclusion

**The procedure is worth rolling out, but we were overconfident in our initial analysis.** The data shows association, not clean causation. Proceed with the rollout, but treat this as a **moderate-confidence investment** rather than a proven winner. Monitor results closely and be prepared to adjust expectations.

---

*Analysis based on 12,053 weekly observations from 709 branches. Standard errors clustered at branch level. Key limitation: non-random selection into treatment violates parallel trends assumption.*
