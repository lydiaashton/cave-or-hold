# **Board Memorandum**

## **New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure: Impact Assessment**

---

### **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improves service scores, but the gain is modest.** Branches that switched show a **+2 point improvement** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This result is statistically reliable, though the true effect may be somewhat smaller due to how branches were selected for early adoption.

---

### **Key Findings**

| Metric | Result |
|--------|--------|
| **Measured Impact** | +2.07 points (p < 0.001) |
| **Confidence Interval** | 1.91 to 2.23 points |
| **Relative Improvement** | 4% above baseline |
| **Branches Analyzed** | 709 (425 switched, 284 control) |

**What Supports the Finding:**
- The result is **highly statistically significant**—unlikely to be random noise
- Switched branches improved (+0.7 points) while control branches declined (−1.3 points)
- The effect held after controlling for staffing and visitor volume
- The gap between groups **widened over the 8 weeks post-switch**

**What Limits Our Confidence:**
- **Selection bias**: Branches chosen to switch first were already on a slightly stronger trajectory before the change
- **Causal uncertainty**: The true effect may be smaller than 2 points (conservative range: +0.5 to +2.0)
- **Limited follow-up**: Only 8 weeks of post-switch data

---

### **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **Did the procedure change service scores?** | **Yes** — the effect is real and statistically significant |
| **Is the effect large?** | **Modest** — approximately 4% improvement, not transformative |
| **Should we roll out to all branches?** | **Yes, with conditions** — proceed in phases with monitoring |
| **Are we certain of the 2-point estimate?** | **No** — selection bias means the true effect may be smaller |

---

### **Recommendation**

**Approve phased rollout.** The evidence confirms the procedure works as intended. However, given the modest effect size and methodological limitations, we recommend:

1. **Expand gradually** to remaining branches over 2–3 months
2. **Track weekly scores** throughout rollout to confirm effects persist
3. **Review at 6 months** to assess long-term sustainability
4. **Document best practices** to maximize benefit at each branch

---

### **Bottom Line (Restated)**

The new procedure **does improve service scores**. The evidence is strong enough to justify rollout, but modest enough that expectations should be calibrated. A phased approach with continued monitoring is the prudent path forward.
