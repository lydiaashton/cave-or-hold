# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact Analysis**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure is associated with a meaningful improvement in service scores.** Branches that switched to the new procedure improved by an average of 2.1 points relative to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and consistent across analytical approaches. We recommend proceeding with the rollout, while continuing to monitor outcomes.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Measurable Improvement After Switch**
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 52.54 | 53.57 | **+1.03** |
| Non-switched branches | 50.60 | 49.51 | **-1.09** |
| **Net effect** | | | **+2.12 points** |

The 2.1-point improvement is:
- **Statistically significant** (p < 0.001)
- **95% confidence interval**: 1.55 to 2.69 points
- **About 4% improvement** relative to the average score of ~50

### **2. Both Statistical and Practical Significance**
- The effect is highly unlikely to be due to chance
- A 2-point gain on a ~50-point scale represents a meaningful service improvement
- Treatment branches improved while control branches declined—a clear divergence

### **3. Important Caveat**
Branches selected for the pilot were already higher-performing before the switch. While our analysis controls for this using standard difference-in-differences methods, we cannot rule out that some unmeasured factor contributed to the results. The evidence supports a positive effect, but continued monitoring is warranted.

## **Recommendations**

| Priority | Action |

| **2** | **Monitor implementation** – Track scores for 3-6 months post-rollout to confirm effects persist |
| **3** | **Document the procedure** – Ensure consistent implementation across all branches |
| **4** | **Investigate control branch decline** – Understand why non-switched branches scored lower after week 15 |
| **5** | **Plan future evaluations** – Use randomized assignment when testing new procedures |

---

## **Final Verdict**

**✓ APPROVE system-wide rollout of the new procedure.**

The data demonstrate a clear, statistically significant improvement in service scores associated with the new shelving and rotation procedure. The +2.1 point effect is both meaningful for patrons and robust across analytical methods. While the non-random selection of pilot branches warrants ongoing monitoring, the evidence is sufficient to justify expanding the procedure to all 709 branches.

---

## **Technical Summary**

- **Data:** 12,053 observations across 709 branches (425 switched, 284 control), weeks 6-22
- **Method:** Difference-in-differences with cluster-robust standard errors
- **Estimate:** +2.12 points (95% CI: 1.55 to 2.69, p<0.001)
- **Limitation:** Non-random branch selection means we cannot claim definitive causality, but the evidence strongly supports a positive association
