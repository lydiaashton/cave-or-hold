# **Board Summary: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence is inconclusive.** Branches that adopted the new procedure showed a +2.1 point improvement in service scores relative to control branches, but this result is **not statistically significant** at conventional levels (p=0.09). The 95% confidence interval ranges from -0.3 to +4.6 points, meaning the true effect could be zero, slightly negative, or moderately positive. **We recommend collecting more data before making a rollout decision.**

---

## **Final Verdict**

**DO NOT PROCEED WITH FULL ROLLOUT AT THIS TIME**

**Rationale:** The data does not provide sufficient evidence that the new procedure improves service scores. While the point estimate is positive (+2.1 points), the statistical uncertainty is too large to justify a system-wide investment. A premature rollout risks:

- Implementing a procedure that may have no real benefit
- Incurring training and operational costs without guaranteed returns
- Disrupting branches that are currently performing adequately

**Path Forward:** Extend the pilot by 6-8 weeks. If the effect remains stable at ~2 points with improved statistical confidence, revisit the rollout decision.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** total: 20 adopted the new procedure (treatment), 14 kept the old procedure (control)
- **17 weeks of data**: Weeks 6–14 (before switch), Weeks 15–22 (after switch)
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach

### **Service Score Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 46.2 | 45.6 | **-0.6** |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 49.6 | 51.0 | **+1.5** |

**Difference-in-Differences Estimate: +2.14 points (4.4% of baseline)**

### **Statistical Significance**
- **p-value = 0.088**: Not significant at the 5% level
- **95% Confidence Interval: -0.3 to +4.6 points**
- **Interpretation**: The data is consistent with no effect, a small negative effect, or a moderate positive effect. We cannot confidently distinguish among these possibilities.

### **What Would It Take to Know More?**
- At this effect size, we would need approximately **30% more data** (about 5-6 additional weeks) to achieve statistical significance
- Alternatively, if the true effect is smaller than observed, even more data would be needed

### **Validity Checks**
- **Parallel trends assumption holds**: Treatment and control branches had similar trends before the switch (p=0.89)
- **Results are robust** to controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume
- **Weekly effects vary**: Some weeks show positive effects, others show none—this inconsistency adds to the uncertainty

---

## **Next Steps & Recommendations**

### **Recommended Actions**

1. **Do not proceed with full rollout yet**: The evidence does not support a confident decision either way.

2. **Continue the pilot for 6-8 more weeks**: This would provide sufficient data to detect whether the observed +2 point effect is real or due to chance.

3. **Consider a stepped-wedge expansion**: If there is pressure to expand, roll out to additional branches in stages while maintaining some control branches for comparison.

4. **Investigate implementation variation**: The week-to-week inconsistency in effects may indicate uneven adoption or execution across branches.

### **Decision Framework**

| After Additional Data | Recommendation |
|----------------------|----------------|
| Effect ≥2 points, p<0.05 | Proceed with full rollout |
| Effect ≥2 points, p>0.05 | Consider phased rollout; weigh costs against uncertain benefits |
| Effect <1 point or negative | Do not rollout; investigate why the procedure isn't working |

---

## **Technical Notes**

- Analysis uses cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch)
- Controls for staffing_index and visits_index
- Service scores are an internal index where ~50 represents average branch performance
