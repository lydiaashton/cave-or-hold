# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores—an unexpected but welcome result.** Branches that switched saw service scores increase by approximately **2.0 points** (a 4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant, consistent over time, and robust to alternative analyses.

**Recommendation: Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. **Clear Positive Effect on Service Scores**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches (90) | 50.01 | 52.29 | +2.28 |
| Non-switched branches (60) | 51.02 | 51.25 | +0.23 |
| **Net effect** | | | **+2.05** |

- **Estimated impact: +2.0 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.3 to 2.8 points)
- **Statistical significance: p < 0.001**
- This represents approximately a **4% improvement** relative to baseline scores

### 2. **Strong Causal Evidence**

The analysis compares branches that switched to those that did not, before and after week 15:

- **Pre-switch (weeks 6–14):** Switched branches scored *lower* than non-switched branches (average gap: -1.0 points), with a stable gap across all 9 weeks
- **Post-switch (weeks 15–22):** Switched branches scored *higher* than non-switched branches (average gap: +1.0 points), consistent across all 8 weeks

This reversal—combined with parallel pre-switch trends—supports the conclusion that the procedure change caused the improvement.

### 3. **Effect Is Immediate and Sustained**

| Period | Effect Size |
|--------|-------------|
| Weeks 15–18 (early) | +2.05 points |
| Weeks 19–22 (late) | +2.05 points |

Branches adapted quickly, and the benefit did not fade over the 8-week post-switch period.

### 4. **Results Hold Under Multiple Checks**

- ✓ Controlling for staffing levels and visit volumes
- ✓ Excluding week 15 (potential adjustment period)
- ✓ Accounting for branch-specific and week-specific factors

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** to the remaining 60 branches currently using the old procedure

2. **Communicate the unexpected benefit** to branch staff—this may increase buy-in and encourage careful implementation

3. **Monitor the first 4–8 weeks** at each newly switched branch to ensure proper adoption and identify any support needs

4. **Continue tracking service scores** to confirm the 2-point improvement is replicated across all branches

5. **Document lessons learned** from the 90 early-adopter branches to support training and smooth implementation elsewhere

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Criterion | Assessment |
|-----------|------------|
| **Did the procedure change service scores?** | ✓ Yes—improved by ~2 points |
| **Is the evidence reliable?** | ✓ Yes—statistically significant (p < 0.001) |
| **Is the effect practically meaningful?** | ✓ Yes—4% improvement, sustained over time |
| **Are there red flags or concerns?** | ✓ None identified |
| **Should we roll out to all branches?** | ✓ **Yes** |

**Verdict: APPROVE system-wide rollout.**

The data provides clear, credible evidence that the new stock rotation and shelving procedure improved service quality. While this outcome was not expected, it represents a genuine benefit with no identified tradeoffs. The board should authorize extending the new procedure to all remaining branches.

---

*Analysis based on 2,550 weekly observations from 150 branches over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22). The switched procedure was implemented at week 15.*
