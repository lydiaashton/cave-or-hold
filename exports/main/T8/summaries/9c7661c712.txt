# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD OF DIRECTORS**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Office  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The data suggest a positive effect, but with important caveats.** Branches that adopted the new procedure show service scores approximately **2 points higher** than the control group after the switch. However, treatment branches had systematically higher scores even before the change, which limits our confidence that the procedure itself caused the improvement. 

**Recommendation: Proceed cautiously with rollout while monitoring closely.** The evidence is suggestive but not definitive.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Overview**
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure at week 15; **60 branches** kept the old procedure
- Analysis uses difference-in-differences methodology

### **Results**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated treatment effect | +2.0 points |
| Statistical significance | p = 0.002 |
| 95% Confidence interval | +0.8 to +3.3 points |
| Effect size | ~4% of baseline score |

### **Important Limitations**

| Concern | Finding | Implication |
|---------|---------|-------------|
| **Pre-existing differences** | Treatment branches scored ~1.5 points higher even before the switch (p = 0.065) | Groups were not perfectly comparable |
| **Selection possibility** | Higher-performing branches may have been chosen for the pilot | Effect could reflect branch quality, not procedure |
| **Placebo test** | A fake "treatment" at week 12 also shows significance (p = 0.025) | Suggests underlying trend differences |
| **Unexpected result** | Staff expected no effect on service scores | Warrants extra scrutiny |

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **Did scores improve for switching branches?** | ✓ Yes – clearly |
| **Can we prove the procedure caused it?** | ✗ No – not definitively |
| **Is there meaningful risk in rolling out?** | Low – the procedure itself is operational, not costly |
| **Is there risk in NOT rolling out?** | Moderate – we may miss a genuine improvement |

### **Verdict: APPROVE ROLLOUT WITH CONDITIONS**

We recommend approving the system-wide rollout, subject to three conditions:

1. **Randomize the rollout sequence** for remaining branches to enable cleaner causal inference
2. **Set up real-time monitoring** with predefined success metrics (minimum +1 point sustained over 8 weeks)
3. **Plan a formal re-evaluation at 6 months** with authority to pause or adjust if results don't materialize

This approach balances the opportunity to capture a genuine improvement against the uncertainty in our current evidence. The procedural change itself carries minimal downside risk, but our implementation strategy should reflect the limitations of this analysis.
