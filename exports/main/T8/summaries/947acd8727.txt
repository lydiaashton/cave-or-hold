# MEMORANDUM

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Rotation Procedure

---

## Executive Summary

After analyzing 17 weeks of service score data from all 709 branches, we find **inconclusive evidence** that the new shelving and rotation procedure improved service quality. Branches that adopted the new procedure showed higher scores after the switch, but they were already improving faster before the change. The true effect is likely smaller than initial comparisons suggest—possibly 0.5 to 1.0 points rather than 1.7 points—and may be zero.

---

## Bottom Line

**Proceed with caution, not full rollout.** The data does not support a confident conclusion that the new procedure caused meaningful improvement. However, it also does not rule out a modest benefit. We recommend a **phased expansion with randomized assignment** to obtain credible evidence before committing system-wide resources.

---

## Final Verdict

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did service scores improve at treated branches? | **Yes** – from 51.8 to 55.5 (+3.7 points) |
| Was this improvement caused by the new procedure? | **Uncertain** – pre-existing trends explain much of the gain |
| Is the effect large enough to justify system-wide rollout? | **Probably not** – best estimate is 0.5-1.0 points, not 1.7 |
| Should we abandon the new procedure? | **No** – it may still provide modest benefits |
| **Recommended action** | **Phased expansion with rigorous evaluation** |

**Confidence Level:** Low to Moderate

The fundamental problem is that branches selected for the initial rollout were already on a stronger improvement trajectory. Without random assignment, we cannot confidently attribute post-switch gains to the procedure itself.

---

## Key Findings

### 1. Surface Results vs. Adjusted Estimates

| Measure | Estimated Effect |
|---------|-----------------|
| Simple before/after comparison | +1.73 points |
| Two-way fixed effects | +1.71 points |
| **Pre-trend adjusted estimate** | **+0.5 to +1.0 points** |
| Segmented regression (level shift) | -0.37 points (not significant) |
| First-difference model | -0.40 points (not significant) |

The simple comparisons overstate the effect because they don't account for pre-existing differences between branches.

### 2. Pre-Existing Trends Are the Core Problem

Before the procedure change (weeks 6-14):
- The gap between treated and control branches grew from 0.2 points to 2.0 points
- Treated branches were gaining ~0.27 points per week more than control branches
- This trend alone would predict continued gap widening after week 14

After the procedure change (weeks 15-22):
- The gap continued growing, reaching 3.8 points by week 22
- But the rate of gap growth was similar to (or slightly slower than) the pre-period
- No clear "jump" occurred at the switch point

### 3. Event Study Shows Continuous Trend, Not Discontinuous Change

The week-by-week coefficients (relative to week 14) show:

| Period | Average Coefficient |
|--------|---------------------|
| Weeks 6-9 (early pre) | 0.77 |
| Weeks 10-13 (late pre) | 1.65 |
| Weeks 15-18 (early post) | 2.53 |
| Weeks 19-22 (late post) | 3.62 |

The pattern is **continuous growth throughout**, not a step-change at week 15. This is inconsistent with a strong causal effect of the procedure change.

### 4. What We Can Confidently Say

- Treated branches have higher service scores than control branches (gap of ~3.8 points)
- This gap existed and was growing before the procedure change
- The procedure may have added 0.5-1.0 points beyond the pre-existing trend
- We cannot rule out zero effect with current evidence
- We also cannot rule out a modest positive effect

---

## Recommendations

### Immediate Actions

1. **Do not proceed with full system-wide rollout** based on current evidence alone
2. **Continue the procedure at current treated branches** – there may be modest benefits
3. **Document selection criteria** used for initial rollout to understand biases

### Next Phase: Rigorous Evaluation

4. **Randomized expansion**: Randomly assign 100-200 additional branches to treatment vs. control
5. **Pre-register analysis plan**: Define success metrics and analysis methods before data collection
6. **Extend observation window**: Collect at least 12-16 weeks of post-implementation data
7. **Success threshold**: Require minimum 1.0 point improvement (adjusted for trends) to justify full rollout

### If Board Wishes to Proceed Without Further Evidence

Should the board decide to move forward despite uncertainty:

- Implement **phased rollout** over 6-12 months
- Build in **evaluation checkpoints** at 3, 6, and 12 months
- Establish **reversal criteria** if subsequent analysis shows no benefit
- Budget for **ongoing monitoring and analysis**

---

## Methodology Summary

- **Data:** 12,053 weekly observations from 709 branches (weeks 6-22, 2026)
- **Treatment:** 425 branches adopted new procedure starting week 15
- **Control:** 284 branches continued with existing procedure
- **Methods:** Difference-in-differences, two-way fixed effects, event study, segmented regression, first-difference models
- **Statistical standards:** Cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch)
- **Key limitation:** Non-random selection into treatment violates parallel trends assumption
