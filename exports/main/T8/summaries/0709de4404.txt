# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Library Cooperative Board  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** December 2025  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Contrary to our initial expectation that the change would have no effect, branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw their service scores increase by approximately **2.0 points** (about 4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant and consistent across multiple analytical approaches.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Final Verdict**

**Roll out the new procedure to all branches.**

The evidence is clear and reliable:
- The improvement is **real** (p < 0.001; confidence interval excludes zero)
- The improvement is **meaningful** (4% gain on a key performance metric)
- The improvement is **efficient** (achieved without additional staffing or resources)
- The **risk of being wrong is low** (strong research design with valid comparison groups)

We initially expected no difference. The data proves otherwise. This is a rare case where a procedural change delivered measurable benefits without added cost. The board should act on this evidence.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measurable Improvement in Service Quality
- Branches using the new procedure showed a **2.0-point increase** in service scores after implementation
- This represents a **4.2% improvement** relative to the average branch score of ~49 points
- The effect is equivalent to about **0.21 standard deviations**—a modest but meaningful gain in service quality

### 2. Strong Statistical Evidence
- The improvement is **highly statistically significant** (p < 0.001)
- We are 95% confident the true effect lies between **1.1 and 2.9 points**
- Results hold even after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### 3. Valid Comparison Design
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure; **60 branches** continued with the old method
- Before the switch (weeks 6–14), both groups had nearly identical trends in service scores
- This parallel pre-trend supports the conclusion that the improvement was caused by the new procedure, not other factors

### 4. How the Improvement Occurred
- The gain in service scores does **not** appear to come from changes in staffing levels or visitor volume
- This suggests the new procedure itself—likely through more efficient operations or better organization—directly improved service delivery

---

## **Analysis Approach**

We used a **difference-in-differences** method, which compares:
- Changes in service scores at branches that switched to the new procedure
- Against changes at branches that kept the old procedure
- Before and after week 15 (when the switch occurred)

This approach isolates the effect of the new procedure from other factors that might affect all branches simultaneously (such as seasonal variations or system-wide changes).

**Data:** 2,550 weekly observations from 150 branches over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all remaining branches

2. **Monitor implementation** during the rollout to ensure consistent adoption and identify any branches that may need additional support

3. **Track service scores** for the next 8–12 weeks post-rollout to confirm the improvement persists at scale

4. **Document best practices** from the 90 branches that already implemented the procedure to support training at remaining locations

5. **Consider further analysis** to understand which specific aspects of the new procedure drive the improvement, potentially revealing additional optimization opportunities

---

## **Caveats**

- The analysis covers an 8-week post-implementation period; longer-term effects remain to be observed
- Service score is one metric; the board may wish to consider other outcomes (e.g., operational costs, staff satisfaction) in the final rollout decision
- A small number of branches showed unusually high or low baseline scores, but the overall pattern is robust
