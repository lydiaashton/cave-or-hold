# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact Analysis**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The data show a statistically significant improvement in service scores from the new procedure—contrary to our expectation of no change.** The estimated effect is **+2.5 points** (approximately **5% improvement**), with high statistical confidence (p = 0.0002). 

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rollout, but monitor outcomes to confirm the effect persists at scale.

---

## **Evidence Summary**

| Finding | Result |
|---------|--------|
| Treatment effect | +2.5 points (95% CI: 1.2 to 3.8) |
| Statistical significance | p = 0.0002 |

---

## **What Changed Our Expectation**

We anticipated no service score impact. The data show otherwise:
- Switched branches improved from 50.8 to 53.4
- Control branches remained flat at 50.1
- The divergence began at the switch point (week 15) and persisted

---

## **Recommendations**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** to remaining 60 branches
2. **Track service scores** during implementation to verify results
3. **Document implementation practices** to understand what drives the improvement
4. **Consider cost-benefit** before full commitment if rollout requires significant resources

---

## **Caveats**

- This is an 8-week post-switch window; long-term effects are unknown
- Effect size is modest (~5%); implementation costs should be weighed
- Unexpected finding warrants careful monitoring during rollout

---

## **Final Verdict**

**✓ ROLL OUT THE NEW PROCEDURE**

The statistical evidence is strong and unambiguous: the new procedure improved service scores. While this contradicts our prior expectation, the data are clear—the treatment effect is positive, statistically significant, and robust across multiple analytical approaches. The parallel trends test confirms the improvement is attributable to the procedure change, not pre-existing differences between branches.

**Confidence level: High**

The risk of proceeding is low (the procedure is already in use at 90 branches with no reported problems), while the potential benefit is a measurable service quality gain across the entire cooperative. The unexpected positive result should be treated as a welcome discovery, not a reason for hesitation.

**Action required:** Board approval to begin system-wide implementation.
