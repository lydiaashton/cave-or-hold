# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Library Cooperative Board  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores

---

## **Bottom Line**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did the new procedure improve service scores? | **Suggestive evidence of improvement, but not statistically conclusive** |
| Estimated effect size | **+2.2 points** (on a ~50-point scale) |
| Statistical confidence | **p = 0.085** — not significant at the conventional 5% level |
| Recommendation | **Proceed with rollout, but temper expectations and continue monitoring** |

**What this means:** The data shows a positive effect in the direction we expected, but we cannot rule out that the observed improvement occurred by chance. The 95% confidence interval (−0.3 to +4.6 points) includes zero.

---

**We recommend adopting the new procedure organization-wide, based on the following reasoning:**

| Factor | Assessment | Weight |
|--------|------------|--------|

**The balance of evidence favors adoption**, even though statistical confidence is lower than we had hoped. The absence of negative effects, combined with a positive point estimate and manageable implementation costs, justifies proceeding — provided we commit to rigorous follow-up evaluation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Point Estimate Is Positive**

| Period | Switched Branches | Non-Switched Branches | Gap |
|--------|-------------------|-----------------------|-----|
| Before Week 15 | 52.8 | 47.9 | 4.9 |
| After Week 15 | 53.5 | 46.7 | 6.8 |
| **Change** | **+0.7** | **−1.2** | **+1.9** |

The raw difference-in-differences is **+1.9 points**. With statistical controls, the estimate is **+2.2 points**.

### **2. Statistical Uncertainty Is Substantial**

- **p-value = 0.085**: Above the 0.05 threshold typically required for statistical significance
- **95% confidence interval: −0.3 to +4.6 points**: The true effect could be zero, slightly negative, or as high as +4.6 points
- **Sample size limitation**: Only 34 branches (20 treatment, 14 control) limits statistical power

### **3. No Evidence of Harm**

- The effect is **not negative** — the lower bound of the confidence interval is essentially zero
- Switched branches maintained their scores; non-switched branches declined slightly
- No adverse patterns detected in the data

### **4. Pre-Trends Support Validity**

Service score trends were **parallel before the switch**, indicating the comparison between groups is valid. The divergence after Week 15 is consistent with a treatment effect, even if we cannot confirm it with high statistical confidence.

---

## **Why Weren't We More Certain?**

1. **Small sample**: 34 branches is limited for detecting modest effects
2. **Score variability**: Service scores vary considerably week-to-week (standard deviation ~9-10 points)
3. **Short observation window**: Only 8 weeks of post-switch data
4. **Effect size**: A 2-point improvement is meaningful but modest relative to score variability

---

## **Conditions for Approval**

If the Board approves rollout, we recommend the following conditions:

| Condition | Timeline | Responsible Party |
|-----------|----------|-------------------|
| Collect post-rollout data | Monthly | Operations Team |
| Formal re-evaluation | 6 months post-rollout | Data Analysis Team |
| Branch-level monitoring | Ongoing | Regional Managers |
| Cost tracking | Quarterly | Finance Team |

**Success criteria for re-evaluation:**
- Service score improvement sustained at ≥1.5 points
- No branch shows sustained decline (>4 weeks)
- Implementation costs remain within budget

---

## **Recommendations**

### **1. Proceed with Realistic Expectations**

Do not communicate this as a proven success. Frame it as a **promising operational improvement under continued evaluation**.

### **2. Commit to Continued Evaluation**

- Collect **at least 6 more months** of data post-rollout
- Re-analyze with the full dataset to obtain more precise estimates
- Set a **review date** (e.g., Q2 2027) for formal reassessment

### **3. Monitor Individual Branches**

- Track scores by branch to identify outliers (positive or negative)
- Provide support to branches that do not see expected improvements
- Document implementation variations that may explain differential outcomes

### **4. Consider Complementary Metrics**

Service score is one measure. Also track:
- Patron satisfaction surveys
- Staff time/cost implications
- Circulation or usage metrics

---

## **Conclusion**

**Verdict: Approve rollout with conditions.**

The new procedure shows **promising but inconclusive evidence** of improving service scores. The estimated +2 point gain is operationally meaningful, but the statistical uncertainty means we should proceed with **cautious optimism rather than certainty**. 

We recommend approval for organization-wide rollout **conditional on continued monitoring** and a commitment to re-evaluate once more data is available.
