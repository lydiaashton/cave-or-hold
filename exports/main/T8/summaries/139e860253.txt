# **Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure produces a modest but reliable improvement in service scores.** The estimated effect is **+1.9 points** (95% CI: 1.3 to 2.5), which is statistically significant but represents only about a 4% improvement. The benefit is not universal—approximately 38% of branches saw no improvement. 

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rollout, but with realistic expectations and monitoring to identify which branches benefit most.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Statistically Significant but Modest Improvement**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| **New procedure** (425 branches) | 50.24 | 51.39 | **+1.14** |
| **Old procedure** (284 branches) | 49.86 | 49.12 | **-0.75** |

**Net Effect: +1.89 points** (p < 0.001; 95% CI: 1.33 to 2.45)

Context: Service scores have a standard deviation of 9.6 points and range from 14 to 82. The 1.9-point effect is meaningful but modest relative to this variation.

### **2. Benefits Are Not Universal**

| Metric | Treatment Branches | Control Branches |
|--------|-------------------|------------------|
| % that improved | 62% | 40% |
| % with >1 point gain | 51% | 33% |

**Important:** 38% of branches using the new procedure did not see improvement. This suggests the procedure works better in some contexts than others.

### **3. Results Are Statistically Robust**

The 1.9-point estimate holds across multiple analytical approaches (all p < 0.001):
- Simple difference-in-differences
- Branch fixed effects
- Branch + week fixed effects
- Controls for staffing and visit levels

### **4. Pre-Existing Trends Were Similar**

Before the switch, treatment and control branches had parallel trends (average difference: 0.38 points). The gap widened after the switch, supporting a causal interpretation.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve phased rollout** to remaining branches, starting with those most similar to successful early adopters.

2. **Investigate heterogeneity**: Analyze which branch characteristics (size, location, staffing, etc.) predict better outcomes to target implementation effectively.

3. **Set realistic expectations**: Communicate that the average improvement is modest (~2 points) and not all branches will benefit.

4. **Establish monitoring framework**: Track service scores post-rollout and identify branches that don't improve for additional support.

5. **Consider complementary interventions**: Since the effect is modest, evaluate whether combining this procedure with other improvements could yield larger gains.

---

## **Final Verdict**

**Proceed with rollout, but treat this as one tool among many—not a transformational change.**

The evidence supports adopting the new procedure system-wide. The effect is real, statistically robust, and comes with no identified downsides. However, the board should understand that:

- **This is incremental improvement, not a breakthrough.** Expect ~2 points on average, not dramatic gains.
- **Some branches will not benefit.** Plan for follow-up support when certain locations don't show improvement.
- **The procedure may be more about resilience than growth.** Control branches declined; treatment branches held steady or improved slightly.

**Bottom line:** The new procedure is worth implementing, but it should be part of a broader service quality strategy rather than relied upon as a standalone solution.
