# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** [Current Date]  
**RE:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Rotation Procedure  

---

## **Bottom Line**

**We cannot determine whether the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure showed larger score gains than those that kept the old procedure (+1.9 points more). However, these branches were already improving faster *before* the switch, making it impossible to separate the procedure's effect from pre-existing differences. 

**The evidence is inconclusive—not supportive, but not definitively negative either.** We recommend pausing the rollout and obtaining better evidence before making a system-wide decision.

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **Did the new procedure improve service scores?** | **Cannot be determined from this data** |
| **Should the board roll out the procedure system-wide?** | **No—not based on this evidence** |
| **Should the board abandon the procedure entirely?** | **No—the procedure may still be beneficial** |
| **What should the board do?** | **Pause rollout; conduct a proper randomized evaluation** |

**Rationale:** The study's fundamental flaw is that treatment and control branches were not comparable before the switch. Treatment branches had higher baseline scores and were already improving faster. This violates the core assumption needed to attribute post-switch differences to the procedure itself. The observed +1.9 point advantage is real—but we cannot know how much (if any) is caused by the procedure versus pre-existing branch characteristics.

**For a rollout decision, inconclusive evidence should be treated as insufficient evidence.** The board should not commit cooperative-wide resources based on data that cannot answer the question at hand.

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Overview**
| | |
|---|---|
| **Branches Analyzed** | 709 total |
| **Treatment Group** | 425 branches (adopted new procedure at week 15) |
| **Control Group** | 284 branches (kept old procedure) |
| **Study Period** | 17 weeks (weeks 6–22) |
| **Metric** | Service Score (average ~50; higher is better) |

### **What the Data Shows**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 48.1 | 49.2 | +1.1 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 50.5 | 53.6 | +3.1 |
| **Difference** | | | **+1.9 points** |

### **Why We Cannot Draw a Firm Conclusion**

**The treatment branches were different from the start:**

| Issue | Evidence |
|-------|----------|
| **Higher baseline** | Treatment branches started 2.4 points higher |
| **Faster pre-existing improvement** | Treatment branches were gaining 0.35 points/week more *before* the switch (p=0.0003) |
| **No jump at switch point** | The gap actually *decreased* slightly from week 14 to 15 (-0.14 points) |
| **Gap increase consistent with pre-trend** | The post-switch gap increase (1.2 points) is what we'd expect if pre-existing trends simply continued |

### **Two Valid Interpretations**

| Interpretation | Supporting Evidence |
|----------------|---------------------|
| **The procedure had no effect** | • Pre-trends explain the observed difference<br>• No discontinuity at switch point<br>• Trend-adjusted model: effect = -0.6 points (not significant) |
| **The procedure may have helped** | • Simple comparison: +1.9 points (highly significant)<br>• Within-branch changes confirm the pattern<br>• Post-switch gap is larger than pre-switch |

**We cannot distinguish between these interpretations with the current data.**

---

## **Recommendations**

### **1. Pause System-Wide Rollout**
The current evidence does not justify a cooperative-wide implementation. The apparent benefits may reflect branch selection rather than procedure effectiveness.

### **2. Investigate Branch Selection**
Understanding why treatment and control branches differed is essential:
- What criteria determined which branches adopted the new procedure?
- Were higher-performing or better-resourced branches preferentially selected?

### **3. Design a Rigorous Evaluation**
To obtain actionable evidence:
- **Randomly assign** branches to treatment and control
- **Verify parallel pre-trends** before implementing
- **Extend the observation period** (minimum 6 months)
- **Pre-specify success criteria** before analysis

### **4. Continue Monitoring**
Track the 425 treatment branches ongoing. Additional data may reveal longer-term effects or variation across branch types.

### **5. Consider Alternative Metrics**
Before a final decision, also examine staff efficiency, patron satisfaction, circulation patterns, and costs.

---

## **Conclusion**

The board hoped the new procedure would improve service scores. The data shows treatment branches improved more than control branches, but **we cannot attribute this difference to the procedure itself** because the branches were already on different trajectories before the switch.

**This is not evidence that the procedure failed—it is evidence that we cannot tell.** A responsible decision requires better study design. We recommend pausing the rollout, investigating the selection process, and conducting a proper randomized evaluation before committing cooperative-wide resources.

---

*Analysis based on branches.csv data covering weeks 6–22, with procedure switch implemented at week 15. Statistical methods: difference-in-differences with and without trend controls, event study analysis.*
