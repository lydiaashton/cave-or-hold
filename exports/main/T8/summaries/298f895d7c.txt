# **MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure produced a real but modest improvement in service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **1.9 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant, though the true improvement could reasonably range from 0.4 to 3.4 points. **The data support a full rollout, but expectations should be tempered—the gain is meaningful, not transformative.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Measurable Improvement in Service Quality**
- The new procedure produced a **1.9-point increase** in service scores (95% confidence interval: 0.4 to 3.4 points)
- This effect is statistically significant (p = 0.013), indicating the improvement is unlikely to be due to chance
- The improvement persisted even after accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume

### **2. Rigorous Analysis Methods**
Our evaluation used a quasi-experimental design that:
- Compared 20 branches that switched to the new procedure against 14 branches that continued with the old procedure
- Analyzed 17 weeks of data (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Controlled for inherent differences between branches and week-to-week fluctuations
- Confirmed that both groups were performing similarly before the switch

### **3. Study Scope**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Branches studied | 34 |
| Branches using new procedure | 20 |
| Branches using old procedure (control) | 14 |
| Weeks of data collected | 17 |
| Total observations | 578 |
| System-wide average service score | 50.75 |

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **Did the new procedure change service scores?** | **Yes** — the effect is statistically significant |
| **Did it improve scores as expected?** | **Yes** — the direction is positive as anticipated |
| **Is the improvement large?** | **Modest** — approximately 4% of the average score |
| **Should we roll out to all branches?** | **Yes** — the evidence supports expansion, with realistic expectations |

**Rationale:** The analysis provides credible evidence that the new procedure improves service scores. While the magnitude is smaller than some may have hoped, a consistent 1.9-point gain across 34 branches represents meaningful system-wide improvement. The absence of negative effects, combined with statistical significance, justifies proceeding with the rollout.

## **Caveats**

- The effect is **real but modest**—approximately 4% improvement on the service score index
- The confidence interval is wide; the true effect could be smaller than observed
- Only 8 weeks of post-switch data are available; long-term sustainability is unproven
- Implementation quality may vary across branches

---

## **Conclusion**

**Verdict: Proceed with rollout.** The new shelving and stock rotation procedure delivers a statistically significant improvement in service scores. While the gain is not dramatic, it is consistent, positive, and achieved without adverse effects. With proper training and monitoring, expanding the procedure to all branches is a sound investment in service quality.
