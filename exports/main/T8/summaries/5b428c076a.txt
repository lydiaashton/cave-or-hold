# Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact Analysis

**To:** Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## Bottom Line

**The evidence does not demonstrate that the new procedure improves service scores.** The estimated +1.6 point effect is not statistically significant and is likely biased upward because branches that switched were already declining relative to others before the change. **We recommend against system-wide rollout based on current evidence.**

---

## Final Verdict

> **DO NOT PROCEED WITH SYSTEM-WIDE ROLLOUT**

**Rationale:**
- The estimated effect is not statistically distinguishable from zero
- The study design violates the parallel trends assumption required for valid causal inference
- The apparent "improvement" is more likely regression to the mean than a true treatment effect
- Post-switch performance was inconsistent (treatment branches underperformed in 5 of 8 weeks)

**What This Means:**
- Current evidence is insufficient to justify the costs and disruption of system-wide implementation
- This does not prove the procedure is ineffective—only that this evaluation cannot demonstrate effectiveness
- The cooperative should either abandon the procedure or conduct a more rigorous evaluation before expanding

---

## Key Findings

### 1. Main Result

| Metric | Value | Interpretation |
|--------|-------|----------------|
| Point estimate | +1.61 points | Not statistically significant |
| P-value | 0.11 | Cannot reject "no effect" |
| 95% CI | -0.36 to +3.58 | Includes zero |

### 2. Critical Problem: Pre-Existing Trends

The parallel trends assumption required for valid causal inference **does not hold**:

| Period | Treatment - Control Gap |
|--------|------------------------|
| Weeks 6-10 (early) | -0.98 points |
| Weeks 11-14 (late) | -3.08 points |

The treatment group was already falling behind by an additional **2.1 points** before the switch occurred. This means the +1.61 "effect" likely captures **regression to the mean**, not the procedure's impact.

### 3. Post-Switch Pattern Is Inconsistent

| Weeks | Pattern |
|-------|---------|
| 15, 21, 22 | Treatment ahead (3 weeks) |
| 16-20 | Control ahead (5 weeks) |

No clear, sustained improvement is visible.

---

## Next Steps

### Recommended Actions

1. **Halt system-wide rollout plans** pending stronger evidence.

2. **If the procedure shows operational value** (e.g., staff efficiency, cost savings) independent of service scores, document those benefits separately.

3. **For future evaluations**, consider:
   - Random assignment of branches to treatment/control
   - Longer pre-period baseline (12+ weeks)
   - Extended post-period observation (16+ weeks)
   - Multiple outcome measures beyond service scores

4. **Revisit this decision** only after collecting more rigorous evidence.

---

## Conclusion

The current analysis cannot distinguish the procedure's effect from pre-existing trends and natural variation. Rolling out system-wide would be premature and not supported by the data. The cooperative should either abandon the procedure or invest in a more rigorous evaluation before expanding.

---

*Analysis based on 578 weekly observations from 34 branches (20 switched, 14 non-switched) over weeks 6-22. Standard errors clustered at branch level.*
