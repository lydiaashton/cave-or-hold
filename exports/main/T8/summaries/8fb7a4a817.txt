# **BOARD MEMORANDUM**

---

## **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2025  
**Subject:** Recommendation on System-Wide Rollout of New Procedure

---

### **BOTTOM LINE**

**We recommend a CAUTIOUS ROLLOUT with continued monitoring.**

The evidence suggests the new procedure does improve service scores, but the effect is modest (~1.6 points, or 3%) and the statistical certainty is lower than conventional standards. There is a **93% probability the effect is positive**, and **no evidence of harm**. Given the directional evidence and low risk, proceeding with implementation is reasonable—but the board should temper expectations and continue tracking outcomes.

---

### **KEY FINDINGS**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Branches in study | 34 (20 treatment, 14 control) |
| Study period | 17 weeks (Weeks 6–22) |
| Switch date | Week 15 |
| **Estimated treatment effect** | **+1.59 points** |
| **Statistical significance** | **p = 0.144 (two-sided); p = 0.072 (one-sided)** |
| **95% Confidence Interval** | **-0.54 to +3.72 points** |
| **Probability effect is positive** | **93%** |

**What This Means:**
- The new procedure likely improves service scores by about 1.6 points (~3% improvement)
- However, we cannot rule out the possibility of no effect (or a very small negative effect)
- The study was not large enough to detect modest effects with high confidence
- Importantly: **no evidence the new procedure harms service quality**

**Supporting Evidence:**
- Treatment branches improved relative to control branches after the switch (+0.38 vs. -1.23 points)
- Week-by-week analysis shows consistently non-negative effects in most post-switch weeks
- Pre-switch trends were stable, validating our comparison method

---

### **CONTEXT**

The organization expected this procedure change to improve service scores. The data **support this expectation**, though the magnitude of improvement is smaller than hoped and the statistical evidence is not as strong as the board may have wanted.

This is a common situation in program evaluation: the effect is in the right direction, but uncertainty remains. The decision now depends on **costs, risks, and the board's tolerance for uncertainty**.

---

### **FINAL VERDICT**

| Consideration | Assessment |
|---------------|------------|
| **Direction of effect** | ✅ Positive (93% probability) |
| **Magnitude of effect** | ⚠️ Modest (~3% improvement) |
| **Statistical certainty** | ⚠️ Below conventional thresholds |
| **Risk of harm** | ✅ Low (negative effects ruled out beyond -0.5 points) |
| **Study limitations** | ⚠️ Underpowered for small effects |

**Our Recommendation: Proceed with Cautious Rollout**

1. **Implement the new procedure system-wide** — the weight of evidence favors improvement
2. **Set realistic expectations** — anticipate ~1-2 point improvements, not dramatic gains
3. **Continue monitoring** — track service scores for 12 weeks post-rollout to confirm effects
4. **Document implementation** — ensure all branches adopt the procedure consistently
5. **Plan for iteration** — if effects are smaller than expected, consider refinements to the procedure

If the board prefers greater certainty before committing:

- Extend the current pilot by 8–12 weeks
- This would narrow the confidence interval by ~40%
- Decision point: Q2 2026
- Trade-off: Delayed benefits vs. reduced uncertainty
