# **Memorandum to the Board**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team

**Subject:** Recommendation on New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure  

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

# **DO NOT ROLL OUT — Evidence Does Not Support the Change**

The new procedure has **not been proven to improve service scores**. The apparent 2-point gain is explained by pre-existing differences between branches, not the procedure itself. Rolling out systemwide would incur costs and disruption without demonstrated benefit.

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Recommendation: Halt rollout. Conduct a randomized controlled trial before any further expansion.**

Our analysis reveals that branches selected for the new procedure were already on a stronger performance trajectory before the switch. When we account for this pre-existing trend, the estimated effect **drops from 2.0 points to essentially zero** (−0.03 points).

---

## **Critical Finding: The Parallel Trends Assumption Fails**

The standard analysis method (difference-in-differences) requires that treatment and control groups would have followed similar trends absent the intervention. **This assumption is violated in our data:**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Pre-switch trend difference | +0.24 points/week |
| Statistical significance | p = 0.0004 |
| Interpretation | Treated branches were already improving faster than control branches **before** the switch |

**What this means:** The 2-point "effect" we initially calculated is almost entirely explained by pre-existing differences between the groups, not the procedure itself.

---

## **Revised Effect Estimates**

| Method | Estimated Effect | Reliability |
|--------|------------------|-------------|
| Standard DiD (ignores pre-trend) | +2.00 points | ❌ Misleading |
| Adjusted for pre-existing trend | **−0.03 points** | ⚠️ Suggests no effect |
| Conservative (week 14 vs. post) | +0.74 points | ⚠️ Upper bound, still uncertain |

---

## **What the Data Actually Shows**

```
Gap Between Treated and Control Groups Over Time

Week 6:   1.54 points
Week 10:  1.80 points  
Week 14:  3.15 points  ← Last week before switch
Week 15+: 3.89 points (average)  ← After switch

The gap was already widening by 0.24 points/week BEFORE the switch.
After accounting for this trend, there is no detectable jump at the switch point.
```

---

## **Why This Happened**

The 425 branches that switched were **not randomly selected**. They:
- Had higher service scores throughout the study period
- Were already improving faster than other branches
- May have been chosen *because* they were better-performing branches

This is **selection bias**, and it makes it impossible to isolate the procedure's effect from the branches' underlying characteristics.

---

## **Recommended Next Steps**

| Action | Rationale |
|--------|-----------|
| **Pause systemwide rollout** | Current evidence does not support the expected benefit |
| **Run a randomized controlled trial** | Randomly assign 50-100 branches to new procedure; this eliminates selection bias |
| **Collect 12+ weeks of post-treatment data** | Better assessment of sustained effects |
| **Re-analyze with proper design** | Make go/no-go decision based on credible evidence |

---

## **Decision Requested**

**We request Board approval to:**
1. **Halt the systemwide rollout** pending stronger evidence
2. **Fund a proper randomized trial** to accurately measure the procedure's impact
3. **Revisit the decision** once credible evidence is available

---

## **Summary**

We expected this procedure to improve service scores. The initial analysis appeared to confirm this. However, **more careful analysis reveals the evidence is not credible**. The branches that switched were already outperforming and improving faster. We cannot conclude the procedure itself caused any improvement.

**Proceeding with rollout based on current evidence would be a mistake.** A proper randomized trial is the responsible next step.
