# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores—contrary to the board's expectation of no effect.** Branches using the new procedure maintained scores **2.3 points higher** than comparable branches during a period of system-wide decline. The effect is statistically robust and consistently positive. 

**Verdict: Proceed with rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. What Changed
| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|---------------|---------------|--------|
| Switched branches (n=90) | 51.82 | 51.25 | -0.57 |
| Non-switched branches (n=60) | 51.93 | 49.08 | -2.85 |
| **Difference** | | | **+2.28** |

**Interpretation**: All branches saw scores decline after week 15. Switched branches declined *less*—the new procedure protected service quality.

### 2. Uncertainty Range
| Confidence Level | Effect Estimate |
|-----------------|-----------------|
| 95% confidence interval | +0.72 to +3.81 points |
| Point estimate | +2.26 points |
| Statistical significance | p = 0.004 |

**Key point**: Even the conservative lower bound (+0.7 points) is positive. There is no credible scenario in this data where the new procedure harmed service scores.

### 3. Effect Size Context
- **4.4%** improvement relative to baseline (~52 points)
- Service scores naturally vary by about **10 points** week-to-week
- Effect is modest but meaningful given the board expected **zero** impact

### 4. Study Strengths
- 150 branches, 2,550 observations
- Pre-switch trends were parallel between groups (p = 0.90)
- Effect persists after controlling for staffing and visitor volume
- No evidence of negative effects

---

## **Final Verdict**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did the new procedure change service scores? | **Yes** |
| Was the change positive or negative? | **Positive** |
| Is the effect statistically reliable? | **Yes** (p = 0.004) |
| Could the true effect be zero or negative? | **Unlikely** (95% CI: +0.7 to +3.8) |
| Should we roll out to all branches? | **Yes** |

**Rationale**: The board expected no difference. The data shows a clear positive difference with no credible downside. Even if the true effect is at the low end of our confidence interval (+0.7 points), the procedure still helps and does not harm. Given the absence of negative effects and the presence of measurable benefits, proceeding with system-wide rollout is the sound decision.

## **Methodology Note**

This analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** design comparing switched vs. non-switched branches before and after week 15. Data covers weeks 6–22 across 150 branches (2,550 observations). The approach isolates the procedure's effect from system-wide factors affecting all branches.
