# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Library Cooperative Board  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores

---

## **Executive Summary**

Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure experienced **higher service scores** compared to branches that maintained the old procedure. The estimated improvement is **approximately 2 points** on the service score index. While this finding is encouraging and aligns with our expectations, the statistical evidence is **moderate rather than definitive**. We recommend proceeding with rollout while continuing to monitor outcomes.

---

## **Bottom Line**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did the new procedure improve service scores? | **Yes, by approximately 2 points** |
| How confident are we? | **Moderately confident** (p = 0.09) |
| Should we roll out to all branches? | **Recommended, with continued monitoring** |

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Positive Effect on Service Scores**

| Period | Switched Branches | Non-Switched Branches | Gap |
|--------|-------------------|-----------------------|-----|
| Before Week 15 | 52.8 | 47.9 | 4.9 |
| After Week 15 | 53.5 | 46.7 | 6.8 |
| **Change** | **+0.7** | **−1.2** | **+1.9** |

The new procedure is associated with a **+1.9 point improvement** in service scores after accounting for underlying trends.

### **2. Statistical Confidence**

- **Point estimate:** +2.2 points (with controls)
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.085
- **95% confidence interval:** −0.3 to +4.6 points

Interpretation: The effect is positive and meaningful, but the sample size (34 branches) limits our ability to rule out chance variation with high confidence.

### **3. No Pre-Existing Differences in Trends**

Service score trends for switched and non-switched branches were **parallel before the change**, confirming that the two groups were comparable and the observed effect is likely attributable to the procedure change.

### **4. Effect Appears to Grow Over Time**

| Post-Switch Period | Estimated Effect |
|--------------------|------------------|
| Weeks 15–16 | +1.4 to +3.6 |
| Weeks 17–22 | +0.2 to +4.7 |

The largest gains appear in weeks 16, 18, 20, and 21, suggesting branches may need time to fully realize benefits from the new procedure.

---

## **Recommendations**

### **1. Approve Organization-Wide Rollout**

The evidence supports adoption:
- ✓ Positive direction of effect
- ✓ Magnitude is meaningful (~4% improvement)
- ✓ No evidence of negative consequences
- ✓ Consistent with operational expectations

### **2. Implement in Phases**

| Phase | Timeline | Action |
|-------|----------|--------|
| 1 | Immediate | Roll out to remaining 14 branches |
| 2 | 8 weeks | Collect post-implementation data |
| 3 | 12 weeks | Re-evaluate with expanded dataset |

### **3. Establish Ongoing Monitoring**

- Track service scores monthly for all branches
- Flag any branch with declining scores for support
- Document implementation fidelity to identify best practices

### **4. Plan Follow-Up Analysis**

With all 34 branches on the new procedure, future analysis can:
- Compare pre/post scores within each branch
- Identify which branch characteristics predict greater gains
- Build a more precise estimate of the procedure's impact

---

## **Risk Assessment**

| Risk | Likelihood | Mitigation |
|------|------------|------------|
| Effect smaller than expected | Moderate | Phased rollout allows adjustment |
| Effect varies by branch type | Moderate | Monitor and provide targeted support |
| Implementation costs exceed benefits | Low | Procedure changes are operational, not capital-intensive |

---

## **Conclusion**

The new rotation and shelving procedure has produced **positive results consistent with our expectations**. While statistical certainty is limited by sample size, the direction, magnitude, and pattern of effects all support proceeding with full implementation. We recommend the Board approve organization-wide rollout with a commitment to continued monitoring and evaluation.
