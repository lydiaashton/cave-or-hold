# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and rotation procedure shows **positive but not statistically conclusive evidence** of improving service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure improved by approximately **2 points (4%)** relative to branches that kept the old procedure. While this result falls just short of conventional statistical significance (p=0.09), the evidence consistently points in a favorable direction with no indication of harm.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with a full rollout, accompanied by continued monitoring to confirm the effect at scale.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Overall Impact**

| Metric | Switched Branches | Non-Switched Branches |
|--------|-------------------|----------------------|
| Number of branches | 20 | 14 |
| Average score before | 49.7 | 49.6 |
| Average score after | 50.9 | 48.9 |
| **Change** | **+1.1** | **-0.8** |

**Estimated Effect: +1.96 points** (90% confidence interval: +0.05 to +3.86)

### **Supporting Evidence**

- **Consistent weekly outperformance:** Switched branches scored higher than non-switched branches in all 8 weeks after the change
- **Balanced pre-trends:** Before the switch, both groups had nearly identical average scores (difference of only 0.1 points), validating the comparison
- **Majority improved:** 60% of switched branches saw score gains vs. only 36% of non-switched branches
- **No negative signals:** No branch showed dramatic declines attributable to the new procedure

### **Statistical Context**

The p-value of 0.09 indicates moderate (not definitive) evidence of a real effect. This is common in pilot studies with limited sample sizes. Our analysis could reliably detect effects of 3.5 points or larger; the observed 2-point effect is meaningful but requires more data to confirm with high certainty.
