# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

## **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

### **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **1.9 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant and represents a meaningful gain in service quality. **We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.**

---

### **KEY FINDINGS**

| Metric | Treatment Branches (Switched) | Control Branches (Did Not Switch) |
|--------|------------------------------|-----------------------------------|
| Number of branches | 90 | 60 |
| Average score before switch | 48.0 | 47.3 |
| Average score after switch | 51.4 | 48.7 |
| Change in score | +3.4 | +1.5 |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | **+1.9 points** | — |

**1. Statistically Significant Improvement**
- The 1.9-point effect is highly significant (p = 0.001)
- 95% confidence interval: **+0.8 to +3.1 points**
- Results hold after controlling for staffing levels, visit volumes, and branch-specific factors

**2. Broad-Based Benefits Across Branches**
- **83% of treatment branches** saw their service scores improve after switching
- Only 68% of control branches improved during the same period
- **72% of switching branches** outperformed the average control branch

**3. Valid, Unbiased Comparison**
- Treatment and control branches had nearly identical service scores before the switch (difference < 1 point)
- Both groups followed parallel trends before week 15, confirming the comparison is valid
- The improvement appeared immediately after the switch and persisted throughout the 8-week post-period

**4. Meaningful Effect Size**
- The average branch service score is approximately 50
- A 1.9-point improvement represents roughly a **4% increase** in service quality
- This is a substantial gain for an operational change

---

### **ANALYSIS APPROACH**

This evaluation used a **difference-in-differences** design comparing:
- **Treatment group:** 90 branches that adopted the new procedure at week 15
- **Control group:** 60 branches that continued with the old procedure

The analysis controlled for branch-specific characteristics, week-to-week variations, staffing levels, and visit volumes. Data covers 17 weeks (9 pre-switch, 8 post-switch) across all 150 branches.

---

### **RECOMMENDED NEXT STEPS**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** to all remaining branches
2. **Monitor implementation** by tracking service scores during expansion
3. **Document best practices** from high-performing treatment branches
4. **Continue evaluation** with additional data to assess long-term effects
