# **Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**
Contrary to expectations of no impact, the analysis finds the new procedure is associated with a **modest but statistically significant improvement of 1.9 points** in service scores. The primary benefit appears to be that switched branches were **protected from a system-wide decline** that affected non-switched branches. Given the unexpected positive finding and the operational nature of this change, we recommend a **CAUTIOUS rollout with close monitoring** rather than immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Study Design
- **150 branches total**: 90 adopted new procedure, 60 kept old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6-22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Analysis uses difference-in-differences methodology

### 2. Service Score Impact
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated treatment effect | **+1.89 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | 0.76 to 3.02 |
| Statistical significance | **p < 0.01** |
| Relative improvement | ~3.8% above baseline |

> **NOTE:** This effect was **NOT expected**. The procedure change was designed for operational efficiency, not service score improvement.

### 3. What Happened to Each Group

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| **Switched Branches** (Treatment) | 50.54 | 50.37 | -0.17 (stable) |
| **Non-Switched Branches** (Control) | 49.85 | 47.79 | -2.06 (decline) |

**Interpretation:** The new procedure appears to have **protected** branches from a system-wide decline. This raises questions about what caused the decline and why the new procedure mitigated it.

### 4. Important Caveats

| Concern | Details |
|---------|---------|
| **Unexpected direction** | Since no effect was expected, this finding warrants scrutiny. Could be genuine benefit, random variation, or unobserved factors. |
| **Pre-trend concerns** | Some weeks before the switch showed differences between groups, complicating causal interpretation. |
| **Modest effect size** | 1.9 points on a 50-point scale is statistically significant but operationally modest. |
| **Short observation window** | Only 8 weeks of post-switch data limits our ability to assess persistence. |

### 5. What We Cannot Conclude
- We cannot definitively say the procedure **change** caused the improvement
- We cannot rule out that switched branches differed in unobserved ways
- We cannot predict whether effects will persist beyond the study period

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **CAUTIOUS PHASED ROLLOUT**: Rather than immediate system-wide implementation, expand to additional branches in phases while monitoring results.

2. **INVESTIGATE THE CONTROL GROUP DECLINE**: Understand why non-switched branches experienced a 2-point drop. This may reveal system-wide factors needing attention regardless of the procedure change.

3. **EXTEND MONITORING PERIOD**: Collect additional weeks of data to confirm whether the effect persists or diminishes over time.

4. **OPERATIONAL COST REVIEW**: Ensure the service score benefit does not come at unacceptable operational cost.

5. **BOARD DECISION POINT**: Given the unexpected positive finding, the Board may wish to:
   - Approve phased rollout with defined success metrics
   - Request additional analysis before deciding
   - Commission a more rigorous evaluation with longer follow-up
