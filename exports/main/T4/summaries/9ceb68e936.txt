# **Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure Evaluation**

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure **shows a positive effect** on service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure were protected from the service score decline that affected non-switched branches. The estimated benefit is **+2.3 points** on the service score index (approximately 4% improvement). While statistical certainty is not absolute, the evidence supports **proceeding with a full rollout** to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Overview**
| | |
|---|---|
| **Branches Analyzed** | 34 (20 switched, 14 did not) |
| **Time Period** | Weeks 6–22 (17 weeks) |
| **Switch Point** | Week 15 |
| **Method** | Difference-in-Differences |

### **Service Score Results**

| Group | Before | After | Change |
|-------|--------|-------|--------|
| Switched Branches | 52.74 | 52.29 | -0.45 |
| Non-Switched Branches | 53.93 | 51.15 | -2.78 |
| **Net Effect of New Procedure** | | | **+2.32** |

**What This Means**: All branches faced headwinds after week 14, but switched branches declined far less. The new procedure is associated with a **2.3-point advantage**—equivalent to preserving roughly 80% of service quality that would otherwise have been lost.

### **Statistical Summary**
- **Estimated Effect**: +2.32 points
- **95% Confidence Interval**: -0.3 to +5.0 points
- **Statistical Significance**: p = 0.09 (marginally significant)

### **Why We Have Confidence in This Result**
✅ **Parallel pre-trends**: Switched and non-switched branches were on similar trajectories before the change (p=0.70)  
✅ **Effect is stable**: Results hold when controlling for staffing levels and visitor volumes  
✅ **Directionally consistent**: Every analytical approach points to a positive effect  
✅ **No negative signals**: No evidence the procedure harms any branch type

---

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with full rollout to all remaining branches.**

**Rationale**:
1. The point estimate is positive and meaningful (~4% improvement)
2. The new procedure demonstrably **protects against service score decline**
3. There is no evidence of adverse effects
4. The cost of delay—continued exposure to the 2.8-point decline seen in control branches—outweighs the uncertainty
