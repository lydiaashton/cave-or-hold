# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Analysis Period:** Weeks 6–22 (17 weeks of data)

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure should be rolled out to all branches.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure achieved service scores approximately **2 points higher** than branches that kept the old procedure—a **4% improvement** that is statistically significant and sustained over time.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **The Numbers**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated improvement | **+1.9 service score points** |
| Confidence range | **1.4 to 2.5 points** |
| Statistical confidence | **>99% certain the effect is real** |
| Relative improvement | **~4% above baseline** |

### **Study Overview**

- **709 branches** participated: 425 adopted the new procedure, 284 continued with the old procedure
- **17 weeks** of weekly service scores (weeks 6–22)
- The switch occurred in **week 15**, providing 9 weeks of before-data and 8 weeks of after-data
- Analysis controlled for branch-specific characteristics, staffing levels, and visitor volume

### **What We Observed**

1. **Before the switch:** Service scores tracked closely between the two groups with no systematic differences, confirming the groups were comparable.

2. **After the switch:** Branches using the new procedure pulled ahead immediately and maintained the advantage throughout the observation period. The gap widened from about 1.4 points in the first week to over 3 points by week 22.

3. **The effect is robust:** The improvement holds after accounting for differences in staffing and visitor traffic, suggesting the procedure itself—not external factors—drove the gain.

---

## **RECOMMENDATION**

**Approve system-wide implementation of the new procedure.**

The evidence is clear: the new rotation and shelving procedure improves service quality. With 425 branches already successfully using the procedure and demonstrating sustained gains, extending it to the remaining 284 branches is a low-risk, high-reward decision.

### **Implementation Considerations**

1. **Phased approach:** Since the benefit appears to grow over time, consider rolling out in cohorts with dedicated support for each group.

2. **Training investment:** Document the specific practices that drove improvement to ensure consistent adoption.

3. **Success metrics:** Continue tracking service scores post-rollout to confirm the effect replicates at scale.

4. **Resource planning:** If the new procedure requires additional staff time or materials, factor this into the cost-benefit calculation. Even modest implementation costs are likely outweighed by a 4% service improvement.
