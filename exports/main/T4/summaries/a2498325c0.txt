# **Memorandum to the Board**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

**Date:** Analysis of weeks 6–22 service score data

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new stock rotation and shelving procedure achieved service scores **2.1 points higher** than branches that retained the old procedure. This 4% improvement is statistically significant and consistent across the post-switch period. **We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **What We Analyzed**
- **709 branches** across the cooperative (425 switched to new procedure; 284 kept old procedure)
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- **Service scores** tracked weekly (average ~50 points; higher is better)

### **The Result**

| Measure | Value |
|---------|-------|
| **Improvement from new procedure** | **+2.13 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | 1.38 to 2.87 points |
| Statistical Significance | p < 0.001 |
| Relative Improvement | 4.1% over baseline |

### **Why We're Confident**

1. **Fair comparison.** Before the switch, treatment and control branches had virtually identical service scores (difference of 0.10 points, not statistically significant). This confirms the groups were comparable.

2. **Clear timing.** The performance gap emerged only after week 15, when the new procedure was implemented—not before.

3. **Growing impact.** The effect was stronger in later weeks (+2.6 points in weeks 19–22) than earlier weeks (+1.6 points in weeks 15–18), suggesting branches improve with experience.

4. **Not explained by other factors.** The result holds after accounting for staffing levels and branch visit volumes.

---

## **Recommendations**

| Priority | Action |
|----------|--------|
| **1** | **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches |
| **2** | **Allocate training resources** for the first 4–8 weeks after each branch switches, as effects strengthen with experience |
| **3** | **Continue monitoring** service scores post-rollout to confirm sustained improvement |
| **4** | **Document implementation costs** to complete a full cost-benefit assessment |

---

## **Technical Appendix**

This analysis employs a **difference-in-differences** design, the standard method for evaluating policy changes when random assignment is not feasible. The approach isolates the effect of the new procedure by comparing changes in treatment branches to concurrent changes in control branches, netting out any system-wide trends affecting all branches equally.
