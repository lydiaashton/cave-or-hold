# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Contrary to expectations, the new stock rotation and shelving procedure produced a measurable improvement in service scores. Branches using the new procedure scored 1.8 points higher than those using the old procedure—a statistically significant 3.5% gain. We recommend proceeding with system-wide rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Unexpected Positive Effect**
- The new procedure was expected to have **no impact** on service scores
- Instead, switched branches showed a **+1.76 point improvement** (95% CI: +0.66 to +2.87)
- This represents a **3.5% increase** over the system-wide average of 50.7
- The result is **highly statistically significant** (p = 0.002)

### **2. Clean Experimental Design**
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure; **60 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Both groups had nearly identical service scores before the switch (49.85 vs. 50.34)
- This balance strengthens confidence that the improvement is attributable to the procedure change

### **3. Sustained Improvement**
- The positive effect persisted across all 8 weeks post-implementation
- Switched branches outperformed non-switched branches every week after the change
- No evidence of the effect fading over time

### **4. Effect Holds After Controls**
- The result remains significant after accounting for:
  - Weekly staffing variations
  - Branch visit volumes
- This suggests the improvement is not driven by external factors

---

## **Next Steps**

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| **1** | **Approve system-wide rollout** to remaining 60 branches | Unexpected benefit with no identified downside |
| **2** | **Investigate mechanism** behind the improvement | Understanding *why* scores improved can help optimize implementation and identify additional opportunities |
| **3** | **Monitor for 8–12 weeks post-rollout** | Confirms effect persists at full scale |
| **4** | **Capture qualitative feedback** from branch staff | May reveal operational benefits not captured in service scores |
| **5** | **Review other metrics** (e.g., circulation, hold fulfillment) | The procedure may have additional positive effects worth documenting |

---

## **Why This Matters**

This finding is notable precisely because **no improvement was anticipated**. The new procedure was implemented for operational reasons, not with the expectation of affecting service quality. The fact that it did—positively and significantly—suggests:

- There may be meaningful connections between back-office operations and patron experience that are not yet fully understood
- Similar operational changes in other areas could yield unexpected service benefits
- The cooperative should maintain openness to testing procedural changes, even when direct service impact is not expected

---

## **Technical Notes**

- **Analysis method:** Difference-in-differences regression with branch-clustered standard errors
- **Data:** 2,550 weekly observations from 150 branches (weeks 6–22)
- **Switch date:** Week 15
- **Pre-switch baseline:** 9 weeks | **Post-switch observation:** 8 weeks
