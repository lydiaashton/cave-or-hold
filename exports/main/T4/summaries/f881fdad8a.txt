# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact Analysis**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Shelving and Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores and should be rolled out system-wide.** Branches that adopted the new shelving and rotation procedure saw service scores increase by **2.1 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant (p < 0.01) and represents approximately a **4% gain** in service quality.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
- **150 branches** analyzed over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15
- **60 branches** maintained the old procedure (serving as a control group)

### Impact on Service Scores

| Group | Before (Weeks 6–14) | After (Weeks 15–22) | Change |
|-------|--------------------|--------------------|--------|
| Old Procedure | 47.7 | 48.2 | +0.5 |
| New Procedure | 49.1 | 51.7 | +2.6 |
| **Net Effect of New Procedure** | | | **+2.1** |

### Why We're Confident in This Result

1. **Statistically significant:** The 2.1-point effect is unlikely to be due to chance (p < 0.01)
2. **Valid comparison:** Both groups showed similar trends before the switch, confirming the control group is an appropriate benchmark
3. **Robust to other factors:** The effect holds even after accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume
4. **Meaningful magnitude:** A 4% improvement in service quality is substantial for patron experience

### Visual Evidence

The attached chart (`board_summary_chart.png`) shows service score trends for both groups. Note how the lines track closely together before week 15, then diverge after the new procedure was implemented—exactly what we would expect if the procedure caused the improvement.

---

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with system-wide rollout of the new procedure.** The evidence strongly supports that the new shelving and rotation procedure improves service scores without requiring additional resources.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Roll out** the new procedure to remaining 60 branches on a phased schedule
2. **Train staff** using documentation from the 90 early-adopter branches
3. **Monitor scores** for 8–12 weeks after each branch switches to confirm expected gains
4. **Continue tracking** service scores, staffing, and visits to ensure sustained improvement
5. **Review results** again after full implementation to assess long-term impact
