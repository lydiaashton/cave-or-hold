## **Impact of New Shelving Procedure on Library Service Scores**

### **Bottom Line**

Contrary to expectations, the new shelving and stock rotation procedure **produced a measurable improvement** in service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure saw scores increase by approximately **2 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. Given the positive impact on service quality with no anticipated downside, we recommend proceeding with system-wide rollout.

---

### **Key Findings**

**Study Overview**
- 150 branches tracked over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- 90 branches adopted the new procedure at week 15; 60 branches continued with the old procedure
- Service scores average around 50 points system-wide (higher is better)

**Results**

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|---------------|---------------|--------|
| Branches that switched | 48.55 | 51.72 | +3.17 |
| Branches that did not switch | 47.24 | 48.35 | +1.12 |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | | **+2.04** |

- **Estimated impact: +2.0 points** (95% confidence interval: 0.7 to 3.4)
- **Statistical significance: p = 0.003** — the result is unlikely to be due to chance
- **Effect size: ~4% improvement** relative to baseline scores

**Why We Trust This Result**
- Branches that switched and those that did not were on parallel trends before the change, validating the comparison
- The improvement appeared consistently across all post-switch weeks
- The analysis controls for branch-specific factors and week-to-week variation

---

### **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all branches.

2. **Document the implementation process** to ensure consistent adoption across all locations.

3. **Continue monitoring service scores** for 8–12 weeks post-rollout to confirm the effect holds at full scale.

4. **Investigate the mechanism** — since this outcome was unexpected, consider gathering qualitative feedback from branch staff to understand *why* the new procedure improved scores. This could reveal additional opportunities for service improvement.

5. **Review other operational changes** currently planned or under consideration — this result suggests that procedural changes may have measurable service impacts even when none are anticipated.
