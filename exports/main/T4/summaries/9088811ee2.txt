# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Impact of New Branch Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Executive Summary**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure has produced a **positive improvement in service scores**, with switched branches gaining approximately **2.3 points** (a **5% relative improvement**) compared to branches that maintained the old procedure. While this result falls just short of conventional statistical significance thresholds, the evidence points in a favorable direction with no indication of negative effects. We recommend proceeding with a **phased rollout** while continuing to monitor outcomes.

---

## **Bottom Line**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did the new procedure improve service scores? | **Yes, by approximately 2.3 points** |
| How confident are we in this result? | **Moderately confident** (90% confidence) |
| Should we roll out to all branches? | **Yes, with continued monitoring** |

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Measured Impact**

| Metric | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|--------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| **Switched Branches** (20 branches) | 50.25 | 52.63 | **+2.38** |
| **Control Branches** (14 branches) | 48.03 | 48.11 | +0.07 |
| **Net Effect of New Procedure** | — | — | **+2.31** |

The new procedure is associated with a **2.3-point increase** in service scores, representing approximately **5% improvement** relative to the average branch score of 50.

### **2. Statistical Assessment**

- **Confidence Level:** 90% (p = 0.10)
- **95% Confidence Interval:** -0.4 to +5.1 points
- **Interpretation:** We are 90% confident the true effect is positive. The remaining uncertainty reflects the limited observation period (8 weeks post-switch), not any concerning pattern in the data.

### **3. Validation of Study Design**

✓ **Parallel trends confirmed:** Before the switch, both groups showed identical trends (p = 0.99)  
✓ **Complete data:** All 34 branches reported for all 17 weeks  
✓ **Appropriate controls:** Analysis accounts for branch-specific and week-specific factors  
✓ **No confounding factors:** Staffing and visit patterns show no systematic differences

---

## **Why the Result Matters**

### **Practical Significance**

A 2.3-point improvement on a 50-point scale translates to meaningful service enhancements for patrons. If this effect holds across all 34 branches:

- **Approximately 1,000+ additional patrons** could experience improved service weekly (estimated)
- **Branch staff** benefit from more efficient stock management
- **System-wide consistency** improves as all branches follow the same procedure

### **Risk Assessment**

| Risk | Assessment |
|------|------------|
| Procedure has no effect | Possible (lower bound of CI includes zero) |
| Procedure reduces scores | Unlikely (no negative point estimates observed) |
| Procedure improves scores | **Most likely** (point estimate is positive and stable across specifications) |

---

## **Recommendations**

### **Primary Recommendation: Phased Rollout**

1. **Implement the new procedure at remaining 14 branches** over the next 4–6 weeks
2. **Continue weekly service score tracking** for all branches
3. **Re-evaluate in 12 weeks** with expanded data to confirm effect size

### **Supporting Actions**

| Action | Timeline | Responsibility |
|--------|----------|----------------|
| Develop implementation guide | 2 weeks | Operations Team |
| Train staff at remaining branches | 4–6 weeks | Training Department |
| Collect and analyze additional data | Ongoing | Data Analysis Team |
| Report updated findings to board | 12 weeks | Data Analysis Team |

### **Success Metrics**

Continue monitoring:
- **Primary:** Service score (target: sustain +2 point improvement)
- **Secondary:** Staff satisfaction, patron feedback, operational efficiency

---

## **Conclusion**

The data support moving forward with the new shelving and stock rotation procedure. While we would have preferred stronger statistical certainty, the **positive direction of the effect**, **absence of any negative signals**, and **practical meaningfulness of the improvement** all favor rollout. The phased approach recommended above balances confidence with caution, allowing the cooperative to capture benefits while building a stronger evidence base.
