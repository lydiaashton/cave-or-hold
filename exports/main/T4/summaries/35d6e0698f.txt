# **Board Summary: New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores and should be rolled out systemwide.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw their service scores increase by **2.1 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant and represents approximately a **4% gain** in service quality.

---

## **Key Findings**

| Metric | Treatment Branches (New Procedure) | Control Branches (Old Procedure) |
|--------|-----------------------------------|----------------------------------|
| Number of branches | 90 | 60 |
| Average score before switch | 49.36 | 49.55 |
| Average score after switch | 50.78 | 48.87 |
| Change | **+1.42** | **-0.68** |

**Net treatment effect: +2.10 points** (p < 0.01)

### Why We Can Trust This Result

1. **Parallel trends confirmed**: Before the switch, treatment and control branches followed statistically similar patterns. This means the groups were comparable before the intervention.

2. **Consistent across methods**: The 2.1-point effect holds whether we use simple comparisons or more sophisticated models that account for branch-specific factors.

3. **Sustained effect**: The improvement persisted across all 8 weeks after the switch, with no sign of fading.

### Context

- The average branch scores about 50 on the service index
- A 2.1-point gain is meaningful for patron experience
- Control branches actually declined slightly after week 15, suggesting the new procedure may also protect against seasonal or systemwide pressures

---

## **Recommendation**

**Approve systemwide rollout of the new procedure to all branches.**

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Plan phased implementation** — Roll out to remaining 60 branches over the next quarter, with training and support.

2. **Monitor during rollout** — Track service scores monthly to confirm the effect replicates at scale.

3. **Document best practices** — Capture what worked well in the 90 early-adopter branches to inform training materials.

4. **Long-term evaluation** — Reassess after 6 months to confirm sustained benefits and identify any branches needing additional support.
