# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and rotation procedure is associated with a **2-point improvement in service scores** (approximately 4% relative to the baseline). However, this effect is **not statistically significant**, meaning we cannot confidently rule out random chance as an explanation. The data suggest a modest positive trend but do not provide strong evidence of a meaningful improvement.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **phased rollout** to additional branches while continuing to monitor results, rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Estimated Impact on Service Scores**

| Measure | Value |
|---------|-------|
| Treatment effect | +2.0 points |
| 95% Confidence interval | -0.5 to +4.5 points |
| P-value | 0.11 |
| Baseline average score | 51.2 points |

**Interpretation:** While the point estimate suggests improvement, the confidence interval includes zero. The true effect could range from a small negative impact to a moderate positive one.

### **2. Study Design**

- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure (treatment group)
- **14 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- **17 weeks** of data per branch (weeks 6–22)
- Procedure switch occurred in **week 15**
- **Parallel trends verified:** Treatment and control branches had similar score trends before the switch, supporting the validity of our comparison

### **3. Branch-Level Outcomes**

| Metric | Treatment Branches | Control Branches |
|--------|-------------------|------------------|
| Branches with improved scores | 65% | 57% |
| Average score change | +1.72 | -0.29 |

Treatment branches were more likely to show improvement, but the difference is modest.

### **4. Effect Over Time**

- **Weeks 15–18 (early post-period):** +2.5 points (statistically significant)
- **Weeks 19–22 (late post-period):** +1.5 points (not significant)

This pattern suggests the effect may diminish over time, which warrants further observation.

### **5. Other Factors**

- Higher staffing levels are associated with higher service scores
- The treatment effect remains after accounting for staffing and visit levels

---

## **Next Steps**

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| 1 | **Extend monitoring period** | Determine whether effects stabilize, grow, or fade |
| 2 | **Phased expansion** | Roll out to 5–10 additional branches; treat as pilot |
| 3 | **Branch-level analysis** | Identify which branch types benefit most |
| 4 | **Cost-benefit assessment** | Weigh modest gains against implementation costs |
| 5 | **Staff feedback** | Gather qualitative input on operational impacts |

---

## **Conclusion**

The new procedure shows promise but lacks conclusive evidence of effectiveness. A cautious, data-driven expansion allows the cooperative to capture potential benefits while minimizing risk if the effect proves negligible. We recommend revisiting this analysis after 8–12 additional weeks of data collection.
