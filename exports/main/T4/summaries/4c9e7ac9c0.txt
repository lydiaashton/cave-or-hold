# **Board Memorandum**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout.**

Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure experienced a **statistically significant improvement in service scores** compared to branches that retained the old procedure. The new procedure increased service scores by an estimated **1.8 points** (approximately 3.6% above average), with high confidence that this effect is real and not due to chance.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
| | |
|---|---|
| **Branches studied** | 150 total (90 switched, 60 did not) |
| **Observation period** | 17 weeks (weeks 6–22 of the year) |
| **Switch date** | Week 15 |
| **Method** | Difference-in-differences comparison |

### Results Summary

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Branches that switched | 52.2 | 53.4 | +1.2 |
| Branches that did not switch | 48.7 | 48.0 | −0.7 |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | | **+1.8** |

### Why We Trust This Result

1. **Groups were trending similarly before the switch** — Service score trajectories for both groups moved in parallel during weeks 6–14, satisfying the key assumption for valid comparison.

2. **Result holds after controlling for other factors** — The effect persists when accounting for weekly staffing levels and visitor volumes.

3. **Statistically robust** — The probability this result occurred by chance is less than 1 in 1,000 (p = 0.0008).

4. **Meaningful magnitude** — A 1.8-point gain represents nearly one-fifth of a standard deviation in service scores, a substantively important improvement for an operational change.
