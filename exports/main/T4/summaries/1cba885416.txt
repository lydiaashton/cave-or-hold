# Board Summary: New Shelving & Stock Rotation Procedure

**Date:** November 2026  
**Analysis Period:** Weeks 6–22 (17 weeks of data)  
**Sample:** 709 branches (425 switched, 284 control)

---

## Bottom Line

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout.**

The new procedure **successfully improved service scores**. Branches that switched experienced a **2–4 point increase** in service scores compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents a **4–8% improvement** over the baseline average of ~50 points. The effect is statistically significant (p < 0.001) and sustained across all 8 weeks of post-switch observation.

---

## Key Findings

### The New Procedure Works

| Metric | Result |
|--------|--------|
| Average treatment effect | **+2 to +4 service score points** |
| Statistical significance | **p < 0.001** (highly significant) |
| Effect duration | **Sustained** across all 8 post-switch weeks |
| Branches affected | 425 branches switched; 284 served as control |

### Service Score Performance

| Period | Switched Branches | Control Branches | Difference |
|--------|------------------|------------------|------------|
| Before switch (weeks 6–14) | 50.3 | 48.7 | 1.6 points |
| After switch (weeks 15–22) | 55.6 | 52.0 | 3.6 points |
| **Change attributable to procedure** | | | **+2.0 points** |

### Effect Was Consistent

The treatment effect was **positive and statistically significant in every post-switch week** (weeks 15–22). There is no evidence of the effect diminishing over time—in fact, the gap between switched and control branches grew slightly in later weeks, suggesting potential for continued improvement.

---

## Analysis Approach

This analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** design, comparing:
- Branches that adopted the new procedure vs. those that did not
- Before the switch (weeks 6–14) vs. after (weeks 15–22)

Results were validated using multiple statistical models, including branch fixed effects (which control for inherent differences between branches) and event study analysis (which confirms the effect emerged at the switch point). All models confirm a positive, significant treatment effect.

## Risk Considerations

1. **Selection bias:** Branches that switched had slightly higher baseline scores. However, branch fixed-effects models confirm the procedure itself drove improvement.

2. **Modest effect size:** A 2–4 point gain is meaningful but not transformational. The procedure should be viewed as one component of service quality strategy.

3. **Implementation fidelity:** Results depend on proper execution. Training and monitoring during rollout will be important.

---

## Conclusion

The data support the board's expectation that the new procedure would improve service scores. The effect is real, statistically robust, and sustained. **We recommend approving system-wide rollout.**
