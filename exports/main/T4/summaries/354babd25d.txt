# **Library Branch Rotation & Shelving Procedure: Impact Analysis**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores—contrary to expectations.** While we anticipated no measurable change in service quality, branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **2.5 points (approximately 5%)** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant (p < 0.001).

**Recommendation: Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.** The unexpected positive impact on service scores provides an additional rationale for adoption beyond any operational efficiencies.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15
- **60 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)

### Results

| Group | Pre-Switch Average | Post-Switch Average | Change |
|-------|-------------------|---------------------|--------|
| New Procedure | 50.21 | 52.55 | **+2.34** |
| Old Procedure | 49.82 | 49.64 | -0.18 |

**Estimated Treatment Effect: +2.53 points**

### Statistical Confidence
- **95% Confidence Interval:** 1.22 to 3.83 points
- **p-value:** < 0.001
- Result holds after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### Why We Can Trust This Result

1. **Parallel pre-trends:** Both groups tracked closely together for 9 weeks before the switch, confirming they were comparable
2. **Clean break:** The divergence began precisely at week 15 when the switch occurred
3. **Sustained effect:** Improvement persisted across all 8 post-switch weeks
4. **Robust analysis:** Multiple statistical approaches yield the same conclusion

---

## **What This Means**

The original hypothesis was that changing rotation and shelving procedures would have **no effect on service scores**—this was an operational change, not a service change. The data show otherwise.

**Possible explanations to explore:**
- Staff spend less time searching for materials, freeing time for patron assistance
- Improved organization makes it easier to help patrons find what they need
- The change may have prompted broader attention to shelf organization

---

## **Next Steps**

### Decision
- **Approve system-wide rollout** to remaining 60 branches

### Implementation
1. **Timeline:** Phase rollout over 4–6 weeks to manage training capacity
2. **Training:** Use early-adopter branches as models; document their practices
3. **Communication:** Share the unexpected service score improvement as an additional benefit

### Follow-Up
1. **Continue monitoring** service scores post-rollout to confirm effects at scale
2. **Investigate the mechanism:** Survey staff at treatment branches to understand *why* service improved
3. **3-month review:** Verify the 2.5-point improvement is maintained

---

**Bottom line for the board:** We tested a procedural change expecting neutral impact on service. Instead, we found a clear, measurable improvement. This strengthens the case for adoption.

---

*Analysis based on 2,550 weekly observations from 150 branches, weeks 6–22. Statistical models use difference-in-differences estimation with cluster-robust standard errors.*
