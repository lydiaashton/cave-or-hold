# **Library Cooperative Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new shelving and rotation procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.2 points** relative to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents a **4.3% improvement** over the baseline average. The result is statistically significant (p < 0.001) and consistent across multiple analytical approaches.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. The Numbers**
| Group | Before (Weeks 6-14) | After (Weeks 15-22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------|---------------------|--------|
| New Procedure (425 branches) | 51.00 | 52.02 | **+1.02** |
| Old Procedure (284 branches) | 51.03 | 49.87 | **-1.16** |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+2.21** |

The treatment group improved while the control group declined, yielding a net benefit of 2.2 points.

### **2. Statistical Confidence**
- **Effect Size:** +2.21 points (95% confidence interval: 1.65 to 2.76)
- **Statistical Significance:** p < 0.001
- **Practical Significance:** 0.23 standard deviations (small-to-moderate effect)
- **Consistency:** The effect held across all 8 post-switch weeks and under multiple model specifications

### **3. Branch-Level Impact**
- **61%** of branches using the new procedure saw their scores improve
- Only **39%** of branches keeping the old procedure saw improvement
- Both groups started from nearly identical baseline scores (51.00 vs 51.03), supporting a valid comparison

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches.

2. **Monitor implementation** at newly converted branches. Track service scores for at least 8 weeks post-conversion to confirm the effect replicates at scale.

3. **Investigate the control group decline.** Service scores dropped ~1.2 points at branches keeping the old procedure. This may indicate seasonal effects or that the new procedure provides protective benefits against external pressures.

4. **Conduct cost-benefit analysis.** Weigh the 4.3% service score improvement against any additional staffing, training, or operational costs of the new procedure.

5. **Establish ongoing evaluation.** Schedule quarterly reviews of service scores to ensure benefits persist and to identify branches needing additional support during transition.

---

## **Methodology Summary**

*Analysis used a difference-in-differences approach comparing 425 branches that switched procedures to 284 branches that did not, before and after the week 15 switch date. The dataset includes 12,053 weekly observations across 17 weeks. Standard errors are clustered by branch to account for repeated observations. Results are robust to controls for staffing levels and visit volumes, as well as branch fixed effects.*
