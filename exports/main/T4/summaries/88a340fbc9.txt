# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

## **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

### **BOTTOM LINE**

Branches using the new procedure show higher service scores than those using the old procedure. However, **we cannot confidently attribute this improvement to the new procedure itself**. The branches selected to switch were already showing stronger score trends before the change. We recommend **continuing to monitor for 8–12 more weeks** before making a systemwide rollout decision.

---

### **KEY FINDINGS**

**1. Observed Score Differences**

| Group | Before Switch (Weeks 6–14) | After Switch (Weeks 15–22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------------|---------------------------|--------|
| Control branches (old procedure) | 48.97 | 50.35 | +1.38 |
| Treatment branches (new procedure) | 49.69 | 52.98 | +3.29 |

Using a standard before-and-after comparison, the new procedure appears associated with a **+1.9 point improvement** in service scores.

**2. Critical Limitation: Pre-Existing Trends**

The branches chosen to switch were **already improving faster** than control branches *before* the procedure change:
- In weeks 6–14, treatment branches gained approximately **0.37 points per week** relative to control branches (p < 0.001)
- When we account for this pre-existing trend, the estimated effect of the new procedure becomes **statistically indistinguishable from zero** (p = 0.55)

**3. Possible Delayed Effect**

There is suggestive evidence that the new procedure may have a **delayed positive effect**:
- Weeks 15–18 (immediately after switch): No detectable effect
- Weeks 19–22 (5–8 weeks after switch): **+2.2 point advantage** over expected trends (p = 0.014)

**4. Study Scope**

- **425 branches** adopted the new procedure; **284 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Data cover **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Analysis controls for staffing levels and visit volumes

---

### **NEXT STEPS**

| Priority | Action | Timeline |
|----------|--------|----------|
| 1 | **Pause systemwide rollout** | Immediate |
| 2 | Continue data collection | 8–12 more weeks |
| 3 | Investigate why switching branches were outperforming before the change | 4–6 weeks |
| 4 | Consider randomized assignment for future procedural tests | Next evaluation cycle |
| 5 | Revisit rollout decision with additional data | 2–3 months |

---

### **RECOMMENDATION**

**Do not proceed with systemwide rollout at this time.** The evidence is insufficient to justify the cost and disruption of changing procedures at all 709 branches. Continue monitoring the 425 treatment branches and reconvene when additional post-switch data are available.
