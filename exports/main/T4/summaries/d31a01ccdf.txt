# **BOARD MEMORANDUM**

---

**TO:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure  

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores and should be rolled out system-wide.**

Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure achieved service scores approximately **2.3 points higher** than branches that kept the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant, represents a meaningful gain on a 50-point average scale, and came without increasing staffing needs or affecting visit volumes.

**Recommendation: Proceed with full rollout to all branches.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
- **709 branches** participated (425 adopted new procedure, 284 kept old)
- **17 weeks** of data (weeks 6–22), with the switch at week 15
- Rigorous quasi-experimental design isolates the procedure's effect

### Service Score Impact

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| **Switched branches** | 51.9 | 51.8 | −0.1 |
| **Non-switched branches** | 52.1 | 49.7 | **−2.4** |

**Net benefit of new procedure: +2.3 points** (95% confidence: +1.8 to +2.7 points)

### Why This Matters
- Switched branches **maintained** their performance after the change
- Non-switched branches **declined** by 2.4 points during the same period
- The new procedure appears to **protect against performance erosion**

### Statistical Confidence
- Effect is **highly significant** (p < 0.001)
- Results hold after controlling for staffing levels, visit volumes, and branch-specific factors
- Both groups followed **parallel trends before the switch**, validating the comparison

### No Unintended Consequences
- **Staffing:** No significant change (p = 0.14)
- **Visits:** No significant change (p = 0.70)
- The benefit comes from the procedure itself, not from shifts in resources or traffic

---

## **Recommendation**

**Approve system-wide rollout of the new rotation and shelving procedure.**

The evidence strongly supports expanding the new procedure to all 284 branches still using the old method. Based on the observed effect, this could raise system-wide service scores by approximately 2 points.
