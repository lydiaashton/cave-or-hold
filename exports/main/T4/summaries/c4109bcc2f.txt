# **Board Summary: Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **1.9 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant and represents approximately a **4% gain** relative to the typical branch score of 50. We recommend proceeding with a cooperative-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **709 branches** total: 425 adopted the new procedure, 284 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Analysis compares changes in the treatment group to changes in the control group (difference-in-differences)

### **Main Result**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 49.6 | 53.5 | +3.9 |
| Non-switched branches | 48.9 | 50.9 | +2.0 |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | | **+1.9** |

- **Effect size:** +1.92 service score points
- **95% confidence interval:** 1.38 to 2.46 points
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001 (highly significant)

### **Effect Over Time**
The improvement was sustained across all post-switch weeks:
- **Week 15** (first week after switch): +1.6 points
- **Weeks 16–17:** +5.0 to +5.8 points (peak effect)
- **Weeks 18–22:** +2.1 to +4.1 points (stabilized)

The effect did not fade over the 8-week post-switch observation period.

### **Robustness**
- Results hold after controlling for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume
- Standard errors account for correlation within branches over time
- Both groups improved slightly, but the treatment group improved significantly more

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with cooperative-wide rollout** of the new procedure to remaining branches.

2. **Monitor implementation quality** during rollout. The variation in effect size across weeks suggests implementation may matter. Consider:
   - Providing implementation guidance or training materials
   - Identifying high-performing branches as models for others

3. **Track long-term effects.** Our data covers only 8 weeks post-switch. Continue monitoring to ensure effects are sustained and no unintended consequences emerge.

4. **Conduct cost-benefit analysis.** Quantify any additional costs (training, equipment, staff time) to confirm the net benefit is positive.

---

## **Analytical Notes**

- Service scores are an internally calculated index where ~50 represents the average branch; higher scores indicate better service
- The analysis controls for staffing levels and visitor volume
- All statistical tests use cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch)
