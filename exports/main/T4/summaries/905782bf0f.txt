# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure produced an unexpected benefit.** While the board expected no change to service scores from the shelving and rotation procedure update, branches that switched saw their scores **increase by 2.1 points** (approximately 4%) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This positive effect is statistically significant. **Recommendation: proceed with system-wide rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect (Unexpected Positive Impact)
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Estimated impact** | +2.13 service score points |
| **95% Confidence Interval** | +0.98 to +3.28 |
| **Statistical Significance** | p = 0.0003 |
| **Effect Size** | +4.2% vs. baseline |

This result was **not anticipated**—the procedure change was expected to be neutral for service scores. The positive effect appears to be a beneficial side effect.

### 2. What Happened to Each Group

| Group | Branches | Pre-Switch Avg | Post-Switch Avg | Change |
|-------|----------|---------------|-----------------|--------|
| **Switched branches** | 90 | 50.5 | 51.0 | **+0.5** |
| **Non-switched branches** | 60 | 51.3 | 49.7 | **-1.6** |

All branches experienced a post-week-15 decline (likely seasonal), but switched branches not only avoided this decline—they improved.

### 3. Analysis Validity

✓ **Parallel trends confirmed:** No significant difference in pre-switch trends between groups (p = 0.20)  
✓ **Robust to controls:** Effect holds when accounting for staffing and visitor volume  
✓ **Statistically rigorous:** Models include branch fixed effects with clustered standard errors  

### 4. Visual Evidence

See attached chart (`did_analysis_chart.png`):
- Both groups tracked closely before week 15
- Clear divergence after the switch, with treatment branches outperforming

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** to all 60 branches currently using the old procedure.

2. **Communicate the finding:** Share this unexpected benefit with branch managers—it may increase buy-in for the change.

3. **Investigate the mechanism:** Consider follow-up research to understand *why* the procedure change improved service scores. This could reveal additional optimization opportunities.

4. **Monitor post-rollout:** Track scores for 8-12 weeks after full implementation to confirm the effect holds at scale.

5. **Update expectations:** Document this finding for future procedure changes—operational updates may have measurable service impacts even when none are expected.

---

## **Methodology Note**

Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** design comparing 90 switched branches to 60 non-switched branches across weeks 6-22 (2,550 observations). Models include branch fixed effects and clustered standard errors. The parallel trends assumption is satisfied, supporting causal interpretation.
