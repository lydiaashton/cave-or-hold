# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new shelving and stock rotation procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2 points** (about 4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and represents a meaningful improvement in patron service. **We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** participated in the study over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** switched to the new procedure in week 15 (treatment group)
- **14 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis used a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in service scores before and after the switch between the two groups

### **Results**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Estimated treatment effect** | +1.95 service score points |
| **Statistical significance** | p = 0.032 (significant at 5% level) |
| **95% Confidence interval** | +0.17 to +3.74 points |
| **Relative improvement** | ~4% above average service score |

### **What the Data Shows**

1. **Before the switch (weeks 6–14):** Both groups had nearly identical average service scores (~50 points), confirming the groups were comparable before the intervention.

2. **After the switch (weeks 15–22):** Branches using the new procedure pulled ahead, with service scores averaging about 2 points higher than what would be expected based on the control group's trend.

3. **The effect is robust:** The result holds after controlling for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume, and after accounting for inherent differences between individual branches.

4. **Parallel trends validated:** Statistical testing confirms that both groups were on similar trajectories before the switch, supporting the validity of the comparison.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new shelving and stock rotation procedure to all 34 branches.

2. **Monitor implementation:** Track service scores during the rollout to ensure the effect is replicated across all branches. Some branches may need additional training or support.

3. **Continue data collection:** Maintain weekly service score tracking to verify the improvement is sustained over time.

4. **Document best practices:** Capture what made the procedure change successful so it can be applied to future operational improvements.

5. **Consider cost-benefit analysis:** If there were any costs associated with the procedure change (training, temporary disruption, etc.), weigh them against the ~4% service improvement to assess overall value.

---

*Analysis prepared using difference-in-differences regression with branch fixed effects. Data covers 578 branch-week observations from weeks 6–22.*
