# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Impact of New Shelving Procedure on Branch Service Scores  

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure is associated with a **modest improvement in service scores** (+2.2 points). While this effect is not statistically definitive (p=0.10), the evidence suggests the new procedure has a positive impact and does not harm service quality. **We recommend proceeding with a broader rollout** while continuing to monitor outcomes.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **What We Analyzed**
- **34 branches** tracked over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **14 branches** kept the old procedure
- Service scores average ~50 points (higher is better)

### **Results**

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|---------------|---------------|--------|
| Branches keeping old procedure | 52.05 | 50.90 | **-1.15** |
| Branches with new procedure | 51.78 | 52.80 | **+1.02** |

**Estimated Effect of New Procedure: +2.22 points** (95% CI: -0.4 to +4.9)

### **What This Means**
1. **Positive impact**: Branches that switched improved relative to those that did not
2. **Protective effect**: The new procedure appears to have helped branches maintain performance while control branches declined slightly
3. **Statistically uncertain but promising**: The data cannot definitively rule out no effect, but the weight of evidence points to a modest benefit
4. **Practically meaningful**: A 2-point gain represents approximately 4% of the average service score

---

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with system-wide rollout of the new procedure**, with the following conditions:

1. **Monitor closely**: Track service scores for 8–12 weeks post-rollout to confirm the effect
2. **Document implementation**: Note any variations in how branches adopt the procedure to identify best practices
3. **Evaluate costs**: Ensure operational costs are justified by the service improvement
4. **Remain flexible**: Be prepared to adjust the procedure based on ongoing feedback and data

---

## **Supporting Analysis**

The analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach—the standard method for evaluating policy changes when some units adopt a change and others do not. This method controls for:
- Pre-existing differences between branches
- System-wide trends affecting all branches (e.g., seasonal patterns)
- Staffing levels and visitor volume
