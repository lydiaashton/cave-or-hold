# **Memorandum to the Board**

## **Title: Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

### **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores—an unexpected but welcome result.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **2.1 points** (approximately **4% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and consistent across all weeks after the switch. Although the change was not expected to affect service scores, the evidence supports rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

### **Key Findings**

1. **Positive Treatment Effect**: The difference-in-differences analysis shows that switched branches improved their service scores by **2.13 points** more than non-switched branches after the procedure change (p < 0.001). The 95% confidence interval is [0.97, 3.30], confirming the effect is reliably positive.

2. **Unexpected Benefit**: This analysis was conducted to verify the procedure change had no negative impact. Instead, we found a meaningful improvement. This suggests the new rotation and shelving method may have ancillary benefits for patron experience that were not anticipated.

3. **Valid Comparison**: Before the switch (weeks 6-14), both groups followed similar trends in service scores. Both groups experienced similar week-to-week fluctuations, confirming the treatment and control groups were comparable.

4. **Consistent Impact**: The improvement appeared in every week after the switch (weeks 15-22), with the treatment group outperforming the control group by 1.1 to 3.8 points each week. There is no evidence the effect is fading over time.

5. **Robust Result**: The finding holds even when accounting for staffing levels and visit volumes, suggesting the improvement stems from the procedure change itself.

6. **Study Scope**: The analysis covers **150 branches** over **17 weeks** (weeks 6-22), with 90 branches adopting the new procedure and 60 branches serving as controls.

---

### **Next Steps**

1. **Approve System-Wide Rollout**: The new procedure delivers improved service scores with no identified downside. Proceed with implementing it across all remaining branches.

2. **Monitor Implementation**: Track service scores during rollout to confirm the effect replicates at other branches. Expect similar 2-3 point improvements.

3. **Investigate the Mechanism**: Since this benefit was unexpected, consider gathering qualitative feedback from staff and patrons to understand *why* the new procedure improved service scores. This could inform future operational decisions.

4. **Document Best Practices**: Work with the 90 early-adopter branches to capture implementation details that may help other branches achieve similar results.

5. **Timeline**: A phased rollout over 8-12 weeks would allow for monitoring while minimizing disruption.
