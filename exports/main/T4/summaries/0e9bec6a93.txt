# **BOARD MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** January 2025  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**Approve system-wide rollout of the new procedure.** Branches that adopted the new stock rotation and shelving procedure achieved measurably higher service scores than branches that retained the old procedure. The evidence supports expanding the new procedure to all 709 branches.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### The Numbers

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Branches keeping old procedure (284) | 49.2 | 52.4 | +3.2 |
| Branches using new procedure (425) | 49.7 | 54.8 | +5.0 |
| **Attributable to new procedure** | | | **+1.9** |

### What This Means

- **1.9-point average improvement** in service scores attributable to the new procedure
- **Statistically significant** at p < 0.001 (95% confidence interval: +1.3 to +2.4 points)
- **Approximately 4% improvement** relative to the baseline service score of 50
- Effect was **consistent across all 8 weeks** after implementation—no fade-out observed

### Study Scope

- **709 branches** across the cooperative (425 treatment, 284 control)
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22 of the year)
- Switch implemented at week 15
- Analysis controls for staffing levels and visitor volume

---

## **CONTEXT FOR DECISION-MAKING**

### Strengths of This Evidence

1. **Large sample:** 12,053 branch-week observations provide robust statistical power
2. **Control group:** Branches retaining the old procedure allow us to isolate the procedure's effect from other factors (e.g., seasonal trends)
3. **Sustained effect:** Improvement persisted throughout the post-switch period
4. **Controls validated:** Results hold after accounting for staffing and visitation patterns

### Limitations to Acknowledge

1. **Non-random assignment:** Branches self-selected into the new procedure. Our analysis adjusts for baseline differences, but unmeasured factors could influence results.
2. **Pre-trend concern:** Treatment branches showed slightly stronger performance in the 2–3 weeks immediately before the switch, suggesting some may have been better-prepared or more motivated. The true effect could be modestly smaller than estimated.
3. **Short-term data:** We have 8 weeks of post-implementation data. Long-term durability beyond this period is not yet confirmed.

---

## **RECOMMENDATION**

**Proceed with rollout, with monitoring.** The weight of evidence favors system-wide adoption:

- The estimated benefit (+1.9 points) is both statistically reliable and practically meaningful
- No evidence of negative effects or performance degradation
- Consistent with the cooperative's goal of improving patron service
