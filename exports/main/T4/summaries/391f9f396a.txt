# **Memorandum to the Board**

## **Subject: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **1.9 points** (approximately **3.8% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and strengthens over time. **We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Overview**
- **709 branches** participated (425 adopted the new procedure; 284 retained the old procedure)
- **17 weeks** of data collected (Weeks 6–22)
- Procedure switch occurred at **Week 15**

### **Impact on Service Scores**

| Group | Pre-Switch Average | Post-Switch Average | Change |
|-------|-------------------|---------------------|--------|
| Branches with New Procedure | 50.4 | 52.4 | +2.1 |
| Branches with Old Procedure | 50.1 | 50.3 | +0.2 |
| **Net Effect** | — | — | **+1.9** |

### **Statistical Confidence**
- **Effect size:** +1.91 points on the service score index
- **95% Confidence Interval:** [1.38, 2.45]
- **P-value:** < 0.001 (highly statistically significant)
- Analysis controlled for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume

### **Benefits Increase Over Time**
The improvement strengthened as branches gained experience with the new procedure:
- **Weeks 15–18:** +1.4 points
- **Weeks 19–22:** +2.4 points

This suggests branches require 2–4 weeks to fully realize the benefits.

---

## **Recommendations**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches.

2. **Allow 2–4 weeks per branch** for staff training and adjustment, recognizing that full benefits emerge after the initial transition period.

3. **Continue monitoring** service scores during rollout to confirm effects hold across different branch types.

4. **Capture best practices** from early-adopter branches to support training for remaining locations.

5. **Conduct a follow-up review** in 6 months to verify long-term sustainability of improvements.

---

## **Methodology Note**

This analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing service score changes for switched branches against non-switched branches over the same period. This method accounts for system-wide trends affecting all branches. Estimates are statistically robust, with standard errors clustered at the branch level and controls for staffing and visitor volume variations.
