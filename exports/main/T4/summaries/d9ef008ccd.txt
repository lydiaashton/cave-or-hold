# **Memorandum to the Board of Directors**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** [Current Date]  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure  

---

## **Bottom Line**

**We recommend rolling out the new stock rotation and shelving procedure to all branches.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores improve by approximately **2 points** (about 4% relative to the average score of 50) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant and consistent across the post-implementation period.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
- **709 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22 of the year)
- **425 branches** switched to the new procedure in week 15; **284 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Analysis compares changes in service scores between the two groups before and after the switch

### Results

| Measure | Value |
|---------|-------|
| Estimated improvement from new procedure | **+2.1 service score points** |
| 95% Confidence interval | +1.7 to +2.4 points |
| Statistical confidence | Greater than 99.9% |
| Relative improvement | Approximately 4% above baseline |

### What This Means in Practice
- The average branch scores about 50 on the service index; the new procedure is associated with scores around 52
- The improvement appeared immediately after implementation and remained stable through all 8 weeks of observation
- The effect is large enough to be meaningful for patron experience across the cooperative

---

## **Important Considerations**

Our analysis identified one factor that warrants attention:

**Pre-existing differences:** Branches that chose to adopt the new procedure early were already showing slightly faster score improvement before the switch occurred. This suggests these branches may have been more motivated or better resourced overall.

**What this means for our estimate:** The true effect of the new procedure is likely somewhat smaller than 2.1 points—possibly in the range of 1.0 to 1.5 points. Even at this lower bound, the improvement remains operationally meaningful.

**Why we still recommend rollout:**
- The post-switch improvement is consistent and sustained
- All branches, not just early adopters, should benefit from the improved procedure
- The risk of negative effects is low; the procedure change is operational, not disruptive

---

## **Recommended Next Steps**

### Phase 1: Rollout (Next 4–8 Weeks)
1. **Implement the new procedure** across all remaining branches
2. **Provide standardized training materials** to ensure consistent implementation
3. **Designate implementation leads** at each branch to address questions

### Phase 2: Monitoring (Ongoing)
1. **Track service scores weekly** for at least 12 weeks post-rollout
2. **Set expectation:** 1–2 point improvement per branch is the target range
3. **Flag underperforming branches** for additional support if scores do not improve

### Phase 3: Evaluation (Quarter 4)
1. **Conduct follow-up analysis** comparing actual outcomes to projections
2. **Document lessons learned** about implementation challenges and successes
3. **Assess cost-benefit** to inform future operational changes

---

## **Conclusion**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure has demonstrated a clear, positive effect on branch service scores. While the exact magnitude of the benefit carries some uncertainty, the direction of the effect is unambiguous and the statistical evidence is strong. A cooperative-wide rollout is warranted, with continued monitoring to ensure the expected benefits are realized.
