# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores—a welcome surprise.** Although we expected the rotation and shelving changes to have no measurable impact on service, branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.2 points** (about **4.4%**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and consistent across most branches. We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **60 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in switched branches against changes in control branches

### Impact on Service Scores
| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|---------------|---------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 50.65 | 52.54 | +1.89 |
| Control branches | 50.84 | 50.51 | -0.34 |
| **Net effect** | | | **+2.23** |

- The **2.23-point improvement** is statistically significant (p < 0.01)
- 95% confidence interval: **1.4 to 3.0 points**
- This represents a **4.4% improvement** relative to the average service score of 51.2

### Why We Can Trust This Result
- **Parallel trends verified**: Before week 15, both groups followed nearly identical trajectories, confirming the groups were comparable
- **Multiple statistical checks**: The result holds after accounting for branch-specific factors, week-to-week variation, staffing levels, and visit volumes
- **Broad-based improvement**: **70% of switched branches** saw their service scores improve after adoption
- **Not driven by other factors**: Staffing and visitor traffic did not change differently for switched branches—the procedure itself explains the gain

### Exceeded Expectations
We anticipated this operational change would be neutral for service scores. Instead, it delivered a meaningful, measurable improvement at no apparent cost to other metrics.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all branches

2. **Communicate the win**: Share results with branch managers to encourage adoption and reinforce that operational changes can positively affect service

3. **Monitor during rollout**: Track implementation fidelity and service scores to confirm the effect persists at scale

4. **Learn from variation**: About 30% of switched branches saw minimal improvement. Understanding what differentiates these branches could help maximize benefits

5. **Document the procedure**: Capture the specific practices that define the "new procedure" to ensure consistent implementation across all branches

---

*Analysis based on weekly service score data from weeks 6–22. The switch occurred at week 15. Statistical methods include difference-in-differences regression with branch and week fixed effects.*
