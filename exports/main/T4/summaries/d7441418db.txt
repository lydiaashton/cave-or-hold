# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

---

## **Subject: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure is associated with a **2.3-point improvement in service scores** (approximately 4% relative to the baseline). While this effect falls just short of conventional statistical significance (p=0.088), the evidence points in a positive direction and supports proceeding with a full rollout—provided continued monitoring is implemented.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### Study Overview
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Analysis used a difference-in-differences approach to isolate the procedure's effect from other factors

### Results Summary

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect on service score | **+2.32 points** |
| 95% Confidence interval | **-0.34 to +4.99** |
| P-value | **0.088** |
| Relative improvement | **~4.4%** |

### Supporting Evidence

1. **Valid comparison groups**: Before the switch, switched and control branches had statistically similar service scores (p=0.73), confirming the groups were comparable at baseline.

2. **Diverging trends after week 15**: Control branches experienced a 2.8-point decline in service scores after week 15, while switched branches remained relatively stable (only 0.45-point decline). The new procedure appears to have protected against this decline.

3. **Robust to controls**: The estimated effect remains stable when accounting for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume, suggesting the result is not driven by these factors.

---

## **RECOMMENDATION**

**Proceed with systemwide rollout** of the new procedure. The positive effect direction, meaningful effect size, and marginally significant statistical evidence collectively support expansion. The risk of inaction (continued score declines observed in control branches) outweighs the uncertainty.
