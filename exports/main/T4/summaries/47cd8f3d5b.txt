# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores—an unexpected but welcome result.** Branches that switched to the new shelving and rotation procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **1.8 points** (approximately **3.6%** improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant (p = 0.003). While the change was not expected to affect service scores, the data shows a clear positive impact.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15 (treatment group)
- **60 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in service scores before and after the switch between the two groups

### **Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 50.03 | 51.47 | +1.44 |
| Non-switched branches | 48.77 | 48.43 | −0.34 |
| **Net effect (DiD)** | | | **+1.78** |

- **Treatment effect: +1.78 points** (95% confidence interval: 0.60 to 2.97)
- **Statistical significance: p = 0.003**
- **Effect size: 3.6%** improvement relative to baseline service scores

### **Why This Matters**
- The procedure change was **not expected to impact service scores**—it was primarily an operational adjustment
- The positive effect appears genuine: switched branches improved while non-switched branches slightly declined during the same period
- Results hold after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume, suggesting the effect is not driven by external factors
- Pre-switch trends were parallel between groups, supporting the validity of the comparison

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with cooperative-wide rollout.** The unexpected service score improvement represents a low-cost gain. Rolling out to remaining branches should yield similar benefits.

2. **Document the change thoroughly.** Since this result was unexpected, capture detailed implementation notes to ensure consistent replication across all branches.

3. **Monitor closely during rollout.** Track service scores as each branch adopts the new procedure. If effects differ from the 1.8-point average, investigate branch-specific factors.

4. **Explore the mechanism.** Consider a follow-up study to understand *why* service scores improved. Was it faster retrieval times, better stock visibility, or improved staff morale? Understanding this could inform future operational changes.

5. **Communicate the win.** Share this result with branch staff—their adoption of the new procedure delivered measurable service improvements beyond what was anticipated.
