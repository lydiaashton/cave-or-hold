# MEMORANDUM

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Shelving and Rotation Procedure

---

## Bottom Line

The new shelving and rotation procedure is associated with a **2.4-point improvement** in service scores (approximately 5% relative to the average score of 47). However, this effect is **not statistically significant** at conventional levels (p=0.10), meaning we cannot confidently rule out that the observed improvement occurred by chance.

**Recommendation:** Do not proceed with immediate system-wide rollout. Instead, extend data collection for 8–12 additional weeks to obtain a more definitive answer before committing resources to full implementation.

---

## Key Findings

### Study Design
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure; **14 branches** maintained the old procedure (control group)
- Procedure switch occurred at **week 15**
- Analysis compares changes in switched branches against changes in non-switched branches

### Results Summary

| Group | Pre-Switch Average | Post-Switch Average | Change |
|-------|-------------------|---------------------|--------|
| Switched Branches | 45.2 | 49.4 | +4.2 |
| Non-Switched Branches | 46.6 | 48.6 | +2.0 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+2.4** |

### Statistical Assessment

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated Effect | +2.40 service score points |
| 95% Confidence Interval | -0.49 to +5.29 points |
| P-value | 0.103 |
| Statistical Significance | Not significant at 0.05 level |

### What This Means

The confidence interval tells us the true effect could reasonably be:
- **Slightly negative** (up to -0.5 points), or
- **Moderately positive** (up to +5.3 points)

Because the interval includes zero, we cannot conclude with confidence that the procedure causes improvement.

### Supporting Evidence

- **Parallel trends validated:** Before the switch, both groups showed similar trends (p=0.74), supporting the validity of our comparison
- **Staffing matters:** Higher staffing levels are significantly associated with better service scores, suggesting operational factors influence outcomes
- **Branch variation:** Substantial differences exist across branches in baseline performance, which our analysis accounts for

## Decision Framework

| If Future Analysis Shows | Recommended Action |
|--------------------------|-------------------|
| Effect > 3 points, p < 0.05 | Proceed with system-wide rollout |
| Effect 1–3 points, p < 0.10 | Consider phased rollout with continued monitoring |
| Effect < 1 point or p > 0.10 | Reconsider procedure or modify before broader adoption |
