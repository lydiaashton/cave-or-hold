# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2025  
**Subject:** Recommendation on System-Wide Rollout of New Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Do not proceed with system-wide rollout at this time.** While initial analysis suggests the new procedure improved service scores by approximately 2 points (4%), deeper examination reveals that branches selected for the new procedure were already on an improving trajectory before the change. We cannot confidently attribute the observed improvement to the procedure itself. Additional evidence is needed before committing resources to a full rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **709 branches** analyzed over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22 of the year)
- **425 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **284 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Service scores average approximately 52 points (higher indicates better service)

### Surface-Level Results
| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|---------------|---------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 51.98 | 54.77 | +2.79 |
| Non-switched branches | 50.60 | 51.36 | +0.76 |
| **Net Difference** | | | **+2.02** |

A basic comparison suggests switched branches improved by **2.02 points more** than non-switched branches—a statistically significant difference that would represent roughly a 4% gain in service quality.

### Why This Result Is Misleading

**The critical assumption for valid comparison is not met.** To attribute the improvement to the new procedure, switched and non-switched branches must have been on similar trajectories before the change. They were not:

| Week | Gap (Switched − Non-Switched) |
|------|-------------------------------|
| Week 6 | +0.8 points |
| Week 10 | +1.1 points |
| Week 14 (last pre-switch week) | +2.5 points |

The gap between groups was **already widening before the procedure change**, indicating switched branches were systematically different and improving faster regardless of the intervention.

When we statistically control for these pre-existing trends, the estimated treatment effect **drops to +0.05 points (not statistically significant)**. This suggests the apparent benefit reflects branch selection rather than procedure effectiveness.

### Additional Context
- Switched branches had higher baseline service scores throughout the study period
- Both staffing levels and visit volumes positively correlate with service scores
- Week-to-week variability was substantial, particularly in the post-switch period

---

## **Recommendation**

**Hold on system-wide rollout.** The current evidence does not support the conclusion that the new procedure causes improved service scores. Proceeding with a full rollout risks investing significant resources in a change that may not deliver meaningful benefits.

## **Appendix: Technical Notes**

- Primary analysis method: Difference-in-Differences regression
- Simple DiD estimate: +2.02 points (p<0.001)
- Fixed-effects estimate (controlling for branch trends): +0.05 points (p=0.95)
- Parallel trends assumption: **Violated** (pre-switch interaction term p=0.001)
- All analyses controlled for staffing index and visits index
