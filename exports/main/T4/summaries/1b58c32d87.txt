# **Memorandum to the Board**

## **Title: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores by approximately 2 points**, contrary to our initial expectation that it would have no effect. This finding is statistically significant (p < 0.01) and represents a meaningful ~4% improvement on the service score scale. While the procedure was implemented for operational reasons, it appears to have delivered an unexpected benefit to patron experience. We recommend proceeding with the system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. **Study Design**
- **150 branches** total: 90 adopted the new procedure, 60 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22): 9 weeks before the switch, 8 weeks after
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in treatment branches to control branches

### 2. **Main Result**
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 48.55 | 50.30 | +1.75 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 49.46 | 53.24 | +3.78 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+2.03** |

- The new procedure added **2.03 points** to service scores beyond normal variation
- **95% confidence interval: 0.82 to 3.25 points** (does not include zero)
- **Statistically significant at the 1% level** (p = 0.001)

### 3. **Why We Can Trust This Result**
- **Parallel trends validated**: Before the switch, both groups moved together (pre-trend test p = 0.19). The divergence only appeared after week 15.
- **Effect holds with controls**: Accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume, the effect remains at **2.18 points** (p = 0.001).
- **Sustained impact**: The treatment-control gap remained elevated throughout all 8 post-switch weeks (ranging from 2.2 to 3.7 points).

### 4. **Unexpected Finding**
Our prior expectation was that the procedure change would be neutral for service scores. The data shows otherwise. This could indicate:
- The new procedure indirectly improves patron-facing operations
- Staff working under the new system may have more capacity for member services
- Better stock organization may improve item findability and checkout experience

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** to all 60 branches currently using the old procedure

2. **Investigate the mechanism**: Conduct follow-up research to understand *why* service scores improved (e.g., staff surveys, patron feedback, time-motion studies)

3. **Monitor post-rollout performance**: Track service scores for 8–12 weeks after full implementation to confirm the effect persists at scale

4. **Document and share learnings**: If specific practices within the new procedure drive the improvement, codify these for training purposes

5. **Revisit expectations**: Use this finding to inform future operational changes—procedures assumed neutral may have meaningful service impacts

---

## **Appendix: Technical Notes**

- Standard errors clustered at the branch level
- Effect size robust to inclusion of staffing_index and visits_index controls
- No evidence of pre-trend divergence between groups
- Data: weeks 6–22; switch occurred at week 15
