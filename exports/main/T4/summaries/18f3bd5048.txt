# **MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure is associated with a **modest improvement in service scores** (approximately 2 points, or 4%), but the evidence is **not statistically conclusive**. We recommend a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Measured Impact on Service Scores**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched Branches (n=20) | 49.9 | 52.0 | +2.1 |
| Control Branches (n=14) | 49.2 | 49.3 | +0.2 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+1.9** |

- **Estimated treatment effect:** +1.9 points on the service score index
- **Percentage improvement:** ~3.8% relative to the average score of 50
- **95% confidence interval:** -0.7 to +4.5 points
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.15 (not significant at conventional 0.05 threshold)

### **2. Study Design & Data Quality**

- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure; **14 branches** maintained the old procedure
- Switch implemented at **week 15**
- **Parallel trends verified:** No significant difference in pre-switch trends between groups, supporting the validity of the comparison
- Complete data with no missing values

### **3. Interpretation**

The positive estimate suggests the new procedure **likely helps**, but we cannot rule out random variation. The true effect could range from a small negative impact (-0.7) to a meaningful positive impact (+4.5 points).

**Why the uncertainty?**
- Moderate sample size (34 branches) limits statistical power
- Service scores have natural week-to-week variability (standard deviation ~10 points)
- Only 8 weeks of post-switch data available

When controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume, the estimated effect remains stable at **+2.1 points**, suggesting the improvement is not driven by changes in these operational factors.

---

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended Actions**

1. **Continue the pilot for 8–12 more weeks** to gather additional post-switch data and improve statistical precision.

2. **Expand the pilot to 5–10 additional branches** on a rolling basis to increase sample size without committing to a full rollout.

3. **Monitor implementation fidelity** to ensure switched branches are fully adopting the new procedures as intended.

4. **Track secondary outcomes** such as staff time efficiency, patron satisfaction, and item retrieval times.

5. **Reconvene in Q1 2027** to review updated analysis with additional data.

### **Decision Framework**

| If True Effect Is... | Recommendation |
|---------------------|----------------|
| +4 points (upper bound) | Strong case for full rollout |
| +2 points (point estimate) | Phased rollout reasonable; weigh against costs |
| 0 to -1 points (lower bound) | Do not proceed with rollout |
