# **Analysis of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**
## Impact on Service Scores

---

### **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure produced an unexpected benefit.** While the rotation and shelving changes were not expected to affect service scores, branches that switched saw their scores increase by an average of **2.0 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents a meaningful 4% improvement and strengthens the case for system-wide adoption.

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout.** The new procedure delivers its intended operational benefits plus an unanticipated improvement in service quality.

---

### **KEY FINDINGS**

**1. Study Design**
- **150 branches** total: 90 switched to the new procedure, 60 kept the old procedure (control group)
- **17 weeks of data**: Weeks 6–14 (before switch) and Weeks 15–22 (after switch)
- Analysis compared changes in switched branches against changes in control branches

**2. Service Score Impact**
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| New Procedure (90 branches) | 51.7 | 53.5 | **+1.8** |
| Old Procedure (60 branches) | 50.2 | 50.1 | **-0.1** |
| **Estimated Effect** | | | **+2.0 points** |

**3. Statistical Confidence**
- **p-value: 0.001** (highly statistically significant)
- **95% Confidence Interval: 0.8 to 3.1 points**
- The positive effect is robust and unlikely to be due to chance

**4. Effect Size**
- Cohen's d = 0.56 (moderate effect)
- On a scale where the average branch scores ~50, a 2-point gain represents approximately **4% improvement**

**5. Validity Check**
- Before the switch, both groups showed parallel trends in service scores
- This supports the conclusion that the improvement is attributable to the new procedure

### **NEXT STEPS**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** to all 60 branches still using the old procedure

2. **Investigate the mechanism**: Since this improvement was unexpected, understanding *why* the new procedure boosted service scores could reveal additional benefits or best practices worth emphasizing during rollout

3. **Monitor during implementation**:
   - Confirm similar gains in newly switched branches
   - Track whether the effect persists over time

4. **Document and communicate**: Share this finding with branch staff—knowing the new procedure may improve patron satisfaction could increase buy-in during the transition

5. **Review other metrics**: Consider whether the new procedure affected staffing efficiency, costs, or other operational measures to capture the full picture of its impact
