# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores—contrary to our expectations.** While we anticipated no measurable effect on service quality, branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by **2.2 points** (approximately **4.6%**) compared to branches using the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and sustained. We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.

---

## **Study Design**

We analyzed weekly service scores from **150 branches** over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22 of the year). The new procedure was implemented at week 15:

| Group | Branches | Procedure |
|-------|----------|-----------|
| Treatment | 90 branches | New procedure (weeks 15–22) |
| Control | 60 branches | Old procedure (entire period) |

This design isolates the effect of the new procedure by comparing changes in the treatment group to changes in the control group, accounting for overall trends affecting all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Unexpected Service Score Improvement

| Group | Before Switch (Weeks 6–14) | After Switch (Weeks 15–22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------------|---------------------------|--------|
| New Procedure Branches | 48.2 | 52.2 | +4.0 |
| Old Procedure Branches | 48.2 | 50.0 | +1.8 |
| **Net Effect** | — | — | **+2.2** |

The **2.2-point improvement** is the estimated effect of the new procedure. This was not anticipated, as the procedure change was expected to affect operational efficiency rather than patron-facing service quality.

### 2. Statistical Confidence

- **Highly statistically significant** (p < 0.01)
- 95% confidence interval: **1.0 to 3.5 points**
- The result is robust to different model specifications

### 3. Valid Comparison

Pre-switch data confirms treatment and control branches were on parallel trajectories:

- **Pre-switch difference between groups:** Essentially zero (−0.02 points on average)
- **No systematic differences** in any pre-switch week
- This supports the conclusion that the improvement is attributable to the procedure change

### 4. Sustained Effect

The improvement held steady across all 8 post-switch weeks, with no evidence of fading. Branches maintained the gain throughout the observation period.

### 5. Possible Explanations

While we did not anticipate this effect, several mechanisms could explain it:

- **Faster item retrieval:** Improved shelving organization may reduce staff time spent locating materials, freeing time for patron assistance
- **Better stock availability:** The rotation procedure may keep high-demand items on shelves more consistently
- **Staff confidence:** Familiarity with a clearer system may improve staff-patron interactions

Further investigation could identify which mechanism(s) are driving the improvement.

---

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with system-wide rollout.** The evidence indicates a real, meaningful improvement in service scores at no apparent cost. Even though this outcome was unexpected, the data is clear and consistent.

### Implementation Considerations

1. **Phased rollout** to manage training and transition workload across the remaining 60 branches

2. **Staff communication**—explain that while the procedure was designed for operational efficiency, it has the added benefit of improving service scores

3. **Continued monitoring** to confirm effects persist after full rollout and to identify any branches where implementation challenges arise

4. **Document best practices** from the 90 early-adopter branches to support smoother transitions

---

## **Methodology Notes**

- Difference-in-differences regression with branch and week fixed effects
- Standard errors clustered by branch
- Controlled for staffing index and visits index
- All tests use robust standard errors
