# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD OF DIRECTORS**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** [Current Date]  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Rotation Procedure  

---

## **Executive Summary**

The new shelving and rotation procedure **significantly improved service scores** at branches that adopted it. Based on 17 weeks of data from 709 branches, we find the new procedure increased service scores by **1.8 points** (p < 0.001). The benefit appears to be growing over time. 

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout.**

---

## **Bottom Line**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did the new procedure improve service? | **Yes** |
| How large is the improvement? | **+1.8 points** on the service score index |
| Is this statistically reliable? | **Yes** (95% confidence interval: 1.3 to 2.4 points) |
| Should we expand to all branches? | **Yes** |

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measured Impact

Branches that switched to the new procedure improved their service scores by **3.8 points** after the change. However, branches that kept the old procedure also improved by **2.0 points** (likely due to seasonal factors or system-wide initiatives). The **net effect attributable to the new procedure is +1.8 points**.

### 2. Effect Size Context

- Represents **17% of a standard deviation** in service scores
- Equivalent to **3.7% improvement** above baseline performance
- For a branch averaging 50 points, this moves them to approximately **52 points**

### 3. Effect Is Growing

The gap between switched and non-switched branches widened over the 8-week post-switch period:

| Week | Treatment-Control Gap |
|------|----------------------|
| 15 | +2.0 points |
| 18 | +2.0 points |
| 22 | +3.1 points |

This suggests branches may continue to benefit as staff gain familiarity with the new procedure.

### 4. Robust Analysis

Our findings hold after controlling for:
- Branch-specific characteristics (fixed effects)
- Weekly staffing levels
- Visitor volume fluctuations
- System-wide temporal trends

---

## **Study Design**

- **709 branches** total (425 switched, 284 did not)
- **17 weeks** of data (weeks 6-22)
- **Switch point:** Week 15
- **Method:** Difference-in-differences regression with clustered standard errors

This quasi-experimental design isolates the procedure's effect from other factors that might influence service scores.

---

## **Recommendations**

### Immediate Actions

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all 284 remaining branches

2. **Develop implementation timeline** with phased adoption to ensure adequate training and support

3. **Continue monitoring** service scores at all branches to track rollout progress

### Follow-Up Actions

4. **Document best practices** from high-performing switched branches for training materials

5. **Re-evaluate in 6 months** to confirm long-term effects and identify any branches needing additional support

6. **Assess resource implications** (staff time, training costs) against the demonstrated service improvement
