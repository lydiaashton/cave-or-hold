# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Recommend system-wide rollout of the new procedure.** Contrary to expectations that the procedural change would have no impact on service quality, branches that adopted the new procedure achieved **significantly higher service scores** than those that kept the old procedure. The improvement is statistically robust and represents a meaningful gain in service quality at no apparent cost to the service score metric.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Overview**
- **150 branches** over **17 weeks** (Weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** switched to the new procedure at Week 15; **60 branches** retained the old procedure as a control group
- Analysis compares service score changes between the two groups before and after the switch

### **Service Score Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Old Procedure (Control) | 48.92 | 48.59 | −0.34 |
| New Procedure (Treatment) | 48.64 | 50.40 | +1.76 |
| **Net Effect of New Procedure** | | | **+2.10** |

### **Statistical Confidence**
- **Estimated effect:** +2.10 points on the service score index
- **95% Confidence Interval:** +0.84 to +3.36 points
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.01
- **Relative improvement:** Approximately 4% above the system baseline (~50 points)

### **Why We Trust This Result**

1. **Parallel pre-trends:** Before Week 15, both groups tracked closely together with no systematic differences. This rules out the possibility that higher-performing branches simply self-selected into the new procedure.

2. **Effect persists after controls:** The result holds after accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume, confirming the improvement is attributable to the procedure itself, not to other branch characteristics.

3. **Consistent post-switch pattern:** In every week after implementation (Weeks 15–22), the treatment group outperformed the control group. The effect did not appear before the switch and did not fade over the observation period.

### **Unexpected Finding**

The board's prior expectation was that the new stock rotation and shelving procedure would have **no measurable effect** on service scores. The data show otherwise: the new procedure is associated with a **clear, positive improvement** in service quality as measured by our service score index. This suggests the procedural change may have beneficial side effects—such as improved staff efficiency, better item availability, or enhanced patron experience—that were not anticipated.

---

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches. The evidence indicates:

- A real improvement in service quality
- No detectable negative consequences on the service score metric
- Benefits that exceed prior expectations

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve rollout** to the 60 branches currently using the old procedure.

2. **Develop implementation plan** including:
   - Staff training schedule
   - Phased timeline to manage operational capacity
   - Branch manager communications

3. **Continue monitoring** service scores during rollout to:
   - Verify the effect holds at full scale
   - Identify branches needing additional support
   - Track whether effects sustain or grow over time

4. **Investigate mechanisms:** Consider qualitative follow-up (e.g., staff surveys, patron feedback) to understand *why* the new procedure improved service scores. This could reveal additional best practices.

5. **Document lessons learned** from the 90 early-adopter branches to support training and smooth implementation.
