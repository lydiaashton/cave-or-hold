# **Library Cooperative Board Memorandum**

---

**To:** Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2025  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Shelving and Rotation Procedure  

---

## **Executive Summary**

The new shelving and rotation procedure shows a **positive but statistically uncertain effect** on branch service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure maintained their performance while control branches experienced a notable decline. This suggests the new procedure **helps protect service quality** rather than drive dramatic improvement.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **phased rollout** while continuing to monitor results and maintain a comparison group for ongoing evaluation.

---

## **Bottom Line**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did the new procedure improve service scores? | **Partially** — it prevented decline rather than driving gains |
| Is the effect statistically significant? | **No** — we cannot rule out random variation (p = 0.115) |
| Should we roll out to all branches? | **Yes, but gradually** — the evidence supports adoption with continued monitoring |

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. What Happened to Service Scores?**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| **20 branches (new procedure)** | 51.41 | 51.27 | **-0.14** |
| **14 branches (old procedure)** | 49.26 | 47.19 | **-2.07** |

**The difference: +1.9 points** — Treatment branches declined significantly less than control branches.

### **2. How Confident Are We?**

- **Estimated effect:** +2.2 points (about 4% of the average score of 50)
- **Statistical confidence:** 95% confidence interval ranges from -0.5 to +4.8 points
- **Interpretation:** The true effect is likely positive, but we lack sufficient data to be fully confident

### **3. Branch-Level Results**

- **55% of treatment branches** (11 of 20) improved after switching
- **36% of control branches** (5 of 14) improved during the same period
- Treatment branches showed more stable performance overall

### **4. Important Context**

- **System-wide decline:** All branches faced headwinds after week 14 (likely seasonal or external factors)
- **The new procedure acted as a buffer:** It helped branches resist this decline
- **Pre-existing differences:** Treatment branches started with slightly higher scores, but trends were similar before the switch

---

## **Recommendations**

### **Immediate Actions**

1. **Approve phased rollout** to remaining branches over the next 2-3 quarters
2. **Retain 5-7 branches** on the old procedure as an ongoing comparison group
3. **Extend monitoring** to at least 12 months post-rollout for stronger evidence

### **Implementation Priorities**

1. **Document the procedure** clearly to ensure consistent adoption across branches
2. **Track implementation quality** — branches that don't improve may need additional support
3. **Monitor staffing and visit patterns** — both significantly affect service scores

### **Decision Triggers**

| If this happens... | Then... |
|-------------------|---------|
| 6-month data shows continued positive effect | Accelerate full rollout |
| Effect disappears or reverses | Pause and investigate causes |
| Certain branches consistently underperform | Provide targeted support or training |

---

## **What We Still Need to Learn**

1. **Long-term sustainability** — Does the effect persist beyond 8 weeks?
2. **Branch-level variation** — Why did 9 of 20 treatment branches not improve?
3. **Cost-benefit analysis** — Are the operational costs of the new procedure justified by the service score benefits?
4. **Seasonal effects** — How does the procedure perform during high-traffic periods?

---

## **Technical Appendix (Summary)**

- **Method:** Difference-in-Differences analysis with branch and week controls
- **Data:** 578 weekly observations from 34 branches (17 weeks)
- **Switch date:** Week 15
- **Model controls:** Branch characteristics, week effects, staffing levels, visit volumes
- **Key limitation:** Short post-switch observation window limits statistical power
