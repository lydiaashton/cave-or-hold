# Board Summary: New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## Bottom Line

**The new procedure improved service scores by approximately 2.1 points (about 4% relative to the average score of 50).** The effect is statistically significant and grew stronger over the observation period. We recommend proceeding with a full rollout to all branches.

---

## Key Findings

### 1. Treatment Effect

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Estimated effect** | +2.12 points |
| **95% Confidence Interval** | [1.58, 2.66] |
| **Statistical significance** | p < 0.001 |
| **Relative improvement** | ~4% above baseline |

The estimate is robust across multiple specifications, including models with branch fixed effects and controls for staffing and visit levels.

### 2. What Happened to Service Scores

| Group | Pre-Switch | Post-Switch | Change |
|-------|------------|-------------|--------|
| **Branches that switched** | 51.85 | 52.52 | +0.67 |
| **Branches that did not switch** | 51.09 | 49.64 | −1.45 |

Branches adopting the new procedure maintained and slightly improved their scores, while control branches experienced a notable decline. This suggests the new procedure helped branches resist broader downward pressures on service quality during this period.

### 3. Effect Over Time

The treatment effect strengthened over the observation period:
- **Weeks 15-17 (early post-period):** Small, statistically insignificant effects
- **Weeks 18-22 (later post-period):** Larger, significant effects reaching +2.2 points by week 22

This pattern indicates branches may need several weeks to fully realize the benefits of the new procedure.

### 4. Important Caveat

Statistical tests detected some pre-existing differences between treatment and control branches before the switch. While these differences diminished near the switch date, they introduce uncertainty into the causal interpretation. The true effect could be somewhat smaller than our estimate suggests.

---

## Next Steps

### Immediate Actions

1. **Approve full rollout** to remaining branches based on positive results
2. **Provide implementation support** — the growing effect over time suggests branches benefit from experience and possibly additional training
3. **Investigate control branch decline** — understand why non-switching branches saw scores drop and whether they need interim support

### Ongoing Monitoring

1. **Continue tracking** service scores for 6-12 more weeks to confirm effects persist
2. **Monitor implementation fidelity** across branches to ensure consistent adoption
3. **Identify variation** — determine which branches benefit most and which may need additional support
4. **Consider randomized evaluation** for future procedure changes to strengthen causal evidence

### Cost-Benefit Consideration

The board should weigh the 2.1-point service score improvement against implementation costs, including training, materials, staff time, and any operational disruptions during transition.

---

## Technical Notes

- **Data:** 12,053 observations (709 branches × 17 weeks)
- **Analysis period:** Weeks 6-22 (switch occurred at week 15)
- **Method:** Difference-in-differences with branch fixed effects and clustered standard errors
- **Sample:** 425 treatment branches, 284 control branches
