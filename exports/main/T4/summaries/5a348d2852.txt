# **Memorandum to the Board**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Office  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure  

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores and should be rolled out system-wide.** Branches that adopted the new procedure achieved service scores approximately **2 points higher** than comparable branches that kept the old procedure. This finding is statistically significant and robust to alternative explanations.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
- **150 branches** participated: 90 adopted the new procedure, 60 retained the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** collected (Weeks 6-22, 2026)
- **Service scores** averaged around 50 across all branches (higher scores indicate better service)

### Results Summary

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| New Procedure (90 branches) | 50.91 | 51.10 | +0.19 |
| Old Procedure (60 branches) | 49.22 | 47.36 | −1.87 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+2.05** |

### Statistical Evidence
- **Estimated effect: +2.05 points** (95% confidence interval: 0.77 to 3.33)
- **P-value: 0.002** (highly statistically significant)
- The result remains significant after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volumes

### What This Means

Although we expected the procedure change to have no effect on service scores, the data show a clear benefit. Branches using the new procedure not only maintained their service quality but also outperformed branches using the old procedure by a meaningful margin. The control group's decline suggests the new procedure may have protected branches from broader operational challenges during this period.

---

## **Recommendation**

**Approve system-wide rollout of the new shelving and stock rotation procedure to all 150 branches.**

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Implement rollout** to remaining 60 branches within the next quarter
2. **Provide training support** using materials developed from the 90 early-adopter branches
3. **Monitor service scores** for 8-12 weeks post-implementation to confirm effects at scale
4. **Report back to the Board** with full rollout results by Q2 2027

---

## **Appendix: Technical Notes**

Analysis employed a difference-in-differences design with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch). The parallel trends assumption is supported by similar pre-switch patterns in both groups. No significant differences in staffing or visit volumes explain the results.
