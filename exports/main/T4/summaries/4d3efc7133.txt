# **Board Memorandum**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Executive Summary**

The new branch rotation and shelving procedure **improves service scores as expected**. Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by **1.9 points** (approximately 4%) compared to branches that maintained the old procedure. The effect is statistically significant, consistent across analytical methods, and appears to strengthen over time.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with system-wide rollout to all 709 branches.

---

## **Bottom Line**

| Question | Answer |

| Did the new procedure improve service scores? | **Yes** |
| How large is the improvement? | +1.87 points (~4% increase) |
| How confident are we? | **High** (p < 0.001, consistent across models) |
| Should we roll out to all branches? | **Yes** |

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Impact on Service Scores**
- **Treatment effect:** +1.87 points on the service score index
- **Confidence interval:** 1.30 to 2.44 points (95% certainty)
- **Relative gain:** 3.7% improvement over the baseline average of 50.7
- **Effect persists** after controlling for staffing levels, visit volumes, and inherent branch differences

### **Study Design**
- **709 branches** total (425 adopted new procedure; 284 kept old procedure)
- **17 weeks** of data (weeks 6–22); procedure changed at week 15
- **12,053 observations** analyzed using difference-in-differences methodology

### **Effect Strengthens Over Time**
Weekly analysis shows the positive effect grew in the weeks following implementation, with the largest gains in weeks 19–22. This suggests staff proficiency with the new procedure improves with experience.

---

## **Considerations**

**Strengths of this analysis:**
- Large sample size (709 branches, 17 weeks)
- Control group provides counterfactual comparison
- Multiple model specifications yield consistent results
- Branch-level controls account for inherent differences between locations

**Limitations to note:**
- Branches self-selected into early adoption (not randomized)
- Pre-existing differences between groups partially adjusted for statistically
- Long-term sustainability beyond 8 weeks post-switch not yet observed

## **Conclusion**

The data confirms what the cooperative expected: the new rotation and shelving procedure improves branch service scores. The effect is statistically robust, practically meaningful, and consistent with the operational hypothesis that improved stock management enhances patron experience. We recommend moving forward with full implementation.
