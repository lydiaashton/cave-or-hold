# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

---

## **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

**Date:** November 2025  
**Prepared by:** Data Analysis Team

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure is associated with a statistically significant improvement in service scores of approximately 2 points.** Based on analysis of 709 branches over 17 weeks, branches that adopted the new procedure showed higher service scores after implementation compared to branches that retained the old procedure. We recommend proceeding with a phased rollout to all branches, with continued monitoring.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **1. Study Overview**
| Component | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| Total branches | 709 |
| Branches using new procedure | 425 |
| Branches using old procedure | 284 |
| Study period | Weeks 6–22 (17 weeks) |
| Observations | 12,053 branch-weeks |
| Switch implemented | Week 15 |

### **2. Main Result: Positive Treatment Effect**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect of new procedure | **+2.0 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | **1.4 to 2.6 points** |
| Statistical significance | **p < 0.001** |

**What this means:** Branches using the new procedure improved by about 1 point after the switch, while control branches declined by about 1 point. The net difference is 2 points on a scale where the average branch scores about 50.

### **3. Effect Over Time**

The positive effect appears sustained throughout the 8-week post-switch period. Service score gaps between treatment and control branches widened from an average of 1.9 points before the switch to 3.9 points after.

### **4. Important Caveat**

Branches selected for the new procedure were already showing stronger service score trends before implementation. This suggests:
- The 2-point estimate may partially reflect pre-existing differences
- The true causal effect could be somewhat smaller than estimated
- Branches chosen for the pilot may have been better positioned to benefit

Even accounting for this, the post-switch improvement is statistically significant and meaningful.

### **5. Robustness**

The treatment effect remains stable when controlling for staffing levels and visit volumes, suggesting the improvement is not simply due to changes in these operational factors.

## **RECOMMENDATION**

**Proceed with rollout of the new procedure to all branches.** The evidence supports a positive effect on service scores, and the magnitude of improvement is meaningful for patron experience. A phased approach with continued monitoring will allow the cooperative to confirm results at scale and address any implementation challenges.

---

*Technical note: Analysis used difference-in-differences regression with branch and week fixed effects and cluster-robust standard errors. The parallel trends assumption shows some violation, suggesting the estimated effect should be interpreted as an upper bound on the true causal effect.*
