# **Memorandum to the Board**

## **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

### **Bottom Line**

**Recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.** Contrary to expectations, the new procedure produced a **statistically significant improvement** in service scores of approximately **2.1 points** (a 4% gain). Branches that kept the old procedure showed no change. This is a favorable surprise with no downside detected.

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. The New Procedure Improved Service Scores—Unexpectedly**
- Branches that adopted the new procedure saw their average service score increase by **2.1 points** after the switch
- Branches that kept the old procedure remained essentially unchanged (+0.1 points)
- The **net effect is +2.1 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.2 to 3.0 points)
- *This contradicts the prior expectation that the procedure change would have no measurable impact on service quality*

**2. Results Are Statistically Robust**
- The improvement is highly statistically significant (p < 0.001)
- The finding holds after accounting for:
  - Differences between individual branches
  - Weekly variations in staffing levels
  - Weekly variations in visitor volume

**3. The Analysis Is Methodologically Sound**
- **150 branches** studied over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure; **60 branches** served as a control group
- Pre-switch trends were parallel between groups, validating the comparison
- No evidence that the groups were diverging before the switch occurred

**4. Practical Significance**
- Average service score across all branches: ~50 points
- A 2.1-point improvement represents a **4% gain** in service quality
- The procedure change was not expected to affect service scores, making this a **pure upside finding**

---

### **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all branches

2. **Communicate the unexpected benefit** to branch managers—this may improve adoption morale

3. **Monitor post-rollout scores** to confirm the effect persists at full scale

4. **Document implementation practices** from the 90 early-adopter branches to support training

5. **Investigate the mechanism**—since this outcome was unexpected, understanding *why* the procedure improved service scores could inform future operational decisions

---

### **Analytical Approach**

This analysis used a **difference-in-differences** design, comparing service score changes for branches that switched procedures against those that did not. This isolates the procedure's effect from other factors affecting all branches (seasonal patterns, system-wide initiatives, etc.).

*Data: Weekly service scores, weeks 6–22, 150 branches. Trend visualization available upon request.*
