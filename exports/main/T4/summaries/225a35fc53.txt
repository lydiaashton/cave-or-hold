# **Impact of New Stock Rotation Procedure on Library Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores by approximately 1.8 points (3.5%).** This finding was unexpected, as the procedure change was not anticipated to affect service quality. However, the data shows a clear, statistically significant improvement that is unlikely to be due to chance. **We recommend proceeding with the system-wide rollout**, while investigating what aspects of the new procedure may be driving this unanticipated benefit.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Unexpected Positive Effect on Service Scores
| Group | Pre-Switch | Post-Switch | Change |
|-------|-----------|-------------|--------|
| Treatment (New Procedure) | 51.2 | 52.4 | +1.2 |
| Control (Old Procedure) | 51.9 | 51.3 | -0.6 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+1.8** |

- **Statistical significance**: p = 0.002 (less than 1% probability this occurred by chance)
- **95% confidence interval**: 0.65 to 2.93 points
- **Effect size**: Approximately 3.5% improvement relative to baseline

### 2. Analysis Validates the Comparison
- **Parallel trends confirmed**: Treatment and control branches tracked together before the switch (p = 0.92), supporting the validity of the comparison
- **All 150 branches** contributed data from both pre- and post-switch periods
- **Effect is specific to service scores**: Staffing levels and visitor volumes showed no differential changes, suggesting the improvement reflects genuine service quality gains rather than external factors

### 3. Why This Matters
Although the procedure change was not expected to impact service scores, the data indicates it has. Possible explanations worth exploring:
- Staff working more efficiently under the new system, freeing time for patron assistance
- Improved stock organization leading to faster item retrieval
- Psychological effect of implementing a visible improvement, boosting staff morale and engagement

---

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with the system-wide rollout to all 60 remaining branches.** The evidence shows a meaningful improvement in service quality at no apparent cost to staffing or visitor experience.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve rollout** of the new procedure to all branches still using the old method

2. **Investigate the mechanism**: Conduct brief interviews or surveys with staff at the 90 early-adopter branches to understand what aspects of the new procedure may be contributing to higher service scores

3. **Document and share learnings**: Capture any insights about why service improved and incorporate into training materials for remaining branches

4. **Monitor during rollout**: Track service scores at newly converted branches to confirm the effect replicates across the system

5. **Continue evaluation**: Reassess in 6 months to confirm the improvement is sustained and identify any branches needing additional support

---

*Analysis based on 2,550 weekly observations from 150 branches (weeks 6–22). Difference-in-differences methodology with cluster-robust standard errors.*
