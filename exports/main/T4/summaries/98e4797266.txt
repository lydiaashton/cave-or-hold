# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD OF DIRECTORS**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure  
**Date:** November 2025  
**Data Period:** Weeks 6–22 (17 weeks, 34 branches, 578 observations)

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

The new stock rotation procedure shows a **modest positive effect** on service scores, but the evidence is **not statistically conclusive**. Switched branches improved by approximately **2 points** (about 4% relative to average scores) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. However, this result carries meaningful uncertainty—we cannot rule out that the observed improvement occurred by chance.

**Recommendation:** Do not mandate system-wide rollout at this time. Continue the pilot and gather additional data to confirm whether the benefit is real and worthwhile.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **1. Estimated Impact on Service Scores**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated improvement | **+2.1 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | **-0.3 to +4.6 points** |
| Statistical significance | **p = 0.09** (not significant at conventional 0.05 threshold) |
| Relative improvement | **~4%** above average |

The confidence interval includes zero, meaning the true effect could be slightly negative, null, or moderately positive.

### **2. Branch-Level Performance**

- **12 of 20 switched branches** (60%) showed improvement after adopting the new procedure
- Average improvement for switched branches: **+1.5 points**
- Average change for control branches: **-0.6 points** (slight decline)
- Substantial variation exists: some branches gained up to 10 points, others lost up to 5 points

### **3. Study Validity**

The analysis uses a rigorous difference-in-differences approach with the following strengths:

✓ **Parallel trends confirmed**: Before the switch, both groups followed similar weekly trajectories  
✓ **Controlled for confounders**: Staffing levels and visitor volumes accounted for  
✓ **Proper statistical methods**: Standard errors adjusted for repeated observations within branches

### **4. Important Limitations**

⚠️ **Limited statistical power**: The study can only reliably detect effects larger than ~9.5 points. The observed 2-point effect may be real but remains uncertain with current data.

⚠️ **Short observation window**: Only 8 weeks of post-switch data limits precision.

⚠️ **Baseline differences**: Switched branches started with higher average scores, though this is statistically adjusted.

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

### **Immediate Actions**

1. **Extend data collection by 8–12 weeks** to improve statistical precision and confirm whether the effect persists.

2. **Document implementation variations** across branches to understand why some benefit more than others.

3. **Conduct cost-benefit analysis** to determine whether a 2-point improvement (if real) justifies the operational costs of the new procedure.

### **Decision Framework**

| Evidence Outcome | Board Decision |
|------------------|----------------|
| Effect confirmed ≥2 points with p < 0.05 | **Approve system-wide rollout** |
| Effect remains 1–3 points with p = 0.05–0.15 | **Continue pilot; expand to additional branches** |
| Effect diminishes or becomes negative | **Discontinue new procedure** |

### **Timeline Recommendation**

- **3 months**: Continue monitoring current pilot branches
- **6 months**: Reconvene with updated analysis and cost-benefit assessment
- **9 months**: Make final rollout decision based on accumulated evidence

---

## **CONCLUSION**

The new procedure shows promise but not proof. A premature system-wide rollout risks investing in a change that may not deliver meaningful benefits. A measured approach—continuing the pilot while gathering more evidence—protects the cooperative's resources while keeping the option open to adopt the procedure if stronger evidence emerges.
