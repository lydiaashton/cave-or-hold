# **Board Memorandum**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure  

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Recommend approval for systemwide rollout.** Contrary to initial expectations, the new procedure produced a measurable improvement in service scores. Branches using the new procedure scored **1.9 points higher** (approximately 4% improvement) than comparable branches using the old procedure. This finding is statistically robust and represents a meaningful gain in service quality at no additional cost.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Unexpected Positive Impact on Service Scores

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Old Procedure (Control) | 49.6 | 48.0 | ▼ -1.7 |
| New Procedure (Treatment) | 51.3 | 51.5 | ▲ +0.2 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+1.9** |

The new procedure did more than maintain scores—it protected branches from the decline observed in control branches while delivering a modest gain.

### 2. Results Are Statistically Reliable

- **Effect size:** +1.93 points (95% confidence interval: +0.73 to +3.14)
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.002 (less than 1% probability this occurred by chance)
- **Methodology:** Difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors
- **Validation:** Pre-treatment trends were parallel between groups, confirming valid comparison

### 3. Study Design Was Sound

- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (2,550 observations)
- **102 branches** adopted new procedure; **48 branches** retained old procedure
- No pre-existing differences in staffing or visit patterns between groups
- Service scores behaved similarly in both groups before the switch

### 4. Contextual Factors

- **Staffing correlation:** Higher staffing levels associate with better service scores, independent of the procedure change
- **Seasonal pattern:** Control branches experienced a notable decline after week 15, suggesting external factors (possibly seasonal) affected service quality
- The new procedure appears to buffer against these external pressures

---

## **Recommendation**

**Approve immediate rollout** of the new stock rotation and shelving procedure to all remaining branches. The evidence indicates:

✓ Measurable service quality improvement  
✓ No additional resource requirements  
✓ Protection against service score declines  
✓ Statistically validated results

## **Caveats and Considerations**

1. **Unexpected finding:** This result was not anticipated. While statistically sound, we recommend close monitoring during rollout to confirm the effect replicates at full scale.

2. **Staffing interaction:** Since staffing levels correlate with service scores, ensure adequate staffing during the transition period.

3. **External factors:** The control group decline after week 15 suggests seasonal or external influences. The new procedure's value may be partly in providing resilience against such factors.
