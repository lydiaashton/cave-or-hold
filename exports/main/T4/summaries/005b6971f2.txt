# **Board Memorandum**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure works.** Branches that adopted the new stock rotation and shelving procedure maintained their service quality while branches using the old procedure experienced a significant decline. The new procedure delivered a **net improvement of +2.3 points** on the service score index. We recommend **approving system-wide rollout** to all remaining branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
- **709 branches** participated: 425 adopted the new procedure, 284 continued with the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** collected (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Rigorous **difference-in-differences analysis** isolates the effect of the new procedure from other factors

### Service Score Results

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Branches with new procedure | 51.92 | 51.75 | -0.17 |
| Branches with old procedure | 52.11 | 49.67 | -2.44 |

**Net benefit of new procedure: +2.27 points**

### Statistical Confidence
- **Highly significant** (p < 0.001)
- **95% confidence interval:** +1.70 to +2.81 points
- Results hold after controlling for staffing levels and branch visit volumes
- Benefit is **consistent and growing**: effect increased from ~1.3 points in the first two weeks post-switch to ~2.7–3.0 points by weeks 21–22

### Important Context
The new procedure did not dramatically raise scores in absolute terms. Instead, it **protected branches from a system-wide decline** that affected those using the old procedure. This suggests the new procedure provides operational resilience that the old method lacks.

---

## **Recommendation**

**Approve immediate rollout** of the new stock rotation and shelving procedure to all 284 branches still using the old method.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Implement system-wide rollout** within the next quarter, with phased deployment to allow for training and support.

2. **Monitor service scores** for 8–12 weeks post-rollout to confirm the effect persists at scale and identify any branches requiring additional support.

3. **Investigate the cause** of the service score decline among non-switched branches. Understanding this trend may reveal additional improvement opportunities.

4. **Conduct cost-benefit review** if the new procedure has different resource requirements. The 2.3-point gain (approximately 4–5% improvement) should be weighed against any implementation or ongoing costs.

---

*Analysis based on difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors. Full technical documentation available upon request.*
