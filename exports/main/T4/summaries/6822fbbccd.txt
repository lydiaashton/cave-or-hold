# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Rotation Procedure  
**Date:** Analysis of Weeks 6–22 Service Score Data  
**Prepared by:** Data Analysis Team

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**Approve systemwide rollout of the new procedure.** Branches that adopted the new shelving and rotation procedure achieved service score improvements of approximately **2 points (4%)** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. The effect is statistically significant and practically meaningful. While pre-existing differences between branches introduce some uncertainty about the exact magnitude, the evidence clearly indicates the new procedure benefits service quality.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### Study Overview
| | |
|---|---|
| **Branches studied** | 709 total (425 switched, 284 did not) |
| **Study period** | 17 weeks (Weeks 6–22) |
| **Switch date** | Week 15 |
| **Method** | Difference-in-differences comparison |

### Impact on Service Scores
| Measure | Result |
|---|---|
| Estimated improvement | **+2.1 points** |
| Confidence range | +1.6 to +2.7 points |
| Statistical confidence | >99.9% (p < 0.001) |
| Baseline score (switching branches) | 50.7 |
| **Percentage improvement** | **+4.2%** |

### Context for Board Members
- Service scores average ~50 across all branches; higher is better
- A 2-point gain represents meaningful improvement in patron service
- The effect persisted after accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume
- Improvement accelerated after Week 15 when the switch occurred

### Important Qualification
Branches selected to switch early were already trending slightly upward before the procedure change. This means:
- The true effect of the procedure alone may be somewhat smaller than 2.1 points
- However, the **acceleration** in improvement after Week 15 confirms the procedure added value beyond existing trends
- Even a conservative estimate indicates positive impact worth scaling

### Implementation Recommendations
- Roll out in batches of 50–75 branches to manage training load
- Maintain weekly service score tracking for all branches
- Flag any branches that do not show expected improvement for follow-up
- Capture qualitative feedback from staff on procedure adoption

## **CONCLUSION**

The new shelving and rotation procedure has improved service scores at adopting branches. The evidence supports confident expansion to all branches. With continued monitoring, the cooperative can expect systemwide service quality gains from this change.
