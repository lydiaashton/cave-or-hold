# **Board Summary: Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure produced an unexpected benefit.** While we anticipated no change in service scores, branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw their scores improve by approximately **2.1 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant and represents a meaningful gain we did not forecast.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Unexpected Improvement in Service Scores**
- **Treatment effect**: +2.1 points (95% confidence interval: 1.0 to 3.3 points)
- **Statistical significance**: p < 0.001 (highly significant)
- Switched branches improved from 52.6 to 53.9 after the change
- Non-switched branches declined slightly from 52.0 to 51.2 over the same period
- **This contradicts our prior expectation** that the procedural change would have no measurable impact on service quality

### **2. The Analysis Is Reliable**
- **Study design**: 150 branches total (90 switched, 60 did not) over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- **Method**: Difference-in-differences analysis comparing switched vs. non-switched branches before and after week 15
- **Parallel trends validated**: Pre-switch trends were statistically similar between groups (p = 0.93), confirming the groups were comparable before the intervention
- **Robust to controls**: The effect persists after accounting for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume

### **3. Consistent Positive Effects**
- The improvement was sustained across all 8 post-switch weeks
- Weekly treatment effects ranged from +3.2 to +4.7 points
- The benefit showed no signs of fading during the observation period

### **4. Context on Effect Size**
- A 2.1-point gain on a ~50-point scale represents approximately a **4% improvement**
- This is a "free" benefit—the procedure change was not undertaken to improve service scores, yet it did so without additional cost

---

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended Actions**
1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** to all remaining branches—this is an unexpected win worth capturing
2. **Document the mechanism**: Investigate *why* the new procedure improved scores (e.g., faster item location, cleaner shelves, staff efficiency) to reinforce best practices
3. **Monitor implementation** to ensure consistent adoption across branches
4. **Track service scores** for the next 8–12 weeks post-rollout to confirm the effect persists at scale

### **Additional Considerations**
- **Training**: Share early learnings from switched branches to accelerate adoption
- **Timeline**: A phased rollout remains prudent to manage operational capacity
- **Broader review**: Consider whether other operational metrics (costs, staff time, patron satisfaction) were also affected

### **Caveats**
- The analysis covers 8 weeks post-switch; longer-term effects should be monitored
- Service scores are one metric; a fuller cost-benefit assessment may be warranted before full rollout
