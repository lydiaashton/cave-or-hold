# **Board Memorandum**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2025  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure is associated with a **modest but meaningful improvement in service scores** (+1.9 points, approximately 4% above baseline). While statistical certainty is moderate rather than definitive, the evidence points in a positive direction with no indication of harm. **We recommend proceeding with a phased rollout** to all branches while continuing to monitor outcomes.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
| Component | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Branches studied** | 34 total (20 switched, 14 did not) |
| **Observation period** | 17 weeks (weeks 6–22) |
| **Implementation date** | Week 15 |
| **Analysis method** | Difference-in-differences with branch fixed effects |

### Results Summary

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Estimated effect on service score** | +1.9 points |
| **Baseline average service score** | 49.9 |
| **Effect size** | ~4% improvement |
| **Confidence level** | 95% of bootstrap samples show positive effect |
| **95% confidence interval** | -0.3 to +4.4 points |

### What Happened

- **Branches that switched** saw their average service score rise from 49.7 to 50.9 (+1.2 points)
- **Branches that did not switch** saw their average score decline from 49.6 to 48.9 (-0.8 points)
- **Net difference**: The new procedure is associated with a ~1.9 point advantage

### Trend Visualization

The chart below tracks average service scores over time. The red dashed line marks week 15, when the new procedure took effect:

![Service Scores Over Time](service_scores_over_time.png)

**Key observation:** After week 15, switched branches (blue) generally maintained or improved scores, while non-switched branches (orange) showed more volatility and a downward trend.

### Analytical Strengths

✓ **Parallel trends verified**: Before the switch, both groups followed similar patterns  
✓ **Controls included**: Analysis accounts for staffing levels and visitor volume  
✓ **Branch differences accounted for**: Each branch serves as its own control  
✓ **Robust inference**: Bootstrap analysis confirms stability of estimates

---

## **Recommendations**

### 1. Proceed with Phased Rollout
The evidence supports a positive effect with low risk of harm. A phased approach allows for continued learning and adjustment.

### 2. Extend Monitoring Period
Current data covers only 8 weeks post-implementation. Track scores for an additional 8–12 weeks to confirm sustained benefits.

### 3. Conduct Cost-Benefit Analysis
Quantify implementation costs (training, time, materials) against the value of a ~4% service score improvement.

### 4. Investigate Branch-Level Variation
Some branches may benefit more than others. Analyze whether branch size, location, or baseline performance affects outcomes.

### 5. Monitor Non-Switched Branches
The decline in control branch scores warrants attention. Determine if this reflects broader trends or specific operational issues.

---

## **Limitations to Consider**

| Limitation | Implication |
|------------|-------------|
| **Marginal statistical significance** (p ≈ 0.10) | Cannot rule out no effect with high confidence |
| **Short post-implementation window** (8 weeks) | Long-term effects remain unknown |
| **Unmeasured external factors** | Seasonal effects or other changes could influence results |

---

## **Decision Request**

The Board is asked to:

1. **Approve phased rollout** of the new procedure to remaining branches
2. **Authorize continued monitoring** with monthly reporting to the Board
3. **Request a follow-up analysis** in 6 months to assess long-term impact
