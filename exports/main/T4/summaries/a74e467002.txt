# **Memorandum to the Board**

## **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

### **Bottom Line**

**Contrary to expectations, the new procedure meaningfully improved service scores.** While we anticipated no change in service quality, branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **2.1 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant and represents approximately a **4% gain** in service quality. 

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout.** The new procedure delivers unexpected benefits with no identified downsides.

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. Unexpected but Clear Improvement**
- The new procedure increased service scores by **+2.15 points** (95% confidence interval: +0.96 to +3.33 points)
- This effect is highly statistically significant (p < 0.001)
- Against an average baseline of ~50 points, this represents a **4.3% improvement**
- *This finding contradicts our prior expectation that the procedural change would be neutral for service scores*

**2. Effect Is Consistent and Sustained**
- Early post-switch period (weeks 15-18): +2.32 points
- Later post-switch period (weeks 19-22): +1.97 points
- The improvement appeared immediately and persisted; there is no evidence the effect is temporary

**3. Why Might This Have Happened?**
While this analysis did not investigate mechanisms, possible explanations include:
- Staff spending less time searching for materials and more time assisting patrons
- Improved organization leading to faster transaction times
- Reduced frustration for both staff and visitors

**4. Rigorous Analysis**
- We used a **difference-in-differences** approach comparing 90 switched branches to 60 control branches
- Methods controlled for time trends, branch-specific characteristics, staffing levels, and visitor volume
- Pre-switch trends between groups were statistically similar, supporting the validity of the comparison

**5. Study Scope**
- **150 branches** total
- **17 weeks** of data per branch (weeks 6-22)
- **2,550 observations** in total

---

### **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches

2. **Document the unexpected benefit** in implementation materials:
   - This finding strengthens the business case beyond any operational efficiencies already anticipated
   - Consider communicating this to branch managers to build support for the change

3. **Monitor during transition**:
   - Track service scores for newly switched branches to confirm similar gains
   - Identify any branches that underperform and may need additional support

4. **Investigate the mechanism** (optional):
   - Consider qualitative follow-up with switched branches to understand *why* service scores improved
   - This could inform future procedural improvements

5. **Plan follow-up evaluation**:
   - Re-assess service scores 3-6 months after full rollout
   - Confirm the effect persists at scale
