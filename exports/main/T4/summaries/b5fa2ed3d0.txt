# **MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched saw significantly better outcomes than those that kept the old method. We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### What We Studied
- **150 branches** tracked over 17 weeks (February through June)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure in mid-April (week 15)
- **60 branches** continued with the old procedure (comparison group)
- Service scores are measured weekly (average branch scores ~50; higher is better)

### What We Found

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Old Procedure | 49.85 | 47.79 | ▼ 2.06 |
| New Procedure | 50.54 | 50.37 | ▼ 0.17 |

**The new procedure prevented a 1.9-point decline in service scores.**

While service scores dropped across the system after mid-April (likely due to seasonal factors), branches using the new procedure held steady while those on the old procedure fell noticeably.

### How Confident Are We?

**Very confident.** The result is statistically significant (p = 0.002), meaning there is less than a 1% chance this finding is due to random variation. The analysis accounted for:
- Differences between individual branches
- Week-to-week fluctuations affecting all branches
- Pre-existing trends (which were similar between groups before the switch)

---

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with system-wide rollout of the new procedure.**

The evidence contradicts the initial expectation that the change would make no difference. Instead, the new procedure demonstrably protects service quality during periods when scores would otherwise decline.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve full rollout** to remaining 60 branches using the old procedure

2. **Track outcomes** during implementation to confirm benefits are replicated

3. **Investigate the system-wide score decline** after week 15 to identify and address external factors

4. **Review implementation costs** (training, staffing, equipment) against the service score benefits to confirm value

---

*Technical note: Analysis uses difference-in-differences regression with branch and week fixed effects and cluster-robust standard errors. Treatment effect estimate: +1.88 points (95% CI: 0.72 to 3.05).*
