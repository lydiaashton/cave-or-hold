# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.3 points** (about **5% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and consistent across the post-switch period. 

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all remaining branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Overview**
- **150 branches** participated: 90 adopted the new procedure, 60 continued with the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data:** Weeks 6–14 (before switch), Weeks 15–22 (after switch)
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, the standard method for evaluating policy changes with treatment and control groups

### **Impact on Service Scores**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 48.8 | 51.0 | +2.2 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 48.1 | 52.6 | +4.5 |
| **Effect attributable to new procedure** | | | **+2.3** |

- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001 (highly significant)
- **95% confidence interval:** 1.1 to 3.7 points
- **Relative improvement:** Approximately 5% over baseline

### **Why We're Confident in This Result**

1. **Clear timing:** Before the switch, treatment branches actually scored slightly *lower* than control branches. After implementation, they consistently scored *higher*—the gap reversed and widened over time.

2. **Parallel pre-trends:** Both groups followed similar trajectories before the switch, validating the comparison.

3. **Robust findings:** The effect holds after accounting for weekly staffing levels, visitor volume, and inherent differences between individual branches.

4. **Consistent performance:** The treatment group outperformed the control group in all 8 weeks after implementation, with gains growing from +0.4 points (week 15) to +2.6 points (week 22).

## **Appendix: Methodology Notes**

Analysis based on 2,550 weekly observations (150 branches × 17 weeks). Effect estimated using difference-in-differences regression with branch fixed effects and cluster-robust standard errors. Pre-trend analysis confirms no significant differential trends between groups prior to implementation.
