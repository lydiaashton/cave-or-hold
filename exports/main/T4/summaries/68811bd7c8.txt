# **Board Summary: Evaluation of New Shelving Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new shelving procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.1 points** (a 4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant (p = 0.001). Although we expected no difference, the data shows a clear benefit. **We recommend proceeding with a full rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

| Finding | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Treatment Effect** | +2.11 points on service score (4% improvement) |
| **Statistical Significance** | p = 0.001 (highly significant) |
| **Treatment Branches** | 90 branches; 75.6% showed improvement |
| **Control Branches** | 60 branches; 53.3% showed improvement |
| **Pre-Trend Validation** | No significant differences before the switch |
| **Persistence** | Effect remained stable across all 8 post-switch weeks |

### What the Data Shows

1. **Clear Improvement**: Treatment branches gained +2.23 points after switching, while control branches gained only +0.12 points. The net effect of +2.11 points is attributable to the new procedure.

2. **Valid Comparison**: Before week 15, treatment and control branches followed parallel trends with no statistically significant differences. This supports a causal interpretation.

3. **Robust Result**: The effect holds after controlling for staffing levels and visit volumes, and remains significant when accounting for branch-level variation.

4. **Broad Benefit**: Three-quarters of treatment branches improved, versus barely half of control branches, indicating the benefit is widespread rather than driven by a few outliers.

## **Next Steps**

| Priority | Action |

| **2** | Monitor service scores at newly converted branches to confirm the effect replicates |

---

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with system-wide rollout.** The evidence indicates the new procedure delivers a meaningful, statistically significant improvement in service quality with no identified drawbacks. Given the cooperative's goal of maximizing service quality across all branches, extending this procedure is justified.

*Analysis prepared from 17 weeks of data (weeks 6–22) covering 150 branches. Methodology: difference-in-differences with cluster-robust standard errors.*
