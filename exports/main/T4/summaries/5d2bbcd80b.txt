# **Memorandum to the Board of Directors**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure  

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.0 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents a **3.8% improvement** and is statistically significant (p < 0.001). We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
| | |
|---|---|
| **Branches studied** | 709 total (425 treatment, 284 control) |
| **Observation period** | 17 weeks (weeks 6–22) |
| **Switch date** | Week 15 |
| **Analysis method** | Difference-in-differences with cluster-robust standard errors |

### Results Summary
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated treatment effect | **+1.98 service score points** |
| 95% Confidence interval | [1.41, 2.55] |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 |
| Effect size | 3.8% improvement over baseline |

### What the Data Shows

**Before the switch (weeks 6–14):** Treatment and control branches performed nearly identically, with average service scores of 52.15 and 52.27 respectively. Week-to-week differences fluctuated randomly around zero, confirming the two groups were comparable before the intervention.

**After the switch (weeks 15–22):** Treatment branches pulled ahead consistently. The treatment group improved by 1.62 points on average, while the control group declined slightly by 0.33 points. The gap between groups widened over time, with the largest differences appearing in weeks 20–22.

**The effect is robust:** The 2-point improvement holds even after accounting for weekly variations in staffing levels and branch visits, both of which also influence service scores.

---

## **Recommendations and Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout.** The evidence strongly supports expanding the new procedure to all remaining branches. The improvement is statistically significant, practically meaningful, and consistent across the post-switch period.

2. **Implement phased conversion.** Consider rolling out the procedure in waves over the next quarter, allowing staff to support branches during transition and address any issues that arise.

3. **Establish ongoing monitoring.** Continue weekly service score tracking for all branches. Set up alerts for branches that fall more than 5 points below their pre-conversion baseline.

4. **Document best practices.** Interview high-performing treatment branches to identify specific practices that drove their success. Incorporate these findings into training materials.

5. **Plan follow-up evaluation.** Conduct a second analysis in 6 months to confirm the effect persists and to assess whether early-adopter branches maintain their advantage.

---

## **Appendix: Technical Notes**

- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences regression model with cluster-robust standard errors at the branch level
- Model controls for staffing index and visits index
- Parallel trends assumption is supported by pre-period data showing no systematic differences between treatment and control groups
- Data source: branches.csv (12,053 observations)
