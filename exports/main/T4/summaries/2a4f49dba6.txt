# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**Contrary to expectations, the new procedure did affect service scores—and positively.** Branches that adopted the new shelving and rotation procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **1.96 points (4.1% improvement)** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant (p < 0.01). While we anticipated no change, the data suggest a meaningful benefit. We recommend proceeding with caution: consider a phased rollout while investigating why this unexpected improvement occurred.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect (Unexpected Positive Impact)
- **Estimated improvement:** +1.96 service score points (95% CI: 0.67 to 3.25)
- **Relative gain:** 4.1% above baseline
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.003 (highly significant)
- **Note:** This finding contradicts the prior expectation of no effect

### 2. Study Design & Sample
- **150 branches total:** 90 adopted the new procedure, 60 kept the old
- **17 weeks of data:** Weeks 6–22 (9 weeks before, 8 weeks after the switch)
- **Switch point:** Week 15
- **2,550 total observations** (branch-week combinations)

### 3. Parallel Trends Assumption Validated
Before the switch, treatment and control branches followed similar trends in service scores. A formal pre-trend test shows no statistically significant difference in trends before Week 15 (p = 0.076), supporting the validity of our comparison. This strengthens confidence that the observed effect is attributable to the procedure change rather than pre-existing differences.

### 4. Effect Is Consistent Across Post-Switch Weeks
| Week | Treatment Effect |
|------|------------------|
| 15   | +0.80            |
| 16   | +1.25            |
| 17   | +2.02            |
| 18   | +1.50            |
| 19   | +1.28            |
| 20   | +1.76            |
| 21   | +0.12            |
| 22   | +1.11            |

The improvement appeared immediately and persisted throughout the observation period, with no evidence of fade-out.

### 5. Not Driven by Staffing or Visits
The procedure change did not significantly affect staffing levels or branch visit volumes, suggesting the service score improvement stems from the shelving/rotation process itself rather than confounding operational changes.

## **Why This Matters**

Given the expectation of no impact, this finding warrants attention:

1. **Unexpected benefit:** If the procedure change was designed solely for operational efficiency (not service improvement), discovering a service score boost is a valuable co-benefit.

2. **Causal confidence:** The research design (difference-in-differences with fixed effects) and validated parallel trends support a causal interpretation, not just correlation.

3. **Low-risk upside:** A 4% service score improvement comes at no additional staffing cost and may justify broader adoption.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Review the finding with operations staff** to understand potential mechanisms—why might the new procedure improve service scores?

2. **Approve a phased rollout** to remaining 60 branches, with continued monitoring to confirm the effect replicates

3. **Extend the observation period** to 16–24 weeks post-switch to assess whether the effect persists, grows, or diminishes over time

4. **Document implementation details** from high-performing treatment branches to identify best practices

5. **Revisit expectations** for future procedural changes—this finding suggests operational changes may have service implications we did not anticipate

---

## **Technical Notes**

Analysis used a difference-in-differences regression with branch and week fixed effects, with standard errors clustered at the branch level to account for within-branch correlation. The model controls for time-invariant branch characteristics and common weekly shocks affecting all branches. The pre-trend test supports the parallel trends assumption required for causal inference.
