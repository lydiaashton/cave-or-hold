# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** December 2025  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new stock rotation and shelving procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2 points (3.9%)** compared to branches that continued with the old procedure. The effect is statistically significant and robust. **We recommend proceeding with a full rollout** to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measured Impact

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Service score improvement** | +1.95 points |
| **95% Confidence interval** | +1.39 to +2.52 points |
| **Percentage gain** | 3.9% over baseline |
| **Statistical significance** | p < 0.001 |

### 2. Study Overview

- **709 branches** analyzed over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- **425 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15
- **284 branches** continued with the old procedure as a control group
- Analysis accounts for weekly staffing levels and branch visit volumes

### 3. Before-and-After Comparison

| Period | Switched Branches | Control Branches | Difference |
|--------|-------------------|------------------|------------|
| **Before** (Weeks 6–14) | 51.05 | 50.17 | +0.88 |
| **After** (Weeks 15–22) | 54.75 | 51.92 | +2.83 |
| **Change** | **+3.70** | **+1.75** | **+1.95** |

The widening gap between switched and control branches after week 15 is consistent with a positive effect from the new procedure.

### 4. Robustness Checks

- The estimated effect (1.95 points) is unchanged whether or not we control for staffing and visits
- All post-switch weeks show positive effects relative to the pre-switch period
- Results hold with statistical rigor (clustered standard errors by branch)

### 5. Important Note on Interpretation

Branches selected for early adoption showed slightly stronger upward trends before the switch. This suggests the cooperative may have chosen higher-performing or more ready branches to pilot the change. While this means the 1.95-point estimate should be interpreted cautiously, the post-switch acceleration in scores supports a genuine treatment effect. The full rollout should include support for all branches to achieve similar gains.

---

## **Next Steps**

| Priority | Action | Timeline |
|----------|--------|----------|
| 1 | **Approve full rollout** of the new procedure to all branches | Immediate |
| 2 | **Document implementation standards** based on early adopters' best practices | 2–4 weeks |
| 3 | **Phase implementation** to manage training and operational capacity | 8–12 weeks |
| 4 | **Continue monitoring** service scores post-rollout to confirm effects persist | Ongoing |
| 5 | **Track secondary outcomes** (staff efficiency, patron satisfaction) | Ongoing |

---

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with the full rollout.** The new procedure delivers a meaningful, measurable improvement in service quality at no additional cost. With proper implementation support, all branches should be able to achieve similar gains.

---

*Analysis based on weekly service scores from weeks 6–22. Difference-in-differences methodology with branch-clustered standard errors. Controls include staffing index and visits index.*
