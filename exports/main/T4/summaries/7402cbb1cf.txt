# **Board Summary: Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure shows a **positive but statistically inconclusive effect** on service scores. Switching branches improved by approximately **2.3 points** (about 5% above baseline) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. While this suggests the new procedure is beneficial, the evidence is not strong enough to rule out random chance. The board should weigh this modest, uncertain gain against implementation costs before deciding on full rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. What We Measured**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure in week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Service scores average ~50 points (higher is better)

### **2. The Results**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated improvement from new procedure | **+2.3 points** |
| Statistical significance | **Not significant** (p = 0.16) |
| 95% confidence interval | **-0.9 to +5.5 points** |
| Minimum effect we could reliably detect | **4.6 points** |

### **3. What This Means**
- The observed 2.3-point gain is **real but imprecisely measured**
- The true effect could be slightly negative (-0.9) or meaningfully positive (+5.5)
- **No week showed worse performance** in switching branches—every post-switch week favored the new procedure
- The study is **underpowered**: it can only confidently detect larger effects (4.6+ points)

---

## **Recommendations**

### **Option A: Cautious Expansion (Recommended)**
Given the consistently positive direction of results and no evidence of harm, proceed with a **phased rollout** to additional branches while continuing to monitor. This balances potential benefits against uncertainty.

### **Option B: Continue Pilot**
Keep the current arrangement for **8–12 more weeks** to gather additional data. This would improve statistical power and provide a clearer answer before any broader commitment.

### **Option C: Full Rollout**
If the board views a 2–3 point improvement as valuable relative to costs, proceed with systemwide implementation. Acknowledge that the evidence is suggestive rather than definitive.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Define success criteria**: What minimum improvement justifies the cost of change? (e.g., "2+ points sustained over 6 months")

2. **Conduct cost-benefit analysis**: Compare the modest service gains against training, implementation, and ongoing operational costs.

3. **Extend data collection**: Continue tracking all branches for at least one more quarter to strengthen the evidence base.

4. **Explore variation**: Investigate whether certain branch types (by size, location, or staffing) benefit more from the new procedure.

---

## **Technical Appendix**

- **Method**: Difference-in-differences regression with robust standard errors
- **Controls**: Staffing index and visits index included; results unchanged
- **Pre-trend check**: No significant difference in trends between groups before the switch (p = 0.60), supporting the validity of the comparison
- **Data**: 578 branch-week observations from the cooperative's weekly reporting system
