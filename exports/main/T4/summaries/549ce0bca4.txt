# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.3 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents a **4.4% improvement** and is statistically significant (p < 0.001). 

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure; **60 branches** kept the old procedure
- The switch occurred at **week 15**
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach with branch and week fixed effects

### Main Results

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Treatment (switched) | 52.15 | 52.30 | +0.15 |
| Control (not switched) | 52.72 | 50.56 | −2.17 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+2.30** |

- **Estimated effect: +2.30 points** (95% CI: 1.0 to 3.6)
- **Statistical significance: p < 0.001**
- **Effect size: 0.24 standard deviations**

### Why This Result Is Reliable

1. **Parallel trends confirmed**: Before the switch, treatment and control branches followed similar trajectories (p = 0.80 for differential pre-trends).

2. **Robust across methods**: The effect holds whether using simple DiD (+2.31), controlling for staffing/visits (+2.28), or applying branch fixed effects (+2.30).

3. **Timing aligns with intervention**: Event study analysis shows the effect emerges only after week 15, with no pre-existing divergence between groups.

4. **Important context**: Control branches experienced a 2.2-point decline after week 15. The new procedure appears to have **protected against deterioration** rather than driving dramatic gains.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all 150 branches.

2. **Monitor during implementation**: Track service scores to confirm effects replicate at scale.

3. **Investigate the control group decline**: Determine why non-switched branches saw scores drop after week 15—this may reveal external factors the new procedure helps mitigate.

4. **Conduct cost-benefit analysis**: Weigh implementation costs against the 4.4% service improvement.

5. **Plan follow-up evaluation**: Collect post-rollout data to assess long-term sustainability and identify branches needing transition support.
