# **Board Summary: New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure Evaluation**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by **1.95 points** (approximately **4% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This result is statistically reliable and consistent across the post-switch period. **We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measurable Improvement
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Branches that switched | 51.93 | 53.26 | **+1.33** |
| Branches that did not switch | 51.15 | 50.53 | **-0.62** |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+1.95 points** |

- **Statistical confidence:** Greater than 99% (p < 0.001)
- **Effect size:** 3.8% improvement relative to baseline

### 2. Study Overview
- **709 branches** tracked over **17 weeks**
- **425 branches** adopted the new procedure at week 15
- **284 branches** maintained the old procedure (comparison group)
- Analysis accounts for branch-specific factors, staffing levels, and visitor volumes

### 3. Consistency of Results
- **66% of switched branches** saw service score improvements
- Effect remained stable across all 8 weeks after the switch
- Results hold after controlling for staffing and visit patterns

### 4. Important Context
- Branches that did not switch experienced a slight decline (-0.62 points)
- This suggests the new procedure may also help branches avoid score erosion
- About one-third of switched branches saw no improvement, indicating implementation quality matters

---

## **Recommendations**

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| **1** | **Approve system-wide rollout** to remaining 284 branches | Clear evidence of improvement; low risk based on pilot results |
| **2** | **Develop implementation support package** for rolling out branches | 34% of switched branches did not improve; training and guidance can help |
| **3** | **Continue monitoring** service scores for 8-12 weeks post-rollout | Confirm effects persist at scale; identify any emerging issues |
| **4** | **Document best practices** from high-performing switched branches | Capture lessons learned to maximize success in remaining branches |

---

## **Questions the Board May Have**

**Q: How confident are we in these results?**  
A: Very confident. The analysis uses a rigorous method (difference-in-differences) that isolates the effect of the procedure change from other factors. The result is statistically significant at the 99%+ confidence level.

**Q: Could something else explain the improvement?**  
A: We controlled for staffing levels, visitor volumes, and branch-specific characteristics. The comparison group (branches that did not switch) helps rule out system-wide factors like seasonal changes.

**Q: What if the effect doesn't hold at scale?**  
A: This is a valid concern. We recommend continued monitoring after rollout and maintaining flexibility to adjust implementation based on ongoing results.

**Q: Why did some branches not improve?**  
A: Implementation quality likely varies. We recommend investigating these branches to identify barriers and develop targeted support.

---

## **Appendix: Technical Summary**

- **Method:** Difference-in-differences regression with branch fixed effects
- **Data:** 12,053 branch-week observations (709 branches × 17 weeks)
- **Treatment effect:** +1.95 points (SE: 0.19; 95% CI: 1.58 to 2.32)
- **Robustness:** Effect unchanged when controlling for staffing and visit covariates
