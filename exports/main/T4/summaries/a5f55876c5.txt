# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

## **New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure: Impact on Service Scores**

---

### **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new stock rotation and shelving procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **1.8 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents a **3.4% improvement** and is statistically significant. We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.

---

### **KEY FINDINGS**

**1. The New Procedure Works**

| Period | Control Branches (old procedure) | Treatment Branches (new procedure) |
|--------|----------------------------------|-----------------------------------|
| Before switch (weeks 6–14) | 51.54 | 51.83 |
| After switch (weeks 15–22) | 52.45 | 54.57 |
| **Change** | **+0.91** | **+2.74** |

The new procedure is associated with a **+1.82 point improvement** in service scores (95% confidence interval: 1.3 to 2.4 points).

**2. The Result Is Statistically Reliable**

- **709 branches** analyzed: 425 adopted the new procedure, 284 kept the old
- **17 weeks of data** with the switch occurring in week 15
- Effect is significant at p < 0.001 (less than 1 in 1,000 chance the result is due to random variation)
- Result holds after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

**3. Context for the Effect Size**

- Average service score across all branches: 52.7 points
- The 1.8-point gain equals about **one-fifth of a standard deviation**—a modest but meaningful improvement for an operational change

**4. Important Caveat**

Treatment branches showed slightly stronger improvement trends even before the switch, suggesting they may have been selected partly because they were already on an upward trajectory. Our analysis accounts for fixed branch differences, but the true effect could be somewhat smaller than estimated.

---

### **NEXT STEPS**

| Action | Rationale |
|--------|-----------|
| **1. Approve system-wide rollout** | Evidence supports expanding the procedure to all 709 branches |
| **2. Monitor during implementation** | Track service scores to confirm the effect holds across different branch types |
| **3. Document best practices** | Capture what worked well in early-adopter branches to support training |
| **4. Plan long-term evaluation** | Re-assess after 6–12 months to ensure effects persist over time |
