# Board Memorandum

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving & Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## Bottom Line

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout.**

Branches that adopted the new shelving and stock rotation procedure achieved **significantly higher service scores** than branches that retained the old procedure. The improvement is statistically robust, sustained over time, and represents meaningful gains in service quality.

---

## Key Findings

### 1. Measurable Improvement

Branches using the new procedure saw service scores increase by **2 to 4 points** relative to control branches. Given that the average branch scores approximately 50 points, this represents a **4–8% improvement** in service quality.

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Branches switched to new procedure | 425 |
| Branches retained old procedure | 284 |
| Observation period | 17 weeks (weeks 6–22) |
| Switch implementation | Week 15 |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 |

### 2. Service Score Comparison

| Period | Switched Branches | Control Branches | Difference |
|--------|------------------|------------------|------------|
| Before switch (weeks 6–14) | 50.3 | 48.7 | 1.6 points |
| After switch (weeks 15–22) | 55.6 | 52.0 | 3.6 points |

The gap between groups widened after the switch, indicating the procedure itself drove improvement beyond pre-existing differences between branches.

### 3. Effect Was Sustained

The positive effect appeared immediately in week 15 and **remained significant in all 8 post-switch weeks**. There is no evidence of the effect diminishing over time.

### 4. Analysis Rigor

Multiple analytical approaches confirm the same conclusion:

- **Simple comparison:** +2.02 points (95% CI: 1.50 to 2.55)
- **Branch fixed effects:** +3.62 points (95% CI: 2.34 to 4.89)
- **Event study analysis:** +2.21 points

All models use cluster-robust standard errors to account for repeated observations within branches.

---

## Limitations & Considerations

1. **Self-selection:** Branches that volunteered to switch had slightly higher baseline scores than control branches. However, the branch fixed effects model—which isolates within-branch changes—still shows a significant positive effect.

2. **Observation window:** We have 8 weeks of post-switch data. Continued monitoring during rollout is recommended to confirm long-term sustainability.

3. **External factors:** Staffing levels and visitor volume both correlate with service scores. These should be monitored during rollout to distinguish procedure effects from operational changes.

---

## Recommended Next Steps

| Priority | Action | Timeline |
|----------|--------|----------|
| 1 | **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure | Immediate |
| 2 | Develop implementation support materials for remaining branches | 2–4 weeks |
| 3 | Establish ongoing service score monitoring during rollout | Ongoing |
| 4 | Investigate specific mechanisms driving improvement (e.g., faster item location, reduced staff time) | 1–3 months |
| 5 | Set branch-level targets expecting 2–4 point improvements | During rollout |

---

## Conclusion

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure has demonstrably improved service scores across the 425 branches that adopted it. The effect is statistically significant, practically meaningful, and sustained over the observation period. **We recommend proceeding with a full system-wide rollout.**
