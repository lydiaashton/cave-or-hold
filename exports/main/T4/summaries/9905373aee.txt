# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

**Date:** November 2026

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure **improved service scores** by approximately 2.1 points (about 4% relative to the baseline average of 50). This effect is statistically significant and consistent across the post-switch period. Contrary to the expectation that the procedure would make no difference, the evidence indicates a meaningful positive impact on branch service quality.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### Study Overview
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **60 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, the standard method for evaluating policy changes with staggered adoption

### Main Results

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated treatment effect | **+2.1 points** on service score |
| 95% Confidence interval | [0.8, 3.4] points |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.01 (highly significant) |
| Relative improvement | ~4% above baseline |

### What the Numbers Mean

The control group (branches that did not switch) saw service scores **decline by 0.7 points** after week 15. Meanwhile, branches that adopted the new procedure saw scores **increase by 1.4 points**. The net effect of the new procedure is the difference between these changes: **+2.1 points**.

### Why We Trust These Results

1. **Parallel trends validated**: Before the switch, service score trends were statistically indistinguishable between the two groups (all pre-period comparisons p > 0.24). This confirms the control group provides a valid counterfactual for what would have happened to switching branches without the change.

2. **Consistent effects over time**: The positive effect appeared immediately after the switch (week 15) and persisted throughout all 8 weeks of post-switch observation, with no signs of fading.

3. **Robust to alternative explanations**: The result holds when controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume, and when accounting for branch-specific characteristics and week-to-week fluctuations.

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to the remaining 60 branches.

2. **Monitor implementation**: Track service scores during rollout to confirm effects persist at scale and identify any branches needing additional support.

3. **Document best practices**: Capture implementation experiences from the 90 switching branches to support training at remaining locations.

4. **Conduct cost-benefit analysis**: If the new procedure has associated costs (training, labor, equipment), weigh them against the 4% service score improvement and any downstream benefits.

5. **Longer-term follow-up**: Continue monitoring beyond the initial 8-week post-switch window to assess whether effects are sustained over time.
