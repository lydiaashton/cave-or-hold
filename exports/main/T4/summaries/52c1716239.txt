# MEMORANDUM

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## Bottom Line

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure is associated with a **modest improvement in service scores** (approximately +2 points on a ~50-point scale, or about 4%). However, this effect is **not statistically significant at conventional levels**. The evidence points in a positive direction but does not provide strong confirmation that the procedure change caused the improvement. We recommend a **cautious continuation of the rollout** while collecting additional data to strengthen the evidence base.

---

## Key Findings

### Study Overview
- **34 branches** participated: 20 adopted the new procedure, 14 maintained the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data**: Weeks 6–14 (before) and Weeks 15–22 (after the switch)
- Analysis compares changes in service scores between switching and non-switching branches

### Results

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched Branches | 47.90 | 48.53 | +0.63 |
| Non-Switched Branches | 51.95 | 50.86 | −1.09 |
| **Net Effect (Difference-in-Differences)** | | | **+1.71** |

### Statistical Confidence
- **Estimated effect**: +1.7 to +2.0 service score points
- **Statistical significance**: p = 0.10 to 0.14 (not significant at the 5% level)
- **95% Confidence Interval**: Approximately −0.4 to +4.4 points
- **Effect size**: 0.18 standard deviations (small-to-moderate)

### Important Context

1. **Baseline differences mattered**: Branches that switched started with lower service scores (47.9 vs. 51.9). Our analysis accounts for this by comparing *changes* within each branch, not raw scores.

2. **Trends were comparable before the switch**: Both groups showed similar week-to-week patterns before week 15, supporting the validity of our comparison.

3. **The control group declined**: Non-switching branches saw service scores drop after week 15 (−1.09 points). The net effect reflects both the treatment group's slight improvement AND the control group's decline.

4. **Other factors**: Higher visit volumes were associated with better service scores. Staffing levels showed no clear relationship.

---

## Next Steps

### Recommended Actions

1. **Proceed with cautious rollout**
   - The positive direction of the effect, combined with likely modest implementation costs, supports continuing the rollout
   - Set realistic expectations: improvements are likely small (1–2 points)

2. **Extend the evaluation period**
   - Collect 8–12 additional weeks of data to:
     - Increase statistical confidence
     - Determine if effects persist or grow over time
     - Rule out short-term novelty effects

3. **Monitor implementation quality**
   - Ensure branches are executing the new procedure consistently
   - Variation in implementation could be masking larger effects

4. **Continue tracking multiple metrics**
   - Service score is one indicator; also monitor operational costs, staff satisfaction, and patron feedback

5. **Consider targeted support**
   - Investigate whether certain branch types (by size, location, or baseline performance) benefit more from the new procedure

### Caveats for Board Consideration

- The analysis cannot definitively prove causation, though the research design is robust
- The confidence interval includes zero, meaning we cannot rule out no effect
- The control group's decline after week 15 may reflect seasonal factors or other unrelated changes
- A final decision should weigh service scores alongside operational costs and staff input
