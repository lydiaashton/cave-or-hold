# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact Analysis**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new shelving and rotation procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw their service scores increase by approximately **2.1 points** (about 4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and benefited most branches. **We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Measurable Improvement in Service Scores**

| Group | Before Switch (Weeks 6-14) | After Switch (Weeks 15-22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------------|---------------------------|--------|
| New Procedure Branches (90 branches) | 50.11 | 52.35 | **+2.23** |
| Old Procedure Branches (60 branches) | 50.61 | 50.73 | +0.12 |

**Net effect of the new procedure: +2.1 points**

### **2. Statistical Confidence**

- The improvement is **statistically significant** (p < 0.01)
- 95% confidence interval: **+0.9 to +3.4 points**
- Results hold after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### **3. Broad-Based Benefits**

- **76% of switched branches** saw their service scores improve
- Only **53% of control branches** improved during the same period
- The effect represents a meaningful **0.23 standard deviation** improvement

### **4. Valid Comparison**

- Before the switch, both groups followed nearly identical trends
- This confirms the improvement is attributable to the procedure change, not pre-existing differences between branches

### **5. Study Scope**

- **150 branches** total (90 switched, 60 did not)
- **17 weeks** of data with the switch occurring at week 15
- **2,550 observations** analyzed

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all 60 branches still using the old method.

2. **Monitor implementation** during the transition to confirm newly switched branches see similar gains.

3. **Document the procedure** thoroughly to ensure consistent implementation across all branches.

4. **Conduct a follow-up evaluation** in 3-6 months to confirm improvements are sustained and to identify any additional operational benefits.

---
