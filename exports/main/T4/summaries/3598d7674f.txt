# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.2 points** (about **4.4%**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and consistent across most branches. **We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **60 branches** continued with the old procedure as a control group
- Analysis compares changes in switched branches against changes in control branches (difference-in-differences method)

### Impact on Service Scores

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|---------------|---------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 50.65 | 52.54 | +1.89 |
| Control branches | 50.84 | 50.51 | -0.34 |
| **Net effect** | | | **+2.23** |

- The **2.23-point improvement** is statistically significant (p < 0.01)
- 95% confidence interval: **1.4 to 3.0 points**
- This represents a **4.4% improvement** relative to the average service score of 51.2

### Consistency and Reliability
- **70% of switched branches** saw their service scores improve after adoption
- The effect holds after accounting for branch-specific characteristics, week-to-week variation, staffing levels, and visit volumes
- **Parallel trends verified**: Before the switch, both groups followed similar trajectories, supporting the validity of the comparison
- Neither staffing nor customer visits changed differently between groups—the procedure itself drove the improvement

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all 150 branches

2. **Monitor implementation** to ensure branches adopt the procedure as intended

3. **Track service scores** post-rollout to confirm the effect persists at scale

4. **Investigate variation**: About 30% of switched branches saw little change or decline. Understanding what differentiates these branches could help maximize benefits during rollout

5. **Maintain adequate staffing**: Since staffing levels positively affect service scores, the new procedure will work best alongside appropriate staffing

---

*Analysis based on weekly service score data from weeks 6–22. The switch occurred at week 15. Statistical methods include difference-in-differences regression with branch and week fixed effects.*
