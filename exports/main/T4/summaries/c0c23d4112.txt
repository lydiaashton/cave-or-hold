# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact Analysis**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new shelving and rotation procedure improved service scores—an unexpected but welcome finding.** While the change was expected to have no effect on service quality, branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.1 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant. We recommend proceeding with the system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **150 branches** analyzed over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure (treatment group)
- **60 branches** kept the old procedure (control group)
- Switch implemented at **week 15**

### Impact on Service Scores

| Group | Before Switch (Weeks 6–14) | After Switch (Weeks 15–22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------------|---------------------------|--------|
| Control (Old Procedure) | 47.7 | 48.2 | +0.5 |
| Treatment (New Procedure) | 49.1 | 51.7 | +2.6 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+2.1** |

The **2.1-point improvement** is the estimated effect of the new procedure after accounting for normal week-to-week variation.

### Statistical Confidence
- **Highly significant** (p < 0.01)
- **95% confidence interval**: 0.9 to 3.2 points
- Result holds after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume
- **Pre-trends validated**: Both groups showed similar patterns before the switch, supporting the validity of the comparison

### What This Means
- Service scores average around 50 points across the system
- A 2.1-point gain represents approximately **4% improvement** in service quality
- **This was an unexpected benefit**—the procedure change was not designed to improve service scores, yet it did

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** to capture the unexpected service quality gains
2. **Investigate the mechanism**—consider qualitative follow-up with branch staff to understand *why* the new procedure improved scores (e.g., faster item location, cleaner shelves, staff confidence)
3. **Monitor implementation** for 8–12 weeks after each branch switches to confirm expected gains are realized
4. **Document and share best practices** from the 90 early-adopter branches to support training
5. **Continue tracking** service scores to ensure the effect persists over time

---

## **Technical Notes**

Analysis used a **difference-in-differences** approach, the standard method for evaluating policy changes when some units adopt a treatment and others do not. Standard errors are clustered at the branch level. A visualization of the trends is attached showing the divergence between treatment and control groups after week 15.
