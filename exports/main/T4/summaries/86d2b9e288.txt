# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure shows a **positive but statistically inconclusive effect** on service scores. Branches that switched saw service scores increase by approximately **2.1 points** (about 4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. However, this result is not statistically significant at conventional levels, meaning we cannot confidently rule out that the observed improvement was due to random variation. 

**Recommendation: Continue monitoring before making a system-wide rollout decision.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** total: 20 adopted the new procedure, 14 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22), with the switch implemented at week 15
- Analysis compares changes in treatment branches to changes in control branches

### **Results Summary**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect on service score | **+2.1 points** |
| Statistical significance | **Not significant** (p ≈ 0.12) |
| 95% Confidence interval | **-0.5 to +4.8 points** |
| Effect size (if real) | **~4% improvement** |

### **What the Data Shows**

1. **Treatment branches had higher baseline scores** (53.8 vs. 46.5 before the switch). Our analysis accounts for this difference.

2. **After the switch**, treatment branches improved by 1.3 points while control branches declined by 0.8 points.

3. **The confidence interval is wide**: The true effect could plausibly be zero, slightly negative, or as high as 5 points. With only 8 post-switch weeks, the study has limited statistical power.

4. **Pre-switch trends were parallel**: The gap between groups was stable before week 15, supporting the validity of the comparison.

5. **Staffing and visit patterns** did not differ systematically between groups and do not explain the results.

---

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended Actions**

1. **Extend the pilot by 8–12 weeks** to gather more post-switch data and improve statistical precision.

2. **Verify implementation quality** at switched branches to ensure the new procedure is being followed as designed.

3. **Consider expanding the pilot** to additional branches if operationally feasible.

4. **Track additional outcomes** (staff time, patron satisfaction, inventory accuracy) that may inform the cost-benefit case.

5. **Prepare for a phased rollout** if extended monitoring continues to show positive effects.

### **Decision Framework**

| If Extended Data Shows... | Then... |
|---------------------------|---------|
| Statistically significant positive effect | Proceed with system-wide rollout |
| Positive but still inconclusive effect | Weigh implementation costs against potential benefits; consider phased adoption |
| No effect or negative effect | Revisit procedure design before broader adoption |

---

## **Technical Notes**

- Analysis uses difference-in-differences regression with branch and week fixed effects
- Standard errors are clustered at the branch level
- Service scores average ~50 across all branches; higher is better
- All 34 branches have complete data for all 17 weeks
