# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2 points** (about 4%) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and remained consistent over the 8-week observation period. **We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

## **Key Findings**

| Finding | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Impact on Service Scores** | +1.99 points average improvement (95% CI: +0.76 to +3.21) |
| **Statistical Confidence** | p = 0.002 (less than 1% chance of random occurrence) |
| **Relative Improvement** | ~4% increase from baseline average of 54 points |
| **Durability** | Effect held steady: +2.12 (weeks 15-18) and +1.85 (weeks 19-22) |
| **Study Scope** | 150 branches total: 90 switched, 60 control; 17 weeks of data |

### Additional Context

- **Parallel trends confirmed:** Before the switch, both groups showed similar service score trajectories, validating the comparison.
- **Protected against decline:** All branches experienced a system-wide score drop after week 14. Switched branches declined only 1.1 points versus 3.1 points for control branches—the new procedure appears to have buffered this downturn.
- **Robust to controls:** The result holds after accounting for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** to the remaining 60 branches

2. **Monitor implementation** over the next 8-12 weeks to confirm effects persist at scale

3. **Document best practices** from the 90 early-adopter branches to support training

4. **Conduct cost-benefit review** if the new procedure requires additional resources—the ~4% service improvement should be weighed against any incremental costs
