## **New Shelving Procedure: Impact on Library Service Scores**

### **Bottom Line**

**Contrary to expectations, the new procedure improved service scores.** While the change was not anticipated to affect service quality, branches that switched saw scores increase by an average of **2.1 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant. Given the positive outcome and no apparent downside, we recommend proceeding with system-wide rollout while continuing to monitor results.

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. Unexpected Positive Effect**
- Treatment branches (90 branches that switched) improved by **3.7 points** after the switch
- Control branches (60 branches that kept the old procedure) improved by only **1.5 points** during the same period
- The **net effect of the new procedure is +2.1 points** (95% confidence interval: 0.9 to 3.4 points)
- This result is highly statistically significant (p < 0.001)

**2. Why We Can Trust This Result**
- **Parallel pre-trends:** Before the switch (weeks 6–14), both groups moved in lockstep—the gap between them stayed stable at about 2 points
- **Clean break at week 15:** The divergence began precisely when the switch occurred, not before
- **No confounding factors:** Staffing levels and visitor volume did not change differentially between groups, ruling these out as alternative explanations

**3. Practical Significance**
- On a scale where the average branch scores about 50, a 2-point gain represents approximately a **4% improvement** in service quality
- The procedure change required no additional resources—this is a pure efficiency gain

**4. Important Caveat**
- This finding was unexpected. The mechanism by which shelving and rotation procedures affect service scores is not yet understood
- Further investigation may reveal *why* this works, which could help optimize the procedure further

---

### **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** to all 150 branches—the data supports adoption, and there is no evidence of harm

2. **Investigate the mechanism:** Work with branch staff to understand *why* the new procedure improves scores (e.g., faster item location, better organization, reduced patron wait times)

3. **Monitor post-rollout:** Track service scores for 6–12 months after full implementation to confirm the effect holds at scale and does not diminish over time

4. **Document lessons learned:** Capture the unexpected positive outcome in cooperative records to inform future operational changes—some procedural adjustments may have unanticipated benefits

5. **Consider a pilot debrief:** Interview staff at switched branches to gather qualitative insights about what changed in their daily operations

---

*Analysis based on 2,550 weekly observations from 150 branches over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22). The switch occurred at week 15. Statistical method: difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors.*
