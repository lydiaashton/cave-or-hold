# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Library Cooperative Board  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving procedure is associated with a **2-point improvement** in service scores. However, this effect is **not statistically significant**—the data are consistent with both a modest benefit and no effect. We recommend **continuing the pilot and collecting more data** before committing to a system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Overview**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **17 branches** adopted the new procedure at week 15; **17 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Analysis compares changes in switching branches against changes in non-switching branches

### **Service Score Results**

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|----------------|---------------|--------|
| Kept Old Procedure | 47.2 | 48.9 | +1.7 |
| Switched to New Procedure | 51.4 | 55.1 | +3.8 |
| **Net Effect of New Procedure** | | | **+2.0** |

### **Statistical Confidence**
- **Estimated effect:** +2.0 points on the service score index
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.12 (not significant at the 5% level)
- **95% confidence interval:** -0.6 to +4.6 points

**What this means:** We cannot confidently rule out that the new procedure has no effect. However, we also cannot rule out a meaningful improvement of up to 4.6 points (roughly 10% of the average score).

### **Important Context**
- Branches that switched already had higher service scores than non-switching branches before the change (51.4 vs. 47.2). This suggests the groups differ in ways beyond the procedure.
- The service score gap between the two groups widened after the switch, but the pattern varies week-to-week.
- Higher staffing levels are associated with higher service scores across all branches.

---

## **Recommendations**

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| **1** | **Continue the pilot for 8–12 more weeks** | More data will clarify whether the 2-point effect is real or noise |
| **2** | **Do not mandate system-wide rollout yet** | Evidence is inconclusive; premature rollout risks adopting an ineffective change |
| **3** | **Verify implementation quality** | Ensure switching branches are following the new procedure correctly |
| **4** | **Consider randomized assignment** for remaining branches | Would strengthen future analysis and support clearer decisions |
| **5** | **Track additional outcomes** (staff time, patron feedback) | Service scores may not capture all relevant impacts |

---

## **Decision Framework**

| If future analysis shows... | Then... |
|-----------------------------|---------|
| Effect remains ~2 points and becomes statistically significant | Proceed with phased rollout |
| Effect grows to 3+ points | Strong case for full rollout |
| Effect shrinks toward zero or becomes negative | Reconsider or revise the procedure |
| Effect remains uncertain after extended data collection | Conduct cost-benefit analysis before deciding |

---

## **Technical Appendix**

- **Method:** Difference-in-differences regression with branch and week fixed effects
- **Standard errors:** Clustered at the branch level to account for repeated observations
- **Controls:** Staffing index and visits index (neither substantially alters the main result)
- **Data quality:** Complete records for all 34 branches across all 17 weeks; no missing values
