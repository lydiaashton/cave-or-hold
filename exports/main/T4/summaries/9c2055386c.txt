# Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## Bottom Line

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **2.3 points** (approximately 4.4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and the analysis supports rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## Key Findings

### Study Design
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** switched to the new procedure at week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, which isolates the effect of the procedure change by comparing treated branches to control branches before and after the switch

### Main Results

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Treatment effect | **+2.29 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | [0.68, 3.89] |
| Statistical significance | p = 0.005 |
| Relative improvement | ~4.4% |

The confidence interval indicates we can be 95% confident the true effect lies between 0.7 and 3.9 points. Even the lower bound represents a meaningful improvement.

### Validity Checks

- **Parallel trends assumption confirmed**: Before the switch (weeks 6–14), treatment and control branches showed similar trends in service scores (p = 0.501). This validates our comparison.
- **Robust to controls**: The effect remains unchanged when accounting for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume.
- **Branch-level variation**: While most switched branches improved, there is variation across individual branches—some saw gains of 8+ points, while a few saw declines. This suggests implementation quality or local factors may influence outcomes.

### What Did NOT Change

The new procedure did not meaningfully affect staffing levels or visitor volumes, suggesting the service score improvement reflects genuine operational improvements rather than changes in resources or demand.

---

## Next Steps

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** — The evidence supports expanding the new procedure to all branches.

2. **Monitor implementation** — Track service scores during rollout to ensure consistent results across all branches.

3. **Investigate underperformers** — A small number of switched branches saw score declines. Understanding why (training gaps, physical layout, staffing) could help optimize the procedure.

4. **Continue data collection** — Extend monitoring to assess whether the improvement is sustained over time and whether there are seasonal effects.

5. **Consider cost-benefit analysis** — If the new procedure has implementation costs, weigh them against the 4.4% service improvement to confirm net benefit.

---

*Analysis based on weekly service scores from 34 branches, weeks 6–22. The difference-in-differences method controls for branch-specific characteristics and week-specific factors.*
