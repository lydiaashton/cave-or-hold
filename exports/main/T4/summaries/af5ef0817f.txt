# **Memorandum to the Board of Directors**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Office  
**Date:** [Current Date]  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Rotation Procedure  

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and rotation procedure shows a **positive effect on service scores**, but the evidence is not statistically definitive. Branches that adopted the new procedure improved by an estimated **2.4 points** (approximately 5% of the average score) compared to branches that retained the old procedure. While this result is promising, the limited sample size means we cannot rule out chance as an explanation. 

**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **phased rollout** while continuing to monitor outcomes. The potential benefit outweighs the risk, but ongoing evaluation is essential.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Impact Estimate**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated treatment effect | **+2.4 points** |
| 95% confidence interval | **-0.6 to +5.4 points** |
| Statistical significance | **p = 0.12** (not significant at 0.05 level) |
| Effect size | **5% of average score** (0.24 standard deviations) |

### **2. What Changed**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| 20 branches that switched | 46.0 | 47.1 | +1.1 |
| 14 branches that did not switch | 51.4 | 50.1 | -1.3 |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | | **+2.4** |

Branches using the new procedure improved while those using the old procedure declined slightly, yielding a net positive effect.

### **3. Analysis Validity**

✓ **Parallel trends assumption supported:** Pre-switch trends showed no significant difference between groups (p = 0.39), indicating the comparison is valid.

✓ **No evidence of harm:** The lower bound of the confidence interval (-0.6) suggests the procedure is unlikely to have a meaningful negative effect.

⚠️ **Limited precision:** With only 34 branches, the study cannot detect small effects with high confidence.

### **4. Study Scope**

- **34 branches total** (20 adopted new procedure, 14 retained old)
- **17 weeks of data** (Weeks 6-22; switch occurred at Week 15)
- **578 branch-week observations**

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended Action: Phased Rollout with Monitoring**

| Action | Timeline | Responsibility |
|--------|----------|----------------|
| **1. Continue rollout** to remaining branches in waves | Immediate | Operations |
| **2. Track service scores** monthly for all branches | Ongoing | Data Office |
| **3. Collect staff feedback** on implementation | Months 1-3 | HR/Operations |
| **4. Formal re-evaluation** with expanded data | Month 6 | Data Office |
| **5. Board review** of full-year results | Month 12 | Board/Data Office |

### **Success Metrics**

Continue monitoring:
- **Primary:** Service score index (target: sustained improvement)
- **Secondary:** Staff workload, patron satisfaction, operational costs

### **Decision Triggers**

| Scenario | Recommended Action |
|----------|-------------------|
| Effect remains positive and grows more precise | Accelerate full implementation |
| Effect diminishes or becomes negative | Pause and investigate causes |
| Effect stable at current level | Complete rollout as planned |

---

## **Conclusion**

The new procedure demonstrates a **meaningful positive effect** on service quality with **no evidence of adverse impact**. While statistical certainty is limited by sample size, the direction and magnitude of the effect support moving forward. A **cautious, monitored rollout** allows the cooperative to capture potential benefits while maintaining the flexibility to adjust based on emerging evidence.

**We recommend approval to proceed with phased implementation.**
