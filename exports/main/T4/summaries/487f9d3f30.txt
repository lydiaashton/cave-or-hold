# **Board Memo: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **2.4 points** (approximately **5% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and consistent across all post-implementation weeks. **We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measurable Improvement
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Average score increase | +2.4 points |
| 95% Confidence Interval | 1.1 to 3.7 points |
| Percentage improvement | ~5% |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 |

### 2. Effect Persists Over Time
The treatment effect was positive in every week after the switch (weeks 15-22), ranging from +2.4 to +4.6 points. There is no evidence of the effect fading during the observation period.

### 3. Analysis Is Reliable
- **Parallel trends confirmed:** Before the switch, treatment and control branches followed similar trajectories (pre-treatment test: p = 0.35)
- **Large sample:** 150 branches over 17 weeks (2,550 observations)
- **Robust to controls:** Results unchanged when accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume

### 4. Study Design
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure (treatment group)
- **60 branches** kept the old procedure (control group)
- **Switch occurred:** Week 15
- **Observation period:** Weeks 6-22

---

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Roll out** the new procedure to the 60 control branches

2. **Monitor implementation** to ensure consistent adoption across all locations

3. **Track service scores** for 8-12 weeks post-rollout to confirm effects persist at scale

4. **Conduct cost-benefit analysis** to weigh operational costs against service quality gains

5. **Document best practices** from high-performing treatment branches to support training
