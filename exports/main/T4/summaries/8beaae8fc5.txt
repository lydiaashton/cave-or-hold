# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure  
**Date:** Analysis of weeks 6–22, 709 branches  
**Recommendation:** Proceed with system-wide rollout

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure **delivered the expected improvement** in service scores. Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure achieved service scores **2.0 points higher** than branches that retained the old procedure. This confirms our hypothesis and supports immediate rollout to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

| Finding | Result |
|---------|--------|
| **Treatment Effect** | +2.03 points (95% CI: 1.31 to 2.75) |
| **Statistical Confidence** | p < 0.001 (highly significant) |
| **Relative Improvement** | ~4% over baseline (~50 points) |
| **Branches Analyzed** | 709 total (425 switched, 284 control) |
| **Observation Period** | 17 weeks (9 before switch, 8 after) |

**What This Means:** The 2-point gain is meaningful in the context of our service score scale. For comparison, this improvement is roughly equivalent to the difference between a "good" and "excellent" rated branch.

**Branch-Level Impact:**
- **59.5%** of switched branches showed score improvement vs. only **35.6%** of control branches
- Switched branches averaged **+0.88 points** while control branches averaged **-1.15 points** during the same period

**Effect Strengthens Over Time:**
The gap between switched and non-switched branches grew from 1.9 points in the first post-switch week to 3.6 points by week 22, suggesting benefits accumulate as staff gain familiarity with the new procedure.

---

## **Why We're Confident in These Results**

1. **Clean identification:** The switch occurred at a single point in time (week 15) across treated branches, creating a natural experiment
2. **Control group validates the finding:** Non-switched branches provide a counterfactual—without the procedure change, scores would have followed their declining trend
3. **Effect persists after controls:** The result holds even accounting for staffing levels and branch traffic
4. **Consistent with expectations:** The positive direction and magnitude align with what operations staff anticipated based on pilot feedback

---

## **Next Steps**

| Action | Timeline | Owner |
|--------|----------|-------|
| **Approve full rollout** | Board meeting | Board |
| **Develop implementation plan** | 2 weeks | Operations |
| **Begin phased rollout** | Weeks 1–8 post-approval | Regional Managers |
| **Monitor service scores** | Ongoing | Analytics Team |
| **Post-rollout evaluation** | 90 days after completion | Analytics Team |

**Rollout Considerations:**
- Approximately 40% of switched branches did not see improvement; targeted support should be available for similar branches during rollout
- Training materials should capture practices from the top-performing switched branches
- Consider prioritizing lowest-scoring branches for early adoption to maximize system-wide benefit

---

## **Conclusion**

The analysis confirms what operations leadership expected: the new stock rotation and shelving procedure improves service quality. With a statistically significant 2-point gain and evidence of growing benefits over time, we recommend proceeding with confidence to implement the new procedure across all 709 branches.
