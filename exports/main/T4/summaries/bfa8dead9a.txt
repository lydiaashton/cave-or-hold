# **Library Cooperative Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new shelving and stock rotation procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **2.1 points** (approximately **4% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and robust across multiple analytical approaches.

**Recommendation: Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure; **60 branches** continued with the old procedure
- The switch occurred at **week 15**, creating a natural experiment
- Analysis compares changes in treated branches to changes in control branches (difference-in-differences)

### **Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 51.27 | 49.65 | −1.62 |
| Treated (new procedure) | 50.52 | 51.03 | +0.51 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+2.13** |

### **Statistical Evidence**
- **Treatment effect: +2.13 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.01 to 3.25)
- **P-value < 0.001** (highly statistically significant)
- Results hold when controlling for staffing levels, visit volumes, and branch-specific factors

### **Practical Impact**
- Represents a **4.2% improvement** in service scores
- Effect size of **0.21 standard deviations** (small-to-moderate practical impact)
- Meaningful given that service scores average around 50 across the cooperative

### **Why We Trust These Results**
- **Parallel trends confirmed:** Before the switch, both groups followed similar trajectories (p = 0.20)
- **No pre-existing differences:** Treated and control branches had similar service score levels before the intervention (p = 0.58)
- **Consistent effects:** The positive impact appears in most weeks after the switch, strongest in weeks 15–17

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches
2. **Develop implementation timeline** with branch managers
3. **Monitor service scores** for 8–12 weeks post-rollout to confirm effects persist at scale
4. **Document procedures** for training and quality assurance
5. **Investigate mechanisms** (e.g., faster retrieval, better organization) to optimize future improvements

---

## **Technical Notes**

- Analysis uses clustered standard errors at the branch level to account for repeated observations
- Results are robust to inclusion of covariates and branch fixed effects
- Service scores are an internally calculated index where higher values indicate better service
