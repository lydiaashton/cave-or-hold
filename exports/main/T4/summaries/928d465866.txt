# **Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure Evaluation**

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure is associated with a **2.6-point improvement in service scores** (approximately 5% on a scale where the average branch scores about 50). The evidence is **promising but not conclusive**—the result is marginally statistically significant (p=0.09). We recommend proceeding with a **phased rollout** while continuing to monitor results.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15
- **14 branches** maintained the old procedure (serving as a control group)
- Data covers **17 weeks** (weeks 6-22) with 578 total observations
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach to isolate the procedure's effect from other factors

### **Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Control branches (old procedure) | 50.1 | 51.1 | +0.9 |
| Switched branches (new procedure) | 48.6 | 52.1 | +3.5 |
| **Net Effect of New Procedure** | | | **+2.6** |

### **Statistical Confidence**
- **Estimated effect:** +2.62 points
- **95% Confidence Interval:** -0.4 to +5.7 points
- **Interpretation:** We are 95% confident the true effect lies between a negligible decline and a meaningful 5.7-point gain. The most likely value is around +2.6 points.

### **Additional Insights**
- The improvement **is not driven by changes in staffing or visitor volume**—the effect holds after controlling for these factors
- Branches that switched started with **lower baseline scores** but showed **stronger improvement**, suggesting the new procedure may particularly benefit struggling branches
- Visual inspection of weekly trends shows the treatment group's scores rising more steeply after week 15 compared to controls

---

## **Recommendations**

### **1. Proceed with Phased Rollout**
Given the positive point estimate and the relatively low risk of implementation, we recommend expanding the new procedure to additional branches, prioritizing those with currently lower service scores.

### **2. Extend the Evaluation Period**
Eight weeks post-switch may be insufficient to capture the full effect. Continue collecting data for another 8-12 weeks to strengthen statistical confidence.

### **3. Document Implementation Practices**
Capture variations in how branches implement the new procedure to identify best practices that may amplify the positive effect.

### **4. Set a Decision Checkpoint**
Reconvene at **week 30-34** to review updated results. If the effect remains positive and the confidence interval no longer includes zero, proceed with full cooperative-wide adoption.

### **5. Conduct Cost-Benefit Analysis**
Once full rollout costs are quantified, weigh the ~5% service improvement against implementation expenses to confirm the business case.
