# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** Impact of New Branch Shelving and Rotation Procedure on Service Scores

**Date:** November 2026

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Despite the initial expectation that the change would make no difference, branches that adopted the new procedure experienced a statistically significant increase in service scores of approximately **2 points** (about 4% improvement). The evidence supports rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### The New Procedure Made a Positive Difference

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Estimated effect** | **+1.97 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | [0.76, 3.18] |
| Statistical significance | p = 0.0015 |
| Baseline average score | ~51 points |
| **Percentage improvement** | **~3.9%** |

### How We Know This

The analysis compared 102 branches that switched to the new procedure against 48 branches that kept the old procedure, tracking both groups before and after the switch (weeks 6–22). This "difference-in-differences" approach isolates the effect of the procedure change from other factors.

**Service Score Changes:**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 51.65 | 53.48 | +1.83 |
| Non-switched branches | 50.23 | 50.09 | -0.14 |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | | **+1.97** |

### The Evidence Is Reliable

- **No pre-existing trends:** Before the switch, both groups followed parallel trajectories (p = 0.96), confirming the comparison is valid.
- **Effect persisted:** Improvement was observed in both early weeks (15–17) and later weeks (18–22) after implementation.
- **Robust finding:** Results hold even when excluding the first week after the switch.

---

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with system-wide rollout of the new procedure.** The data demonstrate a clear, positive impact on service quality that was not anticipated. While the effect is modest in percentage terms (~4%), it is consistent, statistically significant, and comes at no apparent cost to service delivery.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve full rollout** to remaining branches.

2. **Monitor during implementation** to ensure consistent results across all locations.

3. **Document implementation practices** from high-performing branches to support training.

4. **Continue tracking service scores** post-rollout to confirm sustained impact.

---

*Analysis based on 2,550 branch-week observations from 150 branches over 17 weeks. Statistical models control for branch-specific and week-specific factors.*
