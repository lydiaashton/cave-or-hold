# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure maintained their service quality while branches using the old procedure experienced a notable decline. The evidence supports proceeding with a system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **The Numbers**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| **New Procedure (90 branches)** | 50.54 | 50.37 | -0.17 |
| **Old Procedure (60 branches)** | 49.85 | 47.79 | -2.06 |

**Net benefit of new procedure: +1.9 points** on the service score index (statistically significant, p < 0.01)

### **What This Means**

- Service scores across the cooperative declined after week 15, likely due to seasonal or operational factors affecting all branches
- Branches using the **new procedure were protected** from this decline, maintaining stable scores
- Branches using the **old procedure dropped nearly 2 points**, a meaningful decrease in service quality
- The new procedure appears to provide **operational resilience**, not just incremental improvement

### **Confidence in the Result**

- Analysis covers **150 branches** over **17 weeks** (2,550 observations)
- Statistical methods account for branch-specific characteristics and week-to-week variation
- Result is robust after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volumes
- Pre-switch trends between groups were similar, supporting the validity of the comparison

---

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with system-wide rollout of the new procedure.** The evidence indicates the new rotation and shelving method helps branches maintain service quality during challenging periods.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve rollout** to remaining 60 branches currently using the old procedure

2. **Develop implementation timeline** with training and support for transitioning branches

3. **Monitor outcomes** for one quarter post-rollout to confirm effects persist at scale

4. **Investigate the system-wide decline** observed in weeks 15–22 to identify any additional improvements

5. **Schedule follow-up review** in 6 months to assess long-term impact

---

## **Questions?**

The analysis team is available to present findings in person and address any questions about methodology or interpretation.
