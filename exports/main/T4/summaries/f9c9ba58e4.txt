# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Analysis Period:** Weeks 6–22 (17 weeks of data from 150 branches)

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure improved service scores and should be rolled out system-wide.**

Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure achieved service scores approximately **2 points higher** (about 4% improvement) than branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and represents a meaningful gain in service quality.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **Study Overview**
- **150 branches** tracked weekly from week 6 through week 22
- **102 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **48 branches** retained the old procedure as a control group
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** design—the gold standard for evaluating policy changes with treatment and control groups

### **Results Summary**

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|---------------|---------------|--------|
| New Procedure | 53.07 | 52.57 | −0.50 |
| Old Procedure | 51.58 | 49.08 | −2.50 |

**Net Effect of New Procedure: +2.03 points (p < 0.01)**

### **Interpretation**
Service scores declined system-wide after week 15, likely due to seasonal factors. However, branches with the new procedure experienced a much smaller decline. The new procedure effectively **protected service quality**, delivering a 2-point advantage relative to the old procedure.

### **Analytical Rigor**
✓ **Parallel trends verified**: Treatment and control groups followed similar trajectories before the switch (p = 0.58)  
✓ **Robust to controls**: Effect persists when accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume  
✓ **Statistically significant**: Result holds with cluster-robust standard errors at the branch level (p = 0.002)  
✓ **Practically meaningful**: ~4% improvement on a metric where the average branch scores about 50

---

## **RECOMMENDATION**

**Proceed with system-wide rollout of the new procedure to all remaining branches.**

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

1. **Approve rollout plan** with timeline for implementing the new procedure at all 48 remaining branches

2. **Develop implementation guide** documenting transition practices from early-adopter branches

3. **Monitor during rollout** by tracking service scores to ensure consistent results across all branch types and sizes

4. **Schedule follow-up review** in 6 months to confirm sustained improvement and identify any branches requiring additional support

5. **Consider timing** for future procedural changes—implementing before high-traffic periods may maximize benefits
