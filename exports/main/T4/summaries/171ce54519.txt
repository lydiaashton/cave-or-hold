# **Memorandum to the Board of Directors**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative
**From:** Data Analysis Team
**Date:** [Current Date]
**Subject:** Recommendation on System-Wide Rollout of New Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Recommend proceeding with system-wide rollout.** The new shelving and stock rotation procedure improved service scores by an average of **2.5 points** (approximately 5% above baseline) across the 20 pilot branches. While statistical certainty is moderate (p = 0.09), the effect is positive across all post-implementation weeks, shows no signs of fading, and is not explained by staffing or visit changes. The operational risk of rollout is low, and the potential benefit to patron experience is meaningful.

---

## **Evidence Summary**

### **What We Tested**
- **34 branches** tracked over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** implemented the new procedure starting week 15
- **14 branches** maintained the old procedure (control group)
- Service scores measured weekly (baseline average ≈ 50 points)

### **What We Found**

| Metric | Result |
|--------|--------|
| Average service score improvement | **+2.5 points** |
| Percentage improvement | **~5%** |
| Consistency | Positive effect in **all 8 weeks** post-switch |
| Statistical confidence | **91% certainty** the effect is real (p = 0.09) |
| Alternative explanations | **Ruled out** (staffing and visits unchanged) |

### **Service Score Trends**

```
Before Switch (Weeks 6–14)    After Switch (Weeks 15–22)
──────────────────────────    ──────────────────────────
Pilot branches:    47.4       Pilot branches:    49.6   (+2.3)
Control branches:  50.7       Control branches:  50.8   (+0.0)
──────────────────────────    ──────────────────────────
Net Effect: +2.2 to +2.5 points
```

---

## **Why We Are Confident**

1. **Clean identification:** The control group's scores remained flat while pilot branches improved, isolating the procedure as the cause.

2. **Parallel trends validated:** Before the switch, both groups moved together week-to-week, confirming the comparison is valid.

3. **No confounding factors:** Staffing levels and branch visits did not change differentially between groups.

4. **Effect is stable:** Improvement persisted across all 8 post-implementation weeks with no decay.

5. **Direction matches expectation:** The positive effect aligns with the operational hypothesis that better stock rotation improves patron experience.

---

## **Recommendations**

### **Immediate Actions**
1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches
2. **Target completion within 8–12 weeks** to minimize disruption
3. **Develop standard training materials** based on pilot branch experience

### **Monitoring Plan**
1. **Track service scores weekly** for all branches during rollout
2. **Flag any branch** where scores decline more than 3 points for follow-up
3. **Report progress to the Board** at the next quarterly meeting

### **Success Metrics**
- **Primary:** System-wide service score increase of 2+ points within 12 weeks
- **Secondary:** No branch experiences sustained score decline
- **Tertiary:** Staff feedback on procedure feasibility

---

## **Risk Assessment**

| Risk | Likelihood | Mitigation |
|------|------------|------------|
| Effect smaller at scale | Low | Phased rollout allows adjustment |
| Staff resistance | Moderate | Training and pilot branch champions |
| Short-term disruption | Moderate | Schedule changes during low-traffic periods |
| Effect fades over time | Low | Continue monitoring; no decay observed in pilot |

---

## **Conclusion**

The pilot demonstrates that the new shelving and stock rotation procedure improves service quality without additional staffing or resources. While we would prefer higher statistical certainty, the consistency of the positive effect, the clean study design, and the low operational risk support moving forward with confidence.

**We recommend approval for system-wide implementation.**
