# **Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure Evaluation**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores—contrary to our expectations.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **1.9 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents approximately a **4% improvement** on the service score index. The finding is statistically robust and unlikely to be due to chance. We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Unexpected but Measurable Improvement
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect | **+1.9 points** on service score |
| 95% Confidence Interval | 0.7 to 3.1 points |
| Statistical Significance | p = 0.002 (highly significant) |
| Relative Improvement | ~4% above baseline |

This result was unexpected—the procedure change was not anticipated to affect service scores. However, the data show a clear, positive effect.

### 2. Strong Research Design Supports Causal Interpretation

The analysis leveraged a natural experiment:
- **90 branches** switched to the new procedure in week 15
- **60 branches** continued with the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** per branch (weeks 6–22)

**Parallel Trends Confirmed:** Before the switch, both groups had nearly identical service score trajectories (treatment: 47.13, control: 47.05). This validates the comparison.

**After the Switch:** The treatment group pulled ahead by 1.9 points relative to the control group.

### 3. Effect Holds After Controlling for Confounders

The improvement persists after accounting for:
- Weekly staffing levels
- Visitor volume
- Branch-specific characteristics

This indicates the procedure change itself—not external factors—drove the improvement.

### 4. Most Branches Benefited, But Not All

| Outcome | Treatment Branches | Control Branches |
|---------|-------------------|------------------|
| Improved after period | 79% | 60% |
| Declined after period | 21% | 40% |
| Improvement >2 points | 56% | 35% |

While the average effect is positive, approximately **1 in 5 treatment branches** did not see improvement. This suggests implementation quality or local conditions may moderate the effect.

---

## **Recommendations**

### 1. Approve System-Wide Rollout
The evidence supports expanding the new procedure to all branches. Expected average improvement: **~1.9 points** on the service score index.

### 2. Investigate Variation in Outcomes
Since 21% of early-adopter branches did not benefit, we recommend:
- Reviewing implementation fidelity across branches
- Identifying characteristics of branches that saw little or no improvement
- Developing targeted support for branches that may struggle with adoption

### 3. Establish Ongoing Monitoring
- Track service scores quarterly post-rollout
- Flag branches that underperform for additional support
- Re-evaluate the effect after 6–12 months to confirm persistence

### 4. Document Lessons Learned
This evaluation demonstrates that operational changes can have unintended positive effects on service quality. Consider applying similar rigorous evaluation methods to future procedure changes.
