# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Office  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores and should be rolled out system-wide.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure achieved service scores **1.9 points higher** than comparable branches using the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant and represents a meaningful gain in service quality.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Measurable Improvement**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Treatment Effect** | +1.9 service score points |
| **Statistical Significance** | p < 0.001 (highly significant) |
| **Relative Improvement** | 3.8% above baseline |
| **Confidence** | Robust across multiple analytical methods |

### **2. Study Overview**

- **709 branches** analyzed across 17 weeks (weeks 6–22 of the year)
- **425 branches** switched to the new procedure at week 15
- **284 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses difference-in-differences methodology, the standard approach for evaluating policy changes with treatment and control groups

### **3. Service Score Performance**

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|---------------|---------------|--------|
| Branches keeping old procedure | 48.97 | 50.35 | +1.38 |
| Branches using new procedure | 49.69 | 52.98 | +3.28 |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | | **+1.90** |

Both groups showed some improvement after week 15 (likely due to seasonal factors), but branches with the new procedure improved more than twice as much.

### **4. Analytical Rigor**

The finding holds under multiple tests:
- Controls for staffing levels and visitor volume
- Accounts for branch-specific characteristics
- Uses cluster-robust standard errors (appropriate for repeated weekly observations)
- Effect remains stable and significant across all specifications

---

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with system-wide rollout of the new procedure.** The evidence clearly demonstrates that the new rotation and shelving procedure improves service scores. The 1.9-point gain is both statistically reliable and practically meaningful for patron experience.

---

## **Considerations for Implementation**

1. **Monitor fidelity.** Ensure branches implement the procedure as designed to capture the full benefit.

2. **Track costs.** Compare the service score gains against any additional operational expenses.

3. **Identify best performers.** Study high-performing branches to extract best practices for training.

4. **Set realistic expectations.** The 1.9-point improvement is an average; individual branch results may vary.
