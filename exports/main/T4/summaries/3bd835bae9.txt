# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** [Current Date]  
**RE:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Rotation Procedure  

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new shelving and rotation procedure did not demonstrably improve service scores.** Although branches using the new procedure showed larger score gains than those keeping the old procedure, this difference existed *before* the switch was implemented. After accounting for pre-existing trends, there is no statistically significant evidence that the new procedure caused any improvement. 

**Recommendation: Do not proceed with system-wide rollout at this time.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Overview**
| | |
|---|---|
| **Branches Analyzed** | 709 total |
| **Treatment Group** | 425 branches (adopted new procedure at week 15) |
| **Control Group** | 284 branches (kept old procedure) |
| **Study Period** | 17 weeks (weeks 6–22) |
| **Metric** | Service Score (average ~50; higher is better) |

### **What the Raw Data Shows**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 48.1 | 49.2 | +1.1 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 50.5 | 53.6 | +3.1 |
| **Apparent Advantage** | | | **+1.9 points** |

At first glance, treatment branches appear to have gained nearly 2 points more than control branches. **However, this comparison is misleading.**

### **The Critical Issue: Pre-Existing Differences**

Treatment branches were already outperforming and improving faster than control branches *before* the procedure change:

- Treatment branches started with **higher baseline scores** (50.5 vs. 48.1)
- Treatment branches were improving **0.35 points/week faster** even before the switch (p=0.0003)
- The performance gap grew steadily from **1.1 points** (week 6) to **3.7 points** (week 14) *prior to* the procedure change
- There was **no sudden jump** in the gap when the new procedure began at week 15

This pattern indicates that branches selected for the new procedure were already on a stronger improvement trajectory—likely due to factors unrelated to the procedure itself (e.g., management quality, staffing, community demographics).

### **Adjusted Analysis: What the Procedure Actually Contributed**

When we statistically account for the pre-existing trend differences:

| Statistic | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| **Estimated Treatment Effect** | **-0.6 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | [-1.99, +0.86] |
| **P-value** | **0.43** (not statistically significant) |

**Interpretation:** The data is consistent with three possibilities:
1. The new procedure has **no effect** on service scores
2. The new procedure has a **modest negative effect** (up to -2 points)
3. The new procedure has a **modest positive effect** (up to +0.9 points)

We cannot distinguish among these possibilities with the current data. Critically, **we cannot confirm the procedure improves scores.**

---

## **Recommendations**

### **1. Halt System-Wide Rollout**
The evidence does not support expanding the new procedure to all 709 branches. Rolling out an unproven procedure system-wide risks:
- Unnecessary disruption to branch operations
- Staff training costs without demonstrated benefit
- Potential negative impacts on service quality

### **2. Investigate Branch Selection Process**
The strong pre-existing differences between treatment and control groups suggest non-random selection. The board should request documentation on:
- What criteria determined which branches adopted the new procedure?
- Were higher-performing branches preferentially selected?
- Were there geographic, demographic, or operational factors at play?

### **3. Design a Rigorous Evaluation**
To properly assess the procedure's impact, we recommend:
- **Random assignment** of branches to treatment and control groups
- **Baseline measurement** to confirm parallel pre-trends
- **Adequate sample size** to detect meaningful effects
- **Longer observation period** (minimum 6 months post-implementation)

### **4. Continue Monitoring Pilot Branches**
The 425 treatment branches should continue to be tracked. While causal conclusions are limited, ongoing data may reveal:
- Longer-term effects not visible in this 8-week post-switch window
- Heterogeneous effects across different branch types
- Correlations with other operational metrics

### **5. Broaden Outcome Measures**
Service score is one indicator. Before making a final determination, consider:
- Staff time and efficiency metrics
- Patron satisfaction surveys
- Circulation statistics
- Cost per transaction

---

## **Conclusion**

The board's expectation that the new procedure would improve service scores is not supported by the available evidence. The apparent gains are attributable to pre-existing differences between branches, not the procedure itself. 

**A responsible decision requires better evidence.** We recommend pausing the rollout, investigating the selection process, and designing a proper randomized evaluation before committing cooperative-wide resources to this change.

---

*Analysis based on branches.csv data covering weeks 6–22, with procedure switch implemented at week 15. Statistical methods: difference-in-differences with trend controls.*
