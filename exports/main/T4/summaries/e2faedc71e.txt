# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores—contrary to our expectations.** Branches that adopted the new shelving and rotation procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **1.96 points (4.1% improvement)** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and consistent across the post-switch period. 

**Recommendation:** Proceed with system-wide rollout to all remaining branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measurable Improvement
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated improvement | +1.96 service score points |
| 95% Confidence Interval | 0.67 to 3.25 points |
| Relative gain | 4.1% above baseline |
| Statistical significance | p = 0.003 |

### 2. Study Overview
- **150 branches:** 90 adopted new procedure, 60 kept old procedure
- **17 weeks of data:** Weeks 6–22 (9 weeks before, 8 weeks after)
- **Switch occurred:** Week 15
- **Analysis method:** Difference-in-differences with branch and week controls

### 3. Why We Can Trust This Result

**Parallel trends confirmed:** Before Week 15, treatment and control branches moved together—no pre-existing divergence that could explain the result.

**Effect persisted:** The improvement was visible in every post-switch week, not driven by a single outlier week.

**Not explained by other factors:** The effect remains after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volumes. Neither of these changed significantly due to the procedure switch.

**Robust statistics:** Results hold using cluster-robust standard errors that account for branch-level variation.

### 4. What Changed
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Treatment branches | 47.83 | 50.14 | +2.31 |
| Control branches | 48.56 | 48.91 | +0.35 |
| **Net effect** | — | — | **+1.96** |

## **Why This Matters**

We entered this pilot expecting no measurable impact on service scores. The data show otherwise: a modest but real and statistically reliable improvement. Given that:
- The effect is **positive** (higher scores = better service)
- The effect is **significant** (unlikely to be random chance)
- The effect is **consistent** (observed across all post-switch weeks)
- The rollout cost appears **minimal** (procedure change only)

...the evidence supports expanding the new procedure across all branches.
