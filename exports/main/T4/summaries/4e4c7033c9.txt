# **Memorandum to the Board of Directors**

**To:** Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** [Current Date]  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure  

---

## **Executive Summary**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure **successfully improved service scores** across participating branches. Our analysis confirms the procedure should be rolled out system-wide.

**Recommendation: Approve full rollout to all branches.**

---

## **Bottom Line**

Branches that adopted the new procedure achieved service scores **1.9 points higher** than comparable branches using the old procedure. This represents a **4% improvement** and is statistically certain (p < 0.001). The improvement occurred without additional staffing or changes in visitor volume—the procedure itself drove the gain.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measurable Impact on Service Quality

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Treatment Effect | **+1.90 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | 1.55 to 2.25 points |
| Statistical Significance | p < 0.001 |
| Relative Improvement | ~4% vs. average score of 50 |

### 2. Performance by Group

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Branches with New Procedure | 49.69 | 52.98 | +3.28 |
| Branches with Old Procedure | 48.97 | 50.35 | +1.38 |
| **Net Effect of New Procedure** | | | **+1.90** |

### 3. What Drove the Improvement

The analysis tested whether the improvement came from increased staffing or higher visitor traffic. It did not:

- **Staffing levels:** No significant change (p = 0.78)
- **Visitor volume:** No significant change (p = 0.22)

**Conclusion:** The procedure itself—through better organization, faster item location, or improved shelf management—generated the service score gains.

### 4. Study Design Strength

This analysis used a rigorous **difference-in-differences** approach with:
- **709 branches** total (425 treatment, 284 control)
- **17 weeks** of data (weeks 6-22)
- **12,053 observations**
- Controls for branch-specific characteristics and week-to-week variations

Pre-switch trends confirmed that treatment and control branches were on similar trajectories before the intervention, validating the comparison.
