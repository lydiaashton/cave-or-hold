# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Service Score Impact Analysis**

**To:** Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2025  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and rotation procedure shows a **positive but statistically uncertain** effect on service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure improved by an estimated **1.75 points more** than branches that kept the old procedure. However, this result is not statistically significant at conventional levels (p ≈ 0.10), meaning we cannot confidently rule out that the observed difference occurred by chance.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **phased rollout** rather than immediate system-wide implementation, while continuing to collect data to strengthen the evidence base.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Impact Estimate

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Estimated effect** | +1.75 service score points |
| **Percentage improvement** | ~3.5% relative to baseline |
| **95% Confidence interval** | -0.3 to +3.8 points |
| **Statistical significance** | p ≈ 0.10 (not significant at 0.05 level) |

### 2. What Happened to Each Group

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| **Control** (kept old procedure) | 50.60 | 54.96 | +4.36 |
| **Treatment** (adopted new procedure) | 50.26 | 56.37 | +6.10 |

**Critical insight:** Both groups improved after week 15. The treatment group improved more, but roughly 70% of their gain reflects broader trends affecting all branches (e.g., seasonal patterns, system-wide initiatives). The **incremental benefit** attributable to the new procedure is the 1.75-point difference.

### 3. Study Validity

- ✅ **Parallel trends confirmed:** No evidence that treatment and control branches were on different trajectories before the switch (p = 0.87)
- ✅ **Adequate sample:** 34 branches (20 treatment, 14 control), 578 weekly observations
- ✅ **Robust methods:** Analysis controls for branch-specific characteristics, staffing levels, and visit volumes

### 4. Post-Switch Pattern

The treatment group outperformed control in 7 of 8 post-switch weeks, with the gap ranging from -0.8 to +4.0 points week-to-week. This consistency is encouraging despite the statistical uncertainty.

---

## **Interpretation**

The data suggest the new procedure **likely helps**, but we lack the statistical precision to be certain. Three scenarios are plausible:

1. **Real but modest benefit** (~1–2 points) — the procedure works as intended
2. **No true effect** — the observed difference reflects random variation
3. **Larger benefit** (~3–4 points) — we simply lack enough data to detect it confidently

Given that service scores are already improving system-wide, the question is whether the new procedure adds meaningful value beyond existing trends.

---

## **Next Steps**

### Immediate Actions (Next 30 Days)

1. **Continue data collection** for 8–12 additional weeks to increase statistical power
2. **Audit implementation fidelity** at switched branches to confirm the new procedure is being followed correctly
3. **Segment analysis** by branch characteristics (size, location, baseline performance) to identify where the procedure works best

### Rollout Decision Framework

| Scenario | Recommendation |
|----------|----------------|
| **Board is risk-averse** | Wait for more evidence before expanding |
| **Board wants to capture potential upside** | Roll out to 50% of remaining branches now |
| **Board wants maximum evidence quality** | Use stepped-wedge design (randomized phased rollout) |

### Recommended Path: Phased Rollout with Monitoring

1. **Months 1–2:** Switch 7–8 additional branches (randomly selected)
2. **Months 3–4:** Evaluate results; if effect remains positive, proceed with full rollout
3. **Ongoing:** Track service scores monthly to detect any decay or enhancement of effects

---

## **Bottom Line for Decision-Making**

| Consideration | Assessment |
|---------------|------------|
| **Does the procedure help?** | Probably yes, but not certain |
| **Is the benefit large?** | Modest (~3.5% improvement) |
| **Is there harm?** | No evidence of negative effects |
| **Should we roll out everywhere now?** | Not recommended — more evidence needed |
| **Should we abandon the procedure?** | No — results are promising |
