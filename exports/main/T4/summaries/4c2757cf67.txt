# **Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Recommend proceeding with rollout, but with measured expectations.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure showed improved service scores. Our best estimate is a **2-point gain** in service scores, though some uncertainty exists due to pre-existing differences between branches that switched and those that did not. The improvement appears genuine and grows over time, supporting a phased rollout with continued monitoring.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Service Scores Improved After Switch
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches (425) | 50.5 | 52.3 | +1.8 |
| Non-switched branches (284) | 49.6 | 49.2 | −0.3 |

The switched branches improved while non-switched branches declined slightly, yielding a **net difference of approximately 2 points**.

### 2. Statistical Confidence
- The 2-point effect is **statistically significant** (p < 0.001)
- 95% confidence interval: **1.6 to 2.7 points**
- This represents a **4% improvement** over the average service score of 50

### 3. Benefits Accumulate Over Time
The effect was not immediate but strengthened as branches gained experience:
- **Weeks 15-18:** ~1.5 points
- **Weeks 19-22:** ~2.8 points

This suggests staff need time to master the new procedure, or benefits compound as the system matures.

### 4. Important Limitation
Branches that volunteered to switch had **already begun outperforming** control branches in the weeks before the formal switch. This suggests early adopters may have been more motivated or better-resourced. While the post-switch improvement exceeds pre-existing trends, the true effect of the procedure alone may be somewhat smaller than our 2-point estimate.

---

## **Next Steps**

| Action | Rationale |
|--------|-----------|
| **1. Approve phased rollout** | Evidence supports the new procedure, but a phased approach allows for course correction |
| **2. Prioritize motivated branches** | Early adopters showed stronger results; start with branches eager to improve |
| **3. Provide 4-8 weeks of implementation support** | Effects grow over time; hands-on support during transition maximizes benefits |
| **4. Track scores for 8+ weeks post-switch** | Full benefits take time to materialize; avoid premature judgments |
| **5. Document implementation practices** | Capture what successful branches did differently to help others replicate results |
| **6. Re-evaluate after 6 months** | Assess whether effects persist and whether rollout should accelerate |

---

## **Data Summary**
- **709 branches** total (425 switched, 284 control)
- **17 weeks** of data (weeks 6-22; switch occurred at week 15)
- **12,053 observations** analyzed
- Method: Difference-in-differences regression with controls for staffing and visitor volume
