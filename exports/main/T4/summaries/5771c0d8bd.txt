# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

---

## **Subject: Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure achieved service scores **2.1 points higher** (approximately **4% improvement**) than branches that kept the old procedure. This result is statistically significant and has persisted throughout the observation period.

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout.** Contrary to the initial expectation that the change would make no difference, the evidence clearly shows the new procedure enhances service quality.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **Analysis Overview**
| | |
|---|---|
| Branches analyzed | 150 |
| Switched to new procedure | 90 branches |
| Remained on old procedure | 60 branches (control) |
| Observation period | 17 weeks (weeks 6–22) |
| Switch implemented | Week 15 |

### **Impact on Service Scores**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Average improvement** | **+2.08 points** |
| **Statistical confidence** | **p < 0.001** (highly significant) |
| **95% Confidence range** | 0.93 to 3.23 points |
| **Relative gain** | **~4.3%** above baseline |

### **Why We Trust This Result**

1. **Valid comparison**: Before the switch, both groups followed parallel trends—service scores moved together week-to-week. The divergence occurred only after the switch.

2. **Effect is sustained**: Improvement was evident in both the early weeks after switching (+1.6 points) and later weeks (+2.5 points), indicating the benefit does not fade.

3. **Result is robust**: The 2.1-point gain holds across all statistical tests, regardless of whether we account for staffing levels, visitor volumes, or inherent differences between branches.

4. **Controls confirm the finding**: Higher staffing and visitor traffic do predict better scores, but these factors do not explain the treatment effect—the procedure change itself drove the improvement.
