# **MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new shelving and stock rotation procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw an average increase of **2.3 points** on the service score index (approximately **4.7% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is highly statistically significant and consistent across the 425 branches that switched. 

**Recommendation: Roll out the new procedure to all remaining branches.**

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### Study Design
- **709 branches** analyzed over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **425 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **284 branches** continued with the old procedure as a control group
- Analysis used a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in both groups before and after the switch

### Results

| Metric | Control Group (No Switch) | Treatment Group (Switched) |
|--------|---------------------------|----------------------------|
| Average score BEFORE switch | 48.7 | 50.1 |
| Average score AFTER switch | 49.4 | 53.2 |
| **Change in score** | **+0.8** | **+3.1** |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | — | **+2.3 points** |

### Statistical Confidence
- **Treatment effect:** +2.34 points
- **95% Confidence Interval:** 1.76 to 2.92 points
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001 (highly significant)
- The effect remains significant even after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### Validation
Service score trends for both groups followed similar patterns in the 9 weeks before the switch, supporting the validity of the comparison. This indicates the improvement is attributable to the procedure change, not pre-existing differences between branches.

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to the remaining 284 branches

2. **Develop implementation timeline** – Consider phasing the rollout to manage training and transition logistics

3. **Monitor post-rollout performance** – Continue tracking service scores for 8–12 weeks after full implementation to confirm sustained improvement

4. **Document best practices** – Capture lessons learned from the 425 early-adopter branches to support training materials

5. **Consider complementary improvements** – Staffing and visitor volume also affect service scores; evaluate whether additional investments in these areas could amplify the gains from the new procedure
