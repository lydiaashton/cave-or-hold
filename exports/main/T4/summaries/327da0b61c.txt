# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2025  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure  

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores—and we did not expect it to.**

Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by **2.1 points** (approximately 4%) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This finding is statistically significant and robust. Although the operational change was not designed to affect service quality, the data show a clear positive effect. We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

| Finding | Result |
|---------|--------|
| **Main Effect** | +2.1 points on service score (95% CI: 0.9 to 3.4) |
| **Statistical Significance** | p = 0.001 (highly significant) |
| **Effect Size** | ~4% improvement relative to average score of 50 |
| **Robustness** | Effect unchanged (+2.2 points) after controlling for staffing and visits |
| **Timing** | Immediate improvement in Week 15, sustained through Week 22 |
| **Validation** | Pre-switch trends were parallel—supports causal interpretation |

---

## **Why This Is Notable**

The operational team expected this procedural change to have **no measurable impact** on service scores. The change was implemented for logistical reasons, not service improvement. The fact that we observe a consistent positive effect suggests:

1. **Unintended benefit:** The new procedure may improve patron experience in ways not anticipated (e.g., faster item location, cleaner shelves, reduced wait times).

2. **Low-cost improvement:** Since the procedure was already adopted by 90 branches for operational reasons, extending it system-wide requires no additional investment.

3. **Validation of pilot approach:** The phased rollout allowed us to detect this effect before committing all branches.

---

## **Analysis Approach**

We used a **difference-in-differences** design:

- **Treatment group:** 90 branches that switched to the new procedure at Week 15
- **Control group:** 60 branches that continued with the old procedure
- **Data:** 2,550 branch-week observations (17 weeks, weeks 6–22)
- **Method:** Regression with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch)
- **Validation:** Pre-trend test confirms parallel trends before the switch (p=0.51)

---

## **Recommendations**

| Action | Rationale |
|--------|-----------|
| **1. Approve system-wide rollout** | Evidence shows meaningful improvement with no added cost |
| **2. Monitor during expansion** | Track whether effects replicate as remaining 60 branches switch |
| **3. Investigate the mechanism** | Understand *why* scores improved—may reveal additional optimization opportunities |
| **4. Document for future pilots** | This demonstrates value in measuring service outcomes even for operational changes |

---

## **Conclusion**

This analysis reveals an unexpected benefit: a procedural change implemented for operational reasons has measurably improved service quality. The effect is statistically robust, appeared immediately, and has persisted over eight weeks. Given the positive impact and absence of additional costs, we recommend expanding the new procedure to all branches.
