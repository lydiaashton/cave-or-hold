# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Analysis Period:** Weeks 6–22 (17 weeks of branch-level data)

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure achieved service scores approximately **2 points higher** than comparable branches using the old procedure. This improvement is statistically reliable and meaningful for patron experience. **We recommend proceeding with systemwide rollout.**

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### The Evidence

| Metric | Treatment Branches (New Procedure) | Control Branches (Old Procedure) |
|--------|-----------------------------------|----------------------------------|
| Number of branches | 425 | 284 |
| Average score before switch | 50.8 | 48.8 |
| Average score after switch | 52.1 | 48.3 |
| **Change** | **+1.4** | **−0.6** |

**Net improvement attributable to new procedure: +1.9 points** (p < 0.001)

### What This Means

- Service scores average about 50 points across the system. A 2-point gain represents roughly **one-fifth of a standard deviation**—a noticeable improvement in patron-facing service quality.
- The result holds after accounting for branch-specific characteristics, week-to-week variation, staffing levels, and visit volumes.
- Control branches actually saw scores dip slightly after Week 15, while treatment branches improved—suggesting the new procedure may also help branches resist seasonal or systemwide pressures that typically depress scores.

### Important Context

Treatment branches showed slightly stronger upward momentum even before the switch. Accounting for this pre-existing trend, the **conservative estimate of the procedure's effect is 1.0–1.5 points**. Even at this lower bound, the improvement remains positive and statistically significant.

---

## **RECOMMENDATION**

**Approve systemwide rollout of the new procedure.** The evidence indicates a genuine service improvement with no identified downsides. The 425 early-adopter branches provide a strong proof of concept.

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

1. **Develop rollout timeline** for remaining 284 branches (prioritize by branch size, current performance, or readiness)
2. **Create implementation guide** documenting procedures from high-performing early adopters
3. **Establish monitoring dashboard** to track service scores at newly converted branches
4. **Schedule 90-day post-conversion reviews** to confirm effects persist and address implementation challenges
5. **Consider complementary investments** (staff training, shelving equipment) that may amplify the procedure's benefits
