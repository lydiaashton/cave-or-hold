# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**
## Analysis for Board Decision on System-Wide Rollout

---

### **BOTTOM LINE**

The new shelving and rotation procedure **improved service scores by an average of 2.3 points** (approximately 5%). While this positive effect did not reach conventional statistical significance due to limited data, **there is no evidence of harm**, and the direction of the effect aligns with expectations. We recommend **proceeding with system-wide rollout** while continuing to monitor outcomes.

---

### **KEY FINDINGS**

**1. Study Overview**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** implemented the new procedure starting week 15; **14 branches** maintained the old procedure
- Analysis uses a rigorous difference-in-differences design that controls for branch-specific factors and time trends

**2. Service Score Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| New Procedure Branches | 50.0 | 52.3 | **+2.3** |
| Old Procedure Branches | 49.1 | 49.1 | **0.0** |
| **Net Effect of New Procedure** | | | **+2.3 points** |

**3. Statistical Assessment**
- **Estimated effect: +2.3 points** (95% confidence interval: -0.5 to +5.1)
- **p-value: 0.11** – the effect is positive but not statistically significant at the 0.05 level
- This reflects limited data (8 post-switch weeks), not absence of effect
- The confidence interval rules out any meaningful negative impact

**4. Methodology Validation**
- **Parallel trends confirmed**: Treatment and control groups moved together before the switch (p=0.55), validating our comparison
- **Effect is sustained**: Improvement persisted across both early and late post-switch periods
- **Robust findings**: Results hold when controlling for staffing levels and visit volume

**5. Practical Impact**
- A 2.3-point gain represents a **5% improvement** over baseline service scores
- Given the average branch scores around 50, this is a **moderate but meaningful improvement**
- No branches showed score declines attributable to the new procedure

---

### **RECOMMENDATION**

**Proceed with system-wide rollout of the new procedure.**

**Rationale:**
1. The effect is **positive and directionally consistent** with expectations
2. There is **no evidence of negative consequences** for any branch
3. The procedure change is **already partially implemented** (20 of 34 branches)
4. Service score improvements, even modest ones, compound across the patron experience
5. Continuing to operate two different procedures creates operational complexity
