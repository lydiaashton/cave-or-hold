## **Bottom Line**

**The new rotation and shelving procedure improved service scores and should be rolled out to all branches.**

Branches that adopted the new procedure maintained their service scores while non-adopting branches declined. The net benefit is **+2.3 points** on the service score index—a meaningful and statistically significant improvement.

---

## **Key Findings**

### The Evidence

| Metric | Result |
|--------|--------|
| **Treatment Effect** | +2.3 points (95% CI: 1.8 to 2.7) |
| **Statistical Significance** | p < 0.001 |
| **Branches Studied** | 709 (425 switched, 284 did not) |
| **Observation Period** | 17 weeks (weeks 6–22) |

### What Happened

- **Before the switch** (weeks 6–14): Both groups tracked together, averaging ~52 points
- **After the switch** (weeks 15–22): 
  - Switched branches held steady at ~51.8 points
  - Non-switched branches dropped to ~49.7 points
- **Result**: The new procedure prevented a decline and delivered a relative gain of 2.3 points

### Why We're Confident

1. **Parallel pre-trends**: Both groups moved together before the switch, validating the comparison
2. **Controls confirm the effect**: Results hold after accounting for staffing levels, visit volumes, and branch-specific factors
3. **No confounding effects**: The procedure did not change staffing needs or visit patterns—the benefit is direct

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with immediate system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all remaining branches.
