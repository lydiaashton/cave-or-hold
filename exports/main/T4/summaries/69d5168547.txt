# **Board Summary: New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores and should be rolled out system-wide.** Branches that adopted the new procedure maintained their service scores while control branches declined. The improvement is approximately **2 points** (4% of average score), statistically significant, and consistent across all post-switch weeks.

---

## **Key Findings**

| Metric | Switched Branches | Control Branches |
|--------|------------------|------------------|
| Number of branches | 425 | 284 |
| Average score before | 50.9 | 50.7 |
| Average score after | 50.9 | 48.6 |
| **Change** | **0.0** | **-2.0** |

**The new procedure is associated with a 2.0-point improvement in service scores** (95% confidence interval: 1.4 to 2.6 points). This result holds across multiple analytical methods and remains significant after controlling for staffing levels, visitor volume, and branch-specific characteristics.

**Why this matters:** While switched branches held steady, control branches declined—suggesting the new procedure protected against broader downward pressures on service quality during this period.

---

## **Analysis Overview**

- **Data:** 709 branches, 17 weeks (weeks 6-22), 12,053 observations
- **Method:** Difference-in-differences with branch and week fixed effects
- **Switch point:** Week 15
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001

---

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with system-wide rollout of the new procedure.**

The evidence indicates the new procedure delivers measurable service improvements. The effect is robust, statistically significant, and meaningful in practical terms.
