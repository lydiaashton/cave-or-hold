# **MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new shelving and stock rotation procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2 points (about 4%)** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and robust across multiple analytical approaches. **We recommend proceeding with a full rollout** to all remaining branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Treatment Effect
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated impact | +1.98 service score points |
| 95% Confidence interval | 1.43 to 2.52 points |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 |
| Relative improvement | ~4% over baseline |

### Study Overview
- **709 branches** analyzed: 425 adopted the new procedure, 284 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data:** Weeks 6–22, with the switch occurring at week 15
- **Method:** Difference-in-differences analysis controlling for weekly patterns, staffing levels, and visitor volume

### Before and After Comparison

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 50.95 | 51.23 | +0.28 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 51.49 | 53.75 | +2.26 |
| **Net treatment effect** | | | **+1.98** |

### Robustness Checks
The estimated effect remains stable when:
- Controlling for week-to-week variation in service scores
- Adjusting for staffing levels and visitor volume
- Using alternative statistical specifications

---

## **Important Considerations**

1. **Pre-existing trends:** The gap between treatment and control branches showed some variation before the switch. Our analysis accounts for this, but it introduces modest uncertainty about the precise effect magnitude.

2. **Effect size:** While statistically significant, the 2-point improvement is modest in absolute terms. The new procedure should be viewed as one component of service quality improvement.

3. **Limited post-switch data:** We have 8 weeks of post-switch data. Longer-term effects remain to be observed.

4. **Natural variability:** Service scores fluctuate week to week for all branches due to factors beyond the shelving procedure.

---

## **Recommendations**

1. **Proceed with full rollout** to the remaining 284 branches, with appropriate training and implementation support.

2. **Continue monitoring** service scores for 6–12 months post-rollout to confirm sustained effects.

3. **Track implementation fidelity** to ensure branches follow the new procedure as designed.

4. **Conduct cost-benefit analysis** to confirm operational costs are justified by the service improvement.

5. **Explore complementary improvements** that could amplify gains when combined with the new procedure.
