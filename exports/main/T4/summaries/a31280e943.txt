# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Prepared by:** Data Analysis Team

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new shelving and rotation procedure achieved service scores **2.3 points higher** (approximately **5% improvement**) than branches that kept the old procedure. The effect is statistically significant, sustained over time, and not explained by staffing or visitor volume changes.

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout.**

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **Study Overview**
| | |
|---|---|
| Branches studied | 150 |
| Switched to new procedure | 90 branches (60%) |
| Kept old procedure | 60 branches (40%) |
| Observation period | 17 weeks (Weeks 6–22) |
| Switch implemented | Week 15 |

### **Impact on Service Scores**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| **Switched branches** | 49.75 | 53.60 | +3.85 |
| **Non-switched branches** | 49.73 | 51.08 | +1.35 |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | | **+2.50** |

**Adjusted estimate (controlling for staffing and visits): +2.33 points**
- 95% Confidence Interval: 1.0 to 3.7 points
- Statistical significance: p = 0.001

### **Validity Checks**

✓ **Parallel trends confirmed**: Switched and non-switched branches followed similar trajectories before the change (p = 0.16), supporting causal interpretation.

✓ **Effect is sustained**: Improvement persisted across all 8 post-switch weeks, ranging from +1.9 to +4.0 points weekly.

✓ **Result is robust**: Effect remains significant with or without controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume.
