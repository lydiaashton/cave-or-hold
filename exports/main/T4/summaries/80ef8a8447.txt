# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Prepared by:** Data Analysis Office

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure achieved service scores approximately **2 points higher** than branches that kept the old procedure. This finding is statistically reliable and contradicts the initial expectation that the change would make no difference to service quality.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with system-wide rollout of the new procedure.

---

## **Key Findings**

### The Evidence

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Branches with new procedure | 48.6 | 51.7 | +3.2 |
| Branches with old procedure | 47.2 | 48.4 | +1.1 |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | | **+2.1** |

### What This Means

- **150 branches** participated in the evaluation (90 switched, 60 did not)
- Data covers **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- The **2.1-point improvement** represents approximately **4% gain** over the typical branch score of 50
- The result is **statistically significant** (p = 0.003), meaning there is less than a 1% chance this finding is due to random variation
- The improvement persists after accounting for staffing levels and branch visit volumes

### Why We Can Trust This Result

The analysis uses a rigorous "difference-in-differences" approach that compares switched branches against control branches over the same time period. This method isolates the effect of the procedure change from other factors (such as seasonal variations or system-wide trends) that might affect all branches simultaneously.

## **Conclusion**

The initial assumption—that the new procedure would not affect service scores—was incorrect. The data demonstrates a clear, positive impact on service quality. Rolling out the new procedure to all branches is expected to raise system-wide service scores by approximately 2 points, benefiting patrons across the cooperative.

*Technical note: Analysis based on 2,550 weekly observations from 150 branches. Model: difference-in-differences regression with branch fixed effects and clustered standard errors. Treatment effect = 2.11 (95% CI: 0.74 to 3.48, p = 0.003).*
