# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Library Cooperative Board  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Executive Summary**

We evaluated whether the new stock rotation procedure improved branch service scores. The data show a **positive effect of approximately 2 points** (4% above baseline), but this improvement is **not statistically distinguishable from zero** given our current sample size. While the direction of the effect aligns with expectations, we lack sufficient evidence to confirm the procedure caused a meaningful improvement.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **phased rollout** while continuing to collect data. The evidence suggests no harm and possible benefit, but additional weeks of observation would provide greater confidence.

---

## **Analysis Overview**

| Component | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Method** | Difference-in-differences (treatment vs. control branches) |
| **Branches** | 34 total (20 switched, 14 did not) |
| **Period** | 17 weeks (weeks 6–22; switch occurred week 15) |
| **Outcome** | Service score index (baseline average ≈ 50) |

---

## **Results**

### **Primary Finding**

| Estimate | Value |
|----------|-------|
| Effect on service score | **+2.1 points** |
| 95% Confidence interval | **-0.5 to +4.6 points** |
| p-value | **0.125** |

### **Interpretation**

- The **best estimate** is a 2-point improvement, consistent with expectations
- However, the **confidence interval includes zero**, meaning we cannot rule out no effect
- With 95% confidence, the true effect lies somewhere between a slight decline (-0.5) and a moderate gain (+4.6)
- This uncertainty stems from **limited statistical power** (only 34 branches), not from evidence of ineffectiveness

### **Supporting Evidence**

✓ **Pre-trends are parallel**: Before the switch, treatment and control branches moved together (p=0.999)  
✓ **No negative effects detected**: The lower bound of the confidence interval is near zero  
✓ **Results are robust**: Estimates are stable with or without controls for staffing and visitor volume  

---

## **Why Weren't Results Statistically Significant?**

This does **not** mean the procedure failed. Three factors explain the uncertainty:

1. **Small sample**: Only 34 branches limits our ability to detect modest effects
2. **Short post-period**: Only 8 weeks of data after the switch
3. **Natural variation**: Service scores fluctuate week-to-week for many reasons

With more branches or more weeks, the same 2-point effect would likely reach statistical significance.

---

## **Recommendations**

### **1. Proceed with Phased Rollout**
The evidence points in the expected direction with no signal of harm. A staged implementation allows continued learning while moving forward.

### **2. Extend Data Collection**
Continue tracking service scores for an additional 8–12 weeks. This would:
- Narrow the confidence interval by ~30%
- Provide greater confidence in the estimated effect
- Allow detection of any delayed or cumulative effects

### **3. Monitor Implementation Quality**
Ensure all branches follow the new procedure consistently. Variation in implementation could mask true effects.

### **4. Track Complementary Metrics**
Consider adding:
- Patron satisfaction surveys
- Staff time spent on shelving
- Material circulation rates

These may capture benefits not reflected in the service score index.

---

## **Decision Summary**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did service scores improve? | Point estimate says yes (+2 points), but uncertainty remains |
| Is the procedure harmful? | No evidence of negative effects |
| Should we roll out everywhere? | Recommended: phased rollout with continued monitoring |
| Do we need more data? | Yes—additional weeks would strengthen conclusions |
