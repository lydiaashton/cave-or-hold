# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Library Cooperative Board  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 2025  
**SUBJECT:** Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure **improved service scores**. Contrary to the expectation that this change would make no difference, branches that adopted the new procedure saw statistically significant gains. We recommend **proceeding with a system-wide rollout**.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **The Numbers**

| | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Branches with new procedure** | 52.2 | 53.4 | +1.2 |
| **Branches with old procedure** | 48.7 | 48.0 | −0.7 |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | | **+1.9** |

### **Statistical Confidence**

- **Effect estimate:** +1.91 points on the service score index
- **95% confidence interval:** 0.83 to 2.99 points
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001 (less than 1 in 1,000 chance this is random)
- **Practical significance:** 0.19 standard deviations, or 3.8% improvement over baseline

### **Study Strength**

- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (February through June)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure; **60 branches** maintained the old procedure as a comparison group
- Analysis confirms treatment and control branches were on similar trajectories before the switch, validating our conclusions

---

## **What This Means**

The expectation was that changing rotation and shelving procedures would not affect patron-facing service scores. The data shows otherwise. The new procedure is associated with meaningful improvements in how patrons experience our branches.

Two factors contributed to the observed effect:
1. Switched branches improved after adoption (+1.2 points)
2. Non-switched branches declined slightly over the same period (−0.7 points)

This pattern suggests the new procedure may also help branches resist seasonal or operational pressures that typically depress service scores.

---

## **Recommendations**

| Priority | Action |
|---|---|
| **1** | **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all 60 branches still using the old method |
| **2** | **Document the new procedure** in detail to ensure consistent implementation across all branches |
| **3** | **Monitor service scores** quarterly during rollout to confirm effects persist at scale |
| **4** | **Conduct follow-up analysis** in 6 months to assess long-term sustainability of gains |

---

## **Questions for Discussion**

1. What is the timeline for completing the rollout to remaining branches?
2. Are there training or resource needs to support implementation?
3. Should we investigate which aspects of the new procedure drive the improvement (e.g., faster book location, better organization)?
