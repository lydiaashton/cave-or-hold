# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Analysis Period:** Weeks 6–22 (17 weeks of data from 150 branches)

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Although the board expected the change would make no difference, branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure achieved service scores approximately **1.9 points higher** than branches that retained the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant and represents a meaningful gain in service quality.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. The New Procedure Works

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated improvement in service score | **+1.91 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | 1.16 to 2.66 points |
| Statistical Significance | p < 0.001 (highly significant) |

### 2. Context for the Effect

- The average branch service score is approximately **50 points**
- The improvement represents a **3.8% increase** relative to the mean
- In practical terms, this is a modest but meaningful gain—roughly **0.18 standard deviations**

### 3. What Happened Before and After the Switch

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|----------------|---------------|--------|
| Branches keeping old procedure | 49.6 | 48.0 | **−1.7 points** |
| Branches using new procedure | 51.3 | 51.5 | **+0.2 points** |

Branches that switched to the new procedure maintained their service scores after the change, while branches keeping the old procedure experienced a decline. The net difference is the 1.9-point improvement attributed to the new procedure.

### 4. The Analysis Is Credible

- **Parallel trends confirmed:** Before the switch (weeks 6–14), both groups of branches showed similar trends in service scores. This supports the conclusion that the improvement is caused by the new procedure, not pre-existing differences.
- **Robust results:** The finding holds when accounting for branch-specific factors, week-to-week variation, staffing levels, and visitor volumes.

---

## **Next Steps**

### Immediate Actions

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all remaining branches.

2. **Communicate results** to branch managers and staff, emphasizing that the change has been shown to improve service quality.

### Implementation Considerations

3. **Monitor during rollout:** Track service scores at branches as they transition to confirm the effect replicates at scale.

4. **Provide training support:** Ensure consistent implementation across all branches to maintain the observed benefits.

5. **Gather qualitative feedback:** Consider speaking with staff at switched branches to understand what aspects of the new procedure are driving the improvement—this may reveal additional opportunities.

### Ongoing Oversight

6. **Continue weekly monitoring:** Maintain service score tracking to detect any changes in the effect over time.
