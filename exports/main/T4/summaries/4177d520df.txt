# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2025  
**Subject:** Recommendation on System-Wide Rollout of New Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure shows a **positive but statistically uncertain effect** on service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure improved by approximately **1.7 points (3.3%)** more than branches that kept the old procedure. However, we cannot conclude with high confidence that this improvement is attributable to the procedure rather than random variation.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **phased rollout** while continuing to monitor results, rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **What We Studied**
- **34 branches** over 17 weeks (February through June)
- **20 branches** switched to the new procedure in mid-April (week 15)
- **14 branches** continued with the old procedure as a comparison group
- Service scores averaged around 50 points; higher scores indicate better service

### **What We Found**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Branches keeping old procedure | 50.89 | 50.92 | +0.03 |
| Branches adopting new procedure | 52.01 | 53.79 | +1.78 |
| **Net improvement from new procedure** | | | **+1.69** |

### **How Confident Are We?**

| Measure | Result |
|---------|--------|
| Estimated improvement | +1.69 points |
| Confidence range (95%) | -0.31 to +3.69 points |
| Statistical significance | Marginal (p = 0.098) |

**What this means:** The data suggests a real improvement, but there is approximately a 10% chance the observed effect is due to random variation. The true effect could be slightly negative, zero, or as high as +3.7 points.

### **Additional Insights**
- Branches with higher staffing levels and more visits had higher service scores, confirming these are important factors to manage alongside any procedure change
- The improvement trend was consistent across most weeks after the switch
- No branches showed declining performance after adopting the new procedure

---

## **Recommendations**

### **1. Approve Phased Rollout**
Expand the new procedure to additional branches over the next 3–6 months while maintaining some branches on the old procedure for continued comparison.

### **2. Extend Monitoring Period**
Collect at least 6 months of post-implementation data before making a final system-wide decision. The current 8-week window is too short for definitive conclusions.

### **3. Track Implementation Quality**
Document how consistently each branch implements the new procedure. Variation in execution may explain variation in results.

### **4. Establish Decision Checkpoints**
- **3-month review:** Assess early trends and implementation challenges
- **6-month decision:** If the effect remains positive and reaches statistical significance (p < 0.05), approve full rollout. Otherwise, pause and investigate.

### **5. No Immediate Action Required for Non-Switched Branches**
Branches currently using the old procedure should continue as-is until the phased rollout reaches them.

---

## **Questions for Discussion**

1. Does the board prefer a more conservative approach (wait for stronger evidence) or an aggressive rollout (accept some uncertainty)?
2. What resources are available to support branches during implementation?
3. Should we consider additional outcome measures beyond service scores (e.g., patron satisfaction, staff feedback)?
