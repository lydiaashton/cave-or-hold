# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.1 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents a **4% improvement** over the baseline and is statistically significant (p < 0.001). We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
- **150 branches** tracked over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **60 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach to isolate the causal effect of the procedure change

### Service Score Impact

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|----------------|---------------|--------|
| Branches that switched | 50.29 | 53.50 | +3.20 |
| Branches that did not switch | 49.48 | 50.55 | +1.08 |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | | **+2.12** |

- **Estimated effect: +2.1 points** (95% confidence interval: 0.9 to 3.4)
- **Statistically significant** (p < 0.001)
- Effect persists after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### Validity of Results
- **Parallel trends confirmed**: Before the switch, both groups showed similar score trends (no significant pre-existing difference, p = 0.75)
- **Robust to controls**: Results hold when accounting for staffing and visitation changes
- **Robust to branch differences**: Results hold when accounting for branch-specific characteristics

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all branches

2. **Monitor implementation**: Track service scores for 8–12 weeks after full rollout to confirm the effect persists at scale

3. **Document best practices**: Capture the specific practices that distinguish the new procedure for training purposes

4. **Assess resource requirements**: If the new procedure requires additional staff time or materials, weigh the 4% service improvement against implementation costs

5. **Leverage early adopters**: The 90 branches that already switched successfully may serve as mentors during the rollout
