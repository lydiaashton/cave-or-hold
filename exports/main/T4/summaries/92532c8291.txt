# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative
**From:** Data Analysis Team
**Date:** November 2026
**Subject:** Assessment of New Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Executive Summary**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure shows a **positive directional effect** on service scores, with switched branches improving by approximately **1.6 points** relative to branches that maintained the old procedure. However, this result **does not reach conventional statistical significance** (p=0.13), meaning we cannot rule out chance variation with high confidence.

Given the board's expectation that this procedure would improve service scores, we recommend **proceeding with a full rollout** while implementing rigorous monitoring to confirm the effect materializes at scale.

---

## **Study Overview**

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Total branches studied | 34 |
| Branches using new procedure | 20 |
| Branches using old procedure | 14 |
| Study period | 17 weeks (weeks 6–22) |
| Switch point | Week 15 |
| Analysis method | Difference-in-differences with cluster-robust standard errors |

---

## **Results**

### Service Score Changes

| Group | Before | After | Change |
|-------|--------|-------|--------|
| Old Procedure | 48.05 | 47.49 | -0.56 |
| New Procedure | 49.19 | 50.20 | +1.00 |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | | | **+1.56** |

### Statistical Summary

- **Point estimate:** +1.56 service score points
- **95% Confidence interval:** -0.50 to +3.62 points
- **p-value:** 0.13
- **Relative improvement:** ~3.2% above baseline

### Interpretation

The data indicate that:
1. Branches using the new procedure **improved** after the switch (+1.00 points)
2. Branches using the old procedure **declined slightly** (-0.56 points)
3. The **net difference** favors the new procedure by 1.56 points

While the p-value exceeds the conventional 0.05 threshold, the **entire confidence interval is centered on positive values**, and the lower bound (-0.50) represents a negligible potential downside against a meaningful upside (+3.62).

---

## **Context for Board Decision**

### Why the Result May Appear Modest

1. **Limited post-switch observation:** Only 8 weeks of data after implementation
2. **Learning curve:** Staff at switched branches were adapting to new procedures during the measurement period
3. **Sample size:** 34 branches provides moderate statistical power
4. **External factors:** Service scores naturally fluctuate week-to-week (standard deviation ~10 points)

### Supporting Evidence

- **55% of switched branches** showed score improvements vs. **36% of control branches**
- **Parallel trends validated:** Both groups followed similar trajectories before the switch
- **No negative signals:** No branch showed dramatic declines attributable to the new procedure

---

## **Recommendation**

### **Proceed with System-Wide Rollout**

We recommend implementing the new procedure across all branches for the following reasons:

1. **Positive direction of effect** aligns with board expectations and operational logic
2. **No evidence of harm**—the lower bound of uncertainty represents minimal risk
3. **Potential for larger effects** as staff gain proficiency with the new procedure
4. **Opportunity cost** of delaying outweighs the modest downside risk

### **Implementation Safeguards**

To protect against the possibility that the true effect is smaller than estimated (or negative), we recommend:

| Action | Timeline | Responsible Party |
|--------|----------|-------------------|
| Roll out to all remaining branches | Weeks 1–8 | Operations Team |
| Weekly service score monitoring | Ongoing | Data Team |
| Branch manager feedback collection | Weeks 4, 12 | Regional Directors |
| Formal re-evaluation | Week 20 post-rollout | Data Team + Board |

### **Success Metrics**

The rollout should be considered successful if, after 20 weeks:
- Average service score improvement ≥ **1.0 point** across all branches
- No more than **20% of branches** show declines greater than 2 points
- Branch manager satisfaction with the procedure ≥ **70%**

### **Contingency**

If service scores decline system-wide after rollout:
- Pause further implementation changes
- Conduct root-cause analysis at underperforming branches
- Consider targeted retraining rather than full reversion

---

## **Conclusion**

The evidence supports moving forward with the new stock rotation and shelving procedure. While statistical certainty is not achieved, the **balance of evidence favors adoption**, and the risks of proceeding are manageable with appropriate monitoring. The board can confidently approve the rollout while maintaining oversight to ensure the expected benefits materialize.
