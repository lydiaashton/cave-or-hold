# **Board Memorandum**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation procedure shows **promising but inconclusive** results. Switched branches improved their service scores by an estimated **2.2 points (5%)** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. However, this improvement is **not statistically significant**, meaning we cannot confidently conclude the procedure caused the change. We recommend **continued monitoring with targeted expansion** rather than an immediate system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Service Score Impact**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated improvement | +2.2 points |
| Baseline score (switched branches) | 45.0 |
| Percentage improvement | ~5% |
| Statistical significance | Not significant (p = 0.11) |
| 95% Confidence interval | -0.6 to +5.0 points |

**What this means:** The data suggests a positive effect, but there is approximately an 11% probability this result could occur by random chance even if the procedure had no real impact. The true effect could range from slightly harmful to moderately beneficial.

### **2. Branch-Level Performance**

- **16 of 20 switched branches (80%)** improved after adopting the new procedure
- **4 branches (20%)** experienced declines in service scores
- Performance varied considerably:
  - **Best performer:** +11.5 points improvement
  - **Worst performer:** -5.7 points decline

### **3. Control Group Comparison**

Non-switched branches showed minimal change over the same period (+0.5 points), confirming that the observed improvements in switched branches are not attributable to seasonal trends or system-wide factors.

### **4. Study Parameters**

- **34 branches** total (20 switched, 14 did not switch)
- **17 weeks** of data (weeks 6–22)
- Switch occurred at **week 15**
- Analysis controlled for branch-specific characteristics and time trends

---

## **Recommendations**

### **Immediate Actions**

1. **Do not proceed with full system-wide rollout at this time.** The evidence is suggestive but not definitive.

2. **Extend the monitoring period** by at least 8–12 weeks to increase statistical power and determine whether the positive trend persists.

3. **Investigate the 4 underperforming branches** to identify implementation barriers or branch-specific factors that may have limited effectiveness.

### **Conditional Next Steps**

4. **Consider targeted expansion** to branches with characteristics similar to the top performers (branches 9, 6, 7, 33, and 4).

5. **Maintain a control group** of non-switched branches during any expansion to continue measuring impact.

6. **Conduct a cost-benefit analysis** before committing to full rollout. A 5% service improvement must be weighed against implementation costs, staff training, and operational disruption.

7. **Gather qualitative feedback** from branch managers and staff on procedural challenges and suggestions for refinement.

---

## **Conclusion**

The new stock rotation procedure shows promise, with most switched branches experiencing improved service scores. However, the statistical uncertainty warrants a cautious approach. We recommend treating this as a **successful pilot requiring refinement** rather than a proven intervention ready for universal adoption. With additional data and targeted improvements, we can make a more confident recommendation within 2–3 months.
