# **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure is associated with a **2.1-point improvement in service scores** (approximately 4% relative to baseline). While this effect is not statistically definitive at conventional levels, the evidence points in a positive direction. We recommend a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **34 branches** tracked over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure; **14 branches** kept the old procedure
- Switch occurred at **week 15**; analysis compares before/after changes between groups

### Results Summary

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 53.83 | 55.09 | +1.26 |
| Non-switched branches | 46.52 | 45.68 | −0.84 |
| **Estimated effect** | | | **+2.10** |

### Statistical Evidence
- **Parallel trends confirmed**: Before the switch, both groups followed similar trends (p=0.89), validating our comparison approach
- **Effect size**: +2.1 points on the service score index (4.5% improvement)
- **Statistical significance**: p≈0.09–0.10 (marginally significant); 95% confidence interval ranges from −0.3 to +4.7 points
- **Robustness**: Effect remains consistent when controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### Important Context
- Non-switched branches experienced a slight **decline** in scores after week 15 (−0.84 points)
- Switched branches **improved** (+1.26 points), resulting in the 2.1-point differential
- The new procedure may help **maintain or improve** service quality during periods when scores might otherwise drift downward

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with cautious expansion**: Roll out the new procedure to additional branches in phases, prioritizing those with lower baseline scores

2. **Continue monitoring**: Track service scores for at least 8–12 weeks post-implementation at each new branch to confirm the effect persists

3. **Investigate mechanisms**: Analyze whether the improvement stems from faster fulfillment, better organization, or other factors to optimize the procedure further

4. **Set success criteria**: Define a minimum acceptable improvement (e.g., +1.5 points) for deciding whether to complete the system-wide rollout

5. **Review in 3 months**: Reconvene to assess accumulated evidence from the phased rollout before making a final go/no-go decision

---

*Analysis based on difference-in-differences regression with branch fixed effects and clustered standard errors. Data covers weeks 6–22, with procedure switch at week 15.*
