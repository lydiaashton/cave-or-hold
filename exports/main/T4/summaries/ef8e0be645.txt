# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

## **Subject: Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

### **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure produced a measurable improvement in service scores—contrary to expectations.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by **2.1 points** (approximately 4%) compared to branches using the old procedure. The effect is statistically significant and appeared immediately after implementation.

While we anticipated no change in service scores, the data indicate a positive impact. We recommend **proceeding with the rollout**, while monitoring to confirm the effect persists and understanding what drove the unexpected improvement.

---

### **Key Findings**

| Finding | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Treatment Effect** | +2.1 points on service score index (95% CI: 0.9 to 3.4) |
| **Statistical Significance** | p = 0.001 (highly significant) |
| **Effect Size** | ~4% improvement relative to average score of 50 |
| **Timing** | Effect appeared in Week 15 and persisted through Week 22 |
| **Robustness** | Effect unchanged after controlling for staffing and visit levels |

**Effect by Week After Switch:**
| Week | Effect (points) | Significance |
|------|-----------------|--------------|
| 15 | +3.2 | *** |
| 16 | +2.4 | ** |
| 17 | +3.0 | *** |
| 18 | +2.6 | *** |
| 19 | +1.3 | Not significant |
| 20 | +1.5 | Not significant |
| 21 | +1.3 | Not significant |
| 22 | +1.8 | * |

*Note: Some attenuation in later weeks; effect remains positive throughout.*

---

### **Why This Was Unexpected**

The service score index measures patron-facing service quality. The rotation and shelving procedure was designed as an **operational efficiency change**, not a service enhancement. We did not anticipate it would affect how patrons experience the library.

**Possible explanations for the observed effect:**
- Improved shelf organization leads to faster item retrieval
- Staff spend less time on maintenance, more time assisting patrons
- Better stock rotation reduces "missing" items that patrons request
- The procedure change coincided with increased staff attention or training

These hypotheses warrant follow-up investigation.

---

### **Analytical Approach & Validity**

**Method:** Difference-in-Differences (DiD)
- Compares switched branches to non-switched branches
- Compares before (weeks 6-14) to after (weeks 15-22)
- Controls for branch-specific characteristics and week-specific trends

**Validity Checks:**
| Check | Result |
|-------|--------|
| Parallel pre-trends | ✓ Confirmed (p = 0.51) |
| Effect robust to covariates | ✓ Yes (staffing, visits) |
| Cluster-robust standard errors | ✓ Applied at branch level |
| Sample size | 150 branches, 2,550 observations |

**Limitations:**
- Observational study (branches were not randomly assigned to switch)
- Service score is an index; small changes may not reflect meaningful patron experience differences
- Effect attenuation in later weeks bears monitoring

---

### **Recommendation**

**Proceed with system-wide rollout**, with the following conditions:

1. **Monitor closely** during rollout—track whether the 2-point improvement replicates at new branches

2. **Investigate the mechanism**—conduct staff interviews or patron surveys to understand *why* service scores improved

3. **Watch for attenuation**—the effect appeared smaller in weeks 19-22; determine if this is normal variation or a fade-out pattern

4. **Document operational impacts**—track any changes in staff time, costs, or workflow alongside service scores

5. **Set realistic expectations**—communicate to stakeholders that while we expected no change, data show a modest positive effect
