# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores and should be rolled out to all branches.**

Contrary to the expectation that it would make no difference, the new procedure had a **positive and statistically significant effect**. Branches that switched maintained their service scores while non-switching branches declined. The estimated improvement is **+1.9 points** on the service score index (approximately 4% relative to the average score of 50).

**Recommendation: Approve system-wide implementation.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. The New Procedure Works**

| Group | Before | After | Change |
|-------|--------|-------|--------|
| Branches that **did not switch** | 49.1 | 46.9 | ▼ -2.2 |
| Branches that **switched** | 49.9 | 49.6 | ▼ -0.3 |

**Net Effect: +1.9 points** (p < 0.01)

Switching branches avoided the decline that affected non-switching branches, resulting in a meaningful improvement.

### **2. Results Are Reliable**

- **Statistically significant:** The finding is unlikely to be due to chance (less than 1% probability)
- **Valid comparison:** Before the switch, both groups followed similar trends, confirming the analysis is sound
- **Robust:** Results hold across multiple analytical approaches

### **3. Study Scope**

- 150 branches total (90 switched, 60 did not)
- 17 weeks of data (weeks 6–22)
- Switch occurred at week 15

## **Appendix: Technical Notes**

Analysis used a difference-in-differences regression design, the standard method for evaluating policy changes when some units adopt a treatment and others do not. Models controlled for branch-specific characteristics and weekly variations in staffing and visits. Standard errors were clustered at the branch level to account for repeated observations.
