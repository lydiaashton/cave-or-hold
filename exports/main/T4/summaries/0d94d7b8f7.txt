# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact Analysis**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure did not produce the expected improvement in service scores.** While branches that switched do show higher scores after the change, this difference existed before the switch and reflects pre-existing performance gaps rather than a causal effect of the procedure. We cannot conclude the new procedure improved service quality.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Treatment Branches Were Already Outperforming**
- Before the switch (weeks 6-14), switched branches averaged **2.0 points higher** than non-switched branches
- More critically, this gap was **widening by 0.74 points per week** before any procedure change
- This indicates switched branches were on a stronger improvement trajectory from the start

### **2. No Detectable Change at Implementation**
- At week 15 (when the procedure changed), there was **no jump or discontinuity** in service scores
- The gap between switched and non-switched branches continued its pre-existing pattern
- Statistical test for an immediate effect: **not significant (p=0.47)**

### **3. Post-Switch Performance**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Simple before/after difference | +2.1 points |
| Adjusted for pre-existing trends | Not statistically distinguishable from zero |
| Change in improvement rate | Slowed after switch |

The apparent +2 point advantage in the post period is consistent with the pre-existing trend continuing—not with a new effect from the procedure.

### **4. Why the Initial Analysis Was Misleading**
A straightforward comparison suggests the procedure added ~2 points to service scores. However, this ignores that switched branches were already improving faster. When we account for this pre-existing advantage, **there is no remaining effect attributable to the procedure itself.**

## **Recommendations**

| Priority | Action |
|----------|--------|
| **1** | **Do not proceed with system-wide rollout** – Evidence does not support expected benefits |
| **2** | **Investigate branch selection** – Understand why pilot branches were already outperforming |
| **3** | **Design a randomized trial** – If testing continues, randomly assign branches to eliminate selection bias |
| **4** | **Continue monitoring** – Track current pilot branches for 3-6 more months to detect delayed effects |
| **5** | **Review evaluation methods** – Ensure future pilots include proper baseline comparisons |

---

## **Technical Summary**

- **Data:** 12,053 observations across 709 branches (425 switched, 284 control), weeks 6-22
- **Method:** Difference-in-differences with cluster-robust standard errors and pre-trend controls
- **Key issue:** Violation of parallel trends assumption—treatment and control branches had different trajectories before the intervention
- **Conclusion:** No causal evidence that the procedure improved service scores
