# Board Summary: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

**Date:** November 2025  
**Prepared for:** Library Cooperative Board  
**Analysis Period:** Weeks 6–22 (17 weeks of data)

---

## Bottom Line

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure shows a **positive directional effect** on service scores, but the evidence is **not statistically conclusive**. Branches that adopted the new procedure improved by approximately **2 points** on the service score index, while control branches declined slightly. However, given the variability in weekly scores, we cannot rule out that this result occurred by chance.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **phased expansion** rather than immediate system-wide rollout. Continue monitoring for 8–12 additional weeks to achieve greater statistical certainty before making a final commitment.

---

## Key Findings

### What We Measured

| Group | Branches | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|----------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| **New Procedure** | 20 | 47.52 | 48.95 | **+1.43** |
| **Old Procedure** | 14 | 49.28 | 48.81 | **−0.47** |
| **Net Effect** | — | — | — | **+1.90** |

### Statistical Results

- **Estimated improvement:** +1.93 points (approximately 4% gain)
- **95% confidence interval:** −0.4 to +4.2 points
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.11 (not significant at conventional 5% threshold)

### What This Means

1. **The effect points in the expected direction.** Branches using the new procedure improved while control branches did not—consistent with our hypothesis.

2. **The evidence is not yet definitive.** The confidence interval includes zero, meaning the data are compatible with no effect. More observation time is needed.

3. **Weekly variability is high.** Service scores fluctuate by 8–11 points week-to-week, making it difficult to detect modest improvements with only 8 weeks of post-switch data.

4. **No negative effects detected.** There is no evidence the new procedure harms service quality.

## Recommendations

### Immediate Actions

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| **High** | Expand to 5–10 additional branches | Increases sample size and statistical power |
| **High** | Continue weekly data collection for 8–12 more weeks | Reduces uncertainty around the effect estimate |
| **Medium** | Assess operational costs of the new procedure | Ensure benefits justify any additional expenses |
| **Medium** | Survey branch staff on implementation experience | Capture qualitative feedback on workflow changes |

### Decision Framework

| If future analysis shows... | Then... |
|----------------------------|---------|
| Effect > 2 points, p < 0.05 | Proceed with system-wide rollout |
| Effect 1–2 points, p < 0.10 | Consider targeted rollout to high-volume branches |
| Effect ≈ 0 or negative | Revisit procedure design or discontinue |

---

## Technical Appendix

**Methodology:** Difference-in-differences regression with branch fixed effects, controlling for staffing levels and visitor traffic. Standard errors clustered at branch level.

**Data:** 578 observations (34 branches × 17 weeks)

**Key assumption:** Parallel trends—treatment and control branches would have followed similar trajectories absent the intervention. Pre-switch trends support this assumption.
