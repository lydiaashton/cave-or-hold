# **Memorandum to the Board**

## **Title: Impact of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure on Service Scores**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Contrary to expectations, the new procedure improved service scores.** While we anticipated no change to service quality, branches that adopted the new stock rotation and shelving procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **1.9 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This is a statistically significant, positive surprise. Given the low-cost nature of this operational change and the unexpected benefit, we recommend proceeding with the system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Unexpected Positive Effect on Service Scores
- **Treatment effect:** +1.9 points on the service score index (95% confidence interval: 0.7 to 3.1 points)
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.002
- **Context:** With an average branch score of approximately 50, this represents roughly a **4% improvement**

### 2. Why We Can Trust This Result
The analysis compared 90 branches that switched procedures against 60 that did not, using a difference-in-differences design. Several checks confirm this is a real effect, not a statistical artifact:

- **Parallel trends confirmed:** Before the switch, both groups followed statistically indistinguishable trajectories (p = 0.42). The divergence occurred only after implementation.
- **Effect persisted:** Improvements were observed across most post-switch weeks, not concentrated in a single anomalous period.
- **No confounding changes:** Staffing levels and visit volumes showed no differential changes between groups, ruling out obvious alternative explanations.

### 3. What Happened to Each Group
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 51.2 | 50.2 | **-1.0** |
| Treated (new procedure) | 50.8 | 51.5 | **+0.7** |

Branches using the old procedure experienced a slight decline over this period, while switched branches improved. The new procedure appears to have both **raised scores** and **prevented the degradation** seen in control branches.

### 4. Effect Size in Context
- **Cohen's d = 0.19** (small-to-moderate effect)
- This is meaningful for a procedural change requiring no additional budget or staffing
- The effect is **statistically significant but modest**—consistent with a real operational improvement rather than a transformational change

---

## **Next Steps**

### Recommended Actions

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout**
   - The unexpected benefit, combined with no identified downsides, supports expanding the procedure to all branches

2. **Document and share learnings**
   - Capture implementation details from the 90 early-adopter branches
   - Identify which branches achieved the largest gains and why

3. **Monitor during expansion**
   - Track service scores for 8–12 weeks post-implementation at each new branch
   - Set expectation of ~2-point improvement; investigate branches that deviate significantly

4. **Revisit assumptions**
   - Consider why this procedure affected service scores when we expected no impact
   - Understanding the mechanism could inform future operational decisions

5. **Maintain measurement**
   - Continue collecting weekly service scores to verify the effect persists at scale
   - This provides an opportunity to validate the finding with additional data

---

## **Caveats**

- **Observation window:** We have 8 weeks of post-implementation data. Long-term effects remain unknown.
- **Variation across branches:** Individual branch effects ranged from approximately -3 to +9 points. Not every branch will see the average improvement.
- **External validity:** The effect may differ when rolling out to branches with different characteristics than the early adopters.

---

## **Analysis Notes**

- **Data:** 2,550 weekly observations from 150 branches, weeks 6–22
- **Method:** Difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch)
- **Controls:** Staffing index and visits index included to account for concurrent operational factors
