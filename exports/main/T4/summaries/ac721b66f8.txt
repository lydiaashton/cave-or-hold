# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD OF DIRECTORS**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 2025  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Approve system-wide rollout of the new procedure.** The data show the new stock rotation and shelving procedure **significantly improved service scores** by approximately 2.3 points (p < 0.001). This finding contradicts the initial expectation that the change would have no effect. Branches using the new procedure maintained their service quality while control branches experienced a decline, suggesting the new procedure provides meaningful protection against service degradation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| Branches studied | 150 total (90 treatment, 60 control) |
| Observation period | 17 weeks (weeks 6-22, 2025) |
| Switch date | Week 15 |
| Methodology | Difference-in-differences regression with clustered standard errors |

### Impact on Service Scores

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| **Treatment** (new procedure) | 51.2 | 51.2 | 0.0 |
| **Control** (old procedure) | 51.8 | 49.5 | -2.4 |

**Net treatment effect: +2.3 points** (95% CI: 1.0 to 3.6)

### Statistical Confidence
- **p-value:** 0.0005 (highly significant)
- **Effect size:** 0.23 standard deviations (small-to-medium)
- **Robustness:** Results hold after controlling for staffing levels and patron visits

### What This Means in Practice
- The average branch scores approximately 50 on the service index
- A 2.3-point improvement represents roughly **4-5% better service**
- Treatment branches maintained performance; control branches declined
- The new procedure appears to **buffer against service quality erosion**

---

## **Recommendations**

### Immediate Actions

| Priority | Action | Timeline |
|----------|--------|----------|
| **1** | Approve system-wide rollout to all 60 control branches | Next board meeting |
| **2** | Develop implementation schedule with regional coordinators | 30 days |
| **3** | Prepare training materials and staff guidance | 45 days |

### Monitoring & Evaluation

| Action | Responsibility | Timeline |
|--------|----------------|----------|
| Track service scores at each branch post-switch | Operations Team | Ongoing |
| Report implementation progress to board | Executive Director | Quarterly |
| Conduct follow-up impact evaluation | Data Analysis Team | 6 months post-rollout |

### Additional Considerations

1. **Investigate the control group decline.** Understanding why non-switching branches lost 2.4 points could reveal operational vulnerabilities worth addressing system-wide.

2. **Document implementation costs.** Track staff training hours and any temporary service disruptions during rollout for future cost-benefit analysis.

3. **Gather qualitative feedback.** Survey branch managers and staff about their experience with the new procedure to identify practical improvements.

---

## **Limitations & Caveats**

- Analysis covers 8 weeks post-switch; long-term sustainability of effects is unproven
- Minor pre-existing differences between treatment and control groups were observed
- Service score is one metric; operational costs and staff workload should also be considered

---

## **Conclusion**

The evidence is clear and statistically robust: **the new procedure improves service scores.** Given the cooperative's mission to provide high-quality service across all branches, we recommend proceeding with full implementation while maintaining appropriate monitoring and evaluation.
