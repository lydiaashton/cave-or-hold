# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure  

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure shows a **positive but statistically uncertain effect** on service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure improved by approximately **1.9 points** (about 3.6%) relative to branches that kept the old procedure. However, this improvement is **not statistically significant** at conventional confidence levels (p = 0.13), meaning we cannot confidently rule out that the observed improvement occurred by chance.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **phased rollout** rather than immediate system-wide implementation. Continue monitoring for 8–12 additional weeks to obtain more conclusive evidence before making a final decision.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### Study Overview
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Total branches studied | 34 |
| Branches using new procedure | 20 |
| Branches using old procedure | 14 |
| Observation period | Weeks 6–22 (17 weeks) |
| Procedure switch date | Week 15 |

### Service Score Results

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|----------------|---------------|--------|
| **New Procedure** | 53.31 | 55.33 | +2.02 |
| **Old Procedure** | 46.21 | 46.29 | +0.08 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+1.93** |

### Statistical Assessment

| Measure | Value | Interpretation |
|---------|-------|----------------|
| Estimated effect | +1.93 points | ~3.6% improvement from baseline |
| 95% Confidence interval | -0.56 to +4.42 | True effect could be slightly negative or meaningfully positive |
| P-value | 0.13 | Not statistically significant at 0.05 threshold |
| Parallel trends test | p = 0.84 | ✅ Valid—groups followed similar patterns before the switch |

### What This Means

The data suggest the new procedure **may improve service scores**, but the evidence is not yet strong enough to be certain. The confidence interval indicates the true effect could range from a negligible decline to an improvement of more than 4 points. Additional data collection would narrow this range and support a more confident decision.

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

### Recommended Action: Phased Rollout with Continued Evaluation

| Phase | Timeline | Action |
|-------|----------|--------|
| **Phase 1** | Immediate | Continue monitoring current 20 treatment + 14 control branches |
| **Phase 2** | Weeks 23–30 | Roll out to 5–7 additional branches; collect feedback from staff |
| **Phase 3** | Week 31 | Re-analyze all data; make final system-wide decision |

### Rationale

This approach balances three considerations:

1. **Potential benefit:** If the ~2-point improvement is real, earlier adoption captures value sooner
2. **Risk management:** If the effect proves negligible, limiting exposure reduces wasted effort
3. **Evidence quality:** More weeks of data will produce narrower confidence intervals and a clearer answer

### Additional Recommendations

1. **Gather qualitative feedback** from branch managers on operational ease, staff satisfaction, and any implementation challenges
2. **Track secondary metrics** (staffing efficiency, patron wait times) to capture benefits not reflected in service scores
3. **Establish a decision threshold** (e.g., "proceed if effect exceeds 1.5 points with p < 0.10") before Phase 3 analysis begins

---

## **DECISION OPTIONS FOR THE BOARD**

| Option | Recommendation | Risk Level |
|--------|----------------|------------|
| Full immediate rollout | ❌ Not recommended | High—commits resources before evidence is conclusive |
| Phased rollout with monitoring | ✅ Recommended | Moderate—balances opportunity and caution |

---

## **TECHNICAL NOTES**

- **Method:** Difference-in-differences regression with branch fixed effects
- **Standard errors:** Clustered by branch to account for within-branch correlation
- **Key assumption:** Parallel trends (validated—no differential pre-trends detected, p = 0.84)
- **Confounding checks:** Staffing levels and visitor traffic showed no differential changes between groups
