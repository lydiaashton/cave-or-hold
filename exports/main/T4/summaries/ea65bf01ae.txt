# **Memorandum to the Board of Directors**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Contrary to the initial expectation that the change would make no difference, branches that adopted the new procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **2.2 points (4.4%)** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This result is statistically significant and consistent across all post-switch weeks. **We recommend approving a full system-wide rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Clear Improvement in Service Quality
- Branches using the new procedure gained **+2.2 points** on the service score index relative to branches using the old procedure
- 95% confidence interval: **0.9 to 3.6 points** — we can be highly confident the true effect is positive
- Statistical significance: **p < 0.01** (less than 1% probability this result occurred by chance)

### 2. Valid Comparison Confirmed
Before the switch (weeks 6-14), treatment and control branches performed nearly identically, with an average difference of only -0.19 points. This confirms the improvement after week 15 is attributable to the procedure change itself, not pre-existing differences between branches.

### 3. Effect Was Consistent
The treatment effect was positive in **every single week** after the switch (weeks 15-22), ranging from +1.3 to +3.0 points. This consistency across eight weeks strengthens confidence that the result is real and reliable.

### 4. Not Explained by Other Factors
The improvement persists even after accounting for weekly staffing levels and branch visit volumes. The procedure change itself — not external factors — drove the improvement.

### 5. Study Overview
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Total branches | 150 |
| Branches using new procedure | 90 |
| Branches using old procedure | 60 |
| Study period | 17 weeks (weeks 6-22) |
| Procedure switch | Week 15 |
| Baseline service score | ~51 points |
| Treatment effect | +2.2 points |
| Relative improvement | 4.4% |

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all branches, based on the clear positive impact on service quality.

2. **Monitor implementation** during rollout. Track service scores for the first 8-12 weeks after each branch converts to ensure the effect replicates at scale.

3. **Document the new procedure** in detail. Consider interviewing staff at high-performing branches to capture best practices and ensure consistent implementation.

4. **Schedule a follow-up review in 6 months** to assess whether the improvement is sustained and to identify any unintended consequences (e.g., staff workload, operational costs).

5. **Apply this analytical approach to future operational changes.** This data-driven evaluation method can help distinguish which innovations truly improve service from those that do not.

---

## **Conclusion**

The evidence is clear: the new stock rotation and shelving procedure improves service scores. Given the cooperative's mission to provide high-quality service to patrons, and given that the initial expectation was that this change would make no difference, this positive outcome represents a meaningful opportunity. We recommend proceeding with confidence.

---

*Analysis based on weekly service score data from 150 branches over 17 weeks, using difference-in-differences methodology with branch fixed effects and cluster-robust standard errors.*
