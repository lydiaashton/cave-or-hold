# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Recommend proceeding with system-wide rollout.** Contrary to initial expectations, the new rotation and shelving procedure produced a measurable improvement in service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure improved by **1.8 points** relative to those that kept the old procedure (p < 0.01). This represents approximately a **3.6% improvement** in service quality.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. The Numbers
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| 90 branches (new procedure) | 52.21 | 53.37 | +1.16 |
| 60 branches (old procedure) | 48.73 | 47.98 | −0.75 |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | | **+1.91** |

After statistical adjustment for staffing levels, visitor volumes, and branch-specific factors, the estimated effect is **+1.82 points** (95% confidence interval: 0.75 to 2.88).

### 2. Why We Trust This Result
- **Clean comparison**: 90 branches switched, 60 did not—creating a natural experiment
- **Trends were parallel before the switch**: Both groups moved together in weeks 6–14, validating the comparison
- **Result is statistically robust**: The finding holds across multiple model specifications with p = 0.0008
- **Effect is meaningful**: 1.8 points equals about 18% of one standard deviation in service scores

### 3. What Surprised Us
The analysis was undertaken with the expectation that the procedural change would have **no measurable impact** on service scores. The positive effect was unexpected. This suggests the new procedure may be improving behind-the-scenes efficiency in ways that translate to better patron experience, even though that was not the anticipated mechanism.

---

## **Recommendations**

| Priority | Action |
|----------|--------|
| **1** | **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches |
| **2** | **Document the new procedure** thoroughly to ensure consistent implementation |
| **3** | **Continue monitoring** service scores for 8–12 weeks post-rollout to confirm the effect holds at scale |
| **4** | **Investigate the mechanism**: Consider staff feedback or operational audits to understand *why* the new procedure improved scores |
| **5** | **Capture lessons learned**: This unexpected positive outcome suggests other operational changes may have unanticipated benefits worth measuring |

---

## **Risks and Caveats**

- The effect, while statistically significant, is **modest in absolute terms** (1.8 points on a ~50-point scale)
- Some weekly variation exists; the effect was not uniform across all post-switch weeks
- If implementation quality varies during rollout, the effect could be smaller than observed in this pilot

---

## **Conclusion**

This evaluation tested whether a procedural change would affect service quality. The neutral expectation was not borne out—the new procedure **did improve service scores**. Given the low risk and clear benefit, the board should authorize full implementation.

---

*Analysis based on branches.csv: 150 branches, 17 weeks of data (weeks 6–22), 2,550 observations. Statistical models use cluster-robust standard errors clustered by branch.*
