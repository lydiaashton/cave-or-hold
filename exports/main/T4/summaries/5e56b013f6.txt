# **MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new shelving and rotation procedure improved service scores and should be rolled out system-wide.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **1.8 points** (approximately **3.7% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This finding is statistically reliable and consistent across most branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Measurable Improvement**
- Service scores increased by **1.84 points** after branches switched to the new procedure (95% confidence interval: 0.6 to 3.0 points).
- The improvement is statistically significant (p = 0.003), meaning there is less than a 1% chance this result occurred by random variation.
- The effect persists after accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume, confirming the procedure change itself drove the improvement.

### **2. Real-World Impact**
- **Two-thirds (67%)** of branches that switched showed score improvements.
- Only **40%** of branches keeping the old procedure improved during the same period.
- The average switched branch gained 1.2 points; the average control branch declined by 0.6 points.

### **3. Valid Comparison**
- Before the switch, treatment and control branches followed parallel trends—both groups moved up and down together week-to-week.
- Treatment branches started slightly lower (about 1.5 points), but this gap was stable before the change.
- After week 15, when the switch occurred, the gap closed significantly, indicating the procedure caused the improvement.

### **4. Study Scope**
- **150 branches** participated: 90 adopted the new procedure, 60 kept the old.
- **17 weeks of data** collected (weeks 6–22 of the year).
- **2,550 weekly observations** analyzed using standard causal inference methods.

---

## **Recommendations**

| Priority | Action | Timeline |
|----------|--------|----------|
| **1** | Approve system-wide rollout of the new procedure to all 60 remaining branches | Immediate |
| **2** | Develop implementation schedule with regional coordinators | 30 days |
| **3** | Monitor service scores for 8–12 weeks post-switch at each branch | Ongoing |
| **4** | Investigate why ~33% of switched branches did not improve; provide targeted support | 60 days |

---

## **Caveats and Considerations**

- **Not universal:** About one-third of switched branches did not show improvement. Branch-specific factors (layout, staffing stability, local demographics) may affect outcomes.
- **Magnitude:** A 1.8-point gain is meaningful but modest. The procedure should be viewed as one component of service quality improvement, not a complete solution.
- **Long-term effects:** This analysis covers only 8 weeks post-switch. Continued monitoring will confirm whether gains persist.

---

## **Conclusion**

The evidence supports expanding the new shelving and rotation procedure to all branches. The improvement is clear, statistically reliable, and achieved without additional staffing or resources. We recommend proceeding with the rollout while maintaining data collection to track long-term outcomes and support branches that may need additional assistance during transition.
