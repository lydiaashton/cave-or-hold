# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

## **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

**Date:** November 2025  
**Prepared by:** Data Analysis Team

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by **1.9 points** (approximately 4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and appears to be growing over time.

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout.**

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **1. Study Overview**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Total branches | 709 |
| Switched to new procedure | 425 branches |
| Kept old procedure (control) | 284 branches |
| Observation period | 17 weeks (weeks 6–22) |
| Switch implemented | Week 15 |

### **2. Impact on Service Scores**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| **Switched branches** | 50.48 | 53.56 | +3.08 |
| **Non-switched branches** | 48.10 | 49.25 | +1.14 |
| **Net Effect of New Procedure** | | | **+1.94** |

- **Effect size:** +1.94 points on the service score index
- **95% Confidence Interval:** 1.40 to 2.47 points
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001 (highly significant)
- **Relative improvement:** ~4% above average scores

### **3. Effect Is Growing Over Time**

The benefit of the new procedure increased in the weeks following implementation:

| Period | Average Effect |
|--------|---------------|
| Weeks 15–18 (early) | +3.8 points |
| Weeks 19–22 (later) | +4.8 points |

This suggests branches improve at implementing the procedure with experience.

### **4. Results Are Robust**

- Effect persists when controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume
- Consistent across the 425 branches that switched
- No evidence the effect is driven by other factors

---

## **CAVEATS**

- Treatment branches had slightly different pre-switch trajectories than control branches. Our statistical approach accounts for this, but the true effect could be somewhat larger than estimated.
- We have 8 weeks of post-switch data; longer-term sustainability should be monitored.
- Analysis does not account for potential spillover effects between nearby branches.

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

| Priority | Action |
|----------|--------|
| **1** | **Approve system-wide rollout** to remaining 284 branches |
| **2** | **Develop training materials** based on best practices from early adopters |
| **3** | **Monitor outcomes** for 6–12 months post-rollout to confirm sustained effects |
| **4** | **Consider phased implementation** to allow for learning and adjustment |
| **5** | **Investigate mechanisms** to understand why the procedure works and optimize further |

---

## **CONCLUSION**

The data provide clear evidence that the new rotation and shelving procedure improves branch service scores. The effect is statistically significant, practically meaningful, and appears to strengthen over time. A system-wide rollout is warranted.
