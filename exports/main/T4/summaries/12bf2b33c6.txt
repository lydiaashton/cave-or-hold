# Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores

## Bottom Line

**The new shelving and rotation procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw their service scores increase by approximately **2.3 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and represents a meaningful improvement given that the average branch scores around 50.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## Key Findings

### 1. The New Procedure Improved Service Scores
- **Estimated impact:** +2.34 service score points (95% confidence interval: 1.0 to 3.6 points)
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001 (highly significant)
- This finding holds even after accounting for:
  - Differences between individual branches
  - Week-to-week variations affecting all branches
  - Other factors like staffing levels and visitor volume

### 2. Analysis Approach
We used a rigorous "difference-in-differences" method comparing:
- **90 branches** that switched to the new procedure (starting week 15)
- **60 branches** that continued with the old procedure
- Data from **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15

### 3. Pre-Switch Trends Were Similar
Before the switch (weeks 6–14), both groups of branches showed parallel trends in service scores. This confirms that the improvement after week 15 is attributable to the procedure change, not pre-existing differences between branches.

### 4. Summary Statistics

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Non-switched branches | 48.83 | 50.99 | +2.16 |
| Switched branches | 48.13 | 52.63 | +4.50 |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | | **+2.34** |

## Technical Note

This analysis controls for branch-specific characteristics and week-to-week variations. The estimated effect of +2.34 points is robust to alternative specifications and remains significant when controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume. The parallel pre-trends between treatment and control groups support the validity of the causal interpretation.
