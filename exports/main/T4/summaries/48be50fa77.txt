# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure  

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure shows a **positive but statistically inconclusive effect** on service scores. Switched branches improved by approximately **2 points (4%)** relative to non-switched branches, but this result could plausibly be due to random variation. **We recommend against a full system-wide rollout at this time** and instead propose extending the evaluation period to gather stronger evidence.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Estimated Impact on Service Scores**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated improvement | **+2.0 points** |
| Relative improvement | **+4.1%** of average score |
| Statistical significance | **Not significant** (p = 0.14) |
| 95% Confidence Interval | **-0.6 to +4.7 points** |

The confidence interval indicates the true effect could range from a slight negative impact to a meaningful positive impact of nearly 5 points. We cannot distinguish between these possibilities with the current data.

### **2. What the Data Shows**

| Group | Before Switch (Weeks 6-14) | After Switch (Weeks 15-22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------------|---------------------------|--------|
| **Switched Branches** (20 branches) | 47.9 | 49.6 | +1.7 |
| **Non-Switched Branches** (14 branches) | 51.1 | 50.8 | -0.3 |

The analysis isolates the procedure's effect by comparing these changes, yielding the +2.0 point estimate.

### **3. Why We Cannot Draw a Firm Conclusion**

The primary limitation is **sample size**. With 34 branches and considerable week-to-week variation in service scores (standard deviation ~10 points), the study lacks the statistical power to detect effects smaller than about 3-4 points with confidence. The observed improvement may be:

- A real but modest benefit from the new procedure, **or**
- Random fluctuation unrelated to the procedure change

### **4. Validity Checks**

Our analysis meets key requirements for credible causal inference:

- ✓ **Parallel trends**: Before the switch, both groups showed similar week-to-week patterns
- ✓ **Controls included**: Branch-specific characteristics and seasonal week effects
- ✓ **Appropriate methods**: Difference-in-differences with cluster-robust standard errors

---

## **Recommendations**

### **Immediate Decision**

**Do not proceed with full rollout.** The evidence is suggestive but does not meet the threshold of confidence warranted for a system-wide investment.

### **Next Steps**

1. **Extend the evaluation period** (Priority: High)  
   Continue monitoring both groups for an additional 8-12 weeks. More post-switch data will increase statistical power and clarify whether the effect persists.

2. **Consider phased expansion** (Priority: Medium)  
   If operationally feasible, switch an additional 10-15 branches and compare against the current control group to strengthen the evidence base.

3. **Analyze heterogeneity** (Priority: Medium)  
   Examine whether the procedure works better for certain branch types (size, location, baseline performance). Targeted rollout to high-benefit branches may be warranted even without system-wide evidence.

4. **Conduct cost-benefit analysis** (Priority: High)  
   Even if the 2-point improvement is real, weigh this against implementation costs, staff training time, and operational disruptions before committing to expansion.

---

## **Questions for the Board**

1. What level of confidence is required before approving system-wide implementation?
2. What is the budget available for phased expansion and extended evaluation?
3. Are there specific branch characteristics that should prioritize early adoption if we proceed with phased rollout?
