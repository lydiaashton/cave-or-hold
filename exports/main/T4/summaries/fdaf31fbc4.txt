# **MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **2.35 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant (p < 0.001), and we are 95% confident the true improvement lies between **1.1 and 3.6 points**. Given that the average branch scores around 50, this represents approximately a **4.7% improvement** in service quality.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22 of the year)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **60 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in the switched branches against changes in the non-switched branches

### Results Summary

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches (n=90) | 49.96 | 51.40 | +1.44 |
| Non-switched branches (n=60) | 51.28 | 50.26 | −1.01 |
| **Net effect (DiD)** | | | **+2.35** |

### Statistical Validity
- **Parallel trends confirmed:** Before the switch (weeks 6–14), both groups showed similar trends in service scores (p = 0.82), validating our comparison approach
- **Robust to controls:** The effect remains significant after accounting for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume
- **Consistent over time:** The positive effect was observed in 7 of 8 post-switch weeks

### Additional Observations
- **Staffing matters:** Higher staffing levels were associated with better service scores (p < 0.001)
- **Control group decline:** Non-switched branches experienced a slight decline in scores after week 15, suggesting the old procedure may be becoming less effective over time

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all 60 remaining branches

2. **Develop implementation timeline** with support for branches during the transition period

3. **Monitor post-rollout performance** for at least 8 weeks to confirm the effect persists at scale

4. **Document best practices** from the 90 early-adopter branches to support training

5. **Consider staffing implications:** Since staffing levels correlate with service scores, ensure adequate staffing during the transition

---

## **Appendix: Methodology Notes**

- Primary analysis: Difference-in-differences regression with branch fixed effects and clustered standard errors
- Outcome measure: Service score index (average ~50, higher is better)
- All statistical tests account for repeated observations within branches
- Pre-trend analysis confirms no systematic differences between groups before the intervention
