# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD OF DIRECTORS**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure  

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout.**

The new rotation and shelving procedure **improved service scores by 1.9 points** (approximately 4% above baseline). This improvement is statistically significant, consistent across the post-implementation period, and robust to alternative analytical approaches. The evidence supports expanding the procedure to all 709 branches.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### The New Procedure Works

Branches that adopted the new procedure saw significantly greater improvements in service scores than branches that retained the old procedure:

| Metric | Control Branches (Old Procedure) | Treated Branches (New Procedure) |
|--------|----------------------------------|----------------------------------|
| Average score before switch | 48.9 | 49.6 |
| Average score after switch | 50.9 | 53.5 |
| **Improvement** | +2.0 | **+3.9** |

**Net effect of new procedure: +1.9 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.2 to 2.6)

### The Evidence Is Strong

- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001 (less than 1 in 1,000 chance this result is due to random variation)
- **Practical significance:** 3.8% improvement relative to the system average of 51 points
- **Robustness:** The finding holds when controlling for staffing levels and visitor volumes
- **Sample size:** 709 branches, 12,053 weekly observations, 17 weeks of data

### The Timing Supports Causation

Service scores for treated branches began diverging from control branches precisely when the new procedure was implemented (week 15). Pre-implementation trends between the two groups were not significantly different, supporting the conclusion that the procedure change—not pre-existing differences—drove the improvement.

---

## **CONTEXT**

- **425 branches** (60%) adopted the new procedure starting week 15
- **284 branches** (40%) retained the old procedure, providing a valid comparison group
- Data spans weeks 6–22, with the switch occurring at week 15
- Service scores are calibrated so 50 represents the system average; higher scores indicate better service

## **CONCLUSION**

The data provide clear evidence that the new rotation and shelving procedure improves service quality. The effect is meaningful, statistically reliable, and observed across hundreds of branches over multiple weeks. Rolling out the procedure system-wide is expected to raise average service scores by approximately 2 points across the cooperative, benefiting patrons at all locations.

**We recommend the Board approve immediate system-wide implementation.**
