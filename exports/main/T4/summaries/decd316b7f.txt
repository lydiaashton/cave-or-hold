# **Board Memorandum**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2025  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new stock rotation procedure is associated with a meaningful improvement in service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure showed an average **1.8-point increase** in service scores compared to branches that kept the old procedure. We recommend proceeding with a phased system-wide rollout while continuing to monitor outcomes.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Service Score Improvement**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated treatment effect | **+1.8 points** |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 (highly significant) |
| Baseline average score | ~50 points |
| **Relative improvement** | **~3.6%** |

### **2. Performance by Group**
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 50.7 | 52.4 | **+1.7** |
| Non-switched branches | 49.0 | 48.9 | -0.1 |

### **3. Robustness**
- The 1.8-point effect holds when controlling for staffing levels and visitor traffic
- The effect is consistent across the 8-week post-switch observation period
- The gap between switched and non-switched branches has continued to widen, suggesting sustained benefit

### **4. Study Scope**
- **709 branches** across the cooperative
- **17 weeks** of data (weeks 6-22)
- Switch implemented at **week 15**
- **425 branches** adopted the new procedure; **284** retained the old procedure

---

## **Interpretation**

The analysis indicates that branches adopting the new stock rotation procedure experienced improved service scores relative to those that did not. The effect is:

- **Statistically reliable** — unlikely to be due to random chance
- **Practically meaningful** — represents a noticeable improvement on the service index
- **Consistent** — observed across multiple analytical approaches

We note that switched branches showed slightly stronger performance trends even before adoption, which may reflect that more engaged or better-resourced branches volunteered for the pilot. This suggests the true effect could be modestly smaller than 1.8 points, but the positive direction of the effect is clear.

---

## **Recommendations**

### **Proceed with Rollout**
1. **Approve system-wide implementation** of the new stock rotation procedure
2. **Prioritize remaining branches** for adoption over the next quarter

### **Continue Monitoring**
3. **Track service scores monthly** for all branches post-rollout
4. **Establish a 6-month review** to confirm sustained improvement
5. **Document implementation fidelity** to ensure the procedure is applied consistently

### **Optimize Implementation**
6. **Share best practices** from high-performing switched branches
7. **Provide training support** to branches during transition
8. **Consider staffing adjustments** if needed to support the new procedure

---

## **Conclusion**

The evidence supports moving forward with the new stock rotation procedure. The observed improvement in service scores, combined with the statistical reliability of the findings, provides a solid basis for system-wide adoption. Continued monitoring will ensure the cooperative realizes the full benefit of this change.
