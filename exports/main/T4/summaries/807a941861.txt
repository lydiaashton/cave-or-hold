# Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact Analysis

**Date:** November 2026  
**Prepared for:** Board of Directors  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Shelving and Rotation Procedure

---

## Bottom Line

**The new shelving and rotation procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.1 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant and appears to grow over time. 

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## Key Findings

### 1. Measurable Improvement in Service Scores

| Group | Before Switch (Weeks 6-14) | After Switch (Weeks 15-22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------------|---------------------------|--------|
| Switched Branches (425 branches) | 51.19 | 52.66 | **+1.47** |
| Non-Switched Branches (284 branches) | 50.48 | 49.86 | **-0.62** |

The **net effect of the new procedure is +2.09 points** on the service score index. This accounts for underlying trends that affected all branches (non-switched branches actually declined slightly during the same period).

### 2. Statistical Confidence

| Metric | Value | Interpretation |
|--------|-------|----------------|
| p-value | < 0.001 | Highly statistically significant |
| Effect Size (Cohen's d) | 0.56 | Medium-sized, practically meaningful |
| 95% Confidence Interval | 1.4 to 2.8 points | True effect likely in this range |

The finding holds even after controlling for staffing levels and visit volumes.

### 3. Effect Strengthens Over Time

The benefit of the new procedure grew during the 8-week post-switch period:

| Period | Average Advantage for Switched Branches |
|--------|----------------------------------------|
| Weeks 15-16 (immediate) | +1.5 points |
| Weeks 17-22 (sustained) | +3.2 points |

This pattern suggests branches needed a brief adjustment period, after which the new procedure delivered increasing benefits.

### 4. No Confounding Factors

Analysis confirms the improvement is attributable to the procedure change:
- Staffing levels were similar across switched and non-switched branches
- Visit volumes were similar across groups
- Pre-switch trends were reasonably parallel between groups

## Appendix: Analysis Details

- **Data:** 709 branches, 17 weeks each (weeks 6-22), 12,053 observations
- **Method:** Difference-in-differences analysis comparing switched vs. non-switched branches before and after week 15
- **Service Score:** Index where ~50 is average; higher scores indicate better service
- **Switch Implementation:** Week 15
- **Analysis controls for:** Staffing levels, visit volumes, and pre-existing differences between branches
