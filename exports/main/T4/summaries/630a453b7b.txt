# **Library Branch Stock Rotation Procedure: Impact on Service Scores**

**Prepared for the Board of Directors**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new stock rotation and shelving procedure significantly improved service scores.** Contrary to the expectation of no difference, branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by **1.8 points** (approximately **3.7% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and robust across multiple analytical approaches.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measurable Improvement in Service Quality
- **Treatment effect:** +1.80 points on the service score index (95% confidence interval: 0.61 to 2.99)
- **Statistical significance:** p=0.003 (highly unlikely to occur by chance)
- **Practical significance:** ~3.7% improvement relative to the average branch score of 48

### 2. Strong Causal Evidence
The analysis used a rigorous **difference-in-differences** design comparing:
- **90 branches** that switched to the new procedure (treatment group)
- **60 branches** that kept the old procedure (control group)
- **17 weeks** of data (weeks 6-22), with the switch occurring at week 15

This design isolates the effect of the procedure change from other factors affecting all branches (such as seasonal patterns or system-wide initiatives).

### 3. Valid Comparison Between Groups
Pre-switch data (weeks 6-14) shows **no significant difference** in trends between treatment and control branches (p=0.44). This confirms the two groups were on similar trajectories before the intervention, strengthening confidence that the observed improvement is due to the new procedure.

### 4. Effect Appears Immediately and Persists
The week-by-week analysis shows:
- **Pre-switch (weeks 6-14):** No meaningful differences between groups
- **Post-switch (weeks 15-22):** Consistently positive effects, with the strongest gains in weeks 15-16
- The effect remains significant even after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volumes

### 5. Effect Is Consistent Across Branches
- Treated branches improved by an average of **2.91 points** from before to after the switch
- Control branches improved by **1.11 points** (likely due to seasonal or system-wide factors)
- The **net effect attributable to the new procedure is 1.80 points**

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new stock rotation and shelving procedure to all 150 branches.

2. **Monitor implementation** during the rollout to ensure branches are following the new procedure correctly.

3. **Track service scores** for the next 8-12 weeks post-rollout to confirm the effect persists at scale.

4. **Document best practices** from the 90 early-adopter branches to support training at remaining branches.

5. **Consider cost-benefit analysis** if the new procedure has different resource requirements than the old one (the service score improvement should be weighed against any additional costs).
