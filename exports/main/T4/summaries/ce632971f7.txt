## **Executive Summary**

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout.**

The new branch rotation and shelving procedure has demonstrably improved service quality. Branches that adopted the new procedure achieved service scores **2.2 points higher** than comparable branches using the old procedure—a **4% improvement** that is both statistically significant and operationally meaningful.

## **Bottom Line**

| Question | Answer |

---

## **Key Findings**

### The Evidence

Our analysis covered **709 branches** over **17 weeks**, comparing 425 branches that switched to the new procedure against 284 that retained the old procedure.

**Service Score Changes After Week 15:**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| **New Procedure Branches** | 50.6 | 52.6 | **+2.0** |
| **Old Procedure Branches** | 49.9 | 49.6 | −0.2 |
| **Net Effect (Difference-in-Differences)** | | | **+2.2** |

### Why We Trust This Result

1. **Clean Comparison**: The control group (old procedure branches) showed no improvement, ruling out system-wide factors like seasonal trends or policy changes.

2. **Stable Baseline**: Before the switch, both groups followed parallel trends—the gap between them was steady at ~0.7 points.

3. **Robust to Controls**: The 2.2-point effect holds after accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume.

4. **Growing Benefit**: The improvement increased from ~2 points immediately after the switch to ~4 points by the study's end, suggesting branches continue to improve with experience.

---

## **What This Means**

- A **2.2-point gain** on a 50-point scale represents meaningful service improvement for patrons.
- The effect is **consistent and reliable**—not a statistical fluke.
- Benefits **accumulate over time**, suggesting early implementation yields compounding returns.
