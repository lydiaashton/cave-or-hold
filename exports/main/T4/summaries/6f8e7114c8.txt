# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure produced an unexpected improvement in service scores.** Branches that switched saw their scores increase by **2.1 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This result was not anticipated, as the procedure change was not expected to affect service metrics. Given the unexpected nature of this finding, we recommend a **cautious, phased rollout** with additional monitoring before committing to system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **150 branches** total: 90 switched to the new procedure, 60 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring in week 15
- Analysis compares changes in switching branches against non-switching branches

### **Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Branches that switched | 49.0 | 51.3 | **+2.3** |
| Branches that didn't switch | 49.3 | 49.5 | +0.2 |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | | **+2.1** |

### **Statistical Confidence**
- **Effect size:** +2.13 points (95% confidence interval: +0.9 to +3.4)
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.01
- **Practical significance:** 4.3% improvement relative to the average score of 50

### **Validation Checks**
- **Parallel trends confirmed:** Before the switch, both groups tracked closely with no systematic differences.
- **Sustained effect:** Improvement persisted across all 8 post-switch weeks.
- **Not explained by staffing or visits:** The effect remains after controlling for these factors.

---

## **Important Context**

**This result was unexpected.** The stock rotation and shelving procedure was not designed to influence service scores. Possible explanations include:

- **Unmeasured mechanism:** The new procedure may indirectly improve patron experience in ways not initially recognized.
- **Hawthorne effect:** Branches implementing changes may have increased staff attention or effort.
- **Coincidence:** Though statistically unlikely, the result could reflect unobserved factors.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Pause full rollout** pending further investigation into why an operational change affected service scores.

2. **Conduct qualitative follow-up:** Interview staff at switched branches to identify any unintended changes in workflow, patron interaction, or service delivery.

3. **Extend the observation period:** Continue monitoring to confirm the effect persists beyond the initial 8 weeks.

4. **Pilot at additional branches:** Test the procedure at a small number of new branches to verify the effect replicates.

5. **Document findings:** If a causal mechanism is identified, update expectations and training materials accordingly before broader implementation.

---

*Analysis based on weekly service score data from 150 branches, weeks 6–22. Statistical models control for branch-specific characteristics and week-to-week variation.*
