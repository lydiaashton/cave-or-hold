# MEMORANDUM

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## BOTTOM LINE

The new stock rotation procedure shows a **positive direction of effect** on service scores, but the evidence is **not statistically conclusive**. Branches that adopted the procedure improved by approximately 2 points relative to those that did not, representing a 4% gain. However, due to limitations in the study design and sample size, we cannot rule out that this result occurred by chance. The board should weigh the modest observed improvement against implementation costs and the uncertainty in the evidence.

---

## KEY FINDINGS

### 1. Observed Impact on Service Scores

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| 20 branches that switched | 46.7 | 48.5 | +1.8 |
| 14 branches that did not switch | 50.3 | 49.8 | -0.4 |
| **Net estimated effect** | | | **+2.2 points** |

The new procedure is associated with a **2.2-point increase** in service scores, or approximately **4% improvement** relative to the system average of 48.6.

### 2. Confidence in the Result

| Measure | Value | Interpretation |
|---------|-------|----------------|
| Point estimate | +2.16 points | Best estimate of the effect |
| 95% confidence interval | -0.4 to +4.7 | Range of plausible values |
| P-value | 0.10 | 10% chance of this result if no real effect exists |

**What this means:** The data are consistent with a modest positive effect, but also consistent with no effect at all. Conventional statistical standards (p < 0.05) are not met.

### 3. Study Limitations

**Non-comparable groups:** Branches that switched to the new procedure had systematically lower service scores than those that did not, both before and after the switch. This suggests branches may have been selected for the new procedure precisely because they were underperforming.

**Limited post-switch data:** Only 8 weeks of data are available after the change. Effects may take longer to stabilize or may vary seasonally.

**Weekly variation:** The estimated effect fluctuates across post-switch weeks. Only week 22 shows a statistically significant improvement on its own.

### 4. Consistency Across Analyses

All model specifications produced the same point estimate (+2.16 points), which strengthens confidence in the direction of the effect:

- Basic difference-in-differences: +2.16 (p = 0.10)
- With branch fixed effects: +2.16 (p = 0.10)
- Controlling for staffing and visits: +2.16 (p = 0.11)

---

## RECOMMENDATIONS

### For Board Consideration

| Option | Rationale |
|--------|-----------|
| **Proceed with cautious rollout** | The consistent positive point estimate across all models suggests a real effect may exist. A 2-point improvement across 34 branches could meaningfully improve patron experience. |
| **OR continue monitoring** | Extend data collection for 3–6 more months before deciding. More data would narrow the confidence interval and provide greater certainty. |
| **OR targeted rollout** | Prioritize branches with characteristics similar to the 20 early adopters, particularly those with lower baseline scores. |

### Implementation Considerations

If the board chooses to proceed:

1. **Phase the rollout** over 2–3 months to manage staff training and minimize disruption
2. **Continue tracking service scores** to confirm the effect persists at scale
3. **Document implementation practices** at high-performing branches to identify best practices
4. **Set a review date** (e.g., 6 months post-rollout) to reassess based on new data

---

## CONCLUSION

While the evidence does not meet conventional thresholds for statistical certainty, the **consistent positive signal across all analyses** is encouraging. The new procedure shows promise, with an estimated 4% improvement in service scores. The board must decide whether this potential benefit justifies proceeding despite the remaining uncertainty.
