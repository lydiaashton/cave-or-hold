# **Board Summary: New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The data show a **statistically significant improvement** in service scores at branches that adopted the new procedure (+2.1 points, p < 0.01). However, this finding was **unexpected** given the nature of the change (back-office rotation and shelving processes). Before proceeding with a systemwide rollout, we recommend additional verification to rule out alternative explanations.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Observed Effect
- **Net improvement: +2.1 points** on the service score index
- Treatment branches (90 branches) improved from 49.36 to 50.78
- Control branches (60 branches) declined from 49.55 to 48.87
- The effect is **consistent across all 8 post-switch weeks** (ranging from +1.5 to +2.8 points per week)

### 2. Why This Finding Is Unexpected
- The procedure change affects **back-office operations** (stock rotation and shelving), not direct patron-facing services
- Staffing and visit patterns show **minimal correlation** with service scores (r < 0.05), suggesting the mechanism is unclear
- The cooperative did not anticipate measurable service score impacts from this operational change

### 3. Important Limitations
- **Non-random assignment**: Branches self-selected (or were selected) for the new procedure. Unobserved factors—such as more motivated staff, better management, or other concurrent improvements—could explain the result.
- **Short observation window**: Only 8 weeks of post-switch data; effects may not persist.
- **No clear mechanism**: We cannot yet explain *how* shelving and rotation changes would improve patron-facing service scores.

### 4. Analysis Strengths
- Pre-switch trends were parallel between groups (p = 0.71), supporting the validity of the comparison
- Effect persists after controlling for branch-specific characteristics, staffing levels, and visit volumes

---

## **Next Steps**

### Recommended Approach: **Cautious Expansion with Verification**

1. **Do not proceed with immediate systemwide rollout**. The unexpected nature of this finding warrants further investigation.

2. **Conduct qualitative follow-up**: Interview staff and patrons at high-performing treatment branches to identify what actually changed in the patron experience.

3. **Run a randomized pilot**: Select a new random sample of branches to adopt the procedure, with proper randomization to eliminate selection bias.

4. **Extend monitoring**: Continue tracking current treatment and control branches for an additional 8–12 weeks to confirm the effect persists.

5. **Review service score methodology**: Verify that the service score index appropriately captures patron-facing service quality and is not inadvertently measuring back-office metrics.

---

## **Decision Framework**

| If further investigation shows... | Then... |
|----------------------------------|---------|
| Clear mechanism linking procedure to patron experience | Proceed with rollout |
| Effect was due to selection bias or concurrent changes | Limit or modify the procedure |
| Effect does not persist over time | Do not expand; reconsider the procedure |
