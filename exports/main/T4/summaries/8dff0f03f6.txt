# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores—contrary to initial expectations.** Branches that switched to the new rotation and shelving procedure achieved service scores **2.3 points higher** than comparable branches that kept the old procedure. This represents a meaningful 4% improvement. The evidence supports a system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Clear Positive Effect
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Service score improvement | +2.26 points |
| Relative improvement | +4.4% |
| Statistical confidence | 99.6% (p = 0.004) |
| 95% confidence interval | +0.72 to +3.81 points |

### 2. What the Data Shows
- **90 branches** switched to the new procedure at week 15; **60 branches** continued with the old procedure
- After the switch, non-switched branches saw scores **drop 2.85 points**
- Switched branches saw scores **drop only 0.57 points**
- The new procedure appears to have **protected service quality** during a period when scores were trending down system-wide

### 3. Why We Can Trust This Result
- **Parallel pre-trends**: Before week 15, both groups followed nearly identical trajectories (p = 0.90 for trend difference)
- **Large sample**: 2,550 weekly observations across 150 branches
- **Robust to controls**: The effect persists after accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume
- **Statistically significant**: Less than 1% probability this result occurred by chance

### 4. Unexpected Finding
The board anticipated no measurable impact on service scores. The data shows otherwise. This suggests the new procedure may be delivering benefits we did not anticipate—possibly through faster item retrieval, better shelf organization, or reduced staff time spent searching for materials.

---

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all remaining branches.

## **Appendix: Methodology**

This analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** design, comparing changes in service scores for switched branches against changes for non-switched branches. This approach isolates the procedure's effect from other factors affecting all branches during the same period. Data covers weeks 6–22, with the switch occurring at week 15.
