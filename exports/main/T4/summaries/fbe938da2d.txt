# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Impact of New Branch Shelving and Rotation Procedure on Service Scores

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new shelving and rotation procedure saw service scores increase by **2.1 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant and represents approximately a **4% gain** in service quality. We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### The Numbers

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| **Branches with new procedure** (425 branches) | 51.98 | 53.04 | **+1.06** |
| **Branches with old procedure** (284 branches) | 51.88 | 50.81 | **-1.07** |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | | **+2.13** |

### What This Means

- **Confidence in the result:** We are 95% confident the true effect lies between **1.6 and 2.7 points**. The probability this result occurred by chance is less than 1 in 1,000.

- **Broad-based improvement:** **60%** of branches using the new procedure improved their scores, compared to only **38%** of branches using the old procedure.

- **Results are robust:** The finding holds after accounting for differences in staffing levels and visitor volume, and remains significant using conservative statistical methods.

- **Groups were comparable:** Before the switch, treatment and control branches had nearly identical average scores (51.98 vs. 51.88), supporting the validity of the comparison.

---

## **Recommendation**

**Approve system-wide rollout of the new procedure to all remaining branches.**

The evidence indicates the new procedure delivers meaningful, measurable improvements in service quality with no identified downsides.

## **Appendix: Analysis Summary**

- **Data:** 709 branches, 17 weeks of observations (weeks 6–22), 12,053 total records
- **Method:** Difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors
- **Switch date:** Week 15
- **Control variables:** Staffing index, visits index
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001
