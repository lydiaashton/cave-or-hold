# **Memorandum to the Board of Directors**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure is associated with **improved service scores**, with adopting branches gaining approximately **2 points** (about 4% improvement) relative to branches that kept the old procedure. While this positive effect aligns with expectations, the result does not reach conventional statistical significance due to the limited pilot sample. We recommend **proceeding with a broader rollout accompanied by continued monitoring** to confirm the effect at scale.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Direction of Effect Matches Expectations**
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Branches that switched (n=20) | 48.7 | 50.5 | **+1.8** |
| Branches that did not switch (n=14) | 47.6 | 47.5 | **-0.1** |

**Net effect: +1.9 points** (95% confidence interval: -0.3 to +4.2)

### **2. Majority of Switching Branches Improved**
- **14 of 20 branches (70%)** that adopted the new procedure saw score improvements
- **6 of 20 branches (30%)** saw declines, ranging from -0.6 to -3.6 points
- The strongest performers gained **6 to 8 points**, demonstrating the procedure's potential

### **3. Effect Size Is Meaningful**
- The observed improvement (Cohen's d = 0.57) represents a **medium practical effect**
- For context: a 2-point gain on the service score index is comparable to the difference between an average-performing branch and one in the top quartile

### **4. Why Results Are Not Statistically Conclusive**
- The pilot included only **34 branches** over **17 weeks**
- There is substantial variation in how individual branches responded
- With this sample size, we would need an effect larger than 2 points to achieve statistical significance at the 95% confidence level

### **5. No Pre-Existing Trend Differences**
- Treatment and control branches showed parallel trends before the switch
- The observed improvement is not attributable to pre-existing differences between groups

---

## **Recommendations**

### **1. Approve Phased System-Wide Rollout**
Given the positive direction, meaningful effect size, and operational benefits anticipated from standardized procedures, we recommend **approving the new procedure for all branches**, implemented in phases over the next 6 months.

### **2. Build in Safeguards for Underperforming Branches**
The 6 branches that declined during the pilot warrant attention:
- Provide **enhanced training and on-site support** during transition
- Assign implementation mentors from high-performing pilot branches
- Schedule **30-day check-ins** to identify and address issues early

### **3. Establish Ongoing Monitoring**
- Continue weekly service score tracking for all branches
- Set a **6-month review date** to assess aggregate impact
- Define success criteria in advance (e.g., sustained +1.5 point average improvement)

### **4. Document Best Practices**
- Interview staff at the top-performing pilot branches to capture implementation practices
- Create a quick-reference guide for branches during rollout
- Identify branch characteristics (size, layout, staffing) that predict success

---

## **Conclusion**

The pilot results support moving forward with the new procedure. While the evidence is not statistically definitive, the **positive direction, meaningful effect size, and low risk of harm** justify a broader rollout. With proper training, monitoring, and support for struggling branches, the cooperative can reasonably expect service score improvements across the system.
