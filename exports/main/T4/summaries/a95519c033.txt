# **BOARD SUMMARY: New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure Evaluation**

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure is associated with a **modest improvement** in service scores (+2 points), but the evidence is **not statistically conclusive**. We recommend **continuing to monitor** before committing to a full system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### The Numbers

| What We Measured | Result |
|------------------|--------|
| Branches evaluated | 34 (20 switched, 14 did not) |
| Study period | 17 weeks (switch occurred week 15) |
| Estimated improvement | **+2.0 points** on service score index |
| Statistical confidence | **Not significant** (p = 0.12) |
| Likely range of true effect | **-0.5 to +4.5 points** |

### What This Means

- **Positive signal, not proof**: Branches that adopted the new procedure improved slightly more than those that didn't, but we cannot rule out that this difference occurred by chance
- **The improvement is modest**: Even if real, a 2-point gain is small relative to typical week-to-week variation (scores vary by about 10 points normally)
- **No unintended consequences**: The new procedure did not negatively affect staffing levels or branch visits

---

## **Recommendation**

**Do not proceed with immediate full rollout.** Instead:

1. **Collect 8–12 more weeks of data** to determine whether the positive trend strengthens or fades
2. **Gather staff and patron feedback** on the new procedure—numbers alone may not capture operational benefits
3. **If proceeding, use a phased approach** with continued evaluation at each stage

---

## **Why This Matters**

The board expected the new procedure would improve service scores. The data shows a move in that direction, but not enough evidence to justify the cost and disruption of changing all 34 branches simultaneously. A patient, evidence-based approach reduces the risk of adopting a procedure that may not deliver meaningful benefits.
