# **BOARD MEMORANDUM**

## **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

**Date:** November 2025  
**To:** Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Subject:** Recommendation on System-Wide Rollout of New Procedure

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure works.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure achieved service scores **1.9 points higher** than comparable branches that kept the old procedure. This represents a **4% improvement** and is statistically conclusive. We recommend immediate approval of system-wide rollout.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **1. The Numbers**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Treatment effect** | +1.94 points |
| **Confidence interval** | 1.40 to 2.47 points |
| **Statistical confidence** | >99.9% (p < 0.001) |
| **Branches studied** | 709 total (425 switched, 284 control) |
| **Observation period** | 17 weeks (weeks 6–22) |

### **2. What Happened**

- **Switched branches** improved by **+3.1 points** after adoption
- **Non-switched branches** improved by **+1.1 points** over the same period
- **Net effect of new procedure:** **+1.9 points**

### **3. The Effect Is Growing**

The benefit increased over the 8-week post-switch period:

| Period | Average Effect |
|--------|----------------|
| Weeks 15–18 | +3.8 points |
| Weeks 19–22 | +4.8 points |

This suggests staff proficiency with the new procedure improves with time.

---

## **WHY THIS MATTERS**

- Service scores are a key performance metric for member satisfaction
- A 4% improvement across 709 branches represents substantial cumulative gain
- The effect is robust across different staffing levels and visitor volumes
- No negative unintended consequences detected

---

## **RECOMMENDATION**

**Approve system-wide rollout of the new procedure.**

The evidence is clear, statistically sound, and aligns with operational expectations. The procedure delivers measurable improvement in service quality.

## **APPENDIX: METHODOLOGY**

We used a **difference-in-differences** design, comparing service score changes in switched branches against non-switched branches before and after week 15. Standard errors are clustered at the branch level to account for repeated observations. Results are robust to controls for staffing levels and visitor volume.
