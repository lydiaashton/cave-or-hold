# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores and should be rolled out system-wide.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2 points** (about 4%) compared to branches that retained the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant, consistent across post-switch weeks, and not explained by changes in staffing or visitor volume.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measurable Improvement in Service Quality

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect | +1.93 service score points |
| 95% Confidence interval | +0.70 to +3.16 points |
| Relative improvement | ~4% above baseline |
| Statistical significance | p = 0.002 |

The probability that this result occurred by chance is less than 1 in 100.

### 2. Study Design and Data

- **150 branches** total: 90 switched to the new procedure, 60 did not
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- **2,550 observations** (one per branch per week)
- Analysis used a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in switched branches against changes in non-switched branches
- Standard errors account for repeated measurements at each branch

### 3. Effect Is Direct and Consistent

- The improvement was **not driven by staffing changes or visitor volume**—the procedure itself appears to enhance service quality
- Switched branches outperformed non-switched branches in **every week after the switch** (weeks 15–22)
- Pre-switch trends between the two groups were similar, supporting the validity of the comparison

### 4. Practical Significance

While the board expected no difference, the data show a meaningful gain:
- A 2-point increase on a ~50-point scale represents a noticeable improvement in patron experience
- System-wide rollout would lift average service scores across the cooperative

---

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.** The evidence indicates a real, positive effect on service quality with no identified downsides.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Plan system-wide implementation** with a clear timeline for remaining 60 branches
2. **Develop training materials** based on practices from the 90 early-adopter branches
3. **Monitor service scores** during rollout to confirm expected improvements materialize
4. **Conduct follow-up analysis** after full implementation to assess long-term effects and identify any branch-level factors that influence outcomes
