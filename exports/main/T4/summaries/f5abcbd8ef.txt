# **Memorandum to the Board**

## **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

### **Bottom Line**

Branches that adopted the new stock rotation procedure showed **higher service scores** than those that did not. The estimated improvement is **2.6 points** (approximately 5% above baseline). While this result is **positive and consistent with expectations**, the evidence does not meet the conventional threshold for statistical certainty. The board must decide whether this level of evidence supports a full rollout.

---

### **Key Findings**

**What Changed:**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches (n=20) | 48.6 | 52.1 | **+3.5** |
| Non-switched branches (n=14) | 50.1 | 51.1 | **+0.9** |
| **Net effect (Difference-in-Differences)** | | | **+2.6** |

**Statistical Summary:**

| Measure | Value |
|---------|-------|
| Estimated effect | +2.6 service score points |
| 95% Confidence interval | -0.4 to +5.7 points |
| P-value | 0.09 |
| Effect size | ~5% improvement |

**What Supports the Finding:**

1. **Valid comparison:** Before the switch, both groups followed similar trends, confirming the analysis method is sound.
2. **Consistent direction:** The effect remains positive (~2.6 points) regardless of how we adjust for staffing levels or branch visits.
3. **No negative signals:** No branches showed declines attributable to the new procedure.

**What Limits Confidence:**

1. **Statistical uncertainty:** The 95% confidence interval includes zero, meaning chance cannot be ruled out.
2. **Limited data:** Only 8 weeks of post-switch observations across 34 branches.
3. **Modest magnitude:** Even if real, the improvement is meaningful but not transformative.

---

### **Recommendation**

**Proceed with rollout, with continued evaluation.**

**Rationale:**
- The effect is in the expected direction and of practical significance
- No evidence of harm or negative consequences
- Waiting for stronger statistical certainty would require months of additional data collection, delaying potential benefits
- The risk of a false positive (adopting a procedure that doesn't work) appears low compared to the opportunity cost of delaying improvement

**Implementation Approach:**
1. **Roll out to all branches** over the next quarter
2. **Continue tracking** service scores monthly to confirm the effect materializes at scale
3. **Set a review point** at 6 months post-rollout to reassess whether the expected improvement is being realized system-wide
