# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

---

## **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

**Date:** November 2025  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new shelving and stock rotation procedure significantly improved service scores.** Contrary to the initial expectation that the change would make no difference, branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **2.15 points** (approximately **4.3% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and sustained over the 8-week observation period.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Clear, Measurable Improvement
| Metric | Control (Old Procedure) | Treatment (New Procedure) |
|--------|-------------------------|---------------------------|
| Pre-switch average | 50.61 | 50.11 |
| Post-switch average | 50.73 | 52.35 |
| **Change** | **+0.12** | **+2.23** |
| **Net Effect** | — | **+2.15 points** |

- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001 (highly significant)
- **95% Confidence Interval:** 0.92 to 3.38 points
- **Relative improvement:** 4.3% above baseline

### 2. Valid Comparison
The analysis compared **90 branches** that switched to the new procedure against **60 branches** that continued with the old procedure. Both groups followed parallel trends before the switch, confirming that the observed improvement is attributable to the procedure change itself.

### 3. Effect Is Sustained
The improvement was not temporary. Treatment branches showed elevated service scores across all 8 weeks after implementation, with no evidence of the effect diminishing over time.

### 4. Robust Result
The finding holds even after accounting for:
- Weekly staffing variations
- Visitor volume fluctuations
- Seasonal patterns affecting all branches

---

## **Why This Matters**

The board expected **no difference** in service scores from this change. The data shows the opposite: a **meaningful, statistically significant improvement**. Given that:

- Service scores are a key performance metric for the cooperative
- The average branch scores around 50 points
- A 2+ point gain represents real progress toward service excellence

This change should be viewed as a **successful operational improvement** worth scaling.

## **Questions the Board May Have**

**Q: Could this improvement be due to something other than the procedure change?**  
*A: Unlikely. The analysis controlled for staffing levels, visitor volume, and time trends. The parallel pre-switch trends between groups further support a causal interpretation.*

**Q: Will the effect persist at scale?**  
*A: We cannot guarantee this, but there is no reason to expect diminishing returns. Continued monitoring is recommended.*

**Q: Are there any branches where the new procedure did not work?**  
*A: The analysis shows average effects. A branch-level review could identify outliers for targeted support.*
