# **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative
**Date:** November 2026
**Subject:** Recommendation on System-Wide Rollout of New Branch Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure is **associated with improved service scores**, but the evidence is not strong enough to justify an immediate system-wide rollout. Switched branches gained an estimated **1.5 points more** than non-switched branches (approximately 3% improvement). However, this effect is **not statistically distinguishable from zero** at conventional confidence levels. We recommend a **controlled expansion** with continued evaluation rather than full implementation at this time.

---

## **Key Findings**

### What We Measured
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure in week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Weekly service scores tracked for **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- Average service score across all branches: ~50 (higher is better)

### Results Summary

| Metric | Switched Branches | Non-Switched Branches |
|--------|------------------|----------------------|
| Average score before | 47.3 | 48.0 |
| Average score after | 51.6 | 50.8 |
| **Improvement** | **+4.3** | **+2.8** |
| **Net effect** | | **+1.5 points** |

### What This Means
- **Positive direction:** The effect points in the expected direction—switched branches improved more than non-switched branches
- **Uncertain magnitude:** The 95% confidence interval ranges from **-0.6 to +3.7 points**, meaning the true effect could be negligible or moderately positive
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.16 (conventional threshold is p < 0.05)
- **Pre-trends validated:** Branches had similar trajectories before the switch, supporting the validity of the comparison

### Branch-Level Performance
- **90%** of switched branches (18 of 20) showed improvement
- **79%** of non-switched branches (11 of 14) also improved
- **Notable variation:** Some switched branches gained up to 9.9 points; two experienced declines

---

## **Why the Results Are Inconclusive**

1. **Limited sample size:** Only 34 branches over 17 weeks provides modest statistical power
2. **Both groups improved:** Service scores rose across the cooperative, making it harder to isolate the procedure's specific contribution
3. **Natural variation:** Week-to-week score fluctuations are substantial relative to the observed effect

---

## **Recommendations**

### 1. **Do Not Proceed with Full Rollout (Yet)**
The current evidence does not meet the standard for a costly system-wide change. The observed improvement, while positive, could plausibly be due to chance.

### 2. **Expand the Pilot Strategically**
- Add 10–15 more branches to the treatment group
- Maintain at least 10 branches as controls
- Continue weekly tracking for an additional 12–16 weeks

### 3. **Investigate Variation Across Branches**
Analyze whether certain branch characteristics (size, staffing levels, patron volume) predict stronger responses to the new procedure. This could enable **targeted implementation** where the procedure is most effective.

### 4. **Broaden Outcome Measures**
The service score is one metric. Consider adding:
- Patron satisfaction surveys
- Staff time/cost measures
- Circulation or visit metrics

### 5. **Revisit Decision in Q2 2027**
With expanded data, we can achieve sufficient statistical power to detect effects as small as 1 point with 80% confidence.

---

## **Conclusion**

The new procedure shows **promise but not proof**. The board's instinct that this would help service scores is directionally correct—the data points positive. However, responsible stewardship requires stronger evidence before committing cooperative-wide resources. A measured expansion with rigorous tracking will provide the clarity needed for a confident full rollout decision.
