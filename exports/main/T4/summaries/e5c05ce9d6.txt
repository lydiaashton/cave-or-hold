# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD OF DIRECTORS**

**Subject:** Impact of New Stock Rotation Procedure on Library Service Scores  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Analysis Period:** Weeks 6–22 (17 weeks of data from 150 branches)

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new rotation and shelving procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by **1.8 points** (approximately 3.5%) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant and represents a meaningful gain in service quality. **We recommend approving system-wide rollout to all remaining branches.**

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### 1. Clear Positive Impact on Service Quality

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Treatment branches (new procedure) | 51.2 | 52.4 | +1.2 |
| Control branches (old procedure) | 51.9 | 51.3 | −0.6 |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | | **+1.8** |

### 2. Statistically Reliable Results

- **Confidence level:** 95% confident the true effect is between 0.65 and 2.93 points
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.002 (less than 1% probability this result occurred by chance)
- **Effect size:** 3.5% improvement relative to average baseline score of 51.7

### 3. Valid Comparison Confirmed

- Pre-switch trends were parallel between treatment and control groups (p = 0.92), validating the comparison methodology
- All 150 branches contributed data to both pre- and post-switch periods
- The improvement is specific to service quality—staffing levels and visitor volumes remained stable across both groups, indicating the gain reflects genuine operational improvement

### 4. Strong Evidence Base

- **Sample size:** 2,550 weekly observations across 150 branches
- **Treatment group:** 90 branches adopted the new procedure
- **Control group:** 60 branches retained the old procedure
- **Switch implementation:** Week 15

---

## **RECOMMENDATIONS**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all 60 branches currently using the old method

2. **Develop implementation timeline** with target completion within the next quarter

3. **Create training materials** documenting best practices from the 90 early-adopter branches

4. **Establish monitoring protocol** to track service scores during rollout and confirm expected benefits are realized

5. **Schedule follow-up review** in 6 months to assess sustained impact and address any implementation challenges

---

## **CONCLUSION**

The evidence strongly supports expanding the new procedure across the entire library cooperative. The 1.8-point improvement in service scores represents a meaningful enhancement to patron experience, achieved without additional staffing or changes in visitor volume. Proceeding with system-wide rollout is the data-driven choice.
