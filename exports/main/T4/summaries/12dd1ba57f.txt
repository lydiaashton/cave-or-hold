# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative
**Date:** November 2026
**Subject:** Recommendation on System-Wide Rollout of New Branch Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Proceed with system-wide rollout.** Despite initial expectations that the new procedure would not affect service scores, the data shows a clear and statistically significant **positive impact of +2.1 points** on the service score index. Branches that adopted the new procedure improved while control branches remained flat, producing an unexpected but welcome gain in service quality.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **The Numbers**

| Metric | Control (Old Procedure) | Treatment (New Procedure) |
|--------|------------------------|---------------------------|
| Pre-switch average score | 48.92 | 48.64 |
| Post-switch average score | 48.59 | 50.40 |
| **Change** | **-0.34** | **+1.76** |
| **Net Effect** | | **+2.10 points** |

### **Statistical Confidence**
- **Effect:** +2.10 points on service score index (approximately 4% improvement)
- **Confidence Interval:** +0.84 to +3.36 points
- **Significance Level:** p < 0.01 (99% confidence the effect is real)
- **Sample:** 150 branches over 17 weeks (2,550 observations)

### **Why We Trust This Result**

1. **Parallel pre-trends:** Before the switch, both groups moved together with no significant differences. The divergence began only after Week 15.

2. **Robust to controls:** The effect holds after accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume—this is not simply busier or better-staffed branches adopting the change.

3. **Consistent post-switch pattern:** The treatment group outperformed the control group in every single week after implementation.

4. **Rigorous methodology:** Difference-in-differences analysis with cluster-robust standard errors isolates the causal effect of the procedure change.

---

## **What This Means**

The cooperative implemented this change primarily to improve operational efficiency in stock rotation and shelving. **Service score impact was not an expected outcome.** However, the data now shows that the new procedure has delivered a secondary benefit: measurably better service as experienced by patrons.

This finding strengthens the business case for rollout. The cooperative gains both the intended operational improvements and an unexpected boost to service quality.

---

## **Recommendations**

| Action | Priority | Timeline |
|--------|----------|----------|
| **Approve system-wide rollout** to remaining 60 branches | High | Immediate |

| Continue monitoring service scores during rollout | Medium | Ongoing |
| Document operational cost savings from new procedure | Medium | 4-8 weeks |
| Share findings with branch managers to build support | High | Before rollout |

---

## **Risks and Mitigation**

| Risk | Likelihood | Mitigation |
|------|------------|------------|
| Effect does not replicate at scale | Low | Continue monitoring; effect has been consistent across 90 diverse branches |
| Implementation challenges at remaining branches | Medium | Use early adopters as mentors; phased rollout |
| Staff resistance to change | Medium | Communicate positive service score results; involve staff in training design |

---

## **Conclusion**

This evaluation produced a surprising but favorable result. A procedure change implemented for operational reasons has demonstrably improved service quality. The board should approve system-wide rollout with confidence that the new procedure benefits both branch operations and patron experience.
