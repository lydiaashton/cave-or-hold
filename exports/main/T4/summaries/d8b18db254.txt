# **Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure Evaluation**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores as expected.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw their service scores increase by **1.9 points** (approximately 4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This result is statistically significant and consistent across different branch types.

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. The New Procedure Delivered Measurable Improvement

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Treatment effect | **+1.89 points** on service score index |
| 95% Confidence interval | 1.33 to 2.45 points |
| Percentage improvement | **3.8%** from baseline |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 (highly significant) |

### 2. Clear Evidence from the Data

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches (425 branches) | 50.24 | 51.39 | **+1.15** |
| Non-switched branches (284 branches) | 49.86 | 49.12 | **-0.75** |
| **Net effect** | | | **+1.89** |

The control group experienced a slight decline during the same period, while switched branches improved—indicating the new procedure helped branches maintain and enhance service quality.

### 3. Benefits Across All Branch Types

The improvement held consistently regardless of branch characteristics:

- **High-staffing branches:** +2.0 points
- **Low-staffing branches:** +1.8 points  
- **High-visit branches:** +1.5 points
- **Low-visit branches:** +2.2 points

This suggests the procedure is robust and adaptable to different operational contexts.

### 4. Rigorous Analysis Approach

We applied a **difference-in-differences** design—the standard method for evaluating policy changes with partial rollout:

- Compares switched vs. non-switched branches before and after the change
- Controls for pre-existing branch differences and overall time trends
- Uses cluster-robust standard errors (accounting for repeated observations per branch)
- **Data:** 709 branches, 17 weeks, 12,053 observations

---

## **Next Steps**

### Immediate Actions

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** to the remaining 284 branches currently using the old procedure.

2. **Document implementation best practices** from the 425 early-adopter branches to support training and onboarding.

3. **Establish ongoing monitoring** with monthly service score tracking. Target: maintain the ~2-point improvement during and after rollout.

### Follow-Up Considerations

4. **Conduct a cost-benefit analysis** once implementation costs are fully quantified. The 4% service improvement should be weighed against training, staffing, and operational expenses.

5. **Investigate the mechanism**—understanding *why* the procedure works (e.g., faster restocking, better organization, reduced staff time) could help optimize training and identify additional improvement opportunities.

6. **Track long-term sustainability** beyond the initial 8-week post-switch window to ensure effects persist.
