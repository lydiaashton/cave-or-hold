# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Library Cooperative Board  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 2025  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Executive Summary**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure shows a **positive but modest improvement** in branch service scores. Branches that switched to the new procedure maintained their performance while control branches experienced a decline. The estimated benefit is **+2.1 points** (approximately 4% improvement), though this effect falls just short of conventional statistical significance thresholds.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rollout to remaining branches, with continued monitoring to confirm effects persist over time.

---

## **Bottom Line**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did the new procedure help? | **Yes, modestly** |
| How much improvement? | **+2.1 points** on service score (~4%) |
| How confident are we? | **90% confidence** the effect is real (p = 0.10) |
| Should we roll out systemwide? | **Yes, with monitoring** |

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
- **34 branches** tracked over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22 of the year)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure at week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Rigorous difference-in-differences analysis with branch-level controls

### Impact on Service Scores

| Period | Treatment Branches | Control Branches |
|--------|-------------------|------------------|
| Before switch (weeks 6–14) | 52.2 | 51.9 |
| After switch (weeks 15–22) | 52.5 | 50.3 |
| **Change** | **+0.3** | **-1.6** |

**Net effect of new procedure: +2.1 points** (treatment branches held steady while control branches declined)

### Statistical Evidence

| Metric | Value | Interpretation |
|--------|-------|----------------|
| Treatment effect | +2.07 points | Branches switching can expect this improvement |
| P-value | 0.10 | 90% confidence the effect is real |
| 95% Confidence interval | -0.4 to +4.5 points | True effect likely in this range |
| Effect size | 0.22 SD | Small-to-moderate practical significance |

### Why We're Confident in the Design

✓ **Parallel trends verified**: Treatment and control branches moved together before the switch (p = 0.46)  
✓ **Branch differences accounted for**: Analysis controls for inherent differences between branches  
✓ **Other factors controlled**: Staffing and visit levels included in the model  
✓ **Staffing effect confirmed**: Higher staffing correlates with higher scores (p < 0.01), validating our measures  

---

## **What This Means Practically**

A branch adopting the new procedure can expect:
- Service scores to **increase by approximately 2 points**
- This represents a **4% improvement** relative to typical branch performance
- The benefit appears to come from **preventing decline** rather than driving dramatic gains
- Effects are **visible within the first 8 weeks** after switching

---

## **Recommendations**

### Immediate Actions
1. **Approve systemwide rollout** to remaining 14 branches
2. **Stage the rollout** over 4–6 weeks to allow for support and troubleshooting
3. **Prioritize branches** with lower current service scores for early adoption

### Monitoring Plan
4. **Track service scores weekly** for 12 weeks post-switch at each branch
5. **Flag any branch** where scores drop more than 3 points after switching
6. **Report back to the Board** in 6 months with full rollout results

### Additional Considerations
7. **Review staffing levels** at branches showing weaker effects—adequate staffing may be necessary to realize full benefits
8. **Document the procedure** thoroughly to ensure consistent implementation across all branches

---

## **Caveats and Limitations**

- The effect is **positive but not definitive** at the 95% confidence standard (our threshold for "statistical significance")
- We have only **8 weeks of post-switch data**; long-term sustainability is unproven
- **External factors** (seasonal patterns, community events) could influence scores in either direction
- The **control group declined** during the study period; part of the measured benefit may reflect problems with the old procedure rather than gains from the new one

---

## **Conclusion**

While the improvement is more modest than some may have hoped, the evidence points in a **clear positive direction**. The new procedure helps branches maintain service quality during a period when branches using the old procedure saw declines. Given the low cost of implementation and the absence of negative effects, a **systemwide rollout is warranted**.

Continued monitoring will allow us to confirm these results and refine the procedure as needed.
