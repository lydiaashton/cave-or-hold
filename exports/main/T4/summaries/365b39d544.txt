# MEMORANDUM

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2025  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## Executive Summary

**The new shelving and stock rotation procedure significantly improved service scores and should be rolled out to all branches.**

Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by **2.1 points** (approximately 4%) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This result is highly statistically significant and represents a meaningful improvement in patron service quality.

---

## Background

In week 15 of this year, 425 of our 709 branches switched to a new procedure for rotating and shelving stock. The remaining 284 branches continued with the old procedure. We analyzed weekly service scores from weeks 6–22 to evaluate the impact of this change.

---

## Key Findings

### 1. The New Procedure Improved Service Scores

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated improvement | **+2.1 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | 1.5 to 2.7 points |
| Statistical Significance | p < 0.001 (highly significant) |
| Relative Improvement | **4.2%** above baseline |

### 2. Switched Branches Outperformed Control Branches

| Group | Branches | Avg. Change | % Improved |
|-------|----------|-------------|------------|
| **Switched branches** | 425 | +0.67 points | 59% |
| **Control branches** | 284 | -1.47 points | 33% |

### 3. The Benefit Grew Over Time

The gap between switched and control branches widened steadily after the switch:

| Period | Average Gap |
|--------|-------------|
| Before switch (weeks 6–14) | 0.3 points |
| After switch (weeks 15–22) | 2.4 points |
| Week 15 (first week) | 1.8 points |
| Week 22 (most recent) | 3.4 points |

### 4. The Procedure Protected Against a System-Wide Decline

Control branches experienced a notable decline in service scores after week 14. The new procedure appears to have helped switched branches not only avoid this decline but achieve actual improvement.

### 5. Analysis Is Statistically Sound

Our difference-in-differences methodology meets standard validity requirements. Pre-switch trends were parallel between groups (p = 0.23), confirming the comparison is appropriate.

---

## Recommendation

**We recommend immediate rollout of the new procedure to all remaining 284 branches.**

The evidence is clear and robust:
- ✅ Statistically significant positive effect
- ✅ Meaningful magnitude (4% improvement)
- ✅ Effect grew over the observation period
- ✅ Protected against system-wide score decline

---

## Implementation Considerations

1. **Not all branches benefited equally.** About 41% of switched branches did not see improvement. We recommend investigating branch-level factors (staffing levels, training quality, physical layout) that may affect implementation success.

2. **Continue monitoring.** Extend data collection to confirm the effect persists and identify any long-term trends.

3. **Capture best practices.** Document implementation approaches from high-performing switched branches to support other branches during rollout.

---

## Appendix: Methodology

We used a difference-in-differences regression model with branch fixed effects and controls for staffing and visit indices. Standard errors were clustered by branch to account for within-branch correlation. The analysis included 12,053 observations (709 branches × 17 weeks).

**Treatment effect estimate:** 2.10 points (SE: 0.29, p < 0.001)  
**Model R-squared:** 0.63 (with branch fixed effects)
