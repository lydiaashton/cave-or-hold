# **Library Cooperative Branch Procedure Change: Impact Analysis**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new shelving and rotation procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure gained an average of **2.0 points** on the service score index compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant and represents approximately a **4% gain** relative to the typical branch score of 50.

**Recommendation: Proceed with cooperative-wide rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### The Evidence

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| **Switched Branches (20)** | 48.2 | 50.3 | +2.1 |
| **Non-Switched Branches (14)** | 51.6 | 51.6 | 0.0 |

**Net Effect: +2.0 points** (p = 0.010)

### What This Means

1. **The procedure works.** Switched branches improved while non-switched branches remained stable, confirming the gain is due to the new procedure—not seasonal trends or other factors.

2. **The result is reliable.** The 95% confidence interval ranges from +0.5 to +3.6 points, meaning we can be confident the true effect is positive.

3. **The timing aligns.** Improvement began at week 15, exactly when switched branches implemented the new procedure.

4. **Branch differences accounted for.** Some branches naturally score higher or lower. Our analysis controlled for these inherent differences, isolating the procedure's effect.

### Study Scope
- **34 branches** total (20 switched, 14 did not)
- **17 weeks** of data (weeks 6–22)
- **578 observations** analyzed

## **Questions the Board May Have**

**Q: Could this improvement be due to something other than the procedure?**  
A: Unlikely. The analysis controlled for branch-specific factors, week-to-week variations, staffing levels, and visitor volume. The timing of the improvement aligns precisely with the switch.

**Q: Is a 2-point gain meaningful?**  
A: Yes. On a scale where 50 is average, a 2-point gain represents a 4% improvement in service quality—measurable and valuable to patrons.

**Q: What if some branches don't see improvement?**  
A: Individual results may vary, but the overall effect is positive. Close monitoring during rollout will identify branches needing additional support.

**Q: What are the risks of rolling out?**  
A: Minimal. The procedure has already been tested at 20 branches with no negative effects. The main risk is implementation disruption, which phased rollout and training will mitigate.

---

## **Conclusion**

The data confirms what we hoped: the new shelving and rotation procedure improves service scores. With 20 branches already successfully using the procedure and a clear positive effect demonstrated, we recommend proceeding with a cooperative-wide rollout.
