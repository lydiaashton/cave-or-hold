# **Evaluation of New Shelving Procedure on Library Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving procedure shows a **positive effect** on service scores, with switching branches improving by approximately **2 points** more than non-switching branches. While this result does not reach conventional statistical significance, the evidence points in the expected direction. Given the operational investment already made and the absence of any negative signal, we recommend **proceeding with a phased rollout** to all branches while continuing to monitor outcomes.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **17 branches** adopted the new procedure at week 15; **17 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Analysis compares changes in service scores between the two groups before and after the switch

### **Service Score Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Kept Old Procedure | 47.2 | 48.9 | +1.7 |
| Switched to New | 51.4 | 55.1 | +3.8 |
| **Net Effect of New Procedure** | | | **+2.0** |

### **Statistical Assessment**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect | +2.03 points |
| p-value | 0.12 |
| 95% confidence interval | -0.55 to +4.61 points |

**What this means:** The data are most consistent with a modest positive effect. We cannot rule out no effect, but we can be reasonably confident the effect is not meaningfully negative. The upper bound of the interval (+4.6 points) represents a substantively meaningful improvement.

### **Supporting Evidence**

1. **Consistent direction:** Every post-switch week shows the switching branches outperforming non-switching branches by a wider margin than in the pre-switch period.

2. **No adverse signals:** There is no evidence the new procedure harmed service scores at any branch or in any week.

3. **Practical significance:** A 2-point improvement on a ~50-point scale represents approximately a **4% gain** in service quality—operationally meaningful for a cooperative of this size.

---

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with rollout to all branches**, with the following conditions:

1. **Phased implementation:** Complete the rollout over 4–6 weeks to allow for training and adjustment.

2. **Continued monitoring:** Track service scores for at least 8 more weeks post-rollout to confirm the effect stabilizes or strengthens with broader adoption.

3. **Implementation support:** Provide additional training to branches that showed smaller gains in the pilot period.

4. **Re-evaluation checkpoint:** Report back to the board after week 30 with updated analysis. If the effect remains positive but insignificant, consider whether the operational benefits (e.g., staff efficiency, patron feedback) justify continuation.

---

## **Why This Recommendation**

| Consideration | Assessment |
|---------------|------------|
| Direction of effect | ✓ Positive |
| Statistical certainty | ⚠ Moderate (p = 0.12) |
| Risk of harm | ✓ None detected |
| Operational feasibility | ✓ Already implemented at 17 branches |
| Cost of reversal | High (disruption, retraining) |
| Cost of proceeding | Low (continuation of current trajectory) |

The balance of evidence favors proceeding. The absence of statistical significance at the 5% level reflects limited data (17 weeks, 34 branches) rather than evidence of no effect. Waiting for definitive statistical proof would delay benefits without reducing risk.

---

## **Technical Appendix**

- **Method:** Difference-in-differences regression with branch and week fixed effects
- **Standard errors:** Clustered at branch level to account for within-branch correlation
- **Controls:** Staffing index and visits index (neither alters the treatment estimate materially)
- **Software:** Python (statsmodels, linearmodels)
