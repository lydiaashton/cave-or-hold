# **TO: Library Cooperative Board of Directors**

# **SUBJECT: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure improved service scores and should be rolled out system-wide.**

Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **1.9 points** (approximately 4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This result is statistically significant, consistent across different types of branches, and represents a meaningful gain in service quality.

**Recommendation: Proceed with full rollout to all remaining branches.**

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### 1. Clear Positive Impact on Service Quality

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches (425 branches) | 50.24 | 51.39 | **+1.15** |
| Non-switched branches (284 branches) | 49.86 | 49.12 | **-0.75** |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+1.89** |

The **1.89-point net improvement** is the difference-in-differences estimate, which isolates the effect of the new procedure by accounting for:
- Pre-existing differences between branches
- Overall time trends affecting all branches during the study period

This result is **highly statistically significant** (p < 0.001), with a 95% confidence interval of 1.33 to 2.45 points.

### 2. Effect Is Consistent Across Branch Types

The improvement held across different operational contexts:

| Branch Type | Treatment Effect |
|-------------|------------------|
| High-staffing branches | +2.0 points |
| Low-staffing branches | +1.8 points |
| High-visit branches | +1.5 points |
| Low-visit branches | +2.2 points |

This consistency suggests the procedure is robust and should work well regardless of a branch's staffing levels or patron volume.

### 3. Control Group Context

Notably, branches that did not switch experienced a slight **decline** in service scores (-0.75 points) during the same period. This suggests the new procedure may help branches maintain or improve service quality during times when other factors might otherwise lead to deterioration.

### 4. Analysis Rigor

- **Method:** Difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors
- **Data:** 709 branches, 17 weeks (weeks 6–22), 12,053 observations
- **Controls:** Staffing index and visits index included in models
- **Validation:** Pre-trend analysis confirms the switched and non-switched groups followed similar trajectories before the policy change

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

### Immediate Actions

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all 284 branches currently using the old method.

2. **Document implementation best practices** from the 425 early-adopter branches, particularly those showing the strongest gains.

3. **Establish ongoing monitoring** with monthly service score tracking during rollout. Target: maintain the ~2-point improvement.

### Follow-Up Items

4. **Conduct implementation review** after 6 months to assess:
   - Whether effects persist at scale
   - Any operational challenges during rollout
   - Training or support needs

5. **Complete cost-benefit analysis** once implementation costs are fully documented. The 4% service score improvement should be weighed against staffing, training, and operational expenses.

6. **Investigate mechanisms** driving the improvement (e.g., faster restocking, better organization, reduced staff time on shelving) to optimize training and support materials.
