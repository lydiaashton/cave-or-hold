# **Memorandum to the Board of Directors**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new stock rotation and shelving procedure improved service scores by approximately 2.2 points (about 4%). This improvement is statistically significant.** We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
- **34 branches** were studied over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15
- **14 branches** continued with the old procedure (serving as a control group)
- This design allows us to isolate the effect of the new procedure from other factors

### Service Score Results

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|----------------|---------------|--------|
| Branches with new procedure | 50.8 | 53.9 | +3.2 |
| Branches with old procedure | 52.2 | 53.2 | +1.1 |
| **Net effect attributable to new procedure** | | | **+2.1** |

### Statistical Confidence
- **Estimated improvement: +2.2 points**
- **95% confidence interval: 0.7 to 3.7 points**
- **Statistical significance: p < 0.01** (highly reliable result)
- **Relative improvement: approximately 4%** over the baseline service score

### Validity Check
Branches that switched and those that did not showed **parallel trends before the switch** (p = 0.93), confirming that the two groups were comparable and strengthening confidence in our findings.

### Additional Notes
- The improvement was not driven by changes in staffing levels or visitor patterns
- Both groups showed some improvement over time, but branches with the new procedure improved significantly more

---

## **Next Steps**

### Primary Recommendation
**Approve system-wide rollout** of the new stock rotation and shelving procedure to all 34 branches.

### Implementation Plan
1. **Expected benefit:** Service scores should improve by approximately 2 points across all branches once the new procedure is fully implemented
2. **Phased rollout:** Consider implementing in stages to ensure training quality and operational capacity
3. **Continued monitoring:** Track service scores for at least 8–12 weeks post-rollout to confirm the effect holds at scale
4. **Staff training:** Ensure consistent implementation across all branches to achieve the full benefit

### Considerations
- The effect size is modest but meaningful and statistically reliable
- Implementation costs should be weighed against the expected 4% service improvement
- Long-term effects beyond the 8-week observation period should be monitored
