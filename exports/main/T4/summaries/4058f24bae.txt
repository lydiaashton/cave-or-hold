# **Board Summary: Impact of New Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure on Service Scores**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores—an unexpected but welcome result.** While we anticipated the shelving and stock rotation changes would have no effect on service quality, branches that switched saw their scores increase by approximately **2 points** relative to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and robust across multiple tests. 

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout.** The new procedure delivers operational benefits without the service-quality trade-off we had anticipated—and actually produces a modest service improvement.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Positive Effect Despite Neutral Expectations
| Metric | Result |
|--------|--------|
| **Estimated effect** | +2.05 points on service score index |
| **95% Confidence interval** | 0.8 to 3.3 points |
| **Statistical significance** | p = 0.001 |
| **Effect size** | 0.21 standard deviations |

This exceeds our expectation of zero impact. The improvement is small-to-moderate but meaningful and consistent.

### 2. Clear Pattern in the Data
| Group | Before Switch (Weeks 6-14) | After Switch (Weeks 15-22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------------|---------------------------|--------|
| Switched branches (90 branches) | 51.42 | 52.55 | **+1.14** |
| Control branches (60 branches) | 52.12 | 51.20 | **-0.91** |

Switched branches improved while control branches slightly declined, yielding a net effect of **+2.05 points**.

### 3. Branch-Level Results Are Consistent
- **61% of switched branches** showed improvement after adopting the new procedure
- Only **40% of control branches** improved during the same period
- The effect is not driven by a few outlier branches

### 4. Analysis Is Methodologically Sound
- **Pre-trend validation**: Before week 15, switched and control branches followed parallel trends (no significant difference), supporting the validity of our comparison
- **Robust to controls**: Effect persists after accounting for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume
- **Appropriate error handling**: Standard errors account for repeated observations per branch

### 5. Why Might This Have Happened?
While we did not hypothesize a service-score effect, possible explanations include:
- **Improved findability**: Better stock rotation may help patrons locate materials faster
- **Staff efficiency**: The new procedure may free up staff time for patron assistance
- **Morale effect**: Staff working with a more organized system may provide better service

We recommend gathering qualitative feedback from branch staff to understand the mechanism.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** to all 60 branches currently using the old procedure.

2. **Communicate the finding**: Share this result with branch managers to build support for the transition. The fact that service scores improve (rather than suffer) should ease implementation concerns.

3. **Monitor post-rollout**: Track service scores for 8-12 weeks after full implementation to confirm the effect holds at scale.

4. **Capture implementation learnings**: Document practices from the 90 early-adopter branches that could help others achieve the full benefit.

5. **Investigate the mechanism**: Conduct brief staff surveys or focus groups to understand *why* the procedure change improved service scores. This could inform future operational decisions.

6. **Set realistic expectations**: Communicate that the typical branch should expect a 1-3 point improvement, not a dramatic transformation.

---

## **Risk Assessment**

| Concern | Assessment |
|---------|------------|
| Service quality degradation | **Not observed**—scores improved |
| Effect limited to few branches | **Not supported**—61% of switched branches improved |
| Result due to other factors | **Unlikely**—controlled for staffing and visits; pre-trends were parallel |
| Effect may not persist | **Possible**—continued monitoring recommended |

---

## **Analytical Notes**

- **Data**: 150 branches × 17 weeks (weeks 6-22) = 2,550 observations
- **Method**: Difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch)
- **Switch date**: Week 15
- **Software**: Python with statsmodels
