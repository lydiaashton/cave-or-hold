# **Memorandum**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure shows **positive but statistically uncertain improvements** in service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure improved more than those that kept the old procedure, but the evidence is not strong enough to rule out chance. **We recommend a cautious, phased rollout with continued monitoring rather than immediate system-wide adoption.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Overview**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Analysis compares changes in service scores between the two groups

### **Service Score Changes**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Improvement |
|-------|--------------|--------------|-------------|
| **New Procedure** | 44.89 | 48.66 | **+3.77** |
| **Old Procedure** | 48.93 | 50.34 | **+1.42** |

**Estimated Effect of New Procedure: +2.35 points** (approximately 5% improvement relative to the typical branch score of 50)

### **How Confident Are We?**

| Measure | Result | Interpretation |
|---------|--------|----------------|
| **P-value** | 0.096 | Not statistically significant at the standard 0.05 threshold |
| **95% Confidence Interval** | -0.4 to +5.0 points | The true effect could be slightly negative or moderately positive |
| **Parallel Trends Test** | Passed (p=0.56) | ✓ Valid comparison between groups |

### **What This Means**

The data suggest the new procedure **likely helps**, but we cannot be highly confident. The estimated improvement of 2.35 points is meaningful for service quality, yet the statistical uncertainty means we should proceed carefully.

---

## **Recommendations**

### **1. Proceed with Phased Rollout**
Adopt the new procedure gradually rather than system-wide. The evidence points to benefit with no indication of harm, but the uncertainty warrants a measured approach.

### **2. Extend Data Collection**
Gather 8–12 additional weeks of data. This will narrow the confidence interval and provide greater certainty about the true effect size.

### **3. Monitor Implementation Quality**
Track whether branches are correctly implementing the new procedure. Inconsistent execution could be masking the true effect.

### **4. Establish a Decision Checkpoint**
Reconvene after the extended data collection period. If the effect remains positive and statistical confidence improves, proceed with full system-wide adoption.

### **5. Explore Branch-Level Variation**
Analyze whether certain branch types (by size, location, or community) benefit more from the new procedure. This could inform rollout priorities.
