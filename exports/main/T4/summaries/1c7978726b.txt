# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Prepared by:** Data Analysis Team

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **2.0 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents approximately a **4% improvement** and is statistically significant (p < 0.001).

This finding contradicts our initial expectation that the change would make no difference to service quality.

**Recommendation: Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### 1. Clear Improvement in Service Quality

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches (90) | 49.5 | 53.2 | +3.8 |
| Control branches (60) | 48.6 | 50.3 | +1.7 |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | | **+2.0** |

The 2.0-point gain is the estimated causal effect of the new procedure, isolated from other factors like seasonal trends or branch-specific characteristics.

### 2. Strong Statistical Evidence

- **Effect size:** +2.03 service score points
- **95% confidence interval:** 1.28 to 2.79 points
- **P-value:** < 0.001 (highly significant)
- **Data:** 2,550 observations across 150 branches over 17 weeks

### 3. Valid Comparison Between Groups

Before the switch (weeks 6–14), switched and control branches followed parallel trends with a stable gap of about 1 point. After the switch (weeks 15–22), the gap widened to 2–4 points. This pattern supports a causal interpretation—the divergence coincides with the procedure change.

### 4. Efficiency Gain Observed

The service score improvement occurred alongside a small decrease in staffing index at switched branches. This suggests the new procedure may deliver better service with slightly less staffing input—an efficiency gain worth noting for future resource planning.
