# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and rotation procedure **improved service scores by approximately 2.3 points** on a scale where the average branch scores about 50. This represents a **4-5% improvement** in service quality. Statistical analysis indicates a **98.7% probability** that the effect is genuinely positive. We recommend **proceeding with a full rollout** to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Overview**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6-22 of the year)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15
- **14 branches** continued with the old procedure (serving as a control group)
- Analysis compares how treatment branches changed relative to control branches

### **Service Score Results**

| Period | Control (Old Procedure) | Treatment (New Procedure) |
|--------|-------------------------|---------------------------|
| Before Switch (weeks 6-14) | 48.0 | 50.2 |
| After Switch (weeks 15-22) | 48.1 | 52.6 |
| **Change** | **+0.1** | **+2.4** |

**Net Effect of New Procedure: +2.3 points**

### **Confidence in Results**
- **95% confidence interval:** +0.3 to +4.4 points
- **Probability of positive effect:** 98.7%
- **Pre-trend check passed:** Treatment and control branches followed similar patterns before the switch, validating the comparison

### **Interpretation**
- Branches using the old procedure showed **no meaningful change** in service scores
- Branches using the new procedure showed **sustained improvement** across all post-switch weeks
- The benefit **did not fade** over the 8-week observation period

---

## **Recommendations**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new shelving and rotation procedure to all 34 branches

2. **Implement within 4-6 weeks** to capitalize on current momentum and staff familiarity

3. **Monitor service scores** for 8-12 weeks post-rollout to confirm sustained improvement

4. **Document implementation details** to ensure consistent adoption across all branches

5. **Review staffing and visit metrics** periodically to identify any operational side effects

---

## **Technical Notes**

- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences design, the standard approach for evaluating policy changes with treatment and control groups
- Results control for branch-specific characteristics and week-to-week variation
- Bootstrap validation (1,000 simulations) confirms robustness of findings
