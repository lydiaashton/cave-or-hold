# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative
**From:** Data Analysis Team
**Date:** November 15, 2026
**Subject:** Recommendation on System-Wide Rollout of New Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure achieved service scores **2.3 points higher** than they would have otherwise—a **4% improvement**. This effect is statistically significant and consistent across the 90 branches that switched. We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Estimated Effect** | +2.26 service score points |
| **Confidence Interval** | 0.72 to 3.81 points |
| **Statistical Significance** | p = 0.004 (highly significant) |
| **Relative Improvement** | 4.4% over baseline |
| **Branches Studied** | 150 total (90 switched, 60 control) |
| **Study Period** | 17 weeks (weeks 6–22) |

### What the Numbers Mean

1. **The new procedure works.** Switched branches outperformed non-switched branches by a meaningful margin after accounting for pre-existing differences.

2. **The procedure protected against decline.** All branches faced downward pressure on service scores after week 15. Non-switched branches declined by 2.85 points; switched branches declined by only 0.57 points. The new procedure buffered branches from this broader trend.

3. **The evidence is robust.** Pre-switch trends were parallel between groups, validating the comparison. The result holds after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume.

4. **The effect is meaningful.** A 2.3-point gain on a ~50-point scale represents a noticeable improvement in patron experience.

---

## **Recommendation**

**Approve system-wide rollout of the new procedure to all remaining 60 branches.**

## **Methodology Note**

This analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** design, comparing service score changes in switched branches against non-switched branches before and after week 15. This approach isolates the procedure's effect from other factors affecting all branches (seasonal variation, system-wide challenges, etc.). Pre-trend analysis confirms the two groups were on parallel trajectories before the switch, supporting causal interpretation.
