# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

## **Evaluation of New Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

### **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores—an unexpected but welcome result.** While we anticipated no change to service quality, branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **2.2 points** (approximately **4.4% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant. We recommend proceeding with the system-wide rollout, with continued monitoring to confirm the benefit persists.

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. Service Scores Improved Despite Neutral Expectations**
- Treatment branches (new procedure): Service scores increased by **0.26 points** on average after the switch
- Control branches (old procedure): Service scores **declined by 2.00 points** over the same period
- **Net treatment effect: +2.20 points** (95% confidence interval: 0.89 to 3.51)
- The effect is statistically significant (p = 0.001)
- *This positive impact on service scores was not anticipated when the procedure was designed*

**2. The Analysis Is Methodologically Sound**
- We used a difference-in-differences approach, comparing branches that switched to those that didn't, before and after week 15
- The parallel trends assumption is satisfied (pre-switch trends were similar between groups, p = 0.24)
- Results hold after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume
- Standard errors are clustered by branch to account for repeated observations

**3. Benefits Are Widespread Across Branches**
- **51% of treatment branches** saw their service scores improve after adopting the new procedure
- **71% of treatment branches** outperformed the average control branch
- The effect is not driven by a few outlier branches

**4. Important Context**
- Control branches experienced a 2-point decline in service scores during the study period, possibly due to seasonal factors or other system-wide pressures
- Treatment branches essentially **maintained their service quality** while others declined—suggesting the new procedure may provide resilience against factors that typically depress service scores

---

### **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new stock rotation and shelving procedure to all 60 branches currently using the old method

2. **Document the unexpected service benefit** for internal records and future procedure design—this suggests operational changes may have positive downstream effects we don't always anticipate

3. **Continue monitoring** service scores during and after rollout to confirm benefits persist at scale and across all branches

4. **Investigate the mechanism**—consider qualitative follow-up with treatment branch staff to understand *why* service scores improved (e.g., faster item location, better organization, staff confidence)

5. **Develop implementation timeline** with phased adoption to manage training and transition logistics
