# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD OF DIRECTORS**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure  
**Date:** November 2025  
**Data Period:** Weeks 6–22 (17 weeks, 34 branches)

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

The new stock rotation procedure shows a **positive but statistically uncertain** effect on service scores. Branches that switched improved by approximately **2 points** relative to branches that did not switch. However, this result is **not statistically significant**, meaning we cannot rule out that the improvement occurred by chance. 

**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **phased rollout** while continuing to monitor outcomes. The procedure shows no negative operational impacts, and the potential upside justifies cautious expansion.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **Study Overview**
- **34 branches** tracked weekly from Week 6 to Week 22
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure starting Week 15
- **14 branches** maintained the existing procedure (control group)
- Service scores range around 50 points (higher is better)

### **Service Score Changes**

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|---------------|---------------|--------|
| Branches that switched | 47.4 | 49.6 | +2.3 |
| Branches that did not switch | 50.7 | 50.8 | +0.0 |
| **Estimated procedure effect** | | | **+2.2** |

### **What This Means**
- The 2.2-point improvement represents approximately **4–5%** relative to baseline scores
- **Statistical confidence:** The 95% confidence interval ranges from **-0.6 to +5.0 points**
- **Interpretation:** The true effect could be zero, slightly negative, or moderately positive
- **P-value of 0.12** indicates we cannot reject the possibility of no effect at conventional significance levels

### **Operational Impacts**
The new procedure did **not** significantly affect:
- Staffing levels (no change detected)
- Branch visit volumes (no change detected)

This suggests the procedure can be implemented without disrupting operations.

---

## **LIMITATIONS**

1. **Non-random assignment:** Branches were not randomly assigned to switch, so pre-existing differences may influence results
2. **Limited post-switch data:** Only 8 weeks of data after the change; longer-term effects remain unknown
3. **Modest sample size:** 34 branches provide limited statistical power to detect small effects

---

## **RECOMMENDED NEXT STEPS**

| Action | Timeline | Responsibility |
|--------|----------|----------------|
| **1. Continue data collection** | Ongoing | Operations Team |
| **2. Expand to 10–15 additional branches** | Next quarter | Regional Managers |
| **3. Gather qualitative feedback** from switched branches | 4 weeks | Branch Managers |
| **4. Re-evaluate with 6 months of data** | Q2 2026 | Data Analysis Team |

### **Decision Framework**

| If the board prioritizes... | Recommended Action |
|----------------------------|-------------------|
| **Evidence certainty** | Wait for 6 more months of data before expanding |
| **Potential service improvement** | Proceed with phased rollout now |
| **Risk mitigation** | Phased rollout limits exposure if effects prove negative |

---

## **CONCLUSION**

The new procedure shows **promise but not proof**. Given the positive point estimate, absence of negative operational impacts, and low risk of phased expansion, we recommend **proceeding cautiously** while committing to rigorous ongoing evaluation.
