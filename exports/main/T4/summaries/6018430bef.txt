# **BOARD SUMMARY: New Stock Rotation Procedure Evaluation**

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure shows a **positive effect on service scores** (+2.1 points, or ~4%), but the result is **not statistically definitive** with current data. The evidence supports moving forward with rollout, paired with continued monitoring to confirm the benefit.

---

## **What We Found**

| Measure | Treatment Branches (New) | Control Branches (Old) | Difference |
|---------|-------------------------|----------------------|------------|
| Before Week 15 | 50.8 | 52.2 | — |
| After Week 15 | 53.9 | 53.2 | — |
| **Change** | **+3.2** | **+1.1** | **+2.1** |

- **20 branches** switched to the new procedure; **14 branches** kept the old procedure
- All 34 branches were tracked for 17 weeks (weeks 6-22)
- The switch occurred at week 15

### Why We Trust This Comparison
- Treatment and control branches had **similar score trends before the switch**, validating our comparison
- The improvement holds even after accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume
- Most treatment branches (median +4.0 points) outperformed most control branches (median +1.8 points)

---

## **What This Means**

The data point in the right direction. Branches using the new procedure improved more than those that didn't. However, with 34 branches over 17 weeks, we don't yet have enough data to rule out chance as an explanation.

**The effect is promising but not proven.**

---

## **Recommendation**

| Action | Rationale |
|--------|-----------|
| **Approve phased rollout** | The positive effect (+2.1 points) outweighs implementation risk |
| **Monitor for 8-12 more weeks** | Additional data will confirm whether the effect is real |
| **Track branch-level performance** | Identify which branches benefit most and why |
| **Revisit at Q2 board meeting** | Make final go/no-go decision on full rollout with stronger evidence |

---

## **Questions the Board May Have**

**Q: Why aren't you more certain?**  
A: We have 34 branches, which is a modest sample. More weeks of data will tighten our confidence interval.

**Q: Could the improvement be due to something else?**  
A: We tested for this. The improvement persists after controlling for staffing and visits. Pre-switch trends were parallel between groups.

**Q: What if we roll out and there's no real benefit?**  
A: The new procedure replaces an existing one—there's no indication it causes harm. The main risk is opportunity cost if staff time is better spent elsewhere.

**Q: How much improvement should we expect?**  
A: If the effect is real, expect roughly 2 points on the service score index (about 4% above baseline).
