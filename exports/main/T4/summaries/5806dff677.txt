## **Analysis Overview**

**Method:** Difference-in-differences comparison of 425 switching branches against 284 control branches over 17 weeks.

**Key Controls:** Staffing levels, visitor volume, and branch-specific characteristics.

**Data:** 12,053 weekly observations from 709 branches (weeks 6–22, with switch at week 15).

---

## **Key Findings**

1. **Clear Treatment Effect:** Switched branches improved by 2.2 points relative to non-switched branches after accounting for all other factors.

2. **Valid Comparison:** Pre-switch trends were parallel between groups, confirming the control group provides a reliable counterfactual.

3. **Effect Grows Over Time:** The benefit increased from ~2 points immediately post-switch to ~4 points by week 22, suggesting continued improvement as staff gain familiarity.

4. **Not Driven by Confounders:** The effect persists after controlling for staffing and visitation patterns.

5. **Control Group Stable:** Branches keeping the old procedure showed no meaningful change (−0.2 points), ruling out system-wide trends as an explanation.

---

## **Recommendation**

**Approve system-wide implementation of the new rotation and shelving procedure.**

The evidence is clear, statistically robust, and aligns with expectations. The 4% service quality improvement justifies the operational change.
