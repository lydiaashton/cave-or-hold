# **Library Cooperative Branch Procedure Change: Impact Analysis**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new shelving and rotation procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure achieved an average improvement of **2.0 points** on the service score index compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant (p = 0.01) and represents approximately a **4% gain** relative to the typical branch score of 50.

**Recommendation: Proceed with cooperative-wide rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
| Component | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Branches studied** | 34 total (20 switched, 14 did not) |
| **Time period** | 17 weeks (weeks 6–22) |
| **Switch date** | Week 15 |
| **Analysis method** | Difference-in-differences with branch and week controls |

### Results Summary
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Estimated improvement** | +2.04 points |
| **Statistical significance** | p = 0.010 |
| **95% Confidence interval** | +0.49 to +3.59 points |
| **Relative improvement** | ~4% of average score |

### What the Data Shows

1. **Switched branches improved**: Service scores rose from 48.2 (before) to 50.3 (after) — a gain of 2.0 points

2. **Non-switched branches stayed flat**: Scores remained stable at 51.6 before and after the switch date

3. **The effect is real**: After accounting for inherent differences between branches and normal week-to-week variation, the 2-point gain is statistically significant and unlikely to be due to chance

4. **Pre-existing differences**: Switched branches started with lower scores than non-switched branches (48.2 vs. 51.6), but the analysis accounts for this baseline difference

5. **Staffing matters**: Higher staffing levels are associated with better service scores, but the procedure effect holds even after controlling for staffing and visitor volume

## **Technical Notes**

- Analysis used difference-in-differences regression with branch fixed effects (controlling for time-invariant branch characteristics) and week fixed effects (controlling for common temporal trends)
- The parallel trends assumption appears satisfied — both groups showed similar fluctuation patterns before the switch
- Results are robust to inclusion of staffing and visits indices as control variables
