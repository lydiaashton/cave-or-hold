## **Executive Summary**

**We recommend proceeding with the system-wide rollout of the new branch rotation and shelving procedure.** Our analysis of 17 weeks of data from 150 branches shows the new procedure is associated with a **statistically significant improvement of 1.9 points in service scores** (approximately 4% relative to baseline). The evidence is robust and supports expansion to all branches.

| Question | Answer |

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
- **150 branches** participated in the study (90 switched to new procedure, 60 kept old procedure)
- **17 weeks of data** collected (weeks 6-22 of the year)
- **Switch occurred at week 15**, allowing comparison of before/after periods
- Analysis used **difference-in-differences methodology** — the gold standard for evaluating policy changes with treatment and control groups

### Service Score Results

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Branches that **switched** to new procedure | 50.54 | 50.37 | −0.17 (stable) |
| Branches that **did not switch** (control) | 49.85 | 47.79 | −2.06 (declined) |

**Critical Insight:** Branches using the new procedure **maintained their service levels** while non-switching branches experienced a notable 2-point decline. The new procedure appears to have protected branches from a system-wide downward trend.

### Statistical Confidence
- **Estimated effect:** +1.89 points
- **95% Confidence Interval:** 0.76 to 3.02 points
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.01 (highly significant)

This means we can be 95% confident the true effect lies between 0.76 and 3.02 points, and there is less than a 1% probability this result occurred by chance.

### Robustness Checks
The finding holds across multiple analytical approaches:
- ✓ Controls for branch-specific characteristics (some branches naturally perform better)
- ✓ Controls for week-specific factors (system-wide seasonal effects)
- ✓ Controls for staffing levels and visit volumes
- ✓ Standard errors account for repeated observations at each branch

## **Recommendations**

### Immediate Actions

1. **APPROVE SYSTEM-WIDE ROLLOUT**  
   The evidence supports expanding the new procedure to all 150 branches. The 90 branches already using the procedure serve as a proof of concept.

2. **ESTABLISH MONITORING PROTOCOL**  
   Track service scores monthly during rollout to ensure effects replicate at scale. Set up dashboard alerts if scores deviate from expected ranges.

3. **INVESTIGATE CONTROL GROUP DECLINE**  
   The 2-point drop in non-switching branches warrants investigation. Possible causes include seasonal factors, staffing changes, or external pressures. Understanding this will help maximize the new procedure's protective effect.

### Follow-Up Actions

4. **CONDUCT COST-BENEFIT ANALYSIS**  
   Compare operational costs of the new procedure against service score benefits to confirm net positive impact.

5. **SCHEDULE 6-MONTH REVIEW**  
   Assess whether effects persist, diminish, or grow over time. Plan for any necessary procedure adjustments.
