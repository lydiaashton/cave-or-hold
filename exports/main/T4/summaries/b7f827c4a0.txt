# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure  
**Date:** November 2025  
**Prepared by:** Data Analysis Team

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

The new procedure is associated with **higher service scores**, but we cannot confidently conclude the procedure caused this improvement. Branches that adopted the new procedure were already outperforming others before the switch. **We recommend continuing to monitor the situation and consider a randomized rollout for remaining branches rather than proceeding with an immediate system-wide implementation.**

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### What the Data Shows

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Branches that switched | 50.8 | 51.9 | **+1.1** |
| Branches that did not switch | 49.4 | 48.6 | **-0.8** |

**Raw difference:** Branches that switched improved by about **2 points more** than branches that did not switch.

### Why We Cannot Be Certain This Was Caused by the New Procedure

1. **Pre-existing differences:** Branches selected to switch first already had higher service scores before the change (about 1.4 points higher on average).

2. **Widening gap before the switch:** The performance gap between switching and non-switching branches was growing *before* week 15—from 0.4 points in week 6 to 2.7 points in week 14. This suggests switching branches were already on an upward trajectory.

3. **When we account for this trend,** the estimated effect of the new procedure becomes statistically indistinguishable from zero.

### What Supports a Genuine Effect

- The improvement appears specific to service scores (not seen in staffing or visit metrics)
- The gap continued widening after the switch (reaching 4.5 points by week 22)
- The basic statistical estimate remains positive and significant without trend adjustments

---

## **RECOMMENDATIONS**

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| **1** | **Pause system-wide rollout** | Current evidence is suggestive but not conclusive |
| **2** | **Collect 8-12 more weeks of data** | Longer observation period will help separate procedure effects from pre-existing trends |
| **3** | **Randomize future switches** | Random assignment would eliminate selection bias and provide clearer evidence |
| **4** | **Document why certain branches switched first** | Understanding the selection criteria is essential for proper interpretation |
| **5** | **Verify implementation quality** | Ensure branches are actually following the new procedure as designed |

---

## **WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE DECISION**

The board asked whether the new procedure changed service scores. Our honest answer is: **we see improvement in branches that switched, but we cannot rule out that these branches would have improved anyway.**

Given that:
- Service scores are important to branch performance
- The new procedure requires resources to implement
- The evidence is mixed

**A cautious, evidence-building approach is warranted rather than immediate full rollout.**

## **TECHNICAL SUMMARY**

| Model | Effect Estimate | 95% Confidence Interval | Statistical Significance |
|-------|-----------------|------------------------|-------------------------|
| Basic comparison | +1.93 points | [1.39, 2.48] | Significant (p<0.001) |
