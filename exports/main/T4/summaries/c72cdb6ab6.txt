# **Evaluation of New Shelving Procedure on Library Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving procedure shows a **positive effect** on service scores, with switching branches improving by approximately **2 points** relative to branches that kept the old procedure. While this effect is in the expected direction and would represent a meaningful ~4% improvement, the result is **not statistically significant** with the current data. We recommend **proceeding with a phased rollout** while continuing to collect data to confirm the effect.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- Switch occurred at **week 15**: branches adopted the new procedure or kept the old one
- Analysis compares changes in switching branches to changes in non-switching branches

### **Service Score Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Kept Old Procedure | 47.2 | 48.9 | +1.7 |
| Switched to New | 51.4 | 55.1 | +3.8 |
| **Net Effect of New Procedure** | | | **+2.0** |

### **Statistical Assessment**
- **Estimated effect**: +2.03 points (in the expected direction)
- **Statistical significance**: p = 0.12
- **95% confidence interval**: -0.55 to +4.61 points

The data are consistent with a modest positive effect, though we cannot rule out no effect. The upper bound of the confidence interval suggests the true benefit could be as high as 4.6 points.

### **Supporting Evidence**
- The positive effect is **consistent across early and late post-switch periods**
- Switching branches showed **larger gains** than non-switching branches after week 15
- The effect **persists after controlling** for staffing levels and branch visitation patterns

---

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with a phased rollout of the new procedure**, with the following conditions:

1. **Continue monitoring** service scores for all branches over the next 8–12 weeks
2. **Prioritize rollout** to lower-performing branches first, where a 2-point gain would be most impactful
3. **Document implementation** to ensure the procedure is followed consistently across branches
4. **Re-evaluate in Q2** with additional data to confirm the effect achieves statistical significance

This approach balances the positive signal in the data with appropriate caution given the statistical uncertainty.
