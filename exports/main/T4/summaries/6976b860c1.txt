# **Board Memorandum**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improves service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure experienced service score increases of approximately **1.9 points** (about 4%) compared to branches that retained the old procedure. This effect is statistically robust and consistent across multiple analytical approaches. **We recommend proceeding with system-wide rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Treatment Effect on Service Scores**

| Measure | Value |
|---------|-------|
| Estimated improvement | **+1.87 points** |
| 95% Confidence interval | 1.30 to 2.44 points |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 |
| Relative improvement | **3.7%** over baseline |

### **Study Scope**

- **709 branches** total (425 adopted new procedure; 284 retained old)
- **17 weeks** of data (weeks 6–22); switch occurred at week 15
- **12,053 branch-week observations** analyzed

### **Robustness Checks**

The ~1.9 point effect held consistent across:
- Basic difference-in-differences models
- Models controlling for staffing levels and visitor volumes
- Models accounting for inherent differences between branches
- Models accounting for week-to-week fluctuations

### **Effect Trajectory**

The positive impact appears to **strengthen over time**. Weekly analysis shows larger gains in weeks 19–22 compared to weeks 15–18, suggesting staff proficiency with the new procedure improves with experience.

---

## **Important Considerations**

**Branches were not randomly assigned** to the new procedure. Those that switched differed systematically from those that didn't (e.g., slightly higher baseline scores). Our analysis controlled for these differences using branch fixed effects, but we cannot entirely rule out that other factors contributed to the observed improvement. The estimate represents the **best available evidence** rather than definitive causal proof.

---

## **Recommendations**

### **1. Approve System-Wide Rollout**
The evidence supports expanding the new procedure to all branches. The estimated improvement, while modest per branch, represents meaningful service enhancement across the cooperative.

### **2. Monitor Post-Rollout Performance**
- Track service scores for 8–12 weeks following full implementation
- Identify any branches that underperform after switching (may need additional support)
- Confirm whether effects stabilize or continue growing

### **3. Capture Implementation Learnings**
- Document practices from high-performing early adopters
- Incorporate into training materials for remaining branches
- Assess whether staffing or visit patterns affect outcomes

### **4. Strengthen Future Evaluations**
For subsequent procedural changes, consider randomized rollout where operationally feasible and more frequent measurement during transitions.

---

## **Technical Summary**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Treatment effect | 1.87 points |
| 95% CI | [1.30, 2.44] |
| p-value | < 0.001 |
| Baseline average score | 50.67 |
| Score standard deviation | 10.14 |
| Effect size (Cohen's d) | 0.18 |

*Method: Difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch). Controls: staffing index, visits index, branch fixed effects, week fixed effects.*
