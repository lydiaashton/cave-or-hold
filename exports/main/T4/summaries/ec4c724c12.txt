# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD OF DIRECTORS**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure  
**Date:** Analysis Complete  
**Data Period:** Weeks 6–22 (17 weeks of observation)

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure improved service quality and should be rolled out system-wide.**

Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure experienced a **2.2-point increase** in service scores compared to branches that retained the old procedure. This represents a **4% improvement** over the average branch score. The finding is statistically robust (p < 0.001) and persists after accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **Study Overview**
| Component | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| Branches Analyzed | 709 total (425 switched, 284 control) |
| Observation Period | 17 weeks (Weeks 6–22) |
| Switch Date | Week 15 |
| Methodology | Difference-in-Differences with clustered standard errors |

### **Impact Estimates**
| Metric | Estimate |
|--------|----------|
| **Treatment Effect** | +2.21 service score points |
| **95% Confidence Interval** | 1.65 to 2.78 points |
| **Statistical Significance** | p < 0.001 |
| **Relative Improvement** | 4.3% above baseline |

### **Evidence Supporting Causality**

1. **Parallel Pre-Trends Confirmed:** Before the switch, the performance gap between treatment and control branches remained stable (average 0.7 points, standard deviation 0.67), validating the comparison groups.

2. **Effect Independent of Confounders:** The 2.2-point improvement holds after controlling for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume, confirming the procedure itself drove the change.

3. **Control Group Remained Stable:** Branches keeping the old procedure showed no meaningful change (−0.2 points), ruling out system-wide trends as an explanation.

4. **Effect Strengthens Over Time:** The benefit grew from ~2 points immediately post-switch to ~4 points by weeks 21–22, suggesting branches improve with experience.

---

## **RECOMMENDATION**

**Proceed with system-wide rollout of the new procedure.**

The evidence indicates a clear, meaningful, and statistically significant improvement in service quality. The effect size is practically important (4% improvement) and shows no signs of diminishing.
