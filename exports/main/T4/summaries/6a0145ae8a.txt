# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores—contrary to initial expectations.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by **2.3 points more** than branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant (p < 0.001). We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **150 branches** tracked over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **60 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in service scores between groups before and after the switch

### **Service Score Changes**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| New Procedure Branches | 47.7 | 51.4 | +3.7 |
| Old Procedure Branches | 49.4 | 50.7 | +1.3 |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | | | **+2.3** |

### **Statistical Results**
- **Estimated effect:** +2.31 service score points
- **95% Confidence Interval:** 1.02 to 3.60 points
- **p-value:** 0.0005 (highly significant)
- Results control for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume

### **Why This Is Notable**
The board initially expected no measurable impact from the procedural change. The data show otherwise:
- The improvement is **statistically robust** (significant at the 0.1% level)
- The effect is **practically meaningful**: a 2.3-point gain represents roughly 5% of the average branch score (~50)
- The improvement was **consistent** across all 8 post-switch weeks
- The result **holds after controlling** for staffing and visitor volume, suggesting the effect is attributable to the procedure itself

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all 150 branches

2. **Communicate the unexpected positive outcome** to branch managers to build support for adoption

3. **Monitor implementation** during rollout to ensure consistent adoption and identify branches needing support

4. **Track service scores** for 8–12 weeks post-rollout to confirm the effect holds at scale

5. **Investigate the mechanism**: Consider qualitative follow-up with early-adopter branches to understand *why* the new procedure improved scores (e.g., faster item retrieval, better organization, staff efficiency). This could inform future operational improvements.

- Analysis uses difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch)
- Controls included: staffing index and visits index
- Parallel trends assumption appears satisfied: pre-switch score differences between groups were stable (~1.7 points) before the intervention
