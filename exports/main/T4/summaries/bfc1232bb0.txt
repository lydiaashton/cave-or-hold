# **Subject: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**
# **Date: Analysis Period – Weeks 6-22**

---

## **Executive Summary**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure shows a **positive directional effect** on service scores, with switching branches outperforming non-switching branches by approximately **1.7 points** after implementation. However, this improvement does not reach conventional levels of statistical significance (p=0.14) given the current data.

**Bottom Line for the Board**: The evidence is **promising but not definitive**. We recommend a **phased expansion with continued evaluation** rather than an immediate system-wide mandate. This approach balances optimism about the new procedure with prudent stewardship of cooperative resources.

---

## **What We Found**

### **The Numbers**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated improvement from new procedure | **+1.69 points** |
| Statistical confidence (p-value) | **0.14** |
| 95% confidence range | **-0.5 to +3.9 points** |
| Branches studied | **34 total** (20 switched, 14 did not) |
| Weeks of data | **17 weeks** (9 before, 8 after switch) |

### **What This Means**

- **The direction is right**: Branches that switched performed better than expected, while non-switching branches saw their scores decline.
- **The certainty isn't there**: With our current sample, we cannot rule out the possibility that the observed improvement is due to random variation.
- **No evidence of harm**: The data strongly suggests the new procedure does not negatively impact service scores.

## **Why Aren't the Results More Conclusive?**

Three factors limit our ability to draw firmer conclusions:

1. **Sample size**: With 34 branches and only 8 weeks of post-switch data, we can reliably detect effects of about 3+ points. The observed 1.7-point effect is meaningful but falls below this threshold.

2. **Natural variation**: Service scores fluctuate week-to-week (standard deviation of 9 points), which makes it harder to isolate the procedure's impact.

3. **Control group decline**: Part of the apparent benefit comes from the control group's scores dropping after week 14, not just treatment group improvement. This could reflect seasonal patterns or other external factors.

---

## **Recommendations**

### **1. Proceed with Phased Expansion (Recommended)**

| Action | Rationale |
|--------|-----------|
| Invite 5-10 additional branches to adopt the procedure voluntarily | Increases sample size and statistical power |
| Maintain 10+ branches as a control group | Preserves ability to measure impact |
| Collect 8-12 more weeks of data | Would allow detection of effects as small as 1.5 points |

### **2. Establish Clear Success Criteria**

Before system-wide rollout, confirm:
- [ ] Statistically significant improvement (p < 0.05) **OR**
- [ ] Consistent positive effect of 2+ points across multiple time periods **OR**
- [ ] Positive cost-benefit analysis even at the current effect size

### **3. Complement Quantitative Analysis**

- Gather qualitative feedback from branch staff on implementation experience
- Review operational costs and staffing requirements of the new procedure
- Examine whether certain branch types benefit more than others

---

## **Decision Options for the Board**

| Option | Action | Risk |
|--------|--------|------|
| **A – Phased Expansion** (Recommended) | Expand to 5-10 more branches; continue monitoring | Low – maintains flexibility |
| **B – Full Rollout** | Mandate new procedure for all 34 branches | Moderate – commits resources before effect is confirmed |
| **C – Pause and Study** | Keep current 20 branches on new procedure; no expansion | Low – but delays potential benefits |

---

## **Conclusion**

The new procedure is **moving service scores in the right direction**, and there is no evidence of negative impact. While we cannot yet confirm the improvement with high statistical certainty, the pattern in the data is encouraging. A phased expansion allows the cooperative to capture potential benefits while building the evidence base needed for a confident system-wide decision.

We recommend **Option A** and will return to the Board with updated findings after an additional 8-12 weeks of data collection.
