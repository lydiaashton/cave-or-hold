# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure produced an unexpected benefit.** While we anticipated no change in service scores, branches that switched to the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.5 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and represents about a **5% improvement**. The evidence supports rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. **Positive Effect Despite Neutral Expectations**
- Switched branches improved by **2.48 points** on the service score index (95% confidence interval: 1.2 to 3.8 points)
- This result is statistically significant (p < 0.001)
- The effect persisted after accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume
- **Note:** This finding contrasts with our prior expectation of no impact on service scores

### 2. **Valid Comparison Between Groups**
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure; **60 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Before the switch (weeks 6–14), both groups showed parallel trends in service scores
- After week 15: control branches declined slightly (−0.7 points), while switched branches improved (+1.8 points)

### 3. **Effect Size in Context**
- Average service score across all branches: **~51 points**
- A 2.5-point gain represents meaningful improvement in service quality
- This is an unexpected co-benefit of a procedure change intended for operational efficiency

### 4. **Why Might This Have Happened?**
While we did not anticipate a service score impact, possible explanations include:
- Improved stock organization leading to faster item retrieval
- Reduced staff time spent searching for materials, freeing time for patron assistance
- Better shelf presentation improving patron experience
- Staff morale or engagement effects from the new procedure

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** — the new procedure delivers operational changes plus an unexpected service quality improvement

2. **Investigate the mechanism** — consider brief interviews or surveys with switched branches to understand *why* service scores improved (this could inform future procedure changes)

3. **Develop implementation timeline** — phase the rollout to manage training and transition logistics

4. **Monitor outcomes** — continue tracking service scores for 8–12 weeks after each branch switches to confirm the effect holds across all locations

5. **Update expectations for future changes** — this finding suggests operational procedure changes may have downstream effects on service quality worth measuring

---

## **Technical Note**

This analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing the change in service scores for switched branches against the change for non-switched branches over the same period. This controls for overall trends (e.g., seasonal effects) affecting all branches. The parallel trends assumption is satisfied — both groups moved together before the switch, supporting the validity of the comparison. Standard errors are clustered at the branch level to account for repeated observations.
