# **MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure  

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure shows a **positive but statistically uncertain effect** on service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure improved by approximately **1.9 points** (about 4% above baseline) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. However, this result is **not statistically significant at conventional levels** (p = 0.09), meaning we cannot confidently rule out chance as an explanation.

**Recommendation:** Do not proceed with immediate system-wide rollout. Instead, extend the pilot period to gather more evidence before making a final decision.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **1. Treatment Effect**

| Measure | Value |
|---------|-------|
| Estimated improvement | +1.94 service score points |
| 95% Confidence interval | -0.3 to +4.2 points |
| P-value | 0.09 |
| Effect size | ~4% of baseline |

The confidence interval indicates the true effect could range from slightly harmful to meaningfully beneficial.

### **2. Branch Performance**

| Group | Branches | Avg. Score Before | Avg. Score After | Change |
|-------|----------|-------------------|------------------|--------|
| Switched (treatment) | 20 | 48.7 | 50.6 | +1.9 |
| Non-switched (control) | 14 | 47.6 | 47.5 | -0.1 |

- **70% of switched branches** showed improvement after adopting the new procedure
- **50% of control branches** showed improvement during the same period

### **3. Analysis Method**

We used **difference-in-differences regression**, which isolates the effect of the new procedure by comparing:
- Changes in switched branches before vs. after week 15
- Against changes in non-switched branches over the same period

This approach controls for overall time trends and pre-existing branch differences. Standard errors were clustered at the branch level to account for repeated weekly measurements.

---

## **IMPORTANT CAVEATS**

1. **Statistical uncertainty**: The p-value of 0.09 exceeds the conventional 0.05 threshold for statistical significance
2. **Limited post-switch data**: Only 8 weeks of observations after the procedure change
3. **Small sample**: 34 branches total (20 treatment, 14 control)
4. **Pre-existing trends**: Some switched branches showed different performance patterns before the switch, adding uncertainty to estimates

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

### **Recommended Actions**

| Action | Timeline | Responsibility |
|--------|----------|----------------|
| Continue current pilot | Weeks 23-30 | Operations Team |
| Collect additional weekly data | Ongoing | All branches |
| Re-analyze with expanded dataset | Week 31 | Data Team |
| Board decision on rollout | Week 32 | Board |

### **Decision Criteria for Full Rollout**

Proceed with system-wide implementation only if:
- ✓ The effect remains positive (≥ +1.0 points), **AND**
- ✓ Statistical significance reaches p < 0.05, **OR** the 95% confidence interval lower bound exceeds zero

### **Additional Monitoring**

- Track implementation fidelity across switched branches
- Analyze effects on staffing efficiency and visit patterns
- Consider a second randomized cohort to strengthen evidence

---

## **CONCLUSION**

The new procedure demonstrates **promise but not proof**. The positive direction aligns with expectations, but the evidence is insufficient to justify the cost and disruption of a system-wide rollout. A phased, evidence-based approach will ensure the cooperative makes the right decision for all branches and patrons.
