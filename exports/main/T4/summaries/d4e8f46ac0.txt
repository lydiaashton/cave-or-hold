## **Executive Summary**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure shows a **positive directional effect** on service scores, consistent with our expectations. Branches that adopted the new procedure improved their scores by approximately **2 points** relative to branches that kept the old procedure. However, this improvement is **not statistically distinguishable from zero** at conventional confidence levels (p = 0.11).

**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **phased rollout** rather than immediate system-wide implementation. Continue rigorous monitoring to confirm the effect as more data accumulates.

---

## **Bottom Line**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did the new procedure improve service scores? | **Yes, by about 2 points—but we cannot be certain this isn't random variation** |
| Should we roll out to all branches now? | **No—expand gradually while continuing to measure** |
| Is the analysis reliable? | **Yes—the comparison groups behaved similarly before the switch** |

## **Key Findings**

### **1. The New Procedure Shows a Positive Effect**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated improvement | **+2.05 points** (on ~50-point scale) |
| 95% Confidence Interval | **-0.49 to +4.59 points** |
| Statistical Significance | **Not significant (p = 0.11)** |

The point estimate is positive and meaningful in practical terms (~4% improvement), but the confidence interval includes zero, meaning we cannot rule out no effect.

### **2. What Happened to Each Group**

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|----------------|---------------|--------|
| **Control** (old procedure) | 47.09 | 45.96 | **▼ -1.13** |
| **Treated** (new procedure) | 49.36 | 50.25 | **▲ +0.89** |

Branches using the new procedure **maintained their service scores** while control branches experienced a decline. The 2-point difference represents the gap between these two trajectories.

### **3. The Analysis Is Methodologically Sound**

- **Parallel trends confirmed:** Treatment and control groups moved together before the switch (joint pre-trend test p = 0.85)
- **Appropriate controls:** Analysis accounts for staffing levels and visitor volume
- **Robust standard errors:** Accounting for repeated observations within branches

### **4. Why Isn't It Statistically Significant?**

The study has **limited statistical power** due to:
- Only 8 weeks of post-switch data
- Only 20 branches in the treatment group
- Natural week-to-week variability in service scores

A larger sample or longer observation period would narrow the confidence interval.

---

## **Recommendation: Phased Rollout**

We recommend a **cautious expansion** rather than full implementation or halt:

### **Phase 1 (Next 8 Weeks)**
- Add 5-10 additional branches to the new procedure
- Continue weekly monitoring of all branches
- Report updated analysis to the Board in Q2

### **Phase 2 (Decision Point)**
| If Updated Analysis Shows | Then |
|---------------------------|------|
| Effect ≥2 points, p < 0.05 | Proceed with full rollout |
| Effect ≥2 points, p > 0.05 | Continue phased expansion |
| Effect <1 point or negative | Pause and investigate |

### **Rationale**
- The positive point estimate aligns with our expectations and operational observations
- The cost of gradual expansion is low compared to the potential benefit
- Waiting for perfect statistical certainty may delay beneficial changes indefinitely
- Continued monitoring protects against scaling an ineffective procedure
