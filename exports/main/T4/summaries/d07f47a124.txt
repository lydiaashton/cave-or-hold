# **Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure Evaluation**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores—contrary to our expectations.**

Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure maintained service scores approximately **2 points higher** than branches that kept the old procedure. This represents a **3.9% improvement** relative to baseline performance. The effect is statistically significant (p < 0.01) and consistent across all post-implementation weeks.

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. The Effect Was Unexpected But Real

We entered this evaluation expecting no meaningful change to service scores. The data shows otherwise:

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|----------------|---------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 49.14 | 46.92 | **−2.22** |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 49.86 | 49.56 | **−0.31** |

**Difference-in-differences estimate: +1.96 points** (95% CI: 0.78 to 3.13)

The new procedure did not boost scores so much as it **protected branches from a system-wide decline** that affected all branches using the old procedure.

### 2. Results Are Robust

- **Statistically significant:** p < 0.01, even with conservative clustered standard errors
- **Consistent across time:** All 8 post-switch weeks showed positive effects (range: +1.3 to +2.6 points)
- **Holds with controls:** Effect persists when accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume
- **Holds with branch fixed effects:** Effect persists when accounting for individual branch characteristics (p = 0.002)

### 3. Analysis Is Valid

- **Parallel trends confirmed:** Treatment and control branches had statistically similar trends before the switch (p = 0.41)
- **Minimal pre-existing differences:** Treatment branches scored only 0.7 points higher before the switch (not statistically significant)
- **Large sample:** 150 branches, 17 weeks, 2,550 observations

---

## **Next Steps**

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| 1 | **Approve system-wide rollout** | Clear evidence of benefit; no evidence of harm |
| 2 | **Investigate the system-wide decline** | Control branches dropped 2.2 points after week 15—understand why to maximize the new procedure's value |
| 3 | **Monitor during implementation** | Track service scores to ensure effect replicates at scale |
| 4 | **Complete cost-benefit analysis** | Quantify operational costs against service score benefits for ROI confirmation |
| 5 | **Phase the rollout** | Manage operational capacity; consider prioritizing lowest-performing branches |

---

## **What Changed Our Thinking**

This evaluation was designed to confirm that the new procedure would not harm service quality. Instead, it revealed a meaningful improvement we did not anticipate. The evidence is strong enough to warrant changing our recommendation from "proceed with caution" to "proceed with confidence."

---

## **Technical Appendix**

- **Method:** Difference-in-differences regression with branch-clustered standard errors
- **Treatment group:** 102 branches (switched at week 15)
- **Control group:** 48 branches (retained old procedure throughout)
- **Data period:** Weeks 6–22 (9 weeks pre-switch, 8 weeks post-switch)
- **Outcome measure:** Service score index (baseline average ≈ 50; higher is better)
