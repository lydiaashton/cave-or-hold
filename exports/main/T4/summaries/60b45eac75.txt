# MEMORANDUM

**TO:** Board of Directors  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Impact of New Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores

---

## Bottom Line

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by **2.2 points more** than branches that maintained the old procedure. This finding is statistically significant (p < 0.001) and contradicts our initial expectation that the change would make no difference.

---

## Key Findings

### Study Overview
- **150 branches** participated: 90 adopted the new procedure, 60 continued with the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** collected (weeks 6–22), with the switch implemented at week 15
- Analysis employed a **difference-in-differences** methodology, the standard approach for evaluating policy changes with treatment and control groups

### Service Score Results

| Group | Pre-Switch (Weeks 6–14) | Post-Switch (Weeks 15–22) | Change |
|-------|------------------------|--------------------------|--------|
| Switched branches (n=90) | 48.16 | 52.20 | +4.04 |
| Non-switched branches (n=60) | 48.17 | 49.98 | +1.81 |
| **Effect of New Procedure** | | | **+2.23** |

### Statistical Confidence
- **Estimated effect:** +2.23 service score points
- **95% Confidence Interval:** 1.02 to 3.44 points
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001
- **Methodology:** Regression with standard errors clustered at branch level

### Validation Checks
- **Parallel trends confirmed:** Before the switch, both groups had nearly identical service scores (difference of only 0.02 points on average), validating our comparison approach
- **Consistent effects:** The improvement held across multiple statistical specifications, including models controlling for staffing and visit patterns

---

## Recommendation

**Proceed with system-wide rollout of the new procedure to all remaining branches.**

The evidence is clear and robust: the new stock rotation and shelving procedure produces meaningful improvements in service quality. Given that:
- The effect is statistically significant and precisely estimated
- The improvement (~4–5% of baseline score) is operationally meaningful
- No negative unintended consequences were detected in the data

---

## Next Steps

1. **Plan full implementation** across the remaining 60 branches within the next quarter

2. **Establish monitoring protocols** to track service scores during rollout and confirm effects persist at scale

3. **Document best practices** from the 90 early-adopter branches to support training and implementation

4. **Conduct cost-benefit analysis** if the new procedure involves additional expenses, weighing these against the demonstrated service improvements

5. **Consider similar evaluations** for future operational changes to build evidence-based decision-making practices
