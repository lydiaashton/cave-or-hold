# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD OF DIRECTORS**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure  
**Date:** Analysis of Weeks 6–22 Service Score Data  
**Recommendation:** Proceed with System-Wide Rollout

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new shelving and stock rotation procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure experienced a **1.9-point increase** in service scores (approximately 4% improvement) compared to branches that retained the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant (p < 0.001) and not explained by changes in staffing or visitor volume.

**We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### 1. Measurable Improvement in Service Quality

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Branches with New Procedure | 49.69 | 52.98 | +3.28 |
| Branches with Old Procedure | 48.97 | 50.35 | +1.38 |
| **Net Effect (Difference-in-Differences)** | | | **+1.90** |

The new procedure produced a **1.90-point improvement** in service scores (95% confidence interval: 1.55 to 2.25). This represents meaningful progress on a metric where the cooperative average is approximately 50 points.

### 2. The Improvement Is Attributable to the Procedure Itself

Analysis confirms the gain is not driven by external factors:
- **Staffing levels:** No significant change in treatment branches (p = 0.78)
- **Visitor volume:** No significant change in treatment branches (p = 0.22)

The procedure itself—through better organization, faster item retrieval, or improved shelf management—appears to be the direct cause of improved service scores.

### 3. Valid Comparison Between Groups

The analysis relies on the assumption that treatment and control branches would have followed similar trends absent the intervention. The data supports this:
- In the weeks immediately preceding the switch (weeks 12–14), the two groups showed **no statistically significant differences** in their trends
- This validates the comparison and strengthens confidence in the estimated effect

### 4. Broad-Based Participation

- **709 branches** total in the analysis
- **425 branches** (60%) adopted the new procedure
- **284 branches** (40%) retained the old procedure
- **12,053 weekly observations** across 17 weeks

The large sample size and substantial participation in both groups provide a robust basis for decision-making.

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

### Immediate Actions

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new shelving and stock rotation procedure to all remaining branches
2. **Develop an implementation timeline** that allows for orderly transition (e.g., phased rollout by region or branch size)
3. **Allocate resources for training** to ensure consistent adoption across all locations

### Ongoing Monitoring

1. **Continue tracking service scores** post-rollout to confirm effects persist at scale
2. **Identify outlier branches** that underperform after adoption and provide targeted support
3. **Review quarterly** to assess long-term sustainability of the improvement

### Considerations for Implementation

- **Training investment:** Staff familiarity with the new procedure will affect outcomes; budget accordingly
- **Transition period:** Some temporary disruption during rollout is normal; set appropriate expectations
- **Modest but meaningful impact:** The 1.9-point gain is real and valuable, but branches should not expect transformational change overnight

---

## **CONCLUSION**

The evidence is clear: the new procedure improves service quality without requiring additional staffing or generating higher visitor volume. With a statistically significant 1.9-point gain in service scores and a clean implementation pathway, this represents a low-risk, high-confidence opportunity to enhance patron experience across the cooperative.

**We recommend the Board approve immediate planning for system-wide adoption.**

---

*Analysis conducted using Difference-in-Differences regression with branch and week fixed effects. Data covers weeks 6–22, comprising 12,053 observations from 709 branches.*
