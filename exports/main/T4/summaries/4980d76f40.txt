# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Contrary to initial expectations, the new stock rotation and shelving procedure produced a measurable improvement in service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure scored approximately 1.8 points higher than those that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and sustained over the 8-week observation period. We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Unexpected but Clear Improvement**
- The new procedure increased service scores by **+1.76 points** (3.5% above the system average of 50.7)
- While we anticipated no measurable change, the data shows a consistent positive effect
- The improvement is equivalent to **0.18 standard deviations**—modest but meaningful for patron experience

### **2. Statistically Reliable Result**
- **95% confidence interval:** +0.7 to +2.9 points
- **P-value:** 0.002 (highly unlikely to occur by chance)
- Result holds after accounting for staffing levels and branch traffic

### **3. Strong Study Design**
- **150 branches** tracked over 17 weeks (2,550 observations)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure; **60 branches** maintained the old procedure
- Pre-switch service scores were nearly identical between groups (49.85 vs. 50.34), validating the comparison

### **4. Effect Persisted Throughout Observation Period**
- Switched branches outperformed non-switched branches in 7 of 8 post-switch weeks
- No evidence of the effect fading over time
- Weekly advantage ranged from +0.6 to +1.9 points

---

## **Implications**

This finding suggests the procedural changes may have improved service quality through mechanisms we did not anticipate—possibly faster item retrieval, better shelf organization, or reduced staff time spent on restocking. The result warrants further investigation into *why* the change helped, not just *whether* it helped.

---

## **Recommendations**

| Priority | Action | Timeline |
|----------|--------|----------|
| **1** | **Approve rollout** to remaining 60 branches | Next board cycle |
| **2** | **Document implementation details** from early adopters | 2–4 weeks |
| **3** | **Monitor scores** post-rollout to confirm effect at scale | 8–12 weeks |
| **4** | **Investigate mechanisms** driving the improvement (staff surveys, time studies) | Ongoing |
| **5** | **Assess costs** (training, labor hours) against the 3.5% service gain | Before full rollout |

---

## **Caveats**

- Effect size is modest; branches should not expect dramatic transformation
- Observation period covers only 8 post-switch weeks; longer-term effects unknown
- Service score is one metric; patron satisfaction and circulation data should also be reviewed

---

## **Conclusion**

While we entered this pilot expecting no change in service scores, the evidence indicates a real, positive effect. The new procedure delivers measurable value at minimal risk. Proceeding with a full rollout is supported by the data.
