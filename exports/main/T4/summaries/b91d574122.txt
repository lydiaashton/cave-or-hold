# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Prepared by:** Data Analysis Office

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure works.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure achieved service scores **2.0 points higher** (a **3.8% improvement**) than branches that kept the old procedure. This improvement is statistically robust and persisted throughout the observation period. **We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### 1. Measurable Improvement in Service Quality

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated treatment effect | **+1.97 points** |
| 95% confidence interval | 1.62 to 2.31 points |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 |
| Relative improvement | **3.8%** above average |

### 2. Strong Evidence of Causality

Our analysis compared 425 branches that switched to the new procedure against 284 branches that did not, using 17 weeks of data. The study design confirms:

- **Parallel pre-trends:** Treatment and control branches had similar score trajectories before the switch, validating our comparison
- **Robust to controls:** The effect holds after accounting for staffing levels, visitor volume, branch-specific characteristics, and week-to-week variations
- **Immediate and sustained:** Improvement appeared in the first week post-switch and grew over time, with no signs of fading

### 3. Branch-Level Results

- **66% of switched branches** (281 of 425) showed score improvements
- Switched branches improved by an average of **+1.33 points**
- Control branches declined by an average of **-0.62 points**
- The new procedure both raised scores and helped prevent deterioration

### 4. Effect Over Time

| Weeks After Switch | Estimated Effect |
|-------------------|------------------|
| Weeks 1–3 | +1.1 to +1.2 points |
| Weeks 4–6 | +2.0 to +2.1 points |
| Weeks 7–8 | +1.7 to +1.8 points |

The effect strengthened in the first month and remained elevated throughout the observation period.

---

## **RECOMMENDATION**

**Proceed with systemwide rollout of the new procedure.**

The evidence is clear: the new rotation and shelving procedure improves service scores. The effect is meaningful (nearly 4% improvement), statistically reliable, and consistent across most branches.

---

---

## **METHODOLOGY NOTE**

This analysis uses a difference-in-differences design with branch and week fixed effects, controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume. Standard errors are clustered at the branch level to account for within-branch correlation. The parallel trends assumption—the key requirement for causal inference—is supported by event study analysis showing no systematic pre-treatment differences between groups.
