# Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure Evaluation

## Bottom Line

The new procedure shows a **positive but modest improvement** in service scores (+1.7 to +2.0 points, approximately 3-4%). However, the effect is **not statistically significant** at conventional levels. The data supports a cautious rollout, but expectations should be tempered—the improvement is smaller than anticipated and not definitively confirmed.

---

## Key Findings

### What We Measured
- **34 branches** over 17 weeks (February–June)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure in Week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Service scores average ~50 points (higher is better)

### Results

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|----------------|---------------|--------|
| Switched Branches | 47.9 | 48.5 | +0.6 |
| Non-Switched Branches | 52.0 | 50.9 | −1.1 |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | | | **+1.7** |

### Statistical Confidence

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated Effect | +1.7 to +2.0 points |
| P-value | 0.10–0.14 |
| 95% Confidence Interval | −0.4 to +4.4 points |
| Effect Size | 0.18 standard deviations (small) |

**Interpretation**: The data is consistent with a small positive effect, but we cannot rule out no effect or even a negligible negative effect. The confidence interval spans from slightly negative to moderately positive.

### Why Results May Be Weaker Than Expected

1. **Control group decline**: Non-switching branches' scores dropped after Week 15. The net effect reflects both treatment improvement AND control group decline—possibly due to seasonal factors or system-wide changes unrelated to the procedure.

2. **Baseline differences**: Switching branches started with lower scores (47.9 vs 52.0). While our analysis controls for this, it suggests the new procedure may have been targeted to struggling branches.

3. **Implementation variation**: Not all branches may have adopted the new procedure with equal fidelity.

4. **Limited data**: Only 8 weeks of post-switch data reduces our ability to detect smaller effects with confidence.

---

## Recommendation

### Proceed with Rollout—With Conditions

The board should **approve systemwide adoption**, but with the following safeguards:

1. **Set realistic expectations**: Communicate that improvement is likely modest (1–2 points), not transformative.

2. **Continue monitoring**: Track service scores for at least 12 more weeks to confirm the effect persists and accumulates.

3. **Standardize implementation**: Ensure all branches receive consistent training and support for the new procedure.

4. **Review at 6 months**: Reconvene to assess whether the effect has grown, stabilized, or diminished with more data.

5. **Complement with qualitative feedback**: Gather staff and patron input—service scores are one metric among many.

---

## What Would Change Our Conclusion

| Finding | Implication |
|---------|-------------|
| Effect grows to +3+ points with more data | Stronger case for full rollout |
| Effect shrinks toward zero | Consider reverting or modifying the procedure |
| Effect varies significantly by branch type | Target rollout to branches most likely to benefit |
| Staff report improved workflow despite modest score gains | May justify adoption on operational grounds |
