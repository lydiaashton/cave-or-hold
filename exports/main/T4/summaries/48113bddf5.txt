# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD OF DIRECTORS**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Executive Summary**

Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure showed a **statistically significant 2.0-point improvement in service scores** compared to branches that retained the old procedure. The effect is meaningful (approximately 4% above baseline) and consistent across the post-switch period. However, branches that volunteered to switch early were already trending upward before the change, introducing some uncertainty about the true causal effect.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **phased, monitored rollout** to all remaining branches, with continued evaluation to confirm effects and identify which branches benefit most.

---

## **Study Overview**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Period Analyzed** | Weeks 6–22 (17 weeks) |
| **Switch Date** | Week 15 |
| **Total Branches** | 709 |
| **Treatment Group** | 425 branches (switched to new procedure) |
| **Control Group** | 284 branches (retained old procedure) |
| **Outcome Measure** | Weekly service score (average ~51; higher is better) |

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Service Score Changes**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Switched Branches | 51.32 | 52.20 | +0.88 |
| Non-Switched Branches | 50.50 | 49.35 | −1.15 |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | | | **+2.03** |

### **2. Statistical Confidence**

- **Effect Size:** +2.03 points
- **95% Confidence Interval:** +1.31 to +2.75 points
- **Statistical Significance:** p < 0.001
- **Practical Significance:** ~4% improvement over baseline

### **3. Effect Persists and Grows Over Time**

The improvement is not a one-week anomaly. Treatment effects by week post-switch:

| Weeks After Switch | Estimated Effect |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | +2.0 to +2.4 points |
| 3–4 | +2.8 to +3.1 points |
| 5–8 | +2.9 to +3.7 points |

The growing effect suggests branches may realize additional benefits as staff gain familiarity with the new procedure.

### **4. Important Caveat**

Branches that switched early were **already improving relative to non-switched branches before Week 15**. This means:

- The 2.0-point estimate likely reflects the **true effect plus some selection bias**
- The actual effect could be somewhat smaller (but still positive, based on the confidence interval)
- Early-adopter branches may have had unmeasured advantages (e.g., more motivated staff, better management)

---

## **Recommendations**

### **1. Approve Phased Rollout**

Roll out the new procedure to remaining branches in **3–4 waves** over the next 6–9 months. This allows:

- Continued monitoring and course correction
- Identification of branches that may need additional support
- Management of training and implementation resources

### **2. Establish Monitoring Framework**

Track the following for each wave:
- Service scores for 12 weeks post-implementation
- Staff feedback on procedure usability
- Any changes in staffing levels or visit volumes

### **3. Investigate Success Factors**

Analyze which branch characteristics predict stronger improvements:
- Branch size and staffing levels
- Geographic location and community demographics
- Pre-implementation service score trends

### **4. Set Realistic Expectations**

Communicate to stakeholders that:
- Expected improvement is **1 to 3 points** on the service score index
- Full benefits may take **4–8 weeks** to materialize
- Some branches may see minimal or no improvement

---

## **Decision Required**

The Board is asked to:

☐ **Approve** system-wide rollout of the new procedure with phased implementation  
☐ **Approve** resources for ongoing monitoring and evaluation  
☐ **Request** additional analysis before proceeding
