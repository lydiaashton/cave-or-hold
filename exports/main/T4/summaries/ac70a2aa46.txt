# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure  

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure shows a **modest positive association** with service scores (+1.8 to +2.0 points on a ~50-point scale), but this effect is **not statistically significant**. The data do not provide conclusive evidence that the new procedure improves service quality. We recommend **extending the evaluation period** before making a systemwide rollout decision.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Overview**
| Component | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| Branches | 34 total (20 adopted new procedure; 14 maintained old procedure) |
| Time Period | 17 weeks (Weeks 6–22); switch implemented Week 15 |
| Method | Difference-in-Differences regression with branch and week fixed effects |
| Controls | Staffing index and visits index |

### **Treatment Effect Estimates**

| Model Specification | Effect Size | 95% Confidence Interval | P-value |
|--------------------|-------------|------------------------|---------|
| Basic model | +1.77 points | -0.94 to +4.49 | 0.20 |
| With staffing/visit controls | +2.01 points | -0.49 to +4.50 | 0.12 |

### **What This Means**
- **Direction:** The point estimate is positive, suggesting the new procedure may improve service scores
- **Magnitude:** If the effect is real, it represents approximately a **4% improvement** over the average branch score of 50
- **Uncertainty:** The confidence interval includes zero—we cannot rule out that the true effect is zero or even slightly negative
- **Statistical significance:** The effect does not meet the conventional threshold (p < 0.05)

### **Validity Assessment**
✓ **Parallel trends confirmed:** Pre-switch trends did not differ significantly between groups (p = 0.38)  
✓ **Complete data:** All 34 branches have observations for all 17 weeks  
✓ **Appropriate controls:** Results are robust to controlling for staffing and visit levels  

---

## **Recommendations & Next Steps**

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| **1** | **Extend monitoring by 8–12 weeks** | Current post-switch data (8 weeks) may be insufficient to detect a modest effect with confidence |
| **2** | **Do not proceed with systemwide rollout yet** | Evidence is suggestive but not conclusive; premature rollout risks adopting an ineffective procedure |
| **3** | **Analyze heterogeneous effects** | Some branch types (e.g., high-volume, urban) may benefit more than others; targeted rollout could be more effective |
| **4** | **Verify implementation fidelity** | Ensure switching branches are following the new procedure as intended; variation in implementation could mask true effects |
| **5** | **Conduct cost-benefit analysis** | Even if the +2 point effect is real, weigh it against any additional costs or operational burdens |

---

## **Conclusion**

The new procedure shows **promise but not proof**. The board should authorize continued data collection and a follow-up analysis in Q1 2027 before making a final rollout decision. If operational constraints allow, a **phased expansion** to additional branches could accelerate learning while limiting risk.
