# **Board Summary: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores—an unexpected but welcome result.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure scored approximately **2 points higher** than comparable branches using the old procedure. Given the positive outcome and no identified downsides, we recommend proceeding with system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. A Modest but Real Improvement

Although the procedure change was not expected to affect service scores, the data show a clear positive effect:

| Metric | Switched Branches | Control Branches |
|--------|-------------------|------------------|
| Average score BEFORE switch (weeks 6-14) | 53.31 | 52.28 |
| Average score AFTER switch (weeks 15-22) | 52.92 | 49.91 |
| **Change** | **-0.39** | **-2.37** |

**Difference-in-differences estimate: +1.98 points (p = 0.003)**

While switched branches remained nearly stable, control branches experienced a notable decline. The new procedure appears to have **buffered branches against a broader service score decline** occurring during this period.

### 2. The Finding Is Robust

- **Pre-trends were parallel**: Before week 15, both groups moved together (p = 0.53), supporting causal interpretation.
- **Holds with controls**: Effect persists after accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume.
- **Fixed effects model confirms**: +1.98 points (p = 0.003) when controlling for branch-specific and week-specific factors.

### 3. Context on the Effect Size

With an average branch score around 50, a **2-point gain represents roughly 4% improvement**. While modest, this is a meaningful benefit—especially since no operational costs or service disruptions were anticipated from this change.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches.

2. **Monitor during implementation** to ensure the effect holds as more branches transition.

3. **Capture implementation learnings** from the early-adopter branches to smooth the rollout.

4. **Investigate the broader decline** in control branch scores after week 15—this may point to external factors worth addressing.

5. **Re-evaluate in 3-6 months** to confirm the effect is sustained.

---

## **A Note on Expectations**

This analysis was commissioned to verify that the new procedure would not harm service quality. The finding that it actually *improves* scores is an unexpected benefit. Even if future data show a smaller effect, the procedure change appears to be low-risk and potentially valuable.

---

*Analysis based on 2,550 weekly observations from 150 branches over 17 weeks (weeks 6-22). 102 branches switched to the new procedure; 48 served as controls. The switch occurred at week 15.*
