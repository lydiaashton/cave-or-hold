# Library Cooperative Stock Rotation Procedure: Impact Analysis

**Date:** November 2026  
**Prepared for:** Board of Directors  
**Data Period:** Weeks 6–22 (17 weeks, 34 branches)

---

## Bottom Line

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure shows a **positive but statistically uncertain effect** on service scores. Switched branches improved by approximately **2.3 points more** than branches that kept the old procedure. While this suggests the new procedure is beneficial, the evidence is not strong enough to rule out chance. We recommend a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## Key Findings

### Impact on Service Scores

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches (20) | 45.19 | 49.43 | +4.24 |
| Control branches (14) | 46.58 | 48.55 | +1.96 |
| **Net effect** | | | **+2.28** |

### Statistical Confidence

- **P-value:** 0.09–0.11 (not significant at the conventional 0.05 threshold)
- **95% Confidence Interval:** -0.6 to +5.2 points
- **Interpretation:** The true effect could be slightly negative, zero, or moderately positive

### Validity Check: Pre-Implementation Trends

Before the switch (weeks 6–14), switched branches actually scored **1.4 points lower** than control branches on average. After the switch, they scored **0.9 points higher**. This reversal supports the conclusion that the procedure change—not pre-existing differences—drove the improvement.

### Practical Significance

- The average branch service score is approximately 50
- A 2.3-point improvement represents a **4–5% gain**
- Individual branch results varied widely (changes from -2.5 to +8.9 points), suggesting branch-specific factors influence outcomes

---

## Recommendations

### 1. Proceed with Phased Expansion
The point estimate is positive and meaningful in practical terms. Given that the procedure change likely has low implementation costs, expanding to additional branches is reasonable despite statistical uncertainty.

### 2. Continue Systematic Monitoring
- Track service scores for another 8–12 weeks as more branches adopt the procedure
- This will increase statistical power and provide more definitive evidence
- Maintain the current data collection protocol (weekly scores per branch)

### 3. Investigate Branch-Level Variation
Some branches benefited substantially while others saw minimal or negative effects. Analyze whether branch characteristics (size, staffing levels, visit volume, urban vs. rural) predict better outcomes to optimize implementation.

### 4. Conduct Cost-Benefit Review
If the procedure requires additional staff time, training, or equipment, quantify these costs against the ~2-point service score gain. If costs are minimal, the potential upside justifies expansion.
