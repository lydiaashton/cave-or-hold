# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Subject:** Assessment of New Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure is associated with a **2-point improvement** in service scores (approximately 4% relative to the baseline average of 50). However, this improvement is **not statistically significant** at conventional levels (p = 0.11), meaning we cannot confidently rule out that this result occurred by chance. 

**Recommendation:** Do not mandate immediate system-wide rollout. Instead, extend the evaluation period and consider a phased expansion while continuing to monitor results.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Overview**
- **34 branches** participated (20 adopted the new procedure, 14 continued with the old procedure)
- Data covers **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Service scores average approximately 50 across all branches (higher is better)

### **Results Summary**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches (n=20) | 45.69 | 48.99 | +3.30 |
| Non-switched branches (n=14) | 47.31 | 48.54 | +1.23 |
| **Estimated Effect of New Procedure** | | | **+2.08** |

### **Statistical Confidence**
- **Point estimate:** +2.08 points
- **95% Confidence Interval:** -0.55 to +4.65 points
- **p-value:** 0.11

The confidence interval indicates the true effect could reasonably range from a small negative effect to a moderate positive effect. The result does not meet the conventional threshold for statistical significance (p < 0.05).

### **Validation Checks**
- **Pre-trends were parallel:** Both groups showed similar score trajectories before the switch, supporting the validity of the comparison
- **Results are robust:** Accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume does not materially alter the estimated effect
- **Direction is consistent:** All analytical approaches point to a positive (though uncertain) effect

---

## **Next Steps**

| Action | Rationale | Timeline |
|--------|-----------|----------|
| **1. Extend data collection** | 8–12 additional weeks would increase statistical power and confirm whether the effect persists | Immediate |
| **2. Offer phased expansion** | Allow additional interested branches to adopt the procedure while maintaining a control group for comparison | Next quarter |
| **3. Investigate variation** | Some branches showed large improvements while others showed little effect. Understanding which branch characteristics predict success could inform targeted implementation | Ongoing |
| **4. Set decision criteria** | Define in advance what magnitude of improvement would justify full rollout (e.g., "proceed if we observe 2+ points with p < 0.05 after 12 more weeks") | Before next review |

---

## **Conclusion**

The new procedure shows promise, with adopting branches improving their service scores by about 2 points more than non-adopting branches. However, given the statistical uncertainty, this should be treated as **encouraging but preliminary evidence**. A modest investment in additional data collection would provide the clarity needed for a confident system-wide decision.

---

*Analysis based on branches.csv containing 578 weekly observations across 34 branches, weeks 6–22.*
