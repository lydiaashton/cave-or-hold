# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Service Score Impact Analysis**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2025  
**Data Period:** Weeks 6–22 (17 weeks, 34 branches)

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and rotation procedure shows a **small positive association** with service scores, but the evidence is **not statistically conclusive**. We cannot confidently attribute improvement to the new procedure. **We recommend against a full system-wide rollout at this time** and instead propose an extended pilot with additional data collection.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Estimated Impact**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Treatment effect estimate | **+1.7 points** |
| Baseline average score | ~50 points |
| Relative improvement | **~3.4%** |
| Statistical significance | **Not significant** (p > 0.05) |
| 95% confidence interval | **-0.1 to +3.5 points** |

The confidence interval includes zero, meaning we cannot rule out that the observed difference is due to random variation rather than the procedure change.

### **2. Branch-Level Results Are Mixed**
- Of the 20 branches that adopted the new procedure:
  - **10 branches improved** their service scores
  - **10 branches declined** their service scores
- This 50/50 split suggests the procedure does not consistently benefit all branches

### **3. Analysis Approach Was Rigorous**
- We used a **difference-in-differences design**, comparing switched branches to non-switched branches before and after week 15
- Pre-switch trends were **parallel between groups**, validating our comparison
- Results held after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### **4. Effect Size Is Modest**
- Even if the +1.7 point effect is real, it represents less than **0.2 standard deviations**—considered a small effect in practice
- Natural week-to-week variation in scores is larger than the estimated treatment effect

---

## **Next Steps**

| Priority | Action | Timeline |
|----------|--------|----------|
| **1** | **Pause full rollout** – Do not mandate the new procedure across all branches until evidence is stronger | Immediate |
| **2** | **Extend data collection** – Continue monitoring for 8–12 additional weeks to improve statistical power | Q1 2026 |
| **3** | **Investigate branch characteristics** – Identify which types of branches (size, location, staffing) benefit most from the new procedure | Q1 2026 |
| **4** | **Gather qualitative feedback** – Survey branch staff about implementation experience, challenges, and perceived benefits | Q1 2026 |
| **5** | **Consider targeted expansion** – If extending the pilot, prioritize branches with characteristics associated with better outcomes | Q2 2026 |

---

## **Decision Context**

The board asked whether the new procedure **changed** service scores and whether it **improved** them as expected. Our analysis indicates:

- ✓ There is a **positive association** between the new procedure and service scores
- ✗ The evidence is **not strong enough** to confirm causation
- ✗ The effect, if real, is **smaller than anticipated**

Given the operational costs of system-wide implementation, we recommend gathering more evidence before committing all branches to the new procedure.
