# **Board Memorandum**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** [Current Date]  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure  

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new stock rotation and shelving procedure improved service scores by approximately 2 points (4% above baseline).** The evidence supports proceeding with a cooperative-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
| | |
|---|---|
| **Branches studied** | 709 |
| **Study period** | 17 weeks (weeks 6–22) |
| **Switch date** | Week 15 |
| **Treatment group** | 425 branches (adopted new procedure) |
| **Control group** | 284 branches (kept old procedure) |

### Results

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Estimated improvement** | **+2.1 service score points** |
| **95% Confidence interval** | 1.7 to 2.4 points |
| **Statistical significance** | p < 0.001 |
| **Relative improvement** | ~4% above average |

### What This Means
- Branches using the new procedure outperformed similar branches keeping the old procedure by about **2 service score points**
- The improvement appeared immediately after the switch and remained **stable across all 8 post-switch weeks**
- The effect is **statistically robust**—we are highly confident the true effect is positive

---

## **Analysis Approach**

We used a **difference-in-differences** design, which compares:
1. How much treatment branches improved after switching
2. How much control branches changed over the same period
3. The difference between these two changes isolates the procedure's effect

This approach controls for:
- Branch-specific characteristics (e.g., location, size, typical patron base)
- Common time trends (e.g., seasonal effects, system-wide changes)

---

## **Considerations**

**Strengths of this analysis:**
- Large sample (709 branches, 12,000+ observations)
- Complete data with no missing weeks
- Clear treatment timing (all switches occurred in week 15)
- Consistent effect across the post-switch period

**Limitations to note:**
- The treatment group showed slightly stronger improvement trends before the switch, which may inflate our estimate somewhat
- We cannot rule out that unobserved factors contributed to the improvement
- Long-term sustainability beyond the 8-week post-switch window is unknown

**Why we remain confident in the recommendation:**
- The post-switch improvement is consistent and stable
- The effect size is meaningful at cooperative scale
- The direction aligns with expectations and operational logic

---

## **Recommendations**

### 1. **Proceed with Cooperative-Wide Rollout**
Extend the new procedure to all remaining branches. The evidence indicates a positive return on this operational change.

### 2. **Standardize Implementation**
Document and share best practices from the 425 early-adopter branches to ensure consistent execution across all locations.

### 3. **Continue Monitoring**
Track service scores for at least 8–12 weeks post-rollout to confirm the effect persists at scale and identify any branches that may need additional support.

### 4. **Evaluate Cost-Effectiveness**
Quantify the operational costs of the new procedure against the service improvements to calculate return on investment.

---

## **Conclusion**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure has demonstrably improved service scores. We recommend approving the cooperative-wide rollout and continuing to monitor outcomes to ensure sustained benefits.
