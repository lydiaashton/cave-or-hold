# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

**Prepared for the Board of Directors**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new shelving and stock rotation procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **2.1 points** (approximately 4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This finding is statistically robust and contradicts our initial expectation that the change would have no effect. **We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Measurable Improvement in Service Scores**
- **Treatment effect:** +2.13 points on the service score index
- **95% confidence interval:** 1.01 to 3.26 points
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.0002 (highly significant)
- **Relative improvement:** Approximately 4% increase from the baseline average of 52.4

### **2. Study Design & Data Quality**
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure; **60 branches** maintained the old procedure
- **Complete data:** All branches reported for all 17 weeks
- **Switch occurred:** Week 15, allowing 9 weeks of pre-switch and 8 weeks of post-switch observation

### **3. Validity of the Analysis**
- **Parallel trends confirmed:** Before the switch, both groups showed similar trends in service scores. This validates our comparison method.
- **No confounding changes:** Staffing levels and visitor volumes did not change differently between the two groups, suggesting the improvement is attributable to the procedure change itself.

### **4. What the Data Shows**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 52.05 | 51.18 | −0.86 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 52.58 | 53.86 | +1.28 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+2.14** |

The control group experienced a slight decline in service scores over time, while the treatment group improved—making the net effect of the new procedure even more pronounced.
