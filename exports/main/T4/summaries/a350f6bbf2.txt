# **Board Memorandum**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure  

---

## **Executive Summary**

We evaluated whether the new stock rotation and shelving procedure improved branch service scores. The analysis shows a **positive effect of approximately 2 points** on the service score index. However, this result is **marginally statistically significant** (p≈0.09), meaning the evidence is suggestive but not definitive. We recommend a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Analysis Overview**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Branches Studied** | 34 total (20 new procedure, 14 old procedure) |
| **Time Period** | 17 weeks (Weeks 6–22) |
| **Switch Date** | Week 15 |
| **Method** | Difference-in-Differences with branch and week controls |

---

## **Results**

### **Service Score Changes**

| Group | Before (Weeks 6–14) | After (Weeks 15–22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------|---------------------|--------|
| **New Procedure** | 49.6 | 51.0 | **+1.5** |
| **Old Procedure** | 46.2 | 45.6 | **-0.6** |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+2.05** |

### **Statistical Confidence**

- **Estimated Effect:** +2.05 points
- **Statistical Significance:** p ≈ 0.09 (significant at 10% level, not at 5% level)
- **95% Confidence Interval:** -0.3 to +4.6 points

**What this means:** The data suggest the new procedure improves service scores, but we cannot rule out the possibility that the observed improvement is due to random variation. Additional weeks of data would reduce this uncertainty.

### **Why Results May Fall Short of Expectations**

1. **Implementation variation:** Branches may be applying the new procedure inconsistently
2. **Learning curve:** Staff may need more time to fully realize the procedure's benefits
3. **External factors:** Staffing and visitation patterns affect service scores and may mask some of the procedure's impact
4. **Sample size:** With 34 branches and 17 weeks, the study has limited statistical power to detect modest effects

---

## **Recommendations**

### **1. Proceed with Phased Rollout**
Expand the new procedure to 10–15 additional branches over the next quarter. This allows the cooperative to:
- Capture benefits where they exist
- Continue gathering evidence
- Adjust implementation based on learnings

### **2. Extend Data Collection**
Collect at least 12 additional weeks of data to:
- Increase statistical confidence
- Determine if effects grow as staff gain experience
- Identify whether benefits are consistent or vary by season

### **3. Strengthen Implementation Support**
- Provide refresher training to branches already using the new procedure
- Document and share best practices from high-performing branches
- Consider assigning implementation coaches to new adopters

### **4. Set Clear Success Metrics**
Establish thresholds for full rollout:
- **Go:** Effect size ≥2 points with p<0.05 after 12 more weeks
- **Pause:** Effect size <1 point or declining over time
- **Reassess:** Effect size 1–2 points with mixed weekly results

---

## **Conclusion**

The new procedure shows promise but has not yet demonstrated the clear, statistically definitive improvement we anticipated. A measured approach—expanding access while continuing to evaluate—balances the potential benefits against the need for evidence-based decision-making. We recommend revisiting this analysis in Q2 2027 with additional data to make a final determination on system-wide rollout.
