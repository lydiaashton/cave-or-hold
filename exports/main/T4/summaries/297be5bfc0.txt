# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**Date:** [Current Date]  
**Subject:** Recommendation on System-Wide Rollout of New Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout.**

Contrary to initial expectations, the new stock rotation and shelving procedure **did improve service scores**. Branches that adopted the new procedure saw their service scores increase by approximately **2 points** (about 4%) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant, consistent over time, and robust to alternative analyses. The evidence supports extending the new procedure to all remaining branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measurable Improvement in Service Quality

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Effect on service score | **+1.99 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | +0.76 to +3.21 |
| Statistical significance | p = 0.002 |
| Relative improvement | **~4%** vs. baseline |

The new procedure produced a meaningful and reliable improvement in the service score index.

### 2. Effect Was Stable Over Time

| Period | Effect Size |
|--------|-------------|
| Weeks 15-18 (early) | +2.12 points |
| Weeks 19-22 (later) | +1.85 points |

The benefit did not diminish during the 8-week observation period, suggesting the effect is durable rather than a temporary novelty.

### 3. Strong Research Design

- **150 branches total**: 90 adopted the new procedure, 60 kept the old
- **17 weeks of data**: 9 weeks before the switch, 8 weeks after
- **Parallel trends confirmed**: Both groups followed similar trajectories before the switch
- **Controls applied**: Results hold after accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume

### 4. Important Context

Service scores declined across *all* branches after week 14 (approximately -3 points), likely due to seasonal or system-wide factors outside the procedure change. However:

- **Switched branches** declined by only -1.1 points
- **Control branches** declined by -3.1 points

The new procedure appears to have **buffered branches against the broader downturn**, which may explain why the effect was not anticipated.

## **Appendix: Methodology Note**

This analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, which compares the change in service scores for switched branches against the change for control branches. This method isolates the effect of the procedure change from other factors affecting all branches (such as seasonal patterns). Standard errors are clustered at the branch level to account for repeated observations.

*Data source: branches.csv (2,550 observations; 150 branches × 17 weeks)*
