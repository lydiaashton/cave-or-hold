# **Memorandum to the Board**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Prepared by:** Data Analysis Team

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Roll out the new procedure to all branches.** The evidence shows the new rotation and shelving procedure **improved service scores by 2.3 points** (approximately 4-5%). This is a statistically significant, meaningful gain achieved through an operational change alone.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Service Scores Improved After the Switch

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Branches with new procedure | 52.15 | 52.30 | +0.15 |
| Branches with old procedure | 52.72 | 50.56 | −2.17 |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | | **+2.31** |

While control branches experienced a decline in service scores (possibly due to seasonal factors), branches using the new procedure maintained their performance. The **2.3-point difference** is the estimated benefit of the new procedure.

### 2. The Result Is Statistically Reliable

- **P-value < 0.001**: Less than a 0.1% chance this result occurred by random variation
- **95% confidence interval**: 1.0 to 3.6 points improvement
- **Consistent across specifications**: The finding holds when controlling for staffing levels, visitor volume, and inherent branch differences

### 3. The Improvement Was Immediate and Sustained

The service score boost appeared in the first week after implementation (week 15) and persisted through the observation period. The largest gains occurred in weeks 15-19, with effects remaining positive through week 22.

### 4. The Study Design Is Sound

Our analysis used a **difference-in-differences approach**, comparing switched branches to non-switched branches before and after the change. We confirmed that:

- Both groups followed similar trends *before* the switch (validating the comparison)
- The effect is not explained by staffing changes or visitor volume
- Individual branch characteristics do not account for the result

---

## **What This Means in Practice**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Typical service score | ~52 points |
| Improvement from new procedure | +2.3 points |
| Relative gain | +4.4% |
| Effect size | 0.24 standard deviations |

A 0.24 standard deviation improvement is considered **small-to-moderate** in operational research—particularly valuable because it was achieved through process changes rather than additional resources.

| Priority | Action |
|----------|--------|

| **3** | Monitor service scores during rollout to confirm effects persist at scale |

---

## **Data and Methods (Summary)**

- **Dataset**: 150 branches, 17 weeks of observations (weeks 6-22), 2,550 branch-week records
- **Treatment**: 90 branches adopted the new procedure starting week 15
- **Control**: 60 branches continued with the existing procedure
- **Analysis**: Regression with branch-level controls and cluster-robust standard errors

---

**Conclusion:** The new rotation and shelving procedure delivers a clear, measurable improvement in service quality at no apparent cost. We recommend immediate approval for system-wide implementation.
