# **BOARD MEMORANDUM**

---

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure  

---

## **EXECUTIVE SUMMARY**

We evaluated whether the new stock rotation and shelving procedure improved branch service scores using 17 weeks of data from 34 branches. Twenty branches adopted the new procedure in Week 15; fourteen continued with the existing procedure.

**Bottom Line:** The new procedure is associated with a modest improvement in service scores (+2.0 points, or ~4%), but this effect is **not statistically significant**. The evidence is insufficient to support a confident recommendation for system-wide rollout at this time.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **1. Treatment Effect Estimate**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect on service score | **+2.0 points** |
| Statistical significance (p-value) | **0.108** (not significant) |
| 95% Confidence Interval | **-0.4 to +4.5 points** |
| Effect size (relative to average) | **~4% improvement** |

### **2. What the Numbers Show**

```
Service Score Changes by Group

                    Before Switch    After Switch    Change
                    -------------    ------------    ------
Treatment Branches       51.4            55.1         +3.8
Control Branches         47.2            48.9         +1.7
                                                  ─────────
Net Effect (DiD)                                  +2.0*
```
*Not statistically significant at conventional levels

### **3. Interpretation**

- The **confidence interval includes zero**, meaning we cannot rule out that the procedure has no effect
- The true effect could reasonably range from slightly harmful (-0.4) to meaningfully beneficial (+4.5)
- Both treatment and control branches improved after Week 15, but we cannot confidently attribute the larger treatment-group gain to the new procedure

### **4. Analysis Validity**

✓ **Pre-trends are parallel** — Treatment and control branches showed similar trajectories before the switch, supporting the validity of our comparison  

✓ **Results are robust** — Findings hold when controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume  

⚠ **Limited statistical power** — With 34 branches and 8 post-switch weeks, we can only reliably detect effects larger than ~2.5 points

---

## **RECOMMENDATION**

**Do not proceed with immediate system-wide rollout.**

The current evidence does not meet the standard of statistical confidence typically required for cooperative-wide policy changes. A rollout based on this data carries meaningful risk that the procedure may not deliver the expected benefits.

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| **1** | **Extend data collection** by 8–12 weeks | More post-switch observations will increase statistical power and precision |
| **2** | **Continue monitoring** current treatment branches | Track whether effects persist, grow, or diminish over time |
| **3** | **Conduct subgroup analysis** | Identify whether the procedure works better at certain branch types (size, location, baseline performance) |
| **4** | **Document implementation costs** | Quantify training, labor, and operational expenses to inform cost-benefit analysis |
| **5** | **Reconvene in Q1 2027** | Review expanded data and make a final rollout decision |

---

## **ALTERNATIVE: PHASED ROLLOUT OPTION**

If the board wishes to move forward despite inconclusive evidence, we recommend a **phased implementation**:

1. Add 5–10 additional branches to the treatment group
2. Continue rigorous weekly data collection
3. Evaluate results after 12 weeks
4. Make final system-wide decision based on strengthened evidence

This approach balances innovation with evidentiary rigor.

---

## **METHODOLOGY NOTE**

This analysis employed a **difference-in-differences** design with branch fixed effects and cluster-robust standard errors. This approach isolates the effect of the new procedure by comparing changes over time between adopting and non-adopting branches, while controlling for time-invariant branch characteristics and common time trends.
