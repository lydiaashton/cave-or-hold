# MEMORANDUM

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## BOTTOM LINE

**The evidence does not support a system-wide rollout at this time.** Branches that adopted the new procedure showed a modest improvement in service scores (+2.2 points), but this result is not statistically significant and important methodological concerns prevent us from concluding the new procedure caused the improvement. We recommend extending the evaluation period and improving the study design before making a rollout decision.

---

## KEY FINDINGS

### 1. Estimated Effect on Service Scores

| Group | Pre-Switch Average | Post-Switch Average | Change |
|-------|-------------------|---------------------|--------|
| Switched branches (20 branches) | 46.7 | 48.5 | +1.8 |
| Non-switched branches (14 branches) | 50.3 | 49.8 | −0.4 |
| **Estimated effect of new procedure** | | | **+2.2 points** |

### 2. Statistical Confidence

- **P-value: 0.10** — Above the 0.05 threshold for statistical significance
- **95% Confidence Interval: −0.4 to +4.7 points** — The true effect could be zero or slightly negative
- **Interpretation:** We cannot rule out that the observed improvement occurred by chance

### 3. Critical Limitations

**Groups Were Not Comparable:** Branches that switched to the new procedure scored 3–5 points lower than control branches throughout the entire study period, both before and after the switch. This suggests switched branches may have been selected because they were already underperforming, which biases our results.

**Pre-Existing Trends Differed:** Statistical tests confirm that treatment and control branches followed different trajectories before the switch (p < 0.001), violating a key assumption of our analysis method.

**Effects Were Inconsistent:** The estimated benefit varied widely across the eight post-switch weeks, with only the final week showing a statistically significant positive effect.

---

## RECOMMENDATIONS

### Immediate Decision

**Do not proceed with system-wide rollout.** The evidence is insufficient to justify the costs and operational disruption of changing procedures at all 34 branches.

### Next Steps

| Action | Timeline | Purpose |
|--------|----------|---------|
| Continue data collection | 3–6 months | Determine if effects stabilize or grow over time |
| Randomize future rollouts | Next phase | Ensure treatment and control groups are comparable |
| Analyze branch-level heterogeneity | Ongoing | Identify which branch types benefit most |
| Audit implementation fidelity | Within 1 month | Confirm switched branches are following the new procedure correctly |

### If the Board Wishes to Proceed Anyway

Should the board decide to rollout despite the weak evidence, we recommend:
- A phased implementation with continued monitoring
- Clear success metrics defined in advance
- A commitment to reverse course if scores do not improve

---

## ANALYSIS DETAILS

- **Data:** 578 weekly observations from 34 branches (weeks 6–22)
- **Method:** Difference-in-differences regression with branch-clustered standard errors
- **Robustness:** Results consistent across multiple model specifications (with/without controls, with branch fixed effects)
- **Visualizations:** Trend charts and event study analysis available upon request
