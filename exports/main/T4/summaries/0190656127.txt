# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

**To:** Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** [Current Date]  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Shelving and Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new shelving and rotation procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw an average increase of **2.5 points** on the service score index (approximately **5% improvement**), while branches keeping the old procedure showed no meaningful change. The effect is statistically significant, consistent across most branches, and robust to alternative analytical approaches.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all remaining branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Clear Improvement in Service Quality

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 50.13 | 50.14 | +0.01 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 50.81 | 53.39 | +2.58 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+2.57** |

- **Estimated treatment effect:** +2.50 points (95% CI: 1.14 to 3.87)
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001
- **Relative improvement:** ~5% from baseline
- **Effect size:** Medium-to-large (Cohen's d = 0.59)

### 2. Strong Causal Evidence

The analysis used a **difference-in-differences** design, which isolates the effect of the procedure change by comparing:
- Branches that switched vs. those that did not
- Performance before vs. after the switch (week 15)

**Critical validation:** Pre-switch trends were parallel between groups (p = 0.40), confirming that the improvement is attributable to the procedure change rather than pre-existing differences.

### 3. Broad-Based Benefits

- **70% of treatment branches** improved after switching
- Only **55% of control branches** improved during the same period
- Results held after accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume
- Effect was consistent across the 90 treatment branches

### 4. No Apparent Downsides

The analysis found no evidence that the new procedure:
- Disrupted service during transition
- Created negative effects for any subset of branches
- Required compensating changes in staffing or resources

## **About This Analysis**

- **Data:** 150 branches, 17 weeks of observations (weeks 6-22), 2,550 total records
- **Method:** Difference-in-differences regression with branch-level clustered standard errors
- **Controls:** Staffing levels, visitor volume, branch-specific characteristics
- **Confidence:** High—the result is statistically significant and robust across multiple specifications
