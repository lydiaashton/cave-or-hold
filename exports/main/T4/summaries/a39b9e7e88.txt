# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new shelving and rotation procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **1.8 points** (approximately **3.6%** improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and represents a meaningful improvement in branch service quality. **We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15 (treatment group)
- **60 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis compares changes in service scores before and after the switch between the two groups

### **Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 50.03 | 51.47 | +1.44 |
| Non-switched branches | 48.77 | 48.43 | −0.34 |
| **Net effect** | | | **+1.78** |

- **Treatment effect: +1.78 points** (95% confidence interval: 0.60 to 2.97)
- **Statistical significance: p = 0.003** (highly significant)
- **Effect size: 3.6%** improvement relative to baseline service scores

### **Why We Trust These Results**
- **Parallel trends confirmed:** Before the switch, both groups showed similar week-to-week patterns, validating our comparison approach
- **Robust to controls:** Results hold after accounting for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume
- **Clear pattern:** Switched branches improved while non-switched branches slightly declined, ruling out system-wide factors as the cause
