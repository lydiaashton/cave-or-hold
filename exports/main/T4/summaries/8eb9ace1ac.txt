# **Memorandum to the Board of Directors**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Analysis Period:** Weeks 6–22 (17 weeks of data from 34 branches)

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout.**

The new rotation and shelving procedure **improved service scores by 2.3 points** (approximately 4.6% relative to the baseline of 50). This effect is statistically significant and practically meaningful. Branches using the new procedure improved while those keeping the old procedure experienced a slight decline.

---

## **Key Findings**

### What We Tested
We compared 20 branches that adopted the new procedure (at week 15) against 14 branches that continued with the old procedure. This design isolates the effect of the procedure change from other factors affecting all branches.

### Results

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|---------------|---------------|--------|
| New Procedure (20 branches) | 50.45 | 52.08 | **+1.63** |
| Old Procedure (14 branches) | 53.93 | 53.27 | **−0.66** |
| **Net Effect of New Procedure** | | | **+2.29** |

### Statistical Confidence
- **95% confidence interval:** 0.68 to 3.90 points
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.0055 (highly significant)
- The result is robust after controlling for branch-specific characteristics and week-to-week variations

### Additional Observations
- Treatment branches started with lower scores but showed consistent improvement after the switch
- The performance gap between treatment and control branches narrowed from 3.5 points to 1.2 points
- Control branches experienced a modest decline post-week 15, suggesting the old procedure may have emerging limitations

| Priority | Action | Timeline |

## **Conclusion**

The data provide clear evidence that the new rotation and shelving procedure improves branch service scores. The effect is statistically reliable, practically meaningful, and consistent across the 20 branches that adopted the change. We recommend proceeding with a full rollout while continuing to monitor outcomes.
