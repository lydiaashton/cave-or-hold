# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Library Cooperative Board  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** January 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes—the new procedure improves service scores.** Branches that adopted the new stock rotation and shelving procedure achieved service scores approximately **2.2 points higher** (a **4.5% improvement**) than branches that kept the old procedure. The effect is statistically significant and consistent across multiple analytical approaches. We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
- **709 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22 of the year)
- **425 branches** switched to the new procedure in week 15; **284 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Analysis compares service score changes between switching and non-switching branches, before and after the change

### Results Summary

| Measure | Value |
|---------|-------|
| **Service score improvement** | **+2.24 points** |
| 95% Confidence interval | 1.67 to 2.80 points |
| Statistical significance | Highly significant (p < 0.001) |
| Relative improvement | **4.5%** above baseline |

### What the Data Shows

1. **Clear separation after the switch**: Before week 15, both groups tracked similarly. After the switch, adopting branches pulled ahead and maintained higher scores through week 22.

2. **Benefits accumulate over time**: The improvement was modest in the first few weeks after adoption but grew stronger by weeks 20–22. This suggests staff may need a few weeks to fully adapt to the new procedure.

3. **Results hold after accounting for other factors**: The improvement remains significant even after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume, both of which also affect service scores.

---

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with system-wide rollout of the new procedure.** The evidence indicates a meaningful, positive impact on service quality that justifies broader implementation.

---

## **Next Steps**

| Action | Timeline |
|--------|----------|
| Develop rollout schedule for remaining 284 branches | Immediate |
| Prepare training materials based on early adopters' experience | 2–4 weeks |
| Implement in phases to allow for support and troubleshooting | 8–12 weeks |
| Continue monitoring service scores post-rollout | Ongoing |
| Conduct follow-up evaluation after 6 months | 6 months |

---

## **Caveats**

- Branches that volunteered to switch early may have been more motivated or better resourced, which could slightly inflate the estimated effect
- The full benefit may take several weeks to materialize as staff gain familiarity
- Continued monitoring is recommended to ensure effects persist long-term
