# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact Analysis**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new shelving and rotation procedure improved service scores.** Although we expected no change, branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.1 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant and represents about a **4% gain** in service quality. 

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **150 branches** analyzed over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15
- **60 branches** maintained the old procedure (serving as a control group)
- This natural experiment allows us to isolate the procedure's effect from other factors

### Impact on Service Scores

| Group | Before Switch (Weeks 6–14) | After Switch (Weeks 15–22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------------|---------------------------|--------|
| Control (Old Procedure) | 47.7 | 48.2 | +0.5 |
| Treatment (New Procedure) | 49.1 | 51.7 | +2.6 |
| **Net Effect of New Procedure** | | | **+2.1** |

The control group's minimal change (+0.5) shows that external factors (seasonal variation, system-wide initiatives, etc.) had little effect. The treatment group's larger gain (+2.6) indicates the procedure change itself drove the improvement.

### Why We Can Trust This Result

| Check | Result |
|-------|--------|
| **Statistical significance** | p < 0.01 (highly significant) |
| **Confidence interval** | 0.9 to 3.2 points (does not include zero) |
| **Parallel pre-trends** | ✓ Groups tracked together before week 15 (p = 0.25) |
| **Controls for staffing/visits** | ✓ Effect persists after accounting for these factors |
| **Effect size** | ~4% improvement on a ~50-point scale |

### What This Means in Practice
- Service scores average around 50 points across the system
- A 2.1-point gain is **meaningful for patron experience**
- The improvement came **without additional staffing or resources**
- The effect appeared **immediately** after implementation and persisted through week 22

## **Why This Matters**

We entered this analysis expecting no measurable impact on service scores—the procedure change was intended to improve operational efficiency, not patron-facing metrics. The fact that we observed a **clear, positive effect on service quality** suggests:

1. **Staff can implement the new procedure without sacrificing service**
2. **The new workflow may indirectly benefit patrons** (e.g., faster item location, better shelf organization)
3. **There may be additional benefits we haven't yet measured** (staff satisfaction, time savings, etc.)

This is a rare case where an operational change delivered unexpected upside. Capturing and understanding that value should be a priority.

---

## **Technical Appendix**

**Method:** Difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch). This approach compares the change in outcomes for treatment branches against the change for control branches, isolating the effect of the procedure switch from time trends and fixed branch characteristics.

**Model specification:**
```
service_score ~ switched_branch + after_switch + (switched × after) + staffing_index + visits_index
```

**Key coefficient:** The interaction term (switched × after) = 2.08, SE = 0.58, p = 0.001

**Data:** 2,550 observations (150 branches × 17 weeks), no missing values.
