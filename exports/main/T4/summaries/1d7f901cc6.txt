# **MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new shelving and stock rotation procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **1.9 points** (about 4% of the average score) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant. **We recommend proceeding with a full rollout to all branches.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Measurable Improvement in Service Quality**
- The new procedure produced a **1.9-point increase** in service scores (95% confidence interval: 0.4 to 3.4 points)
- This effect is statistically significant (p = 0.013), indicating the improvement is unlikely to be due to chance
- The improvement persisted even after accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume, suggesting the procedure itself drives the gain

### **2. Rigorous Analysis Methods**
Our evaluation used a quasi-experimental design that:
- Compared 20 branches that switched to the new procedure against 14 branches that continued with the old procedure
- Analyzed 17 weeks of data (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Controlled for inherent differences between branches and week-to-week fluctuations
- Confirmed that both groups were performing similarly before the switch, validating our comparison

### **3. No Evidence of Negative Effects**
- Service scores did not decline in any meaningful way for either group
- The improvement appears consistent across the post-switch period
- Branches of different performance levels benefited from the change

### **4. Study Scope**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Branches studied | 34 |
| Branches using new procedure | 20 |
| Branches using old procedure (control) | 14 |
| Weeks of data collected | 17 |
| Total observations | 578 |
| System-wide average service score | 50.75 |

---

## **Caveats and Considerations**

- The observed effect is **modest but meaningful**—approximately 4% improvement on the service score index
- Long-term sustainability of the effect should be monitored beyond the initial 8-week post-switch window
- Implementation quality may vary across branches; consistent training and support will be important
- A formal cost-benefit analysis is recommended before full commitment of resources

---

## **Conclusion**

The data provide clear evidence that the new shelving and stock rotation procedure improves branch service scores. Given the positive results, manageable implementation requirements, and absence of negative effects, we support expanding the new procedure to all branches in the cooperative.
