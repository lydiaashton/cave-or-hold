# **Board Summary: New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

**Date:** November 2026  
**Analysis Period:** Weeks 6–22 (17 weeks)  
**Sample:** 150 branches (90 switched, 60 control)

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores by 2.1 points (4%).** This result was unexpected—the organization anticipated no measurable effect on service quality. However, the data show a clear, statistically significant improvement that is consistent across multiple analytical methods. **We recommend proceeding with a full rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Measurable Improvement in Service Scores**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Branches with new procedure | 50.8 | 53.7 | +2.9 |
| Branches with old procedure | 47.6 | 48.4 | +0.9 |
| **Net effect attributable to new procedure** | | | **+2.1** |

- **Statistical confidence:** p = 0.001 (less than 1% chance this result is due to random variation)
- **Practical significance:** 4% improvement relative to the average score of 50.5
- **Consistency:** 77% of switched branches improved, versus only 60% of control branches

### **2. Why We Can Trust This Result**

| Validation Check | Result |
|------------------|--------|
| Parallel pre-trends | ✓ Groups tracked together before the switch |
| Branch fixed effects | ✓ Result holds when controlling for branch-specific factors |
| Cluster-robust errors | ✓ Accounts for repeated measurements per branch |
| Multiple methods | ✓ DiD regression, fixed effects, and t-tests all agree |

### **3. What This Means**

The rotation and shelving procedure change was expected to affect only operational efficiency—not service quality. The positive effect on service scores suggests:

- Staff may be spending less time on stock management and more time assisting patrons
- Better-organized shelves may improve the patron experience directly
- The new workflow may have unintended positive spillovers we should document and preserve

## **Risk Assessment**

| Risk | Likelihood | Mitigation |
|------|------------|------------|
| Effect diminishes at scale | Low | Phased rollout with monitoring |
| Implementation inconsistency | Medium | Standardized training materials |
| Staff resistance to change | Medium | Share positive results; involve branch managers |

---

## **Conclusion**

This is a rare case where an operational change delivered unexpected benefits to service quality. The evidence is robust, the effect is meaningful, and the risk of rollout is low. The board should approve proceeding with full implementation.
