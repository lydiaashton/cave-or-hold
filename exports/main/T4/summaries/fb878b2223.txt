# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.2 points** (about 4.5% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant (p = 0.001). 

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **150 branches** total: 90 adopted the new procedure, 60 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in treatment branches to changes in control branches

### Results

| Period | Control Branches (Old Procedure) | Treatment Branches (New Procedure) |
|--------|----------------------------------|------------------------------------|
| Pre-switch (weeks 6–14) | 48.88 | 50.30 |
| Post-switch (weeks 15–22) | 46.88 | 50.56 |
| **Change** | **−2.00** | **+0.26** |

- **Treatment effect: +2.26 points** (95% confidence interval: 0.89 to 3.51)
- **Statistical significance: p = 0.001**
- Control branches experienced a notable decline in service scores after week 15, while treatment branches maintained their performance

### Validity Checks
- **Parallel trends confirmed**: Before the switch, both groups showed similar trends over time, supporting the validity of our comparison
- **No pre-existing differences**: Treatment and control branches had statistically similar trajectories before the intervention
- Results hold after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all 60 branches currently using the old method

2. **Monitor implementation**: Track service scores at newly converted branches to ensure they achieve similar gains

3. **Document best practices**: Capture what made the new procedure successful for training purposes

4. **Consider timing**: Since control branches showed declining scores in the post-period, prioritize conversion to prevent further erosion of service quality

5. **Follow-up evaluation**: Re-assess service scores 3–6 months after full rollout to confirm sustained benefits

---

*Analysis based on weekly service score data from 150 branches over 17 weeks. Statistical model uses cluster-robust standard errors to account for repeated observations within branches.*
