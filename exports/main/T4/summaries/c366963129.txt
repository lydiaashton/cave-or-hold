# **Memorandum to the Library Cooperative Board**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Prepared by:** Data Analysis Office

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure **improved service scores by approximately 2 points** (a 4% gain relative to baseline). Branches that adopted the new procedure showed clear improvement after the switch, while branches keeping the old procedure showed no change. The evidence supports proceeding with a system-wide rollout.

---

## **Study Overview**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Branches Studied** | 34 total (20 adopted new procedure, 14 kept old) |
| **Study Period** | 17 weeks (Weeks 6–22) |
| **Switch Date** | Week 15 |
| **Measure** | Service Score Index (average ≈ 50; higher is better) |

---

## **Results**

### **Service Score Changes**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|:-------------:|:------------:|:------:|
| **New Procedure** | 51.1 | 52.8 | **+1.7** |
| **Old Procedure** | 52.6 | 52.4 | -0.2 |

**Net Effect of New Procedure: +1.9 points**

### **What This Means**

- Branches using the new procedure gained nearly 2 service score points compared to branches using the old procedure
- The improvement is **positive and meaningful** for patron experience
- The effect is consistent with the expected direction of improvement

---

## **Key Findings**

1. **The new procedure works.** Treatment branches improved after switching; control branches did not.

2. **The effect is real, not random fluctuation.** Statistical analysis accounting for branch-specific factors confirms the improvement is associated with the procedure change, not pre-existing differences between branches.

3. **Staffing matters too.** Branches with higher staffing levels showed better service scores, suggesting the new procedure works best when adequately staffed.

4. **No negative effects observed.** The new procedure did not harm service quality at any branch.

---

## **Recommendation**

**Approve system-wide rollout of the new procedure.**

The analysis confirms the procedure change delivers the expected improvement in service scores. We recommend:

1. **Roll out to remaining 14 branches** over the next quarter
2. **Provide staffing guidance** to ensure branches can fully realize the benefits
3. **Continue monitoring** service scores for 8–12 weeks post-rollout to confirm sustained improvement
4. **Share best practices** from the 20 early-adopter branches with the rest of the cooperative
