# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure produced a measurable improvement in service scores—contrary to initial expectations.** Branches that switched saw service scores increase by approximately **2 points** (about 4%) relative to branches that kept the old procedure. The effect is statistically significant and held steady over the 8-week observation period. The data supports proceeding with a full rollout, though the unexpected positive impact warrants attention to understand the underlying mechanism.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Unexpected Positive Effect
- The new procedure increased service scores by **+1.99 points** on average (95% confidence interval: +0.76 to +3.21)
- This represents roughly a **4% improvement** relative to the baseline average score of 54 points
- Statistical significance: p = 0.002 (less than 1% probability this is due to chance)

### 2. Effect Was Stable Over Time
| Period | Effect Size |
|--------|-------------|
| Weeks 15-18 (early) | +2.12 points |
| Weeks 19-22 (later) | +1.85 points |

The benefit did not diminish over the observation period, suggesting a sustained impact rather than a temporary novelty effect.

### 3. Rigorous Comparison
- **150 branches total**: 90 switched to the new procedure, 60 remained on the old procedure
- Pre-switch trends were parallel between groups (no pre-existing differences)
- Results hold after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume
- Analysis uses difference-in-differences estimation with branch-level controls

### 4. Broader Context
- All branches experienced a modest decline in service scores after week 14 (approximately -3 points), likely due to seasonal or system-wide factors
- Switched branches declined less (-1.1 points) than control branches (-3.1 points)
- The new procedure appears to have **buffered branches against the broader downturn**

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with rollout** to remaining 60 branches—the data indicates a clear benefit

2. **Investigate the mechanism**—since no effect was expected, understanding *why* service scores improved could:
   - Help refine the procedure further
   - Identify which specific changes drove the improvement
   - Inform future operational decisions

3. **Continue monitoring** post-rollout to confirm the effect persists at system-wide scale

4. **Document learnings** for future procedural changes—this outcome suggests some operational adjustments may have unintended positive spillovers on service quality

---

*Analysis based on weekly service scores from 150 branches over 17 weeks (weeks 6-22). The procedure switch occurred at week 15. Statistical model uses difference-in-differences estimation with branch fixed effects and standard errors clustered at the branch level.*
