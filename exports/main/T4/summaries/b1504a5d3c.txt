# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact Analysis**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new shelving and rotation procedure improved service scores—contrary to expectations.** While the procedure change was not anticipated to affect service quality, branches that adopted it saw their service scores increase by approximately **2.1 points** (about 4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and represents a meaningful, unexpected benefit. We recommend proceeding with the system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Unexpected Improvement in Service Scores**

| Group | Before Switch (Weeks 6-14) | After Switch (Weeks 15-22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------------|---------------------------|--------|
| New Procedure Branches (90 branches) | 50.11 | 52.35 | **+2.23** |
| Old Procedure Branches (60 branches) | 50.61 | 50.73 | +0.12 |

**Net effect of the new procedure: +2.11 points**

### **2. Statistical Confidence**

- The improvement is **statistically significant** (p < 0.01)
- 95% confidence interval: **+0.92 to +3.38 points**
- The analysis controlled for staffing levels and visitor volume

### **3. Broad-Based Benefits**

- **76% of switched branches** saw their service scores improve
- Only **53% of control branches** improved during the same period
- The effect represents about **0.23 standard deviations**—a meaningful improvement

### **4. Valid Comparison**

- Before the switch, both groups followed nearly identical trends (correlation of 0.99)
- This confirms the improvement is attributable to the procedure change, not pre-existing differences

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** to all 60 branches still using the old procedure. The service score improvement is an added benefit beyond the operational goals of the change.

2. **Monitor implementation** during the transition to confirm newly switched branches see similar gains.

3. **Investigate the mechanism**: Since this improvement was unexpected, consider gathering qualitative feedback from staff at switched branches to understand *why* service scores improved. This could inform future operational decisions.

4. **Document and share** this finding with branch managers—the new procedure delivers operational benefits *plus* improved service quality.

5. **Follow-up evaluation** in 3-6 months to confirm the improvement is sustained.

---

*Analysis prepared using difference-in-differences regression with branch-clustered standard errors. 150 branches, 17 weeks of data, 2,550 observations.*
