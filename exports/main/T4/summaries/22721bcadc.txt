# **Memorandum to the Board**

## **Subject: Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

### **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores—contrary to initial expectations.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **2.1 points** (approximately **4% improvement**) compared to branches that retained the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and consistent across all post-switch weeks. We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.

---

### **Key Findings**

| Finding | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Treatment Effect** | +2.13 points (95% CI: 0.97 to 3.30) |
| **Statistical Significance** | p < 0.001 |
| **Baseline Score** | 48.9 (average before switch) |
| **Percentage Improvement** | ~4% |
| **Branches Analyzed** | 150 total (90 switched, 60 control) |
| **Study Period** | 17 weeks (weeks 6-22) |

1. **Unexpected but Clear Result**: The analysis was undertaken with the understanding that the procedural change would likely have no measurable impact on service scores. The data show otherwise: switched branches consistently outperformed control branches after week 15.

2. **Valid Comparison**: Pre-switch trends (weeks 6-14) were parallel between groups, supporting the credibility of the difference-in-differences approach. Both groups experienced similar week-to-week fluctuations before the intervention.

3. **Consistent Across Time**: The treatment group outperformed the control group in every single week after the switch (weeks 15-22), with no evidence of the effect diminishing over time.

4. **Not Explained by Other Factors**: The result holds when controlling for staffing levels and visit volumes, indicating the improvement is attributable to the procedure change itself.

### **Caveats**

- This analysis measures service score impact only; a full cost-benefit assessment should accompany the rollout decision.
- The effect size, while statistically significant, is modest in absolute terms (2 points on a ~50-point scale).
- Continued monitoring is recommended to ensure the effect persists at scale.
