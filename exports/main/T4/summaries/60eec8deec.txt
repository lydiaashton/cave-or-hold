# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.0 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents roughly a **4% improvement** in service quality. The effect is statistically significant and consistent across multiple analytical approaches.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
- **709 branches** tracked over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- **425 branches** adopted the new procedure; **284 branches** kept the old procedure
- Switch occurred in **week 15**
- Analysis compares changes in service scores before and after the switch between the two groups

### Results

| Metric | Control Branches (Old Procedure) | Treatment Branches (New Procedure) |
|--------|----------------------------------|------------------------------------|
| Average score before switch | 49.5 | 50.7 |
| Average score after switch | 49.0 | 52.2 |
| **Change** | **−0.5** | **+1.5** |

**Net effect of new procedure: +1.95 points** (95% CI: 1.4 to 2.5; p < 0.001)

### Why We Trust This Result
- **Parallel trends:** Before the switch, both groups followed similar weekly patterns
- **Controls accounted for:** The effect holds after adjusting for staffing levels and visitor volume
- **Branch differences handled:** Analysis accounts for inherent differences between individual branches
- **Large sample:** Over 12,000 weekly observations provide strong statistical power

### What This Means
- The average branch service score is approximately 50
- A 2-point improvement is meaningful for patron experience
- Control branches actually declined slightly during this period, making the treatment effect more notable

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all remaining branches

2. **Develop training materials** to ensure consistent implementation across all locations

3. **Monitor service scores** for 8–12 weeks post-rollout to confirm the effect persists at scale

4. **Document best practices** from early-adopter branches to support the rollout

5. **Consider follow-up analysis** to understand which aspects of the new procedure drive the improvement (e.g., stock availability, retrieval speed, organization)
