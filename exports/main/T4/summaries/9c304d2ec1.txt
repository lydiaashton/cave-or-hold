# **Evaluation of New Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

**To:** Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Subject:** Recommendation on System-Wide Rollout of New Branch Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Roll out the new procedure to all branches.** Contrary to our initial expectations, the data shows the new procedure **significantly improved service scores**. Branches using the new procedure scored **2.2 points higher** (about 4% better) than comparable branches using the old procedure. The finding is statistically robust and consistent across the 8-week post-switch period.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **What We Tested**
- 90 branches adopted the new procedure in week 15; 60 branches kept the old procedure
- We compared service score changes in switched branches against control branches
- This design isolates the procedure's effect from other factors (seasonal trends, staffing, visitor volume)

### **What We Found**

| Finding | Result |
|---------|--------|
| **Service score improvement** | +2.20 points |
| **Confidence range** | 1.0 to 3.4 points |
| **Statistical confidence** | 99.9% (p = 0.0003) |
| **Pre-switch trends** | No difference between groups (validates comparison) |
| **Effect consistency** | Positive in all 8 post-switch weeks |

### **Why This Is Credible**

1. **Strong research design**: The control group accounts for system-wide changes (e.g., seasonal demand, budget cycles) that might affect all branches.

2. **Pre-trends check passed**: Before week 15, switched and control branches moved together—confirming the groups were comparable.

3. **Result survives controls**: The effect persists after adjusting for staffing levels and visitor volume.

4. **No obvious alternative explanation**: We tested whether excluding the transition week changed the result—it did not (effect remains +2.05 points).

---

## **Recommendation**

**Approve system-wide rollout.** The evidence indicates the new procedure delivers a meaningful, measurable improvement in service quality at no apparent cost to the metric we track. Given that:

- The effect is statistically significant and practically meaningful (~4% improvement)
- The finding is robust across multiple analytical checks
- There is no evidence of negative side effects in the data

The prudent course is to extend the benefit to all 150 branches.

## **Appendix: Technical Summary**

- **Method**: Difference-in-differences regression with branch-level clustered standard errors
- **Sample**: 2,550 observations (150 branches × 17 weeks)
- **Model**: Controls for staffing index and visits index
- **Effect size**: 2.20 points (95% CI: 1.03–3.38; p < 0.001)
- **Parallel trends test**: Not significant (p = 0.88), supporting causal interpretation
