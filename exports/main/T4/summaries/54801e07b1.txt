# **Memorandum to the Board of Directors**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure  

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new stock rotation and shelving procedure improved service scores by approximately 2 points (4% of average). This improvement is statistically significant and consistent across analytical methods. We recommend proceeding with a full rollout to all branches.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. The New Procedure Works

Branches that adopted the new procedure experienced a **2.04-point increase** in service scores compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is:

- **Statistically significant** (p = 0.002)
- **Practically meaningful** (4% improvement on a ~50-point scale)
- **Robust** across multiple analytical approaches

### 2. Study Overview

| | |
|---|---|
| **Branches Studied** | 150 total (90 switched, 60 did not) |
| **Time Period** | 17 weeks (Weeks 6–22) |
| **Switch Date** | Week 15 |
| **Observations** | 2,550 branch-week records |

### 3. Results at a Glance

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (Old Procedure) | 50.83 | 50.34 | −0.49 |
| Treatment (New Procedure) | 49.89 | 51.45 | +1.56 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+2.04** |

Treatment branches improved while control branches declined slightly, yielding a net gain of 2.04 points attributable to the new procedure.

### 4. Analysis Is Reliable

- **No pre-existing differences**: Treatment and control branches showed similar trends before the switch
- **Controls accounted for**: Results hold when adjusting for staffing levels and visitor volume
- **Consistent findings**: Same result across all statistical specifications tested

---

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with full rollout of the new procedure to all remaining branches.**

The evidence strongly supports that the new procedure improves service quality. The 90 branches that switched are serving patrons better than they were before, and better than branches that did not switch.
