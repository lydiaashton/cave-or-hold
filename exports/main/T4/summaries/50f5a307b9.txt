# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD OF DIRECTORS**

---

**TO:** Board of Directors  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 2025  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation & Shelving Procedure  

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure works.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure achieved service scores **2.5 points higher** than comparable branches using the old procedure—a **5% improvement** that is statistically significant and meaningful for patron experience.

**We recommend approving system-wide rollout to all 150 branches.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### The Evidence

| Metric | Treatment Branches (New) | Control Branches (Old) |
|--------|-------------------------|------------------------|
| Number of branches | 90 | 60 |
| Pre-switch score | 50.21 | 59.82 |
| Post-switch score | 52.55 | 49.64 |
| **Change** | **+2.34** | **-0.18** |

**Net effect of new procedure: +2.53 points (p < 0.001)**

### Why We're Confident

1. **Rigorous design:** This is a natural experiment with treatment and control groups observed before and after the change
2. **Parallel pre-trends:** Both groups performed similarly before the switch, validating the comparison
3. **Statistically robust:** The result holds after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume
4. **Precise estimate:** 95% confidence interval is 1.22 to 3.83 points—entirely positive

### What This Means

- A 2.5-point gain represents approximately **one-quarter of a standard deviation** in service scores
- This is a **substantial, noticeable improvement** in branch service quality
- No adverse effects were detected on staffing efficiency or visitor patterns

## **Appendix: Analysis Details**

- **Data source:** Weekly service scores for all branches (weeks 6–22)
- **Method:** Difference-in-Differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors
- **Sample:** 2,550 observations across 150 branches
- **Statistical software:** Python (statsmodels)
