# **Memorandum to the Board**

## **Title: Impact of New Shelving and Rotation Procedure on Library Service Scores**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **2.2 points** (approximately **4.4%**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and consistent. We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **709 branches** total: 425 switched to the new procedure, 284 kept the old procedure (control group)
- Data covers **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach to isolate the effect of the new procedure from other factors

### Main Result
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Estimated effect** | +2.19 service score points |
| **95% Confidence Interval** | [1.62, 2.76] |
| **Statistical significance** | p < 0.001 |
| **Effect size** | 4.4% increase from baseline |

### What This Means
- **Treatment branches** improved by **4.06 points** on average after the switch
- **Control branches** improved by **1.86 points** over the same period
- The **net effect attributable to the new procedure** is **2.2 points**

### Reliability of Findings
- The result holds when controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume
- Pre-switch trends were similar between groups, supporting the validity of the comparison
- The effect is consistent across all post-switch weeks

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches.

2. **Monitor implementation** during rollout. Track service scores for the first 8–12 weeks after each branch switches to ensure similar gains are achieved.

3. **Investigate variation across branches.** Some branches may benefit more than others; understanding this could help optimize implementation.

4. **Conduct cost-benefit analysis.** If the new procedure requires additional staff time or resources, weigh the 4.4% service score improvement against those costs.

5. **Continue data collection.** Ongoing monitoring will confirm the effect persists and identify branches that may need additional support during transition.
