# **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure shows a **positive but statistically inconclusive effect** on service scores. Branches that switched to the new procedure improved by an estimated **1.5 points more** than branches that kept the old procedure (approximately 3% improvement relative to baseline). However, this difference is not statistically significant—we cannot rule out that the observed improvement occurred by chance. 

**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure; **14 branches** kept the old procedure
- Data covers **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- **578 total observations** (weekly scores for all branches)

### Results

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 47.3 | 51.6 | +4.3 |
| Non-switched branches | 48.0 | 50.8 | +2.8 |
| **Difference** | | | **+1.5** |

- **Effect estimate:** +1.53 points
- **95% confidence interval:** -0.59 to +3.65 points
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.16 (not statistically significant)
- **Pre-trend test:** Passed (p = 0.52) — no evidence of differential trends before the switch

### Branch-Level Patterns
- **18 of 20** switched branches showed improvement (mean +4.3 points)
- **11 of 14** non-switched branches also improved (mean +2.8 points)
- Both groups improved overall, but switched branches improved more on average
- Two switched branches saw declines, suggesting the procedure may not benefit all branches equally

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Extend the evaluation period** to increase statistical power and detect smaller effects with greater confidence.

2. **Investigate why some branches benefited more than others.** Examine whether branch characteristics (size, staffing, visit volume) moderate the procedure's effectiveness.

3. **Expand the new procedure to additional branches** while maintaining a control group for ongoing evaluation.

4. **Conduct a cost-benefit analysis** to assess whether operational costs are justified by the modest (~3%) service score improvement.

5. **Evaluate additional outcomes** beyond the service score index, such as staffing efficiency and patron satisfaction.
