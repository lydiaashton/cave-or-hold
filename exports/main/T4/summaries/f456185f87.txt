# **Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores by approximately 2 points (about 4% relative to the baseline of 50).** This effect is statistically significant and consistent across multiple analytical approaches. We recommend proceeding with system-wide rollout while monitoring branch-level implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measurable Improvement in Service Scores
- Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores **increase by 2.05 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure
- 95% confidence interval: **1.5 to 2.6 points** (highly statistically significant, p < 0.001)
- This represents approximately a **4% improvement** relative to the average branch score of 50

### 2. Strong Evidence from Multiple Analyses
| Analysis Approach | Estimated Effect |
|-------------------|------------------|
| Basic difference-in-differences | +2.05 points |
| With staffing/visits controls | +2.07 points |
| Strict pre-period (excluding weeks 13-14) | +2.43 points |

The consistency across specifications strengthens confidence in the result.

### 3. Branch-Level Performance
- **54.6%** of switched branches showed improvement vs. only **36.6%** of non-switched branches
- **71%** of switched branches outperformed the average non-switched branch
- Some variation exists: top performers gained 3+ points, while a minority saw declines

### 4. Important Context
- Non-switched branches experienced a **decline** of 1.3 points during the same period
- Switched branches not only avoided this decline but achieved a net **gain of 0.7 points**
- The new procedure appears to provide resilience against factors that negatively affected service scores system-wide

### 5. Methodology Note
This analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing switched vs. non-switched branches before and after week 15. This method controls for:
- Time-invariant branch characteristics (e.g., location, size)
- System-wide trends affecting all branches (e.g., seasonal patterns, budget changes)

One caveat: switched branches showed slight improvement starting 1-2 weeks before the official switch date, possibly indicating early adoption or anticipation effects. This would make our main estimate **conservative** (the true effect may be slightly larger).

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches

2. **Develop implementation support** for branches that showed limited gains:
   - Identify best practices from top-performing switched branches
   - Provide targeted training where needed

3. **Continue monitoring** service scores post-rollout to:
   - Confirm effects persist at scale
   - Identify any branches needing additional support

4. **Investigate complementary factors**:
   - Staffing and visit patterns also correlate with service scores
   - Consider whether the new procedure works best under certain conditions
