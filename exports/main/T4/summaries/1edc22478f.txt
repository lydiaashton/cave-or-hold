# **Memorandum to the Board**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Office  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure shows a **positive but statistically uncertain effect** on service scores. Switched branches improved by approximately 1.8 points relative to non-switched branches, but this result could plausibly be due to chance. We recommend **continuing the pilot and collecting more data** before deciding on a system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **What We Studied**
- 34 branches tracked over 17 weeks (February through June)
- 20 branches adopted the new procedure in mid-April (week 15); 14 branches kept the old procedure
- Service scores average around 50 points; higher scores indicate better service

### **What We Found**

| Group | Before Mid-April | After Mid-April | Change |
|-------|------------------|-----------------|--------|
| Branches with new procedure | 49.3 | 51.4 | +2.1 |
| Branches with old procedure | 50.9 | 51.1 | +0.2 |
| **Estimated effect of new procedure** | | | **+1.8** |

- The new procedure is associated with a **1.8-point improvement** in service scores
- However, this estimate has considerable uncertainty: the true effect could reasonably range from **slightly negative (-0.5) to moderately positive (+4.2)**
- The result is **not statistically significant** at conventional levels (p = 0.13)

### **Why We Trust This Analysis**
- Switched and non-switched branches were on similar trajectories before the change, supporting a valid comparison
- The analysis accounts for inherent differences between branches and week-to-week variation
- Results are unchanged when controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

---

## **Recommendations**

### **Immediate Action**
**Do not yet roll out the new procedure system-wide.** The evidence is suggestive but not conclusive. A premature rollout risks implementing changes that may not deliver meaningful benefits.

### **Next Steps**

1. **Extend the pilot by 8–12 weeks.** Additional data will allow us to detect smaller effects with greater confidence.

2. **Verify implementation quality.** Confirm that all switched branches are fully and consistently applying the new procedure.

3. **Reassess in Q3 2026.** With more data, we can provide a more definitive recommendation on system-wide adoption.

4. **Conduct a cost-benefit review.** Even if the 1.8-point improvement is real, the board should weigh this against any additional labor, training, or operational costs.
