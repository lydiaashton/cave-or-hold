# **Library Cooperative Procedure Change: Impact Analysis**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new shelving and rotation procedure had a statistically significant positive effect on service scores.** Contrary to the expectation that it would make no difference, branches that adopted the new procedure maintained their service scores while branches using the old procedure experienced a notable decline. **The evidence supports rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **150 branches** analyzed over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** switched to the new procedure at week 15; **60 branches** continued with the old procedure
- This natural experiment isolates the effect of the procedure change using a difference-in-differences approach

### **Service Score Results**

| Group | Before Switch (Weeks 6–14) | After Switch (Weeks 15–22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------------|---------------------------|--------|
| **New Procedure** (90 branches) | 51.24 | 51.19 | −0.05 |
| **Old Procedure** (60 branches) | 51.81 | 49.45 | −2.36 |

### **Statistical Evidence**
- **Treatment effect: +2.23 points** (95% confidence interval: 0.93 to 3.53)
- **p-value < 0.001** — highly statistically significant
- **Effect size:** 0.23 standard deviations (4.4% of baseline average)
- Results are **robust** across multiple model specifications, including controls for staffing levels and visitor traffic

### **Interpretation**
The new procedure appears to have **protected against a decline** in service quality rather than producing dramatic improvement. Control branches experienced a 2.4-point drop after week 15, while treatment branches remained stable. This suggests the old procedure may have become less effective over time, or external factors affected service delivery—and the new procedure helped branches adapt.

### **Validation**
Before the switch (weeks 6–14), both groups showed similar service score patterns with no systematic divergence, supporting the validity of the comparison.

---

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with system-wide rollout of the new procedure.** The evidence indicates it helps maintain service quality, with no indication of negative effects.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Implement rollout plan** — Extend the new procedure to remaining 60 branches

2. **Investigate control group decline** — Understanding the 2.4-point drop could reveal additional improvement opportunities (e.g., seasonal effects, staffing changes, or other operational factors)

3. **Continue monitoring** — Track service scores during rollout to ensure consistent results across all branches

4. **Conduct cost-benefit analysis** — If the new procedure requires additional resources (training, time, equipment), weigh those costs against the 2.2-point service score benefit

---

*Analysis prepared using difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors. Results validated with branch and week fixed effects.*
