# **Memorandum to the Board**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2025  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and rotation procedure is associated with a **modest improvement** in service scores (+2.4 points, or ~5%), but the evidence is **not statistically conclusive**. While the direction of the effect is positive and aligns with expectations, we cannot rule out the possibility that the observed improvement occurred by chance. We recommend a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Service Score Impact**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Branches that switched (20 branches) | 49.2 | 51.2 | **+2.0** |
| Branches that did not switch (14 branches) | 49.4 | 49.0 | **-0.4** |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+2.4 points** |

### **Statistical Confidence**
- **p-value: 0.10** — Exceeds the conventional 0.05 threshold for statistical significance
- **95% confidence interval: -0.5 to +5.4 points** — The true effect could range from slightly negative to moderately positive
- **Interpretation:** Approximately 90% confidence the effect is positive, but not 95% confident

### **Branch-Level Outcomes**
- **65% of switched branches** (13 of 20) improved their scores
- **36% of non-switched branches** (5 of 14) improved their scores
- Individual branch results varied considerably, ranging from -8 to +10 points

### **Validation**
- Treatment and control branches had nearly identical scores before the switch (49.2 vs. 49.4)
- This supports the validity of the comparison between groups

---

## **Recommendations**

### **1. Proceed with Phased Expansion**
The evidence points in the right direction. Roll out the new procedure to additional branches while maintaining a control group for continued evaluation.

### **2. Extend the Observation Period**
Eight weeks of post-switch data provides limited statistical power. Collect 8–12 more weeks of data to improve confidence in the results.

### **3. Investigate Branch-Level Variation**
Some branches improved significantly while others declined. Identify factors that distinguish successful adopters (e.g., staff training, branch size, layout) to improve implementation.

### **4. Establish a Decision Checkpoint**
Re-evaluate after one full quarter of expanded implementation. If the effect remains positive and becomes statistically significant, proceed with system-wide adoption.

---

## **Limitations**

- **Sample size:** 34 branches over 17 weeks limits statistical power
- **Short post-period:** Only 8 weeks of data after the switch
- **External factors:** Weekly score volatility suggests other influences (seasonality, staffing, community events)
- **Non-random assignment:** Branches were not randomly assigned to switch, though pre-switch balance suggests minimal selection bias

---

## **Conclusion**

The new procedure shows promise but requires further validation before system-wide adoption. A cautious, data-driven expansion approach will allow the cooperative to confirm benefits while minimizing risk.
