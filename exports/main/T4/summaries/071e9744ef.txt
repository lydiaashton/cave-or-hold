# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD OF DIRECTORS**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure  

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

The new procedure shows a **positive but statistically uncertain effect** on service scores. Branches using the new procedure improved by approximately **2 points** (about 4%) more than branches keeping the old procedure, but this difference is not statistically significant at conventional levels. The data does not provide strong enough evidence to conclude the new procedure definitively improves service. 

**Recommendation: Do not proceed with immediate system-wide rollout.** Instead, extend the evaluation period and gather more evidence before making a final decision.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### Study Overview
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6-22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure at week 15; **14 branches** maintained the old procedure
- Analysis uses difference-in-differences methodology to isolate the procedure's effect from other factors

### Results Summary

| Group | Pre-Switch Avg (Weeks 6-14) | Post-Switch Avg (Weeks 15-22) | Change |
|-------|----------------------------|-------------------------------|--------|
| Control (Old Procedure) | 49.7 | 50.9 | +1.2 |
| Treatment (New Procedure) | 53.6 | 56.9 | +3.3 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+2.1** |

### Statistical Assessment

| Metric | Value | Interpretation |
|--------|-------|----------------|
| Estimated Effect | +2.1 points | Treatment branches improved 2.1 points more than control |
| 95% Confidence Interval | -0.4 to +4.6 | True effect could be zero or slightly negative |
| p-value | 0.10 | Not statistically significant at 0.05 threshold |
| Effect Size | ~4% of baseline | Modest practical significance |

### Critical Observations

1. **Parallel trends confirmed**: Before the switch, both groups showed similar weekly trends, validating our comparison approach.

2. **Pre-existing differences**: Branches that chose to switch had higher service scores throughout the study (approximately 4 points higher on average), even before the procedure change. This self-selection may bias results upward.

3. **Both groups improved**: Service scores increased for all branches after week 15, suggesting external factors (seasonal patterns, system-wide initiatives) influenced results.

4. **Staffing and visits matter**: Higher staffing levels and visit volumes are significantly associated with better service scores, confirming these are important operational drivers.

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

### Immediate Actions

| Priority | Action | Timeline |
|----------|--------|----------|
| **1** | **Pause system-wide rollout** | Immediate |
| **2** | Continue monitoring current branches for 8-12 additional weeks | Ongoing |
| **3** | Document implementation fidelity at each treatment branch | Within 4 weeks |
| **4** | Prepare randomized assignment protocol for future testing | Within 8 weeks |

### Decision Framework

| Future Evidence | Recommended Action |
|-----------------|-------------------|
| Statistically significant positive effect (p < 0.05) | Proceed with phased rollout |
| Positive but uncertain effect (current state) | Extend evaluation; conduct cost-benefit analysis |
| No effect or negative effect | Discontinue new procedure |

### Additional Considerations

1. **Cost-benefit analysis**: Before any rollout, quantify implementation costs (training, labor, disruption) against the modest potential gain of ~2 service score points.

2. **Branch heterogeneity**: Investigate whether the procedure works better for certain branch types (size, location, patron demographics).

3. **Broader outcomes**: Consider tracking additional metrics (patron satisfaction, circulation rates, operational efficiency) to capture the full impact.

4. **Randomized evaluation**: For future policy changes, randomly assign branches to treatment and control groups to eliminate self-selection bias.

---

## **CONCLUSION**

While the new procedure shows promise, the evidence is insufficient to justify the cost and disruption of a system-wide change. A measured approach—extending the evaluation period, improving the research design, and gathering more data—will enable a more confident decision that serves the cooperative's best interests.
