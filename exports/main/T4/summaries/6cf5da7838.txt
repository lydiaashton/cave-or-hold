# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Impact Assessment of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new stock rotation and shelving procedure significantly improved service scores.** Despite initial expectations that the change would make no difference, branches that adopted the new procedure experienced service score increases of **2.1 points (4.3%)** compared to branches that retained the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant and consistent across the post-implementation period.

**We recommend proceeding with system-wide rollout to all branches.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** switched to the new procedure in week 15; **60 branches** maintained the old procedure
- Service scores average approximately 50 points system-wide (higher scores indicate better service)

### Impact Results

| Measure | Value |
|---------|-------|
| Estimated effect of new procedure | **+2.13 points** |
| Statistical significance | **p = 0.001** |
| Relative improvement | **+4.3%** |
| 95% Confidence interval | **0.87 to 3.39 points** |

### What the Data Shows

**Before the switch (weeks 6–14):** Treatment and control branches performed similarly, with no meaningful difference in service scores.

**After the switch (weeks 15–22):** Treatment branches pulled ahead, averaging 51.3 points compared to 49.5 points for control branches—a gap that emerged only after the procedure change.

### Why We Trust These Results

Our analysis accounts for:
- Inherent differences between individual branches
- Week-to-week variation across the system
- Factors like staffing levels and visitor volume
- The possibility that branches self-selected into the new procedure

Pre-switch data confirms treatment and control branches were on parallel trajectories before the change, supporting the validity of our conclusions.

---

## **Recommendation**

**Approve immediate rollout of the new procedure to all remaining branches.** The evidence indicates this change delivers measurable service improvements without apparent drawbacks.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Authorize system-wide implementation** for the 60 branches still using the old procedure

2. **Establish rollout timeline** with clear milestones and support resources

3. **Continue monitoring service scores** to verify effects persist at full scale

4. **Document procedures and train staff** to ensure consistent implementation

5. **Report back to the board** within one quarter on rollout progress and outcomes
