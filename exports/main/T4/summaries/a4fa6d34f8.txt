# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores more than expected.** While we anticipated no measurable change, branches that switched to the new shelving and rotation procedure saw service scores increase by **2.3 points** (approximately **5% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and sustained. We recommend proceeding with system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **150 branches** total: 90 adopted the new procedure, 60 kept the old one
- **17 weeks of data**: Weeks 6–14 (before switch), Weeks 15–22 (after switch)
- Analysis compares changes in switched branches to changes in non-switched branches (difference-in-differences)

### **Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 49.75 | 53.60 | +3.85 |
| Non-switched branches | 49.73 | 51.08 | +1.35 |
| **Net effect** | | | **+2.50** |

After controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume, the estimated effect is **+2.33 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.0 to 3.7 points; p = 0.001).

### **Why We Trust This Result**

1. **Comparable groups**: Before the switch, both groups had nearly identical average scores (~49.7) and followed parallel trends
2. **Effect is robust**: The result holds whether or not we control for staffing and visits
3. **Effect is sustained**: Improvement persisted across all 8 post-switch weeks with no decay
4. **Statistically significant**: Less than 0.1% probability this result occurred by chance

### **Important Context**
- This was an observational rollout, not a randomized trial
- Service score baseline is ~50; a 2.3-point gain is meaningful
- The mechanism behind the improvement warrants further investigation

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** to the remaining 60 branches
2. **Investigate why the procedure helped** — this was unexpected and understanding the mechanism could inform future improvements
3. **Monitor post-rollout scores** for 8–12 weeks to confirm similar gains across all branches
4. **Consider cost-benefit analysis** if the new procedure has different implementation costs than the old one

---

*Analysis based on weekly service scores from 150 branches, weeks 6–22. Statistical model controls for branch-level variation, staffing index, and visits index. Standard errors clustered by branch.*
