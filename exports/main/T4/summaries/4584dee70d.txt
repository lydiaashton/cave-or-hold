# **Analysis of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Contrary to expectations, the new procedure produced a measurable improvement in service scores.** Branches using the new procedure scored approximately 2 points higher than expected—a statistically significant gain of about 4% relative to the average score. Given this unexpected positive result, we recommend proceeding with the full rollout while continuing to monitor outcomes.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. **Unexpected Positive Effect**
- The new procedure is associated with a **2.04-point increase** in service scores
- This effect is **statistically significant** (p = 0.002), meaning there is less than a 1% chance this result occurred by random variation
- The 95% confidence interval ranges from **0.74 to 3.35 points**—even the conservative estimate shows a positive effect

### 2. **What the Data Shows**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (Old Procedure) | 50.83 | 50.34 | -0.49 |
| Treatment (New Procedure) | 49.89 | 51.45 | +1.56 |
| **Net Effect of New Procedure** | | | **+2.04** |

Treatment branches improved while control branches declined slightly, yielding a net gain attributable to the new procedure.

### 3. **Study Strength**
- **150 branches** across 17 weeks (2,550 observations)
- **90 treatment branches**, 60 control branches
- **Pre-trends were parallel**: Treatment and control branches tracked together before the switch, supporting the validity of the comparison
- Results are robust to controlling for staffing levels, visitor volume, and branch-specific characteristics

### 4. **Why This May Have Been Missed**
The analysis suggests the new procedure may have had indirect benefits not anticipated in the original design—for example, improved staff efficiency, better stock accessibility, or reduced time spent on inventory tasks that freed staff for patron service. Further investigation could identify the specific mechanisms.

---

## **Recommendations**

| Action | Rationale |
|--------|-----------|
| **Proceed with full rollout** | The data shows a clear, positive effect with low risk of harm |
| **Document implementation details** | Capture what branches did differently to ensure consistent execution |
| **Monitor during rollout** | Track service scores to confirm effects persist at scale |
| **Investigate mechanisms** | Survey staff to understand *why* scores improved—this could reveal additional optimization opportunities |

---

## **Caveats**

- This analysis measures association, not causation, though the research design strongly supports a causal interpretation
- The effect size is modest but meaningful given the scale of the cooperative
- Long-term sustainability of the effect should be verified with continued monitoring

---

## **Conclusion**

While the new procedure was not expected to affect service scores, the evidence indicates it has produced a real, positive improvement. This represents a welcome surprise and a low-cost opportunity to enhance service across the cooperative.
