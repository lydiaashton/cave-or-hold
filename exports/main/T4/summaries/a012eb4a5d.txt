# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Office  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure  

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure shows a **positive but statistically inconclusive effect** on service scores. Branches that switched to the new procedure improved by an average of **2.4 points** more than control branches, but this difference could plausibly be due to random variation. **We recommend gathering more data before committing to a system-wide rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure at week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure as a control group
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in switched branches against changes in control branches

### Results

| Metric | Switched Branches | Control Branches |
|--------|------------------|------------------|
| Before switch (weeks 6–14) | 48.8 | 50.3 |
| After switch (weeks 15–22) | 51.0 | 50.1 |
| **Change** | **+2.2** | **-0.2** |

**Estimated Treatment Effect: +2.4 points**  
**95% Confidence Interval: -0.7 to +5.4 points**

### Statistical Significance
- The effect is **not statistically significant** at conventional levels (p = 0.125)
- The confidence interval includes zero, meaning we cannot rule out that the new procedure has no effect
- With the current sample size, we could reliably detect effects of approximately **4 points or larger**

### Validity Checks
- ✅ **Parallel trends confirmed**: Before the switch, both groups followed similar trajectories (p = 0.58), supporting the validity of the comparison
- ⚠️ **Variable weekly effects**: Post-switch improvements were inconsistent week-to-week, with some weeks showing strong gains and others showing no effect

---

## **Next Steps**

### Recommended Actions

1. **Continue monitoring for 8–12 more weeks**
   - The current 8-week post-switch window may be too short to detect a stable effect
   - Extended data would narrow the confidence interval and provide greater certainty

2. **Consider a phased rollout**
   - If operational benefits (e.g., staff efficiency, inventory accuracy) exist beyond service scores, a gradual expansion to additional branches could be warranted
   - Continue tracking service scores to build evidence

3. **Investigate weekly variation**
   - The inconsistent week-to-week effects suggest other factors (staffing, visits, seasonal patterns) may influence scores
   - Understanding these dynamics could help optimize implementation timing

4. **Set a decision threshold**
   - Define the minimum improvement that would justify system-wide adoption (e.g., 3 points, 5 points)
   - Calculate the sample size needed to detect that effect with confidence

---

## **Conclusion**

The new procedure shows **promise but not proof**. The 2.4-point improvement aligns with expectations, but the evidence is not yet strong enough to guarantee system-wide benefits. A modest investment in continued data collection would enable a more confident decision and reduce the risk of adopting a procedure that may not deliver meaningful improvements across all branches.
