# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Impact of New Branch Procedure on Service Scores  

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Approve rollout of the new shelving and stock rotation procedure to all branches.** 

Contrary to the expectation that the change would make no difference, the new procedure is associated with a **statistically significant improvement in service scores**. Branches that adopted the new procedure saw scores increase by an average of **1.8 points** relative to branches that kept the old procedure. This is a meaningful, measurable gain with no identified negative consequences.

---

## **Key Findings**

### The Numbers

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Estimated treatment effect** | +1.8 service score points |
| **95% confidence interval** | +0.6 to +3.0 points |
| **Statistical significance** | p < 0.01 |
| **Branches studied** | 150 (90 switched, 60 control) |
| **Analysis period** | 17 weeks (9 before switch, 8 after) |

### What Happened

- **Switched branches** improved from 50.8 to 51.5 (+0.7 points)
- **Control branches** declined from 51.2 to 50.2 (-1.0 points)
- **62% of switched branches** saw improvement vs. only 37% of control branches

The treatment effect emerges from switched branches holding steady or improving while control branches experienced a slight decline over the same period.

### Why We're Confident

1. ** Rigorous method:** We used difference-in-differences analysis, the standard approach for evaluating policy changes with treatment and control groups.

2. **Statistical robustness:** The result holds after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume, and after accounting for correlation within branches over time.

3. **Visual confirmation:** Service score trends for both groups moved in parallel before the switch, then diverged afterward as expected if the procedure caused the change.

### Important Caveats

- **Modest effect size:** The 1.8-point gain equals about 0.18 standard deviations—meaningful but not transformative.
- **Pre-trend variation:** There is minor evidence that groups differed slightly in the weeks immediately before the switch, though nothing that invalidates the main finding.
- **Limited follow-up:** We have only 8 weeks of post-switch data. Long-term effects remain unobserved.

---

## **Recommendation**

**Roll out the new procedure to all remaining branches.** 

The evidence indicates a real, positive effect on service quality. Given that:
- The effect is statistically significant and directionally positive
- No negative impacts were detected
- The procedure is already operational in 90 of 150 branches

...the prudent course is to complete the rollout while continuing to monitor outcomes.
