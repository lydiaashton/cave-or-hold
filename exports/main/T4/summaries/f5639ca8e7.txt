# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new shelving and rotation procedure saw their service scores increase by **2.1 points** (approximately 4%) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant. **We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measured Impact
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Treatment Effect | **+2.13 service score points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | **+0.98 to +3.28** |
| Statistical Significance | **p = 0.0003** |
| Relative Improvement | **+4.2%** |

### 2. What Happened to Each Group

| Group | Branches | Pre-Switch Avg | Post-Switch Avg | Change |
|-------|----------|---------------|-----------------|--------|
| **Switched to new procedure** | 90 | 50.5 | 51.0 | +0.5 |
| **Kept old procedure** | 60 | 51.3 | 49.7 | −1.6 |

All branches experienced a general decline after week 15 (likely seasonal). The switched branches not only avoided this decline but improved—resulting in a net gain of 2.1 points relative to the control group.

### 3. Analysis Validity

✓ **Parallel trends confirmed:** Before the switch (weeks 6-14), both groups followed similar trajectories (p = 0.20).

✓ **Robust to controls:** Results hold when accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume.

✓ **Statistically rigorous:** Model includes branch fixed effects and clustered standard errors.

### 4. Visual Evidence

The attached chart (`did_analysis_chart.png`) illustrates that both groups tracked together before week 15, then diverged positively after the switch, with the gap persisting through the observation period.

---

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all 60 branches currently using the old method.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Implement rollout** over the next 4-6 weeks with training support for transitioning branches.

2. **Monitor results** for 8-12 weeks post-rollout to confirm the effect persists at scale.

3. **Capture best practices** from the 90 early-adopter branches to support smooth implementation.

4. **Re-evaluate in 6 months** to ensure sustained improvement and identify any branches needing additional support.

---

## **Methodology Summary**

Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** design comparing 90 treatment branches to 60 control branches across 17 weeks (2,550 observations). The model isolates the procedure effect by controlling for time trends and branch-specific characteristics.
