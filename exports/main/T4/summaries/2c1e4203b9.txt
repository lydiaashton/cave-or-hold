# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

## **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

### **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure produced an unexpected but measurable improvement in service scores.** Although the procedure change was not expected to affect service quality, branches that adopted it saw service scores increase by approximately **1.9 points** relative to branches that kept the old procedure. This finding is statistically significant. Given the unexpected nature of this result, we recommend a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than immediate system-wide implementation.

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. Unexpected Positive Effect on Service Scores**
- Treatment branches (90 branches that switched) improved by an average of **3.4 points** from before to after the switch
- Control branches (60 branches that did not switch) improved by only **1.5 points** over the same period
- The **net effect of the new procedure is +1.9 points** on the service score index
- *Note: This effect was not anticipated, as the procedure change was designed for operational efficiency, not service quality*

**2. Statistical Significance**
- The effect is statistically significant (p < 0.01)
- 95% confidence interval: **+0.8 to +3.1 points**
- Results hold across multiple analytical specifications

**3. Consistent Across Most Branches**
- **83% of treatment branches** saw their service scores improve after switching
- Only 68% of control branches improved during the same period
- 72% of switching branches outperformed the average control branch

**4. Valid Comparison Confirmed**
- Treatment and control branches had similar service scores before the switch (48.0 vs. 47.3, difference not statistically significant)
- Both groups followed parallel trends before week 15, supporting the validity of the comparison
- The improvement appeared after the switch and persisted through the observation period

**5. Effect Size in Context**
- The average branch service score is approximately 50
- A 1.9-point improvement represents roughly a **4% increase**
- While statistically significant, this is a modest effect

---

### **Important Considerations**

**Why Might This Have Happened?**

The analysis identifies *that* scores improved, but not *why*. Possible explanations worth investigating:

- **Staff behavior:** The new procedure may have freed up staff time for patron assistance
- **Workflow efficiency:** Faster shelving could mean materials are available sooner
- **Morale effect:** Staff engaged by the change may have provided better service
- **Coincidence:** Some unmeasured factor may have affected treatment branches differently
- **Measurement artifact:** The service score index may capture something indirectly related to shelving

**Study Limitations**

- **Observation period:** Only 8 weeks of post-switch data; longer-term effects are unknown
- **Mechanism unclear:** We cannot explain why an operational change affected service scores
- **Generalizability:** Effects during rollout may differ from effects at steady state

---

### **Recommendations**

**1. Proceed with Cautious, Phased Rollout**
- The data shows a positive effect with no detected harms
- However, given the unexpected nature of the finding, expand gradually rather than all at once
- Consider rolling out to 20–30 additional branches over the next quarter

**2. Investigate the Mechanism**
- Interview staff at high-performing treatment branches to understand what changed
- Document any workflow or behavioral changes that accompanied the procedure switch
- This will help determine whether the effect is sustainable and replicable

**3. Continue Monitoring**
- Track service scores for all branches during continued rollout
- Watch for any branches where scores decline after switching
- Extend the observation period to assess whether effects persist beyond 8 weeks

**4. Revisit After Additional Data**
- Schedule a follow-up review after 6 months of expanded implementation
- If effects persist and the mechanism is understood, proceed with full system-wide adoption
- If effects fade or remain unexplained, reconsider the business case for rollout

---

### **Conclusion**

The new procedure has delivered an unexpected benefit: improved service scores alongside whatever operational benefits were originally intended. While this is welcome news, the surprising nature of the finding warrants a measured approach. A phased rollout with active monitoring will allow the cooperative to capture the benefit while managing the risk that the effect may not hold at scale or over time.
