# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

---

## **Subject: Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

**Date:** November 2025  
**Prepared by:** Data Analysis Team

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by **2.2 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This finding is statistically robust and contradicts our initial expectation that the change would have no effect.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with system-wide rollout to all remaining branches.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **1. The Numbers**

| Metric | Result |
|--------|--------|
| **Treatment effect** | **+2.19 points** on service score |
| **95% Confidence interval** | **0.98 to 3.41 points** |
| **Statistical significance** | **p = 0.0004** |
| **Relative improvement** | **~4% of average score** |

### **2. Study Overview**

- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure at week 15; **60 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Service scores average ~50 points across the cooperative (higher is better)

### **3. What Happened**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 50.99 | 53.26 | **+2.28** |
| Control branches | 50.45 | 50.53 | +0.08 |
| **Net effect (DiD)** | | | **+2.19** |

### **4. Why We Trust This Result**

✓ **Parallel trends verified:** Before the switch, both groups followed statistically indistinguishable trajectories (p = 0.88)

✓ **Robust across methods:** The 2.2-point effect holds under multiple analytical approaches, including controls for staffing and visitor patterns

✓ **Persistent effect:** Improvement sustained throughout the 8-week post-switch period with no sign of fading

✓ **Clean identification:** Branch and week fixed effects control for branch-specific characteristics and common temporal shocks

## **NEXT STEPS**

### **1. Approve System-Wide Rollout**
Extend the new procedure to all 60 branches still using the old method. Expected benefit: **+2.2 points** average service score improvement across the cooperative.

### **2. Implementation Plan**
- Develop a phased transition schedule for remaining branches
- Provide training and support materials based on experience from the 90 early-adopter branches
- Establish a 4-week monitoring period post-switch for each branch

### **3. Ongoing Monitoring**
- Track service scores at newly-switched branches to confirm effect replication
- Flag any branches showing unusual patterns for additional support
- Report quarterly to the board on rollout progress and outcomes

### **4. Follow-Up Analysis**
- Conduct cost-benefit analysis of full rollout (training costs vs. service improvements)
- Investigate *why* the procedure works (faster shelving? better organization? improved workflow?)
- Explore whether effects vary by branch size, location, or staffing levels

## **TECHNICAL APPENDIX**

**Method:** Difference-in-differences regression with branch and week fixed effects. Standard errors clustered at the branch level to account for within-branch correlation.

**Model specification:**
```
service_score = β₀ + β₁(treatment) + branch_fixed_effects + week_fixed_effects + ε
```

**Where:** treatment = switched_branch × after_switch (interaction term)

**Data:** 2,550 observations (150 branches × 17 weeks)

**Software:** Python (statsmodels)

---

## **CONCLUSION**

The evidence is clear: the new rotation and shelving procedure improves service quality. Our initial assumption—that the change would make no difference—was incorrect. The board should approve immediate rollout to all remaining branches.
