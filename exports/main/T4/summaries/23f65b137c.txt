# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores—and the effect is larger than anticipated.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by **2.2 points** (approximately **4.6%**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant, sustained over time, and was not expected based on prior assumptions. We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Study Design**

This was a pilot evaluation to test whether the new procedure would affect service quality. We analyzed weekly service scores from **150 branches** over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22 of the year). The new procedure was implemented at week 15:

| Group | Branches | Procedure |
|-------|----------|-----------|
| Treatment | 90 branches | New procedure (weeks 15–22) |
| Control | 60 branches | Old procedure (entire period) |

This "difference-in-differences" design isolates the effect of the new procedure by comparing changes in the treatment group to changes in the control group, accounting for any overall trends affecting all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Service Score Improvement

| Group | Before Switch (Weeks 6–14) | After Switch (Weeks 15–22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------------|---------------------------|--------|
| New Procedure Branches | 48.2 | 52.2 | +4.0 |
| Old Procedure Branches | 48.2 | 50.0 | +1.8 |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | — | — | **+2.2** |

The **2.2-point improvement** is the estimated causal effect of the new procedure, after accounting for trends that affected all branches equally.

### 2. Statistical Significance

- The effect is **highly statistically significant** (p < 0.01)
- 95% confidence interval: **1.0 to 3.5 points**
- We can be very confident the improvement is real and not due to random variation

### 3. Parallel Trends Validation

A critical assumption of this analysis is that treatment and control branches were on similar trajectories before the switch. The data confirms this:

- **Pre-switch difference between groups:** Essentially zero (−0.02 points on average)
- **No statistically significant differences** in any pre-switch week
- This validates that the post-switch improvement is attributable to the procedure change

### 4. Consistent Effect Over Time

The improvement was sustained across all 8 post-switch weeks, with effects ranging from **2.9 to 4.0 points** each week. There is no evidence the effect is fading—branches maintained the improvement throughout the observation period.

### 5. Effect Size Context

- Average service score across all branches: ~50 points
- The 2.2-point improvement represents a **4.6% increase** over baseline
- For context: a branch scoring 48 would be expected to score about 50 after adopting the new procedure

---

## **Why This Matters**

This pilot was undertaken with neutral expectations—the procedural change was not anticipated to affect service scores. The positive result is therefore noteworthy:

- **Unexpected benefit:** A procedural change designed for operational efficiency also improved patron-facing service quality
- **Low-risk improvement:** The new procedure requires no additional staffing or budget
- **Scalable impact:** Rolling out to remaining 60 branches would lift system-wide service scores

---

## **Next Steps**

### Recommended Actions

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all 60 branches still using the old procedure

2. **Develop implementation timeline**—consider phasing the rollout to manage training and transition workload

3. **Continue monitoring** service scores after full rollout to confirm the effect persists at scale

4. **Document and share learnings**—this unexpected positive outcome may inform future operational changes

### Additional Considerations

- **Training:** Ensure staff at transitioning branches receive adequate training on the new procedure
- **Timing:** The analysis covers 8 weeks post-switch; longer-term effects should be monitored
- **Complementary factors:** The analysis controlled for staffing and visit levels, but branches should ensure these remain stable during transition

---

## **Methodology Notes**

- Analysis used difference-in-differences regression with branch and week fixed effects
- Standard errors clustered by branch to account for repeated observations
- Controlled for staffing index and visits index to isolate the procedure effect
- All statistical tests use robust standard errors
