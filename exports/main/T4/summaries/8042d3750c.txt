# **Board Memorandum**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Executive Summary**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure **moved service scores in the expected direction**, with adopting branches improving by approximately **2.4 points** relative to branches that retained the old procedure. However, the evidence is **not yet strong enough** to confirm the improvement was caused by the procedure rather than normal variation. We recommend **continuing the rollout with enhanced monitoring** to build a stronger evidence base before committing all branches.

---

## **Bottom Line**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did scores improve as expected? | **Yes** — the effect is positive (+2.4 points) |
| Are we confident it worked? | **Not yet** — results could be due to chance |
| Should we roll out to all branches? | **Proceed with caution** — expand while continuing to measure |

---

## **Key Findings**

### The Numbers

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Branches adopting new procedure | 20 |
| Branches keeping old procedure | 14 |
| Estimated improvement from new procedure | **+2.38 points** |
| Range of plausible true effects | **-0.8 to +5.5 points** |
| Confidence level | **86%** (below the 95% standard) |

### What Happened

- **Before the switch:** Treatment branches averaged 48.8; control branches averaged 50.3
- **After the switch:** Treatment branches rose to 51.0; control branches held steady at 50.1
- **Net effect:** Treatment branches gained **2.4 points** relative to control branches

### Why We Can't Be Certain

The study included 8 weeks of post-switch data across 20 branches. This sample size can detect large effects but lacks power to confirm moderate improvements with high confidence. The data is **consistent with a meaningful benefit** but also **consistent with no effect at all**.

---

## **Recommendations**

### Immediate Actions (Next 90 Days)

1. **Expand to 10 additional branches** using a staggered rollout to maintain comparison groups
2. **Standardize implementation** — document and train on the new procedure to ensure consistency
3. **Extend the evaluation period** by 12 weeks to strengthen statistical evidence

### Decision Triggers

| If future analysis shows... | Then... |
|-----------------------------|---------|
| Effect remains +2 or higher with p < 0.05 | **Full rollout recommended** |
| Effect shrinks toward zero | **Pause and investigate implementation** |
| Effect becomes negative | **Revert to old procedure** |

### Additional Metrics to Track

The service score is one measure. We recommend also monitoring:
- Staff time spent on shelving and rotation
- Patron satisfaction surveys
- Circulation and hold fulfillment rates
- Stock turnover rates

---

## **Conclusion**

The new procedure is **moving service scores in the right direction**, which validates the underlying hypothesis. The board faces a choice between:

- **Waiting for definitive proof** (delays potential benefits but reduces risk)
- **Expanding while learning** (captures benefits sooner but accepts some uncertainty)

Given the positive signal and low implementation risk observed to date, we recommend **controlled expansion with continued measurement**. This approach balances innovation with accountability.
