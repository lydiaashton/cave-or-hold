# **Board Summary: New Shelving & Rotation Procedure Impact Analysis**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Recommend system-wide rollout.** Contrary to expectations that the procedure change would have no impact on service scores, the data shows a **statistically significant improvement of approximately 2.4 points** (about **5%**). This represents an unexpected gain with no identified downsides.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. The New Procedure Improved Service Scores

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Branches that switched | 49.96 | 51.40 | +1.44 |
| Branches that did not switch | 51.28 | 50.26 | −1.01 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+2.45** |

Branches using the new procedure improved while those keeping the old procedure slightly declined.

### 2. The Result Is Statistically Reliable

- **95% Confidence Interval**: +1.7 to +3.2 points
- **p-value**: < 0.001 (less than 1 in 1,000 chance this is random noise)
- Finding holds across all analytical methods tested

### 3. Study Design Was Sound

- **150 branches** tracked over 17 weeks (Weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted new procedure at Week 15; **60 branches** continued old procedure
- Both groups showed nearly identical trends before the switch, validating the comparison
- Staffing levels and visitor volumes were similar across groups

### 4. Why This Matters

The board expected **no change** in service scores from this operational shift. Instead, we found:

- A meaningful **positive effect** on patron experience
- No negative impact on staffing efficiency or visit patterns
- An opportunity to improve service without additional cost

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining 60 branches

2. **Begin implementation** within the next quarter, with target completion by mid-year

3. **Track service scores** post-rollout to confirm the effect persists at system-wide scale

4. **Capture lessons learned** from early-adopter branches to streamline training

---

*Analysis based on 2,550 weekly observations from 150 branches. Difference-in-differences methodology with branch and week fixed effects. Full technical documentation available upon request.*
