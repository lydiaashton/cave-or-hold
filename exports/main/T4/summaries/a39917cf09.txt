# **Memorandum to the Board**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Analysis Period:** Weeks 6–22 (17 weeks of data)

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure is associated with a **modest improvement in service scores** (+1.9 points, or ~4%). However, this result is **not statistically significant** when accounting for the structure of the data. The evidence points in a positive direction with no signs of harm, but we cannot confidently rule out the possibility of no effect.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **phased rollout** to remaining branches, accompanied by continued monitoring to confirm the benefit persists.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **What We Tested**
- **34 branches** tracked over 17 weeks
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure as a control group
- We compared how service scores changed in switching branches versus non-switching branches

### **Results**

| | Switched Branches | Non-Switched Branches |
|---|---|---|
| **Before** (weeks 6–14) | 45.7 | 49.5 |
| **After** (weeks 15–22) | 48.1 | 50.0 |
| **Change** | **+2.4** | **+0.5** |

**Net estimated effect of new procedure: +1.9 points**

### **How Confident Are We?**
- The 95% confidence interval ranges from **-0.4 to +4.1 points**
- This means the true effect could be slightly negative, zero, or meaningfully positive
- Statistical significance: **p = 0.12** (above the conventional 0.05 threshold)

### **Supporting Evidence**
- ✓ Switched and non-switched branches followed similar trends before the change
- ✓ No negative effects detected on staffing or visit patterns
- ✓ The point estimate (+1.9) is consistent across multiple analytical approaches
- ⚠ Switched branches started with lower baseline scores, but our method accounts for this

---

## **Recommendations**

### **1. Proceed with Phased Rollout**
The balance of evidence favors adoption:
- Positive point estimate with no evidence of harm
- Lower bound of confidence interval suggests minimal downside risk
- Operational benefits may extend beyond what service scores capture

### **2. Implement Monitoring Protocol**
- Track service scores for **8–12 weeks post-rollout** at each newly converted branch
- Establish a **monthly review** during the rollout period
- Success threshold: Sustained improvement of **1.5+ points**

### **3. Document and Learn**
- Interview branch managers at high-performing switched branches to identify success factors
- Create a brief implementation guide capturing best practices
- Consider a follow-up evaluation after 6 months of full implementation

### **4. Decision Triggers**

| If We Observe | Then |
|---|---|
| Scores improve or hold steady | Continue rollout; document as standard practice |
| Scores decline after initial gain | Pause and investigate; consider branch-specific adjustments |
| No clear pattern after 3 months | Re-convene board to reassess based on expanded data |

---

## **Questions for Discussion**

1. Does a ~4% improvement in service scores justify the operational cost of retraining all branches?
2. Should we prioritize rollout to lower-performing branches first (where the absolute gain may be largest)?
3. What additional metrics should we track alongside service scores during the rollout?
