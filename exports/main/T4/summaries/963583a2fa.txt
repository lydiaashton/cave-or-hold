# **Analysis of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure: Impact on Service Scores**

---

## **Bottom Line**

Contrary to expectations, the new stock rotation and shelving procedure **did change service scores**—and the change was **positive**. Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **1.8 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is **statistically significant** and has been sustained over eight weeks.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measurable Improvement in Service Scores
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Treatment effect | +1.8 points |
| 95% Confidence interval | 0.8 to 2.9 points |
| Statistical significance | p = 0.001 |
| Relative improvement | 3.6% above average |

### 2. Rigorous Analysis Approach
The analysis compared 150 branches over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22), using branches that kept the old procedure as a control group. This approach accounts for:
- Pre-existing differences between branches
- Overall time trends affecting all branches
- Weekly variations in staffing and visitor volumes

### 3. Effect Is Stable Over Time
The improvement has not faded. Service score gains remained consistent at **4.5 to 6.4 points** throughout all eight weeks after implementation, indicating the benefit is durable.

### 4. Clean Data, Reliable Results
- Complete data for all 150 branches across all 17 weeks
- No missing values
- Stable pre-treatment trends between groups, supporting valid comparison

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches.

2. **Monitor implementation** during rollout to ensure consistent adoption and identify branches needing additional support.

3. **Continue tracking service scores** for 8–12 weeks post-rollout to confirm the effect persists at scale.

4. **Document best practices** by investigating why the new procedure improved scores (e.g., faster item location, better organization) to reinforce training.

5. **Review costs** associated with the new procedure (training, time, materials) against the service improvement to confirm net benefit.

---

*Analysis based on 2,550 weekly observations from 150 branches. Statistical models control for staffing levels, visit volumes, and branch-specific characteristics.*
