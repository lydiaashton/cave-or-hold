# **Memorandum to the Board of Directors**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2025  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and rotation procedure is associated with a **modest improvement in service scores** (+1.9 points, approximately 4%). However, this improvement is **not statistically significant** at conventional levels, meaning we cannot confidently rule out that the observed difference occurred by chance. The evidence points in a positive direction but is not definitive. We recommend a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than immediate systemwide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Overview**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure; **14 branches** maintained the old procedure
- The switch occurred at **week 15**
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach to isolate the procedure's effect from other factors

### **Results Summary**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Estimated Effect** | +1.86 service score points |
| **Relative Improvement** | ~3.6% above baseline |
| **Statistical Significance** | p = 0.13 (not significant at 0.05 level) |
| **95% Confidence Interval** | -0.5 to +4.2 points |

### **What the Data Shows**

1. **Treatment branches** (switched to new procedure) maintained stable service scores after the switch (+0.3 points)

2. **Control branches** (kept old procedure) experienced a **decline** in service scores after week 15 (-1.6 points)

3. The **net effect** of the new procedure is **+1.9 points**—the difference between these two trends

4. **Pre-trend validation**: Before the switch (weeks 6–14), both groups followed similar patterns with no systematic differences, supporting the validity of our analysis

5. **Staffing matters**: Higher staffing levels are significantly associated with better service scores, suggesting the new procedure may help branches maintain performance during staffing fluctuations

---

## **Recommendations**

### **Primary Recommendation: Phased Rollout**

1. **Proceed with rollout** to remaining branches, but implement in **phases** rather than all at once

2. **Establish ongoing monitoring** with monthly service score tracking for at least 6 months post-rollout

3. **Document implementation details** at each branch (timing, training, staffing) to identify conditions that affect effectiveness

4. **Set a review checkpoint** at 3 months to evaluate whether improvements are sustained

### **Alternative: Extended Pilot**

If the board prefers stronger evidence before full rollout:
- Extend the pilot to additional branches for another 8–12 weeks
- This would increase statistical power and provide more precise estimates
- Consider targeting branches with lower baseline scores, where the procedure may have greater impact

---

## **Considerations for Decision-Making**

| Factor | Assessment |
|--------|------------|
| **Direction of Effect** | Positive ✓ |
| **Statistical Certainty** | Moderate (not definitive) |
| **Practical Significance** | Modest but meaningful (~4% improvement) |
| **Risk of Harm** | Low (no negative effects observed) |
| **Implementation Cost** | To be weighed against benefits |

---

## **Technical Notes**

- Analysis controls for branch-specific characteristics and week-to-week variation
- Standard errors are clustered at the branch level to account for repeated observations
- Results are robust to controlling for staffing and visit volume
- The parallel trends assumption (required for this analysis) is supported by pre-period data
