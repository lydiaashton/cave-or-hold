# Library Cooperative Board Summary
## New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure Evaluation

---

## Bottom Line

**The new procedure is associated with modestly higher service scores, but we cannot confidently conclude it caused the improvement.** Branches that adopted the new procedure differed systematically from those that kept the old one, making it impossible to isolate the true effect. We recommend **against a system-wide rollout at this time** and propose a more rigorous evaluation before any expansion decision.

---

## Key Findings

### What the Numbers Show

| Group | Branches | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|----------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| New Procedure | 20 | 51.72 | 52.86 | +1.14 |
| Old Procedure | 14 | 53.35 | 52.40 | −0.95 |

**Observed Difference: +2.1 points** (treatment branches improved while control branches declined)

### What the Statistics Tell Us

- **After accounting for staffing and visitor volume**, the estimated effect is **+2.2 points** with marginal statistical significance (p = 0.09)
- **In plain terms:** If the procedure caused the improvement, it's a modest gain—about 4% of the average service score
- **However, there is a critical problem:** The branches selected for the new procedure were already on different trajectories than those that kept the old procedure

### Why We Can't Draw Firm Conclusions

For a valid comparison, treatment and control branches should have followed similar patterns before the switch. **They did not.** Statistical testing confirms the two groups had significantly different pre-intervention trends (p < 0.0001).

**This means the observed +2 point difference may reflect:**
- Pre-existing differences between branches (location, community, management)
- Selection bias in which branches were chosen for the new procedure
- **Not necessarily the procedure itself**

---

## Recommendations

### Decision: Pause System-Wide Rollout

The evidence is **suggestive but not conclusive**. Rolling out the new procedure to all 34 branches based on this analysis carries risk—we may invest resources in a change that doesn't deliver the expected benefit.

### Next Steps

| Priority | Action | Timeline |
|----------|--------|----------|
| **1** | Collect 8-12 additional weeks of data to see if the pattern holds | Immediate |
| **2** | Document why specific branches were selected for the new procedure | 2 weeks |
| **3** | Consider a randomized or matched-pair design for future evaluation | Next quarter |
| **4** | Track additional outcomes (patron satisfaction, staff workload, processing times) | Ongoing |

### If Proceeding with Caution

If the board wishes to move forward despite the uncertainty:
- Implement in phases with continued monitoring
- Establish clear success metrics and a review date
- Be prepared to reverse course if results don't materialize

---

## Questions for Board Discussion

1. What was the basis for selecting which branches received the new procedure?
2. What level of improvement would justify the cost of system-wide implementation?
3. Are there operational or political considerations beyond the service score data?
