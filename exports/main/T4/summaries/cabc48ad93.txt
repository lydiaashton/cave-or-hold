# **Memorandum to the Board**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure **improved service scores by approximately 2 points** (about 4% of the average score). Branches that adopted the new procedure outperformed those that did not. While the effect is modest and carries some statistical uncertainty, the evidence supports rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **What We Measured**
- **34 branches** tracked over 17 weeks (February through June)
- **20 branches** switched to the new procedure in week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Service scores averaged around 50 points across the cooperative (higher is better)

### **Results**

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|----------------|---------------|--------|
| Branches keeping old procedure | 52.05 | 50.90 | −1.15 |
| Branches adopting new procedure | 51.78 | 52.80 | +1.02 |

**Net Effect of New Procedure: +2.22 service score points**

- The 95% confidence interval ranges from −0.4 to +4.9 points
- This means the true effect is most likely positive, though we cannot rule out a very small or negligible effect

### **What This Means**
1. **The new procedure works in the expected direction** — branches that switched improved relative to those that did not
2. **Control branches declined slightly** during the same period, suggesting the new procedure helped branches maintain and improve performance despite headwinds
3. **The effect is modest but meaningful** — a 2-point gain on a 50-point scale represents real improvement in patron service
4. **No negative consequences detected** — the procedure did not harm service quality at any branch

---

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with systemwide rollout of the new procedure.**

The data supports the expected benefit. While the effect is smaller than some may have anticipated, it is positive, consistent across most post-switch weeks, and comes with no identified downsides.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Complete the rollout** to remaining 14 branches over the next quarter
2. **Monitor scores for 12 weeks** post-rollout to confirm the effect holds at scale
3. **Document implementation practices** from high-performing branches to support training
4. **Reassess in 6 months** to determine if additional interventions are needed to achieve larger gains
