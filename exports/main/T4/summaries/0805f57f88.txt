# **Memorandum to the Board of Directors**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2025  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure  

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure is associated with a **modest improvement** in service scores (+1.7 points), but this effect is **not statistically significant** at conventional confidence levels. We cannot confidently conclude that the new procedure caused better service outcomes. The board should consider a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
- **34 branches** participated: 20 adopted the new procedure, 14 continued with the old procedure
- Data covers **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring in **week 15**
- Service scores average around 50 points (higher is better)

### Results

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 50.08 | 53.27 | +3.20 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 50.57 | 55.46 | +4.89 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+1.70** |

### Statistical Confidence
- The **1.70-point improvement** has a **p-value of 0.14** (14% probability this could occur by chance)
- This does **not meet** the standard 5% threshold for statistical significance
- When accounting for staffing and visitor volume, the effect rises to **2.04 points (p=0.07)**—marginally significant but still inconclusive

### Important Context
- **Both groups improved** after week 15, suggesting external factors (seasonal trends, system-wide initiatives, or other changes) boosted scores across all branches
- Pre-switch trends between groups were **reasonably parallel**, supporting the validity of the comparison
- The new procedure shows **no negative effects** on service scores

---

## **Recommendations**

1. **Continue monitoring** the 20 branches using the new procedure for an additional 8–12 weeks to strengthen the evidence base

2. **Proceed with a phased rollout** to additional branches while maintaining a control group for ongoing comparison

3. **Assess implementation fidelity**—branches with stronger adherence to the new procedure may show larger effects worth studying

4. **Conduct a cost-benefit analysis**—even if the 1.7-point improvement is real, determine whether operational costs justify the modest gain

5. **Reconvene in Q2 2026** with updated data to make a final system-wide rollout decision
