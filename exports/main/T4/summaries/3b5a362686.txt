# **Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with system-wide rollout of the new rotation and shelving procedure.** The evidence shows the new procedure meaningfully improves service scores.

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure increased service scores by approximately **1.9 points (3.7%)** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant and practically meaningful. Without intervention, service scores appear to have been drifting downward; the new procedure reversed this trend for adopting branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

| Metric | Switched Branches | Non-Switched Branches |
|--------|-------------------|----------------------|
| Before switch (weeks 6–14) | 51.8 | 49.8 |
| After switch (weeks 15–22) | 52.5 | 48.6 |
| **Change** | **+0.7** | **−1.2** |

**Net effect of new procedure: +1.9 points**

- **Statistical confidence:** p < 0.001 (highly significant)
- **Sample:** 709 branches, 12,053 weekly observations
- **Robustness:** Result holds after controlling for staffing levels, visit volumes, branch characteristics, and seasonal variation

---

## **Why This Matters**

1. **The control group declined.** Branches keeping the old procedure saw service scores drop by 1.2 points. This suggests external factors (seasonal, operational, or otherwise) were putting downward pressure on scores across the system.

2. **The new procedure not only prevented decline—it produced gains.** Switched branches improved by 0.7 points despite the same headwinds.

3. **The effect is consistent.** The improvement appears across multiple model specifications and is not driven by a small subset of branches.

## **Caveats**

- Switched branches had slightly higher baseline scores than non-switched branches. Our analysis accounts for this through difference-in-differences estimation and branch fixed effects.
- The observation window is 17 weeks. Longer-term effects should be monitored.
- Staffing and visit volumes also affect service scores. Branches facing resource constraints may need additional support during transition.
