# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

---

## **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

### **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new stock rotation and shelving procedure experienced service score gains of approximately **2 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents a meaningful 4% improvement on the typical service score of 50. The evidence is statistically strong and the benefit appears to grow over time.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

### **KEY FINDINGS**

#### **1. Measured Improvement**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Treatment effect | **+2.03 points** |
| 95% Confidence interval | 1.48 to 2.57 points |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 (highly reliable) |
| Relative improvement | ~4% increase |

#### **2. What Happened**

- **425 branches** adopted the new procedure starting in week 15
- **284 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Control branches saw service scores **decline** by 1.15 points after week 14
- Treatment branches saw service scores **increase** by 0.88 points after week 14
- The **net difference** of 2.03 points is attributed to the new procedure

#### **3. Benefits Accumulate Over Time**

The improvement was not immediate. Service scores showed:
- **Weeks 15-18:** Modest gains (not statistically distinguishable from zero)
- **Weeks 19-20:** Growing improvement
- **Weeks 21-22:** Strong, significant gains of 1.4 to 1.6 points

This suggests branches need 4-6 weeks to fully realize the benefits of the new procedure.

#### **4. Results Hold After Accounting for Other Factors**

The analysis controlled for:
- Weekly staffing variations
- Weekly visit volume variations
- Seasonal week-to-week patterns

The treatment effect remained significant after accounting for all these factors.

---

### **CONSIDERATIONS**

1. **Implementation timeline matters.** The delayed effect suggests staff training and adjustment time is important. A rushed rollout may not deliver full benefits immediately.

2. **Continued monitoring is warranted.** While the results are strong, ongoing tracking will confirm the effect persists and identify any branches that need additional support.

3. **The control group declined.** Part of the measured benefit reflects that control branches' scores dropped after week 14 while treatment branches held steady and improved. This makes the new procedure even more valuable—it appears to protect against service score erosion.

---

### **NEXT STEPS**

| Action | Timeline |
|--------|----------|
| Approve system-wide rollout | Immediate |
| Develop training materials from early-adopter branches | 2-4 weeks |
| Implement phased adoption (allowing 4-6 weeks per cohort) | 3-6 months |
| Monitor service scores for all branches post-implementation | Ongoing |
| Re-evaluate full impact | 6 months |

---

### **ABOUT THIS ANALYSIS**

This evaluation used a **difference-in-differences** research design—the standard method for assessing policy changes when random assignment is not feasible. The analysis compared treatment and control branches before and after the week 15 switch point, using 12,053 branch-week observations across 709 branches.
