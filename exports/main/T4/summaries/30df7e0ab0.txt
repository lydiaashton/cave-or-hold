# **Memorandum to the Board of Directors**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Office  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure  

---

## **Executive Summary**

We evaluated whether the new stock rotation and shelving procedure improved branch service scores. The evidence shows a **positive direction of effect** but **falls short of statistical certainty**.

**Bottom Line:** Branches using the new procedure scored approximately **2 points higher** on the service index than expected based on comparison branches. However, with the current data, we cannot confidently distinguish this from random variation (p = 0.11). We recommend **continuing the pilot for one more quarter** before making a system-wide rollout decision.

---

## **What We Found**

### The Numbers

| Finding | Result |
|---------|--------|
| **Estimated improvement** | +2.05 points on service index |
| **95% confidence range** | -0.49 to +4.59 points |
| **Statistical confidence** | 89% (below the 95% standard) |
| **Branches in study** | 34 total (20 switched, 14 did not) |
| **Weeks observed** | 17 weeks (8 before switch, 9 after) |

### What This Means in Practice

- The average branch scores about 50 on the service index
- A 2-point improvement represents roughly **4% gains**
- The true effect could reasonably be anywhere from **no effect** to a **meaningful 4-5 point improvement**
- Control branches actually saw scores *decline* by 1.1 points during this period, while switched branches *increased* by 0.9 points

### Why We Can't Be More Certain

The main limitation is **sample size**. With only 9 weeks of post-switch data from 20 branches, the study lacks the statistical power to detect modest but real improvements. This is a data limitation, not evidence that the procedure failed.

### Confidence in the Analysis

The key assumption for this type of analysis—that treatment and control branches were on similar trajectories before the switch—**is well-supported**. Pre-switch trends were statistically indistinguishable (p = 0.85), giving us confidence the comparison is valid.

---

## **Recommendations**

### **Primary Recommendation: Extend the Pilot**

Continue the current arrangement for **one additional quarter (12-13 weeks)** with the following actions:

1. **Maintain current assignments** – Keep the 20 branches on the new procedure and the 14 on the old
2. **Continue weekly scoring** – No changes to current data collection
3. **Reconvene in Q2 2027** – Return to the board with updated analysis

**Rationale:** Doubling the post-switch observation period would increase statistical power substantially. If the true effect is around 2 points, we would have approximately 70-80% power to detect it with a full year of data.

### **Secondary Consideration: Phased Expansion**

If operational priorities favor broader implementation, consider adding **5-10 additional branches** to the new procedure while maintaining the 14 control branches for continued comparison. This would:
- Accelerate benefits if the procedure is effective
- Preserve the ability to measure impact
- Limit exposure if the procedure proves ineffective

### **What We Do Not Recommend**

- **Full system-wide rollout at this time** – The evidence is promising but not yet conclusive enough to justify the operational disruption and cost of converting all 34+ branches
- **Abandoning the new procedure** – There is no evidence of harm, and the point estimate suggests genuine benefit

## **Questions the Board May Have**

**Q: "We were confident this would work. Why aren't the results clearer?"**  
A: The direction is exactly as expected—scores went up in switched branches and down in control branches. The uncertainty is about *precision*, not direction. More data will sharpen the estimate.

**Q: "Could we be wasting resources keeping branches on the old procedure?"**  
A: Possibly. If the true effect is at the high end of our confidence interval (~4-5 points), there is an opportunity cost. This is the trade-off between acting on incomplete evidence versus making a well-informed decision.

**Q: "What if scores drop when we expand?"**  
A: This is unlikely given the data, but possible. The phased expansion option above mitigates this risk while allowing broader implementation.
