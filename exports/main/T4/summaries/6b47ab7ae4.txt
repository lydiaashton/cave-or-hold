# Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact Analysis

## Bottom Line

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure **significantly improved service scores**. Branches that adopted the new procedure saw an average increase of **2.05 points** (approximately **4% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant (p < 0.001) and the analysis supports rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## Key Findings

### 1. Treatment Effect
- **Difference-in-differences estimate: +2.05 points** (95% CI: 0.95 to 3.16)
- Switched branches improved from 50.0 to 52.3 (change: +2.3 points)
- Non-switched branches improved from 51.0 to 51.3 (change: +0.2 points)
- The improvement is above and beyond any general time trends affecting all branches

### 2. Statistical Validity
- **Parallel trends assumption confirmed**: Before the switch (weeks 6-14), switched and non-switched branches followed similar trends (p = 0.88)
- **Robust to controls**: Effect remains significant when controlling for staffing levels and visitor volumes
- **Cluster-robust standard errors** used to account for repeated observations at each branch

### 3. Branch-Level Impact
- **71% of switched branches** saw service score improvements after adoption
- Only **45% of non-switched branches** saw improvements over the same period
- The effect is consistent across most branches, not driven by a few outliers

### 4. Secondary Effects
- **Staffing**: No significant change in staffing requirements (the new procedure does not appear to require more staff)
- **Visits**: Slight increase in visitor volume (marginally significant, p = 0.095), suggesting the procedure change may also improve branch attractiveness

---

## Analysis Details

**Data**: 150 branches, 17 weeks (weeks 6-22), 2,550 observations
- 90 branches adopted the new procedure (treatment group)
- 60 branches kept the old procedure (control group)
- Switch occurred at week 15

**Method**: Difference-in-differences regression with branch-level clustering

---

## Next Steps

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches

2. **Monitor implementation** at newly converted branches to ensure they achieve similar gains

3. **Document the new procedure** in detail to ensure consistent implementation across all branches

4. **Continue tracking** service scores, staffing, and visits to confirm sustained benefits

5. **Consider investigating** why approximately 29% of switched branches did not see improvements—there may be branch-specific factors (e.g., layout, staffing patterns) that affect how well the procedure works
