# **MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Impact of New Branch Shelving and Rotation Procedure on Service Scores

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and rotation procedure **improved service scores by 2.3 points** (approximately 4% relative to the average score of 50). This effect is statistically significant (p < 0.001). Although we initially expected no difference, the data clearly show the new procedure benefits service quality. **We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **150 branches** tracked over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **60 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Analysis compares service score changes between groups before and after the switch

### Results

| Group | Pre-Switch Average | Post-Switch Average | Change |
|-------|-------------------|---------------------|--------|
| Switched branches (new procedure) | 52.15 | 52.30 | +0.15 |
| Non-switched branches (old procedure) | 52.72 | 50.56 | −2.17 |
| **Net Effect of New Procedure** | | | **+2.31** |

- **Treatment effect: +2.31 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.0 to 3.5 points)
- **Statistical significance: p < 0.001**
- Effect remains significant after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### Important Context
- System-wide service scores declined after week 14 (likely due to seasonal or external factors)
- Branches using the new procedure **avoided this decline** and maintained their service quality
- Pre-switch trends were parallel between groups, confirming the comparison is valid

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all 60 remaining branches
2. **Monitor implementation** to ensure consistent adoption across branches
3. **Continue tracking** weekly service scores to confirm the effect persists over time
4. **Document best practices** from high-performing switched branches to support training

---

## **Technical Notes**

- Analysis uses difference-in-differences regression with standard errors clustered by branch
- Parallel trends assumption verified: pre-switch trends were statistically indistinguishable between groups (p = 0.72)
- Results are robust to inclusion of staffing and visitor volume controls
