# Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores

**Date:** November 2026  
**Analysis Period:** Weeks 6-22 (17 weeks)  
**Data:** 578 weekly observations from 34 branches

---

## Bottom Line

The new shelving and rotation procedure is associated with a **2.4-point improvement** in service scores, but this effect is **not statistically significant** at conventional levels. While the direction of the effect is positive (as expected), the evidence is not strong enough to confidently conclude the new procedure causes improved service. The board should consider a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than an immediate systemwide implementation.

---

## Key Findings

### Treatment Effect Estimate

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Point Estimate | +2.36 points |
| 95% Confidence Interval | -0.57 to +5.29 points |
| P-value | 0.115 |
| Effect Size | 5% improvement (0.23 SD) |

### Service Score Changes by Group

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched Branches (New Procedure) | 50.36 | 52.81 | +2.45 |
| Control Branches (Old Procedure) | 46.52 | 46.61 | +0.09 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+2.36** |

### Study Design Strengths

- **Parallel trends verified:** Before the switch, both groups showed similar trends over time
- **Valid control group:** 14 branches maintained the old procedure for comparison
- **Adequate baseline:** 9 weeks of pre-switch data (weeks 6-14)

### Important Limitations

- **Statistical uncertainty:** The confidence interval includes zero, meaning we cannot rule out no effect
- **Limited post-switch data:** Only 8 weeks of observations after the procedure change
- **Pre-existing differences:** Switched branches started with higher service scores (~3.8 points higher), suggesting potential selection bias
- **High variability:** Week-to-week score variation (SD ≈ 10 points) makes modest effects harder to detect

---

## Recommendations

### Immediate Actions

1. **Extend the pilot period by 8-12 weeks** to increase statistical power and narrow confidence intervals

2. **Randomly assign 5-10 additional branches** to the new procedure to eliminate selection bias concerns

3. **Monitor implementation fidelity** to ensure branches are following the new procedure consistently

4. **Conduct a cost-benefit analysis** weighing the ~2-point improvement against any additional staff time or resources required

### Decision Framework

| If the effect... | Then... |
|------------------|---------|
| Remains ~2 points and becomes statistically significant | Proceed with rollout |
| Grows to 3+ points | Strong case for systemwide rollout |
| Shrinks toward zero | Do not roll out; reconsider procedure design |
| Becomes negative | Discontinue the new procedure |

---

## Conclusion

The new procedure shows **promising but inconclusive** results. The positive direction aligns with expectations, but the evidence is not yet strong enough to justify immediate systemwide implementation. Continued monitoring with expanded data collection is recommended before making a final rollout decision.
