# Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores

---

## Bottom Line

**The new shelving and rotation procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **2.2 points** (approximately 4.4%) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant, growing over time, and observed across most branches. **We recommend rolling out the new procedure system-wide.**

---

## Key Findings

### Study Overview
| | |
|---|---|
| **Branches studied** | 709 total (425 switched, 284 did not) |
| **Time period** | 17 weeks (weeks 6-22) |
| **Switch implemented** | Week 15 |
| **Method** | Difference-in-differences with branch fixed effects |

### Main Results
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Treatment effect** | +2.22 service score points |
| **95% Confidence Interval** | [1.64, 2.80] |
| **Statistical significance** | p < 0.001 |
| **Improvement vs. baseline** | 4.4% |

### What Changed

| Period | Treatment Branches | Control Branches | Gap |
|--------|-------------------|------------------|-----|
| **Before switch** (weeks 6-14) | 50.58 | 49.87 | 0.71 |
| **After switch** (weeks 15-22) | 52.58 | 49.64 | 2.93 |
| **Change in gap** | | | **+2.22** |

### Branch-Level Impact
- **69% of switched branches** improved their scores vs. **48% of non-switched branches**
- Average improvement among switched branches: **+2.00 points**
- Average change among non-switched branches: **-0.23 points**

### Effect Is Strengthening Over Time
| Post-Switch Period | Treatment-Control Gap |
|-------------------|----------------------|
| Weeks 15-18 (early) | +2.28 points |
| Weeks 19-22 (late) | +3.58 points |

The widening gap suggests branches are mastering the new procedure with experience.

### Validity Check
Pre-switch trends were parallel between treatment and control groups, supporting the conclusion that the observed improvement is caused by the procedure change rather than pre-existing differences.

---

## Next Steps

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches.

2. **Develop implementation support** drawing on lessons from the 425 early-adopter branches:
   - Create training materials based on best practices from top performers
   - Consider a phased rollout to manage the transition smoothly

3. **Continue monitoring** service scores during rollout to confirm effects persist at scale.

4. **Investigate variation** in outcomes—some branches gained 10+ points while others saw modest declines. Understanding this variation could help maximize benefits.

5. **Conduct cost-benefit analysis** if the new procedure carries additional costs (training, equipment, etc.). The 4.4% service improvement should be weighed against these costs.

---
