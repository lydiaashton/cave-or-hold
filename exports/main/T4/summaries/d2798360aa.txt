# Library Branch Procedure Change: Service Score Analysis

**To:** Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2025  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## Bottom Line

Branches that adopted the new procedure showed **higher service scores** after implementation compared to branches using the old procedure. The estimated improvement is **+1.85 points** on the service score index (approximately 3.7% above baseline). However, because switching branches were already improving faster before the change, we cannot definitively attribute all gains to the new procedure. We recommend a **phased rollout with randomized selection** to confirm the effect before full implementation.

---

## Key Findings

### 1. Measured Improvement

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 49.33 | 49.44 | +0.11 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 50.61 | 52.58 | +1.96 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+1.85** |

The treatment effect is **statistically significant** (p < 0.001) with a 95% confidence interval of 1.29 to 2.41 points.

### 2. Important Caveat: Pre-Existing Differences

The analysis reveals a concern for causal interpretation:

- Switching branches had **higher scores even before the change** (+1.29 points at baseline)
- The gap between groups was **already widening** before week 15 (from -0.26 in week 6 to +2.55 in week 14)
- After the switch, the gap continued its upward trend rather than showing a sharp jump

This pattern suggests switching branches may have been on a stronger improvement trajectory regardless of the procedure change—possibly due to better management, more resources, or other unmeasured factors.

### 3. Data Overview

- **709 branches** analyzed (425 switched, 284 did not)
- **17 weeks** of data (weeks 6-22)
- **12,053 observations** total
- Service scores average ~50 across all branches (higher is better)

---

## Next Steps

### Recommended Actions

1. **Do not proceed with immediate system-wide rollout.** The evidence is promising but not conclusive due to pre-trend differences.

2. **Implement randomized phased rollout:**
   - Randomly assign remaining branches to switch in batches
   - This eliminates selection bias and enables cleaner causal inference
   - Allow 4-8 weeks of observation between batches

3. **Monitor key metrics weekly:**
   - Service scores (primary outcome)
   - Staffing and visit indices (already collected)
   - Any operational costs or staff feedback

4. **Document selection criteria:** Understand why certain branches were chosen to switch first. This will help interpret results and guide future implementations.

5. **Set decision threshold:** Define in advance what effect size would justify full rollout (e.g., +1.5 points sustained over 12 weeks).

### Timeline

| Phase | Action | Duration |
|-------|--------|----------|
| 1 | Randomize and switch first batch | Weeks 1-4 |
| 2 | Monitor and evaluate | Weeks 5-12 |
| 3 | Decide on continued rollout | Week 13 |

---

## Conclusion

The new procedure is **associated with improved service scores**, but the causal relationship remains uncertain due to pre-existing differences between switching and non-switching branches. A randomized rollout will provide the evidence needed to make a confident system-wide decision while still allowing branches to benefit from potential improvements.
