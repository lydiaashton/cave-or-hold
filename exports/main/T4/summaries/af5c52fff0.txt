# **Memorandum to the Board**

## **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

### **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **2.1 points** (approximately 4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This result is highly statistically significant, and the evidence strongly supports rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

**Recommendation: Approve system-wide rollout.**

---

### **Key Findings**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Treatment Effect | +2.11 service score points |
| 95% Confidence Interval | 1.55 to 2.67 points |
| Statistical Significance | p < 0.001 |
| Percentage Improvement | 4.1% |
| Branches Studied | 709 total (425 switched, 284 control) |
| Study Period | 17 weeks (weeks 6–22) |

**1. Clear Positive Effect**
The new procedure produced a measurable improvement in service quality. Treatment branches improved by 1.47 points after the switch, while control branches actually declined by 0.62 points during the same period. The net effect of 2.11 points is robust after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume.

**2. Strong Study Design**
The analysis leverages a natural experiment: 425 branches adopted the new procedure in week 15 while 284 branches continued with the old procedure. Pre-switch trends were parallel between groups, validating the comparison. Standard errors account for repeated observations at each branch.

**3. Benefits Grow Over Time**
The improvement started at approximately 1.5 points in week 15 and grew to approximately 4 points by week 22. This suggests staff become more effective with the new procedure as they gain experience—an encouraging sign for system-wide adoption.

**4. Meaningful Impact at Scale**
With 709 branches across the cooperative, a 2-point average improvement represents substantial aggregate gains in service quality. Given that the average branch scores about 51 points, this improvement is both statistically and practically significant.

---

### **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all remaining 284 branches

2. **Develop implementation timeline** – consider a phased rollout over 4–6 weeks to manage training and transition smoothly

3. **Create training materials** – document best practices from the 425 branches already using the procedure to accelerate adoption

4. **Continue monitoring** – track service scores during rollout to confirm benefits persist at scale and identify any branches needing additional support

5. **Set success metrics** – establish a target of 2+ point improvement per branch as the rollout proceeds

---

### **Methodology Note**

This analysis used a difference-in-differences approach, comparing branches that switched to the new procedure against those that did not, before and after week 15. The model controlled for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume. Standard errors were clustered at the branch level to account for repeated observations.
