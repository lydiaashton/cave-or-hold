# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores—contrary to initial expectations.** While we anticipated no measurable effect, branches that switched to the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.3 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents a **4.4% improvement** and is statistically significant (p < 0.001). 

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure; **60 branches** kept the old procedure
- The switch occurred at **week 15**
- Analysis compares changes in switched branches against non-switched branches (difference-in-differences)

### Results

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 52.15 | 52.30 | +0.15 |
| Non-switched branches | 52.72 | 50.56 | −2.17 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+2.31** |

- **Estimated effect: +2.30 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.0 to 3.6)
- **Statistical significance: p < 0.001**
- **Effect size: 0.24 standard deviations** (small to moderate)

### Why This Result Is Reliable

1. **Pre-switch trends were parallel**: Before week 15, both groups moved in similar patterns, validating our comparison approach.

2. **Effect is robust**: The +2.3 point estimate holds across multiple statistical specifications, including models that control for staffing levels, visit volumes, and branch-specific characteristics.

3. **Timing aligns with intervention**: The positive effect appears only after the switch, with no prior divergence between groups.

4. **Unexpected finding**: The non-switched branches experienced a 2.2-point decline after week 15, while switched branches held steady. The new procedure appears to have **protected against deterioration** in service quality.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all branches.

2. **Monitor during implementation**: Track service scores to confirm effects replicate at scale.

3. **Investigate the decline in non-switched branches**: Understand what caused service scores to drop after week 15 in branches that did not switch. The new procedure may buffer against external pressures.

4. **Document implementation learnings**: Capture any branch-level variations in how the procedure was adopted to support smoother rollout.

5. **Reassess in 6 months**: Evaluate whether effects persist long-term and whether additional adjustments are needed.

---

*This finding exceeded expectations—the procedure change was not anticipated to affect service scores, yet it delivered measurable improvement at no apparent cost to service quality.*
