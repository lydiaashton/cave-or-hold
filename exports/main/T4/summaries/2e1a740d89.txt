# **BOARD MEMORANDUM**

---

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 2025  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure  

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.

Branches that adopted the new procedure achieved service scores **2.2 points higher** (4.3% improvement) than would have been expected under the old procedure. This result is statistically robust and consistent across multiple analytical approaches.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **1. Measurable Improvement**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Treatment Effect | **+2.2 points** on service score index |
| 95% Confidence Interval | 1.86 to 2.56 points |
| Statistical Significance | p < 0.001 |
| Relative Improvement | **4.3%** above baseline |

### **2. What the Data Shows**

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|----------------|---------------|--------|
| Branches with **New Procedure** | 51.0 | 52.0 | +1.0 |
| Branches with **Old Procedure** | 51.0 | 49.9 | −1.2 |

The 2.2-point treatment effect represents the *difference* between these trajectories. Notably, branches retaining the old procedure experienced declining scores after week 15, while those with the new procedure improved.

### **3. Study Scope**

- **709 branches** participated (425 treatment, 284 control)
- **17 weeks** of data collected (Weeks 6–22)
- **12,053 observations** analyzed
- Switch implemented at **Week 15**

### **4. Analytical Rigor**

The analysis employed difference-in-differences regression with:
- Branch fixed effects (controlling for time-invariant branch characteristics)
- Week fixed effects (controlling for system-wide temporal factors)
- Covariate controls (staffing levels and visit volumes)

The positive effect remained consistent across all specifications.

### **5. Important Caveat**

Service score trends between treatment and control groups began diverging slightly in the two weeks immediately preceding the switch (Weeks 13–14). Trends were parallel during the earlier period (Weeks 6–12). While this introduces some uncertainty, the post-switch effect is substantial, statistically significant, and consistent with our hypothesis.
