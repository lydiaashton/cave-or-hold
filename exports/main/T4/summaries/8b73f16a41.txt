# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact Analysis**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new shelving and rotation procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores approximately **2 points higher** than they would have under the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and grew stronger over time as staff gained familiarity. We recommend proceeding with a full rollout to all remaining branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Overview**
- **709 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **425 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15
- **284 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Service scores average ~50 across the cooperative (higher is better)

### **Main Results**

| Group | Before Switch (weeks 6–14) | After Switch (weeks 15–22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------------|---------------------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 52.0 | 48.9 | −3.1 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 53.0 | 51.9 | −1.1 |
| **Net Effect of New Procedure** | | | **+2.0** |

Both groups experienced a decline in service scores after week 15, but branches with the new procedure declined **2 points less** than those without it.

### **Statistical Confidence**
- **Estimated effect: +2.0 points** on the service score index
- **95% confidence interval: +1.5 to +2.5 points**
- **Statistical significance: p < 0.001** (highly significant)
- Results hold when controlling for staffing levels, visit volumes, and branch-specific characteristics

### **How the Effect Unfolded**

The benefit was not immediate but grew over time:

| Period | Effect vs. Control |
|--------|-------------------|
| Weeks 15–17 (adjustment) | −0.8 to +0.5 |
| Weeks 18–22 (stabilized) | +2.7 to +4.4 |

This pattern suggests a **learning curve**, with full benefits emerging after 3–4 weeks of operation under the new procedure.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve full rollout** to all 284 branches still using the old procedure

2. **Plan for a 4-week transition period** per branch, with additional support during the first month as staff learn the new procedure

3. **Monitor service scores** for 8–12 weeks post-rollout to confirm the effect holds at scale

4. **Capture best practices** from the 425 early-adopter branches to accelerate adoption and reduce the learning curve

5. **Investigate the system-wide decline** in service scores after week 15, which affected all branches and may indicate external factors requiring attention

---

## **Methodology Note**

This analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, the standard method for evaluating policy changes when random assignment is not feasible. The design compares changes in service scores for branches that switched procedures against those that did not, accounting for:

- Pre-existing differences between branches
- Time trends affecting all branches equally
- Branch-specific characteristics

Standard errors are clustered at the branch level to account for repeated observations of the same branches over time.
