# **Impact of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure on Service Scores**

**To:** Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2025  

---

## **Bottom Line**

Contrary to expectations, the new stock rotation and shelving procedure **improved service scores by approximately 1.8 points** (95% confidence interval: 0.6 to 3.0 points). This represents a **3.7% improvement** over baseline scores and is statistically significant (p = 0.003). 

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **60 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Analysis compared changes in service scores between the two groups before and after the switch

### Results Summary

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 50.41 | 49.81 | −0.60 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 48.88 | 50.12 | +1.24 |
| **Net Effect of New Procedure** | | | **+1.84** |

### What This Means
- The improvement is **statistically significant**—unlikely to be due to random chance
- The effect size is **modest but meaningful**: about one-fifth of a standard deviation
- Results hold even after accounting for staffing levels and branch visit volumes
- Service scores at treatment branches pulled ahead of control branches after week 15 and maintained the advantage

---

## **Important Context**

- **Expectation vs. Reality:** The board expected no difference in service scores. The data show a clear positive effect.
- **Pre-existing differences:** Treatment branches started with slightly lower scores (48.9 vs. 50.4), but the analysis accounts for this baseline gap.
- **Natural variation:** Service scores fluctuate week-to-week; the study design isolates the procedure's effect from these fluctuations.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to remaining 60 branches

2. **Monitor implementation** to ensure the effect replicates at scale

3. **Investigate why scores improved**—understanding the mechanism (e.g., faster book location, better organization) can help reinforce best practices during training

4. **Track long-term sustainability**—continue monitoring to ensure improvements persist beyond the initial rollout period

5. **Document lessons learned** for future operational changes
