# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** [Current Date]  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure  

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure shows a **positive but statistically inconclusive** effect on service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure improved by approximately **2 points (4%)** more than branches that retained the old procedure, but this difference could reasonably be due to random variation. We **cannot confidently conclude** the new procedure causes improvement based on this data alone.

**Recommendation: Do not proceed with system-wide rollout at this time.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect Summary

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches (20 branches) | 47.5 | 49.0 | **+1.4** |
| Non-switched branches (14 branches) | 49.3 | 48.8 | **-0.5** |
| **Net Difference (Treatment Effect)** | | | **+1.9 points** |

### 2. Statistical Assessment

| Metric | Value | Interpretation |
|--------|-------|----------------|
| Estimated effect | +1.95 points | ~4% improvement relative to baseline |
| P-value | 0.12 | Not statistically significant (threshold: 0.05) |
| 95% Confidence Interval | -0.5 to +4.4 points | True effect could be zero or negative |

**What this means:** There is a 12% probability we would observe this result even if the new procedure had no real effect. By conventional standards, this does not meet the threshold for statistical confidence.

### 3. Methodology Validation

Our analysis passes a critical validity check: before the switch (weeks 6-14), both groups followed parallel trends (p=0.82). This confirms the comparison is appropriate—branches that switched were not systematically different from those that didn't prior to the intervention.

### 4. Important Context

- **High variation in outcomes:** Among switched branches, individual changes ranged from -5.7 to +7.2 points. This suggests the procedure may work better in some branch contexts than others.
- **External factors matter:** Staffing levels and visitor volume significantly affect service scores (both p < 0.05). The procedure effect remains positive after accounting for these factors.
- **Sample size:** 34 branches observed over 17 weeks (8 weeks pre-switch, 8 weeks post-switch).

---

## **Recommendations & Next Steps**

### **Decision: Pause system-wide rollout.**

The evidence is promising but insufficient to justify the cost and disruption of cooperative-wide implementation.

### **Action Plan:**

| Priority | Action | Timeline |
|----------|--------|----------|
| 1 | **Extend data collection** — Gather 8-12 additional weeks of post-switch data to improve statistical power | Immediate |
| 2 | **Investigate variation** — Analyze which branch characteristics (size, location, staffing) predict better outcomes under the new procedure | 4-6 weeks |
| 3 | **Audit implementation** — Verify all switched branches are following the new protocol consistently; identify best practices | 4-6 weeks |
| 4 | **Consider phased expansion** — Add 5-10 volunteer branches to the treatment group to increase sample size | 8-12 weeks |
| 5 | **Re-evaluate** — Return to Board with updated analysis and definitive recommendation | 3 months |

---

## **Appendix: Analysis Details**

- **Method:** Difference-in-differences regression with branch fixed effects
- **Standard errors:** Clustered at branch level (accounts for within-branch correlation)
- **Controls:** Staffing index, visits index
- **Data:** 578 observations (34 branches × 17 weeks)
- **Switch date:** Week 15
- **Pre-trend test:** Passed (p=0.82)
