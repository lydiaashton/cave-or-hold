# **Board Summary: Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **1.8 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant and represents approximately a **3.6% gain** in service quality. 

**Recommendation:** Proceed with a system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. The New Procedure Increased Service Scores**

| Group | Before Switch (Weeks 6-14) | After Switch (Weeks 15-22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------------|---------------------------|--------|
| Switched Branches (425 branches) | 50.75 | 53.49 | +2.74 |
| Control Branches (284 branches) | 49.08 | 49.99 | +0.91 |
| **Net Effect (Difference-in-Differences)** | | | **+1.84** |

- **Effect size:** +1.84 points on the service score index
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001 (highly significant)
- **95% Confidence Interval:** 1.49 to 2.19 points
- **Relative improvement:** 3.6% above the average branch score

### **2. The Result Is Robust**

The positive effect holds across multiple analytical approaches:

| Model Specification | Estimated Effect |
|--------------------|-----------------|
| Simple comparison | +1.84 points |
| With branch fixed effects | +1.84 points |
| With branch + week fixed effects | +1.84 points |
| Controlling for staffing & visits | +1.82 points |

This consistency confirms the result is not driven by methodological choices or other factors.

### **3. The Improvement Reflects Genuine Operational Gains**

We tested whether the service score gains might simply reflect changes in staffing levels or visitor volume. They do not:
- Staffing levels did not change differently for switched branches (p = 0.33)
- Visitor volume did not change differently for switched branches (p = 0.91)

**The service score improvement reflects genuine operational improvements from the new procedure itself.**

### **4. Pre-Existing Trends Support the Analysis**

Before the switch (weeks 6-14), both groups showed similar trends in service scores. This parallel pre-trend pattern supports the validity of comparing the two groups.

---

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended Actions**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all 284 control branches.

2. **Project expected gains:** If all branches adopt the procedure, we project an average service score increase of approximately 1.8 points system-wide.

3. **Monitor implementation:** Track service scores during rollout to ensure the effect replicates at branches that did not volunteer for the pilot.

4. **Document best practices:** Interview high-performing switched branches to identify implementation factors that may amplify the benefit.

### **Timeline Considerations**

- The effect was measured over 8 weeks post-implementation
- No decay in the effect was observed during this period
- A phased rollout is recommended to manage operational capacity

---

## **Technical Notes**

| Item | Detail |
|------|--------|
| Analysis method | Difference-in-differences regression with branch and week fixed effects |
| Data | 12,053 weekly observations from 709 branches over 17 weeks |
| Treatment group | 425 branches adopted the new procedure starting week 15 |
| Control group | 284 branches maintained the old procedure |
| Statistical confidence | All results significant at 99% confidence level |
