# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD OF DIRECTORS**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

**Date:** November 15, 2026

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Contrary to our initial expectation that the change would make no difference, branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure achieved service scores **1.77 points higher** (approximately **3.6% improvement**) than branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and not explained by changes in staffing or visitor volume.

**Recommendation: Approve system-wide rollout of the new procedure to all branches.**

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### 1. Clear Positive Effect on Service Quality

| Group | Branches | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|----------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Treatment (New Procedure) | 90 | 50.03 | 51.47 | **+1.44** |
| Control (Old Procedure) | 60 | 48.77 | 48.43 | **-0.34** |
| **Net Effect** | — | — | — | **+1.77** |

The new procedure produced a **net improvement of 1.77 points** on the service score index.

### 2. Statistically Reliable Result

- **P-value: 0.003** — less than 1% probability this result occurred by chance
- **95% Confidence Interval: +0.58 to +2.96 points**
- Analysis used difference-in-differences methodology with branch-level clustering

### 3. Valid Comparison Confirmed

Statistical testing confirms that treatment and control branches were following **parallel trends before the switch** (p = 0.25). This validates that the post-switch improvement is attributable to the procedure change itself, not pre-existing differences between branches.

### 4. Improvement Reflects Operational Gains

The new procedure did **not** significantly affect:
- Staffing levels (p = 0.51)
- Visitor volumes (p = 0.75)

This indicates the service score improvement stems from the procedure change itself, not from branches receiving more resources or traffic.

### 5. Consistent Improvement Pattern

The gap between treatment and control branches widened steadily after week 15, with weekly differences ranging from +2.2 to +5.0 points in the post-switch period versus +1.3 points average before.

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

1. **Approve full rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all 150 branches.

2. **Develop implementation timeline** with target completion within 6 months.

3. **Capture best practices** from the 90 branches already using the new procedure to support training and adoption.

4. **Monitor outcomes** for 8-12 weeks post-rollout to confirm improvements are sustained at scale.

5. **Complete cost-benefit analysis** to quantify return on investment (benefit demonstrated; operational costs to be confirmed).

---

## **ABOUT THIS ANALYSIS**

- **Data:** 2,550 weekly observations from 150 branches over 17 weeks (weeks 6-22)
- **Method:** Difference-in-differences regression with clustered standard errors
- **Switch point:** Week 15 (9 weeks pre-switch, 8 weeks post-switch)
- **Service score:** Index where average branch scores approximately 50 (higher is better)
