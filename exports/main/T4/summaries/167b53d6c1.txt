# **BOARD MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure  

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores by 2.2 points (4.3% increase).** The evidence is statistically robust. We recommend approving system-wide rollout to all remaining branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

| Finding | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Treatment Effect** | +2.2 service score points |
| **Statistical Confidence** | 95% CI: 0.65 to 3.76; p < 0.01 |
| **Branches Studied** | 150 total (90 switched, 60 control) |
| **Study Period** | 17 weeks (weeks 6–22); switch at week 15 |
| **Effect Size** | Small-to-moderate (Cohen's d = 0.22) |

### What the Data Shows

- **Branches using the new procedure** saw service scores rise from 50.7 to 53.1 (+2.4 points)
- **Branches keeping the old procedure** remained flat: 49.8 to 50.0 (+0.2 points)
- **Net improvement attributable to the new procedure: +2.2 points**

### Why We Trust This Result

1. **Parallel pre-treatment trends** – Before week 15, both groups moved in lockstep (p = 0.67 for trend difference)
2. **Controls confirm the effect** – Results hold after accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume
3. **Branch-level analysis confirms** – Simple pre-post comparison yields identical estimate (+2.2 points, p = 0.001)

---

## **Context for Decision-Making**

The board initially expected no difference in service scores. The data contradicts this expectation—the new procedure produces a measurable, positive effect.

**Is 2.2 points meaningful?** Service scores average 51 points with a standard deviation of 9.8 points. A 2.2-point gain represents:
- 4.3% improvement over the average branch
- Roughly one-fifth of a standard deviation
- Consistent improvement across all post-switch weeks

---

## **Recommendations**

| Priority | Action |
|----------|--------|
| **1** | Approve rollout of new procedure to all 60 branches still using the old method |
| **2** | Track service scores for 8 weeks post-rollout to confirm effect at scale |
| **3** | Document specific practices driving the improvement for training |
| **4** | Conduct cost-benefit analysis if new procedure requires additional resources |

---

## **Risks and Limitations**

- **External validity:** Effect measured over 8 post-switch weeks; long-term sustainability untested
- **Implementation fidelity:** Rollout success depends on consistent adoption across branches
- **Opportunity cost:** Staff time required for training and transition

---

## **Conclusion**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure delivers a statistically significant improvement in service quality. The analysis is methodologically sound, and the effect is large enough to warrant system-wide adoption.

**We recommend proceeding with full rollout.**

---

*Analysis based on 2,550 branch-week observations. Method: Difference-in-differences regression with robust standard errors and branch fixed effects.*
