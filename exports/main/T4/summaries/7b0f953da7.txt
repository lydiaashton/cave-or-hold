# LIBRARY BRANCH PROCEDURE CHANGE ANALYSIS
## Summary for the Board

### BOTTOM LINE
The new shelving/rotation procedure shows a **MODEST POSITIVE EFFECT** on service scores, but the evidence is mixed due to pre-existing differences between branches that chose to switch and those that didn't. The estimated improvement is approximately **1.0 to 1.3 points** on the service score index (about 2% of the average score of 50), with some uncertainty about whether this reflects the true treatment effect or pre-existing branch differences.

### KEY FINDINGS

1. **BASIC COMPARISON**
   - Control branches (no switch): Score dropped from 50.7 to 48.6 (−2.0 points)
   - Treatment branches (switched): Score remained stable at 50.9 to 50.9 (~0 change)
   - Simple difference-in-differences: +2.0 points for switched branches

2. **CRITICAL CAVEAT: PRE-EXISTING TRENDS**
   - Branches that chose to switch were **ALREADY IMPROVING** before the change
   - Treatment branches: +0.22 points/week trend before switch
   - Control branches: −0.21 points/week trend before switch
   - This pre-existing divergence means the simple +2.0 estimate is likely **INFLATED** and captures both the treatment effect AND ongoing trends

3. **ADJUSTED ESTIMATES** (accounting for pre-trends)
   - Local comparison (weeks 13-16 only): +0.5 points (not statistically significant)
   - Change in treatment-control gap: +1.0 points
   - Model with trend controls: +1.3 points (statistically significant, p<0.01)
   - **Most credible range: +1.0 to +1.3 points improvement**

4. **POST-IMPLEMENTATION PATTERNS**
   - Treatment branches maintained slightly higher scores throughout the 8-week post period (gaps of 1.7 to 3.3 points vs. control)
   - Treatment branches showed a small additional upward trend (+0.16 points/week) after the switch compared to control branches
   - **No evidence of negative effects or score deterioration**

5. **SCOPE**
   - 709 branches total: 425 switched (60%), 284 did not (40%)
   - 17 weeks of data (weeks 6-22), with switch implemented at week 15
   - 12,053 branch-week observations analyzed

### NEXT STEPS

1. **PROCEED WITH CAUTIOUS OPTIMISM**
   The weight of evidence suggests a small but real positive effect. Rolling out to all branches is reasonable, but expectations should be modest (~1 point improvement, not 2+ points).

2. **MONITOR CLOSELY DURING ROLLOUT**
   - Track service scores weekly for the first 8-12 weeks after each branch switches
   - Compare actual results to the +1.0 to +1.3 point benchmark
   - Investigate any branches that show score declines

3. **ADDRESS SELECTION BIAS FOR FUTURE EVALUATIONS**
   The fact that switching branches were already improving suggests branches may have self-selected based on readiness or management quality. For future changes, consider:
   - Random assignment of procedure changes when feasible
   - Staggered rollout with random timing
   - More extensive pre-implementation data collection

4. **INVESTIGATE MECHANISMS**
   - Survey branch staff about what aspects of the new procedure work well
   - Identify whether certain branch characteristics (size, location, staffing) moderate the effect
   - Consider whether the benefit comes from the procedure itself or from increased staff attention during the transition

5. **COST-BENEFIT ANALYSIS**
   Before full rollout, quantify:
   - Implementation costs (training, temporary disruption)
   - Whether a ~1 point service score improvement justifies these costs
   - Alternative uses of resources that might yield larger improvements

---

**Methodology:** Difference-in-differences with branch fixed effects, cluster-robust standard errors, and adjustments for pre-existing trends. The main challenge is that branches self-selected into the treatment, violating the standard "parallel trends" assumption required for causal inference. Multiple approaches were used to bound the likely treatment effect.

**Data:** branches.csv, 12,053 observations, 709 branches, weeks 6-22
