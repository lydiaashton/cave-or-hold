# **Board Memorandum**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** [Current Date]  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout.**

Contrary to initial expectations, the new rotation and shelving procedure **produced a measurable improvement** in service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure achieved service scores approximately **2 points higher** (roughly **4% improvement**) than comparable branches that retained the old procedure. This effect is statistically robust and supports expanding the new procedure to all 150 branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Analysis Overview**
| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| Study period | 17 weeks (Weeks 6–22) |
| Branches analyzed | 150 total |
| Treatment group | 90 branches (adopted new procedure at Week 15) |
| Control group | 60 branches (retained old procedure) |
| Observations | 2,550 weekly records |
| Method | Difference-in-differences regression |

### **Results Summary**
| Metric | Control Group | Treatment Group |
|--------|---------------|-----------------|
| Pre-switch average | 47.9 | 48.0 |
| Post-switch average | 48.4 | 50.6 |
| Change | +0.5 | **+2.5** |

**Net effect of new procedure: +2.06 points** (95% CI: 0.86 to 3.27)

### **Statistical Confidence**
- **P-value: 0.0008** — less than 1 in 1,000 chance this result is due to random variation
- **Parallel trends confirmed** — both groups moved similarly before the switch (p=0.22), validating the comparison
- **Result holds** when controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### **Why This Was Unexpected**
The analysis was designed to detect *any* change — positive or negative. The expectation was no effect. Instead:
- The treatment group pulled away from the control group immediately after Week 15
- The gap persisted across all 8 post-switch weeks
- The effect remained significant after accounting for other factors

---

## **Implications**

### **For Service Quality**
A 4% improvement in service scores translates to noticeably better patron experience across the cooperative. Given that the average branch scores around 50, a 2-point gain is meaningful.

### **For Operations**
The new procedure delivers value without requiring additional resources — the improvement comes from process change alone.

### **For Equity**
Rolling out the procedure system-wide ensures all patrons benefit, not just those served by the 90 early-adopter branches.

## **Risk Considerations**

| Risk | Mitigation |
|------|------------|
| Implementation variability across branches | Provide standardized training and support |
| Short-term disruption during transition | Phase rollout; allow adjustment period |
| Effect may diminish over time | Continue monitoring; refresh training as needed |

---

## **Conclusion**

This evaluation produced an unexpected but welcome result: a simple procedural change improved service quality without additional cost. The evidence is strong enough to justify cooperative-wide adoption. We recommend the Board approve the rollout and allocate resources for training and implementation support.
