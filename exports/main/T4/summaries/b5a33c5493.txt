# **Analysis of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Contrary to expectations, the new procedure produced a measurable improvement in service scores.** Branches using the new procedure gained approximately 2 points (4% of average) relative to those using the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant. **We recommend proceeding with the full rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. **Unexpected Positive Effect**
- The new procedure was expected to have **no impact** on service scores
- Instead, treatment branches showed a **2.04-point improvement** relative to control branches
- This effect is **statistically significant** (p = 0.002), with a 95% confidence interval of **0.74 to 3.35 points**
- The result is robust across multiple analytical approaches

### 2. **Before and After Comparison**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (Old Procedure) | 50.83 | 50.34 | -0.49 |
| Treatment (New Procedure) | 49.89 | 51.45 | +1.56 |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | | | **+2.04** |

### 3. **Study Design**
- **150 branches**: 90 adopted the new procedure, 60 retained the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data**: Weeks 6–14 (before), Weeks 15–22 (after)
- **2,550 observations** total
- Pre-switch trends were parallel between groups, supporting the validity of the comparison

### 4. **Why This Matters**
- A procedure change expected to be neutral on service quality instead **improved it**
- This suggests potential **unintended benefits**—perhaps the new rotation method indirectly improves patron experience, staff efficiency, or material availability
- The improvement comes at no apparent cost to service quality

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve full rollout** of the new procedure to all branches
2. **Investigate the mechanism**: Conduct qualitative follow-up to understand *why* service scores improved (e.g., staff interviews, patron feedback)
3. **Document lessons learned**: Capture any incidental benefits that could inform future operational changes
4. **Monitor post-rollout**: Track service scores for 3–6 months to confirm the effect persists at scale

---

## **Conclusion**

This analysis reveals a favorable surprise: an operational change designed to improve stock management also enhanced service quality. The board can proceed with confidence that the rollout will not harm—and is likely to improve—the patron experience.
