# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Prepared by:** Data Analysis Office

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

The new stock rotation procedure shows **promising but inconclusive evidence** of improving service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.3 points (4.5%)** relative to branches that kept the old procedure. However, this effect is **not statistically significant** at conventional levels, meaning we cannot confidently rule out that the observed improvement occurred by chance.

**Recommendation:** Do not proceed with immediate system-wide rollout. Instead, expand the pilot to additional branches while continuing rigorous evaluation.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **Study Overview**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22 of the year)
- **20 branches** switched to the new procedure in week 15; **14 branches** maintained the old procedure as a control group
- Analysis compares service score changes between the two groups before and after the switch

### **Quantitative Results**

| Measure | Value |
|---------|-------|
| Estimated effect on service score | **+2.3 points** |
| Baseline average (pre-switch) | 51.1 points |
| Relative improvement | **4.5%** |
| Statistical significance | p = 0.10 (not significant at 0.05 level) |
| 95% Confidence Interval | **-0.5 to +5.2 points** |

### **What This Means**
- The new procedure is **associated with modest improvement**, but the evidence is not strong enough to conclude the effect is real rather than random variation
- The confidence interval tells us the true effect could reasonably be anywhere from slightly negative to moderately positive
- **Pre-existing trends were parallel** between the two groups before the switch, which validates our comparison approach
- If the effect is real, a 4.5% improvement would be **meaningful for patrons** but should be weighed against implementation costs

### **Visual Evidence**
The attached trend analysis shows that switching branches started with lower service scores but narrowed the gap with control branches after adopting the new procedure. Post-switch, the two groups performed at similar levels.

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

| Action | Timeline | Purpose |
|--------|----------|---------|
| **Extend data collection** | 8–12 additional weeks | Increase statistical power to detect the true effect |
| **Expand pilot to 10–15 more branches** | Next quarter | Improve precision while maintaining a control group |
| **Conduct cost-benefit analysis** | 4 weeks | Determine if 4.5% improvement justifies implementation costs |
| **Gather staff feedback** | Ongoing | Identify implementation barriers and best practices |
| **Analyze branch-level heterogeneity** | 6 weeks | Determine if the procedure works better in certain branch types |

---

## **BOARD DECISION REQUESTED**

We recommend the board **authorize a phased expansion** of the new procedure rather than full system-wide rollout. This approach:

✓ Captures potential benefits if the effect is real  
✓ Limits risk if the effect proves illusory  
✓ Generates stronger evidence for a final decision  
✓ Allows time for cost-benefit assessment  

A decision on system-wide rollout should be deferred until additional data provides greater statistical certainty.
