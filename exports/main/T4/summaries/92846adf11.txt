# **Memorandum to the Board**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** [Current Date]  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure  

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout.** Contrary to initial expectations, the new procedure produced a **statistically significant improvement** in service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure gained approximately **2.1 points** on the service index, while branches using the old procedure showed no meaningful change.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Measurable Improvement in Service Quality**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Treatment (switched to new procedure) | 50.3 | 52.4 | **+2.1** |
| Control (kept old procedure) | 48.3 | 48.4 | **+0.1** |

**Net effect of new procedure: +2.0 points** (4% improvement relative to the average score of 50)

### **2. Results Are Statistically Robust**

- **Treatment effect:** +2.09 points
- **95% Confidence Interval:** [1.19, 3.00] points
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001
- The finding holds after controlling for branch-specific characteristics, staffing levels, and visitor volume

### **3. Study Design Validates the Conclusion**

- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 treatment branches** adopted the new procedure in week 15; **60 control branches** did not
- Pre-switch trends were parallel between groups (p = 0.21), confirming the groups were comparable before the intervention
- The difference-in-differences methodology isolates the procedure's effect from other factors

### **4. Unexpected but Clear Results**

The analysis team initially expected no measurable difference in service scores. The data, however, shows a consistent and statistically reliable improvement. This suggests the new procedure may have beneficial effects that were not anticipated—possibly through improved staff efficiency, better stock accessibility, or other operational gains worth investigating further.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve full rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all branches

2. **Communicate findings** to branch managers, emphasizing the unexpected positive outcome

3. **Document implementation practices** from the 90 early-adopter branches to support training and consistency

4. **Monitor post-rollout performance** to confirm the effect holds at system-wide scale

5. **Investigate mechanisms** behind the improvement (e.g., staff surveys, time-motion studies) to understand *why* the procedure works and identify additional optimization opportunities

---

## **Appendix: Analytical Notes**

- **Method:** Difference-in-differences regression with branch fixed effects
- **Controls:** Staffing index, visits index, branch-specific characteristics
- **Model fit:** R² = 0.69
- **Data source:** Weekly branch-level service scores, weeks 6–22
- **Visualization:** Trend chart available showing parallel pre-trends and divergence post-switch

---

**The evidence supports moving forward with confidence.** While the positive result was unexpected, it represents a genuine improvement in service quality at no apparent cost, since the procedure change was already implemented at 90 branches.
