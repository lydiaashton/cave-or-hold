# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Office  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

Branches that adopted the new procedure showed **modestly higher service scores** (+2 points on average), consistent with our expectations. However, with only eight weeks of post-switch data, we cannot confidently distinguish this improvement from normal variation. **We recommend continuing the pilot for 8-12 more weeks before deciding on systemwide rollout.**

---

## **What We Did**

- Compared 20 branches that switched to the new procedure against 14 branches that kept the old procedure
- Analyzed 17 weeks of service scores (9 weeks before, 8 weeks after the switch)
- Used standard statistical methods that account for differences between branches and week-to-week fluctuations

---

## **What We Found**

| Metric | Result |
|--------|--------|
| **Estimated improvement** | +2.0 service score points |
| **Typical score range** | 45–55 (average ~50) |
| **Confidence interval** | -0.5 to +4.5 points |
| **Statistical confidence** | Not conclusive (p=0.12) |

**In plain terms:** The data point in the direction we hoped—branches using the new procedure scored about 2 points higher than expected. But we cannot rule out that this could be due to chance. More data would give us a clearer answer.

---

## **Why We Need More Time**

1. **Eight weeks is a short window.** Service scores fluctuate week to week. A longer observation period would help separate the procedure's effect from normal variation.

2. **The effect may grow over time.** Staff may still be learning the new procedure. Full benefits might not appear until implementation is routine.

3. **We have a solid foundation.** The analysis shows no red flags—branches that switched were on similar trends before the change, and the post-switch direction is positive.

---

## **Recommendation**

**Continue the pilot through Week 30 (approximately 15 more weeks), then re-evaluate.** This would:

- Double our post-switch observations
- Increase our ability to detect a real effect if one exists
- Allow time for staff to fully adapt to the new procedure

If the +2 point improvement holds or grows with more data, we would have strong evidence to support systemwide rollout.
