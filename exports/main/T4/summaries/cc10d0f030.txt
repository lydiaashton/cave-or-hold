# **Board Summary: New Shelving & Rotation Procedure Impact Analysis**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Recommendation: Approve system-wide rollout of the new procedure.**

The new shelving and rotation procedure increased service scores by approximately **2.4 points** (a **5% improvement**). This result is statistically significant, robust across multiple analytical methods, and shows no adverse effects on staffing or visitor experience.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
| | Treatment Group | Control Group |
|---|---|---|
| **Branches** | 90 | 60 |
| **Procedure** | New method | Existing method |
| **Observation Period** | Weeks 6–22 | Weeks 6–22 |

### Service Score Impact

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Treatment (switched) | 49.96 | 51.40 | +1.44 |
| Control (no switch) | 51.28 | 50.26 | −1.01 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+2.45** |

The treatment group improved while the control group declined slightly, yielding a **net positive effect of 2.45 points**.

### Statistical Confidence
- **95% Confidence Interval:** 1.7 to 3.2 points
- **Statistical Significance:** p < 0.001 (highly significant)
- **Robustness:** Effect remains consistent across all model specifications, including controls for branch characteristics, seasonal week effects, staffing levels, and visitor volume

### Validity Checks
✓ **Parallel pre-trends confirmed** — Both groups tracked closely before the switch (Weeks 6–14)  
✓ **Balanced covariates** — Staffing and visitor patterns were similar between groups  
✓ **No negative trade-offs** — No adverse effects detected on operational measures

### Practical Significance
- System-wide average service score is approximately 50
- A 2.4-point gain represents meaningful improvement in patron experience
- Effect size is comparable to other successful operational interventions in the cooperative
