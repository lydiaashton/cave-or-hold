# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Prepared by:** Data Analysis Team

---

## **Executive Summary**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure is associated with a **statistically significant improvement in service scores**. Branches that adopted the new procedure showed service score gains of approximately **1.9 points** compared to branches that retained the old procedure. Based on these findings, we recommend proceeding with a phased rollout to all branches, accompanied by continued monitoring.

---

## **Bottom Line**

| Finding | Result |
|---------|--------|
| **Effect on service score** | +1.86 points (p < 0.001) |
| **95% Confidence interval** | 1.31 to 2.41 points |
| **Improvement vs. average** | ~4% |
| **Recommendation** | Proceed with rollout |

---

## **Analysis Overview**

### **Study Design**

We analyzed weekly service scores from **709 branches** over **17 weeks** (weeks 6-22 of the year). The new procedure was implemented at **425 branches** starting in week 15, while **284 branches** continued with the existing procedure. This natural experiment allows us to isolate the effect of the new procedure.

### **Methodology**

We used a **difference-in-differences** approach with branch fixed effects and cluster-robust standard errors. This method compares the change in service scores for switching branches against the change for non-switching branches, controlling for branch-specific characteristics and week-to-week variation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Service Scores Improved for Switching Branches**

After controlling for branch characteristics and time trends:

- Switching branches improved by **+1.86 points** relative to non-switching branches
- This effect is **highly statistically significant** (p < 0.001)
- The 95% confidence interval (1.31 to 2.41) excludes zero, indicating a robust positive effect

### **2. Both Groups Faced Headwinds, But Switching Branches Were More Resilient**

Overall service scores declined during the study period for both groups, reflecting broader challenges. However:

| Group | Pre-Switch Trend | Post-Switch Trend |
|-------|------------------|-------------------|
| Non-switching branches | -0.88 pts/week | -1.14 pts/week |
| Switching branches | -0.57 pts/week | -0.91 pts/week |

Branches with the new procedure experienced a **slower rate of decline**, effectively protecting service quality during a challenging period.

### **3. Effect Emerged After Implementation**

Week-by-week analysis confirms the treatment effect appeared following week 15, when the new procedure took effect. The gap between switching and non-switching branches widened in the post-implementation period.

---

## **Recommendations**

### **1. Approve System-Wide Rollout**

The evidence supports expanding the new procedure to all remaining branches. The estimated **~2-point improvement** in service scores represents meaningful progress toward our service quality goals.

### **2. Implement in Phases**

Roll out the procedure in **2-3 waves** over the next quarter to:
- Allow staff training and adjustment time
- Monitor for any implementation issues
- Capture ongoing performance data

### **3. Establish Monitoring Dashboard**

Track the following metrics monthly for 12 months post-rollout:
- Service score by branch
- Staffing index and visits index (to rule out confounding factors)
- Branch-level compliance with the new procedure

### **4. Document Best Practices**

Interview high-performing switching branches to identify:
- Successful implementation approaches
- Staff training methods that worked well
- Any adaptations that enhanced the procedure's effectiveness

The new stock rotation procedure delivers a **measurable, positive impact on service quality**. We recommend the Board approve the rollout and allocate resources for implementation support and ongoing monitoring.
