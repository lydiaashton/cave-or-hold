# **New Shelving Procedure: Impact on Service Scores**
### *Analysis for Board Decision on System-Wide Rollout*

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new shelving and rotation procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **2.1 points** (approximately **4% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant, consistent over time, and not explained by changes in staffing or visitor volume.

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Measurable Improvement**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Treatment effect | +2.1 points |
| 95% Confidence interval | 1.5 to 2.6 points |
| Percentage improvement | 4.1% |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 |

### **2. Rigorous Analysis**
- **Method:** Difference-in-differences comparison (standard causal inference approach)
- **Treatment group:** 425 branches that switched to the new procedure
- **Control group:** 284 branches that kept the old procedure
- **Data:** 12,053 branch-week observations across 17 weeks
- **Robustness:** Results hold after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### **3. Effect Sustained and Growing**
The improvement was consistent across all 8 weeks after implementation, with evidence of strengthening over time:

- **Weeks 15–19:** Average +2.4 points
- **Weeks 20–22:** Average +4.0 points

This pattern suggests branches may continue improving as they gain experience with the new procedure.

### **4. No Pre-Existing Differences**
Service score trends for switched and non-switched branches followed similar patterns before the switch, supporting the conclusion that the observed improvement is caused by the new procedure rather than pre-existing differences between branches.
