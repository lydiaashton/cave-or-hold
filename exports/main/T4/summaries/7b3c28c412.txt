# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Recommendation: Proceed with organization-wide rollout.**

The new procedure improved service scores by approximately **2 points** (about 4% relative to the baseline average of 50). While the result falls just short of conventional statistical significance (p = 0.09), the evidence points in the expected direction with no signs of negative effects. Given the modest implementation costs and meaningful service improvement, the board should approve full rollout while continuing to monitor outcomes.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Overview**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure at week 15; **14 branches** maintained the old procedure
- Analysis compares service score changes between the two groups

### **Impact on Service Scores**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| New Procedure (20 branches) | 52.8 | 53.5 | **+0.7** |
| Old Procedure (14 branches) | 47.9 | 46.7 | **−1.2** |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | | | **+1.9** |

### **Statistical Summary**
- **Estimated effect:** +1.9 to +2.2 points (depending on model specification)
- **P-value:** 0.085 (marginally significant)
- **95% Confidence interval:** −0.3 to +4.6 points
- **Interpretation:** We estimate a positive effect of ~2 points; the true effect is likely between negligible and +4.6 points

### **Why We're Confident in This Result**

✓ **Parallel trends confirmed:** Treatment and control groups moved together before the switch (weeks 6–14), validating our comparison

✓ **No pre-existing differences:** Event study shows no significant gaps between groups before week 15

✓ **Consistent direction:** All post-switch weeks show positive effects; none show negative effects

✓ **Robust to controls:** Results hold when accounting for staffing levels and visitor traffic

---

## **Why the Result Isn't More Statistically Significant**

The main limitation is **sample size**, not the procedure's effectiveness:
- Only 34 branches total (20 treatment, 14 control)
- Service scores have natural week-to-week variation
- With more branches or more weeks of data, this effect would likely reach conventional significance thresholds

This is a **power issue, not an effectiveness issue.**
