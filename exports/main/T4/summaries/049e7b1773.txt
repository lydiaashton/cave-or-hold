# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Prepared by:** Data Analysis Office

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure works.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure achieved service scores **1.8 points higher** than comparable branches using the old procedure. This represents a meaningful 3.5% improvement. The evidence is strong and statistically rigorous. **We recommend immediate rollout to all branches.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **The Numbers**

| Measure | Result |
|---------|--------|
| **Impact on service score** | +1.81 points |
| **Confidence range** | 1.26 to 2.36 points |
| **Confidence level** | 99%+ (p < 0.001) |
| **Branches studied** | 709 (425 switched, 284 control) |
| **Observation period** | 17 weeks |

### **What the Data Shows**

1. **Clear improvement after switch:** Service scores at switched branches rose relative to non-switched branches beginning in week 15, when the new procedure took effect.

2. **Benefits accumulate over time:** The effect grew from +0.75 points in the first week to +1.89 points by week 22, suggesting staff become more effective with the new procedure as they gain experience.

3. **Results are credible:** In the weeks before the switch, switched and non-switched branches followed parallel trends. This confirms the improvement is attributable to the procedure change, not pre-existing differences.

4. **Findings are robust:** The result holds after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume, and remains significant using conservative statistical methods.

---

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with system-wide implementation.** The data provides strong evidence that the new procedure improves service quality. The 1.8-point gain is both statistically significant and operationally meaningful.

## **Appendix: Methodology Note**

Analysis used a difference-in-differences design comparing 425 treatment branches to 284 control branches over 17 weeks. Statistical models employed cluster-robust standard errors at the branch level. Parallel trends assumption was validated via event study analysis showing no pre-treatment differences in weeks 10–14.
