# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

---

## **Subject: Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

### **BOTTOM LINE**

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout of the new procedure.**

The new rotation and shelving procedure produced a **statistically significant improvement** in service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure improved by approximately **2.1 points** (a 4% gain), while branches that kept the old procedure showed no meaningful change.

---

### **KEY FINDINGS**

| Metric | Control Branches (Old Procedure) | Treatment Branches (New Procedure) |
|--------|----------------------------------|-----------------------------------|
| Average score before switch | 48.3 | 50.3 |
| Average score after switch | 48.4 | 52.4 |
| **Change** | **+0.1** | **+2.1** |

**Net Effect of New Procedure: +2.1 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.2 to 3.0 points)

**1. Clear Positive Impact**
- The improvement is highly statistically significant (p < 0.001)
- Results hold after accounting for branch-specific characteristics, staffing levels, and visitor volume
- The effect represents approximately a **4% improvement** over the system average of 50 points

**2. Valid Comparison**
- 150 branches studied over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- 90 branches adopted the new procedure; 60 branches maintained the old procedure as a control group
- Pre-switch trends were parallel between groups, confirming the groups were comparable before the intervention

**3. No Confounding Factors Detected**
- Staffing and visitation patterns were controlled for in the analysis
- The control group's stable performance rules out system-wide factors as the cause of improvement

---

### **NEXT STEPS**

| Priority | Action |
|----------|--------|
| 1 | **Approve system-wide rollout** to all remaining branches |
| 2 | **Develop implementation guidance** based on the 90 branches that successfully adopted the procedure |
| 3 | **Monitor service scores** post-rollout to confirm effects persist at scale |
| 4 | **Document lessons learned** to support training and future operational improvements |

---

### **ANALYTICAL NOTES**

This evaluation used a **difference-in-differences** methodology, the standard approach for policy evaluation when random assignment is not feasible. This design isolates the effect of the new procedure by comparing changes in the treatment group against changes in the control group, netting out any factors that affected all branches simultaneously.

**Data:** 2,550 weekly observations from 150 branches (weeks 6–22)  
**Switch date:** Week 15  
**Statistical model:** Branch fixed effects regression with controls for staffing and visitation indices
