# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure **significantly improved service scores**. Branches that adopted the new procedure saw an average increase of **2.2 points** (approximately **4% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and contradicts our initial expectation of no difference. **We recommend proceeding with a cooperative-wide rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** switched to the new procedure at week 15 (treatment group)
- **60 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in both groups before and after the switch

### **Results**

| Metric | Switched Branches | Non-Switched Branches |
|--------|-------------------|----------------------|
| Average score before switch | 51.0 | 50.4 |
| Average score after switch | 53.3 | 50.5 |
| **Change** | **+2.3** | **+0.1** |

- **Treatment effect: +2.19 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.0 to 3.4 points)
- **Statistical significance: p < 0.001** (highly significant)
- **73% of switched branches** improved vs. only **43% of control branches**

### **Validation Checks**
- **Parallel trends confirmed**: Before the switch, both groups followed similar trajectories (p = 0.88 for trend difference), supporting the validity of our comparison
- **Effect is robust**: Controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume does not change the result
- **Effect size**: The improvement represents about 0.24 standard deviations—a modest but meaningful gain for service quality

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve cooperative-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining 60 branches

2. **Monitor implementation**: Track service scores during rollout to ensure consistent results across all branches

3. **Document best practices**: Investigate whether certain branches achieved larger gains and identify implementation factors that contributed to success

4. **Conduct cost-benefit analysis**: If the new procedure requires additional resources, weigh the 4% service improvement against implementation costs

5. **Establish long-term tracking**: Continue monitoring to confirm the effect persists beyond the initial post-switch period
