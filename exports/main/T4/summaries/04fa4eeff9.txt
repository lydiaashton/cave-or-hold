# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure  

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure shows a **positive but statistically inconclusive effect** on service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure experienced service scores approximately **2 points higher** (about 4% of the average score of 50) than would have been expected based on control branch trends. However, this effect is **not statistically significant** (p = 0.11), meaning we cannot confidently rule out random variation as the cause.

**Recommendation:** Do not proceed with a full system-wide rollout at this time. Continue monitoring the pilot branches for an additional 8-12 weeks to gather more data before making a final decision.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **1. Treatment Effect**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect | **+2.05 points** on service score |
| 95% Confidence Interval | **-0.49 to +4.59 points** |
| p-value | **0.114** |
| Statistically significant | **No** |

### **2. What Happened to Each Group**

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|----------------|---------------|--------|
| Control branches (old procedure) | 47.09 | 45.96 | **-1.13** ↓ |
| Switched branches (new procedure) | 49.36 | 50.25 | **+0.89** ↑ |

Control branches experienced a decline in service scores during the study period, while switched branches maintained and slightly improved their scores.

### **3. Analysis Validity**

The key assumption underlying this analysis—that treatment and control branches were on similar trajectories before the switch—**is supported by the data**:
- Pre-switch trends were statistically indistinguishable between groups (p = 0.85)
- This validates the comparison and suggests the estimate is unbiased

### **4. Effect Over Time**

Weekly effects after the switch ranged from -0.3 to +3.0 points, averaging **+1.5 points**. While no individual week reached statistical significance, all post-switch estimates were positive, indicating a consistent directional effect.

### **5. Study Scope**

- **34 branches total:** 20 switched to the new procedure, 14 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data:** Weeks 6-22 (switch occurred at week 15)
- **578 observations:** One row per branch per week

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

### **Immediate Actions**

1. **Extend the pilot period** by 8-12 weeks to increase statistical power and determine whether the ~2 point effect is genuine.

2. **Continue weekly monitoring** of service scores at all pilot branches with reports to the board.

3. **Gather qualitative feedback** from staff and patrons at switched branches to understand operational impacts beyond the service score metric.

### **Decision Timeline**

| Timeline | Action |
|----------|--------|
| Now | Continue current pilot; no expansion |
| 8-12 weeks | Re-analyze with additional data |
| Q2 2027 | Board decision on system-wide rollout |

### **Decision Framework**

| If the effect... | Then... |
|------------------|---------|
| Remains ~2 points and becomes significant (p < 0.05) | Proceed with full rollout |
| Grows to 3+ points even if not significant | Consider phased expansion |
| Diminishes or turns negative | Halt expansion; investigate causes |
| Stays ~2 points and remains inconclusive | Extend pilot another quarter |

## **TECHNICAL APPENDIX**

- **Method:** Difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch)
- **Controls:** Staffing index, visits index, week fixed effects
- **Software:** Python (statsmodels)
- **Data:** 578 weekly observations from 34 branches
