# **Board Summary: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Recommend proceeding with rollout.** While the new procedure was not expected to affect service scores, our analysis reveals an unexpected benefit: branches using the new procedure achieved service scores approximately **1.9 points (3.8%) higher** than comparable branches keeping the old procedure. This represents a positive side effect with no identified downside.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Unexpected Positive Effect on Service Scores
- The new procedure was designed for stock rotation efficiency, not service quality
- Despite this, treatment branches showed **1.9 points higher service scores** than expected (p = 0.002)
- 95% confidence interval: **+0.7 to +3.1 points**

### 2. The Benefit: Maintaining Quality During Challenging Periods
The data suggests the new procedure helps branches sustain service quality:
- **Control branches** (old procedure): Scores **fell 1.7 points** after week 15
- **Treatment branches** (new procedure): Scores **held steady with a slight gain of 0.2 points**

This indicates the new procedure may provide operational stability that indirectly supports service delivery.

### 3. Analysis Is Reliable
- **Parallel trends verified**: Both groups tracked similarly before the switch, confirming valid comparison
- **No pre-existing differences**: Treatment and control branches had comparable staffing and visit patterns
- **Robust sample**: 150 branches, 17 weeks of data (2,550 observations)

### 4. What We Already Knew
- Staffing levels strongly correlate with service scores (as expected)
- The treatment effect holds even after accounting for staffing and visit variations

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve systemwide rollout** — The procedure delivers its intended operational benefits plus an unexpected service quality bonus

2. **Document the finding** — Update internal expectations to reflect that this procedure may support service quality, not just stock management

3. **Monitor during rollout** — Confirm the effect replicates as remaining branches adopt the procedure

4. **Consider staffing interaction** — Since staffing correlates with service scores, ensure rollout plans account for adequate staffing levels to maximize the benefit

5. **6-month follow-up** — Verify the effect persists after full implementation

---

## **Bottom Line for Discussion**

The board can proceed with confidence: the new procedure meets its operational goals and delivers an unanticipated improvement in service scores. There is no evidence of negative effects, and the positive impact on service quality strengthens the case for systemwide adoption.

---

*Analysis based on branches.csv (150 branches, weeks 6-22, 2,550 observations). Difference-in-differences estimation with cluster-robust standard errors.*
