# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

**Date:** November 2026

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by **1.87 points** relative to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents approximately a **4% improvement** and is statistically significant.

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
- **150 branches** participated: 90 adopted the new procedure, 60 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** collected (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Analysis compares changes in service scores between the two groups before and after the switch

### Results
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 50.56 | 47.98 | -2.57 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 49.63 | 48.93 | -0.70 |

Both groups experienced a decline in service scores after week 15, but **branches with the new procedure declined significantly less**. The net benefit of the new procedure is **+1.87 points** (95% confidence interval: 0.73 to 3.01).

### Statistical Confidence
- The result is highly reliable (p = 0.001)
- Pre-switch trends were similar between groups, supporting the validity of the comparison
- Results hold after accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume

### Variation Across Branches
- **Higher-performing branches** saw larger gains (+2.29 points)
- **Lower-performing branches** also benefited (+1.44 points), though the effect is less pronounced

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all branches

2. **Provide targeted support to lower-performing branches** during implementation to ensure they realize the full benefit

3. **Continue monitoring** service scores quarterly after full rollout to confirm sustained improvement

4. **Investigate the overall decline** in service scores after week 15 (affecting both groups) to identify and address any external factors
