# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Subject:** Recommendation on System-Wide Rollout of New Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure shows **promising but inconclusive results**. Branches that adopted the procedure saw modest service score improvements (approximately 2 points on a 50-point scale), but the evidence is **not statistically significant** at conventional levels. We recommend **continuing the pilot with enhanced monitoring** rather than proceeding with an immediate system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Impact on Service Scores**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Treatment (20 branches) | 48.7 | 50.5 | **+1.8** |
| Control (14 branches) | 47.6 | 47.5 | **-0.1** |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+1.9 points** |

- **95% Confidence Interval:** -0.3 to +4.2 points
- **Statistical Significance:** p = 0.11 (not significant at p < 0.05 threshold)
- **Effect Size:** Medium (Cohen's d = 0.57)

### **Branch-Level Variation**

The procedure did not affect all branches equally:

- **14 of 20 treatment branches (70%)** improved after switching
- **6 of 20 treatment branches (30%)** saw scores decline
- **Top gainers:** +6 to +8 points
- **Largest declines:** -2 to -3.5 points
- **Control branches:** Only 50% improved during the same period

This variation suggests the procedure's effectiveness may depend on branch-specific factors.

### **Data Quality & Validity**

- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **Parallel trends confirmed:** Treatment and control groups showed similar patterns before the switch
- **Controls accounted for:** Staffing levels and visit volumes both correlate with service scores

---

## **Recommendations**

### **1. Do Not Proceed with Immediate System-Wide Rollout**

The current evidence does not meet the threshold for confident system-wide adoption. The observed improvement could plausibly be due to random variation.

### **2. Extend the Pilot Period**

- Collect **8–12 additional weeks** of data to increase statistical power
- This would improve our ability to detect whether the 2-point effect is real

### **3. Investigate Branch-Level Differences**

Prioritize understanding why outcomes varied:
- Which branch characteristics predict success (size, layout, staffing, patron demographics)?
- Were there differences in training quality or implementation fidelity?
- Can we identify branches most likely to benefit?

### **4. Consider a Voluntary, Supported Rollout**

Rather than mandating the procedure:
- Offer it as an **option** for interested branches
- Provide **enhanced training and implementation support**
- Require **participation in ongoing evaluation**

### **5. Establish Clear Success Criteria**

Define in advance what would justify full adoption:
- Example: "+2 points sustained over 12 weeks with p < 0.05"
- Revisit the decision once additional data is collected

---

## **Conclusion**

The new procedure **may improve service scores**, but the evidence is not yet strong enough to justify mandatory system-wide adoption. The board should authorize continued evaluation with targeted support for branches that struggled during the initial pilot. This balanced approach protects against adopting an ineffective procedure while preserving the opportunity to scale a genuinely beneficial one.

---

*Analysis based on weekly service scores from 34 branches (weeks 6–22). Twenty branches adopted the new procedure in week 15; fourteen branches continued with the existing procedure. Service scores are an internal index where approximately 50 represents average performance.*
