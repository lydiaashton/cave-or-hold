# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

## **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

### **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores and should be rolled out system-wide.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw a statistically significant increase in service scores of **2.3 points (4.5% improvement)** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. The evidence is strong and supports immediate approval for full implementation.

---

### **Key Findings**

| Finding | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Treatment Effect** | +2.26 service score points (p < 0.001) |
| **Confidence Interval** | 95% CI: +1.1 to +3.4 points |
| **Effect Size** | 4.5% improvement over baseline |
| **Study Scope** | 34 branches, 17 weeks (weeks 6–22) |
| **Treatment Group** | 20 branches adopted new procedure |
| **Control Group** | 14 branches kept old procedure |

**1. Clear Improvement in Service Quality**
- Treatment branches improved from 50.0 to 52.3 after adopting the new procedure
- Control branches remained stable (49.1 to 49.1)
- The gap widened after week 15 when the switch occurred

**2. Results Are Statistically Robust**
- Highly significant (p < 0.001) even after controlling for branch-specific factors
- Effect persists when accounting for staffing levels and visitor traffic
- The improvement is attributable to the procedure itself, not other operational changes

**3. Research Design Is Sound**
- Treatment and control branches showed parallel trends before the switch
- This validates the comparison and supports causal interpretation
- All 34 branches have complete data across all 17 weeks

---

### **Recommendation**

**Approve immediate system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all remaining branches.

---

### **Next Steps**

1. **Authorize full implementation** across all 14 control branches and any other locations still using the old procedure

2. **Develop training materials** based on practices from the 20 early-adopter branches

3. **Monitor service scores** during rollout to confirm the 4.5% improvement is realized system-wide

4. **Prioritize branches** with lowest current service scores if phased implementation is needed due to resource constraints

5. **Continue tracking** service scores, staffing, and visits quarterly to ensure sustained benefits

---

### **Appendix: Methodology Summary**

The analysis uses a difference-in-differences regression with branch fixed effects. This approach controls for time-invariant differences between branches and isolates the causal effect of the procedure change. Pre-treatment trend analysis confirms treatment and control groups were evolving similarly before the intervention, validating the research design.

*Data source: branches.csv (578 observations, 34 branches, weeks 6–22)*
