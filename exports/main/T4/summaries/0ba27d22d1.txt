# **Library Branch Shelving Procedure: Impact Analysis**

**Prepared for the Board of Directors**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new shelving and rotation procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.1 points** (about 4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and has grown stronger over the 8 weeks since implementation. **We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. The New Procedure Improved Service Scores**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| **Treatment (New Procedure)** | 49.72 | 50.39 | **+0.67** |
| **Control (Old Procedure)** | 49.42 | 47.95 | **-1.47** |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+2.10** |

- The 2.10-point improvement is **highly statistically significant** (p < 0.001)
- This represents approximately a **4% improvement** in service quality
- The effect holds after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### **2. Benefits Are Widespread and Growing**

- **59%** of branches using the new procedure saw their scores improve, compared to only **33%** of branches using the old procedure
- The improvement has **strengthened over time**: the gap between treatment and control branches grew from 1.7 points in week 15 to 3.4 points by week 22
- This suggests branches continue to realize greater benefits as they gain experience with the new procedure

### **3. Study Context**

- **709 branches** total: 425 adopted the new procedure; 284 kept the old procedure
- Data covers **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Control branches experienced score declines during this period, which may reflect seasonal patterns or system-wide factors

---

## **Recommendations**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new shelving and rotation procedure to all remaining branches

2. **Support implementation** with training resources, recognizing that benefits increase with experience

3. **Monitor outcomes** for 8–12 weeks post-implementation at each branch to ensure expected improvements are realized

4. **Investigate the control group decline** to identify any additional opportunities for system-wide improvement

---

## **Technical Note**

*Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch) to account for repeated observations. The model controls for staffing index and visits index. Data includes 12,053 weekly observations from 709 branches.*
