# **Board Summary: Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.1 to 2.5 points** (about 4-5% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and grows over time as staff gain experience. **We recommend proceeding with a full rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measurable Improvement in Service Quality
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Treatment (switched) | 50.7 | 53.3 | +2.6 |
| Control (not switched) | 48.9 | 49.3 | +0.4 |

**Net effect of new procedure: +2.1 points** (p < 0.001)

### 2. Effect Strengthens Over Time
The benefit grows as branches gain experience with the new procedure:

| Period | Average Effect |
|--------|----------------|
| Weeks 15-18 (early) | +0.3 to +1.1 points |
| Weeks 19-22 (later) | +1.5 to +2.7 points |

This suggests staff need 3-4 weeks to fully realize the benefits.

### 3. Analysis Is Robust
- **709 branches** across **17 weeks** (12,053 observations)
- Results hold when controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume
- Results hold when accounting for inherent differences between branches
- Pre-treatment trends support the validity of the comparison

### 4. Practical Significance
On a service score scale where the average branch scores about 50, a **2+ point improvement** represents meaningful progress in patron satisfaction and service delivery.

## **Appendix: Methodology**

**Design:** Difference-in-differences analysis comparing switched vs. non-switched branches before and after week 15.

**Statistical approach:** Fixed effects regression with cluster-robust standard errors at the branch level, controlling for staffing index and visits index.

**Data:** Weekly service scores from weeks 6-22, 2024.
