# **Board Memorandum**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2025  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**We cannot recommend a system-wide rollout of the new procedure.** The data shows no statistically significant improvement in service scores. While branches using the new procedure experienced smaller declines than those using the old procedure, the difference is not large enough to conclude the new procedure caused meaningful improvement.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. No Statistically Significant Improvement

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect | +2.1 points |
| 95% confidence interval | -0.5 to +4.6 points |
| P-value | 0.11 |

The confidence interval includes zero, meaning the true effect could be negative, zero, or moderately positive. At the standard 5% significance threshold, we cannot reject the possibility that the new procedure had no effect.

### 2. Service Scores Declined Across All Branches

Both groups experienced drops in service scores after week 15, suggesting an external factor affected the entire system:

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|---------------|---------------|--------|
| Old procedure (14 branches) | 50.35 | 46.84 | **-3.52** |
| New procedure (20 branches) | 54.27 | 52.69 | **-1.58** |

The new procedure branches declined less, but they still declined. This pattern raises concerns about factors beyond the shelving procedure affecting service quality.

### 3. No Sustained Benefit Over Time

Week-by-week analysis after the switch showed:
- **Week 15:** +5.2 points (temporary spike)
- **Weeks 16-22:** Effects ranged from -4.9 to -0.1 points

Any initial benefit disappeared quickly and was not maintained through the observation period.

### 4. Analysis Was Rigorous

- **Data:** 34 branches, 17 weeks, 578 observations
- **Method:** Difference-in-Differences with branch fixed effects
- **Controls:** Staffing levels and visitor volume
- **Validation:** Pre-period trends were parallel between groups, supporting causal interpretation

---

## **Recommendations**

| Priority | Action |
|----------|--------|
| **1** | **Pause system-wide rollout** — Evidence does not support expected improvements |
| **2** | **Investigate system-wide score decline** — Identify what caused scores to drop after week 15 across all branches |
| **3** | **Extend pilot monitoring** — Continue tracking the 20 pilot branches for additional weeks |
| **4** | **Collect staff feedback** — Survey branch managers and staff about operational impacts not captured in service scores |
| **5** | **Reconvene in Q2 2026** — Reassess with extended data and findings from the investigation |

---

## **Conclusion**

The board's expectation that the new procedure would improve service scores is not supported by the evidence. Proceeding with a full rollout would commit resources to a change with unproven benefits. We recommend pausing, investigating the broader decline in service scores, and gathering additional data before making a final decision.
