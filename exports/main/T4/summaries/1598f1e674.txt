# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new shelving and stock rotation procedure improved service scores by approximately 2 points.** Branches that adopted the new procedure maintained their service levels, while branches using the old procedure experienced a decline. The improvement is statistically significant and consistent across multiple analytical approaches. **We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect
- **Service score increase: +2.04 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.47 to 2.61)
- This result is highly statistically significant (p < 0.001)
- The effect remains robust (+2.07 points) even after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### 2. What Happened to Each Group

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| **Switched branches** (new procedure) | 52.4 | 52.6 | +0.1 |
| **Non-switched branches** (old procedure) | 51.0 | 49.1 | -1.9 |

The new procedure appears to have **protected branches from a decline** that affected those using the old procedure.

### 3. Effect Size Context
- Service scores average around 50 points with a standard deviation of 9.7
- The 2-point improvement represents about **21% of one standard deviation**—a meaningful, practically significant gain

### 4. Data Scope
- **709 branches** analyzed over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- Switch occurred at **week 15**
- Approximately **60% of branches** adopted the new procedure
- **12,053 total observations**

---

## **Important Caveats**

1. **Pre-existing differences**: Branches that switched had slightly higher service scores before the change. Our analysis accounts for this, but some unmeasured factors could influence results.

2. **Short-term analysis**: This covers only 8 weeks after the switch. Longer-term monitoring would confirm whether benefits persist.

3. **Weekly variability**: Service scores fluctuate week-to-week. The analysis averages across this noise to identify the underlying treatment effect.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to remaining branches

2. **Monitor service scores** for the next 3–6 months to confirm sustained improvement

3. **Document the new procedure** in detail to ensure consistent implementation across all branches

4. **Consider a follow-up study** after full rollout to measure long-term sustainability and branch-specific factors

5. **Share learnings** with branches that struggled under the old procedure to support their transition

---

## **Analytical Method**

This analysis used a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in service scores between branches that switched procedures and those that did not, before and after week 15. Results were validated with cluster-robust standard errors (accounting for repeated measures within branches) and controls for staffing and visitor volume.
