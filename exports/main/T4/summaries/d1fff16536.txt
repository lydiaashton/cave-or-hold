# **Memorandum to the Board**

## **Title: Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Contrary to the expectation of no difference, branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw their service scores increase by approximately **2 points** (about 4%) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and robust. **We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect
| Measure | Value |
|---------|-------|
| Estimated impact | +2.0 points on service score index |
| 95% confidence interval | 0.8 to 3.3 points |
| Statistical significance | p = 0.002 (highly significant) |
| Improvement relative to baseline | ~4% |

### 2. What the Data Shows

| Group | Before Switch (Weeks 6-14) | After Switch (Weeks 15-22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------------|---------------------------|--------|
| **Control** (kept old procedure) | 51.6 | 49.1 | **−2.5** |
| **Treatment** (new procedure) | 53.1 | 52.6 | **−0.5** |

The control branches experienced a notable decline in service scores after week 15. The treatment branches also declined, but **much less sharply**—the new procedure appears to have buffered against this decline, resulting in a net benefit of 2.0 points.

### 3. Analysis Method
This analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing the change in service scores for switching branches against the change for non-switching branches. This method controls for:
- Pre-existing differences between branches
- System-wide trends affecting all branches (e.g., seasonal patterns)

### 4. Validity Checks
| Check | Result |
|-------|--------|
| Parallel trends assumption | ✓ Confirmed (p = 0.42 pre-switch) |
| Robustness to controls | ✓ Effect holds with staffing/visitor controls |
| Sample size | 150 branches (90 switched, 60 did not) |
| Observations | 2,550 (17 weeks × 150 branches) |

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with rollout** — The evidence supports expanding the new procedure to all branches. The improvement is both statistically reliable and practically meaningful.

2. **Monitor implementation** — Track service scores during the rollout to ensure the effect replicates at scale.

3. **Investigate the control group decline** — Service scores dropped for non-switching branches after week 15. Understanding this decline (seasonal? operational?) could yield additional insights for improving service across the cooperative.

4. **Consider cost-benefit analysis** — If the new procedure involves additional costs (training, time, materials), weigh these against the 4% service score improvement to confirm the value proposition.
