# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure is associated with a **modest improvement in service scores**. Branches that adopted the new procedure saw their scores improve by approximately **2.9 points** (about 6% of the average score) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. While the evidence points in a positive direction, the statistical certainty is moderate, suggesting the board should consider a phased rollout with continued monitoring.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** switched to the new procedure at week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in switched branches against changes in non-switched branches

### **Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches (treatment) | 48.65 | 50.45 | **+1.80** |
| Non-switched branches (control) | 53.61 | 52.54 | **-1.07** |
| **Net Effect (Difference-in-Differences)** | | | **+2.87** |

- The new procedure is associated with a **2.87-point improvement** in service scores
- This represents approximately **6% of the baseline score** (average ~51 points)
- The effect is meaningful in practical terms—about one-quarter of a standard deviation

### **Statistical Confidence**
- The estimated effect is **positive and directionally consistent** with expectations
- Statistical significance is **moderate** (p ≈ 0.12), meaning there is roughly an 88% confidence level that the effect is real
- The 95% confidence interval ranges from approximately **-0.8 to +6.6 points**, indicating the true effect could be small negative to moderately positive

### **Context**
- Switched branches started with lower scores (48.65 vs. 53.61), making the improvement particularly noteworthy
- Control branches experienced a slight decline during the same period, possibly due to seasonal factors or other external influences
- The analysis controls for branch-specific characteristics and week-to-week variation

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with Cautious Rollout**: The evidence supports expanding the new procedure, but given the moderate statistical certainty, a phased approach is recommended rather than an immediate system-wide mandate.

2. **Extend Monitoring Period**: Continue tracking service scores for at least 8–12 more weeks to:
   - Confirm the effect persists over time
   - Increase statistical confidence with more data
   - Identify any branches where the procedure may not work as well

3. **Investigate Implementation Variation**: Examine whether certain branches achieved better results than others. Understanding what drives successful implementation could help maximize benefits during rollout.

4. **Consider Complementary Factors**: The analysis controlled for staffing and visit levels, but branches may benefit from additional support (training, resources) during the transition to ensure consistent results.

5. **Set Success Metrics**: Define clear targets for the full rollout (e.g., minimum 2-point improvement maintained over 3 months) to evaluate whether the expansion delivers expected value.
