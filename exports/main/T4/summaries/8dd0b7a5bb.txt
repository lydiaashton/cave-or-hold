# **MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw their service scores increase by approximately **1.9 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents a **4% improvement** and is statistically significant. We recommend proceeding with a full rollout to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Treatment Effect
| Measure | Value |
|---------|-------|
| Estimated improvement | +1.93 points |
| 95% Confidence interval | 1.39 to 2.48 points |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 (highly significant) |
| Relative improvement | 3.75% above baseline |

### Study Overview
- **709 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **425 branches** adopted the new procedure; **284 branches** kept the old procedure
- Switch occurred at **week 15**
- Analysis uses difference-in-differences methodology with robust standard errors

### Before and After Comparison

| Group | Pre-Switch Avg | Post-Switch Avg | Change |
|-------|---------------|-----------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 51.24 | 50.40 | −0.84 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 51.59 | 52.70 | +1.11 |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | | | **+1.94** |

### Effect Over Time
The improvement was **sustained throughout the post-switch period**. After week 15, treatment branches consistently outperformed control branches by 2.0 to 2.7 points each week, with no sign of the effect fading.

### Important Caveat
Treatment branches showed a **pre-existing upward trend** before the switch—they were already improving relative to control branches by about 0.36 points per week in the pre-period. This means:
- Some portion of the observed effect may reflect this pre-existing trend
- However, the post-switch gap (averaging 2.3 points) is substantially larger than the pre-switch gap (averaging 0.4 points)
- The acceleration after week 15 supports a genuine treatment effect

---

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with full rollout** of the new procedure to all branches. The evidence indicates a meaningful, sustained improvement in service quality that outweighs the uncertainty from pre-existing trends.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Implement full rollout** – Expand the new procedure to remaining 284 branches with expected service score gains of 1.5–2.0 points per branch.

2. **Monitor for 8–12 weeks** – Track service scores post-rollout to confirm the effect persists at scale and does not fade over time.

3. **Conduct cost-benefit analysis** – Compare service score gains against implementation costs (training, temporary disruption, materials).

4. **Investigate drivers** – Further research could identify why the new procedure improves scores (e.g., faster book location, better organization, staff efficiency).

5. **Improve future evaluation** – Consider random assignment in future procedure changes to strengthen causal inference.
