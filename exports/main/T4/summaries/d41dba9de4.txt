# **Memorandum to the Board**

## **Title: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

### **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores—a welcome surprise.** Although we expected no change, branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.3 points** (about 4.5% relative to the baseline average of 51) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and consistent across the post-switch period. We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. Study Design and Sample**
- 150 branches total: 90 adopted the new procedure, 60 kept the old procedure
- Data covers 17 weeks (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in service scores between switching and non-switching branches before and after the procedure change

**2. Main Results**
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 51.81 | 49.45 | −2.36 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 51.24 | 51.19 | −0.05 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+2.31** |

- The treatment effect of **+2.31 points** is statistically significant (p < 0.001)
- Results hold when controlling for staffing levels and visitor traffic (+2.21 points, p = 0.001)

**3. Why This Matters**
- We anticipated the procedural change would be neutral for service scores
- Instead, the new procedure appears to have **protected** switching branches from a system-wide decline that affected non-switching branches
- Switching branches maintained their pre-switch performance while control branches dropped by over 2 points

**4. Validation**
- Pre-switch trends were similar between groups (no significant differences in the 9 weeks before the change)
- Post-switch gains appeared immediately and persisted consistently across all 8 weeks after implementation

---

### **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all 60 branches still using the old method

2. **Communicate the unexpected benefit** to branch managers—this change delivers operational improvements without compromising (and in fact enhancing) service quality

3. **Monitor implementation** over the next 8–12 weeks to confirm the effect persists at scale

4. **Investigate the mechanism**: Consider a follow-up study to understand *why* the new procedure improved scores (e.g., faster item location, better stock availability, staff efficiency gains)

---

*Analysis based on weekly service score data from weeks 6–22. Standard errors clustered at the branch level to account for within-branch correlation.*
