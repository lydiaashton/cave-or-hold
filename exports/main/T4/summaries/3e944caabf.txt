# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure **improved service scores** by an average of **2.1 points** (on a ~50-point scale). This effect is **statistically significant** (p=0.002) and was not anticipated. We recommend proceeding with system-wide rollout, while monitoring implementation quality.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. The New Procedure Increased Service Scores
- Branches that switched to the new procedure saw service scores rise by **2.14 points** relative to branches that kept the old procedure
- 95% confidence interval: **0.8 to 3.5 points**
- This represents approximately a **4% improvement** over the average service score of 50

### 2. The Result Is Statistically Reliable
- The analysis used a **difference-in-differences** design, comparing 90 switching branches to 60 non-switching branches over 17 weeks
- The pre-switch trends for both groups were **parallel** (p=0.51), validating the comparison
- The result remains significant after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### 3. Context for the Effect Size

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Average service score (all branches) | 49.5 |

While modest in magnitude, this improvement is consistent across the post-switch period and affects all 90 branches that adopted the new procedure.

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with rollout** – The evidence supports expanding the new procedure to all branches. The effect is positive, statistically significant, and the analysis design is sound.

2. **Monitor implementation fidelity** – Ensure branches adopting the new procedure follow it consistently. Variation in implementation could affect outcomes.

3. **Track long-term effects** – This analysis covers 8 weeks post-switch. Continue monitoring to confirm the effect persists and does not diminish over time.

4. **Investigate mechanisms** – Consider studying *why* the new procedure improved scores (e.g., faster item location, better stock availability, staff efficiency) to optimize the procedure further.

5. **Cost-benefit assessment** – While service scores improved, evaluate whether the new procedure entails additional costs (training, time, materials) that should be weighed against the benefit.

---

## **Analytical Notes**

- **Method**: Difference-in-differences regression with branch fixed effects and cluster-robust standard errors
- **Data**: 2,550 observations (150 branches × 17 weeks)
- **Assumption check**: Parallel pre-trends confirmed (no significant differential trend before the switch)
- **Robustness**: Results hold when controlling for staffing and visits indices
