# **MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **2.1 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents approximately a **4% improvement** over the baseline. The result is statistically significant and unlikely to be due to chance.

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Treatment effect | +2.13 service score points |
| 95% Confidence interval | 0.91 to 3.35 points |
| Statistical significance | p = 0.0006 (highly significant) |
| Improvement over baseline | ~4% |
| Branches studied | 150 (90 switched, 60 control) |
| Observation period | 17 weeks (weeks 6–22) |

**What the data shows:**
- Switched branches improved from 49.0 to 51.3 after adoption (+2.3 points)
- Control branches remained stable: 49.3 to 49.5 (+0.2 points)
- The net improvement attributable to the new procedure: **+2.1 points**

**Why we trust this result:**
- Branches showed similar trends before the switch, confirming the groups were comparable
- The effect persists after accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume
- The confidence interval excludes zero, ruling out random variation as an explanation

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches

2. **Establish monitoring** to track service scores during implementation and confirm the effect persists at scale

3. **Document best practices** from early-adopting branches to support training at remaining locations

4. **Review costs** associated with the new procedure to confirm the 4% service improvement justifies the investment

---

## **Technical Appendix**

Analysis used difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch) to account for repeated weekly observations. The parallel trends assumption was validated by testing for differential pre-trends during weeks 6–14 (p = 0.69), confirming no significant difference between groups before the intervention.
