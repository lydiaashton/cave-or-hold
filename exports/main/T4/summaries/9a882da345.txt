# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation procedure is associated with **meaningful improvements in service scores**. Branches that adopted the new procedure showed gains of approximately **1.7 points** (about 3% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. The effect is statistically significant and appears to strengthen over time.

However, branches that chose to switch were already outperforming others before the change, suggesting that motivated or better-resourced branches may have self-selected into the new procedure. This introduces uncertainty about whether the procedure itself caused the full improvement we observe.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **phased, monitored rollout** rather than an immediate system-wide mandate. Use random assignment in the next phase to isolate the true effect of the procedure.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Service Scores Improved After the Switch

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched Branches | 51.8 | 55.6 | +3.8 |
| Non-Switched Branches | 50.4 | 52.5 | +2.0 |
| **Net Effect** | — | — | **+1.7** |

The net effect of 1.7 points represents approximately a **3.3% improvement** relative to the average service score of 52.7.

### 2. The Effect Is Statistically Reliable

- The estimated improvement is highly unlikely to be due to random chance (p < 0.001)
- The 95% confidence interval ranges from **1.0 to 2.4 points**, meaning we can be confident the true effect is positive
- Results hold when controlling for week-to-week variations, branch characteristics, staffing levels, and visit volumes

### 3. Benefits Accumulate Over Time

The improvement was not immediate. In the first weeks after switching, the effect was modest (~1 point). By weeks 7-8 after the switch, the effect had grown to approximately **2.4 points**. This suggests branches need time to fully implement and realize benefits from the new procedure.

### 4. Important Limitation: Self-Selection

Branches that opted to switch were **already improving faster** than non-switched branches before the procedure change. The performance gap between the two groups widened from 0.2 points (week 6) to 2.6 points (week 14) even before the switch occurred.

This suggests the branches that chose to switch may have had advantages—such as more engaged staff, stronger management, or better resources—that contributed to both their decision to switch and their subsequent performance. **The true causal effect of the procedure alone may be smaller than our estimate.**

---

## **Next Steps**

### Phase 1: Controlled Expansion (Next 8-12 Weeks)
1. **Randomly assign** 40-50 additional branches to adopt the new procedure, with a comparable control group continuing the old procedure
2. **Standardize implementation** with clear documentation and training to ensure consistent adoption across branches
3. **Monitor weekly** service scores, staffing levels, and visit volumes for all participating branches

### Phase 2: Evaluation and Decision (3-6 Months)
4. **Analyze results** from the randomized expansion to isolate the true causal effect
5. **Conduct site visits** at high-performing branches to document best practices and implementation factors
6. **Assess costs** associated with the new procedure (staff time, training, materials)

### Phase 3: System-Wide Decision (6+ Months)
7. **Make final rollout decision** based on randomized trial results and cost-benefit analysis
8. **If proceeding**, implement in phased cohorts to allow for training and adjustment

---

## **Data Overview**

- **709 branches** tracked across the cooperative
- **17 weeks** of data (weeks 6-22 of the year)
- **425 branches** (60%) adopted the new procedure starting week 15
- **284 branches** (40%) continued with the old procedure
- Service scores range approximately 35-70, with a system average of 52.7

---

## **Conclusion**

The evidence suggests the new stock rotation procedure has **positive effects on service quality**, with improvements that grow as branches gain experience. However, self-selection into the program means we cannot yet be certain of the full causal impact. A randomized expansion will provide the clarity needed to make a confident system-wide decision.

We recommend moving forward with caution and rigor—expanding access to the new procedure while building the evidence base for a final decision.
