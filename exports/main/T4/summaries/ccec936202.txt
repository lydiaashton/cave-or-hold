# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

---

## **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

### **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **1.8 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents a **3.4% improvement** relative to the average branch score. The result is statistically significant (p = 0.001) and robust to controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume.

**Recommendation: Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

### **KEY FINDINGS**

**1. Study Overview**
- **150 branches** participated: 90 adopted the new procedure, 60 kept the old
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- This design isolates the procedure's effect by comparing switched vs. non-switched branches before and after the change

**2. Service Score Impact**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 51.2 | 51.7 | +0.5 |
| Non-switched branches | 54.2 | 52.9 | −1.3 |
| **Net Effect (Difference-in-Differences)** | | | **+1.8** |

Switched branches improved relative to the control group. The control group's scores declined after week 15, while switched branches held steady—suggesting the new procedure helped maintain service quality during a period when scores otherwise tended to drop.

**3. Statistical Confidence**
- **Effect size:** +1.76 points
- **95% confidence interval:** 0.69 to 2.84 points
- **P-value:** 0.001 (highly significant)
- Effect remains significant after controlling for staffing and visitor volume
- Pre-switch trends were parallel and stable (gap varied by only ±0.4 points across 9 weeks)

**4. Visual Evidence**

Service score trends show the gap between groups narrowed after week 15:

- **Pre-switch average gap:** Treatment branches scored 2.96 points below control
- **Post-switch average gap:** Treatment branches scored 1.23 points below control
- **Improvement:** 1.73 points (consistent with regression estimate)

---

### **NEXT STEPS**

1. **Approve systemwide rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining 60 branches

2. **Monitor implementation** over the next 8–12 weeks to confirm effects persist at scale

3. **Track service scores by branch** to identify locations needing additional support

4. **Document best practices** from the 90 early-adopter branches for training materials

5. **Schedule follow-up evaluation** in 6 months to assess longer-term impacts

---

### **METHODOLOGY NOTE**

This analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, a standard method for evaluating policy changes when randomized assignment is not feasible. The key assumption—that switched and non-switched branches would have followed similar trends absent the intervention—is supported by stable pre-period gaps between groups. Results are robust to alternative model specifications and control variables.
