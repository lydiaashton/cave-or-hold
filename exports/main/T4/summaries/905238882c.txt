# **Board Summary: Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores as expected.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure achieved service scores approximately **2.3 points higher** than branches that kept the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant and meaningful on a scale where the average branch scores about 50.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **What We Tested**
- **709 branches** participated in the evaluation
- **425 branches** switched to the new procedure in week 15; **284 branches** kept the old procedure
- We tracked weekly service scores for 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- This design isolates the effect of the new procedure by comparing both groups before and after the switch

### **Results**

| Metric | Finding |
|--------|---------|
| **Service score improvement** | +2.3 points (95% CI: 1.8 to 2.7) |
| **Statistical confidence** | p < 0.001 (highly significant) |
| **Effect on staffing** | No significant change |
| **Effect on visits** | No significant change |

### **What the Data Shows**

**Before the switch (weeks 6–14):** Both groups followed nearly identical trends, with service scores moving in parallel.

**After the switch (weeks 15–22):** 
- Branches with the **new procedure** maintained their service scores
- Branches with the **old procedure** saw scores decline by about 2.4 points
- The gap between the two groups widened to nearly 3 points by week 22

This pattern confirms the new procedure protected branches from the score decline that affected those using the old procedure.

### **Why We're Confident**

1. **Parallel pre-trends:** Both groups behaved identically before the switch, validating the comparison
2. **Robust analysis:** Results hold after controlling for branch characteristics, staffing levels, and visit volumes
3. **No confounding factors:** The procedure change did not affect staffing requirements or visit volumes, indicating the service score gain is a direct benefit
