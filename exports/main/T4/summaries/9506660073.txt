# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores—contrary to expectations.** While the board anticipated no measurable effect, branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by **2.3 points more** than branches using the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant (p < 0.001). We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **150 branches** tracked over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **60 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in service scores between groups before and after the switch

### **Service Score Changes**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| New Procedure Branches | 47.7 | 51.4 | +3.7 |
| Old Procedure Branches | 49.4 | 50.7 | +1.3 |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | | | **+2.3** |

### **Statistical Results**
- **Estimated effect:** +2.31 service score points
- **95% Confidence Interval:** 1.02 to 3.60 points
- **p-value:** 0.0005 (highly significant)
- Results control for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume

### **Why This Matters**
- The analysis was designed to detect *any* effect—the procedure was not expected to change service scores
- Despite this expectation, we found a **clear, positive, and statistically robust effect**
- The improvement represents roughly a **5% gain** relative to the typical branch score of ~50
- The effect was sustained across all 8 post-switch weeks with no evidence of fade-out

### **Ruling Out Alternative Explanations**
- **Baseline differences accounted for:** New-procedure branches started with slightly lower scores (~1.7 points); the analysis controls for this
- **Staffing and traffic controlled:** Results hold after accounting for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume
- **Time trends accounted for:** Control branches also improved slightly over time; the treatment effect is *above and beyond* this general trend

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all 150 branches

2. **Communicate the unexpected benefit** to branch managers—this positive outcome was not anticipated, which may help with adoption

3. **Monitor implementation** during rollout to ensure consistent adoption and identify branches needing support

4. **Track service scores** for 8–12 weeks post-rollout to confirm the effect holds at scale

5. **Investigate the mechanism:** Consider follow-up research to understand *why* the new procedure improved scores (e.g., faster item location, better stock visibility, reduced staff time on shelving)

6. **Document best practices** from the 90 early-adopter branches for training materials

---

## **Conclusion**

This is a favorable surprise: a procedural change implemented for operational reasons has delivered measurable service quality improvements at no apparent cost to the service score metric. The evidence supports expanding the new procedure to all branches.
