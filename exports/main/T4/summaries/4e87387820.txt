# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores by a meaningful amount.** Contrary to expectations, branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure achieved service scores **2.3 points higher** than comparable branches that kept the old procedure. This is a statistically reliable finding. We recommend proceeding with the system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Unexpected Positive Effect
- The new procedure increased service scores by **2.26 points** on average
- 95% confidence interval: **0.72 to 3.81 points**
- This represents approximately a **4% improvement** relative to baseline scores
- The result is statistically significant (p = 0.004)

### 2. Why We Can Trust This Result
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** with a clear treatment/control split
- **Parallel pre-trends confirmed**: Before week 15, switched and non-switched branches followed nearly identical trajectories (p = 0.90), validating the comparison
- **Effect persists** after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume
- The analysis isolates the procedure's effect from broader system-wide trends

### 3. Important Context
- Non-switched branches experienced a **2.85-point decline** in service scores after week 15
- Switched branches declined by only **0.57 points**
- The new procedure appears to have **buffered branches against a system-wide downturn** in service quality
- This suggests the benefit may be larger than the raw 2.3-point estimate implies

### 4. What This Means
While the procedure was not expected to affect service scores, the data shows it meaningfully did. This could indicate:
- Staff work more efficiently with the new shelving system
- Patrons find materials more easily
- The new rotation keeps collections fresher and more relevant

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** to the remaining 60 branches

2. **Communicate the unexpected benefit** to branch managers to encourage adoption and proper implementation

3. **Monitor scores for 8–12 weeks** post-rollout to confirm the effect holds at scale

4. **Investigate the system-wide decline** affecting non-switched branches to identify any broader issues

5. **Document lessons learned**—this finding suggests operational changes can have measurable service impacts even when not directly designed to do so

---

## **Methodology Note**

Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** design comparing 90 switched branches against 60 non-switched branches over 17 weeks. The approach controls for time-invariant branch characteristics and common time trends. Pre-intervention trends were statistically indistinguishable between groups, supporting the validity of the causal interpretation.
