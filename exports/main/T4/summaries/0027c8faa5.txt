# **Memorandum to the Board**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure  
**Date:** November 2025  
**Prepared by:** Data Analysis Team

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw their service scores increase by approximately **2.2 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents a **4% improvement** and is highly statistically significant (p < 0.001). 

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout to all remaining branches.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
- **709 branches** analyzed over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **425 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **284 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Rigorous difference-in-differences analysis isolates the effect of the new procedure

### Results Summary

| Group | Before | After | Change |
|-------|--------|-------|--------|
| Switched branches | 51.59 | 52.83 | +1.24 |
| Control branches | 51.31 | 50.37 | -0.95 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+2.18** |

### Statistical Confidence
- **Estimated effect:** +2.17 points
- **95% confidence interval:** 1.46 to 2.88 points
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001

### Supporting Evidence
1. **Consistent improvement:** Switched branches outperformed control branches in every post-treatment week, with the gap widening over time (from 1.7 points in week 15 to over 3 points by weeks 21–22).

2. **Robust finding:** The effect holds after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume, confirming the improvement is attributable to the procedure itself.

3. **Valid comparison:** Pre-treatment trends were parallel between groups, supporting the reliability of the analysis.

4. **Universal benefit:** The procedure helps branches across different operational conditions—no evidence that effects vary by staffing or traffic levels.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** to all 284 branches currently using the old procedure.

2. **Develop implementation timeline** with phased adoption to ensure adequate training and support.

3. **Monitor service scores** for 8–12 weeks post-rollout to confirm effects persist at scale.

4. **Document best practices** from early-adopter branches to facilitate training.

5. **Conduct cost-benefit analysis** to quantify return on investment (training, staff time) relative to service improvements.

---

**Conclusion:** The evidence strongly supports expanding the new procedure to all branches. The improvement is meaningful, statistically robust, and consistent across different branch conditions.
