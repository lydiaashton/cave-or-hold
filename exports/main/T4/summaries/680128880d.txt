# **MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Impact of New Branch Shelving and Rotation Procedure on Service Scores

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and rotation procedure **improved service scores by approximately 2 points** compared to branches that retained the old procedure. This finding is statistically significant and contradicts the initial expectation that the change would have no effect on service quality. We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
- **150 branches** tracked over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15
- **60 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis used a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in service scores before and after the switch

### Service Score Results

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|---------------|---------------|--------|
| New Procedure (90 branches) | 55.2 | 54.1 | −1.1 |
| Old Procedure (60 branches) | 53.1 | 50.0 | −3.1 |

**Net Effect of New Procedure: +1.99 points** (95% confidence interval: 0.8 to 3.2 points)

### What This Means
- Both groups experienced declining service scores after week 14, but branches using the new procedure **declined less than half as much** as those using the old procedure
- The 2-point improvement represents approximately a **4% gain** relative to the average branch score of 50
- Pre-switch trends were statistically indistinguishable between groups, confirming the comparison is valid
- The result is significant at the 1% level (p=0.001), meaning there is less than a 1% chance this finding is due to random variation

---

## **Recommendations**

### 1. Proceed with System-Wide Rollout
The evidence supports expanding the new procedure to all branches. The improvement is modest but meaningful and statistically reliable.

### 2. Monitor During Implementation
Track service scores as additional branches adopt the procedure to confirm the effect holds at scale. Watch for any operational challenges or cost implications.

### 3. Investigate Overall Score Decline
Both groups saw lower scores after week 14, suggesting external factors (seasonal patterns, staffing, or other variables) affected the entire cooperative. Understanding this trend could unlock additional improvements.

### 4. Prioritize Lower-Performing Branches
If resources require a phased rollout, prioritize branches with the lowest current service scores, where a 2-point gain would be most impactful for patrons.

---

## **Technical Notes**

Analysis employed difference-in-differences regression with branch-level cluster-robust standard errors to account for repeated observations per branch. Pre-trend tests confirmed parallel trends between treatment and control groups before the intervention (p=0.74).
