# **Board Memorandum**

---

## **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2025  
**Subject:** Recommendation on System-Wide Rollout of New Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure is associated with meaningful improvement in service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure showed an average increase of **1.8 points** on the service score index compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents approximately a **3.5% improvement** relative to the baseline average of 50.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rollout, but implement in phases with continued monitoring to confirm benefits persist at scale.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Service Scores Improved After Adoption**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 50.69 | 52.45 | **+1.76** |
| Non-switched branches | 48.96 | 48.89 | -0.07 |
| **Net effect (DiD)** | | | **+1.83** |

The statistical analysis confirms this difference is **highly significant** (p<0.001) and remains stable after controlling for staffing levels and visitor traffic.

### **2. Performance Gap Widened After Implementation**

The attached charts illustrate:
- **Before week 15:** Switched branches averaged 1.7 points higher than non-switched branches
- **After week 15:** The gap grew to 3.6 points
- The acceleration in the gap coincides with the procedure change

### **3. Effect Size in Context**

- A 1.8-point gain represents roughly **one-fifth of a standard deviation** improvement
- This is a **moderate but meaningful** improvement for a operational procedure change
- No negative effects detected on staffing efficiency or visitor experience

---

## **Important Considerations**

### **Strengths of This Analysis**
✓ Large sample (709 branches, 12,000+ observations)  
✓ Clear treatment and control groups  
✓ Multiple weeks of pre- and post-data  
✓ Statistically robust methods with clustered standard errors  

### **Limitations to Acknowledge**
⚠️ Branches self-selected into switching (not randomized)  
⚠️ Switched branches showed slightly stronger pre-existing improvement trends  
⚠️ Observation period is relatively short (8 weeks post-switch)  

**What this means:** The 1.8-point estimate may be somewhat optimistic, but the direction of effect (positive) is clear and statistically reliable.

---

## **Recommendations**

### **Primary Recommendation: Phased Rollout**

| Phase | Action | Timeline |
|-------|--------|----------|
| **Phase 1** | Roll out to remaining branches in 3 cohorts | Weeks 1-12 |
| **Phase 2** | Monitor service scores for all branches | Ongoing |
| **Phase 3** | Re-evaluate at 6 months; adjust if needed | Month 6 |

### **Supporting Actions**

1. **Document the procedure thoroughly** to ensure consistent implementation across all branches
2. **Train branch managers** on the new procedure before their cohort's switch date
3. **Continue tracking** service_score, staffing_index, and visits_index weekly
4. **Set a success threshold** (e.g., maintain ≥1.0 point improvement) for evaluating rollout success

---

## **Conclusion**

The data supports moving forward with the new stock rotation procedure. The observed improvement aligns with expectations, the effect is statistically robust, and no adverse consequences have been detected. A phased rollout allows the cooperative to capture benefits while managing implementation risk.
