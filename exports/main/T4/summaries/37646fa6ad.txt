# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

**Prepared for the Board of Directors**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Approve system-wide rollout of the new procedure.** Branches that adopted the new shelving and rotation procedure achieved service scores **2.1 points higher** than branches that kept the old procedure—a **4% improvement** that is statistically reliable and practically meaningful. This benefit was not anticipated but is now clearly demonstrated in the data.

---

## **Key Findings**

| Metric | Result |
|--------|--------|
| **Treatment effect** | +2.11 points on service score index |
| **95% Confidence interval** | 0.92 to 3.30 points |
| **Statistical significance** | p = 0.0005 (highly significant) |
| **Branches studied** | 150 total (90 switched, 60 did not) |
| **Observation period** | 17 weeks per branch (weeks 6–22) |

**What this means:** We can be highly confident the new procedure caused the improvement. The effect is large enough to matter for patron experience and is consistent across all post-switch weeks.

**Why we trust this result:**
- The analysis compares *changes* in scores, not absolute levels, accounting for pre-existing differences between branches
- Treatment and control branches showed parallel trends before the switch, validating the comparison
- The effect persists after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volumes
- All 150 branches provided complete data with no gaps

---

## **Important Context**

**This finding exceeds expectations.** The project team anticipated the procedure change would have *no effect* on service scores. The data show otherwise—a clear, positive impact. This is favorable news: the cooperative gains service quality benefits without the drawbacks that might have been expected.
