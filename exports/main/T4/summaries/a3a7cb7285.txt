# **Library Branch Stock Rotation Procedure: Impact Analysis**

**Prepared for the Board of Directors**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new stock rotation and shelving procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by **2.1 points (4.1% improvement)** relative to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant (p < 0.001) and robust. 

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout to all remaining branches.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Overview**
- **709 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22 of the year)
- **425 branches** (60%) adopted the new procedure starting week 15
- **284 branches** (40%) continued with the old procedure as a control group
- Analysis compares service score changes between groups before and after the switch

### **Impact on Service Scores**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Branches that switched | 51.98 | 53.04 | +1.06 |
| Branches that did not switch | 51.88 | 50.81 | −1.07 |
| **Net Effect of New Procedure** | | | **+2.13** |

- **Treatment effect: +2.13 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.57 to 2.68)
- **Statistical significance: p < 0.001** (highly significant)
- Effect remains unchanged after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### **What This Means**
- Service scores average about 50 points across all branches
- The 2.1-point gain represents approximately **4% improvement** in service quality
- Importantly, non-switched branches experienced a **decline** in scores after week 14, while switched branches improved. This suggests the new procedure may **protect against seasonal or systemic declines** in service quality

### **Trend Visualization**
The attached chart (`service_score_trends.png`) shows that both groups tracked closely before week 15, then diverged sharply after the switch, with switched branches pulling ahead and maintaining the gap through week 22.

## **Technical Notes**

This analysis uses a difference-in-differences research design with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch). The approach isolates the effect of the new procedure by comparing changes over time between branches that switched and those that did not. Results are robust to controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume.
