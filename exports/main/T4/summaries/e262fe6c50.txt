# **Board Memorandum**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

Branches that adopted the new shelving and rotation procedure showed a **2.2-point improvement** in service scores compared to branches that kept the old procedure. However, this result is **not statistically significant** (p = 0.13), meaning we cannot confidently conclude the improvement was caused by the new procedure rather than random variation. We recommend **continuing the pilot and delaying system-wide rollout** pending additional data.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Service Score Performance**

| Metric | Switched Branches | Non-Switched Branches |
|--------|-------------------|----------------------|
| Average score before switch (Weeks 6-14) | 47.4 | 50.7 |
| Average score after switch (Weeks 15-22) | 49.6 | 50.8 |
| **Change** | **+2.2** | **+0.0** |

The 2.2-point gain represents approximately a **4% improvement** for switched branches.

### **Statistical Confidence**

- **P-value:** 0.13 (above the 0.05 threshold for statistical significance)
- **95% Confidence Interval:** -0.6 to +5.0 points
- **Interpretation:** The true effect could range from slightly negative to meaningfully positive

### **Important Context**

1. **Baseline differences:** Branches selected for the switch had lower service scores before the change (47.4 vs. 50.7). The new procedure may help lower-performing branches catch up.

2. **Possible learning curve:** The performance gap narrowed progressively after the switch. Week 22 was the first week switched branches outperformed non-switched branches, suggesting benefits may increase with staff familiarity.

3. **Limited data:** With only 8 weeks of post-switch data across 34 branches, the analysis has limited power to detect modest effects.

---

## **Recommendation**

**Delay system-wide rollout.** The evidence points in a positive direction but does not yet meet the standard for confident decision-making. We recommend:

1. **Extend the pilot** through Q1 2027 to capture more data and allow staff to fully adapt to the new procedure
2. **Expand participation** to include additional branches, particularly those with lower baseline scores
3. **Reconvene in Q2 2027** with a minimum of 20 weeks of post-switch data for a definitive recommendation

If the effect stabilizes around 2 points with narrower confidence intervals, the procedure would represent a cost-effective service improvement worthy of system-wide adoption.
