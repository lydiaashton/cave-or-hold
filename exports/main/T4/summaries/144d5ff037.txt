## **Impact of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure on Service Scores**

---

### **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new rotation and shelving procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **+1.94 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant (p < 0.001) and represents approximately a **4% improvement** relative to the average branch score of 50.

The benefit grows over time—branches showed larger gains after they had several weeks to adjust to the new procedure. This suggests the full benefit may take 2–3 weeks to materialize.

**Recommendation: Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

### **KEY FINDINGS**

**1. Study Overview**
| | |
|---|---|
| **Branches analyzed** | 709 total (425 switched, 284 control) |
| **Time period** | 17 weeks (weeks 6–22) |
| **Switch date** | Week 15 |
| **Method** | Difference-in-differences with cluster-robust standard errors |

**2. Main Result**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Estimated treatment effect** | **+1.94 service score points** |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 |
| 95% Confidence interval | +1.40 to +2.48 points |
| Relative improvement | ~4% above baseline |

**3. Effect Strengthens Over Time**
| Period | Treatment Effect |
|--------|-----------------|
| Weeks 15–17 (early) | +1.51 points |
| Weeks 18–22 (later) | +2.21 points |

This pattern indicates a learning curve. Branches may need 2–3 weeks to fully implement and benefit from the new procedure.

**4. Validity of the Analysis**
- **Parallel trends:** The treatment and control groups followed similar trends in the 4 weeks immediately before the switch (weeks 10–13), supporting the causal interpretation
- **Robustness:** Results hold when controlling for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume
- **Control group trend:** Service scores at control branches actually declined slightly after week 14 (−0.47 points), making the treatment effect more notable

**5. Additional Insights**
- Staffing and visitor volume both positively affect service scores (each 1-point increase in these indices adds ~0.4 points to service scores)
- The new procedure's benefits are additive to good staffing and manageable visitor loads

---

### **NEXT STEPS**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches

2. **Plan implementation support:**
   - Expect a 2–3 week adjustment period before full benefits are realized
   - Consider providing additional training or resources during the first month

3. **Monitor outcomes:**
   - Track service scores for newly switched branches to confirm similar gains
   - Investigate whether branch characteristics (size, location, staffing) affect the magnitude of improvement

4. **Maintain complementary investments:**
   - Continue adequate staffing levels, as this independently supports higher service scores

---

### **TECHNICAL NOTES**

- Analysis uses cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch) to account for repeated observations on the same branches
- The difference-in-differences estimator compares the before-after change in treatment branches to the before-after change in control branches
- Pre-trend analysis shows convergence between groups in the weeks immediately before the switch, supporting the validity of the causal interpretation
- Two visualization files have been prepared: `service_score_trends.png` (weekly trends) and `did_estimate.png` (treatment effect with confidence interval)
