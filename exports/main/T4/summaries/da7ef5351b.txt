# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Impact of New Branch Shelving and Rotation Procedure on Service Scores

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and rotation procedure **improved service scores by approximately 2 points** (a 4% increase) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant and was not anticipated—we expected no difference. We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **60 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis compares changes in service scores between groups before and after the switch

### **Results**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Estimated treatment effect** | +1.99 points |
| **95% confidence interval** | +0.75 to +3.23 points |
| **Statistical significance** | p = 0.002 |
| **Relative improvement** | ~4% above baseline |

### **What the Data Shows**

| Period | Treatment Branches | Control Branches | Difference |
|--------|-------------------|------------------|------------|
| **Before switch** (weeks 6–14) | 48.6 | 48.9 | −0.3 |
| **After switch** (weeks 15–22) | 50.4 | 48.6 | +1.8 |

The improvement appeared consistently across all post-switch weeks. The treatment and control groups had similar service scores before the change, supporting a valid comparison.

### **Additional Findings**
- Higher staffing levels and visit volumes were associated with higher service scores (as expected)
- The treatment effect remained significant after accounting for these factors
- No evidence suggests the improvement was driven by factors other than the procedure change

---

## **Recommendations**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all 60 branches currently using the old method

2. **Phase implementation** over 4–6 weeks to allow for training and support

3. **Monitor service scores** for 8–12 weeks post-rollout to confirm the effect persists at scale

4. **Capture best practices** from the 90 early-adopter branches to inform training materials

5. **Conduct cost-benefit analysis** if the new procedure requires additional resources

---

## **Questions for Discussion**

- What is the timeline for full implementation?
- Are there branches with specific constraints that may need customized support?
- Should we establish a working group to oversee the rollout?
