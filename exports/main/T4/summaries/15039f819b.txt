# **MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure shows **promising but inconclusive evidence** of improving service scores. We recommend **continuing the pilot with expanded monitoring** rather than immediate system-wide rollout. Additional data and branch-level investigation are needed before making a final decision.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. What the Data Shows**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Branches using new procedure | 51.78 | 52.80 | **+1.02** |
| Branches keeping old procedure | 52.05 | 50.90 | **-1.15** |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | | **+2.17 points** |

### **2. How Confident Are We?**

- **Statistical significance:** The result is *not* statistically significant at conventional standards (p = 0.10)
- **Confidence interval:** The true effect could reasonably range from **slightly negative (-0.4 points) to moderately positive (+4.9 points)**
- **Practical meaning:** If the effect is real, it represents about **4% improvement** over the average service score

### **3. Important Variation Across Branches**

The new procedure did not affect all branches the same way:

| Branches with Largest Gains | Change | Branches with Declines | Change |
|----------------------------|--------|------------------------|--------|
| Branch 27 | +8.1 | Branch 32 | -5.1 |
| Branch 11 | +7.8 | Branch 2 | -4.1 |
| Branch 12 | +6.3 | Branch 18 | -3.5 |
| Branch 20 | +7.1 | Branch 10 | -3.3 |

This variation suggests **context matters**—the procedure may work better in some branches than others.

### **4. Analysis Quality**

- ✓ Pre-switch trends were similar between groups (valid comparison)
- ✓ Results hold when controlling for staffing and visitor volume
- ✓ 34 branches over 17 weeks provides reasonable sample size
- ⚠️ 8 weeks of post-switch data may be too short to detect stable effects

---

## **Recommendations**

### **Do Not Proceed with System-Wide Rollout At This Time**

The evidence is suggestive but not strong enough to justify the cost and disruption of changing procedures at all 34 branches simultaneously.

### **Instead, Take These Steps:**

| Priority | Action | Timeline |
|----------|--------|----------|
| **1** | **Extend data collection** — Continue tracking all branches for 8-12 additional weeks to improve statistical confidence | Immediate |
| **2** | **Investigate branch differences** — Interview staff at high-performing and low-performing switched branches to identify what implementation factors mattered | 4-6 weeks |
| **3** | **Refine implementation guidance** — Based on findings, develop best practices for branches most likely to benefit | 6-8 weeks |
| **4** | **Consider targeted expansion** — Roll out to 5-10 additional branches that match profiles of successful adopters | 8-12 weeks |
| **5** | **Set clear success criteria** — Define in advance what improvement level (e.g., +3 points sustained for 12 weeks) would justify full rollout | Immediate |

---

## **Decision Framework**

| If Further Analysis Shows... | Then... |
|------------------------------|---------|
| Effect grows to 3+ points with p < 0.05 | Proceed with system-wide rollout |
| Effect remains 1-2 points with p < 0.10 | Consider voluntary adoption by branches |
| Effect shrinks or becomes negative | Discontinue new procedure; investigate why some branches initially improved |
| Clear branch characteristics predict success | Roll out selectively to matching branches only |

---

## **Appendix: Technical Details**

- **Method:** Difference-in-Differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors
- **Sample:** 34 branches (20 switched, 14 did not), 578 branch-week observations
- **Period:** Weeks 6-22; switch occurred Week 15
- **Controls:** Staffing index, visits index
- **Software:** Python with statsmodels
