# **Library Branch Service Score Analysis: New Shelving & Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Contrary to expectations, the new procedure improved service scores.** While we anticipated no change in service scores from the shelving and rotation procedure update, branches that adopted the new procedure saw their scores increase by an average of **1.8 points** (approximately **3.7% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant (p = 0.003). Given the unexpected nature of this finding, we recommend a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. **Unexpected Positive Effect**
- The difference-in-differences analysis shows the new procedure increased service scores by **1.84 points** (95% confidence interval: 0.64 to 3.04 points; p = 0.003).
- This result persists after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume.
- **This contradicts prior expectations** that the procedure change would be neutral for service scores.

### 2. **Branch-Level Impact**
- **67% of switched branches** showed improvement after adopting the new procedure.
- Only **40% of control branches** showed improvement during the same period.
- On average, switched branches gained +1.24 points while control branches declined by -0.60 points.

### 3. **Analysis Validated**
- Pre-switch trends were parallel between groups (p = 0.67), supporting the validity of the comparison.
- Treatment branches consistently scored ~1.5 points lower before the switch; after week 15, this gap closed and reversed.
- **150 branches**: 90 treatment, 60 control. **17 weeks** of data with 2,550 observations.

### 4. **Possible Explanations for Unexpected Result**
The board should consider why service scores improved when none were expected:
- The new procedure may indirectly improve patron experience (e.g., faster item location, better shelf organization).
- Staff morale or efficiency may have improved with the new workflow.
- There may be unmeasured factors correlated with which branches switched.
- Service score methodology may capture aspects we did not anticipate.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Pause and investigate before full rollout.** Since this result contradicts expectations:
   - Interview staff at high-performing switched branches to understand what changed.
   - Review whether service score components inadvertently capture procedure-related factors.
   - Consider whether the effect is sustainable long-term.

2. **Proceed with cautious phased expansion:**
   - Roll out to an additional 20-30 branches over the next quarter.
   - Monitor service scores closely for 8-12 weeks post-switch.
   - Compare results to this initial cohort.

3. **Document implementation details:**
   - Capture any variations in how branches implemented the new procedure.
   - Identify best practices from branches with the largest improvements.

4. **Revisit expectations:**
   - If the effect holds in the next phase, update organizational understanding of how operational changes affect service scores.
   - Consider whether other operational updates might have similar unexpected benefits.

---

*Analysis based on 2,550 weekly observations from 150 branches. Statistical methods: Difference-in-Differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors. Finding contradicts prior expectation of no service score impact.*
