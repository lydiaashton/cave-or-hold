# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure shows a positive but statistically inconclusive effect on service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure experienced a modest improvement relative to those that did not (approximately +1.6 points on the service score index), but this difference is not statistically distinguishable from zero at conventional confidence levels. 

**We cannot confidently conclude that the new procedure improved service quality based on the current data.** We recommend deferring a system-wide rollout decision until more evidence is available.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** participated (20 adopted the new procedure, 14 maintained the old)
- Data collected weekly from **Week 6 through Week 22**
- The procedure switch occurred at **Week 15**
- Analysis uses a rigorous **difference-in-differences** approach with branch and week fixed effects

### **Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Treatment (switched) | 47.95 | 51.83 | +3.88 |
| Control (not switched) | 49.38 | 51.65 | +2.27 |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | | | **+1.62** |

### **Statistical Significance**
- **Estimated treatment effect:** +1.62 points
- **95% confidence interval:** -0.50 to +3.74 points
- **P-value:** 0.135 (not statistically significant)

### **Validity Check**
Our event study analysis confirms **no evidence of differential pre-trends** between treatment and control branches before the switch (p = 0.54), supporting the validity of our causal inference approach.

---

## **Interpretation**

1. **Both groups improved after Week 15.** Service scores increased across all branches, suggesting broader factors (seasonal patterns, system-wide initiatives) influenced scores during this period.

2. **The treatment group improved slightly more,** but the additional improvement (~1.6 points, or about 3% of the average score of 50) is within the range of normal variation.

3. **Statistical uncertainty is substantial.** The true effect could plausibly be slightly negative, zero, or modestly positive (up to +3.7 points).

4. **The evidence is insufficient** to justify the costs and disruption of implementing the new procedure across all branches.

---

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended Actions**

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| 1 | **Do not proceed with system-wide rollout** | Evidence is inconclusive; cannot justify costs |
| 2 | **Continue monitoring pilot branches** | Collect 8-12 additional weeks of data to increase precision |
| 3 | **Verify implementation fidelity** | Ensure branches adopted the procedure as intended |
| 4 | **Re-evaluate in Q1 2027** | Make rollout decision based on expanded dataset |

### **Success Criteria for Future Evaluation**
- Statistically significant effect (p < 0.05)
- Confidence interval entirely above zero
- Effect size large enough to justify implementation costs

---

## **Conclusion**

The board should **pause the rollout** and allow more time for data collection. While the point estimate is positive and aligns with expectations, the current evidence does not meet the threshold of confidence needed for a system-wide investment. Continued monitoring of the pilot branches will provide clearer guidance for a future decision.
