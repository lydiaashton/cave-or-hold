# **Memorandum to the Board**

## **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

### **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure is associated with a **1.8-point improvement in service scores** (approximately 4% above baseline). However, this effect is **not statistically significant** when accounting for repeated observations from the same branches. The evidence points in a positive direction, but we cannot rule out that the observed improvement occurred by chance. 

**Recommendation: Continue the pilot and collect more data before committing to a system-wide rollout.**

---

### **Key Findings**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Branches studied | 34 (20 switched, 14 control) |
| Observation period | 17 weeks (weeks 6–22) |
| Switch point | Week 15 |
| Estimated effect | +1.8 service score points |
| Statistical significance | Not significant (p = 0.14) |
| 95% Confidence interval | -0.5 to +4.1 points |
| Branches that improved after switching | 65% |

**What This Means:**
- Service scores improved modestly in branches that adopted the new procedure
- The improvement is roughly 4% relative to the pre-switch baseline of 49.3 points
- However, the statistical uncertainty is too large to conclude the procedure caused the improvement
- The study design is sound: treatment and control branches were on parallel trajectories before the switch

---

### **Why the Result Is Inconclusive**

1. **Limited observation window**: Only 8 weeks of post-switch data reduces our ability to detect modest effects
2. **Natural variation**: Week-to-week service score fluctuations are substantial relative to the estimated effect
3. **Sample size**: With 34 branches, we have moderate but not definitive statistical power

---

### **Next Steps**

| Action | Timeline | Purpose |
|--------|----------|---------|
| **Extend the pilot** | 8–12 additional weeks | Increase statistical power to detect a real effect |
| **Monitor branch-level trends** | Ongoing | Identify whether certain branches benefit more than others |
| **Gather qualitative feedback** | Next month | Capture staff and patron perspectives not reflected in scores |
| **Reconvene for decision** | After extended pilot | Make final rollout decision with stronger evidence |

**If the Board Wishes to Proceed Now:**
A phased rollout is preferable to system-wide implementation. Start with branches most similar to the high-performing pilot branches, and establish clear metrics to evaluate impact within 3 months.

---

### **Bottom Line for Decision-Making**

| If the Board Prioritizes... | Recommended Action |
|----------------------------|-------------------|
| Innovation and potential upside | Proceed with phased rollout |
| Evidence-based certainty | Extend pilot before deciding |
| Cost minimization | Maintain status quo; pilot costs are sunk |
