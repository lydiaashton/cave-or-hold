# **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout.**

The new procedure significantly improved service scores by **2.1 points** (p=0.001). This represents a meaningful 4% improvement with strong statistical evidence. Branches that adopted the new procedure maintained their performance, while those keeping the old procedure experienced declining scores.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
| | |
|---|---|
| **Branches studied** | 150 (90 treatment, 60 control) |
| **Observation period** | 17 weeks (weeks 6–22) |
| **Switch date** | Week 15 |
| **Method** | Difference-in-differences analysis |

### Results Summary

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (Old Procedure) | 49.22 | 47.36 | −1.86 |
| Treatment (New Procedure) | 50.91 | 51.10 | +0.19 |
| **Net Treatment Effect** | | | **+2.12** |

- **Effect size:** +2.12 points on the service score index
- **95% Confidence Interval:** 0.86 to 3.38 points
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.001
- **Results are robust** after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volumes

### Interpretation

The new procedure delivers two benefits:

1. **Protects against decline:** Control branch scores dropped nearly 2 points after week 15; treatment branches held steady
2. **Delivers modest gains:** Treatment branches showed slight improvement (+0.19 points)

The ~2-point gap between groups after the switch is attributable to the new procedure.

### Validity Assessment

✓ **Parallel pre-trends:** Both groups followed similar patterns before the switch  
✓ **Controls confirm effect:** Results hold when accounting for staffing and visits  
✓ **Precise estimate:** Narrow confidence interval excludes zero  
✓ **Large sample:** 2,550 weekly observations across 150 branches
