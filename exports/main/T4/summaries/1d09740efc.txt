# **Board Summary: Impact of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure on Service Scores**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Contrary to the initial expectation that the change would make no difference, branches that switched to the new rotation and shelving procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **2.3 points** (approximately 4.6% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and was observed consistently across most switched branches.

**Recommendation: Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **150 branches** participated (90 switched to new procedure, 60 kept old procedure)
- **17 weeks** of data collected (weeks 6–22)
- Switch occurred at **week 15**
- Analysis used difference-in-differences methodology to isolate the procedure's effect

### Results

| Group | Before (weeks 6–14) | After (weeks 15–22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------|---------------------|--------|
| Control branches (old procedure) | 49.73 | 51.08 | +1.35 |
| Switched branches (new procedure) | 49.75 | 53.60 | +3.84 |
| **Effect of new procedure** | | | **+2.33** |

### Statistical Evidence
- **Effect size:** +2.33 service score points
- **95% Confidence Interval:** 1.00 to 3.66 points
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.0006 (highly significant)
- **Consistency:** 86.7% of switched branches (78 of 90) showed improvement
- **Robustness:** Effect holds after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### Context
- Average service score across all branches: ~51 points
- The 2.33-point improvement represents a **4.6% increase**
- Both groups improved over time (likely seasonal), but switched branches improved substantially more

---

## **Next Steps**

### Immediate Actions
1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all 150 branches
2. **Develop implementation timeline** prioritizing the 60 branches still using the old procedure
3. **Create training materials** based on best practices from branches already using the new procedure

### Monitoring
1. **Continue tracking service scores** post-rollout to confirm the effect persists
2. **Gather staff feedback** on the new procedure's efficiency and ease of use
3. **Review after 3 months** to assess whether improvements are sustained

---

## **Conclusion**

The data provides strong evidence that the new stock rotation and shelving procedure **improves service quality**. While the cooperative initially expected no difference, the analysis shows a clear, statistically significant benefit. Rolling out the new procedure system-wide is supported by the evidence and is likely to raise service scores across all branches.

---

*Analysis based on branches.csv (2,550 observations, 150 branches, weeks 6–22). Models used clustered standard errors by branch.*
