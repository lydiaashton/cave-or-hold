# **MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation procedure shows a **positive but statistically uncertain** effect on service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure improved by approximately **2 points** on the service score index relative to branches that kept the old procedure. However, this result is **not statistically significant** at conventional confidence levels, meaning we cannot confidently rule out that the observed improvement occurred by chance. 

**Recommendation: Do not proceed with system-wide rollout at this time.** Instead, extend the pilot period to gather more evidence before making a final decision.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Service scores average around 50 points; higher scores indicate better service

### Results Summary

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|----------------|---------------|--------|
| Control (Old Procedure) | 47.09 | 45.96 | −1.13 |
| Treatment (New Procedure) | 49.36 | 50.25 | +0.89 |

**Estimated Effect of New Procedure: +2.02 points** (approximately 4% improvement from baseline)

### Statistical Confidence
- **95% Confidence Interval:** −0.5 to +4.7 points
- **p-value:** 0.12 (above the 0.05 threshold for statistical significance)
- The confidence interval includes zero, meaning the data is consistent with no effect, a small negative effect, or a moderate positive effect

### What We Can Conclude
1. **The new procedure did not harm service scores.** Treatment branches improved while control branches declined slightly.
2. **The evidence is suggestive but not conclusive.** The estimated 2-point improvement is meaningful if real, but we lack sufficient data to confirm it.
3. **Results hold after adjusting for staffing and visitor volume.** These factors affect service scores but do not explain the observed difference between groups.

---

## **Next Steps**

### Immediate Actions

| Action | Timeline | Responsible Party |
|--------|----------|-------------------|
| Continue pilot with current 20 treatment branches | Ongoing | Operations Team |
| Collect 8–12 additional weeks of data | Next 3 months | Data Team |
| Survey branch staff on operational impacts | Next 4 weeks | HR/Operations |

### Decision Framework

**Proceed with rollout if:**
- Extended data shows statistically significant improvement (p < 0.05)
- Effect size exceeds 1.5 points (approximately 3% improvement)
- Staff feedback indicates operational benefits

**Discontinue if:**
- Extended data shows no effect or negative effect
- Staff report significant operational burdens

### Board Decision Required

We request the Board authorize:
1. **Extension of the pilot period** through Q2 2027
2. **No system-wide rollout** until conclusive evidence is available
3. **Budget allocation** for continued data collection and staff surveying

---

## **Conclusion**

The new procedure shows promise but lacks definitive evidence of effectiveness. A measured approach—extending the pilot while gathering more data—protects the cooperative from adopting a procedure that may not deliver meaningful improvements while preserving the option to scale if results strengthen. We will return to the Board in Q2 2027 with updated findings and a final recommendation.
