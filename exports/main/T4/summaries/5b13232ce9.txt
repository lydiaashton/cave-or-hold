# Board Memorandum

## **Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Analysis Period:** Weeks 6–22 (17 weeks of data)  
**Branches Studied:** 709 (425 switched to new procedure, 284 retained old procedure)

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores and should be rolled out system-wide.**

Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure achieved service scores **2.16 points higher** than comparable branches using the old procedure. This represents approximately a **4% improvement** over baseline performance. The effect is statistically significant (p < 0.001), robust to alternative specifications, and appears to strengthen over time.

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide implementation.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Primary Result**

| Measure | Value |
|---------|-------|
| Treatment Effect | **+2.16 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | **+1.60 to +2.73 points** |
| Statistical Significance | **p < 0.001** |
| Relative Improvement | **~4% above baseline** |

### **What This Means**

The average branch service score is approximately 53 points. The new procedure adds roughly 2 points to this score. We are 95% confident the true effect lies between 1.6 and 2.7 points. The probability this result occurred by chance is less than 1 in 1,000.

### **Why We Trust This Result**

1. **Valid Comparison:** Before the switch (weeks 6–14), treatment and control branches followed nearly identical service score trends. This "parallel trends" pattern validates our before-and-after comparison.

2. **Growing Benefit:** The improvement was modest immediately after the switch (~1 point in week 15) but grew steadily to ~3.3 points by week 22. This pattern suggests branches master the new procedure over time.

3. **Robust Analysis:** The result holds when controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume, and when using cluster-robust standard errors that account for repeated observations at each branch.
