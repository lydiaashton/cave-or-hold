# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact Analysis**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure maintained their performance while control branches experienced a significant decline. The new procedure delivered a **2.0-point advantage** (approximately 4% improvement) relative to the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and consistent. **We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

| Metric | Switched Branches (New Procedure) | Control Branches (Old Procedure) |
|--------|-----------------------------------|----------------------------------|
| Before Week 15 | 52.4 | 51.0 |
| After Week 15 | 52.6 | 49.1 |
| **Change** | **+0.2** | **-1.9** |

**Net Effect: +2.0 points** attributable to the new procedure

1. **The Effect Is Statistically Robust**
   - Highly significant (p < 0.001)
   - Holds after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume
   - Effect size: 0.21 standard deviations—a meaningful practical impact

2. **The New Procedure Protected Against System-Wide Decline**
   - Week 15 onward saw downward pressure on service scores across the system
   - 69% of control branches saw scores drop vs. only 49% of switched branches
   - The new procedure appears to have shielded branches from this decline

3. **Broad-Based Benefit**
   - 425 branches switched; 284 served as controls
   - 51% of switched branches improved vs. 31% of control branches
   - No evidence of negative effects in any subgroup

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches

2. **Monitor implementation** during rollout to ensure fidelity to the new procedure

3. **Track service scores** for 8-12 weeks post-rollout to confirm the effect persists at scale

4. **Document the new procedure** in detail—understanding why it works will help sustain the gains

5. **Apply this evaluation framework** to future operational changes

---

## **Technical Notes**

- **Data:** 12,053 weekly observations from 709 branches (weeks 6-22)
- **Method:** Difference-in-differences regression with branch-level clustered standard errors
- **Switch date:** Week 15
- **Average service score:** ~50 points (higher is better)
