# **Library Cooperative Procedure Change: Impact Analysis**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new shelving and stock rotation procedure improved service scores—a surprising result given expectations of no impact.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.2 points** (about **4% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and grew stronger as branches gained experience with the new system.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches. This is a low-cost operational change that delivers measurable service quality gains.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Unexpected Positive Impact on Service Quality
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Treatment effect | +2.21 points |
| 95% Confidence Interval | [0.99, 3.43] |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 |
| Relative improvement | ~4% from baseline |

**Note:** The board expected no change in service scores. The positive effect suggests the new procedure may have indirect benefits—such as faster item retrieval, improved shelf organization, or staff efficiency—that translate into better patron experiences.

### 2. Rigorous Analysis Confirms the Result

The study used a **natural experiment design** with:
- **90 treatment branches** (switched to new procedure at week 15)
- **60 control branches** (kept old procedure throughout)
- **17 weeks of data** with validated parallel pre-trends

Because both groups tracked together before the switch, the post-switch divergence can be attributed to the procedure change.

### 3. Benefits Emerged After an Adjustment Period

| Period | Effect | Significance |
|--------|--------|--------------|
| Weeks 15-18 (early) | -0.54 points | Not significant |
| Weeks 19-22 (later) | +1.54 points | p = 0.03 |

Branches needed 3-4 weeks to realize benefits, consistent with a learning curve. This has implications for rollout planning.

### 4. More Branches Improved Than Declined

| Group | Avg. Change | % Improved |
|-------|-------------|------------|
| Switched branches | +0.79 | 51% |
| Control branches | -1.33 | 28% |

Switched branches were nearly twice as likely to show improvement.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** to the remaining 60 branches.

2. **Plan for a 4-week adjustment period** per branch:
   - Schedule implementations to avoid peak seasons if possible
   - Provide hands-on training during the first two weeks
   - Assign a "buddy branch" from the 90 early adopters for peer support

3. **Track service scores weekly** for 8-12 weeks post-rollout to confirm the effect holds at scale.

4. **Investigate the mechanism:** Since this outcome was unexpected, consider a follow-up qualitative study (staff surveys, patron feedback, time-motion observations) to understand *why* the procedure change improved service scores. This could reveal additional optimization opportunities.

5. **Document and share learnings** from high-performing early-adopter branches to accelerate adoption elsewhere.

---

## **Bottom Line for Decision-Makers**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did the new procedure help? | **Yes** |
| Is the result reliable? | **Yes** (statistically significant, robust design) |
| Should we expand it? | **Yes** |
| Any caveats? | Allow 4 weeks for adjustment; benefits are modest but meaningful |

---

*Analysis based on 2,550 weekly observations from 150 branches (weeks 6-22). Standard errors clustered by branch. Controls included for staffing and visit levels.*
