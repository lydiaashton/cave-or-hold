# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure is **associated with improved service scores**, with switched branches gaining an average of **2 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. While this result falls just short of conventional statistical significance (p=0.11), the evidence is directionally consistent with our expectations: **70% of switched branches improved**, and the estimated effect represents a meaningful 4% gain relative to the baseline. We recommend **proceeding with rollout** while continuing to monitor outcomes.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Treatment Effect**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated improvement | **+2.0 points** |
| 95% Confidence interval | **-0.5 to +4.8 points** |
| P-value | **0.11** |
| Branches showing improvement | **14 of 20 (70%)** |

### **Score Changes by Group**
| Group | Before (Weeks 6-14) | After (Weeks 15-22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------|---------------------|--------|
| **Switched to new procedure** | 47.9 | 49.6 | **+1.7** |
| **Kept old procedure** | 51.1 | 50.8 | -0.3 |

**Difference-in-Differences Estimate: +2.0 points**

### **Branch-Level Variation**
Among the 20 branches that switched:
- **14 branches improved** (ranging from +0.3 to +6.8 points)
- **6 branches declined** (ranging from -0.4 to -5.1 points)
- This variation suggests the procedure may work better in some contexts than others

### **Pre-Switch Validation**
- Trends were parallel before the switch (no significant difference between groups)
- This supports the validity of our comparison and causal interpretation

---

## **Interpretation**

The data supports the hypothesis that the new procedure improves service scores, though with some uncertainty:

1. **Positive signal:** The point estimate (+2.0) is in the expected direction and of practically meaningful magnitude
2. **Majority benefit:** Seven out of ten switched branches saw improvements
3. **Statistical uncertainty:** With only 8 weeks of post-switch data, we lack the precision to rule out chance entirely
4. **No harm detected:** The lower bound of our confidence interval (-0.5) suggests minimal risk of negative effects

---

## **Recommendations**

### **1. Proceed with Phased Rollout**
Given the positive directional evidence and low risk of harm, we recommend beginning rollout to remaining branches while continuing to collect data.

### **2. Prioritize Branches Similar to Top Performers**
Investigate which branch characteristics correlate with larger gains and prioritize similar branches in the next rollout phase.

### **3. Support Struggling Branches**
The 6 branches that declined may need additional training or support to implement the procedure effectively.

### **4. Continue Monitoring**
- Track service scores for at least 12 more weeks
- Establish a quarterly review process to assess ongoing performance
- Set a threshold for reevaluation if scores decline systematically

### **5. Document Implementation Details**
Capture any variations in how branches implement the procedure to identify best practices.

---

## **Conclusion**

The new rotation and shelving procedure shows **promising evidence of improving service quality**. While we would have preferred stronger statistical confirmation, the weight of evidence—positive point estimate, majority of branches improving, and no signal of harm—supports moving forward with implementation. Continued monitoring will provide greater certainty and help optimize the procedure across all branches.
