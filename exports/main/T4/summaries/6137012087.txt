# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure **did not produce a statistically significant improvement** in service scores. While branches that adopted the new procedure experienced a smaller decline than those that kept the old procedure (+1.9 point relative difference), this result could reasonably occur by chance. **We do not recommend a full rollout at this time.** Instead, we suggest extending the evaluation period and investigating why service scores declined across all branches after week 15.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** total: 20 adopted the new procedure, 14 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Analysis compares service score changes before and after the switch between the two groups

### **Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| **Switched branches** (20 branches) | 54.27 | 52.69 | **-1.58** |
| **Control branches** (14 branches) | 50.35 | 46.84 | **-3.52** |

- **Estimated effect: +1.94 points** (switched branches declined less than control branches)
- **95% confidence interval: -0.6 to +4.5 points**
- **p-value: 0.14** (not statistically significant at conventional thresholds)

### **What This Means**

1. **The effect is uncertain.** The data are consistent with effects ranging from slightly harmful (-0.6 points) to moderately beneficial (+4.5 points). We cannot conclude the procedure improved service scores.

2. **Both groups declined.** Service scores dropped across all branches after week 15. This suggests external factors (seasonal patterns, staffing changes, visitor volume) affected the entire system.

3. **The new procedure may have helped cushion the decline.** Switched branches fell less than control branches, but we lack sufficient evidence to confirm this wasn't random variation.

4. **Pre-switch trends were comparable.** Before week 15, both groups followed similar patterns, supporting the validity of our comparison.

---

## **Recommendations**

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| **1** | **Pause full rollout** | Evidence does not support the expected improvement |
| **2** | **Extend data collection** | More post-switch weeks would increase statistical power |
| **3** | **Investigate system-wide decline** | Understand why all branches saw scores drop after week 15 |
| **4** | **Review implementation quality** | Ensure switched branches are following the new procedure consistently |
| **5** | **Consider additional metrics** | Evaluate staff efficiency, patron feedback, and circulation rates alongside service scores |

---

## **Decision Framework**

If the board wishes to proceed despite uncertainty:

- **Roll out to 5–10 additional branches** as a pilot
- **Collect 8–12 more weeks of data** before evaluating
- **Set a clear threshold** for success (e.g., +2 points with p < 0.10)
- **Prepare to reverse** if results remain inconclusive or negative
