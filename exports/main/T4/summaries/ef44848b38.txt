# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure is associated with a modest improvement in service scores (+1.7 points, or ~3%), but the evidence is not statistically conclusive. Both switched and non-switched branches saw service score improvements after week 15, with switched branches improving slightly more. We recommend **proceeding with caution**—the procedure shows promise but warrants continued monitoring before a full system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** total: 20 adopted the new procedure, 14 continued with the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22), with the switch implemented at week 15
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, which isolates the effect of the new procedure by comparing changes in switched branches against changes in non-switched branches

### **Service Score Results**

| Group | Pre-Switch (Weeks 6–14) | Post-Switch (Weeks 15–22) | Change |
|-------|------------------------|--------------------------|--------|
| Switched Branches | 50.26 | 56.37 | +6.11 |
| Non-Switched Branches | 50.60 | 54.96 | +4.36 |
| **Difference (Treatment Effect)** | | | **+1.75** |

- **Estimated effect of new procedure: +1.75 points** on the service score index
- **Statistical significance: p = 0.10** (marginally significant; 95% confidence interval: -0.3 to +3.8)
- This represents approximately **3.3% improvement** relative to the average service score

### **Validity Check: Parallel Trends**
A critical assumption of this analysis is that switched and non-switched branches were on similar trajectories before the change. The data supports this:
- Pre-switch trends were **statistically indistinguishable** (p = 0.87)
- Both groups started from similar baseline scores (~50 points)

### **Important Context**
- **Both groups improved** after week 15, suggesting broader factors (seasonal patterns, system-wide initiatives, etc.) also boosted service scores
- The new procedure's **incremental benefit** over these general improvements is what we estimate at +1.75 points
- Staffing and visit levels did not significantly explain the variation in service scores

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Continue Monitoring**: Extend the evaluation period to 6–12 months to determine if the effect persists, grows, or diminishes over time.

2. **Consider Phased Rollout**: Given the suggestive (but not definitive) positive results, consider expanding the new procedure to additional branches while maintaining a control group for ongoing comparison.

3. **Investigate Mechanisms**: Conduct qualitative interviews with branch staff to understand *why* the new procedure may be working (e.g., faster restocking, better organization, reduced staff time on shelving).

4. **Track Additional Metrics**: Monitor operational efficiency measures (staff hours, restocking time, patron complaints) alongside service scores to capture the full impact.

5. **Re-evaluate at 6 Months**: Schedule a follow-up analysis to reassess whether the evidence is now strong enough to justify system-wide implementation.

---

## **Technical Notes**
- Standard errors are clustered at the branch level to account for repeated observations
- Results are robust to controlling for staffing and visit indices
- Analysis conducted using difference-in-differences regression methodology
