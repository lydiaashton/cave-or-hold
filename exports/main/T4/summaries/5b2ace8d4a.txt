# **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Contrary to expectations, the new procedure did improve service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **2.2 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents approximately a **4% improvement** over the typical service score of 50. The finding is statistically robust (p < 0.001) and was consistent across all post-implementation weeks. While this outcome was not anticipated, the evidence supports proceeding with the rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **150 branches** total: 90 adopted the new procedure, 60 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data**: Weeks 6–14 before the switch, Weeks 15–22 after
- Analysis used a difference-in-differences approach with branch and week fixed effects

### Results
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Treatment (new procedure) | 50.45 | 53.11 | +2.66 |
| Control (old procedure) | 49.65 | 50.12 | +0.46 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+2.20** |

### Why We Trust This Result
- **Parallel pre-trends confirmed**: Treatment and control branches moved together before the switch (joint test p = 0.999), ruling out pre-existing differences as an explanation
- **Effect emerged only after the switch**: The gap between groups widened starting in week 15, not before
- **Consistent across time**: Treatment branches outperformed control branches in every post-switch week (gaps ranged from 2.1 to 3.8 points)
- **Not driven by other factors**: Changes in staffing levels and visitor volume did not differ between groups
- **Precise estimate**: 95% confidence interval is 1.4 to 3.0 points—the true effect is very unlikely to be zero

### Important Caveat
This improvement was **not expected**. The board should consider:
- Whether service scores may capture aspects of branch operations that the new procedure inadvertently affects
- Whether there are unmeasured costs (staff time, training burden) not reflected in the service score
- Whether the effect will persist long-term or represents a short-term "novelty" boost

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with caution on rollout**: The data supports expansion, but the unexpected nature of the result warrants careful monitoring

2. **Investigate the mechanism**: Understanding *why* the new procedure improved scores (even unintentionally) could help maximize benefits and avoid unintended consequences elsewhere

3. **Track implementation costs**: Monitor staff hours, training time, and any operational friction during rollout to ensure net benefits remain positive

4. **Set a review checkpoint**: Reassess after 3–6 months to confirm the effect persists and no negative side effects emerge

5. **Consider a pilot at remaining branches**: If uncertainty remains, convert a small subset of the 60 control branches first before full system-wide adoption
