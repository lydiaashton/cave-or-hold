# **Board Summary: New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure Evaluation**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores—contrary to expectations.** While we anticipated no measurable impact on service scores, branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw their scores increase by approximately **2.1 points** (about **4% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant. The analysis supports rolling out the new procedure to all branches as a beneficial operational change.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **150 branches** total: 90 adopted the new procedure (treatment group), 60 kept the old procedure (control group)
- **17 weeks of data**: Weeks 6–14 (before switch) and Weeks 15–22 (after switch)
- **2,550 observations** analyzed using difference-in-differences methodology

### **Main Result**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Service score improvement | **+2.06 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | [0.86, 3.27] |
| Statistical significance | **p = 0.0008** (highly significant) |
| Relative improvement | **~4%** over baseline |

### **Expected vs. Actual**
| | Expectation | Actual Finding |
|---|-------------|----------------|
| Effect on service score | No change | **+2.1 point increase** |
| Recommendation | Neutral | **Proceed with rollout** |

### **What This Means**
- Switched branches improved from an average of **48.0** to **50.6** after adoption
- Control branches showed minimal change (**47.9** to **48.4**)
- The **2.1-point difference** is attributable to the new procedure
- **This is a positive surprise**: the procedure delivers benefits we did not anticipate

### **Supporting Evidence**
1. **Parallel trends validated**: Before the switch, both groups followed similar trends (p=0.22), confirming the comparison is valid
2. **Robust to controls**: Results hold when accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume
3. **Consistent effect**: The improvement is visible across post-switch weeks in the data

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended Actions**
1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to remaining 60 branches
   - This is now supported by both operational logic AND empirical evidence of service improvement
2. **Communicate the unexpected benefit** to branch managers to encourage adoption
3. **Document best practices** from the 90 early-adopter branches to support training

### **Ongoing Monitoring**
- Continue tracking service scores for 8–12 weeks post-rollout at each branch
- Investigate *why* the procedure improved scores (was it faster service? better organization? staff morale?)
- Watch for any branches that don't achieve similar improvements (may need additional support)

### **Considerations**
- **Why did this work?** The procedure was designed for operational efficiency, not service quality. Understanding the mechanism could inform future improvements.
- Analysis covers a 17-week period; long-term effects should be monitored
- Effect size (~4%) is meaningful—equivalent to moving a branch from the 50th to roughly the 55th percentile
