# **Memorandum to the Board of Directors**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure  
**Date:** Analysis of Weeks 6–22 Service Data  
**Recommendation:** Proceed with System-Wide Rollout

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure works.** Branches that adopted the new shelving and stock rotation procedure achieved service scores **1.9 points higher** than comparable branches using the old procedure. This represents a **4% improvement** and is statistically certain (p < 0.001). We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all remaining branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### The New Procedure Delivered Measurable Improvement

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| **Branches with New Procedure** | 49.69 | 52.98 | +3.28 |
| **Branches with Old Procedure** | 48.97 | 50.35 | +1.38 |
| **Net Effect of New Procedure** | | | **+1.90** |

### The Result Is Statistically Sound

- **Treatment effect:** +1.90 service score points
- **95% confidence interval:** 1.55 to 2.25 points
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001 (less than 1 in 1,000 chance this is random)
- **Analysis method:** Difference-in-Differences regression controlling for branch-specific factors and week-to-week variation

### The Improvement Comes from the Procedure Itself

The analysis tested whether the improvement was simply due to better staffing or higher visitor traffic. It was not:

| Factor | Effect of New Procedure | Significance |
|--------|------------------------|--------------|
| Staffing levels | No change | Not significant |
| Visitor volume | No change | Not significant |
| **Service score** | **+1.90 points** | **Highly significant** |

This confirms the procedure itself—better organization, faster item location, or improved shelf management—drove the improvement.

### The Comparison Is Fair

For this analysis to be valid, treatment and control branches must have been on similar trajectories before the switch. The data confirms this:

- In the three weeks immediately before the switch (weeks 12–14), there were **no statistically significant differences** between the two groups
- Both groups followed parallel trends leading into the intervention

---

## **Recommendation**

**Approve system-wide rollout of the new procedure to all remaining branches.**

The evidence is clear: the new procedure improves service quality without requiring additional staffing or generating additional traffic. The effect is modest but meaningful and statistically certain.
