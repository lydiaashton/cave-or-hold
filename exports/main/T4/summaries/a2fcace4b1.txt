# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

---

## **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

**Date:** November 2026  
**Prepared by:** Data Analysis Team

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout.**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure **significantly improves service scores**. Branches using the new procedure gained an average of **2.1 points** on the service score index—a meaningful 4% improvement. The effect is statistically robust, grows over time, and justifies expanding the procedure to all 709 branches.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **1. Clear Positive Impact on Service Quality**

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|---------------|---------------|--------|
| Switched branches (425) | 50.73 | 53.26 | +2.53 |
| Non-switched branches (284) | 48.86 | 49.29 | +0.43 |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | | **+2.12** |

The **2.12-point net improvement** is the estimated causal effect of the new procedure, after accounting for pre-existing differences between branches and general time trends.

### **2. Effect Is Statistically Solid**

- **95% Confidence Interval:** 1.56 to 2.68 points
- **P-value:** < 0.001 (highly significant)
- **Effect size:** 0.21 standard deviations (small-to-medium)
- Results hold after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### **3. Benefits Accumulate Over Time**

The improvement was not immediate but strengthened as branches gained experience:

| Weeks Since Switch | Estimated Effect |
|-------------------|------------------|
| 1 week | +0.94 points |
| 4 weeks | +1.76 points |
| 8 weeks | +3.33 points |

This learning curve suggests **further gains are likely** as branches become more proficient with the new procedure.

### **4. No Major Concerns Identified**

- Staffing and visitation patterns were similar between switched and non-switched branches before the change
- The effect is consistent across different statistical specifications
- No evidence of negative unintended consequences in the data

- **Capture best practices:** Document what high-performing early adopters are doing well
- **Support struggling branches:** Identify and assist any branches that do not see expected improvements
- **Continue evaluation:** Track service scores for at least 6 months post-rollout

---

## **ABOUT THIS ANALYSIS**

**Data:** 12,053 weekly observations from 709 branches (weeks 6–22)  
**Method:** Difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors  
**Switch date:** Week 15  
**Metric:** Service score index (average ≈ 50; higher is better)

**Limitations:** Branches were not randomly assigned to switch; however, the analytical approach accounts for pre-existing differences, and the growing effect over time supports a causal interpretation.
