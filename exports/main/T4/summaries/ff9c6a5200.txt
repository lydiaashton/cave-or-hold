# **MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2025  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure  

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Recommend proceeding with systemwide rollout.** The new shelving and stock rotation procedure produced a statistically significant improvement in service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure saw scores increase by approximately **1.9 points** relative to branches that continued with the old procedure. On a scale where the average branch scores about 50, this represents a meaningful ~4% improvement.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Analysis Overview**

| Component | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| Branches studied | 34 total (20 switched, 14 did not) |
| Observation period | 17 weeks (weeks 6–22) |
| Switch date | Week 15 |
| Method | Difference-in-differences with branch and week fixed effects |

### **Service Score Results**

| Period | Treatment Branches (New Procedure) | Control Branches (Old Procedure) |
|--------|-----------------------------------|----------------------------------|
| Before switch (weeks 6–14) | 49.7 | 49.6 |
| After switch (weeks 15–22) | 50.9 | 48.9 |
| **Change** | **+1.2 points** | **-0.7 points** |

**Estimated effect of new procedure: +1.9 points**  
**95% Confidence Interval: +0.4 to +3.5 points**  
**Statistical significance: p = 0.012**

### **Why We Trust This Result**

1. **Parallel trends verified:** Treatment and control branches followed similar service score patterns before the switch, supporting the validity of the comparison.

2. **Robust to controls:** The effect persists after accounting for weekly staffing levels and customer visit volumes.

3. **Consistent post-switch:** The treatment-control gap widened in every week after the switch, with no reversal of the effect.

4. **Large sample:** 578 branch-week observations provide adequate statistical power.

---

## **Recommendations & Next Steps**

### **1. Approve Systemwide Rollout**
The evidence supports expanding the new procedure to all 14 branches currently using the old method. Expected benefit: ~2-point service score improvement per branch.

### **2. Implementation Timeline**
- **Months 1–2:** Train staff at remaining branches
- **Months 3–6:** Monitor service scores during transition
- **Month 6+:** Evaluate long-term sustainability of gains

### **3. Ongoing Monitoring**
Track the following metrics during rollout:
- Weekly service scores by branch
- Staff time per transaction (efficiency gain?)
- Patron satisfaction surveys (if available)

### **4. Further Analysis Opportunities**
- Identify which branches benefited most (may reveal best practices)
- Assess whether effects grow or diminish over time
- Quantify operational cost savings, if any
