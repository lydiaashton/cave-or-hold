# **Memorandum to the Board**

## **Subject: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

### **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores by approximately 2.3 points (4.5% above baseline).** This result was unexpected—management anticipated no measurable effect—but the data show a clear, statistically significant benefit. Branches that adopted the new procedure maintained their service levels while non-adopting branches declined. **We recommend proceeding with system-wide rollout.**

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. Study Overview**
- **150 branches** tracked over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure at week 15; **60 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Analysis compares service score changes between the two groups before and after the switch

**2. Impact on Service Scores**

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|----------------|---------------|--------|
| Branches keeping old procedure | 51.81 | 49.45 | −2.36 |
| Branches adopting new procedure | 51.24 | 51.19 | −0.05 |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | | **+2.31** |

- The **+2.31 point effect** is statistically significant (p < 0.001)
- Results are unchanged when accounting for staffing levels and visitor traffic

**3. Why We Trust This Result**
- **Parallel pre-trends confirmed**: Switching and non-switching branches followed similar trajectories before week 15, validating the comparison
- **Consistent post-switch gains**: The benefit appeared immediately after the switch and persisted across all subsequent weeks
- **Robust to controls**: The effect holds regardless of whether we adjust for other weekly factors

**4. Important Context**
The analysis reveals that service scores declined system-wide after week 15, but branches using the new procedure were **protected from this decline**. This suggests the new procedure may provide operational resilience beyond its direct effect on scores.

---

### **Recommendations**

| Priority | Action |
|----------|--------|
| **1** | Approve immediate rollout of the new procedure to all 60 remaining branches |
| **2** | Prioritize branches showing the steepest score declines for early implementation |
| **3** | Document implementation practices from early adopters to support training |
| **4** | Continue monitoring service scores for 8–12 weeks post-rollout to confirm sustained effect |

---

### **Bottom Line for Decision-Making**

Despite expectations that this operational change would have no measurable impact on patron experience, the evidence shows otherwise. The new procedure delivers a **meaningful, statistically reliable improvement** in service scores at no apparent cost. Delaying rollout would mean foregoing these gains at 60 branches.
