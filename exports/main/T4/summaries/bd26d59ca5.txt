# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

---

## **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

### **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure improved service scores and should be rolled out system-wide.**

Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **2.2 points** (approximately 4.4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This result is statistically significant, robust across multiple analytical methods, and represents a meaningful gain in service quality.

---

### **KEY FINDINGS**

**1. The New Procedure Worked**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Branches Using New Procedure | 51.40 | 53.99 | +2.59 |
| Branches Using Old Procedure | 50.45 | 50.85 | +0.40 |
| **Net Effect of New Procedure** | | | **+2.2** |

The 2.2-point improvement is the estimated causal effect after accounting for baseline differences between branches, overall time trends, and weekly variations in staffing and visitor volume.

**2. Strong Statistical Evidence**

- **95% Confidence Interval:** 1.9 to 2.6 points
- **Statistical Significance:** p < 0.001 (highly significant)
- **Robustness:** Result holds across five different model specifications with varying controls

**3. Benefits Took Time to Materialize**

| Period | Effect Size | Statistical Significance |
|--------|-------------|-------------------------|
| Weeks 15–18 (immediate) | Small (0.0 to 0.3 pts) | Not significant |
| Weeks 19–22 (later) | Clear (1.1 to 1.7 pts) | Significant |

Branches needed **4–5 weeks** to fully implement the new procedure and realize its benefits. This is important for setting expectations during rollout.

**4. Validation of Study Design**

Before the switch, branches that would later adopt the new procedure actually had *lower* service scores than control branches. This strengthens confidence in the result—the improvement cannot be attributed to pre-existing upward trends in the treatment group.

---

### **STUDY OVERVIEW**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Branches Studied** | 709 |
| **Study Period** | 17 weeks (weeks 6–22) |
| **Switch Date** | Week 15 |
| **Early Adopters** | 425 branches |
| **Control Group** | 284 branches |
| **Service Score Scale** | Average ~50 points (higher is better) |

---

### **RECOMMENDATIONS**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches

2. **Allow 4–6 weeks for implementation** at each branch before evaluating results, as benefits take time to materialize

3. **Monitor service scores** during rollout to ensure consistent results across different branch types and regions

4. **Document implementation best practices** from the 425 early-adopter branches to support training at remaining locations

5. **Conduct a follow-up evaluation** in 3–6 months to assess whether the improvement is sustained long-term

---

### **ANALYTICAL METHOD**

This analysis used a **difference-in-differences** design, the standard approach for evaluating policy changes when some units adopt a treatment and others do not. All models controlled for branch-specific characteristics, week-specific factors, staffing levels, and visitor volume to isolate the effect of the procedure change itself.
