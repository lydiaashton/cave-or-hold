# **Board Summary: Impact of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure on Service Scores**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Contrary to the expectation that the change would make no difference, branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **1.74 points** (approximately 3.4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and robust. **We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.**

---

## **Study Design**

- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (Weeks 6-22)
- **90 branches** (60%) switched to the new procedure at Week 15
- **60 branches** (40%) continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in service scores between the two groups before and after the switch

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Service Scores Improved After the Switch

| Group | Before (Weeks 6-14) | After (Weeks 15-22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------|---------------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 50.34 | 50.62 | +0.28 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 49.85 | 51.86 | +2.02 |

**Net effect of new procedure: +1.74 points** (p = 0.002)

### 2. The Result Is Statistically Reliable

- The improvement is **highly statistically significant** (p < 0.01)
- We can be confident this is not due to random chance
- The effect holds even after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### 3. Pre-Switch Trends Were Similar (Validating the Comparison)

- Before Week 15, both groups showed **parallel trends** in service scores
- No evidence that switching branches were already on a different trajectory
- This supports the validity of our comparison

### 4. Effect Size in Context

- Average service score across all branches: ~50 points
- The 1.74-point improvement represents a **3.4% gain**
- This is a **meaningful operational improvement** for a process change

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches

2. **Monitor implementation** to ensure the effect persists at scale:
   - Track service scores for newly converted branches
   - Watch for any diminishing returns as more branches adopt the procedure

3. **Document the new procedure** to capture what worked:
   - Identify any branch-specific factors that enhanced or reduced the benefit
   - Create training materials based on successful implementations

4. **Consider further optimization**:
   - The effect is positive but modest; explore whether additional refinements could amplify the benefit
   - Investigate whether the procedure interacts with staffing or visit volume (both are significant predictors of service scores)
