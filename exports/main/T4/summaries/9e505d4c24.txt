# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

## **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

The new procedure shows a **positive association with service scores**, but the evidence is **not statistically conclusive**. Branches that adopted the new procedure maintained their service scores while control branches experienced a decline. The estimated improvement is approximately **2 points (about 4%)**, but we cannot rule out the possibility that this result occurred by chance. 

**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **phased rollout with continued monitoring**, or gather more data before committing to system-wide implementation.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **Study Overview**
| | |
|---|---|
| **Branches Analyzed** | 34 total (20 switched to new procedure, 14 kept old) |
| **Study Period** | 17 weeks (Weeks 6–22) |
| **Switch Date** | Week 15 |
| **Method** | Difference-in-differences analysis |

### **Main Result**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| **New Procedure** | 52.2 | 52.5 | +0.3 |
| **Old Procedure** | 51.9 | 50.3 | −1.6 |
| **Estimated Effect** | | | **+1.9 points** |

- **Point Estimate:** +1.9 points on the service score index
- **95% Confidence Interval:** −0.5 to +4.3 points
- **Statistical Significance:** p = 0.12 (not significant at the 0.05 level)

### **Interpretation**

1. **The effect is positive but uncertain.** The data suggest the new procedure improves service scores, but the confidence interval includes zero, meaning we cannot be confident the effect is real rather than due to random variation.

2. **The benefit is partly defensive.** The apparent improvement comes largely from the treatment group *avoiding the decline* that affected control branches, rather than producing a large absolute gain.

3. **Pre-existing trends were similar.** Before the switch, both groups showed parallel trends in service scores, supporting the validity of the comparison.

4. **Practical significance.** If the effect is real, a 2-point improvement represents about 4% of the average service score—modest but meaningful for member experience.

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

### **Recommended Approach: Phased Rollout with Monitoring**

1. **Expand implementation** to additional branches while continuing to track service scores
2. **Set a formal review date** (e.g., 12 weeks post-rollout) to reassess with more data
3. **Monitor control branches** to understand why their scores declined after Week 15
4. **Track secondary metrics** (staffing efficiency, member complaints, circulation) to capture benefits not reflected in service scores

### **Alternative: Extended Study Before Full Rollout**

If the Board prefers higher certainty before committing:
- Continue observing current treatment and control branches for 8–12 additional weeks
- Consider randomizing the procedure across remaining branches to strengthen causal inference
- Investigate whether seasonal factors or other operational changes affected the control group

---

## **CONCLUSION**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure shows **promise but not proof**. The evidence points to a modest positive effect on service scores, and the procedure appears to protect against declines. However, statistical uncertainty means we cannot be confident this effect would replicate at scale.

Given that the procedure does not appear harmful and may provide stability during periods when service scores otherwise decline, a **cautious rollout with monitoring** is warranted. The Board should plan to revisit this decision with additional data before committing to permanent system-wide adoption.

---

*Analysis prepared from weekly service score data, 34 branches, Weeks 6–22. Difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch).*
