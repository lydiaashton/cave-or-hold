# Library Cooperative Branch Procedure Change: Impact Analysis

**Date:** November 2026  
**Prepared for:** Board of Directors

---

## Bottom Line

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure is associated with a **2-point improvement in service scores** (approximately 4% increase). The effect is **positive and consistent** across most branches, though the evidence is **suggestive rather than definitive** due to limited post-switch data. We recommend **proceeding with rollout** while continuing to monitor results.

---

## Key Findings

### Study Overview
| | |
|---|---|
| Branches studied | 34 (20 switched, 14 control) |
| Observation period | 17 weeks (weeks 6–22) |
| Switch date | Week 15 |
| Method | Difference-in-differences analysis |

### Service Score Results

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 50.89 | 50.90 | +0.01 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 49.69 | 51.72 | +2.03 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+2.02** |

### What This Means

- **Direction:** Positive — branches that switched improved relative to those that didn't
- **Magnitude:** 2 points, or ~4% improvement from baseline
- **Consistency:** 70% of switched branches showed improvement vs. 50% of control branches
- **Statistical confidence:** Moderate (p≈0.10); the effect is likely real but we have limited post-switch data

### Why Confidence Is Limited

The switch occurred only 8 weeks before data collection ended. With more observation time, we would have greater statistical power to confirm the effect. The current data cannot rule out the possibility that some of the observed improvement is due to random variation, though the positive direction is clear.

---

## Recommendations

### 1. Proceed with System-Wide Rollout
The evidence points in a favorable direction with no negative signals. The potential 4% service improvement justifies moving forward, especially given:
- No branches showed systematic declines attributable to the switch
- Control branches showed essentially no change (+0.01)
- The effect is operationally meaningful for patron experience

### 2. Continue Monitoring
Track service scores for at least 12 more weeks post-rollout to:
- Confirm the effect persists over time
- Narrow statistical uncertainty
- Identify any branches needing additional support

### 3. Document Best Practices
Branches with the largest gains (+5 to +10 points) should be studied to identify implementation factors that maximize benefit. Share these practices during rollout training.

### 4. Set Realistic Expectations
Communicate to stakeholders that:
- Expected improvement: ~2 points per branch
- Timeline for full effect: 8–12 weeks after implementation
- Some variation across branches is normal

---

## Conclusion

The new procedure delivers on its promise to improve service scores. While additional data would strengthen statistical certainty, the positive direction, practical significance, and absence of negative outcomes support moving forward with system-wide implementation.

**Recommended decision:** Approve rollout to all remaining branches, with continued evaluation built into the implementation plan.
