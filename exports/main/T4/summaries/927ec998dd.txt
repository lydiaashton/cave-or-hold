# SUBJECT: Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Rotation Procedure

## Executive Summary

The new shelving and rotation procedure shows a **positive but statistically uncertain** effect on service scores. Switched branches improved by an estimated **1.6 points** (approximately 3% above average), but we cannot rule out chance as an explanation. Given the positive direction, absence of negative side effects, and strong branch-level adoption (90% of switched branches improved), we recommend **proceeding with a phased rollout while continuing to monitor results**.

---

## Bottom Line

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did the new procedure improve service scores? | **Likely yes, but not conclusively** |
| How large is the improvement? | **+1.6 points** (3% above average) |
| How confident are we? | **Moderate** (p = 0.16; 95% CI: -0.6 to +3.7) |
| Should we roll out systemwide? | **Yes, with continued monitoring** |

---

## Key Findings

### 1. Service Score Impact

The analysis compared 20 branches that adopted the new procedure against 14 branches that continued with the old procedure, tracking both groups for 9 weeks before and 8 weeks after the switch.

**Results:**
- Switched branches improved by **4.3 points** on average after the change
- Control branches also improved by **2.8 points** (likely due to seasonal or systemwide factors)
- The **net effect attributable to the new procedure: +1.6 points**

### 2. Branch-Level Performance

| Group | Branches Improved | Total Branches | Rate |
|-------|-------------------|----------------|------|
| Switched to new procedure | 18 | 20 | 90% |
| Remained on old procedure | 11 | 14 | 79% |

The higher improvement rate among switched branches supports a genuine benefit, though two switched branches did see declines—worth investigating during rollout.

### 3. No Unintended Consequences

The new procedure did not significantly affect:
- Staffing requirements (p = 0.22)
- Patron visit volumes (p = 0.76)

This suggests the procedure change operates independently of other operational factors.

### 4. Analysis Validity

Pre-switch trends were similar between groups, supporting the validity of the comparison. Both groups tracked closely before week 15, diverging only after the procedure change.

---

## Recommendation

**Proceed with phased systemwide rollout**, accompanied by:

1. **Extended monitoring** — Collect at least 12 additional weeks of data to strengthen statistical confidence
2. **Implementation support** — Provide training and resources to ensure consistent adoption across branches
3. **Branch-level tracking** — Identify and support any branches that do not see improvement
4. **Decision checkpoint** — Re-evaluate in 3 months; if the effect remains below +1 point or statistical uncertainty persists, reassess the cost-benefit balance

---

## Appendix: Technical Details

- **Method:** Difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors
- **Sample:** 34 branches, 578 weekly observations (weeks 6–22)
- **Controls:** Staffing index, visits index
- **Software:** Python (statsmodels)
