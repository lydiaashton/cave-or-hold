# **BOARD SUMMARY: New Branch Procedure Evaluation**

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new rotation and shelving procedure is associated with a **modest improvement** in service scores, but the evidence is **not statistically conclusive**. Switching branches improved by approximately **1.6 points more** than non-switching branches. However, given the uncertainty in the data, we cannot confidently rule out the possibility that this result occurred by chance.

**Recommendation:** Approve a **phased rollout** with continued evaluation rather than immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### What We Measured
- **34 branches** tracked for **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** switched to the new procedure at week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Service scores average around 50 (higher is better)

### Results

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated improvement from new procedure | **+1.6 points** |
| 95% confidence interval | **-0.4 to +3.7 points** |
| Statistical significance | **Not significant (p = 0.12)** |

### What This Means
- Both switching and non-switching branches saw service scores rise after week 15
- Switching branches improved slightly more (+3.9 vs. +2.3 points)
- The **additional 1.6-point gain** attributed to the new procedure is promising but falls short of statistical certainty
- The true effect could reasonably be anywhere from slightly negative to meaningfully positive

### Validation
- Pre-switch trends were similar between groups, supporting a valid comparison
- The procedure did not affect staffing levels or visitor volume

---

## **Next Steps**

| Action | Timeline |
|--------|----------|
| Expand new procedure to 5–10 additional branches | Immediate |
| Continue weekly service score tracking | Ongoing |
| Gather staff feedback on operational impact | 4 weeks |
| Re-analyze with additional data | 8–12 weeks |
| Board decision on full rollout | Next quarter |

---

## **Why the Uncertainty?**

The limited number of weeks post-switch (only 8 weeks) and natural variation in weekly scores create statistical noise. More data will clarify whether the observed improvement reflects a real effect or random fluctuation.
