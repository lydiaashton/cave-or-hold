# Memorandum to the Board

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

**Date:** November 15, 2026

---

## Bottom Line

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure shows a **modest positive association with service scores** (+1.6 points on average), but this effect is **not statistically distinguishable from zero** given the current data. We recommend a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## Key Findings

### 1. Estimated Effect on Service Scores

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Difference-in-Differences Estimate | +1.6 points |
| 95% Confidence Interval | -0.5 to +3.8 points |
| P-value | 0.14 |
| Effect Size (vs. baseline) | ~3% |

Branches that adopted the new procedure improved slightly relative to those that kept the old procedure, but the evidence is not strong enough to conclude the procedure caused the improvement.

### 2. Study Design Strengths

- **Parallel pre-trends:** Before the switch (weeks 6-14), both groups showed similar trends in service scores, supporting the validity of the comparison
- **Adequate control group:** 14 branches maintained the old procedure, providing a meaningful baseline
- **Sufficient observation window:** 9 weeks of pre-switch and 8 weeks of post-switch data

### 3. Important Context

- **Selection bias:** Branches that switched had lower baseline scores (48.9 vs. 54.3), suggesting they may have been chosen for the new procedure due to prior underperformance
- **High weekly variation:** Service scores fluctuate considerably week-to-week (standard deviation ~8-9 points), making it difficult to detect modest effects
- **Sample size limitations:** With 34 branches and 17 weeks, the study has limited power to detect small but meaningful effects

---

## Recommendations

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| **1** | Extend monitoring period | Additional weeks of data will improve statistical confidence |
| **2** | Randomize remaining branches | If expanding, randomly assign branches to eliminate selection bias |
| **3** | Conduct cost-benefit analysis | Even if the +1.6 effect is real, verify it justifies operational costs |
| **4** | Pilot expansion (5-10 branches) | Test scalability before full rollout |
| **5** | Investigate heterogeneity | Examine whether effects vary by branch size, location, or baseline performance |

---

## Data Summary

- **34 branches** total (20 switched to new procedure, 14 kept old procedure)
- **17 weeks** of observation (weeks 6-22; switch occurred at week 15)
- **578 observations** (one per branch per week)
- **Service score range:** Approximately 35-65 (higher is better; system average ~50)
