# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Library Cooperative Board  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure shows a **positive but statistically inconclusive effect** on service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure improved their service scores by approximately **2.2 points** (about 5% of the average score) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. However, this improvement is not statistically significant at conventional levels (p=0.10), meaning we cannot confidently rule out that the observed improvement occurred by chance.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **phased rollout** while continuing to monitor results. The evidence suggests the new procedure may help, but it is not definitive enough to justify immediate system-wide adoption.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **1. Overall Effect**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched Branches (20 branches) | 45.0 | 47.8 | +2.7 |
| Control Branches (14 branches) | 48.7 | 49.2 | +0.5 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+2.2** |

- **Estimated effect:** +2.24 points on the service score index
- **95% confidence interval:** -0.46 to +4.94 points
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.104 (not significant at the 0.05 threshold)

### **2. What This Means**

The confidence interval tells us the true effect could reasonably be:
- **As high as +5 points** (meaningful improvement)
- **As low as -0.5 points** (no effect or slight harm)
- **Most likely around +2 points** (modest improvement)

Because zero falls within this range, we cannot conclude with confidence that the procedure caused the improvement.

### **3. Study Validity**

A critical assumption is that switched and control branches would have followed similar trends without the intervention. **The data supports this assumption:**
- Pre-switch trends show no significant divergence between groups
- This strengthens confidence that any observed difference is attributable to the procedure change

### **4. Effect Over Time**

Weekly analysis reveals an **inconsistent pattern** after the switch:
- Some weeks show positive effects, others show neutral or negative effects
- This variability suggests the effect may not be stable, or that other factors (seasonal patterns, staffing changes) are influencing scores

### **5. Practical Significance**

- A 2.2-point improvement represents about **5% of the average service score** (~47 points)
- If real, this would be a meaningful improvement for patron experience
- The study had 80% power to detect effects of 3.85 points or larger; smaller effects may go undetected

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

### **Recommended Approach: Phased Rollout**

Given the suggestive but inconclusive evidence, I recommend:

1. **Invite 5-10 additional branches** to adopt the procedure voluntarily over the next 4-6 weeks
2. **Continue rigorous monitoring** with the same evaluation framework
3. **Reassess in 3 months** with the expanded dataset (more branches + more weeks)
4. **Make a system-wide decision** based on stronger evidence

### **Additional Actions**

- **Document implementation fidelity** – ensure switched branches are fully following the new procedure
- **Gather qualitative feedback** from staff at switched branches about operational challenges or benefits
- **Monitor costs** associated with the new procedure to assess cost-effectiveness

### **Decision Options Summary**

| Option | Pros | Cons |
|--------|------|------|
| **Full Rollout Now** | Captures potential benefits quickly; uniform procedures | Risk of adopting an ineffective procedure; implementation costs |
| **Phased Rollout** (Recommended) | Allows continued learning; limits risk | Delays potential benefits; temporary inconsistency |
| **Extended Evaluation Only** | More definitive evidence before committing | Opportunity cost if procedure is effective |

---

## **TECHNICAL NOTES**

- **Method:** Difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch)
- **Data:** 34 branches, 17 weeks each (weeks 6-22), 578 total observations
- **Switch date:** Week 15
- **Controls tested:** Staffing index and visits index (results unchanged)
- **Software:** Python with statsmodels package
