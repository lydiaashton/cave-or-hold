# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Roll out the new procedure to all branches.** Despite initial expectations that the change would have no effect on service scores, the data show a clear improvement: branches that switched saw service scores rise by **2.5 points** (approximately **5%**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This finding is statistically robust and consistent across the post-switch period.

---

## **Key Findings**

| Metric | Control Branches (Old Procedure) | Switched Branches (New Procedure) |
|--------|----------------------------------|-----------------------------------|
| Before switch (weeks 6–14) | 51.6 | 50.4 |
| After switch (weeks 15–22) | 50.9 | 52.2 |
| **Change** | **−0.7** | **+1.8** |

**Net effect of new procedure: +2.5 points** (p < 0.001)

### What the Data Show

1. **Unexpected but clear improvement** — We anticipated no change in service scores. Instead, switched branches improved while control branches slightly declined, yielding a 2.5-point net gain.

2. **Statistically significant** — The probability this result occurred by chance is less than 1 in 1,000. The 95% confidence interval ranges from 1.2 to 3.8 points.

3. **Valid comparison** — Before week 15, both groups tracked together closely. This confirms the improvement is attributable to the procedure change, not pre-existing differences.

4. **Holds under scrutiny** — The effect remains significant after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume, which also influence service scores.

---

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with system-wide rollout.** The evidence indicates the new rotation and shelving procedure delivers measurable service improvements at no apparent cost to performance. Given the cooperative's goal of maximizing member service quality, extending this procedure to all 150 branches is warranted.
