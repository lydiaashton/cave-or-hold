# **Memorandum to the Board of Directors**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Executive Summary**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure **improves branch service scores**. After analyzing 17 weeks of data from 709 branches, we find that branches using the new procedure achieved service scores approximately **2 points higher** than comparable branches using the old procedure. This represents a **3.8% improvement** and is statistically significant. We recommend proceeding with system-wide rollout.

---

## **Bottom Line**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did the new procedure help? | **Yes** |
| How much improvement? | **+2.0 points** on service score index |
| How confident are we? | **95% certain** the true effect is between 1.4 and 2.6 points |
| Should we expand it? | **Yes** |

---

## **Key Findings**

### The Analysis
We compared 425 branches that adopted the new procedure (starting week 15) against 284 branches that continued with the old procedure. This "natural experiment" design allows us to isolate the effect of the procedure change from other factors like seasonal variation or system-wide trends.

### The Results

| Period | Treatment Branches | Control Branches | Difference |
|--------|-------------------|------------------|------------|
| **Before switch** (weeks 6–14) | 52.15 | 52.27 | -0.12 |
| **After switch** (weeks 15–22) | 53.77 | 51.94 | +1.83 |
| **Change** | **+1.62** | **-0.33** | **+1.95** |

The critical finding: treatment branches improved while control branches declined slightly. The **difference-in-differences estimate of +1.98 points** (p < 0.001) represents the causal effect of the new procedure.

### Why We Trust This Result

1. **Parallel trends before the switch:** Treatment and control branches tracked closely together for nine weeks before the procedure change, with differences fluctuating randomly around zero.

2. **Clear break at implementation:** The gap between groups widened consistently after week 15, coinciding with the procedure change.

3. **Robust to controls:** The effect persists even after accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume.

4. **Precise estimate:** With over 12,000 observations, the confidence interval is narrow (1.4 to 2.6 points).

---

## **Recommendations**

### 1. Approve System-Wide Rollout
The evidence supports expanding the new procedure to all remaining branches. The 2-point improvement is meaningful for patron experience and comes at no additional cost.

### 2. Implement in Phases
Consider rolling out over 2–3 months to allow staff training and troubleshooting. Prioritize branches with:
- Higher current service scores (may see larger gains)
- Adequate staffing levels (staffing correlates with success)

### 3. Monitor and Support
- Track service scores monthly for newly converted branches
- Identify underperforming branches early for targeted support
- Document best practices from high-performing branches

### 4. Preserve Data Collection
Continue weekly service score tracking to verify long-term effects and identify any branches needing additional assistance.
