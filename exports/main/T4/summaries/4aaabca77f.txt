# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

---

## **Subject: Evaluation of New Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Contrary to our initial expectation that the change would make no difference, branches that adopted the new procedure experienced a **2.0-point increase** in service scores compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents approximately a **4% improvement** and is statistically significant (p < 0.001). 

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** switched to the new procedure at week 15; **60 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Analysis uses a rigorous difference-in-differences design that isolates the effect of the procedure change

### Results Summary

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|----------------|---------------|--------|
| Control (Old Procedure) | 48.56 | 48.91 | +0.35 |
| Treated (New Procedure) | 47.83 | 50.14 | +2.31 |
| **Effect of New Procedure** | | | **+1.96** |

- **Estimated impact:** +1.96 service score points
- **95% confidence interval:** 1.05 to 2.87 points
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001

### Why We Trust This Result

1. **Parallel trends verified:** Treatment and control groups had statistically similar trends before the switch (p = 0.31), supporting the validity of the comparison

2. **Robust to controls:** The result holds when accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume

3. **Clear visual evidence:** Service scores for treated branches diverged from control branches only after week 15, exactly when the new procedure began

### What This Means

- Service scores system-wide average around 50 points
- A 2-point improvement is meaningful for patron experience
- The effect appears consistent and sustained across the 8-week post-switch observation period
