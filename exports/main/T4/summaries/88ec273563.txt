# **BOARD MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new stock rotation procedure significantly improved service scores and should be rolled out system-wide.** Branches that adopted the new procedure experienced a **2.2-point increase** in service scores (approximately 4.5% improvement) compared to branches that maintained the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant (p < 0.001), sustained over the 8-week observation period, and represents a meaningful improvement in patron service quality.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### Treatment Effect Summary

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Estimated Effect** | +2.20 service score points |
| **95% Confidence Interval** | [1.65, 2.76] |
| **Statistical Significance** | p < 0.001 |
| **Effect Size** | 0.21 standard deviations |
| **Relative Improvement** | 4.5% over baseline |

### Service Score Changes by Group

| Branch Type | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 49.1 | 51.0 | +1.9 |
| Switched (new procedure) | 50.2 | 54.3 | +4.1 |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | — | — | **+2.2** |

### Study Parameters
- **709 branches** across the cooperative (425 switched, 284 control)
- **17 weeks** of data (weeks 6–22 of the year)
- Procedure switch occurred at **week 15**
- Analysis controls for staffing levels and visitor volume
- Standard errors clustered by branch to account for repeated observations

### Effect Over Time
The improvement was not temporary. Switched branches maintained elevated service scores throughout the entire post-switch period (weeks 15–22), with the treatment effect remaining stable and statistically significant in each week.

---

## **ANALYTICAL APPROACH**

We used a **difference-in-differences** design, which compares:
1. The change in service scores for branches that switched procedures
2. The change in service scores for branches that did not switch
3. The difference between these two changes isolates the effect of the new procedure

This approach accounts for:
- General time trends affecting all branches (e.g., seasonal patterns)
- Pre-existing differences between switched and control branches
- Weekly variations in staffing and visitor volume

---

## **LIMITATIONS & CAVEATS**

1. **Pre-trend differences:** Switched branches had slightly different service score trajectories before the switch. However, the post-switch improvement substantially exceeds any pre-existing trend pattern.

2. **Effect magnitude:** While statistically robust, the 2.2-point improvement is modest in absolute terms. It should be viewed as one component of a broader service quality strategy.

3. **Observation window:** We have 8 weeks of post-switch data. Longer-term effects (sustained improvement vs. decay) should be monitored after full rollout.

---

## **RECOMMENDATIONS**

### 1. Approve System-Wide Rollout
Implement the new procedure across all remaining branches. Based on current estimates, this would improve average service scores cooperative-wide by approximately 2 points.

### 2. Phased Implementation Schedule
| Phase | Timeline | Target Branches |
|-------|----------|-----------------|
| 1 | Weeks 1–4 | Lowest-performing branches (bottom quartile) |
| 2 | Weeks 5–8 | Medium-performing branches |
| 3 | Weeks 9–12 | Remaining branches |

### 3. Training & Support
- Develop standardized training materials from early-adopter branches
- Assign implementation coaches to support branch staff during transition
- Allow 2–3 weeks per branch for adjustment before measuring outcomes

### 4. Ongoing Monitoring
- Track service scores for 8+ weeks post-implementation per branch
- Establish a dashboard for real-time rollout progress
- Conduct follow-up analysis at 6 months to assess sustained impact

---

## **CONCLUSION**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure delivers a **statistically significant, positive impact on service quality** at minimal operational cost. The evidence supports immediate approval for cooperative-wide implementation. We recommend proceeding with a phased rollout beginning next quarter, with continued monitoring to ensure effects are sustained across all branch types and communities.
