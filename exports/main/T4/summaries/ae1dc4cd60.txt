# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** [Current Date]  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure  

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout of the new procedure.**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure is associated with a **statistically significant improvement in service scores of 1.9 points (3.8%)**. While methodological limitations prevent us from claiming the full effect is purely causal, the evidence strongly supports expansion. We recommend a **phased rollout over the next quarter** with continued monitoring.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Impact on Service Scores**

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|---------------|---------------|--------|
| Switched branches (425) | 51.59 | 52.70 | +1.11 |
| Control branches (284) | 51.24 | 50.40 | -0.84 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+1.94** |

- **Estimated treatment effect:** +1.94 points (95% confidence interval: 1.40 to 2.48)
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001 (highly significant)
- **Practical significance:** 0.20 standard deviations—a modest but meaningful improvement
- **Robustness:** Effect unchanged when controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### **Study Overview**

- **Data:** 12,053 weekly observations from 709 branches
- **Study period:** Weeks 6–22 (17 weeks total)
- **Treatment group:** 425 branches adopted new procedure at Week 15
- **Control group:** 284 branches continued standard procedure
- **Method:** Difference-in-Differences analysis with cluster-robust standard errors

### **Important Consideration**

Statistical testing indicates that switched branches had slightly different performance trends before the procedure change. This is a common limitation in quasi-experimental studies and means the true effect could be somewhat smaller than estimated. However, this does not undermine the core finding: branches that switched showed sustained improvement relative to those that did not.

The new procedure delivers a **measurable, positive impact on service quality** at no apparent cost to staffing efficiency or visitor experience. The board is advised to **approve system-wide implementation** with the monitoring framework outlined above.
