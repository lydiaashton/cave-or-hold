# **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

**To:** Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2025  
**Subject:** Recommendation on System-Wide Rollout of New Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Roll out the new procedure to all branches.** Despite initial expectations that the change would have no effect on service scores, the data show a clear and statistically significant improvement. Branches using the new procedure achieved service scores **2.2 points higher** than expected—a meaningful 4% gain that is highly unlikely to be due to chance.

---

## **Key Findings**

### What We Tested
We compared 90 branches that adopted the new procedure against 60 branches that kept the old procedure, tracking service scores for 17 weeks (9 weeks before and 8 weeks after the switch in week 15).

### What We Found

| Group | Before | After | Change |
|-------|--------|-------|--------|
| Branches with new procedure | 50.45 | 53.11 | +2.66 |
| Branches with old procedure | 49.65 | 50.12 | +0.46 |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | | **+2.20** |

### Why We Trust This Result

1. **The groups were comparable before the switch.** Treatment and control branches followed nearly identical trends in the 9 weeks before implementation (statistical test: p = 0.999). This validates our comparison.

2. **The effect is statistically robust.** The probability of observing this result by chance is less than 1 in 1,000 (p < 0.001). We are 95% confident the true effect lies between **1.4 and 3.0 points**.

3. **The effect is consistent.** Treatment branches outperformed control branches in every single week after the switch, with no signs of the effect fading over time.

4. **The effect is not explained by other factors.** Changes in staffing levels and visitor volume do not account for the improvement. The gain appears to come from the procedure itself.

5. **Most branches benefited.** 62 of 90 treatment branches (69%) showed improvement after switching.

---

## **What This Means**

- **Average service score:** ~50 points
- **Expected improvement per branch:** +2.2 points
- **Relative gain:** ~4%

While we anticipated no change, the new procedure has demonstrably improved service quality. The consistency and statistical strength of this finding suggest it is a real effect, not a statistical anomaly.

---

## **Recommendations**

| Action | Priority |
|--------|----------|
| **Approve system-wide rollout** to remaining 60 branches | High |
| **Track scores at newly converted branches** to confirm similar gains | High |
| **Document implementation practices** from top-performing branches | Medium |
| **Review in 6 months** to confirm sustained improvement | Medium |

---

## **Bottom Line for Decision-Makers**

> *We expected no difference. The data show otherwise.* The new procedure improves service scores by a meaningful margin with high statistical confidence. There is no evidence of harm and clear evidence of benefit. **Proceed with full rollout.**
