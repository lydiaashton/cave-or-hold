# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**
### Analysis for Board Decision on System-Wide Rollout

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure shows a **positive but statistically inconclusive effect** on service scores. While switched branches improved relative to non-switched branches (+2.4 points), the evidence is not strong enough to confirm the procedure caused this change. **We recommend a phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than an immediate system-wide implementation or abandonment of the initiative.

---

## **Key Findings**

### What We Analyzed
- **34 branches** over **17 weeks** (February–June)
- **20 branches** implemented the new procedure in week 15; **14 branches** maintained the standard procedure
- Service scores tracked weekly (average branch scores ~50; higher is better)

### What We Found

| Metric | Switched Branches | Non-Switched Branches |
|--------|------------------|----------------------|
| Before week 15 | 46.0 | 51.4 |
| After week 15 | 47.1 | 50.1 |
| **Change** | **+1.1** | **−1.3** |

**Net improvement attributable to new procedure: +2.4 points**

### Why We Cannot Be Certain

| Statistical Measure | Result | Interpretation |
|--------------------|--------|----------------|
| Point estimate | +2.4 points | Positive direction |
| 95% Confidence interval | −0.15 to +4.59 | Includes zero |
| p-value | 0.11 | Not statistically significant |
| Practical significance | ~5% improvement | Potentially meaningful if real |

**The data are consistent with three possibilities:**
1. The procedure has no effect (most conservative interpretation)
2. The procedure provides a modest benefit of 2–4 points
3. The procedure provides a larger benefit that requires more time to materialize

### Important Context

- **Switched branches started with lower baseline scores** (46.0 vs. 51.4), suggesting they may have had more room for improvement
- **Non-switched branches declined slightly** during the post-period, which could reflect seasonal patterns or external factors
- **The positive direction aligns with expectations**, even if statistical certainty is limited

---

## **Recommendations**

### For Immediate Action

| Recommendation | Rationale |
|---------------|-----------|
| **Approve phased rollout** to 10–15 additional branches | Expands sample size to improve statistical power |
| **Extend monitoring period** by 12 weeks | Allows time for full implementation effects to emerge |
| **Document implementation quality** at each branch | Ensures fidelity and identifies best practices |
| **Reconvene in Q3** with updated analysis | Enables data-driven final decision |

### For Final Decision Criteria

The board should consider system-wide rollout if future data show:
- **Statistically significant effect** (p < 0.05)
- **Sustained improvement** of 2+ points over 8+ weeks
- **Positive effect across branch types** (not limited to specific subgroups)

---

## **Risk Assessment**

| Scenario | Probability | Impact |
|----------|-------------|--------|
| Procedure delivers expected benefit | Moderate | Positive |
| Procedure has no meaningful effect | Moderate | Neutral (limited rollout cost) |
| Procedure underperforms expectations | Low | Manageable (reversible) |

**Overall risk: Low to Moderate** — The phased approach limits exposure while preserving upside potential.

---

## **Conclusion**

The analysis does not confirm the improvement we anticipated, but it also does not contradict it. The positive direction of the effect, combined with the relatively short observation window, suggests **cautious optimism is warranted**. A measured, evidence-based expansion allows the cooperative to capture potential benefits while maintaining accountability for results.
