# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**
### *Analysis for Board Decision on System-Wide Rollout*

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure **improves service scores by an average of 2.7 points** (approximately 6%). Branches that adopted the new procedure showed gains while control branches remained flat. The effect strengthens over time, suggesting growing benefits as staff gain experience. **We recommend proceeding with system-wide rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **What We Measured**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (February–June)
- **20 branches** implemented the new procedure starting week 15; **14 branches** maintained the old procedure
- Service scores average ~50 points; higher scores indicate better service

### **Results**

| Group | Before | After | Change |
|-------|--------|-------|--------|
| Branches keeping old procedure | 44.9 | 44.9 | — |
| Branches adopting new procedure | 46.9 | 49.4 | **+2.5** |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | | **+2.7 points** |

### **Why This Matters**

1. **The effect is real and positive.** Treatment branches improved; control branches did not. This pattern isolates the procedure change as the cause.

2. **Benefits grow with experience.** The improvement was +3.9 points in the first month and +5.1 points in the second month, indicating staff proficiency increases the payoff.

3. **Results are robust.** The finding holds after accounting for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume.

4. **No negative consequences observed.** Service scores did not decline in any subgroup during the transition.

### **Confidence Level**

The result is **positive and directionally clear** but carries some statistical uncertainty (p=0.10). This means:
- The observed improvement is unlikely to be due to chance alone
- We cannot claim absolute certainty, but the evidence favors adoption
- The growing effect over time strengthens confidence in the finding

---

## **Recommendation**

**Approve system-wide rollout of the new procedure.**

The analysis confirms the expected improvement in service scores. While statistical certainty is not absolute, the positive direction, increasing magnitude over time, and absence of negative signals support moving forward.
