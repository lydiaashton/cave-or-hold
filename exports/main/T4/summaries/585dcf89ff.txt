# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact Analysis**

## **Bottom Line**

**Contrary to expectations, the new shelving and rotation procedure produced a measurable improvement in service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.0 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.2 to 2.8 points) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents about a **4% improvement** relative to the baseline score of 50.

While the procedure change was not expected to affect service scores, the data shows a clear, statistically significant positive effect.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches, with monitoring to confirm the effect holds at scale.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **150 branches** total: 90 adopted the new procedure (treatment group), 60 kept the old procedure (control group)
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring in week 15
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in treatment branches to changes in control branches

### Results Summary

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 50.59 | 51.02 | +0.43 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 50.26 | 52.62 | +2.36 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+1.98** |

### Statistical Evidence
- **Estimated effect:** +1.98 service score points
- **95% confidence interval:** 1.19 to 2.77 points
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001 (highly significant)
- The effect remains significant after controlling for:
  - Branch-specific characteristics (fixed effects)
  - Week-to-week variation (time fixed effects)
  - Staffing levels and visitor volume

### Validity Checks
- **Parallel trends assumption confirmed:** Before the switch, treatment and control branches had statistically similar service scores (p = 0.56), indicating the groups were comparable
- **Pre-treatment gaps fluctuated around zero**, while **post-treatment gaps were consistently positive**, matching the expected pattern if the new procedure caused the improvement

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new shelving and rotation procedure to all 150 branches

2. **Monitor implementation closely** during the rollout:
   - Track service scores to confirm the ~2-point improvement materializes across all branches
   - Since this effect was unexpected, verify the result holds as more branches adopt the procedure
   - Watch for any branches that may need additional training or support

3. **Document and investigate the mechanism:**
   - Consider gathering qualitative feedback from staff on why the new procedure may be improving service
   - Understanding the "why" could help optimize other operations

4. **Schedule follow-up evaluation** in 3–6 months to:
   - Confirm sustained improvements
   - Identify any branches underperforming relative to expectations
   - Assess whether additional refinements to the procedure are warranted
