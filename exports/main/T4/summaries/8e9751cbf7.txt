# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Recommend the new procedure for system-wide rollout.** Branches that adopted the new procedure showed a statistically significant improvement in service scores of approximately **2.1 points** (about 4% above baseline) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is robust, persists after controlling for staffing and visitor volume, and shows no signs of being driven by pre-existing differences between branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **150 branches** participated in the evaluation (90 switched to new procedure, 60 kept old procedure as control)
- **17 weeks** of data collected (Weeks 6-22)
- New procedure implemented at **Week 15**
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in the treatment group to changes in the control group

### **Service Score Impact**

| Group | Pre-Switch Average | Post-Switch Average | Change |
|-------|-------------------|---------------------|--------|
| Control (Old Procedure) | 48.92 | 48.59 | -0.34 |
| Treatment (New Procedure) | 48.64 | 50.40 | +1.76 |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | | | **+2.10** |

### **Statistical Confidence**
- **Effect size:** +2.10 points on service score index
- **95% Confidence Interval:** +0.84 to +3.36 points
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.01 (highly significant)
- **Relative improvement:** ~4% increase from baseline (~50 points)

### **Validation Checks**
1. **Pre-trend analysis:** Before Week 15, treatment and control branches followed parallel trends with no significant differences (p = 0.76). This supports the causal interpretation that the procedure change—not pre-existing branch characteristics—drove the improvement.

2. **Covariate controls:** The effect remains significant (+1.99 points, p < 0.01) after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume, indicating the improvement is not simply due to better-staffed or busier branches adopting the new procedure.

3. **Event study pattern:** The treatment-control gap was near zero before Week 15, then widened consistently after implementation, with the treatment group outperforming in every post-switch week.

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new stock rotation and shelving procedure to all 60 branches currently using the old procedure.

2. **Develop implementation timeline** with consideration for:
   - Staff training requirements
   - Phased rollout to manage resource constraints
   - Communication plan for branch managers

3. **Continue monitoring** service scores during and after rollout to:
   - Confirm the effect persists at scale
   - Identify any branches that may need additional support
   - Detect any unintended consequences

4. **Document best practices** from the 90 early-adopter branches to support training and implementation at remaining branches.

5. **Consider cost-benefit analysis** to quantify the operational costs of the new procedure against the service quality improvements documented here.

---

## **Analytical Notes**

- All statistical models use cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch) to account for repeated observations within branches
- The difference-in-differences estimator isolates the causal effect of the procedure change by netting out common time trends affecting all branches
- Service score index has a system average of approximately 50; higher scores indicate better service
