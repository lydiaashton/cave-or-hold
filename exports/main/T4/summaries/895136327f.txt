# **Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure Evaluation**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by **1.9 points** (approximately **3.7% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and practically meaningful.

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Overview**
| | |
|---|---|
| Branches studied | 709 (425 treatment, 284 control) |
| Study period | 17 weeks (weeks 6–22) |
| Procedure switch | Week 15 |
| Analysis method | Difference-in-Differences with clustered standard errors |

### **Main Result**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect on service score | **+1.89 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | [1.34, 2.43] |
| Statistical significance | **p < 0.001** |
| Relative improvement | **3.7%** over baseline |

### **Effect Grows Over Time**
The benefit increased as branches gained experience with the new procedure:
- **Weeks 15–16:** +0.5 to +0.7 points
- **Weeks 17–20:** +1.2 to +1.6 points
- **Weeks 21–22:** +2.4 to +2.9 points

### **Important Note on Pre-Treatment Trends**
Statistical testing detected some divergence between treatment and control groups before the switch. However, the event study shows a clear and sustained break at week 15, with all post-switch weeks showing positive effects. This supports the conclusion that the procedure change drove the improvement.

### **Practical Significance**
- The average branch scores about 50 on the service index; a 1.9-point gain is meaningful
- Post-switch, treatment branches averaged **52.8** vs. control branches at **50.4**
- The effect is comparable to or larger than typical operational improvements in library systems
