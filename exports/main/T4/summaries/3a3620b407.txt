# **Library Cooperative Procedure Change: Impact Analysis**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new shelving and rotation procedure significantly improved service scores.** Contrary to the expectation that the change would make no difference, branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **+2.1 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This result is statistically significant (p = 0.001), and we recommend proceeding with a full rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **150 branches** total: 90 adopted the new procedure, 60 kept the old procedure (control group)
- **17 weeks of data**: 9 weeks before the switch (weeks 6–14), 8 weeks after (weeks 15–22)
- Analysis used a difference-in-differences approach with standard errors clustered by branch

### Results

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 49.55 | 48.87 | −0.68 |
| Switched (new procedure) | 49.36 | 50.78 | +1.42 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+2.10** |

- **Estimated effect: +2.10 points**
- **95% Confidence Interval: 0.83 to 3.38**
- **P-value: 0.001** (significant at the 1% level)
- **Practical significance: 4.3% improvement** from baseline service scores

### Interpretation
- Branches using the new procedure improved their service scores while control branches experienced a slight decline
- The 2.1-point improvement is both statistically reliable and practically meaningful
- The effect was consistent across all 8 post-switch weeks
- Other operational factors (staffing levels, visit volumes) did not differ systematically between groups, supporting the conclusion that the procedure change itself drove the improvement

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with full rollout** to all 150 branches. Expected system-wide improvement: approximately 2 points on the service score index.

2. **Monitor implementation** during rollout to ensure the effect replicates at scale. Consider phasing to allow for operational learning.

3. **Document best practices** from the 90 early-adopter branches to help others maximize benefits.

4. **Continue evaluation** with additional post-rollout data to assess long-term persistence and identify branches needing support.

5. **Complete cost-benefit analysis** if the new procedure involves additional costs, weighing them against the value of a 4% service score improvement.
