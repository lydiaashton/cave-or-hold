# **Board Summary: Impact of New Branch Rotation Procedure on Service Scores**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by **1.8 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents a **3.6% improvement**, is statistically significant (p < 0.001), and supports rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

| Finding | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Treatment Effect** | +1.82 points (95% CI: 1.27 to 2.36) |
| **Statistical Significance** | p < 0.001 (highly significant) |
| **Practical Impact** | 3.6% improvement over baseline |
| **Sample Size** | 709 branches, 17 weeks, 12,053 observations |
| **Analysis Method** | Difference-in-differences with controls for staffing and visits |

**What the numbers mean:**
- Switched branches improved from 50.7 to 53.5 (after the change)
- Non-switched branches improved from 49.1 to 50.0 (same period)
- The *additional* improvement attributable to the new procedure: **1.8 points**

---

## **Caveats**

1. **Pre-existing differences:** Switched branches had slightly higher scores before the change. The analysis accounts for this, but the true effect may be modestly smaller.

2. **Limited time horizon:** Only 8 weeks of post-switch data are available. Long-term sustainability of the effect is unknown.

3. **Implementation variation:** Effects may vary by branch depending on how faithfully the new procedure was adopted.

---
