# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

**Prepared for the Board of Directors**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new shelving and stock rotation procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw their service scores increase by **2.4 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents approximately a **5% improvement** on the service score index. The result is statistically reliable and sustained over the 8-week observation period.

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Clear Positive Effect on Service Quality

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched Branches (New Procedure) | 50.1 | 53.2 | +3.1 |
| Non-Switched Branches (Old Procedure) | 48.7 | 49.4 | +0.8 |
| **Net Effect of New Procedure** | | | **+2.4** |

The 2.4-point net improvement isolates the effect of the procedure change from other factors that affected all branches (such as seasonal patterns or system-wide initiatives).

### 2. Results Are Statistically Reliable

- **Confidence Interval:** We are 95% confident the true effect lies between **1.8 and 3.0 points**
- **Statistical Significance:** The probability this result occurred by chance is less than 1 in 1,000
- **Robustness:** The finding holds after accounting for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume

### 3. Trends Support Causal Interpretation

In the 9 weeks before the switch, both groups of branches moved in parallel—neither group was systematically improving faster than the other. The divergence began precisely when the new procedure was implemented (week 15), strengthening confidence that the procedure change caused the improvement.

### 4. Improvement Was Sustained

The gains did not fade over time. Service scores for switched branches remained elevated throughout all 8 weeks of the post-switch period, suggesting the benefit is durable rather than temporary.

---

## **Recommendations**

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| **1** | **Approve system-wide rollout** to all remaining branches | Clear evidence of service improvement with no identified downsides |
| **2** | **Develop implementation guidelines** to ensure consistent adoption | Variation in execution could reduce expected benefits |
| **3** | **Monitor scores during rollout** to confirm similar gains across all branches | Early detection of implementation issues allows course correction |
| **4** | **Gather staff and patron feedback** to understand why the new procedure works | Insights can inform future operational improvements |

---

## **About This Analysis**

- **Data:** 12,053 weekly observations from 709 branches over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- **Method:** Difference-in-Differences regression, the standard approach for evaluating policy changes when some units adopt earlier than others
- **Switch Date:** Week 15 (419 branches adopted the new procedure; 290 retained the old procedure)
