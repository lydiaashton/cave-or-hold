# **Board Memo: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores—an unexpected but welcome finding.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **2.4 points** (approximately **5% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. While this outcome was not anticipated, the effect is statistically significant and consistent. We recommend proceeding with the rollout, with careful monitoring to confirm the result holds at scale.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect
- The new procedure increased service scores by **2.37 points** on average (95% confidence interval: 1.07 to 3.68 points)
- This represents approximately a **5% improvement** relative to the average branch score of 48.5
- The result is highly statistically significant (p < 0.001)

### 2. Unexpected Nature of the Result
The cooperative did not anticipate service score changes from this operational adjustment. The positive effect suggests the new procedure may have unintended benefits—perhaps through improved staff workflow, faster item retrieval, or better patron experience. This warrants further investigation.

### 3. Robustness
The finding holds across multiple analytical approaches:
- **Basic model:** +2.37 points
- **With staffing and visitor controls:** +2.35 points
- **With branch fixed effects:** +2.38 points

### 4. Validity of the Analysis
- **Parallel trends assumption supported:** Before the switch (weeks 6-14), treatment and control branches showed no systematic difference in their service score trends (joint pre-treatment test: p = 0.35)
- **Consistent post-treatment effects:** Every week after the switch (weeks 15-22) showed positive treatment effects, ranging from +2.4 to +4.6 points
- **Large sample:** 150 branches (90 treatment, 60 control) observed over 17 weeks (2,550 total observations)

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** to the remaining 60 branches—the data supports adoption

2. **Monitor closely during implementation** given the unexpected nature of the finding; track whether the effect replicates at scale

3. **Investigate the mechanism** — conduct qualitative follow-up with treatment branches to understand *why* service scores improved (staff feedback, patron comments, workflow observations)

4. **Document operational changes** that may explain the result for future procedure design

5. **Continue tracking service scores** for at least 8-12 weeks post-rollout to confirm persistence of the effect

---

## **Technical Notes**

Analysis used a difference-in-differences design with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch). An event study analysis confirms no pre-treatment differences and shows the treatment effect emerged immediately after week 14 and remained positive throughout the observation period.
