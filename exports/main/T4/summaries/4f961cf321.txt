# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

## **Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

---

### **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure improved service scores by approximately 2 points (4% relative improvement), contrary to the expectation of no effect.** This improvement is statistically significant and consistent across analytical approaches. We recommend proceeding with system-wide rollout.

---

### **KEY FINDINGS**

**1. Treatment Effect**
- Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores **1.98 points higher** than would be expected based on control branch trends
- This effect is statistically significant (p < 0.01)
- The result holds when controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

**2. Context Matters**
- System-wide, service scores declined after week 14 (control branches dropped 2.4 points on average)
- Switched branches were more resilient, declining only 0.4 points
- The new procedure appears to have **protected branches from a broader service score decline**

**3. Validity Checks**
- **Pre-treatment trends were parallel**: Before the switch, treatment and control branches followed similar trajectories (p = 0.29 for trend difference)
- **Effect is robust**: Results unchanged when accounting for staffing and visit volume
- **Consistent impact**: 49% of switched branches improved vs. only 27% of control branches

**4. Effect Size**
- +1.98 points represents approximately **4% improvement** relative to baseline service scores (~50)
- Given the average branch scores around 50, this is a meaningful operational improvement

---

### **NEXT STEPS**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all 60 branches currently using the old method

2. **Monitor implementation** over the next 8-12 weeks to confirm effects persist at scale

3. **Investigate why** the new procedure buffered against the system-wide score decline—this protective effect may reveal additional operational insights

4. **Consider timing**: Since scores declined system-wide after week 14, investigate whether seasonal factors or other changes occurred that the new procedure helped mitigate

---

### **METHODOLOGY NOTE**

Analysis used a difference-in-differences design comparing 90 switched branches to 60 control branches over 17 weeks (weeks 6-22). Standard errors were clustered at the branch level to account for repeated observations. The parallel trends assumption was tested and supported.
