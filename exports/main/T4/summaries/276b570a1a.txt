# **Library Branch Stock Rotation Procedure: Impact Analysis**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new stock rotation procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure gained an additional **1.7 points** on the service score index compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents a **3.3% improvement** and is statistically significant. The effect grows over time, suggesting branches continue to benefit as they gain experience with the new system.

**Recommendation: Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Clear Performance Improvement
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched Branches | 51.8 | 55.6 | +3.8 |
| Non-Switched Branches | 50.4 | 52.5 | +2.0 |
| **Net Effect of New Procedure** | | | **+1.7** |

- The 1.7-point improvement is **highly statistically significant** (p < 0.001)
- Results are robust across multiple analytical approaches

### 2. Benefits Accumulate Over Time
The improvement is not immediate—it grows as branches gain experience:
- **Weeks 1-2 after switch:** ~1.0 point improvement
- **Weeks 7-8 after switch:** ~2.4 points improvement

This pattern indicates the procedure delivers increasing value as staff become proficient.

### 3. Effect Is Independent of Other Factors
The improvement cannot be explained by:
- Changes in staffing levels
- Changes in visitor volumes
- General time trends (both groups improved; switched branches improved more)

### 4. Implementation Note
Branches that volunteered to switch were already higher-performing, suggesting **motivated branches adopted the change first**. A system-wide rollout should include:
- Clear implementation guidelines
- Staff training to ensure consistent adoption
- Support for lower-performing branches to realize full benefits
