# **Board Summary: Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

Contrary to our initial expectation that the new procedure would make no difference, **the new rotation and shelving procedure significantly improved service scores**. Branches that adopted the new procedure saw their service scores increase by approximately **2.1 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant (p = 0.001) and represents a meaningful improvement in branch performance.

**Recommendation: Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **60 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Analysis compares changes in treatment branches to changes in control branches (difference-in-differences)

### Results

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect of new procedure | **+2.12 points** |
| Statistical significance | **p = 0.001** |
| 95% Confidence interval | **+0.86 to +3.38 points** |
| Effect size | **0.21 standard deviations** |

### What This Means in Practice

| Group | Post-Switch Average Score |
|-------|---------------------------|
| Control branches (old procedure) | 47.4 |
| Treatment branches (new procedure) | 51.1 |
| **Attributable to new procedure** | **+2.1** |

The new procedure accounts for roughly **4% improvement** over the average service score of 50.

### Validity Checks

- **Parallel trends supported**: Before the switch, treatment and control branches maintained a stable gap of ~1.7 points. After the switch, the gap widened to ~3.7 points—the additional 2 points aligns with our estimated effect.

- **Robust across specifications**: The finding holds whether we control for staffing levels, visitor volume, or neither.

- **No pre-trend concerns**: The effect appears only after week 15, when the new procedure was implemented.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all 150 branches

2. **Monitor implementation** to ensure branches follow the new procedure correctly

3. **Continue tracking service scores** to confirm the effect persists at scale

4. **Investigate mechanisms** through follow-up with branch staff to understand why the new procedure improved scores

5. **Document and share best practices** to support smooth adoption across all branches

## **Technical Notes**

- Analysis uses cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch)
- Service scores average ~50; higher scores indicate better service
- Results control for weekly staffing and visitor volume
- Data source: branches.csv (2,550 observations)
