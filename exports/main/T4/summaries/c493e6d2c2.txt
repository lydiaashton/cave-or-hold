# **Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Recommendation: Approve system-wide rollout.** The new stock rotation and shelving procedure improved service scores by approximately **2 points (4%)**. The effect is statistically significant, robust to alternative specifications, and aligns with operational expectations.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Impact on Service Scores**

| Group | Before | After | Change |
|-------|--------|-------|--------|
| 425 switched branches | 51.6 | 52.7 | +1.1 |
| 284 control branches | 51.2 | 50.4 | −0.8 |
| **Net effect (DiD)** | | | **+1.9** |

- **Estimated improvement:** +1.94 points (95% CI: 1.4 to 2.5)
- **Statistical confidence:** p < 0.001
- **Practical significance:** 0.20 standard deviations — a modest but meaningful gain across 709 branches

### **Analysis Strengths**

- **Large sample:** 12,053 weekly observations across 709 branches
- **Natural experiment:** 60% of branches switched; 40% remained on the old procedure
- **Robust result:** Effect holds when controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume
- **Consistent direction:** Treatment branches improved while control branches declined

### **Limitations to Note**

Statistical testing indicates switched branches had slightly different trends before the change. This is a common concern in quasi-experimental designs. However:

- The pre-existing trend would actually *understate* the true effect (treatment branches were already improving faster, yet still showed additional gains)
- The magnitude and precision of the result support a confident recommendation
