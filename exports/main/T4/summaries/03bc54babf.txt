# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure **did not produce a statistically significant improvement** in service scores. Switched branches maintained their scores while non-switched branches declined, resulting in an estimated **1.9-point relative benefit**. However, this effect could plausibly be zero (95% confidence interval: -0.6 to +4.5 points). We **cannot confirm** the procedure improved service scores as anticipated.

**Recommendation:** Do not proceed with system-wide rollout at this time. Instead, extend the evaluation period and gather more data before making a final decision.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Service Score Performance**

| Group | Before (Weeks 6-14) | After (Weeks 15-22) | Change |
|-------|--------------------|--------------------|--------|
| Switched Branches (20) | 51.4 | 51.3 | -0.1 |
| Non-Switched Branches (14) | 49.3 | 47.2 | -2.1 |

### **Statistical Results**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated Effect | +1.93 points |
| 95% Confidence Interval | -0.63 to +4.49 points |
| P-value | 0.14 |
| Statistical Significance | **Not significant** (p > 0.05) |

### **Interpretation**

1. **No clear improvement detected:** While switched branches performed better relative to control branches, the evidence is not strong enough to conclude the procedure caused a real improvement.

2. **Control group declined:** Non-switched branches experienced a 2.1-point drop in service scores after week 15. This decline was statistically significant and may reflect seasonal factors or other external influences.

3. **Treatment group held steady:** Switched branches maintained their service levels, suggesting the procedure may have helped buffer against the decline seen in control branches.

4. **More data needed:** With only 8 weeks of post-switch data across 34 branches, the analysis lacks power to detect small-to-moderate effects with confidence.

---

## **Why Results Differ from Expectations**

Several factors may explain why the anticipated improvement was not clearly observed:

- **Insufficient time:** Branches may need more than 8 weeks to fully realize benefits from the new procedure
- **Implementation variation:** Not all switched branches may have adopted the procedure uniformly
- **External factors:** System-wide pressures (seasonal demand, staffing changes) may have masked positive effects
- **Effect size smaller than expected:** The true benefit may be modest rather than substantial

---

## **Next Steps**

| Action | Timeline | Owner |
|--------|----------|-------|
| **Pause system-wide rollout** | Immediate | Operations |
| **Continue monitoring current switched branches** | Ongoing | Data Team |
| **Collect 12-16 additional weeks of data** | Next 4 months | Data Team |
| **Audit implementation fidelity at switched branches** | 30 days | Operations |
| **Re-evaluate with expanded dataset** | Q2 2027 | Board & Data Team |

### **Decision Criteria for Future Rollout**

Proceed with system-wide adoption if future analysis shows:
- Statistically significant positive effect (p < 0.05), OR
- Consistent positive effect across multiple evaluation periods, OR
- Clear operational benefits (cost savings, efficiency gains) that justify adoption even without score improvements

---

## **Conclusion**

The new procedure shows **promise but not proof**. Switched branches maintained service scores during a period when control branches declined, which is a positive signal. However, the data does not support a confident conclusion that the procedure improves service scores. A measured approach—continuing the pilot while gathering more evidence—protects the cooperative from premature rollout while keeping the option open if benefits materialize with time.
