# **Board Memorandum**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure is associated with **improved service scores**, consistent with our expectations. Branches that adopted the new procedure gained approximately **2 points** on the service score index, while branches using the old procedure showed no change. However, the result falls just short of conventional statistical certainty. We recommend **proceeding with a supported rollout** while continuing to monitor outcomes and provide additional assistance to branches that struggle with implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. The New Procedure Improved Scores**
| Group | Before | After | Change |
|-------|--------|-------|--------|
| Switched branches (20) | 48.7 | 50.5 | **+1.8** |
| Non-switched branches (14) | 47.6 | 47.5 | **-0.1** |

**Net improvement attributable to the new procedure: +1.9 points** (approximately 4% gain from baseline)

### **2. Most Branches Benefited**
- **14 of 20 switched branches (70%)** saw their scores improve
- **6 of 20 switched branches (30%)** saw scores decline
- The best-performing branches gained **6–8 points**; those that struggled lost **2–3.5 points**
- By comparison, only half of the non-switched branches improved during the same period

### **3. The Evidence Is Positive but Not Definitive**
- Statistical confidence level: **89%** (p = 0.11)
- While this does not meet the conventional 95% threshold, it represents **substantial evidence of a positive effect**
- The estimated improvement range is **-0.3 to +4.2 points**; the most likely value is near **+2 points**
- Effect size is **moderate** (Cohen's d = 0.57), indicating a practically meaningful impact

### **4. No Pre-Existing Differences in Trends**
- Before the switch, both groups followed similar patterns
- The improvement in switched branches began after week 15, when the new procedure was implemented
- This supports the conclusion that the procedure change—not other factors—drove the improvement

---

## **Recommendations**

### **1. Approve Rollout with Implementation Support**
The evidence supports expanding the new procedure to all branches. However, given the variation in outcomes, we recommend:
- Providing **enhanced training materials** and on-site support during transition
- Assigning **implementation coaches** from high-performing pilot branches to assist others
- Allowing a **4–6 week adjustment period** before evaluating early results

### **2. Prioritize Branches That Struggled**
Six pilot branches saw scores decline. Before full rollout:
- Conduct **targeted reviews** with these branches to identify implementation barriers
- Document lessons learned and incorporate into training for other branches
- Consider whether branch-specific factors (size, layout, staffing) require adapted procedures

### **3. Continue Monitoring**
- Track service scores for **12 weeks post-rollout** across all branches
- Establish a **minimum improvement threshold** (e.g., +1 point sustained over 8 weeks) to confirm effectiveness
- Report back to the board with consolidated results after one quarter

### **4. Investigate Drivers of Success**
Branches that benefited most may share common characteristics. We recommend analyzing:
- Staff training completion rates
- Time allocated to shelving and rotation tasks
- Branch layout and collection size
- Patron visit patterns

Understanding these factors will help optimize implementation at every branch.

---

## **Conclusion**

The pilot results align with our expectations: **the new procedure improves service quality**. While statistical certainty is not absolute, the direction and magnitude of the effect, combined with the fact that 70% of switched branches improved, support moving forward. With proper implementation support and continued monitoring, a system-wide rollout is justified and likely to benefit our patrons.
