# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

**Date:** November 15, 2026

---

## **Bottom Line**

**We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

Branches that adopted the new stock rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores improve by approximately **2 points** (a **4% gain**). The improvement is statistically reliable, held up across multiple analytical tests, and came with no apparent downsides. With 425 branches already using the procedure successfully, we are confident extending it system-wide.

---

## **What We Did**

When the new procedure launched in week 15, some branches switched while others kept the old approach. This gave us a natural experiment:

| Group | Branches | Before Switch | After Switch |
|-------|----------|---------------|--------------|
| New procedure | 425 | 51.0 | 52.5 |
| Old procedure | 284 | 49.9 | 49.4 |

By comparing how each group changed, we isolated the effect of the new procedure from other factors (like seasonal fluctuations or branch-specific characteristics).

---

## **What We Found**

| Finding | Details |
|---------|---------|
| **Service score improvement** | +2.0 points (95% confidence: +1.5 to +2.6) |
| **Statistical confidence** | p < 0.001 (less than 0.1% chance this is random) |
| **Relative gain** | ~4% improvement over baseline |
| **Consistency** | Effect held across all model specifications |
| **Controls** | Effect persists after accounting for staffing and visitor levels |

The improvement is modest but meaningful. On a 50-point scale, a 2-point gain represents real progress in member experience.

---

## **Important Context**

**What gives us confidence:**
- Large sample (709 branches, 12,000+ observations)
- Clean research design (treatment vs. control groups)
- Effect is statistically robust
- Results consistent across different analytical approaches

**What to keep in mind:**
- Week-to-week scores naturally fluctuate; the 2-point effect is an average
- We have only 8 weeks of post-switch data; long-term effects may differ
- Treatment branches started slightly ahead of control branches, but our analysis accounts for this
