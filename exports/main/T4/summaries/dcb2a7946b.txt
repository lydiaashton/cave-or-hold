# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

## **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

### **Bottom Line**

Branches that adopted the new procedure showed **significantly higher service scores** after implementation. The estimated improvement is **1.8 points** on the service score index (approximately 3-4% relative to the average score of 50). However, because branches that switched were already improving faster than others before the change, this estimate may be optimistic. We recommend a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than immediate system-wide implementation.

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. Measured Impact**
- Branches using the new procedure scored **1.82 points higher** than expected after accounting for control branch trends
- 95% confidence interval: **1.29 to 2.35 points**
- Statistically significant (p < 0.001)

**2. Raw Score Comparison**
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 49.16 | 51.14 | +1.98 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 49.67 | 53.49 | +3.82 |

**3. Study Scope**
- 709 branches total (425 adopted new procedure; 284 kept old procedure)
- 17 weeks of data (weeks 6-22)
- Switch occurred at week 15
- Analysis controlled for staffing levels and visitor volume

**4. Critical Limitation**
- Treatment branches were **already improving faster** than control branches before the switch
- The gap between groups grew from -0.75 points (week 6) to +1.44 points (week 14) during the pre-switch period
- This suggests the **1.82-point estimate likely overstates the true effect**
- The actual benefit may be smaller, though still positive

---

### **Next Steps**

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| **1** | Proceed with phased rollout | Evidence supports positive effect, but uncertainty warrants caution |
| **2** | Expand in waves, not all at once | Creates new comparison groups to validate the effect |
| **3** | Continue monitoring all branches | Track whether improvements are sustained over time |
| **4** | Interview branch staff | Understand *why* the procedure works to optimize implementation |
| **5** | Consider randomized pilot for next phase | Would eliminate pre-trend concerns and provide cleaner estimates |

---

### **Recommendation**

**Approve a phased expansion** of the new procedure with the following conditions:

1. Roll out to additional branches in 2-3 waves over the next quarter
2. Establish monthly score tracking with predefined success thresholds
3. Allocate resources for staff feedback collection during implementation
4. Return to the Board in 90 days with updated evidence before committing to system-wide adoption

---

### **Conclusion**

The new rotation and shelving procedure is associated with improved service scores, and the evidence supports moving forward with broader implementation. However, the pre-existing differences between adopting and non-adopting branches mean we should temper expectations and build in robust monitoring. A phased, data-driven rollout will allow us to confirm the benefits while managing risk.
