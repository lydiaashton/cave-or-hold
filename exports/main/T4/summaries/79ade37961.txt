# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure: Impact on Service Scores**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Contrary to the initial expectation that the change would make no difference, branches that adopted the new procedure saw their service scores increase by approximately **2.0 points** (about 4% relative to the average score of 51.9) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is **statistically significant** (p = 0.002).

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
- **150 branches** participated: 90 adopted the new procedure, 60 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Analysis compared changes in service scores between the two groups before and after the switch

### Impact on Service Scores

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect on service score | **+2.03 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | [0.77, 3.28] |
| Statistical significance | p = 0.002 |
| Effect size (relative to average) | +3.9% |

The entire confidence interval is positive, indicating a genuine improvement rather than random variation.

### What the Data Shows

| Period | Control Branches (old procedure) | Treatment Branches (new procedure) |
|--------|----------------------------------|-----------------------------------|
| Before switch (weeks 6–14) | 51.58 | 53.07 |
| After switch (weeks 15–22) | 49.08 | 52.57 |
| **Change** | **-2.50** | **-0.50** |

Both groups experienced a decline in scores after week 14 (likely due to seasonal factors affecting all branches), but treatment branches declined **much less** than control branches. The new procedure appears to have buffered branches against this decline.

### Analysis Confidence
- Results account for staffing levels and visitor volume
- Statistical methods properly handle repeated measurements from the same branches
- The positive effect is consistent across the post-switch period

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches.

2. **Monitor implementation** during the rollout. Track service scores for the first 8–12 weeks after each branch switches to verify similar gains are achieved.

3. **Document best practices** from early-adopter branches to support training and smooth implementation at remaining branches.

4. **Continue data collection** on service scores to verify the effect persists over time and does not diminish as branches become accustomed to the new procedure.

5. **Consider a follow-up review** in 6 months to assess long-term impact and identify any additional optimization opportunities.
