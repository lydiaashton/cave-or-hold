# **Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**
Contrary to our initial expectations, the new procedure is associated with a **statistically significant improvement of 1.9 points** in service scores. While we anticipated no measurable effect on service quality, branches that adopted the new procedure maintained their performance while non-adopting branches experienced a notable decline. **We recommend proceeding with system-wide rollout**, with continued monitoring to confirm these unexpected benefits.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Study Design
- **150 branches total**: 90 adopted the new procedure, 60 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6-22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Analysis uses **difference-in-differences methodology** with branch and week fixed effects

### 2. Service Score Impact
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated treatment effect | **+1.89 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | 0.76 to 3.02 |
| Statistical significance | **p < 0.01** |
| Relative improvement | ~3.8% above baseline |

### 3. What Happened to Each Group

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| **Switched Branches** (Treatment) | 50.54 | 50.37 | -0.17 (stable) |
| **Non-Switched Branches** (Control) | 49.85 | 47.79 | -2.06 (decline) |

**Key Insight**: The new procedure appears to have **protected** branches from a system-wide decline that affected non-switched branches during this period.

### 4. Why This Matters
We expected the procedure change to be operationally neutral regarding service scores. The data suggest otherwise:
- The effect is **statistically robust** across multiple model specifications
- The benefit is **practically meaningful** (~2 points on a 50-point scale)
- The mechanism appears to be **preventive**—maintaining scores rather than boosting them

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **PROCEED WITH ROLLOUT**: The evidence supports expanding the new procedure to all branches. The unexpected service score benefit strengthens the business case.

2. **DOCUMENT THE FINDING**: This outcome was not anticipated. Capture this as a positive unintended benefit of the operational change.

3. **INVESTIGATE THE MECHANISM**: Why did non-switched branches decline while switched branches held steady? Understanding this could inform future operational decisions.

4. **CONTINUE MONITORING**: Track service scores for 3-6 months post-rollout to confirm the effect persists at scale.

5. **REVISIT EXPECTATIONS**: Consider whether other operational changes may have unmeasured service impacts worth tracking.
