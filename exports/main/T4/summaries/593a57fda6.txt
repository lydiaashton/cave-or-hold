# **Board Memorandum**

## **New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure: Impact Assessment**

---

### **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure works.** Branches that switched show a **statistically significant +2 point improvement** in service scores compared to branches that kept the old procedure. We recommend **approving system-wide rollout** of the new procedure, with continued performance monitoring.

---

### **Key Findings**

| Metric | Result |
|--------|--------|
| **Service Score Impact** | +2.07 points (p < 0.001) |
| **Confidence Interval** | 1.91 to 2.23 points |
| **Relative Improvement** | 4% above baseline |
| **Branches Analyzed** | 709 (425 switched, 284 control) |
| **Study Period** | 17 weeks (Weeks 6–22) |

**What This Means:**
- The average branch scores around 50; the new procedure lifts scores by approximately 2 points
- The effect is **highly reliable**—the 95% confidence interval excludes zero by a wide margin
- Control branches actually declined slightly after Week 15 (−1.3 points), while switched branches improved (+0.7 points)
- The gap between groups **widened over time**, suggesting the benefit may grow as staff gain familiarity

**Controls Applied:**
Analysis accounted for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume, confirming the effect is attributable to the procedure change itself.

---

### **Considerations**

1. **Pre-Switch Trends:** Branches selected for early adoption showed slightly stronger trajectories before the switch. The true effect may be modestly smaller than estimated (conservative range: +0.5 to +2.0 points).

2. **Effect Size:** While statistically robust, a 2-point gain is **meaningful but not transformative**. Expectations should be calibrated accordingly.

3. **Sustainability:** The post-switch period covers only 8 weeks. Longer-term effects remain to be confirmed.

---

### **Next Steps**

| Action | Timeline | Owner |
|--------|----------|-------|
| **Approve system-wide rollout** | Immediate | Board |

---

### **Recommendation**

**Approve full rollout.** The evidence confirms the new procedure delivers the intended improvement in service quality. The statistical evidence is strong, the direction matches expectations, and the operational risk is low. Continued monitoring will ensure the effect persists at scale.
