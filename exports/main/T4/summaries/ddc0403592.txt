# **Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure is associated with a **2.2–2.5 point improvement in service scores** (approximately 4–5% above the baseline average of 50). This effect is **marginally statistically significant** (p ≈ 0.09) and appears consistent across the post-implementation period. The evidence supports a **cautious rollout** of the new procedure to all branches, with continued monitoring to confirm the effect persists at scale.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Study Design**
- **34 branches** analyzed over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure at week 15 (treatment group)
- **14 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in the treatment group to changes in the control group

### **2. Main Result**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 47.4 | 49.6 | +2.3 |
| Non-switched branches | 50.7 | 50.8 | +0.0 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+2.2** |

- The regression-adjusted estimate is **2.5 points** (95% CI: -0.4 to 5.3)
- This represents a meaningful improvement relative to the typical branch score of ~50

### **3. Parallel Trends Assumption Validated**
- Pre-switch trends (weeks 6–14) show **no statistically significant differences** between treatment and control groups
- This supports the validity of the difference-in-differences approach
- Both groups exhibited similar week-to-week variation before the switch

### **4. Effect Persists Over Time**
- Positive effects observed in **all 8 post-switch weeks**
- Effects range from +1.2 to +4.0 points across individual weeks
- No evidence of the effect fading over the observation period

### **5. Alternative Explanations Ruled Out**
- **Staffing levels**: No significant differential change between groups (p = 0.85)
- **Branch visits**: No significant differential change between groups (p = 0.16)
- This suggests the service score improvement is attributable to the procedure change itself, not to changes in staffing or foot traffic

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with cautious rollout** of the new procedure to remaining branches, given the positive (though not definitively conclusive) evidence

2. **Continue monitoring** service scores for at least 8–12 weeks post-rollout to:
   - Confirm the effect persists at scale
   - Identify any branches where the procedure underperforms

3. **Consider a phased implementation** to manage operational risk and allow for course correction

4. **Document best practices** from the 20 early-adopter branches to support training at remaining locations

5. **Re-evaluate after full rollout** with a full year of data to assess long-term impacts and cost-effectiveness

---

## **Caveats**

- The effect is **marginally significant** (p ≈ 0.09), meaning there is approximately a 9% chance the observed improvement could be due to random variation
- The confidence interval includes zero, so we cannot rule out no effect with high confidence
- The analysis covers only 8 weeks post-implementation; longer-term effects remain unknown
- Service score is an index measure; the practical meaning of a 2.5-point improvement should be validated against operational metrics

---

*Analysis prepared using difference-in-differences regression with branch-clustered standard errors. Data covers weeks 6–22, with procedure switch implemented at week 15.*
