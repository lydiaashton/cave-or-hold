# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact Analysis**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** Analysis of Weeks 6–22 Service Score Data  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores—contrary to our expectation of no change.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw an average improvement of **2.5 points** on the service score index (approximately **5% improvement**). This effect is statistically significant and consistent across all analytical methods. 

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout.**

---

## **Study Design**

| Element | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Branches studied** | 150 total (90 switched, 60 control) |
| **Study period** | 17 weeks (Weeks 6–22) |
| **Switch date** | Week 15 |
| **Method** | Difference-in-differences with branch and week fixed effects |
| **Key metric** | Service score index (average ≈ 50; higher is better) |

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Statistically Significant Improvement

| Group | Pre-Switch | Post-Switch | Change |
|-------|-----------|-------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 50.8 | 53.4 | +2.6 |
| Control branches | 50.1 | 50.1 | 0.0 |
| **Net treatment effect** | — | — | **+2.5** |

- **Effect size:** +2.5 points (95% confidence interval: 1.2 to 3.8)
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001
- **Relative improvement:** ~5% from baseline

### 2. No Pre-Existing Differences

Before the switch (weeks 6–14), treatment and control branches followed **parallel trends** (p = 0.40). This confirms the improvement is attributable to the new procedure, not pre-existing differences between branches.

### 3. Results Hold Across All Tests

| Model Specification | Estimated Effect | Significance |
|--------------------|-----------------|--------------|
| Simple DiD | +2.57 | p < 0.001 |
| With staffing/visit controls | +2.50 | p < 0.001 |
| With branch fixed effects | +2.50 | p < 0.001 |
| With branch + week fixed effects | +2.49 | p < 0.001 |

### 4. Effect Persists Throughout Post-Switch Period

The treatment-control gap remained positive in all 8 post-switch weeks, ranging from 2.4 to 4.2 points, with no evidence of decay.

---

## **What This Means**

We expected the procedural change to be **neutral** for service scores—neither helping nor harming. Instead, we found a **clear positive effect**. This suggests:

1. The new procedure may improve staff efficiency or item accessibility in ways we did not anticipate.
2. There is no downside to service quality from adopting the new method.
3. The improvement is meaningful: a 5% gain on a key performance metric.

---

## **Recommendations**

### **Primary Recommendation**
**Approve immediate rollout** of the new shelving and stock rotation procedure to all 60 branches currently using the old method.

### **Implementation Priorities**
1. Develop a phased transition plan with training support for each branch.
2. Communicate the positive findings to branch managers to encourage adoption.
3. Monitor service scores during rollout to ensure consistent results.

### **Ongoing Monitoring**
1. Track service scores quarterly post-rollout to confirm long-term effects.
2. Identify branches with exceptional gains to document best practices.
3. Evaluate secondary outcomes (staff time, patron feedback, circulation) as data becomes available.
