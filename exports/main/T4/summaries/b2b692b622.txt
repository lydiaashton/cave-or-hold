# **Memorandum to the Board of Directors**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Executive Summary**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure has produced a **positive but modest improvement** in branch service scores. Switched branches outperformed non-switched branches by an average of **1.75 points** (approximately 3% improvement) after implementation. While this effect is in the expected direction, the statistical evidence is **suggestive rather than definitive** (p = 0.10). We recommend a **phased expansion** with continued evaluation rather than an immediate system-wide rollout.

---

## **What We Did**

We analyzed weekly service scores from **34 branches** over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22). Twenty branches adopted the new procedure starting week 15; fourteen branches continued with the standard procedure, serving as a comparison group. This design allows us to isolate the effect of the new procedure from other factors affecting all branches.

---

## **What We Found**

### **Service Score Changes**

| | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Branches with New Procedure** | 50.26 | 56.37 | **+6.11** |
| **Branches with Old Procedure** | 50.60 | 54.96 | +4.36 |
| **Net Effect of New Procedure** | | | **+1.75** |

### **Key Takeaways**

1. **The new procedure helped, but less than expected.** The +1.75 point improvement is positive and meaningful for patrons, but smaller than anticipated.

2. **The evidence is not statistically conclusive.** There is approximately a 10% chance this result could occur even if the procedure had no real effect. This does not mean the procedure doesn't work—it means we need more data to be certain.

3. **All branches improved after week 15.** Both groups saw service score gains, suggesting favorable external factors (seasonal patterns, increased staffing, or other initiatives) benefited the entire cooperative.

4. **Pre-implementation trends were similar.** Branches that switched were on the same trajectory as those that didn't, validating our comparison approach.

---

## **Recommendations**

| Action | Rationale |
|---|---|
| **1. Expand to 10–15 additional branches** | A larger treatment group will provide clearer evidence while limiting risk if the effect is smaller than hoped. |
| **2. Maintain 10+ branches on the old procedure** | Preserving a control group enables continued rigorous evaluation. |
| **3. Re-assess in 6 months** | More data will either confirm the benefit or reveal the effect was due to chance. |
| **4. Gather staff feedback** | Frontline perspectives may reveal operational benefits not captured by service scores alone. |
| **5. Track implementation fidelity** | Ensure branches are applying the procedure consistently; variation may be masking the true effect. |

---

## **Bottom Line for Decision-Making**

The new procedure **has not harmed** service quality and shows **promising improvement**. However, the evidence is not yet strong enough to justify the cost and disruption of a full system-wide rollout. A **measured expansion with continued monitoring** balances the potential benefits against the uncertainty in the current results.
