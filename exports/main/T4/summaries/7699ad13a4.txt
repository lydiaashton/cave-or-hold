# **Memorandum to the Board**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure  
**Date:** November 15, 2026

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure works.** Branches that adopted the new stock rotation and shelving procedure achieved service scores **2.1 points higher** than branches that kept the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant, consistent across different staffing levels, and has grown stronger over time. We recommend immediate rollout to all remaining branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### The New Procedure Delivered Measurable Improvement

| Metric | Result |
|--------|--------|
| **Treatment effect** | +2.14 points on service score |
| **Confidence interval** | 1.6 to 2.7 points (95% confidence) |
| **Statistical significance** | p < 0.001 |
| **Relative improvement** | ~4% gain from baseline of ~50 |

### The Effect Is Real and Growing

The analysis compared 425 branches that switched to the new procedure against 284 branches that kept the old procedure. While service scores declined system-wide after week 14 (likely seasonal), branches with the new procedure declined **2.16 points less** than control branches.

The benefit accumulated over time:
- **Weeks 1-2 after switch:** +1.0 point average
- **Weeks 3-5 after switch:** +1.9 points average  
- **Weeks 6-8 after switch:** +3.1 points average

This pattern suggests staff proficiency with the new procedure improves with practice.

### The Effect Is Consistent

The improvement held across all staffing conditions:
- Low-staffed branches: **+2.1 points**
- Medium-staffed branches: **+2.1 points**
- High-staffed branches: **+2.3 points**

This indicates the procedure itself drives the gain, not staffing advantages.

### The Analysis Is Sound

- **Pre-trends check:** Switched and control branches had nearly identical service score trends in the 7 weeks before the switch (average difference: -0.13 points), validating our comparison.
- **Branch-level controls:** Results hold when accounting for fixed differences between individual branches.
- **Robust standard errors:** All confidence intervals account for repeated observations within branches.

---

## **Recommendation**

**Approve system-wide rollout of the new procedure to all 284 branches still using the old method.**
