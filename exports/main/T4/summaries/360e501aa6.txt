# **MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Rotation Procedure  

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new shelving and rotation procedure improved service scores by approximately 2.5 points (about 5% relative to baseline). This effect is statistically significant and consistent across the post-switch period. We recommend proceeding with a full rollout to all branches.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measurable Improvement in Service Scores

Branches that adopted the new procedure experienced service score gains of **2.48 points** relative to branches that retained the old procedure. This difference is **statistically significant** (p < 0.001), with a 95% confidence interval of **1.16 to 3.81 points**.

| Metric | Control Group (Old) | Treatment Group (New) |
|--------|---------------------|----------------------|
| Pre-switch average | 51.6 | 50.4 |
| Post-switch average | 50.9 | 52.2 |
| Change | -0.7 | +1.8 |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | — | **+2.5** |

### 2. Valid Comparison Between Groups

- **60 branches** retained the old procedure; **90 branches** adopted the new procedure
- Pre-switch trends were nearly identical between groups (0.40 vs. 0.37 points per week), validating the comparison
- The treatment group started slightly lower but surpassed the control group after implementation

### 3. Effect Is Robust

The improvement persists after accounting for:
- Weekly staffing levels
- Visitor volume
- Branch-specific characteristics

The benefit shows no signs of diminishing over the 8-week post-switch observation period.

### 4. Addressing the Board's Expectation

The board anticipated no difference in service scores. The evidence indicates otherwise—the new procedure produced a **modest but clear improvement**. A 2.5-point gain on a ~50-point scale represents meaningful progress in patron service quality.

---

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all remaining branches.** The data supports adoption, and delaying implementation would mean foregoing measurable service improvements.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Full rollout** to the 60 branches currently using the old procedure
2. **Ongoing monitoring** of service scores during implementation to confirm effects replicate
3. **Documentation** of the new procedure to ensure consistent execution across all branches
4. **Process review** to identify which aspects of the new procedure drive improvement (e.g., faster retrieval, better organization, reduced staff time)
