# **Memorandum to the Board**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2025  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure works.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure achieved service score improvements of **2.0–2.3 points** (approximately **4–5%**) relative to branches that kept the old procedure. The effect is statistically significant, consistent across analytical methods, and strongest for branches that needed it most. **We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Significant Improvement in Service Scores**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches (425) | 51.3 | 53.5 | **+2.3** |
| Non-switched branches (284) | 51.0 | 51.2 | +0.2 |
| **Net Effect (Difference-in-Differences)** | | | **+2.0 to +2.3** |

The statistical analysis confirms this effect is real and not due to random variation:
- **p-value < 0.001** (highly significant)
- **95% confidence interval: +1.9 to +2.6 points**

### **2. Results Hold Under Rigorous Testing**

We validated the finding using multiple approaches:
- Basic difference-in-differences regression
- Models controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume
- Branch fixed-effects models accounting for inherent branch differences

All methods produced consistent estimates around **+2.0 to +2.3 points**.

### **3. Pre-Trends Confirm Valid Comparison**

Before the switch (weeks 6–14), both groups followed parallel trends with no systematic divergence. This supports the conclusion that the post-switch improvement is attributable to the procedure change, not pre-existing differences.

### **4. Broad-Based Benefits**

- **73% of switched branches** saw their service scores improve
- Median improvement: **+2.2 points**
- Only 27% of switched branches saw no improvement or decline

### **5. Greatest Gains for Underperforming Branches**

The new procedure disproportionately helped branches that were struggling:

| Initial Performance | Average Improvement |
|---------------------|---------------------|
| Lowest quartile     | **+3.8 points**     |
| Second quartile     | +2.3 points         |
| Third quartile      | +1.6 points         |
| Highest quartile    | +1.3 points         |

This is strategically valuable: the procedure helps close performance gaps across the system.

## **Recommendations**

### **Immediate Actions**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all 284 branches still using the old method

2. **Prioritize implementation** at lower-performing branches first, where the impact will be greatest

3. **Develop implementation support** – training materials, checklists, and on-site assistance to ensure faithful adoption

### **Monitoring & Evaluation**

4. **Track service scores monthly** post-rollout to confirm effects persist at scale

5. **Identify and address outliers** – branches that don't improve may need additional support or may be experiencing implementation issues

6. **Document lessons learned** from the pilot branches to refine the rollout process

### **Strategic Considerations**

7. **Conduct cost-benefit analysis** – weigh the ~4–5% service improvement against any additional staffing, training, or operational costs

8. **Consider complementary initiatives** – since staffing and visitor volume also correlate with service scores, evaluate whether the new procedure works synergistically with other improvements

---

## **Methodology Notes**

- **Data:** 12,053 weekly observations from 709 branches (weeks 6–22)
- **Design:** Difference-in-differences with branch fixed effects
- **Standard errors:** Clustered at the branch level to account for repeated observations
- **Controls:** Staffing index and visits index (both scaled to zero mean)

The analysis meets standard criteria for causal inference in quasi-experimental settings.

---

## **Conclusion**

The evidence is clear and actionable. The new stock rotation and shelving procedure delivers meaningful, measurable improvements in service quality. The board can proceed with confidence in approving a system-wide rollout.
