# **Board Summary: New Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure Evaluation**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw their service scores increase by **1.8 points** (approximately **3.5% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and consistent across analytical approaches.

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Overview**
| | |
|---|---|
| **Branches studied** | 709 total (425 treatment, 284 control) |
| **Study period** | 17 weeks (Weeks 6–22) |
| **Switch date** | Week 15 |
| **Method** | Difference-in-Differences analysis |

### **Results**

| Group | Before | After | Change |
|-------|--------|-------|--------|
| Control (Old Procedure) | 51.2 | 52.0 | +0.8 |
| Treatment (New Procedure) | 51.5 | 54.0 | +2.6 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+1.8** |

- **Treatment effect:** +1.84 points (95% CI: 1.28 to 2.39)
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001
- **Effect size:** 3.5% improvement relative to baseline

### **What This Means**
- The improvement is **real and measurable**, not due to random variation
- The effect is **practically meaningful**—nearly 2 points on a scale where the average branch scores around 50
- Results hold after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume
- All branches had complete data, strengthening confidence in the findings
