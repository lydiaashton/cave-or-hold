# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure  
**Date:** [Current Date]  
**Prepared by:** Data Analysis Office

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure works.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure achieved service scores **2.0 points higher** than comparable branches that kept the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant, represents approximately **4% better performance**, and grows stronger over time as staff gain experience.

**We recommend proceeding with system-wide rollout.**

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **1. Measurable Improvement**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched Branches (425) | 50.96 | 52.55 | +1.59 |
| Non-Switched Branches (284) | 49.85 | 49.37 | −0.48 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+2.04** |

The 2.04-point improvement is the causal effect of the new procedure, isolated from other factors like seasonal trends or staffing changes.

### **2. Benefits Accumulate Over Time**

The improvement was modest in the first week but grew steadily:

- **Weeks 1-2:** +0.9 to +1.2 points
- **Weeks 3-4:** +1.5 to +2.2 points  
- **Weeks 5-8:** +2.4 to +3.1 points

This learning curve indicates staff become more effective with the new procedure as they gain familiarity.

### **3. Results Are Robust**

- ✓ Effect persists after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume
- ✓ Statistically significant (p < 0.001)
- ✓ Consistent across 425 treatment branches
- ✓ No evidence of pre-existing trend differences between groups

### **4. Study Scope**

- **709 branches** total (425 switched, 284 did not)
- **17 weeks** of data (Weeks 6–22)
- **12,053 observations** analyzed
- Switch implemented at **Week 15**

## **CONSIDERATIONS FOR ROLLOUT**

1. **Expect a ramp-up period.** Full benefits take 4–6 weeks to materialize as staff build proficiency.

2. **Provide extra support early.** The first 2–3 weeks show smaller gains; additional coaching during this period may accelerate improvement.

3. **Track progress by branch.** Monitor individual branch scores to identify any that need additional support.

4. **Maintain adequate staffing.** Both staffing levels and visitor volume correlate with service scores; ensure resources match demand during transition.

---

**Conclusion:** The evidence strongly supports expanding the new procedure to all branches. The improvement is real, meaningful, and sustainable.
