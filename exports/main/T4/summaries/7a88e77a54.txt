# **Board Summary: Impact of New Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new shelving and stock rotation procedure saw their service scores increase by approximately **2 points** relative to branches that kept the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant and consistent across the 90 branches that switched. 

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Clear Positive Effect
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches (90) | 51.4 | 52.6 | **+1.1** |
| Control branches (60) | 52.1 | 51.2 | **-0.9** |

**Net effect: +2.05 points** (p = 0.001)

### 2. The Evidence Is Strong
- **Statistically significant**: The probability this result occurred by chance is less than 1 in 1,000
- **Valid comparison**: Before the switch, both groups followed similar trends, confirming the control group is an appropriate benchmark
- **Broad-based improvement**: 61% of switched branches improved, versus only 40% of control branches
- **Holds up under scrutiny**: The result persists after accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume

### 3. What This Means in Practice
- A typical branch scoring 50 should expect to reach approximately **52** after switching
- The effect size (0.21 standard deviations) represents a small-to-moderate but meaningful improvement in service quality
- No branches experienced significant negative effects from the switch

## **Questions the Board May Have**

**Q: Why did some branches switch and others didn't?**
A: The initial rollout was phased. Our analysis confirms the non-switched branches are a valid comparison group.

**Q: Is a 2-point improvement worth the cost of rollout?**
A: The analysis does not include implementation costs. However, the procedure change appears to be primarily operational (shelving and rotation methods) rather than capital-intensive. Staff training and transition time are the main costs to consider.

**Q: Could the improvement be temporary?**
A: We only have 8 weeks of post-switch data. Continued monitoring is recommended to confirm the effect persists.

## **Appendix: Analysis Details**

- **Data**: 150 branches × 17 weeks = 2,550 observations
- **Method**: Difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors
- **Switch date**: Week 15 of the observation period
- **Confidence interval**: 0.8 to 3.3 points (95% confidence)
