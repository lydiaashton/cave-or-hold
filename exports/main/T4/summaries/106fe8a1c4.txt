# **Memorandum to the Board**

**Subject:** Impact of New Branch Procedure on Service Scores  
**Date:** November 15, 2026

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new stocking and shelving procedure **improved service scores by 2.3 points** (approximately 4.4%). This result was statistically significant and unexpected—our prior assumption was that the procedure change would have no measurable effect on service quality. Based on this evidence, **we recommend proceeding with system-wide rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### What We Studied
- **150 branches** over **17 weeks** (February through June)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure; **60 branches** continued with the old procedure
- The change was implemented at **week 15**
- We compared service score trends between the two groups before and after the switch

### What We Found

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|----------------|---------------|--------|
| Branches with new procedure | 52.15 | 52.30 | +0.15 |
| Branches with old procedure | 52.72 | 50.56 | −2.17 |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | | **+2.30** |

- **Effect size:** +2.30 points on the service score index (95% confidence interval: 1.0 to 3.6)
- **Statistical confidence:** p < 0.001 (less than 0.1% probability this result is due to chance)
- **Practical significance:** 4.4% improvement relative to baseline; equivalent to 0.24 standard deviations

### Why This Result Is Credible

1. **The groups were comparable beforehand.** Treatment and control branches showed parallel trends in the 9 weeks prior to the switch, with no systematic differences in how their scores were moving.

2. **The effect appears only after the switch.** Week-by-week analysis confirms the divergence between groups began at week 15, not before.

3. **The finding holds across multiple analytical approaches.** Whether we account for staffing levels, visitor volume, or branch-specific characteristics, the estimated effect remains stable at approximately 2.3 points.

4. **An important context:** Control branches experienced a notable decline after week 15 (−2.2 points). The new procedure appears to have **protected service quality** against factors that negatively affected branches using the old procedure.

---

## **Recommendations**

| Priority | Action |
|----------|--------|
| **1** | **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all 60 branches still using the old method |
| **2** | **Investigate the control group decline** to understand what external factors may have affected service scores after week 15 |
| **3** | **Monitor scores during rollout** to confirm effects replicate at full scale |
| **4** | **Document implementation costs** to complete a cost-benefit assessment |

---

## **Caveats**

- This analysis covers a 17-week period; long-term effects remain to be confirmed
- The control group's decline warrants investigation—it may reflect seasonal patterns or other external factors
- Service score is one metric; consider complementing with patron satisfaction surveys and operational cost data
