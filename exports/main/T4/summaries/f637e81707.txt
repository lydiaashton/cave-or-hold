# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Contrary to expectations, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.1 points** (about **4% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and consistent across most branches. We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Unexpected but Clear Improvement
- Branches using the new procedure improved by **2.4 points** on average after the switch
- Branches keeping the old procedure showed minimal change (**0.2 points**)
- The **net effect of the new procedure is +2.1 points** (p < 0.001)
- *This finding was not anticipated—the procedure change was not expected to affect service scores*

### 2. Effect Size and Practical Significance
- The improvement represents about **0.22 standard deviations**—a small-to-moderate but meaningful effect
- In percentage terms, this is a **4.2% improvement** over baseline scores
- Given that the average branch scores around 50, a 2-point gain is operationally meaningful

### 3. Consistency Across Branches
- **73% of switched branches** saw their scores improve after adoption
- Only **48% of control branches** improved during the same period (consistent with random variation)
- The effect held even after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### 4. Parallel Trends Validate the Analysis
- Before the switch (weeks 6–14), treatment and control branches tracked closely together
- After the switch (weeks 15–22), treatment branches pulled away with consistently higher scores
- This pattern supports the conclusion that the procedure change caused the improvement

### 5. Possible Explanations for the Unexpected Result
While the procedure was designed for stock management rather than service, the improvement may stem from:
- Staff spending less time searching for items, freeing time for patron assistance
- Better organization leading to faster fulfillment of requests
- Improved staff morale or confidence with the new system

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all remaining branches

2. **Investigate the mechanism**—conduct brief interviews or surveys at switched branches to understand *why* service scores improved (this could reveal additional best practices)

3. **Monitor implementation** during the first 8–12 weeks at each new branch to ensure the procedure is being followed correctly

4. **Track service scores** post-rollout to confirm the 2-point improvement is replicated across all branches

5. **Consider a follow-up review** in 6 months to assess whether the improvement is sustained over time
