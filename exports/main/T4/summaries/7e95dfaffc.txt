# **Board Summary: Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw their service scores increase by approximately **2.1 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant (p < 0.001) and represents a meaningful gain on the service score index, where the average branch scores about 50.

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Measurable Improvement in Service Scores**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches (90) | 52.6 | 53.9 | +1.3 |
| Non-switched branches (60) | 52.0 | 51.2 | −0.9 |
| **Net effect (DiD)** | | | **+2.1** |

- **Treatment effect**: +2.1 points (95% confidence interval: 1.0 to 3.3 points)
- **Statistical significance**: p < 0.001 (highly significant)
- The effect persists after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### **2. The Analysis Is Reliable**

- **Study design**: Natural experiment with 150 branches (90 switched, 60 did not) observed over 17 weeks
- **Method**: Difference-in-differences analysis comparing switched vs. non-switched branches before and after week 15
- **Parallel trends validated**: Pre-switch trends were statistically similar between groups (F-test p = 0.93), confirming the comparison is valid
- **Robustness**: Results hold with cluster-robust standard errors and when controlling for staffing and visits

### **3. Consistent Positive Effects Over Time**

- Improvement was sustained across all 8 post-switch weeks
- Weekly treatment effects ranged from +3.2 to +4.7 points
- No evidence of effect fading during the observation period

### **4. Effect Size in Context**

- A 2.1-point gain on a ~50-point scale represents approximately **4% improvement**
- Switched branches outperformed non-switched branches by about 3 points in the post-period
- The benefit appears stable and does not diminish over time
