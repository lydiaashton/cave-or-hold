# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD OF DIRECTORS**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Office  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Despite initial expectations that the procedural change would have no measurable impact on service quality, our analysis shows branches adopting the new rotation and shelving procedure achieved service scores approximately **2 points higher** (about 4% improvement) than branches maintaining the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and robust. 

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Overview**
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22 of the year)
- **102 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **48 branches** continued with the old procedure as a control group
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** method, the standard approach for evaluating policy changes with treatment and control groups

### **Results Summary**

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Net Change |
|-------|---------------|---------------|------------|
| New Procedure Branches | 53.07 | 52.57 | -0.50 |
| Old Procedure Branches | 51.58 | 49.08 | -2.50 |
| **Difference (Effect)** | | | **+2.03** |

### **Interpretation**

Service scores declined system-wide after week 15, likely due to seasonal factors or other external conditions. However, **branches with the new procedure experienced a much smaller decline** than those keeping the old procedure. The new procedure effectively buffered branches against this downturn, delivering a net 2-point advantage.

### **Statistical Confidence**

| Test | Result |
|------|--------|
| Treatment effect estimate | +2.03 points |
| Statistical significance | p = 0.002 (highly significant) |
| Confidence interval | +0.77 to +3.28 points |
| Parallel trends assumption | ✓ Confirmed (p = 0.58) |
| Robust to staffing/visits controls | ✓ Yes |

---

## **Why This Matters**

This finding is notable because the procedure change was not originally expected to affect service scores. The positive impact suggests the new rotation and shelving method may have unintended benefits—perhaps improving staff efficiency, reducing time spent searching for materials, or enabling better responsiveness to patron needs. These mechanisms warrant further investigation as the rollout proceeds.
