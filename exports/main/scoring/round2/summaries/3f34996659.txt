# **Memorandum to the Board of Directors**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2025  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure shows a **positive but statistically uncertain effect** on service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure improved their service scores by approximately **2 points more** than branches that kept the old procedure. However, this difference is not statistically significant at conventional levels (p ≈ 0.12), meaning we cannot confidently rule out that the observed improvement occurred by chance.

**Recommendation:** Do not proceed with a full system-wide rollout at this time. Instead, extend the pilot program to gather more data, then re-evaluate in the second quarter.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
- **34 branches** participated over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22 of the year)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure as a control group
- Service scores average around 50 points across all branches; higher scores indicate better service

### Results

| Group | Before Switch (Weeks 6–14) | After Switch (Weeks 15–22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------------|---------------------------|--------|
| **New Procedure Branches** | 51.4 | 55.1 | +3.8 |
| **Old Procedure Branches** | 47.2 | 48.9 | +1.7 |
| **Net Effect (Difference-in-Differences)** | | | **+2.0 points** |

### What This Means
- The new procedure is associated with a **2-point improvement** in service scores beyond what would have happened anyway
- However, the **statistical evidence is not strong enough** to confirm this effect is real (p ≈ 0.12)
- The true effect could reasonably range from **slightly negative to +4.5 points**
- Both groups improved after week 14, but the new procedure branches improved somewhat more

### Validity Checks
- ✓ **Parallel trends confirmed**: Both groups followed similar patterns before the switch, supporting a valid comparison
- ✓ **No confounding factors**: Changes in staffing levels and visitor volumes do not explain the results
- ✓ **Appropriate methodology**: Analysis accounts for inherent differences between branches

| Action | Timeline | Responsibility |
|--------|----------|----------------|
| **Extend pilot program** | Continue through Q1 | Operations Team |

### If Proceeding with Rollout
Should the Board wish to move forward despite the uncertainty, we recommend a **phased implementation** rather than immediate system-wide adoption, with continued monitoring at each stage.
