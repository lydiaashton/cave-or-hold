# **Board Summary: New Branch Procedure Evaluation**

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure shows a **modest positive effect on service scores** (approximately +2 points on the ~50-point scale), but the evidence is **not statistically definitive**. We recommend a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. What We Measured
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure; **14 branches** kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data**: 9 weeks before the switch (weeks 6-14) and 8 weeks after (weeks 15-22)
- Service scores average around 50; higher scores indicate better service

### 2. The Bottom-Line Result
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 45.7 | 48.1 | +2.4 |
| Non-switched branches | 49.5 | 50.0 | +0.5 |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | | **+1.9** |

The new procedure is associated with a **1.9-point improvement** in service scores, representing roughly a **4% gain** from baseline.

### 3. Confidence in the Result
- The effect is **positive but not statistically conclusive** (approximately 90% confidence, below the conventional 95% threshold)
- This means there is meaningful evidence of benefit, but we cannot rule out that some portion of the observed gain is due to random variation
- The pattern is **consistent across multiple analytical approaches**, which strengthens our confidence

### 4. Trends Over Time
- Before the switch, both groups followed similar week-to-week patterns (validating our comparison)
- After the switch, the gap between switched and non-switched branches **narrowed consistently**
- The improvement was **gradual rather than immediate**, suggesting branches may need time to fully realize the benefits

### 5. What Else We Checked
- Staffing levels and visit volumes were similar across groups and do not explain the observed effect
- Branch-specific characteristics (size, location, baseline performance) were controlled for in the analysis
- No negative unintended consequences were detected in the available data

---

## **Recommendations**

### **Proceed with Cautious Expansion**

| Action | Rationale | Timeline |
|--------|-----------|----------|
| **Expand to 10-15 additional branches** | Positive effect direction supports rollout; phased approach manages risk | Next 4-6 weeks |
| **Continue data collection for 12+ more weeks** | Longer observation period will clarify whether effect is stable and significant | Ongoing |
| **Monitor branch-level variation** | Identify which branches benefit most to inform targeting | Monthly review |
| **Set clear success criteria** | Define threshold for full rollout (e.g., +2 points with 95% confidence) | Before expansion |
| **Revisit full rollout decision** | Make go/no-go decision after additional data is collected | Q2 2026 |

---

## **What Success Looks Like**

We recommend proceeding to system-wide rollout when:
- ✓ The effect remains positive (+1.5 points or higher)
- ✓ Statistical confidence reaches 95% (p < 0.05)
- ✓ No negative effects emerge in extended monitoring
- ✓ Branch feedback indicates the procedure is operationally feasible

---

## **Limitations**

- **8-week post-switch period** may be too short to detect stable, long-term effects
- **External factors** (seasonal variation, community events) could influence service scores
- **Service score is one metric**; operational costs and staff satisfaction were not assessed
