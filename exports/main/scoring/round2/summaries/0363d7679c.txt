# **Board Memorandum**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2025  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure shows a **positive effect on service scores** in the expected direction, but the evidence is **not yet strong enough** to justify immediate system-wide rollout. We recommend **continuing the pilot while expanding data collection** to reach a more definitive conclusion.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **The Numbers**

| Measure | Result |
|---------|--------|
| Estimated improvement from new procedure | **+1.9 points** |
| Baseline service score | 50.4 points |
| Percentage improvement | **3.8%** |
| Statistical confidence | **Not significant** (p = 0.11) |
| Range of plausible effects | -0.4 to +4.2 points |

### **What We Observed**

- **Treatment branches** (new procedure): Scores rose from 50.8 to 53.2 after the switch (+2.4 points)
- **Control branches** (old procedure): Scores rose from 49.9 to 50.5 over the same period (+0.7 points)
- **Net effect**: Approximately 1.9 points attributable to the new procedure

### **Why We Can't Be Certain**

The improvement is in the expected direction and aligns with our hypothesis. However, with only 8 weeks of post-switch data across 34 branches, we lack the statistical power to distinguish this effect from normal week-to-week variation. The true effect could reasonably be:
- A meaningful improvement (up to +4 points)
- A modest improvement (around +2 points, as observed)
- No effect at all

### **Validation Checks Passed**

✓ Treatment and control branches had **similar trends before the switch**  
✓ No evidence of **negative impacts** on any branch  
✓ Effect is **consistent across specifications** (with and without controls)

---

## **Recommendations**

### **1. Do Not Yet Roll Out System-Wide**

The current evidence does not meet the threshold for a confident investment decision. A premature rollout risks implementing a procedure that may not deliver meaningful benefits across all branches.

### **2. Extend the Pilot by 12 Weeks**

Continuing data collection through week 34 would approximately double our post-switch observations, narrowing the confidence interval and allowing a more definitive assessment.

### **3. Add Implementation Cost Tracking**

Begin measuring:
- Staff hours required under each procedure
- Training time and costs
- Any equipment or supply changes

This will enable a full cost-benefit analysis once we have clearer effect estimates.

### **4. Plan for Decision Point in Q2 2026**

Reconvene with updated analysis to make a final recommendation. At that time, we can provide:
- A more precise estimate of the service score effect
- Cost per point of improvement
- Identification of branch types that benefit most

---

## **Summary for Decision-Makers**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did scores improve where the new procedure was adopted? | **Yes** |
| Can we attribute this to the new procedure? | **Probably, but not certainly** |
| Is the improvement large enough to matter? | **Potentially** (4% is meaningful if real) |
| Should we roll out to all branches now? | **Not yet** |
| Should we abandon the new procedure? | **No** — evidence is promising |

---

**Bottom line:** The new procedure is **promising but unproven**. Invest in more data before committing to system-wide implementation.
