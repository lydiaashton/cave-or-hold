# **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

The evidence **leans positive but is not conclusive**. Branches using the new procedure improved by an estimated **1.5 points more** than comparable branches (approximately 3% improvement). There is roughly a **92% probability the true effect is positive**, but the result is not statistically significant at conventional levels (p = 0.16). 

The main limitation is **statistical power**: with only 34 branches, this study could only reliably detect effects larger than 2.3 points. The observed 1.5-point effect may be real but too small to confirm with confidence.

**Recommendation:** Approve a **phased rollout** while continuing to collect data. This balances the likelihood of genuine benefit against the uncertainty in the estimate.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure; **14 branches** kept the old procedure
- Data covers **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- **578 total observations** (weekly scores for all branches)

### Results

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 47.3 | 51.6 | +4.3 |
| Non-switched branches | 48.0 | 50.8 | +2.8 |
| **Difference** | | | **+1.5** |

- **Effect estimate:** +1.53 points
- **95% confidence interval:** -0.59 to +3.65 points
- **P-value:** 0.16
- **Probability effect is positive:** ~92%
- **Pre-trend test:** Passed (p = 0.52) — validates the comparison

### Branch-Level Patterns
- **18 of 20** switched branches showed improvement
- **11 of 14** non-switched branches also improved
- Service scores rose across the system; switched branches rose slightly more

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve phased expansion** to additional branches while maintaining evaluation.

2. **Continue data collection** — 6–12 more months would provide adequate power to confirm or rule out an effect of this size.

3. **Monitor branch-level variation** — investigate why two switched branches saw declines while others gained substantially.

4. **Assess implementation costs** against the observed ~3% service improvement.

5. **Track complementary metrics** (staff efficiency, patron feedback) to capture benefits the service score may not reflect.
