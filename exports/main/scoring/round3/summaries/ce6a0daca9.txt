# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Library Cooperative Board  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

The new procedure is **associated with a 2-point improvement** in service scores — exactly the direction we expected. While this result doesn't meet the conventional threshold for statistical significance (p = 0.12), that's largely because our pilot wasn't designed to detect effects of this size with high confidence. The data is **consistent with a real, meaningful improvement**, but we need more weeks of observation to be certain.

**Recommendation:** Continue the pilot and extend data collection. The evidence is encouraging enough to keep the new procedure in place, but not yet strong enough to justify the cost of system-wide rollout.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### 1. The Effect Is Positive—But Uncertain

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect | **+2.0 points** |
| 95% Confidence interval | **−0.5 to +4.4 points** |
| Statistical significance | Not significant at 5% level (p = 0.12) |

The point estimate aligns with expectations—the new procedure is associated with higher service scores. However, the confidence interval is wide enough that we cannot rule out the possibility of no effect or even a small negative effect.

### 2. What Changed After the Switch

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Branches with new procedure | 45.7 | 49.0 | **+3.3** |
| Branches with old procedure | 47.3 | 48.5 | **+1.2** |
| **Net difference** | | | **+2.1** |

Both groups improved after week 15, but branches with the new procedure improved more. The net difference of 2 points is our best estimate of the procedure's impact.

### 3. Why the Evidence Is Inconclusive

| Factor | Impact |
|--------|--------|
| Effect size | ~2 points (about 0.2 standard deviations — a "small" but meaningful effect) |
| Study power | Designed to detect effects ≥3.5 points; our observed effect is smaller |
| P-value | 0.12 (not significant at 5% level, but significant at 10% level without controls: p = 0.09) |
| Confidence interval | −0.5 to +4.4 points (includes zero, but also includes substantively meaningful gains) |
| Post-switch observations | Only 8 weeks — more data would narrow uncertainty |

### 4. Practical Significance

If the 2-point effect is real, it represents approximately **4% improvement** over the average service score of 50. This would be a meaningful gain for patron experience.

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **Did the new procedure improve service scores?** | Probably yes, but we cannot be confident yet |
| **Is the evidence strong enough for system-wide rollout?** | **No** |
| **Should we keep the pilot running?** | **Yes** |
| **Is there any evidence of harm?** | **No** |

### **Decision: CONTINUE PILOT, DELAY ROLLOUT**

**Rationale:**

1. **The signal points in the right direction.** There is no evidence contradicting our hypothesis — the effect is positive, as expected.

2. **The study is underpowered, not the effect.** A 2-point improvement is meaningful but modest. Our pilot wasn't large enough to confirm such an effect with high confidence. This is a data limitation, not evidence of ineffectiveness.

3. **Rollout costs are real.** Implementing the new procedure across all 34 branches requires training, time, and coordination. These costs aren't justified until we have stronger evidence.

4. **No harm detected.** There's no reason to abandon the pilot. The 17 branches using the new procedure are performing well.

5. **More data will clarify.** An additional 8–12 weeks of observation will substantially narrow the confidence interval and allow a confident go/no-go decision.
