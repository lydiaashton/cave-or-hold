# **Board Memorandum**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure **likely improves service scores**, but our study is too small to prove it definitively. The estimated improvement is **2.2 points (5%)**, with **80% of switched branches showing gains** and **no evidence of harm**. The statistical uncertainty (p = 0.11) reflects limited sample size rather than weak effects—the confidence interval barely includes zero, and the most plausible values are positive.

**Recommendation depends on implementation costs:**
- **If rollout costs are low:** Proceed with system-wide adoption. The evidence leans positive, harm is unlikely, and waiting for certainty means delaying potential benefits.
- **If rollout costs are high:** Expand to 10–15 additional branches while maintaining some controls. This would provide definitive evidence within 2–3 months before full commitment.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Service Score Impact**

| Metric | Value | Interpretation |
|--------|-------|----------------|
| Estimated improvement | +2.2 points | Meaningful gain |
| Percentage improvement | ~5% | Substantial for service quality |
| P-value | 0.113 | Not conclusive, but not dismissible |
| 95% Confidence interval | -0.6 to +5.0 | Negative effect is at the extreme edge |
| Branches that improved | 16 of 20 (80%) | Consistent positive pattern |

**What p = 0.113 actually means:** If the procedure had no effect, we'd see results this favorable about 11% of the time by chance. This exceeds the conventional 0.05 threshold, but it's far from evidence of "no effect." With only 34 branches, the study lacks power to detect moderate effects with high confidence.

### **2. No Evidence of Harm**

- Only **4 of 20 switched branches** experienced declines
- Average decline among those branches: **-3.9 points** (modest)
- No branch showed catastrophic failure
- The procedure appears safe to implement

### **3. Control Group Validation**

Non-switched branches showed minimal change (+0.5 points) over the same period, confirming that improvements in switched branches are not attributable to seasonal trends or system-wide factors.

---

## **Recommendations**

### **Primary Recommendation: Phased Rollout**

Given the positive signal, absence of harm, and meaningful effect size, we recommend **proceeding with expansion** while continuing to monitor:

1. **Roll out to all remaining branches** over the next 8–12 weeks
2. **Continue tracking service scores** weekly to confirm the effect persists at scale
3. **Document implementation lessons** from the 4 underperforming branches to support smoother adoption

### **Alternative: Cautious Expansion**

If the board prefers greater certainty before full commitment:

1. **Expand to 10–15 additional branches** (not all remaining)
2. **Keep 5–10 branches as controls** for another 8–12 weeks
3. **Reconvene with definitive evidence** by Q1 2027

### **Implementation Priorities**

4. **Support the 4 underperforming branches** with additional training or troubleshooting
5. **Capture qualitative feedback** from high-performing switched branches to identify best practices
6. **Set a review date** (e.g., 90 days post-rollout) to evaluate whether expected improvements materialized

---

## **Conclusion**

This is not a case of "inconclusive = do nothing." The evidence points in a positive direction: most branches improved, none were seriously harmed, and the estimated effect is meaningful for service quality. The statistical uncertainty stems from sample size limitations, not from ambiguous results.

The board's decision should hinge on **implementation costs and reversibility**, not on whether p < 0.05. If the procedure is affordable and can be adjusted if needed, the evidence supports moving forward.
