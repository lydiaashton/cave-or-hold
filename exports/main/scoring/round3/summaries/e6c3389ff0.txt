# **Board Summary: New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure shows a **positive but statistically uncertain effect** on service scores. The estimated improvement is 2.1 points (≈4%), but the result is not statistically significant (p=0.11), meaning we cannot confidently distinguish this from random variation. **We do not recommend a full system-wide rollout at this time.** Instead, we suggest extending the pilot to gather more evidence before making a system-wide decision.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **14 branches** kept the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in the switching branches against changes in the non-switching branches

### **Results**

| Metric | Treatment Branches (Switched) | Control Branches (Not Switched) |
|--------|------------------------------|--------------------------------|
| Pre-switch average score | 53.3 | 46.2 |
| Post-switch average score | 55.3 | 46.3 |
| **Change** | **+2.0** | **+0.1** |

**Estimated causal effect of new procedure: +2.1 points** (95% confidence interval: -0.5 to +4.8)

### **Supporting Evidence**
- **75% of switching branches** (15 of 20) improved after the change, versus **50% of control branches** (7 of 14)
- The parallel trends assumption holds: treatment and control groups showed similar patterns before the switch
- The effect is robust to controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### **Limitations**
- The effect does not reach conventional statistical significance (p=0.11)
- The 95% confidence interval includes zero, so the true effect could be negative, null, or as high as +4.8 points
- Only 8 weeks of post-switch data are available; longer-term effects remain unknown

---

## **Final Verdict**

**Do not proceed with system-wide rollout.** The evidence is insufficient to justify the cost and disruption of changing procedures at all 34 branches. The data suggest a possible benefit, but we cannot rule out that the observed improvement is due to chance.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Extend the pilot period**: Continue collecting data for another 8–12 weeks to reduce uncertainty and narrow the confidence interval.

2. **Consider limited expansion**: If there is strong operational interest, expand to 5–10 additional branches while maintaining a control group for comparison.

3. **Investigate heterogeneity**: Some branches saw gains of 5–9 points while others saw declines. Understanding which branch characteristics predict success could improve targeting.

4. **Conduct cost-benefit analysis**: Before any expansion, quantify the implementation costs and weigh them against the uncertain ~2-point service score gain.

5. **Reconvene in Q2**: With additional data, the board can make a more confident go/no-go decision on system-wide adoption.

---

*Analysis prepared using difference-in-differences regression with branch fixed effects and cluster-robust standard errors.*
