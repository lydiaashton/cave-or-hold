# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Library Cooperative Board  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The evidence does not support rolling out the new procedure system-wide at this time.** 

While branches that switched showed some improvement relative to their own baseline, they still underperformed compared to branches that kept the old procedure in 6 of 8 weeks after the switch. The estimated effect (+2.2 points) is not statistically significant (p=0.10), and the confidence interval includes zero and potentially negative effects.

**Recommendation:** Do not proceed with system-wide rollout. Either (1) continue the pilot with closer monitoring to understand why switched branches continue underperforming, or (2) pause and investigate what's working better at control branches before making changes.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **1. The Critical Context: Switched Branches Started Behind**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched Branches (20 branches) | 45.0 | 47.8 | +2.7 |
| Control Branches (14 branches) | 48.7 | 49.2 | +0.5 |
| **Gap (Switched − Control)** | **-3.7** | **-1.4** | **+2.2** |

The +2.2 point "effect" reflects switched branches **closing the gap**, not exceeding control branch performance. Even after the switch, switched branches scored lower than control branches on average.

### **2. Weekly Performance After Switch**

| Week | Switched vs. Control |
|------|---------------------|
| 15 | Essentially equal |
| 16 | Essentially equal |
| 17 | Essentially equal |
| 18 | Switched **3.3 points lower** |
| 19 | Switched **3.8 points lower** |
| 20 | Switched **4.1 points lower** |
| 21 | Switched **1.7 points lower** |
| 22 | Switched **1.5 points higher** |

**Switched branches underperformed in 6 of 8 post-switch weeks.**

### **3. Statistical Uncertainty**

- **95% confidence interval:** -0.46 to +4.94 points
- This range includes: no effect, modest benefit, and modest harm
- **Probability the true effect is positive:** ~94% (but this is not the same as statistical significance)
- The study is underpowered: only 34 branches total

### **4. Possible Explanations**

The data raises questions that need answers before rollout:
- Were switched branches systematically different (e.g., smaller, understaffed, serving different communities)?
- Is the new procedure harder to implement correctly?
- Are control branches doing something the new procedure doesn't capture?

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

### **Recommended Approach: Pause and Investigate**

1. **Compare branch characteristics** between switched and control groups to check for systematic differences
2. **Interview staff** at both switched and control branches to understand operational differences
3. **Review implementation** at switched branches—are they following the procedure as designed?
4. **Consider a matched comparison** pairing similar branches rather than treating all switched branches as one group

### **Decision Options**

| Option | Recommendation |
|--------|---------------|
| **Full Rollout** | ❌ Not supported by evidence |
| **Phased Rollout** | ⚠️ Only if investigation reveals implementation issues that can be fixed |
| **Pause and Investigate** | ✓ Recommended—understand why switched branches still underperform |
| **Extend Pilot** | ✓ Acceptable if paired with deeper investigation |

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

### **❌ DO NOT APPROVE SYSTEM-WIDE ROLLOUT**

**Rationale:**
1. **Switched branches still lag behind** control branches after the procedure change
2. **Statistical evidence is inconclusive**—the observed improvement could be due to chance
3. **Six of eight weeks** showed switched branches performing worse than controls
4. **Unexplained baseline differences** between groups raise validity concerns

**What Would Change This Verdict:**
- Additional data showing switched branches consistently matching or exceeding control branch performance
- Statistical significance at p < 0.05 with clustered standard errors
- Evidence that baseline differences between groups do not explain the results
- Qualitative feedback from staff confirming the new procedure is workable and beneficial

**Timeline for Reconsideration:** Recommend reconvening this discussion in 3–4 months after further investigation and/or extended data collection.

---

## **TECHNICAL NOTES**

- **Method:** Difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors
- **Data:** 34 branches, 17 weeks each, 578 observations
- **Key limitation:** Switched branches had lower baseline scores, raising questions about comparability
