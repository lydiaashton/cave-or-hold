# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD OF DIRECTORS**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Prepared by:** Data Analysis Office

---

## **Bottom Line**

**We cannot conclude that the new procedure improves service scores.** The observed 1.6-point gain is not statistically distinguishable from zero, and weekly results are inconsistent—treatment branches outperformed control branches in only 4 of 8 post-switch weeks. 

**Recommendation:** Do not proceed with system-wide rollout based on current evidence. Either (a) extend the pilot to gather more data, or (b) discontinue and reallocate resources to interventions with stronger evidence of effectiveness.

---

## **Study Overview**

| Component | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Branches studied** | 34 total (20 switched; 14 retained old procedure) |
| **Study period** | 17 weeks (weeks 6–22) |
| **Implementation date** | Week 15 |
| **Method** | Difference-in-differences with cluster-robust standard errors |

---

## **Results**

| Measure | Value | Interpretation |
|---------|-------|----------------|
| **Estimated effect** | +1.62 points | Modest positive point estimate |
| **95% confidence interval** | -0.44 to +3.67 | Includes zero; effect could be null |
| **Statistical significance** | p = 0.12 | Not significant at p < 0.05 |
| **Statistical power** | 55% of detectable threshold | Study underpowered to detect this effect size |

**Critical finding:** The effect is only 55% of the minimum detectable effect with 80% statistical power. Even if the true effect is +1.6 points, this study design cannot reliably distinguish it from noise.

---

## **Weekly Post-Switch Performance**

| Week | Treatment − Control | Direction |
|------|---------------------|-----------|
| 15 | +1.54 | ✓ Treatment better |
| 16 | −0.01 | ✗ Control better |
| 17 | −1.50 | ✗ Control better |

**Treatment branches outperformed control in only 4 of 8 weeks.** This inconsistency suggests either (a) no real effect, (b) effect depends on unmeasured factors, or (c) implementation varies substantially across branches.

---

## **Key Observations**

1. **Both groups improved after week 15**, but the difference between them is unstable
2. **Pre-implementation trends were parallel**, validating the study design
3. **The confidence interval includes negative values**, meaning we cannot rule out slight harm
4. **Study was underpowered** to detect effects of this magnitude with confidence

---

## **Recommendations**

### **Option A: Extend the Pilot (If procedure has low implementation cost)**

| Action | Timeline | Success criterion |
|--------|----------|-------------------|
| Continue current pilot | 12 additional weeks | Effect reaches p < 0.05 |
| Add implementation fidelity measures | Immediate | Identify best-performing branches |
| Collect qualitative staff feedback | 30 days | Document barriers and facilitators |

### **Option B: Discontinue (If resources are constrained)**

| Rationale | Consideration |
|-----------|---------------|
| Evidence is inconclusive after 17 weeks | More data may not change conclusion |
| Opportunity cost of continued pilot | Resources could test other interventions |
| No clear signal of benefit | 4 of 8 weeks favored control group |

### **What Would Change Our Recommendation?**

| Evidence threshold | Decision |
|--------------------|----------|
| Effect > 2.5 points with p < 0.05 | Recommend rollout |
| Effect remains 1–2 points with p > 0.10 | Recommend discontinuation |
| Effect becomes negative | Recommend immediate reversal |

---

## **Conclusion**

The data do not support a system-wide rollout decision. The procedure shows **neither clear benefit nor clear harm**. The board should decide whether additional data collection is worth the cost, or whether to redirect resources to interventions with stronger evidence.
