# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

---

## **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

**Date:** November 2026  
**Analysis Period:** Weeks 6–22  
**Prepared by:** Data Analysis Team

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

The new stock rotation procedure shows a **positive signal** (+2 points) but **not definitive proof** of effectiveness. Branches that adopted the new procedure saw their service scores decline less than branches that kept the old procedure. However, the result is marginally significant (p = 0.09), and the effect represents protection against decline rather than absolute improvement. Only 30% of switched branches actually improved. We recommend **extending the evaluation period before making a full rollout decision**.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### Study Design
- **34 branches** participated: 20 switched to the new procedure, 14 retained the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** collected (weeks 6–22); the switch occurred at week 15
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach with cluster-robust standard errors

### Results Summary

| Group | Pre-Switch Average | Post-Switch Average | Change |
|-------|-------------------|---------------------|--------|
| Switched Branches (Treatment) | 54.27 | 52.69 | **−1.58** |
| Non-Switched Branches (Control) | 50.35 | 46.84 | **−3.52** |
| **Difference-in-Differences Effect** | | | **+1.94** |

### Statistical Assessment
- **Estimated effect:** +1.94 points (the new procedure cushioned the decline)
- **95% Confidence Interval:** −0.35 to +4.61 points
- **p-value:** 0.09 (marginally significant; does not meet conventional 0.05 threshold)
- **Pre-trend validation:** No evidence of differential trends before the switch (p = 0.29), supporting the validity of the comparison

### Critical Context

1. **No absolute improvement:** The new procedure did not produce score increases—it appears to have **mitigated a broader decline** affecting the entire cooperative.

2. **Limited branch-level success:** Only **6 of 20 switched branches (30%)** saw their scores improve after adoption. The remaining 14 switched branches still experienced declines.

3. **Effect magnitude:** A 2-point difference represents approximately **4% of the average service score** (~50 points). Operational significance should be weighed against implementation costs.

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

| Decision | **Do Not Proceed with System-Wide Rollout at This Time** |
|----------|----------------------------------------------------------|
| Rationale | Evidence is **promising but inconclusive**. The effect is in the expected direction and marginally significant, but the confidence interval includes zero and only 30% of branches benefited. |
| Risk of Acting Now | Potential waste of resources if the effect proves spurious with more data |
| Risk of Waiting | Minimal—the procedure is already working in some branches; 8 additional weeks of data will not cause harm |

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

1. **Extend the evaluation period.** Collect 12–16 additional weeks of data to determine if the effect strengthens with more post-switch observations.

2. **Investigate the cooperative-wide decline.** Both groups saw service scores drop after week 15. Examine whether external factors (seasonal patterns, staffing changes, or system-wide issues) affected all branches.

3. **Analyze successful adopters.** Study the 6 switched branches that improved to identify conditions under which the new procedure works best (e.g., branch size, location, baseline performance).

4. **Set a decision checkpoint.** Reconvene in 3 months with updated data. If the effect remains at ~2 points with p < 0.05, proceed with phased rollout.

5. **Define success criteria.** Establish clear thresholds for adoption (e.g., "minimum 3-point sustained improvement with p < 0.05, and at least 50% of branches showing positive change").

---

## **RECOMMENDATION**

**Continue the pilot program and gather more evidence before deciding on system-wide adoption.** The new procedure shows promise, but the current data does not support the cost and disruption of a full rollout. Reassess after 12–16 additional weeks of data collection.
