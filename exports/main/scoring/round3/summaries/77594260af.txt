# **Memorandum to the Board of Directors**

**To:** Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2025  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure  

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure did not improve service scores.** Scores declined for all branches after week 15. Branches that adopted the new procedure experienced a smaller decline than those that did not, but this difference is **not statistically significant**. We **cannot conclude** the new procedure had a meaningful positive effect. **We recommend against system-wide rollout at this time** and suggest gathering more data before making a decision.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **What Actually Happened to Service Scores**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| **Branches with new procedure** | 54.27 | 52.69 | **▼ -1.58** |
| **Branches with old procedure** | 50.35 | 46.84 | **▼ -3.52** |

**Critical observation:** Service scores **decreased for both groups** after week 15. The new procedure did not produce the improvement we expected.

### **Relative Comparison**

The difference-in-differences estimate of **+1.94 points** means switched branches declined approximately 2 points *less* than control branches. However:

- **p-value = 0.14** — This difference could easily be due to random variation
- **95% confidence interval: -0.6 to +4.5 points** — The true effect could be slightly negative or moderately positive
- **Not statistically significant** at conventional thresholds (p < 0.05)

### **Additional Concerns**

1. **System-wide decline:** All branches saw scores drop after week 15, suggesting external factors (seasonal patterns, staffing changes, visitor behavior) affected the entire cooperative
2. **Limited data:** Only 8 weeks of post-switch data across 34 branches provides limited statistical power
3. **Pre-existing differences:** Treatment branches started with higher scores (54.27 vs. 50.35), which may affect comparability

---

## **Recommendations**

### **1. Do Not Proceed with System-Wide Rollout**
The evidence does not support the expected improvement in service scores. Rolling out the procedure cooperative-wide would incur costs without demonstrated benefit.

### **2. Continue the Pilot and Collect More Data**
Extend the observation period to:
- Increase statistical power
- Determine if the effect stabilizes or changes over time
- Better understand the system-wide score decline

### **3. Investigate the Overall Decline**
Prioritize understanding why **all branches** experienced score drops after week 15. This may reveal more impactful improvement opportunities than the procedure change.

### **4. Re-evaluate in 3–6 Months**
With additional data, reassess whether:
- The relative benefit for switched branches becomes statistically significant
- Service scores recover or continue declining
- Implementation quality varies across branches

---

## **Conclusion**

The new procedure did not deliver the service score improvement we expected. While switched branches declined less than control branches, we cannot confidently attribute this to the procedure itself. **We recommend pausing the rollout, investigating the broader score decline, and collecting more data before making a system-wide decision.**
