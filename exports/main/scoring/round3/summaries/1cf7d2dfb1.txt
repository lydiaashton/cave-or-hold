# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Analysis Period:** Weeks 6–22 (17 weeks of data)

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure shows a **positive signal but inconclusive evidence** of improvement. Switched branches gained an estimated **1.5 points more** than non-switched branches, but the statistical uncertainty is too large to confidently attribute this to the procedure rather than random variation. The 95% confidence interval (-0.6 to +3.7 points) includes zero, meaning **no effect is a plausible explanation** for what we observed. We recommend **continued evaluation before expansion**.

---

## **Key Findings**

### What We Compared
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure in week 15
- **14 branches** continued with the old procedure (comparison group)
- Service scores tracked weekly before and after the change

### Results Summary

| Metric | Switched Branches | Non-Switched Branches |
|--------|------------------|----------------------|
| Average score before | 47.3 | 48.0 |
| Average score after | 51.6 | 50.8 |
| **Improvement** | **+4.3** | **+2.8** |
| **Net effect** | | **+1.5 points** |

### Statistical Evidence

| Measure | Value | Interpretation |
|---------|-------|----------------|
| Point estimate | +1.53 points | Best guess of the true effect |
| 95% Confidence interval | -0.59 to +3.65 | Range of plausible true effects |
| P-value | 0.16 | 16% chance of this pattern if no real effect exists |
| Pre-trend test | p = 0.52 | Groups were comparable before the switch ✓ |

### What This Means
- **Direction is encouraging:** The estimate points in the expected direction
- **Uncertainty is substantial:** The true effect could be zero, slightly negative, or moderately positive
- **Both groups improved:** Service scores rose across the system, making it harder to isolate the procedure's specific contribution
- **Study design is sound:** No evidence that switched branches were on different trajectories before the change

---

## **Final Verdict**

### **DO NOT PROCEED WITH SYSTEM-WIDE ROLLOUT AT THIS TIME**

**Rationale:**

The evidence does not meet the standard of proof needed for a system-wide investment. While the point estimate is positive, the confidence interval includes zero and the p-value (0.16) exceeds conventional thresholds for statistical significance. Approving full rollout on this evidence alone carries meaningful risk that resources would be spent on changes with no real benefit.

**What We Recommend Instead:**

1. **Extend data collection** by 8–12 weeks to improve statistical precision
2. **Maintain the current study design** with both switched and non-switched branches
3. **Reconvene for a second review** once additional data is available
4. **Document implementation costs** to enable future cost-benefit analysis

**When to Reconsider:**

A system-wide rollout would be justified if follow-up analysis shows:
- A statistically significant effect (p < 0.05), OR
- A narrower confidence interval entirely above zero, OR
- Clear evidence of cost savings that justify even a modest service improvement

---

## **Conclusion**

The new procedure is **promising but unproven**. The board should resist the temptation to act on encouraging but inconclusive results. Continued evaluation with the current study design is the most responsible path forward.
