# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Library Cooperative Board  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

The new procedure **likely improves** service scores, but the evidence is not definitive. Our best estimate is a **2-point increase** (approximately 4% improvement), with roughly **94% probability** the true effect is positive. However, because the result does not meet conventional statistical significance thresholds (p = 0.12), we cannot rule out that the observed improvement is due to chance.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **phased rollout** rather than full implementation or complete pause. Expand the new procedure to additional branches while continuing to monitor outcomes. This captures potential benefits while limiting risk if the effect proves illusory.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### 1. Study Design
We analyzed 17 weeks of data (weeks 6–22) from 34 branches. Seventeen branches switched to the new procedure at week 15; seventeen branches kept the old procedure. We compared how service scores changed before and after the switch for both groups.

### 2. Main Result

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect of new procedure | **+2.0 points** |
| 95% Confidence interval | **−0.5 to +4.4 points** |
| Statistical significance | Not significant at 5% level (p = 0.12) |
| Probability effect is positive | **~94%** |

### 3. What Happened to Scores

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| New Procedure Branches | 45.7 | 49.0 | +3.3 |
| Old Procedure Branches | 47.3 | 48.5 | +1.2 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+2.1** |

### 4. Interpretation
- **If the effect is real:** A 2-point improvement represents approximately **4%** of the average service score—a meaningful but modest gain worth pursuing.
- **The uncertainty:** The confidence interval includes zero, so we cannot be fully confident the procedure caused the improvement.
- **Risk/reward asymmetry:** The potential upside (+4.4 points) is **9 times larger** than the potential downside (−0.5 points).
- **Pre-existing differences:** The two groups had different score patterns before the switch, which adds uncertainty to the comparison.

### 5. Additional Observations
- Branches with higher visitor traffic tended to have higher service scores.
- Staffing levels showed no clear relationship with service scores in this analysis.
- Some weeks showed stronger effects than others, suggesting the impact may vary over time.

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| **Did the new procedure improve service scores?** | Probably yes, but not certain |
| **Is the evidence strong enough for full rollout?** | No |
| **Is the evidence strong enough to abandon the procedure?** | No |
| **What should the board do?** | **Phased rollout with continued monitoring** |

### Rationale

The data presents a **favorable risk/reward profile**:

1. **The likely scenario (94% probability):** The procedure provides a modest but meaningful improvement. Rolling it out captures these benefits across more branches.

2. **The unlikely scenario (6% probability):** The procedure has no effect or a slight negative effect. A phased approach limits exposure and allows for course correction.

3. **The cost of waiting:** If the effect is real, delaying full implementation means foregoing 4% service improvements across the cooperative for additional months.

4. **The cost of acting:** Implementation costs are one-time; if the effect proves illusory, branches can revert to the old procedure.

### Recommended Path Forward

| Phase | Action | Timeline |
|-------|--------|----------|
| **Phase 1** | Expand new procedure to 10–15 additional branches | Next 4 weeks |
| **Phase 2** | Collect 8–12 weeks of additional data | Ongoing |
| **Phase 3** | Re-evaluate with expanded dataset | Week 30–34 |
| **Decision Point** | Full rollout, modification, or discontinuation | Based on Phase 3 results |

### Success Metrics for Re-evaluation

At the Phase 3 decision point, the board should consider:
- Has the effect size remained stable or grown?
- Has statistical significance been achieved (p < 0.05)?
- Are implementation costs within expectations?
- Do certain branch types show stronger/weaker effects?

---

## **CONCLUSION**

The new procedure shows **promising but inconclusive** evidence of improvement. A phased rollout balances the opportunity to capture likely benefits against the risk that the effect may not be real. Continued monitoring will enable a confident final decision within 2–3 months.
