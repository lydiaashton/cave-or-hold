# MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD

## Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## BOTTOM LINE

The new procedure is associated with a **2.6-point improvement in service scores** (approximately 5% increase). The result is **not statistically significant at the 0.05 level** (p = 0.10), but the evidence points in the expected direction with no signs of harm. Given the low risk and meaningful potential benefit, we recommend **proceeding with a phased rollout** while continuing to collect data to confirm the effect.

---

## FINAL VERDICT

**Yes, roll out the new procedure—with conditions.**

The data supports adoption, but not unconditional system-wide implementation. The 5% improvement is operationally meaningful, the direction matches our hypothesis, and there is no evidence of negative effects anywhere in the data. The uncertainty (p = 0.10) reflects limited data, not a weak effect. A phased rollout allows the cooperative to capture benefits while building stronger evidence.

---

## KEY FINDINGS

### 1. Study Design
- **34 branches total:** 20 adopted the new procedure, 14 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22); the switch occurred at week 15
- This "natural experiment" design isolates the effect of the new procedure from other factors

### 2. Service Score Impact

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches (n=20) | 48.6 | 52.1 | +3.5 |
| Control branches (n=14) | 50.1 | 51.1 | +0.9 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+2.6** |

- The 2.6-point estimate represents the effect attributable to the new procedure
- **95% confidence interval:** -0.4 to +5.7 points
- **P-value:** 0.10 (marginally significant)

### 3. Branch-Level Results
- **70% of switched branches** (14 of 20) showed improvement after adoption
- **50% of control branches** (7 of 14) showed improvement over the same period
- Results vary across branches, suggesting implementation or local factors matter

### 4. Validity Checks
- **Parallel trends confirmed:** No significant difference in pre-switch trends between groups (p = 0.82)
- **Results robust to controls:** Adjusting for staffing levels and visitor volume does not change the conclusion
- The analysis is methodologically sound; uncertainty stems from sample size, not design flaws

---

## NEXT STEPS

1. **Phased rollout:** Begin expanding the new procedure to remaining branches over the next 8–12 weeks

2. **Continue monitoring:** Track service scores for all branches during rollout to confirm the effect holds at scale

3. **Document best practices:** Investigate high-performing adopters to identify implementation factors that maximize benefit

4. **Decision checkpoint:** Re-evaluate in 3 months with expanded data. If the effect remains positive (regardless of statistical significance), complete full rollout

5. **Track implementation fidelity:** Ensure consistent execution across branches; variation may be masking the true effect

---

## ANALYST'S NOTE

The 2.6-point improvement is meaningful in practical terms (5% gain on a ~50-point scale), and there is no evidence of harm. The main limitation is statistical precision—we have moderate confidence the effect is positive, but not the high confidence typically required for major policy decisions. A phased approach balances the opportunity for improvement against the risk of premature system-wide change.
