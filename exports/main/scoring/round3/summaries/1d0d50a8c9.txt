## Executive Summary

The new shelving and rotation procedure shows a **positive but statistically inconclusive** effect on service scores. Switched branches improved by an estimated **1.6 points** (approximately 3% above average), but the evidence is too weak to rule out chance. We recommend **continuing the pilot and extending data collection** before making a systemwide rollout decision.

## Bottom Line

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did the new procedure improve service scores? | **We cannot say with confidence** |
| What does the data suggest? | **Possible small improvement** (+1.6 points) |
| How confident are we? | **Low** — result is consistent with no effect |
| Should we roll out systemwide? | **Not yet** — gather more evidence first |

## Key Findings

### 1. Service Score Impact

The analysis compared 20 branches that adopted the new procedure against 14 branches that continued with the old procedure, tracking both groups for 9 weeks before and 8 weeks after the switch.

**Results:**
- Switched branches improved by **4.3 points** on average after the change
- Control branches also improved by **2.8 points** (likely due to seasonal or systemwide factors)
- The **net effect attributable to the new procedure: +1.6 points**
- **95% Confidence Interval: -0.6 to +3.7 points** (includes zero)
- **p-value: 0.16** (1 in 6 chance of this result if no true effect exists)

### 2. Branch-Level Performance

| Group | Branches Improved | Total Branches | Rate |
|-------|-------------------|----------------|------|
| Switched to new procedure | 18 | 20 | 90% |
| Remained on old procedure | 11 | 14 | 79% |

The higher improvement rate among switched branches is encouraging, though two switched branches did see declines.

### 3. No Unintended Consequences

The new procedure did not significantly affect:
- Staffing requirements (p = 0.22)
- Patron visit volumes (p = 0.76)

This suggests the procedure change operates independently of other operational factors.

### 4. Analysis Validity

Pre-switch trends were similar between groups, supporting the validity of the comparison. Both groups tracked closely before week 15, diverging only after the procedure change.

---

## Final Verdict

**DO NOT PROCEED WITH SYSTEMWIDE ROLLOUT AT THIS TIME**

**Rationale:**

1. **Statistical uncertainty is too high.** With p = 0.16, we cannot distinguish a genuine effect from random variation at conventional confidence levels.

2. **Effect size is modest.** At 0.15 standard deviations, the estimated effect is below the threshold for even a "small" effect by conventional standards.

3. **More data will clarify the picture.** Extending the evaluation by 8-12 weeks would substantially improve our ability to detect a true effect.

4. **No urgency to decide.** There are no detected harms from the new procedure, and no pressing operational need to standardize immediately.

**Recommended Path Forward:**

| Action | Timeline | Responsible Party |
|--------|----------|-------------------|
| Continue pilot with current 20 switched branches | Immediate | Operations Team |
| Extend data collection period | 8-12 weeks | Data Analysis Team |
| Investigate 2 branches with declining scores | 4 weeks | Regional Managers |
| Re-evaluate with extended data | 3 months | Board of Directors |

**Decision Criteria for Re-evaluation:**
- If effect remains below +1.0 point → **Do not roll out**
- If effect exceeds +2.0 points with p < 0.05 → **Proceed with rollout**
- If effect is between +1.0 and +2.0 points → **Consider cost-benefit analysis**

- **Method:** Difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors
- **Sample:** 34 branches, 578 weekly observations (weeks 6–22)
- **Controls:** Staffing index, visits index
- **Software:** Python (statsmodels)
