# **MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2025  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure  

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

The new procedure **likely improves service scores by about 2 points (4%)**, but our 8-week study cannot confirm this with statistical certainty. The evidence leans positive: there is an 89% probability the true effect is beneficial, and the risk of harm appears minimal.

**Final Verdict:** **Proceed with system-wide rollout if implementation costs are low.** If significant resources are required, continue the phased approach and collect 3–6 more months of data before full commitment.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### Study Design
- **34 branches** participated (20 adopted new procedure, 14 kept old procedure)
- **17 weeks** of data collected (weeks 6–22)
- New procedure implemented at **week 15**
- Service scores average ~50 (higher is better)

### Results Summary

| | New Procedure Branches | Old Procedure Branches |
|---|---|---|
| Before switch (weeks 6–14) | 47.8 | 52.5 |
| After switch (weeks 15–22) | 49.1 | 51.9 |
| **Change** | **+1.3** | **-0.6** |

**Net effect of new procedure: +1.9 points (4% improvement)**

### Statistical Evidence

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Estimated effect | +2.06 points |
| 95% confidence interval | -0.49 to +4.61 points |
| p-value | 0.11 |
| Probability effect is positive | ~89% |

### What This Means

1. **Suggestive but not definitive:** The p-value of 0.11 means we cannot reject the possibility of no effect at conventional thresholds, but the evidence leans meaningfully positive.

2. **Downside risk is minimal:** The confidence interval's lower bound (-0.5 points) suggests any negative effect would be trivial.

3. **Study was underpowered:** We would need approximately 50% more data (about 900 observations vs. 578) to achieve statistical significance at this effect size.

4. **Treatment branches started behind:** The 20 branches that switched had lower baseline scores. The new procedure appears to have helped them partially catch up.

---

## **FINAL VERDICT**

### Decision Framework

| Implementation Cost | Recommendation | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| **Low** (minimal training, no equipment) | **Full rollout** | Upside (~4% improvement) outweighs minimal downside risk |
| **Moderate** (some training, minor adjustments) | **Phased rollout + monitoring** | Balance potential benefits against resource commitment |
| **High** (major training, new equipment, staffing changes) | **Pause and collect more data** | Need greater certainty before significant investment |

### Our Recommendation

**Assuming low-to-moderate implementation costs:**

> **Proceed with system-wide rollout over the next 3–6 months, while continuing to monitor service scores.** The probable benefit (4% improvement) is operationally meaningful for patron experience, and the risk of harm is negligible.

### Success Metrics for Ongoing Monitoring

| Metric | Target | Timeline |
|---|---|---|
| Service score improvement | +2 points or more | 6 months post-rollout |
| Staff satisfaction | No decline | Quarterly surveys |
| Implementation issues | <10% branches report problems | 3 months post-rollout |

If service scores do not show improvement after 6 months of system-wide implementation, the board should reconsider the procedure.

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

1. **Assess implementation costs** across all remaining branches
2. **Develop rollout timeline** (recommended: 3–6 months)
3. **Establish monitoring dashboard** for ongoing service score tracking
4. **Schedule 6-month review** with the board to evaluate results
