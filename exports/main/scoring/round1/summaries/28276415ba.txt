# **Memorandum to the Board of Directors**

**To:** Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2025  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure  

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure **likely has a positive effect** on service scores, though the evidence is not yet statistically definitive. Branches that switched experienced smaller declines in service quality compared to those that did not. Given the positive direction of the effect, manageable implementation costs, and operational benefits already observed, **we recommend proceeding with system-wide rollout** while continuing to monitor outcomes.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Overview**
- **34 branches** participated: 20 adopted the new procedure, 14 maintained the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22), with the switch implemented at week 15
- Analysis compares service score changes between the two groups before and after implementation

### **Service Score Impact**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| **Branches with new procedure** | 54.27 | 52.69 | **-1.58** |
| **Branches with old procedure** | 50.35 | 46.84 | **-3.52** |

**Estimated benefit of new procedure: +1.94 points**

This means the new procedure prevented approximately **2 points of decline** in service scores. On a scale where the average branch scores around 50, this represents a **4% relative improvement**.

### **Statistical Confidence**
- The estimated effect is **positive but not statistically significant** at conventional levels (p = 0.14)
- The 95% confidence interval ranges from **-0.6 to +4.5 points**
- This uncertainty reflects the limited sample size and observation period, not necessarily absence of effect

### **Why We Still Recommend Rollout**

1. **Effect is in the expected direction** — The new procedure performed as anticipated
2. **Practical significance** — A 2-point improvement is operationally meaningful
3. **Parallel pre-trends** — Before the switch, both groups followed similar trajectories, validating our comparison
4. **External factors affected all branches** — Both groups saw declines after week 15, suggesting seasonal or system-wide pressures. The new procedure appears to have provided protection against these headwinds
5. **Low risk** — No evidence of negative effects; implementation costs are manageable

---

## **Recommendations**

### **1. Approve System-Wide Rollout**
Proceed with implementing the new procedure across all remaining branches. The positive point estimate, combined with operational feedback from early-adopter branches, supports expansion.

### **2. Continue Monitoring**
Establish quarterly reviews of service scores for 12 months post-rollout to:
- Confirm the effect persists at scale
- Identify branches where implementation may need additional support
- Detect any emerging issues early

### **3. Investigate Broader Decline**
Both groups experienced score declines after week 15. We recommend examining:
- Seasonal staffing patterns
- Visitor volume changes
- External factors (e.g., community events, competing services)

Understanding these factors will help set realistic expectations and identify additional improvement opportunities.

### **4. Document Implementation Best Practices**
Capture lessons learned from the 20 early-adopter branches to support smooth rollout and consistent execution across the cooperative.

---

## **Conclusion**

While we cannot claim statistical certainty with the current data, the evidence points in a favorable direction. The new procedure appears to have cushioned branches against broader service score declines. Given the cooperative's commitment to continuous improvement and the low risk associated with rollout, **we recommend moving forward with system-wide implementation.**
