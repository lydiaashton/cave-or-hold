# **Library Branch Shelving Procedure: Impact Analysis**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative
**From:** Data Analysis Team
**Date:** Analysis based on weeks 6-22 service data

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving procedure is associated with a **modest improvement in service scores** (approximately +1.8 points or 3.8%), but the evidence is **not statistically conclusive** at conventional confidence levels. The data suggest a positive effect, but we cannot rule out the possibility that the observed improvement occurred by chance.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **cautious, phased rollout** to additional branches with continued monitoring, rather than immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect Estimate
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated improvement | **+1.83 points** |
| Percentage gain | **3.8%** |
| 95% Confidence interval | **-0.33 to +4.00 points** |

The confidence interval indicates the true effect could range from slightly negative to as high as 4 points.

### 2. Statistical Significance
- **P-value: 0.097** — marginally significant at the 10% level, but does not meet the conventional 5% standard
- Practical interpretation: There is approximately a 10% probability of observing this improvement (or larger) even if the new procedure had no real effect

### 3. Study Validity Checks
- **Parallel trends confirmed:** Before the switch (weeks 6-14), treatment and control branches showed similar score trajectories with no systematic divergence (p = 0.46)
- **Consistent post-switch pattern:** After week 15, the treatment-control gap was positive in all 8 weeks, ranging from +2.1 to +4.5 points
- **Results robust** to controls for staffing levels and visitor volume

### 4. Study Design
- **34 branches total:** 20 adopted the new procedure, 14 maintained the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data:** 9 weeks before the switch, 8 weeks after
- **Method:** Difference-in-differences analysis with branch-clustered standard errors

---

## **Next Steps**

### Recommended Actions

1. **Expand gradually** — Roll out the new procedure to 5-10 additional branches over the next quarter rather than all remaining branches at once

2. **Extend data collection** — Gather 8-12 more weeks of data to increase statistical power and confirm whether the effect persists over time

3. **Monitor implementation quality** — Verify that branches are following the new procedure consistently; variation in execution could be masking a stronger true effect

4. **Track additional metrics** — Consider measuring patron satisfaction, staff time savings, or item retrieval speed to capture benefits not reflected in the service score

5. **Set clear decision criteria** — Define in advance what evidence threshold (e.g., p < 0.05 with 2+ point improvement) would justify full system-wide adoption

### Risk Balance

| If we roll out now | If we wait for more evidence |
|-------------------|------------------------------|
| ✓ Service improves sooner if effect is real | ✓ Avoids costs if effect is not real |
| ✗ Incurs costs if effect is not real | ✗ Delays benefits if effect is real |

**The phased approach balances these risks** by allowing continued learning while limiting exposure.
