# **Memorandum to the Board**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure shows a **positive effect consistent with expectations**, but the evidence is **not yet statistically definitive**. Branches that adopted the new procedure improved their service scores by approximately **2.2 points** relative to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents about a **5% improvement** over the average service score.

However, with the current data, we cannot rule out that this improvement occurred by chance (p=0.10). The true effect could reasonably range from slightly negative to nearly +5 points.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **phased rollout** while continuing rigorous evaluation. The evidence points in the expected direction, but additional data will provide greater confidence before full system-wide adoption.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. The New Procedure Shows Positive Effects**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Branches with New Procedure (20 branches) | 45.0 | 47.8 | +2.7 |
| Branches with Old Procedure (14 branches) | 48.7 | 49.2 | +0.5 |
| **Estimated Effect of New Procedure** | | | **+2.2** |

The new procedure is associated with a **2.24-point improvement** in service scores after accounting for underlying trends.

### **2. Statistical Confidence Is Limited**

- **95% Confidence Interval:** -0.46 to +4.94 points
- **P-value:** 0.104 (above the conventional 0.05 threshold)
- **Interpretation:** The data are consistent with a meaningful positive effect, but also compatible with no effect

This uncertainty stems primarily from the limited number of branches (34 total) rather than any flaw in the analysis.

### **3. The Analysis Is Methodologically Sound**

- **Parallel trends verified:** Switched and control branches followed similar trajectories before the switch, supporting the validity of the comparison
- **Robust to controls:** Results hold when accounting for staffing levels and visit volumes
- **Appropriate methods:** Used difference-in-differences regression with standard errors clustered by branch

### **4. Effects Vary Week-to-Week**

Post-switch improvements were not uniform across all weeks. Some weeks showed stronger gains while others showed minimal change. This variability is common in service metrics and may reflect:
- Normal week-to-week fluctuation
- Staff learning curves with the new procedure
- External factors (holidays, staffing changes, etc.)

---

## **What This Means for the Cooperative**

| Scenario | Likelihood Based on Data | Implication |
|----------|-------------------------|-------------|
| New procedure has meaningful positive effect | **Moderate to High** | Rollout would improve patron experience |
| New procedure has small or no effect | **Moderate** | Rollout would have neutral impact |
| New procedure has negative effect | **Low** | Unlikely based on observed direction |

The weight of evidence favors the expected outcome—that the new procedure improves service scores—but certainty requires more data.

---

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended: Phased Rollout with Continued Evaluation**

1. **Expand adoption gradually** – Invite 5-10 additional branches to implement the new procedure over the next quarter

2. **Maintain evaluation framework** – Continue collecting weekly service scores using the same methodology

3. **Set decision checkpoint** – Reconvene in 3 months (approximately 12 additional weeks of data) to reassess with stronger statistical power

4. **Document implementation** – Track any deviations from the intended procedure to ensure fidelity

### **Alternative Options**

| Option | When to Consider |
|--------|------------------|
| **Full immediate rollout** | If the Board prioritizes potential benefits over statistical certainty |
| **Extended evaluation only** | If the Board prefers near-certain evidence before any additional adoption |
