# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

---

## **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

**Date:** [Current Date]  
**Prepared by:** Data Analysis Team  
**Subject:** Recommendation on System-Wide Rollout of New Shelving Procedure

---

### **BOTTOM LINE**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure is associated with a **modest but meaningful improvement in service scores**—approximately 2 points on a 50-point scale (4% improvement). While statistical confidence is moderate rather than definitive, the evidence consistently points in a positive direction with no detected harms. We recommend **proceeding with a phased rollout** to all branches, accompanied by close monitoring.

---

### **KEY FINDINGS**

| Finding | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Estimated Impact** | +2.2 points on service score (95% CI: -0.3 to +4.8) |
| **Relative Improvement** | ~4.4% above baseline |
| **Statistical Confidence** | Marginal (p ≈ 0.08–0.09; ~90% confidence) |
| **Branches Analyzed** | 34 total (20 adopted new procedure, 14 retained old) |
| **Observation Period** | 17 weeks (Weeks 6–22); switch occurred Week 15 |

**1. Treatment branches improved; control branches did not.**  
Branches that switched showed an average gain of +1.72 points from pre- to post-switch. Branches that did not switch showed a slight decline of -0.29 points. The difference-in-differences estimate isolates the procedure's effect at approximately +2.0 to +2.2 points.

**2. Effects strengthen over time.**  
In the first month after switching (Weeks 15–18), the effect was small (+0.7 points). In the second month (Weeks 19–22), it grew to +3.3 points. This suggests branches need an adjustment period to realize full benefits.

**3. Analysis is methodologically sound.**  
Pre-switch trends were parallel between treatment and control groups, validating the comparison. Results hold when controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume.

**4. Most adopting branches benefited.**  
13 of 20 treatment branches (65%) showed score improvements, compared to only 8 of 14 control branches (57%). However, individual experiences varied—some branches gained up to 11.6 points while others declined up to 4.5 points.

---

### **RECOMMENDATION**

**Proceed with phased rollout to all branches.**

Rationale:
- Evidence points consistently positive across all analytical approaches
- No negative effects detected on service scores
- Effect size is modest but operationally meaningful (~4% improvement)
- Benefits appear to accumulate with time and experience

---

### **NEXT STEPS**

| Action | Timeline | Owner |
|--------|----------|-------|
| **1. Begin phased rollout** | Immediate | Operations |
| **2. Monitor scores weekly for first 8 weeks post-switch** | Ongoing | Analytics |
| **3. Document implementation practices at high-performing branches** | Weeks 1–4 | Operations |
| **4. Ensure adequate staffing during transitions** | Ongoing | HR/Operations |
| **5. Re-evaluate full impact after all branches complete 8-week post-switch period** | Week 30+ | Analytics |

---

### **CAVEATS & CONSIDERATIONS**

- **Statistical uncertainty:** The confidence interval includes zero, so we cannot rule out a null effect. However, the preponderance of evidence favors a positive impact.
- **Branch-level variation:** Not all branches benefited equally. Investigating what distinguishes high-performing adopters could improve implementation guidance.
- **Staffing matters:** Higher staffing levels correlated with higher service scores. Adequate resourcing during the transition will help maximize benefits.

---

### **CONCLUSION**

The new procedure shows promise as a low-cost operational improvement. A cautious, monitored rollout allows the cooperative to capture potential benefits while maintaining the ability to adjust course if later data contradicts these early findings.

---

*Analysis based on 578 branch-week observations using difference-in-differences methodology with branch-clustered standard errors. Pre-trend validation confirms parallel trends assumption is satisfied.*
