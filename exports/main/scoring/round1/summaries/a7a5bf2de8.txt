# **Board Summary: New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure Evaluation**

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure shows a **positive but statistically inconclusive effect** on service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure improved by an average of 2 points (approximately 4%) more than branches that kept the old procedure. However, this difference is not statistically significant at conventional levels, meaning we cannot confidently rule out that the observed improvement occurred by chance. 

**Recommendation: Proceed with a cautious, phased rollout with continued monitoring rather than immediate system-wide implementation.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect Estimate

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated improvement from new procedure | **+2.05 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | **-0.49 to +4.59 points** |
| Statistical significance (p-value) | **0.114** (not significant) |
| Effect size relative to average score | **~4% improvement** |

### 2. What the Data Shows

- **Treatment branches** (20 branches that switched): Average service score increased from 49.4 to 50.3 (+0.9 points)
- **Control branches** (14 branches that did not switch): Average service score decreased from 47.1 to 46.0 (-1.1 points)
- **Net difference**: The treatment group outperformed the control group by 2.0 points after the switch

### 3. Branch-Level Results

- **55% of treatment branches** (11 of 20) showed improvement after switching
- **36% of control branches** (5 of 14) showed improvement during the same period
- Treatment branch improvements ranged from **-5.1 to +11.6 points**, indicating substantial variation in how branches responded

### 4. Validity Checks

- **Parallel trends assumption holds**: Before the switch, treatment and control branches showed similar week-to-week trends (p=0.60), supporting the validity of our comparison
- **Other factors matter**: Staffing levels and visit volumes are positively associated with service scores, as expected

---

## **Next Steps**

### Recommended Actions

1. **Do not proceed with immediate system-wide rollout.** The evidence is suggestive but not conclusive.

2. **Extend the observation period.** Continue monitoring for an additional 8-12 weeks to:
   - Increase statistical power
   - Determine if the positive trend persists or strengthens
   - Identify whether effects accumulate over time

3. **Investigate branch-level variation.** The wide range of outcomes suggests the procedure may work better in some contexts. Analyze which branch characteristics (size, staffing, visit volume, community demographics) predict success.

4. **Consider a staggered expansion.** Roll out the new procedure to 5-10 additional branches as a second pilot wave, using lessons learned to refine implementation.

5. **Monitor implementation fidelity.** Ensure branches that switched are actually following the new procedure as designed. Variation in adherence could explain inconsistent results.

6. **Revisit the decision in Q2.** With additional data, we can provide a more definitive recommendation on system-wide adoption.

---

## **Technical Notes**

- **Analysis method**: Difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch)
- **Data**: 578 observations across 34 branches, 17 weeks (weeks 6-22)
- **Switch point**: Week 15
- **Model controls**: Staffing index and visits index included in final specification
