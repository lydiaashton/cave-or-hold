# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

---

## **Subject: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

**Date:** November 2025  
**Prepared by:** Data Analysis Team

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.0–2.3 points** (about **4–5%**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically robust and consistent across analytical methods.

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout.**

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **1. Measurable Improvement in Service Quality**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches (425) | 51.3 | 53.5 | **+2.3** |
| Non-switched branches (284) | 51.0 | 51.2 | +0.2 |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | | **+2.0 to +2.3** |

### **2. Statistically Robust Results**

- **Effect size:** +2.3 points (95% confidence interval: +1.9 to +2.6)
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001 (highly significant)
- Results hold after controlling for staffing levels, visitor volume, and branch-specific characteristics

### **3. Broad-Based Improvement Across Branches**

- **73% of switched branches** saw their service scores improve
- Median improvement: **+2.2 points**
- The effect is not driven by a few outliers—improvement was widespread

### **4. Greatest Benefit for Underperforming Branches**

Branches with lower initial performance saw the largest gains:

| Initial Performance | Average Improvement |
|---------------------|---------------------|
| Lowest quartile | +3.8 points |
| Second quartile | +2.3 points |
| Third quartile | +1.6 points |
| Highest quartile | +1.3 points |

This suggests the new procedure is particularly effective at helping struggling branches catch up.

### **5. Valid Comparison Confirmed**

Analysis of pre-switch trends (weeks 6–14) confirms both groups were on similar trajectories before the procedure change, supporting the validity of our comparison.

---

## **ANALYSIS APPROACH**

We used a **difference-in-differences** design—the gold standard for evaluating policy changes when randomized trials aren't feasible:

- **Treatment group:** 425 branches that adopted the new procedure at week 15
- **Control group:** 284 branches that continued with the old procedure
- **Data:** 12,053 weekly observations across 709 branches (weeks 6–22)
- **Method:** Compared changes in service scores between groups, before and after the switch

This approach isolates the effect of the procedure change from other factors affecting all branches (e.g., seasonal variations, system-wide initiatives).
