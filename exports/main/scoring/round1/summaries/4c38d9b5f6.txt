# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Library Cooperative Board  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Impact of New Branch Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure is associated with a **modest improvement in service scores** (+2.3 points, approximately 5%). However, the evidence is **not statistically conclusive** at conventional levels. The board should consider a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** to balance potential benefits against implementation costs.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Overview**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **14 branches** maintained the old procedure as a control group
- Analysis compares service score changes between the two groups before and after the switch

### **Impact on Service Scores**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect of new procedure | **+2.31 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | **-0.43 to +5.05** |
| Statistical significance | **Not significant at 5% level (p=0.10)** |
| Relative improvement | **~4.6%** |

### **What This Means**
- Branches that switched improved by **2.38 points** on average; control branches improved by only **0.07 points**
- The procedure likely has a **positive effect**, but we cannot rule out the possibility of no effect
- There is **no evidence of harm** from the new procedure
- Pre-switch trends were nearly identical between groups, supporting the validity of the comparison

---

## **Recommendations**

### **Primary Recommendation: Phased Rollout**

Proceed with expanding the new procedure to additional branches while continuing to collect data. This approach:
- Captures potential benefits sooner rather than later
- Generates more data to confirm the effect
- Allows for course correction if issues emerge

### **Supporting Actions**

1. **Extend the evaluation period** — Collect 8–12 additional weeks of data to increase statistical confidence

2. **Monitor implementation quality** — Ensure consistent adoption across branches; variation in implementation may be diluting the measured effect

3. **Track costs alongside benefits** — If the new procedure requires additional staff time or resources, compare those costs against the ~5% service improvement

4. **Review branch-level results** — Identify whether certain branch types benefit more, which could inform targeted implementation

---

## **Decision Framework**

| Board Priority | Recommended Action |
|---------------|-------------------|
| Maximize service quality | Roll out broadly; effect is likely positive |
| Minimize resource risk | Continue pilot; gather more data first |
| Balanced approach | **Phased rollout with monitoring** (recommended) |
