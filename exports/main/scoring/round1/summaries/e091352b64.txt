# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 2025  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure  

---

## **Executive Summary**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure has produced the expected improvement in service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure showed a **net gain of approximately 1.9 points** (roughly 4% above baseline) compared to branches that retained the old procedure. While statistical certainty is moderate, the weight of evidence supports proceeding with a system-wide rollout.

**Recommendation:** Approve phased rollout to all branches with continued monitoring.

---

## **Bottom Line**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did the new procedure improve service scores? | **Yes** — by approximately 1.9 points |
| Is the result statistically reliable? | **Moderately** — 95% confidence the effect is positive |
| Should we roll out to all branches? | **Yes** — with phased implementation and ongoing monitoring |

---

## **Analysis Overview**

### Study Design
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22 of the year)
- **20 branches** switched to the new procedure in week 15 (treatment group)
- **14 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Service scores averaged ~50 across all branches (higher is better)

### Methodology
We used a **difference-in-differences** approach, which isolates the effect of the new procedure by comparing:
1. How treatment branches changed before vs. after switching
2. How control branches changed over the same period
3. The difference between these two changes

This method accounts for broader trends (e.g., seasonal effects) that might affect all branches simultaneously.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Service Score Changes

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 49.7 | 50.9 | +1.2 |
| Non-switched branches | 49.6 | 48.9 | -0.8 |
| **Net effect (DiD)** | — | — | **+1.9** |

### Statistical Confidence

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect | +1.9 points |
| Bootstrap standard error | 1.2 points |
| 95% confidence interval | -0.3 to +4.4 points |
| Probability effect is positive | 95.5% |
| Probability effect exceeds 1 point | 77.4% |

### What This Means
- The new procedure is **very likely beneficial** (95%+ confidence)
- The most probable improvement is **1.5 to 2.5 points**
- There is a small chance the true effect is negligible or slightly negative

## **Supporting Evidence**

1. **Parallel pre-trends:** Before week 15, both groups showed similar score patterns with no systematic differences, validating our comparison approach.

2. **Robust to controls:** The result holds when accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume, which also influence service scores.

3. **Consistent across methods:** Both regression analysis and bootstrap resampling produce similar estimates (~1.9 points).

4. **Practical significance:** A 4% improvement in service scores is meaningful for patron satisfaction and operational performance.

---

## **Recommendations**

### Immediate Actions

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to remaining 14 branches
2. **Implement in phases** (e.g., 4–5 branches per month) to manage training and support
3. **Document best practices** from the 20 early-adopter branches

### Ongoing Monitoring

1. **Continue tracking service scores** for at least 12 more weeks to confirm sustained benefits
2. **Monitor implementation fidelity** to ensure branches follow the new procedure correctly
3. **Investigate branch-level variation** to identify factors that maximize benefits

### Future Analysis

1. **Cost-benefit assessment:** Quantify implementation costs against the value of improved service scores
2. **Long-term impact study:** Assess whether benefits persist, grow, or diminish over time
3. **Mechanism analysis:** Understand *why* the new procedure works (e.g., faster retrieval, better organization, staff efficiency)

---

## **Caveats and Limitations**

- **Moderate statistical certainty:** While evidence points positive, we cannot rule out a smaller effect with high confidence
- **Short observation window:** Eight weeks post-implementation may not capture long-term dynamics
- **Unmeasured factors:** External events (staffing changes, local conditions) could influence results

---

## **Conclusion**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure has delivered the expected improvement in service scores. The evidence supports expanding the procedure to all branches while maintaining careful monitoring. This change positions the cooperative to provide better service to patrons across the system.
