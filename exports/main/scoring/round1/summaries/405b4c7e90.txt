# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Library Cooperative Board  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

The new procedure shows a **positive effect in the expected direction**, but the evidence is **not strong enough to justify a system-wide rollout**. Branches using the new procedure scored approximately **2 points higher** on average than expected, consistent with our hypothesis. However, statistical uncertainty means we cannot confidently attribute this improvement to the procedure itself rather than random variation.

**Recommendation:** Continue the pilot with extended monitoring before making a rollout decision.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### 1. The Effect Is Positive—But Uncertain

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect | **+2.0 points** |
| 95% Confidence interval | **−0.5 to +4.4 points** |
| Statistical significance | Not significant (p = 0.12) |

The point estimate aligns with expectations—the new procedure is associated with higher service scores. However, the confidence interval is wide enough that we cannot rule out the possibility of no effect or even a small negative effect.

### 2. What Changed After the Switch

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Branches with new procedure | 45.7 | 49.0 | **+3.3** |
| Branches with old procedure | 47.3 | 48.5 | **+1.2** |
| **Net difference** | | | **+2.1** |

Both groups improved after week 15, but branches with the new procedure improved more. The net difference of 2 points is our best estimate of the procedure's impact.

### 3. Practical Significance

If the 2-point effect is real, it represents approximately **4% improvement** over the average service score of 50. This would be a meaningful gain for patron experience. However, given the statistical uncertainty, the true effect could reasonably be:

- **As low as −0.5 points** (negligible negative effect)
- **As high as +4.4 points** (substantial positive effect)

### 4. Why the Evidence Is Inconclusive

- **Limited post-switch data:** Only 8 weeks of observations after the change
- **Modest sample size:** 34 branches total, split between treatment and control
- **Pre-existing differences:** The two groups had different score patterns before the switch, which complicates comparison
- **Natural variation:** Service scores fluctuate week-to-week for many reasons unrelated to the procedure

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

### Immediate Actions

1. **Extend data collection by 8–12 weeks.** This will:
   - Narrow the confidence interval
   - Provide clearer evidence on whether the effect is real
   - Allow detection of smaller but meaningful effects

2. **Maintain current pilot structure.** Keep the 17 branches on the new procedure and 17 on the old procedure to preserve the comparison.

3. **Document implementation details.** Track whether branches are following the new procedure consistently—variation in execution could obscure true effects.

### Decision Timeline

| Timeline | Action |
|----------|--------|
| **Now** | Continue pilot; collect additional data |
| **8–12 weeks** | Re-analyze with extended dataset |
| **After re-analysis** | Make rollout decision based on updated evidence |

### Decision Criteria

| Result | Recommendation |
|--------|----------------|
| Effect remains ~2 points with tighter confidence interval (CI excludes 0) | **Proceed with rollout** |
| Effect grows larger and becomes significant | **Prioritize rollout** |
| Effect shrinks toward zero or becomes negative | **Discontinue new procedure** |
| Effect remains positive but inconclusive | **Consider phased rollout** with continued monitoring |

---

## **CONCLUSION**

The new procedure is performing as hoped—service scores moved in the positive direction. However, the board should resist making a permanent commitment until the evidence is clearer. A modest investment in additional monitoring now will prevent a costly mistake later, whether that means rolling out an ineffective procedure or abandoning a beneficial one prematurely.

**We recommend continuing the pilot and reconvening in 8–12 weeks with updated analysis.**
