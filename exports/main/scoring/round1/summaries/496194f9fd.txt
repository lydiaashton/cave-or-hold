# **Memorandum to the Board**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2025  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure shows a **positive but inconclusive effect** on service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure improved by approximately **2 points** relative to those that kept the old procedure. While this moves in the expected direction, the evidence is **not statistically strong enough** to confirm the improvement is real rather than random variation. We recommend a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than immediate system-wide adoption.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **34 branches** participated (20 adopted new procedure, 14 kept old procedure)
- **17 weeks** of data collected (weeks 6–22)
- New procedure implemented starting **week 15**
- Service scores average around 50 (higher is better)

### Results Summary

| | New Procedure Branches | Old Procedure Branches |
|---|---|---|
| Before switch (weeks 6–14) | 47.8 | 52.5 |
| After switch (weeks 15–22) | 49.1 | 51.9 |
| **Change** | **+1.3** | **-0.6** |

**Net effect of new procedure: +1.9 points**

### What This Means

✓ **Direction is correct** – Service scores improved as expected  
✓ **Branches that switched gained ground** – They narrowed the gap with control branches  
⚠ **Effect is modest** – About 2 points on a ~50-point scale (approximately 4%)  
⚠ **Statistical uncertainty is high** – We cannot rule out that this result occurred by chance  
⚠ **Switching branches started behind** – They scored lower even before adopting the new procedure

### Visual Evidence

Two charts accompany this memo:
1. **Service Score Trends** – Shows weekly averages for both groups over time
2. **Event Study** – Shows the week-by-week difference between groups with confidence intervals

The charts show the treatment group trending upward after the switch, but confidence intervals remain wide throughout the post-switch period.

---

## **Recommendations**

### 1. Proceed with Cautious, Phased Rollout
The positive direction supports moving forward, but uncertainty warrants a measured approach. Roll out the new procedure to additional branches in stages rather than all at once.

### 2. Extend the Evaluation Period
Eight weeks of post-implementation data is limited. Continue collecting service scores for at least 3–6 more months to determine whether effects:
- Strengthen over time
- Stabilize at current levels
- Diminish

### 3. Establish Clear Success Criteria
Define what constitutes meaningful improvement before full rollout. For example:
- Minimum 3-point improvement with statistical significance (p < 0.05)
- Sustained improvement over 12+ weeks

### 4. Investigate Branch Selection Patterns
Branches that volunteered to switch had lower baseline scores. Understanding why certain branches adopted early—and whether they benefit differently—could inform targeted rollout decisions.

### 5. Monitor Implementation Fidelity
Ensure branches adopting the new procedure are implementing it consistently. Variation in execution could mask true effects.

---

## **Decision Framework**

| Scenario | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Effect grows to 3+ points with statistical significance | Proceed with full rollout |
| Effect remains at ~2 points without significance | Continue phased approach; reassess in 6 months |
| Effect diminishes or turns negative | Halt rollout; investigate implementation issues |

---

## **Conclusion**

The new procedure shows promise and moves service scores in the right direction. However, the evidence is not yet strong enough to justify immediate system-wide adoption. A phased rollout with rigorous continued evaluation balances the potential benefits against the uncertainty in the current findings.
