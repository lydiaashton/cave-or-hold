# **MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2025  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure  

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure shows a **modest positive effect** on service scores, but the evidence is **not statistically conclusive**. Branches that adopted the new procedure improved their service scores by approximately **2 points** relative to branches that kept the old procedure. However, this improvement could plausibly be due to chance variation.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **phased rollout** and continue monitoring for 3–6 months before making a system-wide implementation decision.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### Study Design
- **34 branches** participated (20 adopted new procedure, 14 kept old procedure)
- **17 weeks** of data collected (weeks 6–22)
- New procedure implemented at **week 15**
- Service scores average ~50 (higher is better)

### Results Summary

| | New Procedure Branches | Old Procedure Branches |
|---|---|---|
| Before switch (weeks 6–14) | 47.8 | 52.5 |
| After switch (weeks 15–22) | 49.1 | 51.9 |
| **Change** | **+1.3** | **-0.6** |

**Net effect of new procedure: +1.9 points** (treatment improvement minus control change)

### What This Means

1. **Positive direction, uncertain magnitude:** The new procedure is associated with improved service scores, but we cannot rule out that this improvement occurred by chance.

2. **Treatment branches started behind:** The 20 branches that switched had lower baseline scores (about 4.6 points lower). The new procedure appears to have helped them partially catch up.

3. **No evidence of growing benefits:** The effect does not appear to strengthen over the 8-week post-implementation period.

4. **Controls don't change the conclusion:** Accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume does not alter the main finding.

---

## **RECOMMENDATIONS**

### Immediate Actions

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Continue phased rollout to interested branches | Positive direction warrants continued adoption, but uncertainty argues against mandating system-wide |
| **2** | Extend monitoring period by 3–6 months | Eight weeks is insufficient to detect stable effects; longer data will improve decision confidence |
| **3** | Investigate baseline differences between branches | Understanding why some branches started at a disadvantage could inform targeted implementation |

### Decision Criteria for Full Rollout

We recommend proceeding to system-wide implementation only if future data shows:
- Effect size of **3+ points** on service score index, **and**
- **Statistical significance** at p < 0.05 level

### Additional Analysis Opportunities

- Examine whether certain branch characteristics (size, location, staffing) predict better outcomes
- Assess cost-benefit tradeoffs if implementation requires additional resources
- Survey branch staff on operational impacts not captured in service scores
