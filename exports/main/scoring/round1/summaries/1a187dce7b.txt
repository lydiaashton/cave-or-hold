# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

---

## **Subject: Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

**Date:** November 2026  
**Analysis Period:** Weeks 6–22 (17 weeks)  
**Data:** 709 branches, 12,053 weekly observations

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure experienced a statistically significant increase of **1.9 points** on the service score index compared to branches that retained the old procedure. We recommend approving a systemwide rollout.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **Study Overview**
- **425 branches** switched to the new procedure at week 15; **284 branches** continued with the old procedure
- **9 weeks** of pre-switch data (weeks 6–14) and **8 weeks** of post-switch data (weeks 15–22)
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, which isolates the effect of the new procedure by comparing changes in the treatment group to changes in the control group

### **Service Score Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|:-------------:|:------------:|:------:|
| Control (old procedure) | 49.4 | 48.6 | −0.8 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 50.8 | 51.9 | +1.1 |
| **Net Effect of New Procedure** | | | **+1.9** |

### **Statistical Confidence**
- **Treatment effect:** +1.93 points
- **95% Confidence Interval:** 0.86 to 3.01 points
- **P-value:** 0.0004 (significant at the 1% level)
- **Interpretation:** We are 95% confident the true effect lies between 0.9 and 3.0 points; the probability this result occurred by chance is less than 1 in 1,000

### **Validation Checks**
- **Parallel trends confirmed:** Treatment and control branches followed similar trajectories before the switch, supporting the validity of the comparison
- **Covariate balance:** Staffing levels and visitor volume were similar across groups, ruling out these factors as alternative explanations
- **Robustness:** The effect persists after controlling for staffing and visits in regression models

### **Practical Significance**
- The average branch scores approximately **50** on the service index
- A **1.9-point gain** represents roughly a **4% improvement** in service quality
- Control branches actually declined slightly post-period (−0.8 points), making the treatment group's improvement even more notable

---

## **RECOMMENDATION**

**Proceed with systemwide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all remaining branches.

## **APPENDIX: METHODOLOGY NOTE**

*Analysis employs difference-in-differences regression with branch fixed effects and cluster-robust standard errors (clustered at branch level). This approach controls for time-invariant branch characteristics and accounts for within-branch correlation over time. The parallel trends assumption was verified by examining pre-period trajectories.*
