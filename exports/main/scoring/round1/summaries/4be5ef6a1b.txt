# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD OF DIRECTORS**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Prepared by:** Data Analysis Office

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation procedure shows a **positive but statistically uncertain** effect on service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure improved by approximately **2 points** more than branches that kept the old procedure. However, this result is not statistically significant at conventional levels (p=0.12), meaning we cannot confidently rule out chance as an explanation. The true effect could range from slightly negative (-0.5 points) to meaningfully positive (+4.4 points).

**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **phased rollout** while continuing to monitor results. The evidence is promising enough to warrant expansion, but not strong enough to justify immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Overview**
- **34 branches** participated: 20 adopted the new procedure, 14 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Analysis compares service score changes before and after the switch between the two groups

### **Service Score Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 47.3 | 48.5 | +1.2 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 45.7 | 49.0 | +3.3 |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | | | **+2.0** |

### **Statistical Summary**
- **Estimated effect:** +1.95 points (adjusted for staffing and visitor levels)
- **95% Confidence Interval:** -0.49 to +4.39 points
- **P-value:** 0.117
- **Relative improvement:** ~4% above treatment group baseline

### **Validity Assessment**
✓ **Parallel trends confirmed:** Both groups showed similar trajectories before the switch  
✓ **No evidence of harm:** Lower bound of confidence interval is near zero  
✓ **Results robust:** Effect size remains stable after controlling for staffing and visitor volume

The new procedure is associated with improved service scores, but the evidence is not yet conclusive. A phased expansion allows the cooperative to capture potential benefits while managing risk and building a stronger evidence base for a future system-wide decision.
