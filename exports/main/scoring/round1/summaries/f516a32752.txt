# **Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure Evaluation**

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure is associated with a **modest improvement in service scores** (+1.9 points, or ~4%), but this effect is **not statistically significant** at conventional levels. The evidence is suggestive but not conclusive. We recommend a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** tracked over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15 (treatment group)
- **14 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in both groups before and after the switch

### **Results**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Estimated treatment effect** | +1.89 service score points |
| **Percentage improvement** | 3.8% above baseline |
| **Statistical significance** | p = 0.107 (not significant at 0.05 level) |
| **95% Confidence Interval** | -0.41 to +4.19 points |

### **What This Means**
- Branches that switched to the new procedure improved their service scores by about **2.4 points** after the switch
- Control branches (old procedure) improved by only **0.7 points** over the same period
- The **net effect** attributable to the new procedure is approximately **1.9 points**
- However, the confidence interval includes zero, meaning we cannot rule out that this improvement occurred by chance

### **Supporting Evidence**
- **Parallel pre-trends confirmed**: Before week 15, treatment and control branches showed similar trends (p = 0.60), validating our comparison approach
- **Visits matter**: Higher visitor volumes are significantly associated with better service scores, suggesting the procedure may work better in busier branches
- **No negative effects**: There is no evidence the new procedure harmed service quality at any branch

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Continue the pilot** for an additional 8–12 weeks to gather more data and improve statistical precision

2. **Expand monitoring** to track:
   - Branch-level variation (some branches may benefit more than others)
   - Staff time/cost implications of the new procedure
   - Patron satisfaction metrics beyond the service score index

3. **Consider targeted rollout** to branches with characteristics similar to the high-performing treatment branches

4. **Reconvene in Q2** with updated analysis to make a final go/no-go decision on system-wide implementation

---

## **Technical Notes**

- Analysis controls for branch fixed effects, staffing levels, and visitor volume
- Standard errors are clustered at the branch level to account for repeated observations
- Service score baseline average: 50.4 points (higher is better)
