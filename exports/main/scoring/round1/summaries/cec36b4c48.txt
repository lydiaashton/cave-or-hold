# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Analysis Period:** Weeks 6–22 (17 weeks of data)

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure **improved service scores as expected**, with switched branches gaining an estimated **1.5 points more** than non-switched branches. However, the evidence is **not statistically conclusive**—we cannot confidently rule out that some of this improvement occurred by chance. We recommend a **phased expansion with continued evaluation** rather than immediate system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### What We Compared
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure in week 15
- **14 branches** continued with the old procedure (comparison group)
- Service scores tracked weekly before and after the change

### Results Summary

| Metric | Switched Branches | Non-Switched Branches |
|--------|------------------|----------------------|
| Average score before | 47.3 | 48.0 |
| Average score after | 51.6 | 50.8 |
| **Improvement** | **+4.3** | **+2.8** |
| **Net effect** | | **+1.5 points** |

### What This Means
- **Direction is correct:** The new procedure moved service scores in the expected direction
- **Magnitude is modest:** The 1.5-point net gain represents approximately **3% improvement** relative to baseline scores
- **Uncertainty remains:** The 95% confidence interval ranges from -0.6 to +3.7 points, meaning the true effect could be zero or moderately positive
- **Broadly beneficial:** 18 of 20 switched branches (90%) showed improvement after adoption

### Validity Check
Pre-switch trends were similar between groups, confirming that switched and non-switched branches were comparable before the procedure change. This supports the credibility of our estimates.

---

## **Recommendations**

### 1. Proceed with Cautious Expansion
The positive direction of results supports expanding the new procedure, but the statistical uncertainty argues against immediate system-wide implementation.

### 2. Extend the Evaluation Period
Collect 8–12 additional weeks of data to:
- Increase statistical confidence in the effect estimate
- Determine whether benefits are sustained over time
- Identify any seasonal patterns in performance

### 3. Investigate Branch-Level Variation
Two switched branches experienced score declines after adoption. Understanding why some branches benefit more than others will help target implementation and provide support where needed.

### 4. Assess Implementation Costs
Before full rollout, quantify the operational costs of the new procedure relative to the observed service score gains to ensure cost-effectiveness.

### 5. Monitor Additional Outcomes
Consider tracking supplementary metrics (staff efficiency, patron wait times, circulation rates) to capture the full impact of the procedure change.

---

## **Conclusion**

The new rotation and shelving procedure shows **promising results consistent with expectations**. While we lack definitive statistical proof of effectiveness, the positive direction, broad branch-level improvements, and sound study design support continued investment in this approach. A phased rollout with rigorous monitoring offers the best path forward.
