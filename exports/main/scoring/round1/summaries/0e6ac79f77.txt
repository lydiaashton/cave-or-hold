# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation procedure shows a **positive effect on service scores** (+1.75 points, approximately 3.4% improvement), consistent with expectations. However, the statistical evidence is **suggestive rather than definitive** (p = 0.10). Given the positive direction, meaningful effect size, and absence of negative impacts, we recommend **proceeding with rollout while continuing to monitor outcomes**.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Overview**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure at week 15; **14 branches** maintained the old procedure as a control group
- Service scores average ~50 points; higher scores indicate better service

### **Impact on Service Scores**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| **Branches with New Procedure** | 52.01 | 53.79 | **+1.78** |
| **Branches with Old Procedure** | 50.89 | 50.92 | +0.03 |
| **Net Effect (New Procedure)** | — | — | **+1.75** |

### **Statistical Summary**
- **Estimated effect:** +1.75 points
- **95% confidence interval:** -0.34 to +3.84 points
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.10 (marginally significant)
- **Practical significance:** ~3.4% improvement over baseline

### **Supporting Evidence**
1. **Direction matches expectations** — Service scores improved where the new procedure was implemented
2. **Control group remained stable** — Rules out system-wide factors driving the improvement
3. **Pre-trends were parallel** — Treatment and control branches had similar trajectories before the switch, validating the comparison
4. **Results hold with controls** — Effect persists after accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume

---

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with system-wide rollout, with monitoring.**

**Rationale:**
- The effect is in the expected direction and operationally meaningful
- No evidence of adverse effects on service quality
- Waiting for stronger statistical certainty would delay potential benefits across all branches
- The confidence interval suggests the true effect is likely positive (the lower bound is only slightly below zero)
