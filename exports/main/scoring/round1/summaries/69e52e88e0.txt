# **MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** [Current Date]  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure  

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new shelving and stock rotation procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw their service scores increase by approximately **2.1 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This finding is statistically significant and contradicts the initial expectation that the procedure change would make no difference.

**Recommendation:** Approve system-wide rollout of the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Impact on Service Scores

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Branches keeping old procedure | 47.6 | 48.4 | +0.8 |
| Branches with new procedure | 50.8 | 53.7 | +2.9 |
| **Effect of new procedure** | | | **+2.1** |

The new procedure contributed approximately **2.1 points** of improvement beyond normal week-to-week variation. On a scale where the average branch scores about 50, this represents a **4% improvement** in service quality.

### Statistical Confidence

- **Confidence level:** 99% (p < 0.01)
- **Confidence interval:** The true effect is very likely between 0.8 and 3.4 points
- **Effect size:** 0.22 standard deviations (small-to-moderate practical significance)

### Study Quality

- **150 branches** participated (90 switched, 60 did not)
- **17 weeks** of data per branch (9 weeks before, 8 weeks after)
- **Complete data:** No branches dropped out; all observations accounted for
- **Valid comparison:** Branches showed similar trends before the switch, supporting a causal interpretation

---

## **Context**

The analysis controlled for:
- Weekly staffing levels
- Visitor volume
- Permanent differences between individual branches

The positive effect persisted after accounting for all these factors, indicating the procedure change itself drove the improvement.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches.

2. **Monitor during implementation:** Track service scores for 8-10 weeks after each branch switches to confirm the effect replicates across the system.

3. **Document best practices:** Capture what switching branches did to implement the procedure successfully, and share with branches yet to switch.

4. **Consider cost implications:** If the new procedure has different operating costs than the old one, complete a cost-benefit analysis to quantify the return on investment.

5. **Long-term tracking:** Continue monitoring service scores quarterly to ensure improvements are sustained over time.

---

## **Conclusion**

The data provides clear evidence that the new shelving and stock rotation procedure improves service quality. While we expected no difference, the new procedure delivered a measurable, statistically significant gain. Rolling this out system-wide should raise service scores across the cooperative.
