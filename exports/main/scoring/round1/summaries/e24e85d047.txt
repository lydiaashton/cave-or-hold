# **Board Memorandum**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure shows a **positive but statistically uncertain effect** on service scores. Branches that switched improved by approximately **2.1 points (4.3%)** relative to branches that kept the old procedure. However, this result is **not statistically significant** (p=0.11), meaning we cannot confidently rule out random variation as the cause. 

**Recommendation:** Do not proceed with a full rollout at this time. Instead, **extend the pilot program** to gather more data and reduce uncertainty before making a system-wide decision.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
| | |
|---|---|
| **Branches studied** | 34 total (20 switched, 14 did not) |
| **Observation period** | 17 weeks (weeks 6–22) |
| **Switch date** | Week 15 |
| **Method** | Difference-in-differences comparison |

### Results Summary

| Metric | Switched Branches | Non-Switched Branches |
|--------|-------------------|----------------------|
| Average score before switch | 49.7 | 50.9 |
| Average score after switch | 51.7 | 50.9 |
| **Change** | **+2.0 points** | **0.0 points** |

**Estimated effect of new procedure: +2.15 points**  
**95% Confidence Interval: -0.46 to +4.77 points**  
**Statistical significance: p = 0.11**

### What This Means
- The **point estimate is positive** and aligns with expectations
- The **confidence interval includes zero**, so we cannot be confident the effect is real
- There is an **11% chance** of observing this result (or larger) even if the procedure has no true effect
- **70% of switched branches** improved vs. **50% of non-switched branches**
- The analysis confirms **no pre-existing differences** in trends between the two groups, validating our comparison

### Why the Uncertainty?
With only 34 branches, this study can reliably detect effects of about **3.6 points or larger**. The observed 2.1-point effect is meaningful but smaller than what this study design can confirm with high confidence.

---

## **Next Steps**

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| **1** | **Extend the pilot by 8–12 weeks** | More data will narrow the confidence interval and clarify whether the effect is real |
| **2** | **Investigate branch-level variation** | Some branches gained up to 10 points; others lost ground. Identify what distinguishes successful adopters |
| **3** | **Define decision criteria in advance** | Set a clear threshold (e.g., p<0.05 or lower CI bound >0) for when to proceed with full rollout |
| **4** | **Consider phased expansion** | If operationally feasible, gradually switch additional branches while continuing evaluation |
| **5** | **Monitor secondary outcomes** | Track whether the procedure affects staffing efficiency, patron wait times, or other operational metrics |

---

## **Conclusion**

The new procedure is **promising but unproven**. The evidence points in the right direction, and there is no indication of harm. However, the current data does not meet the standard of evidence needed to justify the cost and disruption of a cooperative-wide rollout. 

**Extending the evaluation period is the most prudent course of action.** This will provide the board with greater confidence in the decision while allowing branches already using the new procedure to continue benefiting from any real improvements.
