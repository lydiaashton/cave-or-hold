# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Library Cooperative Board  
**FROM:** Data Analytics Team  
**DATE:** Current  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure  

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure shows a **positive but statistically inconclusive effect** on branch service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure improved by approximately **1.7 points** relative to branches that kept the old procedure, but this difference is not statistically significant at conventional levels (p=0.14). 

The 95% confidence interval ranges from **-0.5 to +3.9 points**, meaning we cannot rule out either no effect or a modest positive effect. However, the data suggest the new procedure is unlikely to be harmful.

**Recommendation:** Do not mandate system-wide rollout at this time. Instead, continue monitoring existing adopters and consider voluntary expansion while collecting additional data to reach a more definitive conclusion.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Overview**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Total branches | 34 |
| Branches using new procedure | 20 |
| Branches using old procedure | 14 |
| Weeks of data | 17 (weeks 6-22) |
| Switch implemented | Week 15 |

### **Service Score Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 52.31 | 49.93 | **-2.38** |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 50.48 | 49.91 | **-0.57** |

**Difference-in-Differences Estimate: +1.69 points**

Treatment branches maintained relatively stable scores after adoption, while control branches experienced a notable decline. The +1.69 point estimate reflects that treatment branches performed better than they would have if they had followed the control group's trajectory.

### **Statistical Assessment**

| Measure | Value |
|---------|-------|
| Estimated effect | +1.69 points |
| Standard error | 1.13 |
| P-value | 0.144 |
| 95% Confidence interval | -0.52 to +3.91 |
| Effect as % of baseline | 3.3% |

**Interpretation:** The effect is not statistically significant at the 0.05 level. With the current sample size, we have 80% power to detect effects of approximately 3.2 points or larger. The observed effect (1.69 points) falls below this threshold.

### **Validity Checks**
- **Parallel trends assumption:** Supported. Pre-switch data shows no significant differential trend between treatment and control groups (p=0.94).
- **Control variables:** Accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume does not materially change the estimated effect.
- **Staffing impact:** Higher staffing is significantly associated with higher service scores (p=0.01).

---

## **Next Steps**

### **Immediate Actions**

1. **Maintain status quo on rollout** — Evidence is insufficient to justify mandatory system-wide adoption.

2. **Continue monitoring** the 20 branches already using the new procedure. Each additional week of data improves statistical power.

3. **Invite voluntary adoption** by 5-10 additional branches to increase sample size while preserving a control group for ongoing evaluation.

### **Further Analysis (Recommended Timeline: 8-12 Weeks)**

1. **Extend observation period** — Collect 8-10 additional weeks of data to improve precision of the effect estimate.

2. **Subgroup analysis** — Examine whether effects vary by branch size, location, urban/rural status, or baseline performance.

3. **Cost-benefit assessment** — Quantify operational costs of the new procedure relative to the magnitude of service score improvements.

4. **Staff feedback** — Gather qualitative input from branch managers on implementation experience and perceived benefits.

### **Decision Framework**

| Future Finding | Recommended Action |
|----------------|-------------------|
| Effect confirmed positive (p<0.05) | Proceed with phased system-wide rollout |
| Effect remains positive but inconclusive | Continue voluntary adoption with monitoring |
| Effect turns negative or null | Revert treatment branches to old procedure |

---

## **Appendix: Methodology Notes**

- **Analytical approach:** Difference-in-differences regression
- **Standard errors:** Cluster-robust (clustered by branch) to account for within-branch correlation over time
- **Controls:** Staffing index and visits index
