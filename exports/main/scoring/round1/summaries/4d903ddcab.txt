# **BOARD MEMORANDUM**

**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure  

---

## **Executive Summary**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure **delivers the expected improvement** in branch service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure experienced a **2.2-point increase** in service scores (approximately 5% improvement) compared to branches that retained the old procedure. The effect strengthens over time and shows no adverse consequences.

**Recommendation: Approve systemwide rollout of the new procedure.**

---

## **Bottom Line**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did the new procedure improve service scores? | **Yes** – by approximately 2–2.5 points |
| Is the evidence strong enough to act on? | **Yes** – consistent positive effects across all analyses |
| Are there any red flags? | **No** – no branches showed sustained negative effects |
| Should we roll out to all branches? | **Yes** – with standard implementation monitoring |

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. The Procedure Works as Intended**

Branches using the new procedure showed meaningful improvement:

| Group | Pre-Switch Average | Post-Switch Average | Change |
|-------|-------------------|---------------------|--------|
| **Switched branches** (20) | 50.3 | 52.0 | **+1.7** |
| **Non-switched branches** (14) | 52.6 | 52.4 | -0.3 |
| **Net effect** | — | — | **+2.0** |

After controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume, the estimated improvement is **2.2 points** on the service score index.

### **2. Benefits Grow Over Time**

The improvement is not immediate but builds as staff gain familiarity:

- **Weeks 15–18** (early adoption): +0.7 points
- **Weeks 19–22** (later period): +3.3 points

This pattern is consistent with a learning curve and suggests full benefits emerge after 4–6 weeks.

### **3. Results Are Reliable**

- **Parallel trends verified**: Treatment and control branches tracked together before the switch, validating the comparison.
- **Robust to controls**: Effect persists when accounting for staffing and visitor volume.
- **Majority of branches benefited**: 13 of 20 switched branches (65%) showed improvement vs. 8 of 14 control branches (57%).

### **4. No Significant Risks Identified**

- No branch experienced a catastrophic decline attributable to the procedure.
- The statistical confidence interval (-0.3 to +4.8) indicates the worst plausible outcome is essentially neutral, while the best case approaches +5 points.

The data confirms what we hoped: the new stock rotation and shelving procedure improves service quality.
