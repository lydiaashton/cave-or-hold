# **Board Memorandum**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2025  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation procedure shows **promising but inconclusive evidence** of improving service scores. Branches using the new procedure gained approximately **2 points** on the service score index compared to branches that kept the old procedure. However, this effect is **not statistically definitive**, and the improvement appeared several weeks after implementation rather than immediately. We recommend **extending the pilot period** before committing to a system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measured Impact on Service Scores

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|---------------|---------------|--------|
| Branches with new procedure (20 branches) | 50.6 | 55.5 | +4.9 |
| Branches with old procedure (14 branches) | 50.1 | 53.3 | +3.2 |
| **Attributable to new procedure** | | | **+2.0** |

- **Effect size:** +2.0 points (approximately 4% improvement over baseline)
- **Statistical confidence:** Marginal (p=0.069); the 95% confidence interval ranges from -0.2 to +4.2 points
- **Practical meaning:** The true effect could be zero, modestly positive, or moderately positive

### 2. Timing of the Effect

The improvement did not occur immediately when branches switched procedures in week 15:

- **Weeks 15-18:** No clear benefit from the new procedure
- **Weeks 19-20:** Strongest positive effect (+4 to +5 points)
- **Weeks 21-22:** Effect diminished; treatment and control branches performed similarly

This pattern suggests either a **learning curve** for staff adapting to the new procedure or that the effect may not be sustained over time.

### 3. Context and Considerations

- **Both groups improved** after week 15, suggesting seasonal factors or other cooperative-wide changes may have influenced scores
- **Pre-existing differences** existed between treatment and control branches before the switch, which complicates interpretation
- **Staffing levels** were positively associated with service scores across all branches

---

## **Next Steps**

### Immediate Actions (Next 30 Days)

1. **Extend the pilot by 8-12 weeks** to determine whether the effect stabilizes, grows, or fades entirely.

2. **Conduct branch-level interviews** with the 20 treatment branches to identify implementation challenges and best practices.

3. **Review weeks 19-20 in detail** to understand what drove the strongest positive results during that period.

### Decision Timeline

| Timeline | Action |
|----------|--------|
| **Now** | Continue current pilot; no system-wide changes |
| **60 days** | Re-analyze data with additional weeks; assess effect sustainability |
| **90 days** | Board decision on phased rollout or pilot modification |

### Rollout Considerations (If Proceeding)

Should the board decide to move forward before additional data is collected:

- **Phase the rollout** starting with branches most similar to high-performing pilot branches
- **Provide enhanced training and support** during the first 4-6 weeks at each branch
- **Establish clear success metrics** and a timeline for evaluating each phase

---

## **Conclusion**

The new stock rotation procedure shows potential to improve service scores, but the evidence is not yet strong enough to justify the cost and disruption of a system-wide rollout. Extending the pilot period represents the most prudent path forward, allowing the cooperative to make a fully informed decision with greater confidence.
