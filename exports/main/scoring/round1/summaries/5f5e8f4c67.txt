# **Memorandum to the Board**

**Subject:** Impact of New Branch Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure on Service Scores

**Date:** November 15, 2026

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new shelving and stock rotation procedure saw service scores rise by an average of **2.7 points (approximately 6%)** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. The improvement grew stronger over time, and there is no evidence of negative effects. **We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **What We Measured**
- **34 branches** tracked weekly from week 6 through week 22
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Service scores average around 50 points (higher is better)

### **Results**

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|---------------|---------------|--------|
| Branches keeping old procedure | 44.9 | 44.9 | No change |
| Branches using new procedure | 46.9 | 49.4 | **+2.5 points** |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | | **+2.7 points** |

### **Why We're Confident in This Result**

1. **Clean comparison:** Before the switch, both groups of branches were trending similarly. The divergence appeared only after some branches switched procedures.

2. **Effect strengthens over time:** The improvement was larger in weeks 19–22 (+5.1 points) than in weeks 15–18 (+3.9 points), suggesting branches get better at using the new procedure with experience.

3. **Not explained by other factors:** The result holds even after accounting for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume.

### **Caveats**
- The statistical evidence is **suggestive but not definitive** (p=0.10). This is common with pilot studies of this size.
- We have only 8 weeks of post-switch data; long-term sustainability is unproven.

---

## **Recommendation**

**Approve full rollout of the new procedure to all branches.**

The evidence points in a positive direction, the effect appears to grow with experience, and there are no warning signs. Waiting for more data would delay benefits to patrons at branches still using the old procedure.
