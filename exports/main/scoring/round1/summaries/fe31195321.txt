# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** [Current Date]  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure  

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores and should be rolled out system-wide.**

Branches that adopted the new shelving and stock rotation procedure saw service scores increase by **2.0 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents a **4% improvement** on a scale where the average branch scores about 50 points. The result is statistically robust and consistent across multiple analytical approaches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **What We Studied**
- **709 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (Weeks 6–22)
- **425 branches** adopted the new procedure starting Week 15; **284 branches** continued with the old procedure
- We compared service score changes between the two groups, before and after the switch

### **Results**

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|----------------|---------------|--------|
| Branches with New Procedure | 51.26 | 53.53 | +2.27 |
| Branches with Old Procedure | 51.00 | 51.22 | +0.22 |
| **Net Effect of New Procedure** | | | **+2.05** |

- **Impact: +2.0 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.4 to 2.6 points)
- **Confidence level: >99.9%** that this effect is real, not due to random chance
- Benefits appear to **grow over time**, suggesting branches improve with experience

### **What Else We Learned**
- Higher staffing levels and visitor volumes are also associated with better service scores
- Weekly score fluctuations are normal (scores vary by about 10 points week-to-week)
- The improvement from the new procedure is independent of staffing and visit changes

---

## **Recommendations**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new shelving and stock rotation procedure to all remaining branches.

2. **Allow 4–6 weeks for adjustment** at each branch, as the data shows benefits increase with experience.

3. **Monitor service scores during implementation** to identify branches that may need additional training or support.

4. **Consider staffing levels** when setting service score targets, as adequate staffing independently supports better performance.
