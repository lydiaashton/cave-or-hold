# **Library Branch Shelving Procedure: Impact Analysis**

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and rotation procedure is associated with a **modest improvement in service scores** (approximately +2.3 points, or about 5% above baseline). However, the evidence is **not statistically conclusive**—we cannot rule out that the observed improvement occurred by chance. The effect also varies across branches: 14 of 20 switching branches improved, while 6 saw declines.

**Recommendation:** Do not proceed with immediate system-wide rollout. Instead, continue monitoring for one more quarter and investigate why some branches benefited more than others. Use those insights to inform a phased implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. **Estimated Impact**
| Measure | Value |
|---------|-------|
| Service score improvement | +2.3 points |
| 95% confidence interval | -0.4 to +5.0 points |
| Statistical significance | Marginal (p ≈ 0.10) |
| Improvement relative to baseline | ~5% |

The confidence interval includes zero, meaning the true effect could range from slightly negative to meaningfully positive.

### 2. **What Happened to Service Scores**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches (20 branches) | 50.03 | 52.27 | **+2.25** |
| Non-switched branches (14 branches) | 49.11 | 49.06 | **-0.05** |

Branches that adopted the new procedure improved; those that kept the old procedure remained flat.

### 3. **Not All Branches Benefited Equally**

Among the 20 branches that switched:
- **14 branches (70%)** saw improvements
- **6 branches (30%)** saw declines
- **Range:** From -7.0 to +11.2 points

This variation suggests branch-specific factors influence how well the new procedure works.

### 4. **Trends Before the Switch Were Similar**

Analysis confirms that switched and non-switched branches had comparable service score trends before week 15, supporting the validity of the comparison.

### 5. **Results Hold After Accounting for Other Factors**

The estimated effect remains stable when controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume, and when excluding the transition week (week 15).

## **Next Steps**

| Priority | Action | Timeline |
|----------|--------|----------|
| **1** | Continue collecting service score data for both groups | Next 8-12 weeks |
| **2** | Interview staff at high-performing and low-performing switched branches to identify implementation differences | 4-6 weeks |
| **3** | Develop branch readiness criteria (size, layout, staffing) based on findings | 6-8 weeks |
| **4** | Offer voluntary adoption to additional branches with enhanced training support | After Q1 review |
| **5** | Reconvene board to review full quarter of data and decide on cooperative-wide rollout | End of next quarter |

---

## **Decision Framework**

| If... | Then... |
|-------|---------|
| Effect remains positive and becomes statistically significant (p < 0.05) | Proceed with phased cooperative-wide rollout |
| Effect remains positive but inconclusive | Continue voluntary adoption with monitoring |
| Effect diminishes or turns negative | Pause rollout and reassess procedure design |

---

*Analysis based on 17 weeks of data (weeks 6-22) from 34 branches (578 observations). Switch implemented week 15. Method: Difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors.*
