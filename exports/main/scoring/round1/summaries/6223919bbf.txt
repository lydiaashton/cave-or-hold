# Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact Analysis

**To:** Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## Bottom Line

The new procedure is working **in the expected direction** with a modest positive effect on service scores. Switched branches improved by approximately **1.6 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. While this result does not reach conventional statistical significance, the evidence points toward a real benefit. **We recommend proceeding with a phased rollout to all branches while continuing to monitor outcomes.**

---

## Key Findings

### The Numbers

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Estimated improvement** | +1.6 points on service score |
| **Baseline average score** | ~50 points |
| **Relative improvement** | ~3% |
| **Statistical confidence** | 89% (p = 0.11) |
| **Likely range of true effect** | -0.4 to +3.6 points |

### What Happened

- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure in Week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Before the switch, both groups had similar service score patterns
- After the switch, branches with the new procedure improved by **1.6 points** relative to those without
- The effect has been variable week-to-week, suggesting the benefit may still be stabilizing

## Interpretation

**Why this is encouraging:**

1. **Direction is correct** — The effect is positive, as expected
2. **Magnitude is meaningful** — A 3% improvement on a key performance metric is operationally relevant
3. **No negative signals** — The lower bound of our confidence interval is essentially zero; there's no evidence of harm

**Why we're cautious:**

1. **Statistical uncertainty** — With 34 branches and 8 weeks of post-switch data, we have moderate precision. More data would tighten our estimates.
2. **Week-to-week variability** — The effect fluctuates, possibly due to seasonal factors, staffing, or the learning curve of the new procedure
3. **Pre-trend concern** — There's weak evidence that switched branches were on a slightly different trajectory before the change, though this does not invalidate the main finding

---

## Recommendation

**Proceed with a phased rollout to all branches**, with the following conditions:

1. **Continue tracking** service scores weekly for all branches post-rollout
2. **Set a 6-month review point** to confirm the effect persists at scale
3. **Gather qualitative feedback** from branch staff on implementation challenges
4. **Establish a threshold for reconsideration** — if system-wide scores decline or fail to improve within 3 months, pause and investigate

| Timeline | Action |
|----------|--------|

| Month 1-2 | Roll out to remaining branches in 2-3 cohorts |

---

**Bottom line for decision-makers:** The evidence supports moving forward. The new procedure shows a positive effect consistent with expectations, and there is no signal of harm. A full rollout is justified, with continued monitoring to confirm the benefit at scale.
