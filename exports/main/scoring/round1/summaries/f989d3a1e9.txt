# Library Cooperative Shelving Procedure Evaluation
## Board Summary Report

---

## BOTTOM LINE

**Do not proceed with systemwide rollout of the new shelving and rotation procedure.** The available data does not support the claim that the new procedure improves service scores. The apparent gains observed in switching branches reflect pre-existing differences between those branches and the rest—not a causal effect of the procedure.

---

## KEY FINDINGS

### Study Overview
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Total branches | 709 |
| Branches adopting new procedure | 425 (60%) |
| Branches keeping old procedure | 284 (40%) |
| Observation period | 17 weeks (weeks 6–22) |
| Procedure switch date | Week 15 |
| Service score scale | Average ~50; higher is better |

### What the Data Shows

**1. Surface-Level Result (Misleading)**
- Switching branches showed service scores **2.1 points higher** after the switch compared to non-switching branches
- This would represent a ~4% improvement if the procedure caused it

**2. The Critical Problem**
- **Before the switch occurred**, branches that would later adopt the new procedure were already improving faster than others
- The performance gap was widening by **0.31 points per week** during weeks 6–14 (pre-switch)
- This pattern invalidates the comparison needed to claim the procedure caused improvement

**3. Rigorous Analysis (Accounting for Pre-Trends)**
- When controlling for pre-existing trends, the estimated effect is **−0.3 points** (essentially zero)
- 95% confidence interval: **−1.7 to +1.1 points**
- Result is **not statistically significant**—indistinguishable from random variation

### Why This Happened

Branches that volunteered to adopt the new procedure were likely already better-managed, better-staffed, or otherwise on an upward trajectory. This **selection bias** explains the apparent improvement, not the procedure itself.

---

## RECOMMENDATIONS

### Immediate Action
| Recommendation | Rationale |
|---------------|-----------|
| **Pause systemwide rollout** | No evidence the procedure improves outcomes |
| **Continue monitoring** | Track service scores at current switch branches |
| **Document lessons learned** | Improve future evaluation designs |

### If Further Evaluation Is Warranted

1. **Randomized Controlled Trial**
   - Randomly assign branches to adopt the procedure
   - Eliminates selection bias
   - Provides credible causal evidence

2. **Extended Data Collection**
   - Gather additional weeks of post-switch data
   - Detect any delayed or cumulative effects

3. **Qualitative Assessment**
   - Survey branch staff on operational efficiency
   - Gather patron feedback on service experience
   - Identify benefits not captured in service scores

4. **Cost-Benefit Analysis**
   - Quantify training and implementation costs
   - Weigh against any demonstrated benefits

---

## METHODOLOGICAL NOTE

This analysis used **difference-in-differences** methodology, the standard approach for evaluating policy changes with treatment and control groups. The key assumption—that treatment and control groups would have followed parallel trends absent the intervention—was **violated** in this case. Branches self-selecting into the new procedure were already diverging from control branches before the switch, making causal inference unreliable.

**For future evaluations:** Randomized assignment is strongly preferred. When voluntary adoption is necessary, ensure sufficient pre-intervention data to validate the parallel trends assumption before drawing conclusions.
