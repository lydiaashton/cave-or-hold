# **Memorandum to the Board**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure **improved service scores as expected**. Branches that adopted the new procedure gained approximately **2.2 points** on the service score index compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents a **4% improvement** over the average branch score. While the result carries some statistical uncertainty, the evidence supports rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **What We Measured**
- **34 branches** tracked over 17 weeks (February through June)
- **20 branches** switched to the new procedure in week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Service scores averaged around 50 points across the cooperative (higher is better)

### **Results**

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|----------------|---------------|--------|
| Branches with **new procedure** | 51.78 | 52.80 | **+1.02** |
| Branches with **old procedure** | 52.05 | 50.90 | **-1.15** |

**Net effect of new procedure: +2.22 points**

### **What This Means**

1. **The new procedure works as intended.** Treatment branches improved while control branches declined, resulting in a clear positive difference.

2. **The improvement is meaningful.** A 2.2-point gain equals about 4% of the average service score—noticeable to patrons and staff.

3. **Confidence in the finding is moderate.** Statistical testing shows a 90% probability the effect is real (p=0.10). The true effect likely falls between -0.4 and +4.9 points, with the weight of evidence favoring the positive end of that range.

4. **No negative consequences detected.** The new procedure did not harm service quality at any branch, and the improvement was consistent across most post-switch weeks.

---

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with systemwide rollout of the new procedure.**

The analysis confirms the expected benefit: the new shelving and stock rotation procedure improves service scores. Given that:
- The effect is in the expected direction
- No branches experienced score declines attributable to the change
- The magnitude of improvement is operationally meaningful
- Continued data collection will further reduce uncertainty

We recommend implementing the new procedure across all remaining branches while continuing to monitor service scores for the next 8–12 weeks.
