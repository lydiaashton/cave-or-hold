# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Prepared by:** Data Analysis Team

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**Roll out the new procedure to all branches.** The new stock rotation and shelving procedure **improved service scores by 2.3 points** on average—a meaningful 4-5% gain. This finding is statistically robust and contradicts the initial expectation that the change would have no effect on service quality.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### The New Procedure Improved Service Scores

| Measure | Result |
|---------|--------|
| Average effect | **+2.28 points** |
| 95% Confidence interval | **1.00 to 3.56 points** |
| Statistical significance | **p = 0.0005** (highly significant) |

### Study Overview

- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** switched to the new procedure in week 15
- **60 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis controls for branch-specific characteristics and week-to-week variations

### Evidence Is Reliable

✓ **Parallel trends confirmed:** Treatment and control branches had statistically identical service score trends before the switch (p = 0.45)

✓ **Effects are sustained:** Positive impacts observed consistently across all 8 weeks after implementation

✓ **No pre-existing differences:** Event study analysis shows no systematic gaps between groups before the policy change

---

## **RECOMMENDATION**

**Proceed with system-wide rollout.** The data provide strong evidence that the new procedure improves service quality. The effect is:

- **Statistically significant** (unlikely to be due to chance)
- **Practically meaningful** (~5% improvement on the service index)
- **Durable** (sustained over the 8-week observation period)

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

1. **Approve full rollout** of the new procedure to all 60 remaining branches

2. **Develop implementation timeline** with branch operations team

## **TECHNICAL NOTES**

*Analysis uses difference-in-differences methodology with branch and week fixed effects—the standard approach for evaluating policy changes with treated and control groups. Standard errors are clustered by branch to account for within-branch correlation. Full event study results and diagnostic plots are available upon request.*
