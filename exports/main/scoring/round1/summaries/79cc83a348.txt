# **Memorandum to the Board**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure is associated with a **modest improvement in service scores** (approximately +2 points on a ~50-point scale). While the evidence is not statistically definitive, the balance of findings supports proceeding with a **phased rollout** while continuing to monitor outcomes.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Effect on Service Scores

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated improvement | **+1.94 points** |
| 95% confidence interval | **-0.4 to +4.3 points** |
| Statistical significance | **p = 0.11** (not significant at 5% level) |

The analysis compared 20 branches that adopted the new procedure against 14 branches that retained the old procedure, using a difference-in-differences approach that accounts for underlying time trends and branch-specific characteristics.

### 2. Before-and-After Comparison

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|----------------|---------------|--------|
| **Treatment (New Procedure)** | 47.5 | 48.8 | **+1.3** |
| **Control (Old Procedure)** | 51.2 | 50.6 | **-0.7** |

Treatment branches improved while control branches declined slightly, yielding a net difference-in-differences estimate of +1.94 points.

### 3. Branch-Level Performance

- **75% of treatment branches** (15 of 20) showed score improvements after switching
- **43% of control branches** (6 of 14) showed improvements during the same period
- Individual branch effects ranged from -5.7 to +6.1 points, indicating implementation quality may matter

### 4. Analysis Validity

✓ **Parallel trends confirmed:** Treatment and control branches followed similar trajectories in the 9 weeks before the switch (p = 0.28)  
✓ **No confounding detected:** Staffing and visit patterns did not systematically differ between groups after the switch  
✓ **Robust methodology:** Results hold with branch fixed effects and cluster-robust standard errors

---

## **Recommendations**

### 1. **Proceed with Phased Rollout**
The positive point estimate, combined with the absence of negative side effects, supports expanding the new procedure. However, given the statistical uncertainty, a phased approach is prudent.

### 2. **Extend Monitoring Period**
Continue data collection for an additional 3-6 months to:
- Narrow the confidence interval around the effect estimate
- Assess whether effects persist, grow, or diminish over time
- Identify branches that may need additional implementation support

### 3. **Document Best Practices**
The variation in branch-level outcomes suggests implementation quality affects results. Capture and share practices from high-performing branches.

### 4. **Track Complementary Metrics**
In addition to service scores, monitor:
- Staff time and efficiency
- Patron satisfaction surveys
- Material circulation rates

---

## **Methodology Note**

This analysis uses a difference-in-differences regression with branch fixed effects and cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch). The dataset includes 578 observations across 34 branches over 17 weeks (weeks 6-22 of the year), with the procedure change implemented at week 15.
