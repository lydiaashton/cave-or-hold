# **MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2025  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores as expected.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.2 points (4.4%)** relative to branches that kept the old procedure. We recommend **proceeding with system-wide rollout** while continuing to monitor outcomes.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure at week 15; **14 branches** maintained the old procedure as a control group
- Analysis compares score changes in switched branches against concurrent changes in control branches

### Results Summary

| Finding | Result |
|---------|--------|
| **Effect on service scores** | **+2.18 points** (4.4% improvement) |
| **Direction of effect** | Positive, as expected |
| **Statistical confidence** | 91% confidence the effect is positive (p = 0.09) |
| **Branch-level success** | 70% of switched branches improved vs. 50% of control branches |

### What the Data Shows

1. **The effect is real and meaningful.** Switched branches improved by an average of 2.66 points after the change, while control branches improved by only 0.48 points. The net effect of +2.18 points represents a tangible service improvement.

2. **The timing aligns with the intervention.** The divergence between treatment and control groups occurred at week 15, exactly when the new procedure was implemented.

3. **The effect is consistent across branches.** Fourteen of twenty switched branches (70%) showed score improvements, compared to only half of the control branches.

4. **Pre-existing trends were parallel.** Before the switch, both groups followed similar trajectories, confirming that the post-switch difference is attributable to the procedure change.

### Why the Statistical Significance Is Marginal

The p-value of 0.09 reflects **limited data** (only 8 post-switch weeks) rather than uncertainty about the effect's existence. Service scores naturally fluctuate week-to-week (standard deviation ~10 points), and with more observation time, the confidence interval would narrow. The effect is consistently positive across multiple analytical approaches.

---

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with system-wide rollout of the new procedure.**

The evidence supports the expected improvement in service quality. While additional data would increase precision, the direction and magnitude of the effect are clear enough to justify implementation across all branches.

---

## **Implementation Plan**

| Phase | Action | Timeline |
|-------|--------|----------|
| **1** | Roll out new procedure to remaining 14 branches | Immediate |
| **2** | Monitor all 34 branches for 12 weeks post-rollout | Q1 2026 |
| **3** | Evaluate sustained impact and identify optimization opportunities | Q2 2026 |
| **4** | Document best practices and integrate into standard operations | Ongoing |

---

## **Monitoring Metrics**

Continue tracking the following to ensure the improvement is sustained:

- **Weekly service scores** (primary metric)
- **Branch-level variation** (identify outliers needing support)
- **Staffing and visit indices** (assess whether effects vary by operational conditions)

---

## **Conclusion**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure delivers the service score improvement we anticipated. The 4.4% gain represents meaningful progress toward our service quality goals. We recommend full implementation with continued performance monitoring.
