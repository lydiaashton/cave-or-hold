# **Memorandum to the Board of Directors**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analytics Team  
**Date:** Current  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure  

---

## **Executive Summary**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure shows **promising but not yet conclusive evidence** of improving service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure performed approximately **1.7 points better** than comparable branches that retained the old procedure. While this positive effect aligns with our expectations, the result does not reach conventional statistical significance (p=0.14), meaning we cannot rule out chance as an explanation.

**Recommendation:** Approve a **phased expansion** to additional volunteer branches while continuing to collect data. A full system-wide rollout should await more definitive evidence, but the positive direction of the effect and absence of any negative impact support continued investment in the new procedure.

---

## **What We Did**

When some branches adopted the new procedure in week 15 and others did not, we had an opportunity to rigorously evaluate its impact. We analyzed **578 weekly observations** from **34 branches** over **17 weeks**, comparing branches that switched to those that did not, both before and after the change.

This "natural experiment" design allows us to isolate the effect of the new procedure from other factors that might affect service scores (such as seasonal variations, staffing changes, or branch-specific characteristics).

---

## **What We Found**

### **Service Score Performance**

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|---------------|---------------|--------|
| Branches using **new procedure** | 50.48 | 49.91 | -0.57 |
| Branches using **old procedure** | 52.31 | 49.93 | -2.38 |
| **Estimated effect of new procedure** | | | **+1.69** |

**Key interpretation:** All branches experienced a decline in service scores after week 15 (likely due to seasonal or system-wide factors). However, branches using the new procedure declined **less** than those using the old procedure. The new procedure appears to have **protected** branches from roughly 1.7 points of decline.

### **Statistical Confidence**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect | +1.69 points |
| 95% Confidence interval | -0.5 to +3.9 points |
| Statistical significance | p = 0.14 (not significant at 0.05 level) |

**What this means:** We estimate the true effect is likely between a small negative effect (-0.5) and a moderate positive effect (+3.9). The data are **most consistent with a modest positive effect**, but we lack sufficient evidence to be confident the effect is not zero.

### **Why Isn't This Statistically Significant?**

1. **Limited sample size:** With 34 branches and only 8 weeks of post-switch data, we can reliably detect effects of about 3 points or larger. The observed effect (1.7 points) is smaller than this threshold.

2. **Natural variation:** Service scores vary considerably week-to-week (standard deviation of 9 points), which makes it harder to detect modest improvements.

3. **Short observation window:** We have only 8 weeks of data since the switch. More time would improve our ability to distinguish signal from noise.

### **Supporting Evidence**

- **No pre-existing differences:** Before the switch, treatment and control branches showed parallel trends, validating our comparison approach.
- **No evidence of harm:** The lower bound of our confidence interval (-0.5) suggests the new procedure is unlikely to be detrimental.
- **Staffing matters:** As expected, higher staffing levels correlate with better service scores, but this does not explain the treatment effect.

---

## **What This Means for the Cooperative**

### **The Good News**
- The effect is in the **expected direction** (positive)
- The new procedure shows **no signs of harming** service quality
- Branches using the new procedure **outperformed** those that did not, even if we cannot be fully confident this wasn't due to chance

### **The Caution**
- We **cannot confirm** the improvement is real rather than random variation
- A system-wide rollout based on current evidence carries the risk of investing in a procedure that may not deliver meaningful benefits

---

## **Recommendations**

### **1. Approve Phased Expansion (Recommended)**
- Invite **5-10 additional branches** to adopt the new procedure voluntarily
- Maintain at least 10 branches as a control group for continued comparison
- This increases statistical power while limiting exposure if the effect proves illusory

### **2. Extend Data Collection**
- Continue monitoring for **at least 12 additional weeks**
- With more data, we could detect effects as small as 1 point with confidence
- Target: Have definitive evidence by the next board cycle

### **3. Establish Decision Triggers**

| Future Finding | Action |
|---------------|--------|
| Effect confirmed positive (p<0.05, effect >1 point) | Proceed with system-wide rollout |
| Effect remains positive but inconclusive | Continue phased expansion |
| Effect turns negative or zero | Halt expansion; reassess procedure design |

### **4. Complementary Evaluation**
- Gather **qualitative feedback** from branch staff on implementation experience
- Conduct **cost analysis** to ensure benefits justify operational changes
- Examine whether effects **vary by branch type** (urban/rural, large/small)

---

## **Bottom Line for Decision-Makers**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did the new procedure improve service scores? | **Probably, but we're not certain** |
| Could the procedure be harmful? | **Very unlikely** |
| Should we roll out to all branches now? | **Not yet—wait for more evidence** |
| Should we abandon the new procedure? | **No—the evidence is encouraging** |

## **Appendix: Technical Details**

- **Method:** Difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors
- **Sample:** 34 branches (20 treatment, 14 control), 578 observations
- **Period:** Weeks 6-22 (9 weeks pre-switch, 8 weeks post-switch)
- **Controls:** Staffing index, visits index
- **Validation:** Parallel trends assumption confirmed (p=0.94 for pre-period differential trend)
