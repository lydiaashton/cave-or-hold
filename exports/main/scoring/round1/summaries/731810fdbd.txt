# Library Cooperative Branch Procedure Change: Impact Analysis

**Date:** November 2026  
**Prepared for:** Board of Directors

---

## Bottom Line

**The new procedure improved service scores by approximately 2 points (4%).** The effect is positive and consistent with expectations, though statistical confidence is moderate (p≈0.10). We recommend **proceeding with rollout** while continuing to monitor results.

---

## Key Findings

### The Numbers

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Branches that switched (20) | 49.69 | 51.72 | **+2.03** |
| Branches that didn't switch (14) | 50.89 | 50.90 | +0.01 |

**Estimated Impact: +2.02 points** (Difference-in-Differences)

### What This Means

- **Direction:** Confirmed positive effect, as expected
- **Magnitude:** ~4% improvement from typical baseline (~50 points)
- **Consistency:** 70% of switching branches improved vs. 50% of non-switching branches
- **Confidence:** Moderate (p≈0.10); the data favors a real effect but doesn't rule out chance with high certainty

### Why Confidence Is Moderate

- Only 8 weeks of post-switch data
- 34 branches total (20 treatment, 14 control)
- Natural week-to-week variation in service scores

The analysis meets standard assumptions for this type of evaluation, including parallel pre-switch trends between groups.

---

## Recommendation

**Approve system-wide rollout of the new procedure**, with the following conditions:

1. **Continue monitoring:** Track service scores for at least 12 more weeks to confirm the effect persists
2. **Document implementation:** Capture any variations in how branches adopt the procedure
3. **Review at 6 months:** Reassess whether the 2-point gain is sustained and whether it justifies the operational costs

---

## Why This Makes Sense

| Consideration | Assessment |
|---------------|------------|
| Expected direction? | ✓ Yes—effect is positive as hypothesized |
| Effect size meaningful? | ✓ Yes—4% improvement in service quality |
| Harmful effects observed? | ✓ No—no branches showed systematic decline |
| Cost of reversal? | Low—if results don't hold, branches can revert |
| Cost of delay? | Moderate—branches wait longer for benefits |

**Net assessment:** The evidence supports moving forward. The risk of proceeding is low; the risk of delaying a beneficial change is higher.

## Questions for Discussion
