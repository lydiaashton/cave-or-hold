# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Library Cooperative Board  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure shows a **positive but statistically inconclusive effect** on service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure averaged approximately **2 points higher** on the service score index compared to branches that kept the old procedure. However, this difference is not statistically significant at conventional levels, meaning we cannot confidently conclude the new procedure caused the improvement. The data is consistent with effects ranging from a slight decrease (−0.5 points) to a moderate increase (+4.4 points).

**Recommendation:** Do not proceed with system-wide rollout at this time. Continue monitoring and consider a more controlled pilot to obtain clearer evidence.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### 1. Study Design
We analyzed 17 weeks of data (weeks 6–22) from 34 branches. Seventeen branches switched to the new procedure at week 15; seventeen branches kept the old procedure. We compared how service scores changed before and after the switch for both groups.

### 2. Main Result

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect of new procedure | **+2.0 points** |
| 95% Confidence interval | **−0.5 to +4.4 points** |
| Statistical significance | Not significant (p = 0.12) |

### 3. What Happened to Scores

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| New Procedure Branches | 45.7 | 49.0 | +3.3 |
| Old Procedure Branches | 47.3 | 48.5 | +1.2 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+2.1** |

### 4. Interpretation
- **If the effect is real:** A 2-point improvement represents approximately **4%** of the average service score—a meaningful but modest gain worth pursuing.
- **The uncertainty:** The confidence interval includes zero and ranges from slightly negative to moderately positive. We cannot rule out that the observed improvement is due to random variation rather than the procedure itself.
- **Pre-existing differences:** The two groups had different score patterns before the switch, which adds uncertainty to the comparison.

### 5. Additional Observations
- Branches with higher visitor traffic tended to have higher service scores.
- Staffing levels showed no clear relationship with service scores in this analysis.
- Some weeks showed stronger effects than others, suggesting the impact may vary over time.

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

### Immediate Actions

1. **Pause system-wide rollout.** The current evidence does not justify the cost and disruption of rolling out an unproven procedure to all 34 branches.

2. **Continue data collection.** Gather 8–12 additional weeks of data to:
   - Increase statistical precision
   - Determine if effects grow, shrink, or stabilize over time
   - Better distinguish true effects from random variation

3. **Consider a stepped-wedge pilot.** Randomly assign remaining branches to switch in different weeks. This approach would:
   - Provide stronger causal evidence
   - Allow all branches to eventually benefit if the procedure proves effective
   - Generate more precise estimates for decision-making

4. **Verify implementation quality.** Ensure branches adopting the new procedure are implementing it consistently. Variation in execution could mask true effects.

### Decision Framework

| Future Evidence | Recommended Action |
|-----------------|-------------------|
| Effect becomes significant and positive | Proceed with full rollout |
| Effect remains inconclusive but positive | Consider phased rollout with monitoring |
| Effect becomes significant and negative | Discontinue new procedure |
| Effect shrinks toward zero | Maintain status quo |

---

## **CONCLUSION**

The new procedure shows promise but lacks conclusive evidence of effectiveness. A modest investment in additional data collection and a more rigorous pilot design will enable a confident go/no-go decision on system-wide rollout.
