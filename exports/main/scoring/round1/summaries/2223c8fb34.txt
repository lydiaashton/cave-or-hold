# Board Summary: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## Bottom Line

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure shows a **positive effect in the expected direction**, with service scores improving by approximately **2 points** (about 4%) at branches that adopted it. However, this result is **not statistically significant**, meaning we cannot rule out that the observed improvement is due to random variation rather than the procedure itself. 

**Recommendation:** Proceed with caution. The evidence is suggestive but not conclusive. Consider expanding the pilot to more branches and collecting additional data before committing to a full system-wide rollout.

---

## Key Findings

### Study Design
- **34 branches** participated: 20 adopted the new procedure, 14 continued with the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Analysis compares changes in service scores between the two groups before and after the switch

### Results

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Treatment (new procedure) | 47.52 | 48.95 | **+1.43** |
| Control (old procedure) | 49.28 | 48.81 | −0.47 |
| **Estimated Effect of New Procedure** | | | **+1.93** |

- **Point estimate**: +1.93 points improvement (approximately 4% gain)
- **95% confidence interval**: −0.36 to +4.22 points
- **Statistical significance**: p = 0.108 (not significant at the 5% level)

### What This Means

1. **The effect is in the expected direction** ✓ — Service scores improved at treatment branches relative to control branches.

2. **The evidence is not definitive** ⚠ — The confidence interval includes zero, so we cannot confidently distinguish the effect from random noise.

3. **The effect size is modest** — Even if real, a 2-point gain on a 50-point scale is meaningful but not transformative.

4. **The study may be underpowered** — With only 34 branches and 17 weeks of data, we may lack sufficient statistical power to detect a real but small effect.

---

## Recommendation

Given that the effect points in the expected direction and the potential benefits outweigh minimal risks, we recommend:

1. **Expand the pilot** — Invite additional branches to adopt the new procedure over the next quarter.

2. **Continue monitoring** — Collect at least 8–12 more weeks of data to improve statistical precision.

3. **Reassess in 6 months** — With more data, we can determine whether the effect becomes statistically significant and whether the magnitude justifies full rollout.

4. **Track implementation costs** — Ensure any operational costs of the new procedure are documented and weighed against the service score gains.

---

## Decision Framework

| Scenario | Interpretation | Action |
|----------|---------------|--------|
| Effect remains ~2 points, becomes significant | Real, modest benefit | Proceed with rollout |
| Effect grows larger | Stronger benefit than expected | Accelerate rollout |
| Effect shrinks toward zero | Likely no real benefit | Reconsider or modify procedure |
| Effect becomes negative | Procedure may be harmful | Halt and investigate |

---

## Technical Notes

- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences design with branch fixed effects.
- Controls for staffing levels and visitor traffic.
- Standard errors are clustered at the branch level.
- Service scores average approximately 50 points; higher is better.
