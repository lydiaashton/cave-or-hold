# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**Recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.** Contrary to initial expectations, the new shelving and rotation procedure produced a measurable improvement in service scores. Branches that switched saw scores increase by **2.0 points** (approximately **4% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This is a statistically robust finding with no apparent downside.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Unexpected Positive Effect on Service Quality

| Group | Pre-Switch Average | Post-Switch Average | Change |
|-------|-------------------|---------------------|--------|
| Switched branches (90 branches) | 51.1 | 53.6 | +2.5 |
| Non-switched branches (60 branches) | 52.3 | 52.8 | +0.5 |

The **net effect of the new procedure is +2.03 points** after accounting for underlying trends and branch-specific factors.

### 2. Statistically Robust Result

- **95% Confidence Interval**: 1.15 to 2.90 points
- **Statistical Significance**: p < 0.001
- The result holds after controlling for staffing levels, visitor volume, and fixed differences between individual branches.

### 3. Valid Comparison

- Pre-switch trends were parallel between groups (p = 0.84), confirming the two groups were comparable before the change.
- 150 branches total; 90 adopted the new procedure, 60 kept the old one.
- Data covers 17 weeks (weeks 6–22), with the switch at week 15.

### 4. Why This Matters

While the procedure change was not expected to affect service scores, the data shows it did—positively. This suggests the new shelving and rotation method may have secondary benefits (e.g., faster item retrieval, cleaner shelves, improved staff workflow) that patrons notice. There is no evidence of negative effects.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches.

2. **Communicate the finding** to branch managers—this change delivers unexpected service benefits.

3. **Monitor during rollout** to ensure consistent implementation and confirm the effect holds at scale.

4. **Track service scores** for 8–12 weeks post-rollout as a quality check.

5. **Investigate the mechanism**—consider gathering qualitative feedback to understand *why* the new procedure improved scores. This could inform future operational decisions.

---

*Analysis prepared using difference-in-differences regression with branch fixed effects and robust standard errors. Data: 2,550 branch-week observations from 150 branches.*
