# MEMORANDUM

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## BOTTOM LINE

Branches that adopted the new stock rotation procedure showed **modest improvement in service scores**, gaining approximately 2 points relative to branches that kept the old procedure. However, this result is not statistically significant at conventional levels, meaning we cannot rule out that the observed improvement occurred by chance. Given the positive direction of the effect and the relatively low risk of the procedure change, we recommend **proceeding with a phased rollout while continuing to monitor outcomes**.

---

## KEY FINDINGS

### Performance Comparison

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| 20 branches that switched | 46.7 | 48.5 | +1.8 |
| 14 branches that did not | 50.3 | 49.8 | −0.4 |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | | **+2.2 points** |

### What This Means

- **Direction:** Positive — switched branches improved while non-switched branches slightly declined
- **Magnitude:** Approximately 4% improvement relative to the average service score of 48.6
- **Confidence:** Moderate — the 95% confidence interval ranges from −0.4 to +4.7 points
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.10 (not meeting the conventional 0.05 threshold)

### Important Context

1. **Branches were not evenly matched.** Those selected to switch had systematically lower service scores before the change (averaging 3–5 points below non-switched branches). This suggests they may have been chosen specifically because they needed improvement.

2. **The effect grew over time.** Early post-switch weeks showed minimal improvement, but by week 22, switched branches outperformed non-switched branches for the first time in the observation period. This pattern is consistent with a learning curve as staff adapted to the new procedure.

3. **Results held across multiple analyses.** Controlling for staffing levels, visitor volume, and branch-specific characteristics did not change the estimated effect, suggesting the finding is robust.

---

## RECOMMENDATION

**Proceed with a phased system-wide rollout over the next 6–12 months.**

### Rationale

| Consideration | Assessment |
|--------------|------------|
| Expected benefit | +2 points service score (positive direction) |
| Risk of harm | Low — no evidence of negative effects |
| Implementation cost | Moderate — training and transition time |
| Opportunity cost | Delaying denies potential benefits to 14 branches still using old procedure |
| Quality of evidence | Moderate — positive signal but not definitive |

On balance, the potential upside outweighs the risks, particularly since the procedure change appears to carry minimal downside.

2. **Establish monitoring dashboard** — Track service scores weekly for all branches during transition
