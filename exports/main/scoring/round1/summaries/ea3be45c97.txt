# **Board Summary: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Recommend proceeding with systemwide rollout.** The new procedure produced a statistically significant improvement in service scores, increasing them by approximately **1.9 points (3.8%)** relative to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is robust and meaningful for customer experience.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Positive Treatment Effect
- Branches using the new procedure saw service scores **1.9 points higher** than expected based on control branch trends
- This effect is **statistically significant** (p = 0.002), meaning we can be highly confident it's not due to random chance
- The 95% confidence interval ranges from **+0.7 to +3.1 points**

### 2. The New Procedure Protected Against Decline
Interestingly, the benefit appears to come from preventing deterioration:
- **Control branches** (old procedure): Scores **declined by 1.7 points** after week 15
- **Treatment branches** (new procedure): Scores **increased by 0.2 points** after week 15

This suggests the new procedure helps branches maintain service quality during periods when scores would otherwise drop.

### 3. Valid Comparison
- **Parallel trends confirmed**: Before the switch, both groups showed similar week-to-week patterns, validating our comparison
- **Groups were balanced**: No significant differences in staffing or visit patterns between treatment and control branches before the switch
- **Sample size**: 150 branches over 17 weeks (2,550 observations), with 102 branches using the new procedure

### 4. Additional Insights
- **Staffing matters**: Higher staffing index is associated with better service scores (each 1-point increase in staffing index correlates with ~0.94 point increase in service score)
- The treatment effect persists even after accounting for staffing and visit variations

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve systemwide rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches

2. **Monitor implementation**: Track service scores during rollout to ensure the effect replicates at scale

3. **Investigate the staffing relationship**: Since staffing strongly correlates with service scores, consider whether the new procedure works best with adequate staffing levels

4. **Timing consideration**: The switch occurred at week 15. If rolling out mid-year, plan for potential transition effects

5. **Follow-up evaluation**: Conduct a 6-month post-rollout assessment to confirm sustained benefits

---

## **Technical Notes**

Analysis used a difference-in-differences approach comparing branches that switched procedures (treatment) to those that did not (control), before and after week 15. Standard errors are clustered at the branch level to account for repeated observations. The parallel trends assumption was tested and supported (pre-treatment interaction p = 0.875).
