# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Rotation Procedure  
**Date:** Analysis of weeks 6–22, switch implemented week 15  
**Data:** 34 branches, 578 weekly observations

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure is associated with a **2-point improvement in service scores** (approximately 4%), but the evidence is **not statistically definitive**. We recommend a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than an immediate systemwide mandate.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Service Score Impact

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| **Branches that switched** (20 branches) | 48.2 | 50.3 | **+2.0** |
| **Branches that did not switch** (14 branches) | 51.6 | 51.6 | 0.0 |
| **Estimated effect of new procedure** | | | **+2.0 points** |

- **Statistical confidence:** p = 0.105 (not significant at the conventional 5% threshold)
- **95% confidence interval:** -0.4 to +4.5 points
- **Practical significance:** A 2-point gain represents roughly 4% improvement on a scale where 50 is average

### What the Numbers Mean

The analysis compares branches that adopted the new procedure against those that did not, before and after the switch. The key question is whether switched branches improved *relative to* non-switched branches after accounting for any overall trends.

- Switched branches improved by 2.0 points after adoption
- Non-switched branches showed no change over the same period
- The **2.0-point difference** is our best estimate of the procedure's effect

### Important Context

1. **Lower starting point:** Switched branches began with lower service scores (48.2 vs. 51.6). The new procedure helped narrow this gap, but switched branches still score slightly below non-switched branches on average.

2. **Mixed results across branches:** Among switched branches, 65% showed improvement while 35% declined. Effects ranged from -5.9 to +9.5 points, suggesting the procedure works better in some settings than others.

3. **Efficiency signal:** Switched branches maintained or improved service scores despite a slight decrease in staffing levels, hinting at potential efficiency gains.

4. **Limited data:** Only 8 weeks of post-switch data are available. More time would increase confidence in the findings.

---

## **Recommendations**

### 1. Proceed with Phased Rollout
The point estimate is positive and operationally meaningful. Given the low risk and potential benefit, expanding the procedure to additional branches is warranted—with careful monitoring.

### 2. Extend the Evaluation Period
Collect **8–12 additional weeks of data** to:
- Increase statistical confidence
- Determine whether effects persist or fade over time
- Identify seasonal patterns that may affect outcomes

### 3. Investigate What Drives Success
Analyze which branch characteristics (size, urban/rural location, baseline staffing, visitor volume) predict better outcomes. Use findings to:
- Prioritize rollout to branches most likely to benefit
- Provide targeted support to branches at risk of poor outcomes

### 4. Establish Monitoring Thresholds
During rollout, track:
- Service scores (alert if any branch declines by more than 3 points)
- Staffing efficiency
- Visitor traffic

### 5. Set a Decision Checkpoint
Reconvene in **12 weeks** with updated data to make a final systemwide adoption decision based on:
- Whether the effect remains positive
- Whether statistical significance is achieved
- Whether implementation costs are justified by benefits

---

## **Conclusion**

The evidence suggests the new procedure **likely helps but is not proven**. A 4% service score improvement would be valuable for the cooperative, and there is no signal of harm. The prudent path is to **continue rolling out while learning**—expanding access to the procedure while gathering the data needed for a definitive systemwide decision.
