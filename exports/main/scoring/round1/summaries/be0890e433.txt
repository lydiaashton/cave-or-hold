# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Rotation Procedure  

---

## **Executive Summary**

**Recommendation: Do not proceed with system-wide rollout.**

After analyzing 17 weeks of service score data from all 709 branches, we find **no evidence that the new shelving and rotation procedure improved service scores**. Branches that adopted the new procedure were already outperforming other branches before the change, and there was no measurable improvement attributable to the procedure itself.

Rolling out the procedure system-wide would incur costs without demonstrated benefit. We recommend pausing the expansion and conducting a properly randomized trial before any further investment.

---

## **Background**

Beginning in week 15 of this year, 425 branches (60%) implemented a new shelving and rotation procedure, while 284 branches (40%) continued with the standard approach. Service scores are tracked weekly for all branches (average score ≈ 50 points; higher is better). The board requested this analysis to determine whether the new procedure should be adopted cooperative-wide.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Branches Selected for the Pilot Were Already Outperforming**

This is the most critical finding. Before the procedure change (weeks 6-14):

| Metric | Switched Branches | Non-Switched Branches | Gap |
|--------|------------------|----------------------|-----|
| Average service score | 52.5 | 50.6 | **+1.9 points** |
| Weekly improvement rate | Faster | Slower | **+0.74 pts/week** |

The branches chosen for the new procedure were already higher-performing and improving faster than other branches. This selection bias means we cannot attribute any subsequent difference to the procedure itself.

### **2. No Improvement Occurred When the Procedure Was Implemented**

If the new procedure had an effect, we would expect to see a noticeable jump in service scores at week 15. We observed **no such jump**:

- The performance gap between switched and non-switched branches actually *decreased* slightly (-0.08 points) from week 14 to week 15
- Statistical testing confirms no significant discontinuity at implementation (p = 0.47)

### **3. After Implementation, the Advantage Grew More Slowly**

| Period | Average Gap | Weekly Trend |
|--------|-------------|--------------|
| Before switch (weeks 6-13) | 1.8 points | +0.74 points/week |
| After switch (weeks 15-22) | 4.1 points | -0.23 points/week |

After the procedure was implemented, switched branches continued to outperform—but the gap grew at a **slower rate** than before. This suggests the new procedure did not accelerate improvement and may have dampened it.

---

## **Why a Simple Comparison Is Misleading**

A basic before-and-after comparison would suggest switched branches improved about 2 points more than non-switched branches. However, this entirely reflects pre-existing differences, not the procedure's effect. Our analysis controls for these pre-existing trends and finds **no causal benefit** from the new procedure.

---

## **Recommendations**

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| **1** | **Pause system-wide rollout** | No evidence of benefit; avoids unnecessary costs |
| **2** | **Investigate selection criteria** | Understand why pilot branches were already outperforming |
| **3** | **Design a randomized trial** | Randomly assign branches to eliminate selection bias |
| **4** | **Continue monitoring pilot branches** | Track for 3-6 more months to detect longer-term effects |
| **5** | **Review pilot selection process** | Ensure future tests use proper experimental design |

---

## **Questions the Board May Have**

**Q: Could the procedure still be beneficial—we just haven't seen it yet?**  
A: Possible, but unproven. We recommend extended monitoring before any expansion.

**Q: Why were certain branches chosen for the pilot?**  
A: This analysis cannot answer that question. We recommend reviewing the selection criteria to understand whether certain branch characteristics (size, location, staffing) influenced the choice.

**Q: Should we revert switched branches to the old procedure?**  
A: Not necessarily. There is no evidence of harm, only lack of proven benefit. Continue monitoring and evaluate again after additional data is collected.

**Q: What would a proper test look like?**  
A: Randomly assign a subset of branches to the new procedure and compare to a randomized control group. This eliminates selection bias and provides credible evidence of causation.

---

## **Technical Appendix**

- **Data:** 12,053 observations (709 branches × 17 weeks)
- **Method:** Difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors
- **Key limitation:** Non-random assignment of branches to treatment violates the "parallel trends" assumption required for causal inference
- **Statistical software:** Python with statsmodels package
