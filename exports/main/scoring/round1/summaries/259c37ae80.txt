# Library Cooperative Stock Rotation Study
## Analysis of New Branch Procedure Impact on Service Scores

---

## BOTTOM LINE

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure shows a **positive but statistically uncertain** effect on service scores. Branches that switched to the new procedure improved their service scores by an estimated **1.8 points** more than branches that kept the old procedure. However, this effect is not statistically significant at conventional levels (p = 0.12), meaning we cannot rule out that the observed improvement could be due to random variation.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with caution. The evidence suggests a modest benefit, but is not strong enough to mandate a system-wide rollout. Consider a phased expansion with continued monitoring, or gather additional data before making a final decision.

---

## KEY FINDINGS

### Study Design
- **34 branches** total: 20 switched to the new procedure, 14 kept the old procedure (control group)
- **17 weeks** of data (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring in week 15
- **9 weeks** of pre-switch data, **8 weeks** of post-switch data
- Service scores average around 50 (higher is better)

### Results Summary

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 50.87 | 51.11 | +0.24 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 49.32 | 51.37 | +2.05 |
| **Difference** | | | **+1.81** |

### Statistical Analysis
- **Point estimate:** +1.81 service score points
- **95% Confidence Interval:** -0.49 to +4.11 points
- **P-value:** 0.123 (not statistically significant at p < 0.05)
- **Effect size:** Approximately 3.6% of the average service score

### Interpretation
1. **Positive direction:** All analyses consistently show the new procedure is associated with higher service scores, never lower.
2. **Uncertain magnitude:** The true effect could range from slightly negative (-0.5 points) to moderately positive (+4.1 points).
3. **Practical significance:** Even at the lower end of the confidence interval, the effect is small. At the upper end, it would represent a meaningful improvement.

### Data Quality Notes
- Service scores showed considerable week-to-week volatility in both groups (e.g., week 12 saw unusually high scores system-wide, week 13 saw unusually low scores).
- The treatment group started with slightly lower service scores than the control group before the switch, but both groups showed similar trends over time.
- The analysis controlled for staffing levels and visitor volume, which did not substantially change the results.

---

## NEXT STEPS

### Immediate Actions
1. **Continue monitoring:** Keep collecting service score data for both groups for at least 8–12 more weeks to increase statistical power.
2. **Investigate mechanisms:** Interview staff at switched branches to understand what aspects of the new procedure may be driving improvements.
3. **Check for heterogeneity:** Analyze whether the effect varies by branch size, location, or other characteristics.

### Decision Framework

| Factor | Consideration |
|--------|---------------|
| **Cost of rollout** | If the new procedure is low-cost and easy to implement, the potential upside may justify adoption despite uncertainty. |
| **Cost of waiting** | If delaying rollout has minimal cost, gathering more data would reduce decision risk. |
| **Strategic priorities** | If improving service scores is a high priority, even a modest expected gain may warrant action. |
| **Risk tolerance** | The analysis shows no evidence of harm; the main risk is that the benefit is smaller than expected. |

### Recommended Approach
**Phased rollout with evaluation:**
1. Switch an additional 5–10 branches to the new procedure.
2. Monitor service scores for 8–12 weeks.
3. Re-analyze with the larger sample to obtain more precise estimates.
4. Make a final system-wide decision based on the updated evidence.

---

## TECHNICAL APPENDIX

### Methodology
This analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** design, comparing changes in service scores for branches that switched procedures versus those that did not, before and after the switch. This approach controls for:
- Time-invariant branch characteristics (via branch fixed effects)
- System-wide temporal trends (via week indicators)
- Observable confounders (staffing index, visits index)

### Limitations
1. **Statistical power:** With only 8 weeks of post-switch data and 34 branches, the study may be underpowered to detect modest effects.
2. **Parallel trends:** Some pre-switch weeks showed differential trends between groups, though this appears to reflect random volatility rather than systematic differences.
3. **Short follow-up:** The analysis captures only immediate effects; longer-term impacts are unknown.

- File: branches.csv
- Observations: 578 (34 branches × 17 weeks)
- Variables: branch_id, week, switched_branch, after_switch, service_score, staffing_index, visits_index
