# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure

**Date:** [Current Date]

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, proceed with system-wide rollout.** The new shelving and stock rotation procedure significantly improved service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure saw scores increase by **2.0 points** (a 4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This result is statistically significant and the evidence strongly supports expanding the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
- **150 branches** participated: 90 adopted the new procedure, 60 continued with the old
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Analysis compares service score changes between adopting and non-adopting branches

### Results Summary

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Branches that switched | 48.55 | 51.72 | +3.17 |
| Branches that did not switch | 47.24 | 48.35 | +1.12 |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | | **+2.04** |

### Statistical Evidence
- **Estimated impact: +2.04 points** on the service score index
- **95% confidence interval: 0.72 to 3.37 points**
- **Statistical significance: p = 0.003** (less than 1% chance this result occurred randomly)
- **Effect size: 4.2% improvement** relative to baseline

### Why We Can Trust This Result
The analysis accounts for two important factors:
1. **Branch differences:** Some branches naturally score higher or lower than others
2. **Week-to-week variation:** Service scores fluctuate across the year

Additionally, branches that switched were on **parallel trajectories** to those that didn't before the change (p = 0.31), meaning the improvement is attributable to the procedure itself, not pre-existing differences.

---

## **Recommendations and Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new shelving and stock rotation procedure to all 60 branches still using the old procedure.

2. **Develop implementation guidance** based on practices from high-performing switched branches to ensure consistent execution.

3. **Monitor service scores** for 8–12 weeks following full rollout to confirm benefits persist at scale.

4. **Conduct a cost-benefit analysis** to quantify operational costs against the documented service quality gains.

5. **Report back to the board** within one quarter on rollout progress and updated service score trends.
