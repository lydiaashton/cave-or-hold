# **MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure shows a **positive effect in the expected direction** (+2.1 points on service score), but the evidence is **not statistically conclusive**. Given the board's expectation of improvement and the absence of any negative signal, we recommend a **phased rollout** while continuing to collect data for a more definitive evaluation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **The Numbers**

| Measure | Result |
|---------|--------|
| Estimated improvement | **+2.1 points** (4% above baseline) |
| 95% Confidence interval | **-0.5 to +4.6 points** |
| Statistical significance | **p = 0.13** (not conclusive) |
| Branches studied | 34 (20 switched, 14 did not) |
| Observation period | 17 weeks (weeks 6–22) |

### **What the Data Shows**

1. **Positive direction, as expected**: Branches that switched improved relative to those that did not
2. **Not statistically definitive**: The confidence interval includes zero, meaning we cannot rule out no effect
3. **No negative effects detected**: The lower bound of the confidence interval is only slightly below zero
4. **Pre-existing differences**: Switching branches had higher baseline scores (~53 vs. ~46), but pre-switch trends were parallel

### **Why We Can't Be More Certain**

The main limitation is **sample size**. With only 34 branches, the analysis has limited power to detect modest effects. A larger sample or longer observation period would provide greater confidence.

---

## **Recommendations**

### **1. Proceed with Phased Rollout**

| Rationale | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| Effect is in expected direction | +2.1 points aligns with board expectations |
| No evidence of harm | Lower confidence bound is only -0.5 points |
| Practical significance | A 4% improvement would be meaningful at scale |

**Implementation approach:**
- Roll out to remaining branches over 2–3 months
- Continue tracking service scores weekly
- Document any implementation challenges

### **2. Extend Data Collection**

Collect **8–12 additional weeks** of post-implementation data to:
- Increase statistical power
- Detect whether effects grow over time (learning curve)
- Confirm the initial positive signal

### **3. Monitor Complementary Metrics**

Service score is one measure. Also track:
- Patron satisfaction surveys
- Material circulation rates
- Staff time spent on shelving
- Operational costs

### **4. Establish Success Criteria**

Before full rollout completion, define:
- **Minimum acceptable improvement**: e.g., +1.5 points sustained over 8 weeks
- **Review checkpoint**: e.g., re-evaluate after 12 more weeks of data
- **Exit criteria**: What would trigger reverting to the old procedure?

---

## **Risk Assessment**

| Risk | Likelihood | Mitigation |
|------|------------|------------|
| No real improvement | Moderate | Continue monitoring; phased approach limits exposure |
| Effect smaller than expected | Likely | 4% improvement still valuable at cooperative scale |
| Implementation variation | Possible | Document procedures; provide training support |
| Opportunity cost | Low | Procedure change is not resource-intensive |

---

## **Conclusion**

The data provide **encouraging but not definitive** evidence that the new procedure improves service scores. The estimated effect is positive, aligns with expectations, and shows no sign of harm. Given the low risk and potential benefit, a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** is the prudent path forward.
