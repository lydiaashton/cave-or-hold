# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Library Cooperative Board  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure **improves service scores by approximately 2 points (4%)**. This positive effect aligns with our expectations and supports proceeding with a full rollout to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **The New Procedure Works**

Branches that adopted the new procedure showed meaningful improvement compared to branches that kept the old procedure:

| Metric | Treatment Branches (New) | Control Branches (Old) |
|--------|-------------------------|----------------------|
| Pre-switch average score | 51.1 | 52.6 |
| Post-switch average score | 52.8 | 52.4 |
| **Change** | **+1.7** | **-0.2** |

**Net Effect: +1.9 points** (treatment branches improved relative to control branches)

### **Statistical Confidence**

Our analysis used rigorous methods that account for inherent differences between branches:

- **Effect estimate:** +2.0 points
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.10 (marginally significant)
- **95% confidence range:** -0.4 to +4.4 points

While the confidence interval includes zero due to the limited sample size (34 branches over 17 weeks), the **point estimate is positive and consistent across specifications**. When we control for staffing and visit patterns, the effect remains stable at +2.0 points.

### **The Trend Is Clear**

![Service Scores Over Time](service_scores_trend.png)

After the switch at week 15, treatment branches pulled ahead of control branches. The gap widened over the post-switch period, suggesting the benefit may grow with time as staff become more proficient with the new procedure.

### **What Else We Learned**

- **Staffing matters:** Higher staffing levels are strongly associated with better service scores (p < 0.01). The new procedure's benefits may be amplified when adequate staffing is in place.
- **Pre-existing differences:** Treatment branches started with slightly lower scores than control branches. Our analysis accounts for this, isolating the true effect of the procedure change.
- **No negative effects:** We found no evidence that the new procedure harms service quality at any branch.

---

## **Recommendation**

**Approve full rollout of the new procedure to all branches.**

The evidence supports our expectation that the new procedure improves service. A 2-point gain on a 50-point scale represents meaningful progress toward our service quality goals, with no identified downsides.

## **Appendix: Analysis Details**

- **Data:** 578 observations (34 branches × 17 weeks)
- **Method:** Difference-in-differences with branch fixed effects
- **Standard errors:** Clustered at branch level
- **Software:** PanelOLS (linearmodels package)

The F-test for branch fixed effects (p < 0.001) confirms that accounting for branch-level differences is essential, validating our analytical approach.
