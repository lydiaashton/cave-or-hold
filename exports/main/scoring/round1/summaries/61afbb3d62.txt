# **Analysis of New Shelving Procedure Impact on Library Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure unexpectedly improved service scores.** Although the shelving and rotation change was not designed to affect service quality, branches that adopted it saw their service scores increase by approximately **2.3 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and represents roughly a **5% improvement** over the average branch score of 50.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with systemwide rollout. The operational change delivers an unanticipated service quality benefit at no additional cost.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Study Overview
- **150 branches** tracked over 17 weeks (weeks 6-22)
- **90 branches** switched to the new procedure at week 15; **60 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Analysis compares score changes between the two groups before and after the switch

### 2. Impact on Service Scores
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| New Procedure Branches | 49.06 | 52.24 | +3.18 |
| Old Procedure Branches | 49.05 | 49.95 | +0.90 |
| **Attributable to New Procedure** | | | **+2.28** |

The **2.28-point improvement** is statistically significant (p < 0.001) and persists after accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume.

### 3. Why We Trust This Result
- **Parallel pre-trends confirmed:** In the 9 weeks before the switch, treated and control branches moved together (average difference: 0.01 points; statistical test p = 0.40)
- **Effect appears at switch point:** Scores diverge precisely when the new procedure began, not before
- **Effect persists:** The improvement remains positive across all 8 post-switch weeks

### 4. Unexpected Nature of Finding
This operational change was not intended to affect service scores. The positive impact suggests:
- The new procedure may indirectly improve patron experience (e.g., faster book location, better organization)
- Staff may work more efficiently under the new system, freeing time for patron services
- There may be secondary benefits worth investigating

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve systemwide rollout.** The evidence supports extending the new procedure to all 60 remaining branches.

2. **Document the business case.** This analysis provides quantitative support that an operational change can deliver service quality improvements without additional investment.

3. **Investigate the mechanism.** Consider a follow-up study to understand *why* scores improved—this could reveal best practices to amplify the effect.

4. **Continue monitoring.** Track service scores as remaining branches adopt the procedure to confirm effects generalize across all branch types and sizes.

5. **Share findings with branch managers.** Communicating this unexpected benefit may improve staff buy-in for future operational changes.
