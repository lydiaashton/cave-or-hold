# **Memorandum to the Library Cooperative Board**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Analysis Period:** Weeks 6–22 (17 weeks of data from 34 branches)

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure is **associated with improved service scores**, but the evidence is **not statistically conclusive**. Branches that adopted the new procedure showed approximately **2 points higher service scores** (about 4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. The board should weigh this promising but uncertain signal against implementation costs and risk tolerance before deciding on a full rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Overview**
- **34 branches** tracked weekly from week 6 through week 22
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15
- **14 branches** continued with the standard procedure (control group)
- Service scores average ~50 points; higher scores indicate better service

### **Impact on Service Scores**

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|----------------|---------------|--------|
| New Procedure Branches | 49.9 | 52.0 | **+2.1** |
| Standard Procedure Branches | 49.2 | 49.3 | +0.2 |
| **Net Effect of New Procedure** | | | **+1.9 points** |

### **Statistical Confidence**
- **Estimated effect:** +1.9 to +2.1 service score points
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.10–0.13 (above the conventional 0.05 threshold)
- **95% confidence interval:** -0.4 to +4.5 points
- **Interpretation:** The data suggest a positive effect, but we cannot rule out the possibility that the true effect is zero or slightly negative

### **Branch-Level Variation**
- **70%** of branches using the new procedure showed score improvements
- **57%** of branches using the standard procedure showed improvements
- Effects varied widely: some branches gained up to 10 points, others declined

---

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with caution.** The evidence points in a favorable direction but does not meet the threshold for high-confidence decision-making. We recommend one of the following approaches:

### **Option A: Continue Monitoring (Low Risk)**
- Collect 8–12 additional weeks of data
- This would increase statistical power and provide a clearer answer
- Best if implementation costs are significant or the board prefers high certainty

### **Option B: Phased Rollout (Moderate Risk)**
- Expand the new procedure to 10–15 additional branches
- Continue rigorous measurement and evaluation
- Allows learning while limiting exposure if the effect proves illusory
- Best if the board sees meaningful potential benefit and accepts moderate uncertainty

### **Option C: Full Rollout (Higher Risk)**
- Implement across all remaining branches immediately
- Justifiable only if implementation costs are low and the board is comfortable acting on suggestive (but not definitive) evidence

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Board decision** on which option to pursue based on risk tolerance and budget considerations

2. **If Option A or B:** Establish a timeline for the next evaluation (recommend 8–12 weeks)

3. **If Option B or C:** Develop implementation plan including:
   - Staff training requirements
   - Timeline for transition
   - Continued data collection protocol

4. **Investigate variation:** Analyze which branch characteristics (size, location, staffing) correlate with larger improvements to inform targeted implementation

---

## **Technical Appendix**

**Methodology:** Difference-in-differences regression with branch-clustered standard errors, controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume.

**Validity checks:**
- Pre-switch trends were parallel between groups (p = 0.52), supporting the validity of the comparison
- Results were consistent whether or not staffing and visit controls were included
- No evidence of systematic differences between treatment and control groups before the switch

**Data source:** branches.csv (578 observations: 34 branches × 17 weeks)
