# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

**To:** Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure is associated with a **positive improvement in service scores**, averaging **+2.7 points** (approximately **6% increase**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. While this result is **marginally statistically significant** (p=0.10), the consistent positive direction and growing effect over time suggest the new procedure is beneficial. **We recommend proceeding with a full rollout** while continuing to monitor results.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure as a control group
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in service scores between groups before and after the switch
- This design controls for branch-specific characteristics and overall time trends

### **Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 44.9 | 44.9 | 0.0 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 46.9 | 49.4 | +2.5 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+2.7** |

- **Point estimate:** +2.65 service score points
- **95% confidence interval:** -0.4 to +5.7 points
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.10 (marginally significant)
- **Effect size:** ~6% improvement relative to baseline

### **Supporting Evidence**

1. **Parallel trends confirmed:** Before the switch, both groups showed similar trends in service scores (p=0.55), validating the comparison method.

2. **Effect grows over time:** The improvement strengthens as branches gain experience with the new procedure:
   - Weeks 15–18 (early period): +3.9 points
   - Weeks 19–22 (late period): +5.1 points
   
   This pattern suggests a learning curve or cumulative benefit.

3. **Results are robust:** The effect remains similar when accounting for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume.

### **Limitations**

- The result does not reach conventional statistical significance (p<0.05), meaning we cannot rule out chance with high confidence
- Only 8 weeks of post-switch data are available; longer-term effects are unknown
- Branches were not randomly assigned to switch, though the analysis controls for this

---

## **Next Steps**

| Priority | Action | Timeline |
|----------|--------|----------|
| 1 | **Proceed with full rollout** of the new procedure to all branches | Immediate |
| 2 | **Continue monitoring** service scores to confirm effects persist | 3–6 months |
| 3 | **Document best practices** from high-performing branches | Ongoing |
| 4 | **Consider phased randomized evaluation** for future procedure changes | As needed |

---

## **Conclusion**

The evidence supports rolling out the new shelving and stock rotation procedure to all branches. The positive effect on service scores, combined with the growing benefit over time and absence of negative signals, indicates the change is moving the cooperative in the right direction. Continued monitoring will ensure the benefits are sustained and help identify opportunities for further improvement.

---

*Analysis based on weekly service score data from 34 branches, weeks 6–22. Switch occurred week 15. Statistical analysis used difference-in-differences regression with branch-clustered standard errors.*
