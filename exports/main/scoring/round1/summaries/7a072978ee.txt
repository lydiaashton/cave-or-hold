# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure is associated with a **modest improvement in service scores** (+1.75 points, or approximately 3.7%), but the evidence is **not statistically conclusive** at conventional confidence levels. While 90% of branches that adopted the new procedure showed improvement (versus only 50% of branches using the old procedure), the limited sample size prevents us from ruling out chance as an explanation. 

**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **phased rollout** to additional branches while continuing to monitor results, rather than an immediate system-wide adoption or rejection.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Overview**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Total branches | 34 |
| Branches using new procedure | 20 |
| Branches using old procedure | 14 |
| Observation period | Weeks 6–22 |
| Procedure switch date | Week 15 |
| Total observations | 578 |

### **Service Score Impact**

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|----------------|---------------|--------|
| Switched branches (treatment) | 50.83 | 53.25 | +2.41 |
| Non-switched branches (control) | 49.88 | 50.55 | +0.67 |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | | **+1.75** |

### **Statistical Assessment**
- **Estimated effect:** +1.75 points (3.7% improvement)
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.14 (not significant at the 0.05 level)
- **95% confidence interval:** -0.58 to +4.07 points
- **Interpretation:** The data suggest a positive effect, but we cannot be highly confident the effect is different from zero

### **Branch-Level Results**
- **18 of 20 switched branches (90%)** improved their scores after adoption
- **7 of 14 non-switched branches (50%)** improved during the same period
- The consistency of improvement across treatment branches is a positive signal

### **Analysis Validity**
✓ **Parallel trends confirmed:** No significant difference in pre-switch trends between groups (p = 0.51)  
✓ **Results robust:** Effect remains similar when controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume  
✓ **No obvious confounders:** The timing and group assignment support causal interpretation

## **Next Steps**

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| **1** | Extend data collection 8–12 weeks | Increases statistical power to detect true effect |
| **2** | Expand to 10–15 additional branches | Phased rollout balances opportunity with risk |
| **3** | Analyze branch characteristics | Identify which branches benefit most from the new procedure |
| **4** | Conduct cost-benefit analysis | Compare service gains against implementation costs |
| **5** | Set clear decision thresholds | Define what evidence level justifies full rollout |

---

## **Decision Framework**

| Scenario | Recommended Action |
|----------|-------------------|
| Effect remains ~1.5–2.0 points with p < 0.10 | Proceed with full rollout |
| Effect grows to 3+ points with p < 0.05 | Accelerate rollout |
| Effect diminishes or becomes negative | Halt expansion and investigate causes |
| Effect stays uncertain after more data | Pilot in willing branches only |
