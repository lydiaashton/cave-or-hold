# Memo to the Board: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure Evaluation

**To:** Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Impact of New Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores

---

## Bottom Line

Branches that adopted the new stock rotation and shelving procedure showed **modest improvement in service scores** relative to branches that kept the old procedure. The estimated gain is approximately **2 points** on the service score index (about 4% improvement). While this effect is in the expected direction, the evidence is **not statistically conclusive**—we cannot rule out that the observed improvement may be due to chance. Given the positive signal and likely modest implementation costs, we recommend **proceeding with the rollout while continuing to monitor results**.

---

## Key Findings

### What We Analyzed
- **34 branches** over **17 weeks** (February through June)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure starting Week 15; **14 branches** kept the old procedure
- We compared changes in service scores between the two groups, accounting for pre-existing differences between branches

### Results

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|----------------|---------------|--------|
| Branches with New Procedure | 47.9 | 48.5 | +0.6 |
| Branches with Old Procedure | 52.0 | 50.9 | -1.1 |
| **Estimated Effect of New Procedure** | | | **+1.7 to +2.0** |

The new procedure is associated with a **1.7 to 2.0 point improvement** in service scores after accounting for underlying differences between branches and time trends.

### Confidence in the Results

| Measure | Value |
|---------|-------|
| Estimated improvement | +1.7 to +2.0 points |
| Statistical confidence (p-value) | 0.10 to 0.14 |
| 95% confidence interval | -0.4 to +4.4 points |
| Effect size | Small-to-moderate (0.18 standard deviations) |

**What this means:** The data points in the expected direction—service scores improved where the new procedure was adopted. However, because the confidence interval includes zero, we cannot be fully certain the procedure caused the improvement. Additional data would strengthen our conclusions.

### Important Context

1. **Branches were different from the start:** Branches selected for the new procedure had lower baseline service scores (47.9 vs. 52.0). Our analysis accounts for this, but simple before/after comparisons would be misleading.

2. **The control group declined:** Branches keeping the old procedure saw service scores drop after Week 15. Part of the estimated effect reflects this decline, not just improvement in treatment branches. This may indicate seasonal factors or other system-wide changes.

3. **Pre-existing trends were similar:** Both groups showed comparable week-to-week patterns before the switch, supporting the validity of our comparison.

---

## Recommendations

### 1. Proceed with Rollout (Conditional)
The evidence, while not definitive, suggests the new procedure does no harm and may provide modest benefit. We recommend continuing the rollout with the following conditions:

- Set realistic expectations: improvements are likely small (2-4%)
- Document implementation practices at each branch to identify best practices
- Track operational costs to ensure benefits outweigh any added expenses

### 2. Extend Data Collection
Collect **8-12 additional weeks** of service score data to:
- Increase statistical confidence in the estimated effect
- Determine whether improvements persist or grow over time
- Distinguish genuine effects from short-term adjustment patterns

### 3. Deepen the Analysis
- Examine whether certain branch types (size, location, staffing) benefit more
- Gather qualitative feedback from staff and patrons on the new procedure
- Review whether implementation fidelity varies across branches

### 4. Establish Ongoing Monitoring
Create a quarterly review process to track:
- Service scores by branch
- Staff time spent on rotation and shelving
- Patron satisfaction metrics

## Summary for Decision-Making

| Question | Answer |

| Is there evidence of harm or negative effects? | **No**—scores did not decline in treatment branches |
| Should we roll out to all branches? | **Yes, with continued monitoring** |
