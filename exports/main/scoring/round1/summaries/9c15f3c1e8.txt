# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Library Cooperative Board  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation procedure shows a **positive but statistically uncertain** effect on service scores. Branches that switched saw an average improvement of approximately **2 points (4%)**, but with the current data we cannot rule out that this improvement occurred by chance. 

**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **phased rollout** while continuing to collect data. The evidence suggests benefit with no detected harm, but additional weeks of observation would provide greater confidence.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **The Evidence**

| Finding | What It Means |
|---------|---------------|
| **+2.1 point average improvement** | Branches using the new procedure scored higher after switching |
| **95% confidence interval: -0.5 to +4.6** | The true effect could range from slightly negative to moderately positive |
| **p-value = 0.125** | Not statistically significant at the conventional 0.05 threshold |
| **Pre-trends balanced (p=0.999)** | Groups were comparable before the switch—supports validity of the comparison |

### **Study Overview**

- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure at week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Service scores average ~50 points; higher scores indicate better service

### **What the Data Show**

Before the switch, both groups followed similar trends. After week 15, branches using the new procedure showed a modest uptick in service scores relative to those that did not switch. However, the small number of branches limits our ability to detect small effects with confidence.

---

## **Interpretation**

### **Why Isn't This Statistically Significant?**

The study has **limited statistical power** due to the number of branches (34). This does not mean the procedure doesn't work—it means we need more data to be confident. With the current sample, we can reliably detect only effects larger than about 3–4 points.

### **Practical Significance vs. Statistical Significance**

Even if the true effect is at the lower end of our estimate (~2 points), this represents a **4% improvement** in service scores. For a cooperative serving thousands of patrons, this could meaningfully improve the user experience across the system.

### **No Evidence of Harm**

Importantly, there is **no indication that the new procedure worsens service**. The lower bound of our confidence interval (-0.5 points) suggests any negative effect would be negligible.

---

## **Recommendations**

### **1. Proceed with Phased Rollout**

Given the positive point estimate and absence of detected harm, we recommend moving forward with implementation—but in stages:

- Roll out to an additional 5–10 branches per month
- Continue tracking service scores for all branches
- This approach balances potential benefit with prudent caution

### **2. Extend the Evaluation Period**

Collect **8–12 additional weeks** of data to:

- Improve statistical power
- Confirm whether the effect persists over time
- Identify any branches where the procedure is less effective

### **3. Monitor Complementary Metrics**

Service score is one measure. Also track:

- Patron satisfaction surveys
- Circulation and hold fulfillment rates
- Staff time spent on stocking activities
- Operational cost changes

### **4. Document Implementation Quality**

Ensure consistent adoption across branches:

- Provide clear training materials
- Check that branches are following the new procedure as designed
- Variation in implementation could explain mixed results

---

## **Decision Framework**

| Board Priority | Recommended Action |
|----------------|-------------------|
| **Maximize potential benefit** | Proceed with full rollout; evidence points positive |
| **Balance benefit with certainty** | Phased rollout with continued monitoring (recommended) |
| **Require statistical certainty** | Wait 8–12 weeks for more data before deciding |

---

## **Technical Appendix**

**Method:** Difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch)

**Model:** Service score ~ switched_branch + after_switch + treatment_interaction + staffing_index + visits_index

**Software:** Python (pandas, statsmodels)

**Data:** 578 observations across 34 branches and 17 weeks

**Key Assumption:** Parallel trends—the control and treatment groups would have followed similar trajectories absent the intervention. This assumption is supported by pre-period data (joint pre-trend test p=0.999).
