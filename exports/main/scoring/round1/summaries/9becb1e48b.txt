# **Board Memorandum**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure  

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.** The data indicate the new stock rotation and shelving procedure **improves service scores by approximately 2.3 points** (about 4% relative to baseline). Branches that adopted the new procedure maintained their service levels, while branches that kept the old procedure experienced a notable decline. The evidence supports the expected benefit, though we recommend continued monitoring as the full rollout proceeds.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **The Numbers**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| **Branches with New Procedure** | 52.74 | 52.29 | -0.45 |
| **Branches with Old Procedure** | 53.93 | 51.15 | -2.78 |
| **Net Benefit of New Procedure** | | | **+2.32** |

**What this means:** The new procedure is associated with a **2.3-point higher service score** compared to what would have occurred under the old procedure. In practical terms, switched branches were largely protected from the service score decline that affected non-switched branches.

### **Study Scope**
- **34 branches** across the cooperative (20 switched, 14 did not)
- **17 weeks** of data (Weeks 6–22)
- **578 branch-week observations**
- Rigorous difference-in-differences analysis with statistical controls

### **Confidence in the Result**
- **90% confidence** that the effect is positive (p=0.088)
- **95% confidence interval:** -0.3 to +5.0 points
- **Pre-trends verified:** Switched and non-switched branches were on parallel trajectories before the change
- **Results hold** after accounting for staffing levels and visitor volumes

---

## **Why This Matters**

1. **The effect is in the expected direction.** The board anticipated improvement, and the data confirm this expectation.

2. **The new procedure provides protection.** Even though all branches faced some headwinds after week 14, those with the new procedure weathered them significantly better.

3. **No evidence of harm.** There is no indication the new procedure negatively affects any branch type, regardless of staffing or traffic levels.

4. **The cost of delay is real.** Non-switched branches declined by nearly 3 points. Each week of delayed rollout represents lost service quality.

---

## **Recommended Next Steps**

| Action | Timeline | Owner |
|--------|----------|-------|
| **Approve full rollout** to remaining 14 branches | Immediate | Board |
| **Develop implementation schedule** (phased over 4–6 weeks) | 2 weeks | Operations |
| **Create monitoring dashboard** to track service scores post-rollout | 3 weeks | Analytics |
| **Document best practices** from high-performing switched branches | 4 weeks | Operations |
| **Re-evaluate results** with 12 additional weeks of data | 3 months | Analytics |

### **Success Metrics**
- Service scores at newly switched branches should remain within 2 points of pre-switch levels
- Cooperative-wide average service score should increase by 1.5–2.5 points within 8 weeks of full rollout
- No branch should experience a decline greater than 4 points attributable to the transition

---

## **Conclusion**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure delivers the improvement the board expected. While statistical certainty is not absolute (there is approximately a 10% chance the observed effect reflects random variation), the weight of evidence favors a **real, meaningful benefit**. The risk of maintaining the status quo—continued service score erosion at non-switched branches—outweighs the risk of proceeding.

**Recommendation: Approve full rollout.**
