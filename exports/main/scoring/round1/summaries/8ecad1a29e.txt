# **Board Summary: New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure Evaluation**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores as expected.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure achieved service scores **1.96 points higher** than they would have under the old procedure (95% CI: 1.42 to 2.50 points). This represents a **4% improvement** and is highly statistically significant (p < 0.001).

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout.**

---

## **Study Overview**

| | Treatment Group | Control Group |
|---|---|---|
| **Procedure** | New rotation/shelving | Existing procedure |
| **Branches** | 425 | 284 |
| **Pre-switch average** | 50.8 | 49.4 |
| **Post-switch average** | 51.9 | 48.5 |
| **Raw change** | +1.1 | -0.8 |

**Analysis period:** 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)  
**Switch date:** Week 15  
**Method:** Difference-in-differences with cluster-robust standard errors

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Confirmed Positive Effect
The new procedure delivered the expected improvement:
- **+1.96 points** on the service score index
- Effect is precisely estimated (tight confidence interval)
- Result is robust to controls for staffing levels and visitor volume

### 2. Meaningful Impact
- Service scores average ~50 across the system; a 2-point gain is operationally meaningful
- The control group's scores declined slightly post-switch (-0.8 points), suggesting the new procedure may also buffer against seasonal or external pressures

### 3. Valid Comparison
Pre-switch trends were parallel between groups, supporting the credibility of the causal estimate. The treatment group maintained a stable 1–2 point advantage throughout the pre-period, with no unusual divergence before the switch.
