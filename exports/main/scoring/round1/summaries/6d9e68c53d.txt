# **MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure shows a **modest positive effect** on service scores (+2.2 points, ~4% improvement), but the evidence is **not statistically definitive**. We recommend a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Overview**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (Weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Switch implemented at **Week 15**

### **Service Score Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| **Treatment (Switched)** | 51.72 | 52.86 | **+1.14** |
| **Control (No Switch)** | 53.35 | 52.40 | **-0.95** |
| **Net Effect** | — | — | **+2.09** |

### **Statistical Evidence**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated treatment effect | +2.21 points |
| P-value | 0.094 |
| 95% Confidence Interval | -0.38 to +4.79 points |
| Effect size | ~4.3% improvement |

**Interpretation:** The effect is positive and marginally significant (at the 10% level), but we cannot rule out the possibility of no effect at the conventional 5% significance level.

### **Validity Checks**
✓ **Pre-trends were parallel:** Treatment and control groups had similar trends before the switch, supporting the validity of our comparison.

✓ **Model controls work as expected:** Staffing and visitor traffic both significantly predict service scores, confirming the model captures meaningful relationships.

---

## **Recommendations**

### **1. Proceed with Phased Rollout**
The positive direction of effect, combined with no evidence of harm, supports expanding the procedure—but gradually. Implement in additional branches over the next 1–2 quarters rather than all at once.

### **2. Extend the Monitoring Period**
Current data covers only 8 weeks post-implementation. Collect at least 12–16 more weeks of data to confirm whether effects persist, grow, or diminish.

### **3. Track Implementation Quality**
Ensure branches follow the new procedure consistently. Variation in implementation could be masking the true effect.

### **4. Conduct Cost-Benefit Analysis**
Quantify the operational costs of the new procedure and compare against the 4% service score improvement to determine return on investment.

### **5. Reassess in 90 Days**
Schedule a follow-up review once additional data is collected. Decision criteria:

| Outcome | Action |
|---------|--------|
| Effect strengthens (p<0.05) | Full system-wide rollout |
| Effect remains marginal | Continue phased approach |
| Effect disappears | Revert or redesign procedure |

---

## **Limitations**

- **Short post-implementation window** (8 weeks) limits detection of long-term effects
- **Modest sample size** (34 branches) reduces statistical power
- **Unobserved factors** (seasonal effects, staff changes) could influence results

---

## **Conclusion**

The new procedure shows promise but warrants cautious optimism. A phased rollout balances the potential benefits against the uncertainty in the current evidence. Continued monitoring will provide the clarity needed for a confident system-wide decision.
