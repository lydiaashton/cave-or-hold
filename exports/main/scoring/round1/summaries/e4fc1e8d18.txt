# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

---

## **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

**Date:** November 2025  
**Prepared by:** Data Analysis Team  
**Data Period:** Weeks 6–22 (17 weeks of observations)

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new shelving and stock rotation procedure shows a modest positive effect on service scores.** Branches that switched maintained their performance while control branches declined, resulting in a **~2-point improvement** (approximately 4% relative to average scores). The evidence is **suggestive but not definitive**—there is about a 10% chance this result could be due to random variation.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **phased rollout** to all branches, with close monitoring to confirm the effect persists.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **1. The Numbers**

| Measure | Value |
|---------|-------|
| Branches studied | 34 (20 switched, 14 did not) |
| Observations | 578 branch-week records |
| Estimated effect | **+2.1 points** on service score |
| Statistical confidence | p = 0.10 (marginally significant) |
| 95% Confidence range | -0.4 to +4.5 points |

### **2. What Changed**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 52.2 | 52.5 | +0.3 |
| Non-switched branches | 51.9 | 50.3 | **-1.6** |

The benefit appears to come from **preventing decline** rather than driving improvement. Branches keeping the old procedure saw their scores drop; branches with the new procedure held steady.

### **3. Why We Trust This Analysis**

- **Parallel trends verified:** Treatment and control branches moved together before the switch (no pre-existing differences)
- **Controls included:** Results account for staffing levels and visitor volume
- **Rigorous method:** Difference-in-differences with branch-level fixed effects and clustered standard errors

### **4. Context**

- Average service score across all branches: **51.8** (scale where ~50 is average)
- A 2-point gain represents a **small-to-moderate** effect—meaningful for operations but not transformative
- Staffing levels remain an important driver of service quality (independent of this procedure change)

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

| Priority | Action | Timeline |
|----------|--------|----------|
| 1 | Begin phased rollout to remaining 14 branches | Immediate |
| 2 | Track service scores weekly for 8–12 weeks post-switch | Ongoing |
| 3 | Investigate why control branch scores declined | Within 4 weeks |
| 4 | Assess whether staffing levels interact with the procedure effect | Within 8 weeks |
| 5 | Return to Board with 6-month follow-up analysis | 6 months |

---

## **CAVEATS & RISKS**

⚠️ **Statistical uncertainty:** The result does not meet the conventional threshold for statistical significance (p < 0.05). There is meaningful uncertainty around the true effect.

⚠️ **Limited post-switch data:** Only 8 weeks of data after the change; longer-term effects are unknown.

⚠️ **External factors:** Unmeasured factors (seasonal patterns, local events) could influence results.

---

## **CONCLUSION**

The new procedure shows **promise but not proof**. The direction of the effect is positive, the magnitude is operationally meaningful, and the analysis is methodologically sound. However, the statistical evidence is not strong enough to guarantee success at every branch.

A **cautious, monitored rollout** balances the potential benefit against the uncertainty. If the effect holds, the cooperative stands to gain modest but real improvements in service quality across all branches.
