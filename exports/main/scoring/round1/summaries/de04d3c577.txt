# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD OF DIRECTORS**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure  
**Date:** November 2025  
**Prepared by:** Data Analysis Team

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

The new procedure shows a **modest positive effect** on service scores (+2 points, or 4%), but the evidence is **not statistically conclusive**. Importantly, there is **no evidence of harm**. We recommend a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than an immediate full implementation or abandonment of the new procedure.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### 1. Service Scores Improved Slightly in Switched Branches
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| **New Procedure (20 branches)** | 47.5 | 48.8 | **+1.3** |
| **Old Procedure (14 branches)** | 51.2 | 50.6 | **-0.7** |

The new procedure is associated with a **2-point improvement** relative to branches that kept the old procedure.

### 2. Results Are Promising but Not Definitive
- The estimated effect could reasonably range from **slightly negative (-0.5) to meaningfully positive (+4.3)**
- There is an **11% probability** this result occurred by chance even if the procedure has no real effect
- Conventional statistical standards require this probability to be below 5% for a definitive conclusion

### 3. The New Procedure Does Not Appear to Harm Service Quality
- The worst plausible scenario suggests minimal negative impact
- Switched branches maintained or improved their scores while control branches declined slightly
- This is reassuring for any rollout decision

### 4. Staffing Levels Influence Service Scores
- Branches with higher staffing consistently achieve better service scores
- This factor should be considered alongside any procedural changes

### 5. Individual Branch Characteristics Matter
- Some branches consistently outperform or underperform regardless of procedure
- This suggests branch-specific factors (location, management, community) play a larger role than the shelving/rotation procedure itself

---

## **RECOMMENDATION: PHASED ROLLOUT WITH MONITORING**

We recommend **Option C** from the analysis:

| Action | Timeline | Success Metric |
|--------|----------|----------------|
| **Phase 1:** Roll out to 5-10 additional branches | Weeks 1-8 | Service scores stable or improving |
| **Phase 2:** Evaluate and adjust | Weeks 9-12 | No branch shows >3 point decline |
| **Phase 3:** Complete rollout if Phase 1-2 succeed | Weeks 13+ | System-wide score improvement |

**Triggers to Pause or Revert:**
- Any branch shows sustained score decline of 3+ points over 4 weeks
- System-wide average declines by 2+ points
- Staff feedback indicates significant operational problems

---

## **WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE COOPERATIVE**

| If We Roll Out | If We Maintain Status Quo |
|----------------|---------------------------|
| Potential 4% service improvement across all branches | No change to current service levels |
| Operational consistency across all branches | Continued two-system complexity |
| Risk of modest score decline (unlikely) | Missed opportunity if new procedure is beneficial |

---

## **TECHNICAL SUMMARY**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Treatment effect | +1.94 points |
| 95% Confidence interval | -0.45 to +4.33 |
| Statistical significance | Not significant (p = 0.11) |
| Data scope | 34 branches, 17 weeks, 578 observations |
| Method | Difference-in-differences regression |

---

**DECISION REQUESTED:** Approve phased rollout of the new procedure with monitoring protocol as outlined above.
