# **Library Cooperative Shelving Procedure Evaluation**
## Analysis of New Branch Rotation and Stocking Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving procedure shows a **positive but not statistically conclusive** effect on service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure improved by approximately **2.5 points** relative to branches that kept the old procedure. However, this result does not meet the conventional threshold for statistical significance (p=0.10), meaning we cannot confidently rule out random variation as an explanation.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **phased rollout** to additional branches while continuing to monitor results, rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Total branches | 34 |
| Branches using new procedure | 20 |
| Branches using old procedure | 14 |
| Study period | 17 weeks (weeks 6–22) |
| Switch occurred | Week 15 |

### Service Score Results
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| New Procedure | 46.0 | 47.1 | +1.1 |
| Old Procedure | 51.4 | 50.1 | –1.3 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+2.4** |

### Statistical Summary
- **Estimated treatment effect:** +2.5 points (approximately 5% of baseline)
- **95% confidence interval:** –0.5 to +5.4 points
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.10 (not significant at p < 0.05)

### What the Trends Show
- **Before the switch:** Both groups showed relatively stable trends
- **After the switch:** Treatment branch scores trended upward (+0.35 points/week) while control branch scores trended downward (–0.14 points/week)
- **Baseline difference:** Branches that adopted the new procedure started with lower service scores (46.0 vs. 51.4), suggesting they may have had more room for improvement

---

## **Interpretation**

**Why this matters:** A 2.5-point improvement would be meaningful for patron experience if the effect is real. The direction of the evidence favors the new procedure, but the sample size and study duration limit our confidence.

**Why we're cautious:** The confidence interval includes zero, meaning the true effect could be slightly negative, neutral, or substantially positive. More data is needed to reach a definitive conclusion.

**Why proceed anyway:** The potential upside outweighs the risks. There is no evidence of harm, and the procedure change appears operationally feasible based on the 20 branches already using it.

---

## **Next Steps**

### 1. Phased Expansion (Next Quarter)
- Roll out the new procedure to **5–10 additional branches**
- Prioritize branches with lower baseline service scores, where effects may be larger
- Document implementation costs and staff feedback

### 2. Continued Monitoring
- Track service scores for **at least 8–12 weeks** post-implementation for each new adopter
- Collect qualitative feedback from branch staff on operational impacts
- Review results quarterly with the board

### 3. Decision Criteria for Full Rollout
Proceed with system-wide implementation if follow-up data shows:
- Consistent positive effects across new adopters
- Improved statistical significance (p < 0.05) with larger sample
- No adverse operational impacts or increased costs

### 4. Further Analysis Opportunities
- Investigate whether effects vary by **branch size, location, or baseline performance**
- Assess whether **additional training** could amplify positive effects
- Evaluate **cost-benefit** relative to the magnitude of improvement

---

## **Conclusion**

The evidence suggests the new shelving procedure improves service scores, but we lack the statistical confidence to declare it a definitive success. A phased rollout allows the cooperative to capture potential benefits while gathering the additional data needed for a confident system-wide decision.
