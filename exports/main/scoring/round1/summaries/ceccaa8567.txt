# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

---

## **New Shelving Procedure: Impact on Service Scores**

---

### **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new shelving and stock rotation procedure saw their service scores increase by approximately **2.1 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and grew stronger over time, reaching about **2.6 points** in the weeks after branches had time to adjust.

**Recommendation: Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

### **KEY FINDINGS**

**Study Overview**
- **709 branches** participated: 425 adopted the new procedure, 284 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22); the switch occurred in week 15
- Analysis compares changes in treatment branches to changes in control branches (difference-in-differences)

**Service Score Results**

| Period | New Procedure Branches | Old Procedure Branches | Gap |
|--------|------------------------|------------------------|-----|
| Before switch (weeks 6–14) | 52.5 | 50.6 | 1.9 |
| After switch (weeks 15–22) | 53.6 | 49.5 | 4.1 |
| **Estimated Impact** | | | **+2.1 points** |

- The **2.1-point improvement** is statistically significant (p < 0.001)
- This represents approximately a **4% improvement** relative to the average service score
- Control branch scores declined after week 14, while treatment branch scores increased—strengthening confidence that the new procedure caused the improvement

**The Effect Built Over Time**

The improvement was not immediate. Branches needed time to adjust:

| Period After Switch | Estimated Effect |
|---------------------|------------------|
| Weeks 15–17 (early) | ~0.2 points (not significant) |
| Weeks 18–22 (later) | ~2.6 points (significant) |

This pattern is consistent with a learning curve as staff became proficient with the new procedure.

---

### **NEXT STEPS**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new shelving and stock rotation procedure to all remaining branches.

2. **Plan for a 3–4 week adjustment period** per branch before evaluating success. Early results may underestimate the true benefit.

3. **Provide implementation support.** Since the effect grew over time, ongoing training and troubleshooting will help branches reach full benefit faster.

4. **Continue monitoring service scores** post-rollout to confirm the effect persists at scale and identify branches needing additional support.

5. **Investigate the decline in control branch scores** after week 14 to understand whether external factors affected service and whether additional interventions are needed.
