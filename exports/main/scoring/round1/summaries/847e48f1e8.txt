# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Subject:** Recommendation on System-Wide Rollout of New Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation procedure is associated with a **modest improvement in service scores** (+1.75 points, or approximately 3.4%), but the evidence is **not statistically conclusive** at conventional levels. We recommend a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Overview**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **14 branches** maintained the old procedure as a control group
- Analysis compares service score changes between the two groups using a difference-in-differences approach

### **Results Summary**

| Group | Pre-Switch Average | Post-Switch Average | Change |
|-------|-------------------|---------------------|--------|
| **Branches that switched** | 52.01 | 53.79 | **+1.78** |
| **Branches that did not switch** | 50.89 | 50.92 | +0.03 |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | — | — | **+1.75** |

### **Statistical Confidence**
- **Estimated effect:** +1.75 points on the service score index
- **95% confidence interval:** -0.34 to +3.84 points
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.10 (suggestive but not definitive)
- **Practical significance:** Approximately 3.4% improvement relative to baseline

### **What This Means**
1. Branches that switched showed improvement; branches that did not switch remained stable
2. The positive effect holds even after accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume
3. The result is **promising but not definitive**—we cannot rule out random variation with current data
4. There is **no evidence of harm** from the new procedure

---

## **Recommendations**

### **1. Proceed with Cautious Expansion**
The point estimate is positive and operationally meaningful. No negative effects were detected.

### **2. Extend the Observation Period**
Collect 8–12 additional weeks of data to increase statistical confidence before full rollout.

### **3. Document Implementation Practices**
Identify and share best practices from high-performing switched branches to maximize benefits.

### **4. Conduct Cost-Benefit Review**
Weigh the modest service score improvement against training and implementation costs.

### **5. Establish Success Metrics for Full Rollout**
If proceeding system-wide, set a target improvement (e.g., +2 points) and timeline for formal reassessment.

---

## **Decision Options for the Board**

| Option | Action | Rationale |
|--------|--------|-----------|
| **A** | Approve phased rollout to remaining branches | Positive trend; low risk; allows continued learning |
| **B** | Delay decision pending additional data | Maximizes statistical confidence before commitment |
| **C** | Approve full system-wide rollout immediately | Accelerates benefits but accepts greater uncertainty |

**Analyst Recommendation:** Option A (phased rollout)

---

## **Technical Appendix**

- **Method:** Difference-in-differences regression with branch-clustered standard errors
- **Validity check:** Pre-treatment trends were parallel between groups (p = 0.86)
- **Robustness:** Results unchanged when controlling for staffing and visitor volume
- **Data:** 578 observations from branches.csv
