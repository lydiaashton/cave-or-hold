# **Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure Evaluation**

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure shows a **positive but statistically inconclusive** effect on service scores. Branches that switched to the new procedure improved by approximately **2 points (4%)** more than branches that kept the old procedure, but this difference could reasonably be due to random variation. We **cannot confidently conclude** the new procedure causes improvement based on this data alone.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. What the Data Shows
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches (20 branches) | 47.5 | 49.0 | **+1.4** |
| Non-switched branches (14 branches) | 49.3 | 48.8 | **-0.5** |
| **Difference (Treatment Effect)** | | | **+1.9 points** |

### 2. Statistical Confidence
- **P-value: 0.12** (using appropriate branch-level clustering)
- This means there's a 12% probability we'd see this result even if the new procedure had no real effect
- Conventionally, we need p < 0.05 (5% probability) to claim statistical significance
- **95% confidence interval: -0.5 to +4.4 points** — the true effect could be zero or even slightly negative

### 3. Pre-Trends Check (Validates Our Analysis)
Before the switch (weeks 6-14), both groups followed parallel trends (p=0.82). This confirms our comparison method is valid — the groups were behaving similarly before the intervention.

### 4. Context
- Service scores average around 50; a 2-point improvement represents about 4% gain
- Staffing and visitor volume significantly affect scores (both p < 0.05), but the procedure effect remains positive after accounting for these factors
- Individual branch results varied widely: some switched branches improved by 7+ points, others declined by 5+ points

## **Recommendations & Next Steps**

### **Do NOT roll out to all branches yet.** The evidence is promising but not conclusive.

### Recommended Actions:

1. **Extend the observation period** — Collect 8-12 more weeks of data. With more post-switch observations, we can detect smaller effects with greater confidence.

2. **Investigate variation across branches** — The wide range of outcomes (-5.7 to +7.2 points among switched branches) suggests the procedure may work better in some contexts. Analyze which branch characteristics predict success.

3. **Consider a phased expansion** — Rather than all-or-nothing, switch 5-10 additional branches and monitor results. This creates more statistical power while limiting risk.

4. **Review implementation quality** — Variation in outcomes may reflect inconsistent adoption of the new procedure. Audit whether all switched branches are following the protocol correctly.

5. **Re-evaluate in 3 months** — With additional data, we can provide a definitive recommendation on system-wide rollout.
