# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation procedure is associated with a modest improvement in service scores (approximately +2 points, or 4% above baseline), but this effect is **not statistically significant** at conventional levels. With the current data, we cannot confidently conclude that the new procedure improves service quality. The board should consider either (a) extending the evaluation period to gather more data, or (b) proceeding with a phased rollout while continuing to monitor outcomes.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in service scores between switching and non-switching branches

### **Results**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect on service score | **+2.1 points** |
| 95% Confidence interval | **-0.5 to +4.6 points** |
| Statistical significance (p-value) | **0.125** (not significant) |
| Effect size relative to baseline | **~4% improvement** |

### **What This Means**
- The point estimate suggests a **positive effect**, but the confidence interval includes zero
- We cannot rule out that the true effect is **zero** or even **slightly negative**
- The data are also consistent with an improvement of up to **4.6 points**

### **Validity Checks**
- **Parallel trends assumption holds**: Service score trends were similar between groups before the switch (p=0.999)
- **Results are robust**: Similar estimates with and without controlling for staffing and visitor volume
- **Limited statistical power**: With only 34 branches, the study can only detect relatively large effects

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended Actions**

1. **Continue monitoring for 8–12 more weeks**
   - Additional data would improve statistical power
   - Could detect smaller effects with greater confidence

2. **If proceeding with rollout, use a phased approach**
   - Implement in stages rather than all at once
   - Continue tracking service scores to confirm effects

3. **Consider complementary metrics**
   - Service score is one measure; also track patron satisfaction, circulation, and operational efficiency
   - The new procedure may have benefits not captured by service scores alone

4. **Document implementation fidelity**
   - Ensure branches are following the new procedure consistently
   - Variation in implementation could dilute measured effects

### **Decision Framework**

| If the board prioritizes... | Recommended action |
|----------------------------|-------------------|
| **Statistical certainty** | Wait for more data before full rollout |
| **Potential upside** | Proceed with phased rollout; the effect is likely positive |
| **Risk avoidance** | Keep current mixed approach; no clear harm detected |
