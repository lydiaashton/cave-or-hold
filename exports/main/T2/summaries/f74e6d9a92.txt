# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw their service scores increase by approximately **1.6 points (3.2% improvement)** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is highly statistically significant (p < 0.001) and appears to grow over time. We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. **Clear Positive Impact on Service Scores**
- **Switched branches** (425 branches): Service scores increased from 50.24 to 51.39 (+1.14 points)
- **Control branches** (284 branches): Service scores decreased from 49.86 to 49.12 (-0.75 points)
- **Net treatment effect**: +1.89 points using simple difference-in-differences; +1.60 points with branch and week fixed effects (our most rigorous estimate)

### 2. **Statistically Robust Results**
The finding holds across multiple analytical approaches:
- Basic comparison: +1.89 points (p < 0.001)
- With branch fixed effects: +1.59 points (p < 0.001)
- With branch and week fixed effects: +1.61 points (p < 0.001)
- Full model controlling for staffing and visits: +1.60 points (p < 0.001)

### 3. **Benefits Accumulate Over Time**
The positive effect appears to strengthen as branches gain experience with the new procedure:
- Early post-switch period (weeks 15-18): ~1.9 point advantage
- Late post-switch period (weeks 19-22): ~2.7 point advantage

This suggests staff may need time to fully realize the procedure's benefits.

### 4. **Greatest Gains for Lower-Performing Branches**
Branches that started with below-median service scores saw larger improvements (+1.58 points) compared to higher-performing branches (+0.72 points). The new procedure also helped prevent the decline observed in control branches.

### 5. **No Pre-Existing Differences**
Analysis of the pre-switch period (weeks 6-14) shows no systematic differences in trends between branches that would later switch versus those that wouldn't, supporting the validity of our causal interpretation.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all 284 branches still using the old method.

2. **Plan for implementation support**, recognizing that full benefits may take 4-8 weeks to materialize as staff gain familiarity.

3. **Prioritize lower-performing branches** in the rollout sequence, as they stand to gain the most.

4. **Continue monitoring** service scores during rollout to confirm the effect persists at scale and identify any branches needing additional support.

5. **Document the new procedure's best practices** from early-adopter branches to accelerate learning across the cooperative.

---

**Analysis Details:** Data covers 709 branches over 17 weeks (weeks 6-22), with 12,053 branch-week observations. The switch occurred at week 15. All statistical models used cluster-robust standard errors at the branch level to account for repeated observations.
