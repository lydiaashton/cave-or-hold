# **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **2.2 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.4 to 3.0 points) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents approximately a **4% improvement** over the baseline service score of 50. The result is highly statistically significant (p < 0.001), and the evidence strongly supports rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **150 branches** total: 90 adopted the new procedure, 60 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data**: Weeks 6–14 before the switch, Weeks 15–22 after
- Analysis used a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in treatment branches to changes in control branches while accounting for branch-specific and week-specific factors

### Results
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Treatment (new procedure) | 50.45 | 53.11 | +2.66 |
| Control (old procedure) | 49.65 | 50.12 | +0.46 |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | | | **+2.20** |

### Statistical Validity
- **Parallel trends confirmed**: Treatment and control branches showed similar patterns before the switch (p = 0.999), validating the comparison
- **Highly significant**: The improvement is unlikely to be due to chance (p < 0.001)
- **Precise estimate**: We are 95% confident the true effect lies between 1.4 and 3.0 points
- **Not explained by other factors**: The improvement is not driven by changes in staffing levels or visitor volume

### Consistency
- **62 of 90 treatment branches** (69%) showed improvement after switching
- The effect was consistent across all post-switch weeks, with treatment branches outperforming control branches by 2–4 points each week

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all 60 branches currently using the old procedure

3. **Document best practices**: Investigate what the highest-performing treatment branches did differently to maximize benefits

4. **Consider complementary improvements**: Since staffing and visitor volume did not change significantly, explore whether combining the new procedure with other interventions could yield additional gains

5. **Schedule follow-up review**: Reassess service scores 3–6 months after full rollout to confirm sustained improvement
