# **Memorandum to the Board**

## **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

### **Bottom Line**

The new procedure is associated with a **modest improvement in service scores of approximately 1.9 points** (on a scale where the average branch scores about 50). However, this effect is **not statistically significant at conventional levels** (p ≈ 0.10), meaning we cannot rule out that the observed improvement occurred by chance. The evidence is **suggestive but not conclusive**.

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. Study Design**
- 34 branches total: 20 adopted the new procedure (treatment), 14 kept the old procedure (control)
- Data covers 17 weeks (weeks 6–22); the switch occurred in week 15
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in treatment branches to changes in control branches

**2. Main Result**
- Treatment branches improved by **1.43 points** after the switch (from 47.52 to 48.95)
- Control branches declined by **0.47 points** over the same period (from 49.28 to 48.81)
- **Net effect (DiD estimate): +1.90 points** (95% confidence interval: approximately -0.4 to +4.2)

**3. Statistical Significance**
- The p-value is approximately **0.10**, which means there is about a 10% probability of observing this result if the new procedure had no real effect
- This falls short of the conventional 5% threshold for statistical significance
- The effect is **positive and directionally consistent with expectations**, but the evidence is not strong enough to be certain

**4. Pre-Treatment Trends**
- Before the switch, treatment branches consistently scored **1–4 points lower** than control branches
- The gap between groups did not show a systematic widening or narrowing trend before week 15, supporting the validity of the comparison

**5. Post-Treatment Pattern**
- The effect appears strongest in **week 16** (+4.5 points, statistically significant)
- Effects in subsequent weeks are positive but smaller and not individually significant
- This suggests the benefit may be real but variable, or that branches are still adjusting to the new procedure

**6. Other Factors**
- Higher visitor volumes are associated with higher service scores (statistically significant)
- Staffing levels show a positive but less certain relationship with service scores

---

### **Next Steps**

**1. Continue Monitoring**
- Collect additional weeks of data to increase statistical power
- The current 8-week post-switch window may be too short to detect a stable effect
- Re-analysis with more data could provide a clearer answer

**2. Investigate Variation Across Branches**
- Some branches may benefit more than others depending on size, staffing, or visitor volume
- A heterogeneous effects analysis could identify which branches are best suited for the new procedure

**3. Consider a Phased Rollout**
- Given the suggestive (but not definitive) positive results, consider expanding the new procedure to additional branches while continuing to monitor outcomes
- This would generate more data and allow refinement of the procedure before a full system-wide rollout

**4. Cost-Benefit Assessment**
- If the new procedure requires additional staff time or resources, weigh the ~2-point service score improvement against implementation costs
- Even a modest improvement may be worthwhile if costs are low

---

### **Recommendation**

The data provides **tentative support** for the new procedure, with a positive effect in the expected direction. However, the statistical uncertainty means we cannot be confident the improvement is real rather than random variation. I recommend **continuing the pilot with expanded monitoring** rather than an immediate full rollout, while gathering more data to reach a firmer conclusion.
