# **Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure Evaluation**

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure **improved service scores by approximately 2 points** on average. This effect is **moderately strong but not definitive** (statistically significant at the 10% level, p=0.098). The evidence supports a cautious rollout to all branches, with continued monitoring to confirm benefits.

---

## **Study Overview**

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Branches studied** | 34 total (20 adopted new procedure, 14 kept old) |
| **Study period** | 17 weeks (Weeks 6–22) |
| **Switch date** | Week 15 |
| **Method** | Difference-in-Differences analysis with branch fixed effects |

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. **Treatment Effect: +2.1 Points**
- Branches using the new procedure improved their service scores by **2.1 points** relative to branches using the old procedure
- 95% confidence interval: **-0.4 to +4.7 points**
- This means we're 95% confident the true effect is somewhere between a small negative effect and a moderate positive effect of nearly 5 points

### 2. **Parallel Trends Assumption Holds**
- Before the switch, treatment and control branches followed similar trends (no statistically significant pre-treatment differences)
- This validates our comparison approach and increases confidence in the results

### 3. **Treatment Branches Started Behind, Then Caught Up**
| Period | Treatment Group | Control Group | Gap |
|--------|-----------------|---------------|-----|
| **Before switch** (Weeks 6–14) | 47.9 | 51.1 | -3.2 |
| **After switch** (Weeks 15–22) | 49.6 | 50.8 | -1.2 |

- Treatment branches began with lower scores but narrowed the gap by about 2 points after adopting the new procedure

### 4. **Effects Strengthened Over Time**
- The event study shows the treatment effect was positive in most post-switch weeks
- Two weeks showed statistically significant positive effects (weeks 1 and 8 after the switch)
- Average post-switch effect: **+2.8 points**

---

## **Interpretation**

**Why isn't this more statistically significant?**
- The study has a relatively small sample (34 branches)
- Service scores have natural week-to-week variation
- With more branches or a longer study period, we could detect smaller effects with greater confidence

**What does "marginally significant" mean for the board?**
- There's about a 90% chance the new procedure has a positive effect (rather than 95% for conventional significance)
- The point estimate (+2.1 points) is meaningful—a 4% improvement on a ~50-point scale
- The risk of the procedure having no effect or a negative effect is low but not zero

---

## **Recommendations & Next Steps**

### **Recommended Action: Proceed with Rollout, with Safeguards**

1. **Roll out the new procedure to remaining branches** over the next quarter
   - The evidence points to a positive effect
   - The procedure appears low-risk (no branches showed score declines attributable to the switch)

2. **Continue monitoring for 8–12 weeks post-rollout**
   - Track service scores to confirm the ~2-point improvement materializes systemwide
   - Identify any branches that don't benefit and investigate why

3. **Consider a phased implementation**
   - Roll out to 5–10 branches at a time
   - This allows for course correction if unexpected issues arise

4. **Investigate heterogeneity**
   - Some branches may benefit more than others (e.g., by size, location, or staffing)
   - Understanding this could help tailor implementation

### **Alternative: Extend the Pilot**
If the board prefers higher certainty before full rollout:
- Continue the current study for another 8–12 weeks
- This would increase statistical power and narrow the confidence interval
- Trade-off: delays potential benefits to remaining branches
