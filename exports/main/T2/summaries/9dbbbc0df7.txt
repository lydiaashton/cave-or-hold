# **Board Memo: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure shows a **positive but not statistically significant** effect on service scores. Branches that switched to the new procedure improved their service scores by approximately **2.2 points more** than branches that kept the old procedure—a **4.7% improvement** relative to the average score. However, due to the limited sample size and natural variability in weekly scores, we cannot rule out that this improvement occurred by chance. **We recommend a cautious, phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** analyzed over **17 weeks** (Weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** switched to the new procedure; **14 branches** kept the old procedure (control group)
- Switch occurred in **Week 15**, allowing 9 weeks of before-data and 8 weeks of after-data

### **Service Score Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 45.0 | 47.8 | **+2.7** |
| Non-switched branches | 48.7 | 49.2 | **+0.5** |
| **Difference (DiD)** | | | **+2.2** |

### **Statistical Assessment**
- **Effect size**: +2.2 points (4.7% of average score; small-to-moderate practical significance)
- **Statistical significance**: p ≈ 0.10–0.14 (not significant at the conventional 0.05 threshold)
- **Confidence**: We estimate roughly a **90% probability** the effect is positive, but only ~85–90% confidence it's not due to random variation

### **Validity Checks**
- ✅ **Parallel trends confirmed**: Before the switch, both groups showed nearly identical weekly trends (+0.42 vs. +0.48 points/week), supporting the validity of our comparison
- ✅ **No pre-existing differences in trends**: The groups were moving in parallel before the intervention
- ⚠️ **Limited statistical power**: With only 34 branches and 8 post-switch weeks, the study may be underpowered to detect smaller but meaningful effects

### **Visualization**
A chart showing service scores over time has been saved as `service_scores_analysis.png`. It illustrates that both groups tracked closely before Week 15, with the switched branches showing a modest upward shift afterward.

## **Next Steps & Recommendations**

### **1. Proceed with Phased Rollout**
Given the positive direction of the effect and no evidence of harm, we recommend **expanding the new procedure to additional branches** while continuing to collect data. This balances the potential benefit against the uncertainty.

### **2. Extend the Evaluation Period**
Collect **at least 12–16 more weeks of data** post-rollout to:
- Increase statistical power
- Determine if the effect persists or grows over time
- Identify any seasonal patterns

### **3. Monitor Implementation Quality**
Track whether branches are **fully implementing** the new procedure. Variation in implementation fidelity could be masking larger effects.

### **4. Consider Complementary Metrics**
In addition to service scores, monitor:
- Staff time required for shelving
- Patron satisfaction surveys
- Item availability/fill rates

### **5. Set a Decision checkpoint**
Reconvene in **3 months** with updated data. If the effect remains positive and achieves statistical significance (p < 0.05), proceed with system-wide implementation.

## **Technical Appendix**

Analysis used a **difference-in-differences** regression design with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch). The model controls for week fixed effects to account for system-wide temporal trends. All analyses were conducted in Python using standard statistical packages.
