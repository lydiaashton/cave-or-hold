# **Analysis of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Contrary to initial expectations that the change would have no effect, branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores that were **1.9 points higher** (on average) than branches that kept the old procedure, after accounting for underlying trends. This difference is statistically significant (p < 0.01). We recommend proceeding with a full rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **60 branches** kept the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in service scores before and after the switch between the two groups

### Results

| Group | Before Switch (Weeks 6–14) | After Switch (Weeks 15–22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------------|---------------------------|--------|
| **New Procedure** | 49.63 | 48.93 | −0.70 |
| **Old Procedure** | 50.56 | 47.98 | −2.57 |
| **Difference** | | | **+1.87** |

- Both groups experienced a decline in service scores after week 14 (likely due to seasonal or external factors), but branches using the **new procedure declined much less**
- The **net benefit of the new procedure is +1.87 points** on the service score index (95% confidence interval: 0.73 to 3.01)
- This result holds even after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### Visual Evidence

The trend chart (saved as `service_scores_trend.png`) shows that both groups followed similar patterns before week 15, supporting the validity of the comparison. After the switch, the treatment group's scores remained more stable relative to the control group.

### Statistical Confidence
- The estimated effect is statistically significant at the 1% level (p = 0.001)
- Pre-switch trends were parallel between groups, validating the analysis approach
