# **Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure Evaluation**

## **Bottom Line**

The evidence does **not support** a confident conclusion that the new procedure improved service scores. While branches that switched show higher average scores after the change, this pattern was already emerging *before* the switch occurred. The analysis cannot distinguish the procedure's effect from pre-existing differences between branches. **We recommend against a system-wide rollout until further evidence is gathered.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. What the Basic Analysis Shows
- **709 branches** were studied over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **425 branches** (60%) adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **284 branches** (40%) kept the old procedure
- Simple comparison suggests switched branches improved by approximately **1.9 points** on the service score index relative to non-switched branches

### 2. Why This Estimate Is Not Reliable

**The critical problem:** Branches that switched were already on a different trajectory *before* the change.

| Period | Average Gap (Switched − Non-Switched) |
|--------|--------------------------------------|
| Weeks 6–14 (before switch) | 2.02 points |
| Weeks 15–22 (after switch) | 3.88 points |

The gap was **widening steadily before the switch** (growing by ~0.32 points per week). After the switch, it continued widening at a similar rate (~0.23 points per week). There is **no visible jump** at week 15 when the new procedure began.

### 3. Statistical Tests Confirm the Concern

| Test | Result | Interpretation |
|------|--------|----------------|
| **Placebo test** (fake switch at week 12) | "Effect" of 1.56 points (statistically significant) | The method detects "effects" even when no change occurred |
| **Narrow window** (weeks 11–18 only) | Effect of 0.67 points (not statistically significant) | Effect disappears when focusing on period closest to switch |
| **Pre-trend test** | Gap widening at 0.32 pts/week before switch (p<0.001) | Parallel trends assumption violated |

### 4. What We Can Conclude

- Switched branches consistently outperformed non-switched branches throughout the study period
- This difference existed before the procedure change and continued afterward
- **We cannot attribute the difference to the new procedure** — it may reflect:
  - Branches that chose to switch were already higher-performing or improving faster
  - Unobserved factors affecting both the decision to switch and service scores
  - Natural variation in branch performance over time

## **Next Steps**

### Recommended Actions

1. **Do not proceed with system-wide rollout** based on current evidence. The risk of implementing an ineffective (or potentially harmful) procedure across all branches is too high.

2. **Extend the evaluation period.** Collect data for additional weeks to:
   - Determine if the gap continues widening or stabilizes
   - Assess whether any effect emerges with more time

3. **Improve the evaluation design:**
   - Consider **randomized assignment** of branches to the new procedure for future pilots
   - Collect **branch-level characteristics** (size, location, staffing levels) to better control for differences
   - Track **additional outcome measures** beyond service score (e.g., patron satisfaction, circulation, wait times)

4. **Conduct qualitative research.** Interview staff at switched vs. non-switched branches to understand:
   - Implementation challenges
   - Perceived benefits or drawbacks
   - Whether the procedure is being applied consistently

5. **Re-evaluate in 8–12 weeks** with additional data and a stronger research design.
