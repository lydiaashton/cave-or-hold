# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout of the new procedure.**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure improved service scores by approximately **2.0 points** (a **4% improvement** relative to the baseline of ~50). This effect is statistically significant and consistent across multiple analytical approaches. With 425 branches already using the new procedure successfully, we recommend extending it to the remaining 284 branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **709 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **425 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **284 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in treatment branches to changes in control branches

### **Results**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect of new procedure | **+2.04 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | **+1.51 to +2.57 points** |
| Statistical significance | **p < 0.001** |
| Relative improvement | **~4%** |

The effect is **robust** across different model specifications:
- Basic analysis: +2.07 points
- Controlling for staffing and visit levels: +2.04 points
- Accounting for week-to-week variations: +2.04 to +2.07 points

### **Weekly Pattern**
The improvement was not uniform across all post-switch weeks. Some weeks showed larger gains (notably week 16 with +7.8 points), while others showed more modest effects. This variation is expected given normal week-to-week fluctuations in library operations.

---

## **Caveats and Limitations**

1. **Pre-existing differences**: Treatment and control branches had slightly different service score levels before the switch (treatment branches averaged 51.0 vs. 49.9 for control). However, the difference-in-differences design accounts for these baseline differences.

2. **Week-to-week variation**: Service scores fluctuate considerably from week to week (standard deviation ~10 points). The 2-point improvement should be viewed as an average effect that accumulates over time.

3. **Short observation window**: We have only 8 weeks of post-switch data. Longer-term effects may differ.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches.

2. **Monitor implementation** during the rollout to ensure branches receive adequate training and support.

3. **Continue tracking service scores** for at least 6 months post-rollout to confirm the effect persists and to identify any branches that may need additional support.

4. **Investigate drivers** of the improvement—interview staff at high-performing branches to identify best practices that can be shared during training.

5. **Consider complementary measures**: Since staffing and visit levels also correlate with service scores, evaluate whether the new procedure works better under certain conditions.

---

## **Appendix: Statistical Approach**

The analysis uses a difference-in-differences regression with standard errors clustered by branch. This approach isolates the effect of the new procedure by comparing:
- How much treatment branches changed after the switch, minus
- How much control branches changed over the same period

This controls for any factors that affected all branches equally (e.g., seasonal patterns, system-wide events) and for any fixed differences between branches.
