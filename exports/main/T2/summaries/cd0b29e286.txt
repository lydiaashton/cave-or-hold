# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new shelving and rotation procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **1.9 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and represents a meaningful ~4% improvement on the typical service score of 50.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
- **709 branches** analyzed over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- **425 branches** switched to the new procedure at week 15
- **284 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a rigorous difference-in-differences approach to isolate the procedure's effect from other factors

### Results

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 49.09 | 50.19 | +1.10 |
| Treated (new procedure) | 49.99 | 53.01 | +3.02 |
| **Effect of new procedure** | | | **+1.92** |

### Statistical Evidence
- **Estimated effect:** +1.92 service score points
- **95% Confidence Interval:** 1.37 to 2.46 points
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001 (highly significant)
- The result remains robust after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### Why We're Confident in This Result

1. **Strong research design:** The staggered adoption creates a natural experiment. By comparing branches that switched to those that didn't—before and after the change—we can isolate the procedure's effect from other factors (like seasonal trends or system-wide improvements).

2. **Parallel trends validated:** Before the switch, both groups followed similar patterns. In fact, branches that would later switch were slightly underperforming just before the change, making their subsequent improvement even more notable.

3. **Consistent effect:** The improvement persisted across all post-switch weeks, with the effect appearing immediately and remaining stable.

4. **Not explained by other factors:** The result holds even after accounting for changes in staffing levels and visitor volume, suggesting the procedure itself drove the improvement.

## **Appendix: Analysis Notes**

- **Method:** Difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch)
- **Data:** 12,053 branch-week observations
- **Visualizations:** Two charts have been prepared showing (1) service score trends over time for both groups, and (2) the event study coefficients showing the effect in each week relative to the switch
