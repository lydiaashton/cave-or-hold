# **Board Summary: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure shows a **positive but statistically inconclusive effect** on service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure improved by an average of **2.4 points** on the service score index compared to branches that kept the old procedure. However, this difference is not statistically significant (p=0.14), meaning we cannot rule out that the observed improvement occurred by chance. The board should consider a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **34 branches** total: 20 adopted the new procedure, 14 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks** of data (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Service scores average around 50 points (higher is better)

### Results

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| **New Procedure** (20 branches) | 48.77 | 50.98 | **+2.21** |
| **Old Procedure** (14 branches) | 50.31 | 50.14 | -0.17 |
| **Difference** | | | **+2.38** |

### Statistical Assessment
- **Estimated effect:** +2.38 points on service score
- **95% Confidence Interval:** -0.79 to +5.54 points
- **P-value:** 0.14 (not statistically significant at conventional 0.05 threshold)

### What This Means
- The **true effect could range from slightly negative (-0.8) to moderately positive (+5.5)** points
- There is a **~86% probability** the procedure has some positive effect, but we lack sufficient evidence to be confident
- The parallel trends assumption appears valid: both groups showed similar patterns before the switch

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Continue the pilot** with the current 20 branches for an additional 8–12 weeks to gather more data and increase statistical power

2. **Monitor implementation fidelity** at switched branches to ensure the new procedure is being applied consistently

3. **Consider a stepped-wedge rollout** where remaining branches adopt the procedure in waves, allowing continued evaluation while expanding access

4. **Track secondary outcomes** (staffing efficiency, patron satisfaction, circulation rates) that may capture benefits not reflected in the service score

5. **Re-evaluate in Q2** with the expanded dataset; if the effect remains around +2 points with tighter confidence intervals, proceed with full rollout

---

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed cautiously.** The new procedure shows promise but lacks definitive proof of effectiveness. A measured expansion with rigorous monitoring balances potential benefits against the risk of implementing an unproven change system-wide.
