# **Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure Evaluation**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by **2.1 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.5 to 2.6 points) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents a **4% improvement** over baseline scores. The result is statistically robust and consistent across different types of branches.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Study Design
- **709 branches** total: 425 switched to the new procedure (treatment group), 284 kept the old procedure (control group)
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22), with the switch implemented in week 15
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in service scores between switching and non-switching branches before and after week 15

### 2. Main Results
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 49.5 | 52.3 | +2.8 |
| Non-switched branches | 48.5 | 49.2 | +0.8 |
| **Net effect (DiD)** | | | **+2.1** |

- The **2.1-point improvement** is highly statistically significant (p < 0.001)
- We are 95% confident the true effect lies between **1.5 and 2.6 points**

### 3. Robustness Checks
- Results hold when controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume
- Effect is consistent across branches with high/low staffing and high/low visits
- Results remain significant even when excluding potentially anomalous weeks

### 4. Practical Significance
- Service scores average around 50 across the system; a 2-point gain is meaningful
- The improvement translates to better patron experience at switching branches
- No evidence the procedure works better or worse for any particular type of branch
