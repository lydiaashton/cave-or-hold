# Analysis of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

## Bottom Line

The new procedure is associated with a **modest improvement in service scores** (approximately +1.8 points, or 3.4% above baseline), but this effect is **not statistically significant** at conventional levels. We cannot confidently conclude the new procedure caused the improvement, though the direction of the effect is positive and consistent with expectations.

## Key Findings

### Study Design
- **34 branches** tracked over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15 (treatment group)
- **14 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach to isolate the effect of the procedure change

### Service Score Results

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 54.79 | 57.22 | +2.42 |
| Non-switched branches | 48.74 | 49.39 | +0.65 |
| **Difference-in-Differences Effect** | | | **+1.77** |

### Statistical Confidence
- **Estimated effect:** +1.77 points on the service score index
- **95% confidence interval:** -0.91 to +4.45 points
- **p-value:** 0.19 (not statistically significant at the 0.05 level)

This means the true effect could plausibly range from a small negative impact to a moderate positive impact of up to 4.5 points.

### Branch-Level Patterns
- **85%** of switched branches showed improvement vs. **57%** of non-switched branches
- Switched branches had more consistent gains (standard deviation of 3.04 vs. 4.37 points)
- The treatment group started with higher baseline scores (54.79 vs. 48.74), suggesting they may have been selected based on prior performance

### Important Context
- Service scores fluctuate week-to-week for all branches
- Control branches experienced larger staffing improvements during the study period, which may have boosted their scores independently
- The parallel trends assumption (required for this analysis) appears reasonable but not perfect—the gap between groups varied somewhat before the switch

## Next Steps

### Recommended Actions

1. **Continue monitoring for one more quarter**
   - The current 8-week post-switch window is relatively short
   - Additional data would improve statistical power and confidence in the estimate

2. **Consider a phased rollout with continued evaluation**
   - Given the positive (though uncertain) direction of effects, expanding to additional branches is reasonable
   - Implement as a controlled rollout rather than system-wide, allowing for ongoing comparison

3. **Investigate implementation variation**
   - Some switched branches saw large gains (+10 points) while others declined (-4 points)
   - Understanding what differentiates high-performing adopters could improve overall results

4. **Track complementary metrics**
   - Staffing and visit patterns differ between groups and affect service scores
   - Consider whether the new procedure interacts with staffing levels or patron volume

### Decision Framework for the Board

| If the board prioritizes... | Recommendation |
|----------------------------|----------------|
| **Minimizing risk** | Wait for more data before full rollout |
| **Capturing potential gains** | Proceed with phased expansion; the upside (up to +4.5 points) outweighs the small downside risk |
| **Evidence-based certainty** | Extend the evaluation period by 8–12 weeks |
