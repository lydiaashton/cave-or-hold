# **Analysis of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure is associated with a **modest improvement** in service scores (+1.6 points, or about 3%), but this effect is **not statistically significant** at conventional levels. The data do not provide strong evidence that the new procedure improves service, but they also do not rule out a meaningful benefit. We recommend a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** total: 20 adopted the new procedure (treatment), 14 kept the old procedure (control)
- **17 weeks** of data per branch (weeks 6–22)
- The switch occurred in **week 15**
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in treatment branches to changes in control branches

### **Results**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect on service score | **+1.62 points** |
| 95% confidence interval | **-0.44 to +3.67 points** |
| P-value | **0.123** (not statistically significant) |
| Effect size relative to baseline | **+3.3%** |

### **What the Numbers Mean**

- **Before the switch**, treatment branches averaged 47.95; control branches averaged 49.38
- **After the switch**, treatment branches averaged 51.83; control branches averaged 51.65
- Treatment branches improved by **3.88 points**, while control branches improved by **2.27 points**
- The **net effect** attributable to the new procedure is 1.62 points

### **Validity Checks**

- **Parallel trends assumption**: Pre-switch trends between treatment and control branches show no systematic differences, supporting the validity of the analysis
- **Covariate adjustment**: Controlling for staffing and visit levels does not materially change the results
- **Weekly patterns**: The post-switch effect varies week-to-week, with some weeks showing gains and others showing no difference

---

## **Interpretation**

The evidence is **suggestive but inconclusive**:

1. **The point estimate is positive** (1.6 points), which aligns with the expectation that the new procedure would help
2. **However, statistical uncertainty is high**—the true effect could be slightly negative (-0.4) or moderately positive (+3.7)
3. **The effect, if real, is modest**—about 3% above baseline scores

This pattern could reflect:
- A genuine but small improvement that requires more data to detect confidently
- Variation in how well different branches implemented the new procedure
- External factors affecting all branches during the study period

---

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended Actions**

1. **Continue the pilot for 8–12 more weeks** to increase statistical power and observe whether effects persist or grow

2. **Gather qualitative feedback** from branch staff on:
   - Implementation challenges
   - Perceived benefits or drawbacks
   - Suggestions for refinement

3. **Consider a staged rollout**:
   - Offer the new procedure to additional branches voluntarily
   - Monitor outcomes before committing to system-wide adoption

4. **Investigate heterogeneity**:
   - Do certain branch types (size, location, traffic) benefit more?
   - Are there implementation best practices that could amplify the effect?

### **Decision Framework**

| If the board prioritizes... | Recommendation |
|----------------------------|----------------|
| **Evidence certainty** | Wait for more data before full rollout |
| **Potential upside** | Proceed with phased rollout; 3% improvement could be meaningful at scale |
| **Risk tolerance** | The procedure shows no harm; low-risk to expand gradually |

---

## **Conclusion**

The new procedure shows **promise but not proof**. A cautious, data-driven expansion—combined with ongoing measurement—would allow the cooperative to capture potential benefits while managing implementation risk.
