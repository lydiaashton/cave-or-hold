**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new stock rotation and shelving procedure is associated with a statistically significant improvement in service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2 points (3.9%)** compared to branches that continued with the old procedure. We recommend proceeding with a full rollout, while monitoring implementation quality and continuing to track outcomes.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect
- **Estimated improvement:** +1.95 service score points (95% confidence interval: 1.39 to 2.52)
- **Percentage gain:** 3.9% improvement over the baseline average score of 50.7
- **Statistical significance:** Highly significant (p < 0.001)

### 2. Study Design
- **709 branches** analyzed over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- **425 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15
- **284 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis controls for staffing levels and branch visit volumes

### 3. Pattern of Effects
| Period | Switched Branches | Control Branches | Gap |
|--------|-------------------|------------------|-----|
| Before (Weeks 6–14) | 51.05 | 50.17 | +0.88 |
| After (Weeks 15–22) | 54.75 | 51.92 | +2.83 |
| **Change** | **+3.70** | **+1.75** | **+1.95** |

The gap between switched and control branches widened after the procedure change, consistent with a positive treatment effect.

### 4. Robustness
- The effect remains stable (1.95 points) whether or not we control for staffing and visits
- Both staffing and visits are positively associated with service scores, as expected
- Post-period effects are consistently positive across all weeks after the switch

### 5. Important Caveat
Switched branches showed slightly stronger improvement trends *before* the procedure change (the gap grew from -0.42 to +2.09 points in the pre-period). This suggests branches selected for early adoption may have been on a more positive trajectory. However:
- The post-period gap (average +2.83) exceeds all but the final pre-period week
- The acceleration in the gap after week 15 is consistent with a treatment effect
- The estimated effect is economically meaningful and statistically robust

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with full rollout** of the new procedure to remaining branches, with phased implementation to manage operational capacity.

2. **Establish implementation standards** to ensure consistent adoption across all branches. Document best practices from early-adopting branches.

3. **Continue monitoring** service scores for at least 8–12 weeks post-rollout to confirm effects persist at scale.

4. **Track secondary outcomes** including staffing efficiency and patron satisfaction to capture the full impact of the procedure change.

5. **Consider a formal evaluation** after full rollout to assess long-term sustainability of the improvement and identify any branches that may need additional support.

## **Appendix: Analytical Approach**

This analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** design, comparing changes in service scores for branches that adopted the new procedure versus those that did not, before and after week 15. Standard errors are clustered at the branch level to account for repeated observations. The model controls for weekly staffing levels and visit volumes.
