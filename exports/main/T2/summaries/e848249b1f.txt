# **MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2025  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure is associated with a **modest improvement in service scores** (+2.2 points, or ~4%), but the evidence is not statistically conclusive. We recommend a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in the treatment group to changes in the control group

### Results

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect of new procedure | **+2.18 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | **−0.34 to +4.70 points** |
| Statistical significance | **p = 0.09** (marginally significant) |
| Relative improvement | **~4.4%** above baseline |

### Supporting Evidence

1. **Branch-level outcomes:** 14 of 20 switched branches (70%) showed score improvements after the switch, compared to only 7 of 14 control branches (50%).

2. **Parallel trends check:** Before the switch, treatment and control branches had similar trajectories (average difference of −0.6 points), supporting the validity of the comparison.

3. **Post-switch pattern:** After week 15, switched branches consistently outperformed control branches in 6 of 8 weeks, with an average gap of +1.6 points.

### Limitations

- The confidence interval includes zero, meaning we cannot rule out no effect
- Week-to-week variability is substantial (standard deviation ~10 points)
- Only 8 weeks of post-switch data available; longer-term effects are unknown
- Some pre-period weeks showed elevated differences, though the immediate pre-switch period was stable

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Continue monitoring** the 20 switched branches for at least 8 more weeks to confirm the effect persists and to gather more precise estimates.

2. **Expand the pilot** to 5–10 additional branches on a trial basis to increase statistical power and test the procedure in different branch contexts.

3. **Investigate heterogeneity** to identify which types of branches benefit most (e.g., by size, location, or staffing levels).

4. **Cost-benefit analysis:** Before full rollout, quantify the operational costs of the new procedure against the estimated 4% service score improvement.

5. **Decision point:** Revisit this analysis in Q1 2026 with additional data. If the effect remains positive and precision improves, proceed with system-wide implementation.
