## **BOTTOM LINE**

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout, with monitoring.**

Branches that adopted the new procedure showed a **statistically significant improvement of 1.8 points** in service scores (approximately **3.4% improvement**). The effect is consistent across all 8 post-switch weeks and robust to controlling for staffing levels, visitor volume, and branch-specific characteristics. However, some caution is warranted due to pre-existing differences between branches that chose to switch versus those that did not.

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### 1. Treatment Effect
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Service score improvement | **+1.81 points** |
| 95% Confidence interval | [1.45, 2.18] |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 |
| Relative improvement | **3.4%** |
| Effect size | 0.18 standard deviations |

### 2. Study Design
- **709 branches** total (425 adopted new procedure, 284 kept old procedure)
- **17 weeks** of data (weeks 6–22)
- Switch occurred in **week 15**
- Analysis uses difference-in-differences with branch fixed effects

### 4. Robustness
The result holds when controlling for:
- Weekly staffing levels

## **CAVEATS AND LIMITATIONS**

**Pre-existing trends:** Branches that chose to adopt the new procedure were already showing slightly upward momentum in service scores before the switch (the treatment-control gap widened from -1.1 to +1.8 points during the pre-period). This suggests some **selection bias**—branches that opted in may have been more motivated or better-managed.

**What this means:** The true effect of the new procedure is likely **positive but may be somewhat smaller** than the 1.8-point estimate. The branch fixed effects model accounts for time-invariant differences, which provides confidence that most of the observed effect is genuine.

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all 709 branches.

2. **Implement phased rollout** over 4–6 weeks to allow for training and adjustment.

3. **Continue monitoring** service scores for all branches post-rollout to:
   - Confirm the effect persists at scale
   - Identify any branches that struggle with implementation
   - Detect any unintended consequences

4. **Document implementation best practices** from the 425 early-adopter branches to support training.

5. **Re-evaluate in 3 months** with full post-rollout data to confirm long-term impact.

---

## **APPENDIX: Analysis Method**

This analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing the change in service scores for branches that switched procedures versus those that did not, before and after week 15. The model controls for weekly staffing levels, visitor volume, and branch-specific fixed effects to isolate the causal impact of the procedure change.
