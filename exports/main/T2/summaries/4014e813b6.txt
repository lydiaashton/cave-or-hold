# MEMORANDUM

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## BOTTOM LINE

The data provide **suggestive but inconclusive evidence** that the new stock rotation procedure improved service scores. While branches that adopted the new procedure showed a modest improvement relative to those that did not, the result is not statistically significant, and important methodological concerns limit our ability to draw firm conclusions. **We cannot confidently recommend a system-wide rollout based on this evidence alone.**

---

## KEY FINDINGS

### 1. What the Data Show

| Group | Pre-Switch Avg | Post-Switch Avg | Change |
|-------|---------------|-----------------|--------|
| Switched branches (20) | 46.7 | 48.5 | +1.8 |
| Non-switched branches (14) | 50.3 | 49.8 | -0.4 |
| **Estimated effect** | | | **+2.2 points** |

The difference-in-differences estimate suggests the new procedure increased service scores by approximately **2.2 points** (about 4% of the baseline average of 48.6).

### 2. Statistical Significance

- **P-value: 0.10** — This exceeds the conventional 0.05 threshold for statistical significance
- **95% Confidence Interval: -0.4 to +4.7 points** — The true effect could plausibly be zero or even slightly negative
- In practical terms: if there were no real effect, we would observe a result this large about 10% of the time by random chance

### 3. Important Methodological Concerns

**Parallel Trends Assumption Violated:** The difference-in-differences method requires that treatment and control branches would have followed similar trends absent the intervention. Our analysis shows this assumption does not hold:
- Switched branches scored systematically **3–5 points lower** than non-switched branches throughout the entire observation period
- Pre-switch trends differed significantly between groups (p < 0.001)
- This suggests switched branches may have been selected precisely because they were underperforming

**Inconsistent Weekly Effects:** The estimated effect varies considerably across post-switch weeks, with only the final week (week 22) showing a statistically significant positive effect. This pattern is inconsistent with a stable, sustained improvement.

### 4. Robustness Checks

We tested multiple model specifications, all yielding consistent but imprecise estimates:
- Adding branch fixed effects: +2.16 points (p = 0.10)
- Controlling for pre-period branch performance: +2.16 points (p = 0.10)
- Controlling for staffing and visit indices: +2.16 points (p = 0.11)

The consistency across specifications is reassuring, but none overcome the fundamental limitation that the groups were not comparable at baseline.

---

## NEXT STEPS

### Recommended Actions

1. **Do not proceed with system-wide rollout yet.** The evidence is too weak to justify the costs and disruption of changing procedures at all 34 branches.

2. **Extend the observation period.** Eight weeks of post-switch data may be insufficient to detect a stable effect, especially if there is a learning curve. Continue collecting data for at least 3–6 more months.

3. **Consider a randomized rollout.** For future evaluations, randomly assign branches to treatment and control conditions to ensure comparability. This would provide much stronger causal evidence.

4. **Investigate heterogeneity.** The effect may differ by branch characteristics (size, location, staffing levels). Understanding which branches benefit most could inform a targeted rather than universal rollout.

5. **Monitor implementation fidelity.** Verify that switched branches are actually implementing the new procedure as intended. Variation in implementation could obscure real effects.

### Alternative Interpretation

If the board is inclined to proceed despite the statistical uncertainty, the potential upside (a 2-point improvement affecting 20 branches) should be weighed against:
- Implementation costs and staff training time
- Risk of disruption during transition
- Opportunity cost of not pursuing other improvements
