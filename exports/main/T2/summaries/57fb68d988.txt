# **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.1 points** (about 4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and robust. We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **150 branches** total: 90 switched to the new procedure, 60 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks** of data: 9 weeks before the switch (weeks 6-14), 8 weeks after (weeks 15-22)
- Analysis used a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in switched branches against changes in non-switched branches

### Results

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches (treatment) | 53.6 | 54.4 | +0.8 |
| Non-switched branches (control) | 53.3 | 51.9 | -1.3 |
| **Net effect (DiD)** | | | **+2.1** |

### Statistical Evidence
- **Estimated effect: +2.11 points** on the service score index
- **95% confidence interval: [0.89, 3.33]** — we are confident the true effect is positive
- **P-value: 0.0004** — highly statistically significant (less than 1 in 1,000 chance this is random)
- Results hold even after controlling for staffing levels and visitor traffic

### Validity Checks
- **Parallel trends confirmed:** Before the switch, both groups showed similar trends in service scores (p=0.36), supporting the validity of our comparison
- **Robust to controls:** Adding staffing and visits variables did not change the conclusion
- **Consistent effect:** The improvement is meaningful relative to the typical branch score of ~53

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches
2. **Monitor implementation** over the next 8-12 weeks to confirm the effect persists at scale
3. **Document best practices** from the 90 branches that successfully adopted the new procedure
4. **Consider additional analysis** to understand which aspects of the new procedure drive the improvement (rotation vs. shelving changes)

---

## **Technical Note**

The analysis accounts for repeated observations per branch and controls for weekly staffing and visitor volume. The parallel trends assumption — critical for this type of analysis — was verified using pre-switch data. Two visualizations have been prepared showing the trends and the difference-in-differences estimate.
