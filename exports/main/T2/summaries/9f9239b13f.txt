# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new shelving and rotation procedure improved service scores.** Contrary to the expectation that it would make no difference, branches that adopted the new procedure saw their service scores increase by approximately **2 points** (about 4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and the evidence supports rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Main Result: Positive Impact on Service Quality
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect | **+1.97 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | [0.76, 3.18] |
| Statistical significance | p = 0.0015 |
| Baseline average score | ~51 points |
| Percentage improvement | **~3.9%** |

### 2. Analysis Approach
This analysis used a **difference-in-differences** design, which compares:
- **Treatment group:** 102 branches that switched to the new procedure
- **Control group:** 48 branches that kept the old procedure
- **Before period:** Weeks 6–14 (before the switch)
- **After period:** Weeks 15–22 (after the switch)

This method accounts for underlying trends and branch-specific characteristics, isolating the effect of the procedure change itself.

### 3. Supporting Evidence
- **Parallel trends confirmed:** Before the switch, both groups showed similar trends in service scores (p = 0.96), validating the comparison.
- **Effect sustained over time:** The improvement was observed in both the early weeks (15–17) and later weeks (18–22) after the switch.
- **Robust result:** The finding holds even when excluding the first week after the switch (which could reflect adjustment effects).
