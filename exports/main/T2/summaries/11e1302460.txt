# **Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure Evaluation**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new rotation and shelving procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **1.9 points** (approximately 4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This result is statistically significant and consistent across different types of branches. We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measurable Improvement in Service Quality
- **Treatment effect: +1.89 points** on the service score index (95% confidence interval: 1.33 to 2.45 points)
- This represents approximately a **3.8% improvement** from the baseline average score of 50.1
- The result is highly statistically significant (p < 0.001)

### 2. Clear Before/After Pattern
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches (425 branches) | 50.24 | 51.39 | **+1.15** |
| Non-switched branches (284 branches) | 49.86 | 49.12 | **-0.75** |
| **Net effect (Difference-in-Differences)** | | | **+1.89** |

The control group (non-switched branches) actually experienced a slight decline in service scores during the same period, while switched branches improved—suggesting the new procedure helped branches maintain or improve service quality during the transition.

### 3. Effect Is Consistent Across Branch Types
The improvement was observed regardless of branch characteristics:
- **High-staffing branches:** +2.0 points
- **Low-staffing branches:** +1.8 points
- **High-visit branches:** +1.5 points
- **Low-visit branches:** +2.2 points

This consistency suggests the procedure works well across diverse operational contexts.

### 4. Analysis Method
We used a **difference-in-differences** approach, which is the standard method for evaluating policy changes when some units adopt a treatment and others don't. This method:
- Compares switched branches to non-switched branches
- Controls for pre-existing differences between branches
- Accounts for overall time trends affecting all branches
- Uses cluster-robust standard errors to account for repeated observations per branch

**Data:** 709 branches, 17 weeks (weeks 6–22), 12,053 observations

## **Next Steps**

### Recommended Actions

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all 284 remaining branches currently using the old method.

2. **Develop implementation support materials** based on lessons learned from the 425 early-adopter branches. Consider documenting best practices from the highest-performing switched branches.

3. **Monitor service scores during rollout** to ensure the effect persists as the procedure scales. Set up monthly tracking with a target of maintaining the ~2-point improvement.

4. **Investigate the mechanism**—while we know the procedure works, understanding *why* (e.g., faster restocking, better organization, reduced staff time on shelving) could help optimize implementation and training.

5. **Consider a formal cost-benefit analysis** if implementation costs are available. The 4% service score improvement should be weighed against any additional staffing, training, or operational expenses.

### Caveats

- The analysis covers 8 weeks of post-switch data. Longer-term effects should be monitored to ensure the improvement is sustained.
- Some variation in weekly scores is normal; the key finding is the consistent positive gap between switched and non-switched branches after the policy change.
