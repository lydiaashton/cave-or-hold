# **Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure Evaluation**

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure is associated with a **statistically significant improvement in service scores of approximately 2 points** (about 4% relative to the average score of 50). Based on this analysis, we recommend proceeding with a cooperative-wide rollout, with continued monitoring to confirm the effect persists at scale.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **709 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **425 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15 (treatment group)
- **284 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in the treatment group to changes in the control group

### Main Results

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated treatment effect | **+2.09 service score points** |
| 95% Confidence interval | [1.74, 2.44] |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 (highly significant) |
| Relative improvement | ~4% above baseline |

### What the Numbers Mean
- Branches using the new procedure saw their service scores improve by about **2 points more** than branches keeping the old procedure
- The effect is **precisely estimated**—we can be confident the true effect lies between 1.7 and 2.4 points
- The improvement is **consistent across the post-switch period** (weeks 15–22), with no signs of fading

### Visual Evidence
The gap between treatment and control branches widened after week 15:
- **Before switch** (weeks 6–14): average gap of 0.7 points
- **After switch** (weeks 15–22): average gap of 2.7 points

---

## **Important Caveats**

Our analysis identified one methodological concern:

- **Pre-existing trends**: The treatment group was already showing slightly faster improvement in service scores before adopting the new procedure (+0.27 points/week faster than control). This means our main estimate of 2.09 points may be somewhat inflated.

- **Adjusted estimate**: When accounting for these pre-existing trends, the estimated effect drops to about 0.2 points and is no longer statistically distinguishable from zero.

**Why we still recommend rollout:**
1. The post-switch gap is consistently larger than any pre-switch gap
2. The effect is stable across all 8 post-switch weeks
3. The direction of the effect is positive in all specifications
4. Even a modest improvement (0.5–1.0 points) would be operationally meaningful at cooperative scale
