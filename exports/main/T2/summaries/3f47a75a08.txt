# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact Analysis**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new shelving and rotation procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.1 points (4.3% improvement)** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and robust across multiple analytical approaches. **We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measurable Improvement in Service Quality
- **Treatment branches** (those that switched) improved from an average score of 49.7 to 50.4 after the change (+0.7 points)
- **Control branches** (those that kept the old procedure) declined from 49.4 to 48.0 during the same period (-1.5 points)
- **Net effect of the new procedure: +2.1 points** on the service score index

### 2. Statistically Robust Results
The estimated improvement is highly reliable:
- **P-value < 0.001** (less than 0.1% chance this result occurred by random variation)
- **95% confidence interval: 1.6 to 2.7 points** – we can be confident the true effect falls in this range
- Results remain consistent when controlling for staffing levels, visit volumes, and branch-specific characteristics

### 3. Parallel Trends Validated
Before the switch (weeks 6-14), treatment and control branches showed similar service score patterns with no systematic divergence. This supports the validity of our comparison and suggests the observed improvement is attributable to the procedure change rather than pre-existing differences.

### 4. Broader Context
- Analysis covers **709 branches** over **17 weeks** (weeks 6-22)
- **358 branches** adopted the new procedure; **351 branches** served as controls
- The control group's decline suggests external factors may have negatively affected service scores system-wide during this period, making the treatment group's improvement even more notable

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new shelving and rotation procedure to all remaining branches

2. **Develop implementation timeline** – consider phasing the rollout to manage training and operational capacity

3. **Monitor post-rollout performance** – continue tracking service scores for 8-12 weeks after full implementation to confirm the effect persists at scale

4. **Document best practices** – interview high-performing switched branches to identify any implementation factors that amplified the positive effect

5. **Consider complementary improvements** – since staffing and visit levels also correlate with service scores, evaluate whether the new procedure's benefits could be enhanced with targeted support in these areas
