# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and rotation procedure is associated with a **2-point improvement in service scores** (approximately 4% relative to the baseline average of 51). However, this effect is **not statistically significant** at conventional levels, meaning we cannot confidently conclude the procedure caused the improvement. The data are consistent with both a modest benefit and no effect at all. **We recommend a cautious, phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Estimated Impact**
- **Treatment effect**: +2.0 points on the service score index
- **95% Confidence Interval**: -0.5 to +4.5 points
- **Interpretation**: While the point estimate suggests improvement, the confidence interval includes zero, meaning the observed effect could be due to random variation

### **2. Statistical Evidence**
- **P-value**: 0.11 (above the conventional 0.05 threshold for statistical significance)
- When controlling for staffing and visit levels, the effect is slightly larger (+2.2 points) with marginal significance (p=0.085)
- The analysis properly accounts for the fact that observations from the same branch are correlated over time

### **3. Study Design Validity**
- **Parallel trends assumption holds**: Treatment and control branches had similar service score trends before the switch (p=0.57), supporting the validity of our comparison
- 20 branches adopted the new procedure; 14 branches continued with the old procedure
- All branches had complete data for all 17 weeks (weeks 6-22)

### **4. Branch-Level Results**
| Metric | Treatment Branches | Control Branches |
|--------|-------------------|------------------|
| Number of branches | 20 | 14 |
| % with score improvement | 65% | 57% |
| Average score change | +1.72 | -0.29 |

### **5. Effect Over Time**
- **Early post-period (weeks 15-18)**: +2.5 points (statistically significant, p=0.046)
- **Late post-period (weeks 19-22)**: +1.5 points (not significant, p=0.34)
- This pattern suggests the effect may diminish over time, warranting further investigation

### **6. Other Factors**
- Staffing levels are positively associated with service scores (p=0.015)
- The treatment effect persists after accounting for staffing and visit levels

## **Next Steps**

1. **Continue monitoring**: Extend the observation period to determine whether the effect stabilizes, grows, or fades over time

2. **Phased expansion**: Consider rolling out the new procedure to additional branches in stages, treating each phase as a learning opportunity

3. **Investigate heterogeneity**: Analyze whether certain types of branches (by size, location, or staffing) benefit more from the new procedure

4. **Cost-benefit analysis**: Evaluate whether the modest potential improvement justifies the operational costs of system-wide implementation

5. **Qualitative feedback**: Gather input from branch staff about the new procedure's practical impacts, which may reveal benefits not captured in the service score metric

- **Method**: Difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch)
- **Data**: 578 observations (34 branches × 17 weeks)
- **Switch date**: Week 15
- **Analysis period**: Weeks 6-22 (9 weeks pre-switch, 8 weeks post-switch)
