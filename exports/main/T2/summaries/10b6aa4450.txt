# **Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new procedure saw their service scores increase by approximately **2 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant (p < 0.01) and represents about a 4% improvement relative to the typical branch score of 50. **We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. The New Procedure Had a Positive, Measurable Impact

Using a difference-in-differences analysis (comparing switched branches to non-switched branches before and after week 15), we estimate the new procedure increased service scores by **+2.05 points** (95% confidence interval: 0.8 to 3.3 points).

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 52.12 | 51.20 | -0.91 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 51.42 | 52.55 | +1.14 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+2.05** |

### 2. The Result Is Statistically Robust

The finding holds across multiple analytical approaches:
- **Basic model:** +2.05 points (p = 0.001)
- **With controls** (staffing and visitor traffic): +2.04 points (p = 0.001)
- **With branch fixed effects** (controlling for branch-specific characteristics): +2.03 points (p = 0.001)

### 3. Pre-Switch Trends Support the Analysis

Before the switch (weeks 6-14), both groups showed similar patterns in service scores with no systematic divergence. This parallel pre-trend strengthens confidence that the observed post-switch difference is attributable to the procedure change rather than pre-existing differences between branches.

### 4. The Effect Is Meaningful in Context

A 2-point improvement on a scale where the average branch scores about 50 represents a **4% gain** in service quality. This is a practically significant improvement for library patrons.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all 150 branches.

2. **Monitor implementation** during the rollout to ensure branches are adopting the procedure correctly and consistently.

3. **Continue tracking service scores** post-rollout to confirm the effect persists at scale and to identify any branches that may need additional support.

4. **Consider investigating mechanisms** – the analysis controlled for staffing levels and visitor traffic, both of which also positively affect service scores. Future analysis could explore whether the new procedure improves efficiency, reduces wait times, or enhances staff-patron interactions.

5. **Document and share best practices** from the 90 branches that piloted the new procedure to support smooth adoption across the cooperative.

---

*Analysis based on 2,550 weekly observations from 150 branches over 17 weeks (weeks 6-22). The switch occurred at week 15. Statistical models used cluster-robust standard errors to account for repeated observations within branches.*
