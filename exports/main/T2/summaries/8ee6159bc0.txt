# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation procedure is associated with a **modest improvement in service scores** (approximately 2.3 points, or 4.5%), but this effect is **not statistically significant** at conventional levels. The evidence is suggestive but not conclusive. We recommend a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in service scores between switching and non-switching branches before and after the policy change

### **Results**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect of new procedure | **+2.3 points** on service score |
| Baseline average score | 51.1 points |
| Percentage improvement | **4.5%** |
| Effect size | 0.23 standard deviations (small-to-moderate) |
| Statistical significance | **p = 0.10** (not significant at 0.05 level) |
| 95% Confidence Interval | **-0.5 to +5.2 points** |

### **Interpretation**
- Branches that switched to the new procedure improved their service scores by about 4 points after the switch, while control branches improved by about 1.7 points. The **net attributable effect is 2.3 points**.
- However, the confidence interval includes zero, meaning we **cannot rule out the possibility that the true effect is zero** (or even slightly negative).
- The **parallel trends assumption is satisfied**: before the switch, both groups showed similar week-to-week trends in service scores, supporting the validity of our analysis.
- The effect, if real, would be **practically meaningful** (4.5% improvement) but **modest in magnitude**.

### **What the Data Shows Visually**
The attached trend chart shows that:
- Switching branches started with lower service scores (by about 2.7 points on average)
- After the switch, the gap between switching and non-switching branches narrowed considerably
- Post-switch, switching branches performed roughly on par with control branches

## **Next Steps**

1. **Continue monitoring**: Extend data collection for another 8–12 weeks to increase statistical power and determine whether the effect strengthens over time as staff become more familiar with the new procedure.

2. **Phased expansion**: Consider rolling out the new procedure to an additional 10–15 branches while maintaining a control group of 5–10 branches. This would improve our ability to detect the true effect.

3. **Investigate heterogeneity**: Examine whether the procedure works better in certain types of branches (e.g., by size, location, or staffing levels) to identify where it may be most effective.

4. **Cost-benefit analysis**: Before full rollout, assess the implementation costs (training, logistics, staff time) against the potential 4.5% service score improvement to determine if the benefits justify the investment.

5. **Qualitative feedback**: Gather input from branch managers and staff about their experience with the new procedure to identify implementation challenges or best practices.

---

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with cautious expansion, not full rollout.** The data provides encouraging but inconclusive evidence that the new procedure improves service scores. A measured approach—expanding to additional branches while continuing to evaluate—balances the potential benefits against the uncertainty in the current evidence.
