# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and rotation procedure is associated with a **modest improvement** in service scores (+2.4 points, or ~5%), but the evidence is **not statistically conclusive**. While the direction of the effect is positive and aligns with expectations, we cannot rule out the possibility that the observed improvement occurred by chance. The board may consider a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Overall Impact**
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Branches that switched (20 branches) | 49.2 | 51.2 | **+2.0** |
| Branches that did not switch (14 branches) | 49.4 | 49.0 | **-0.4** |
| **Net Effect (Difference-in-Differences)** | | | **+2.4 points** |

### **2. Statistical Confidence**
- **p-value: 0.10** — This exceeds the conventional 0.05 threshold for statistical significance
- **95% confidence interval: -0.5 to +5.4 points** — The true effect could range from slightly negative to moderately positive
- In practical terms: we estimate about a 90% probability the effect is positive, but cannot be 95% confident

### **3. Branch-Level Results**
- **65% of switched branches** (13 of 20) saw their scores improve
- **36% of non-switched branches** (5 of 14) saw their scores improve
- This 29-percentage-point gap suggests the procedure change had a real effect, though outcomes varied considerably across branches

### **4. Pre-Switch Balance**
- Treatment and control branches had nearly identical average service scores before the switch (49.2 vs. 49.4)
- This supports the validity of the comparison — the groups were similar before the intervention

---

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended Actions**

1. **Proceed with cautious expansion** — The evidence points in the right direction. Consider rolling out the new procedure to additional branches while maintaining a control group for continued evaluation.

2. **Extend the observation period** — Eight weeks of post-switch data provides limited statistical power. Collecting 8–12 more weeks of data would substantially improve confidence in the results.

3. **Investigate branch-level variation** — Some branches improved by 6–10 points while others declined. Understanding what distinguishes successful adopters (staff training, branch size, layout, etc.) could improve implementation.

4. **Monitor key metrics during rollout** — Track service scores, staffing levels, and visit volumes to ensure the procedure change does not have unintended consequences.

5. **Set a decision checkpoint** — Re-evaluate after one full quarter of expanded implementation. If the effect remains positive and becomes statistically significant, proceed with system-wide adoption.

---

## **Limitations**

- **Sample size**: Only 34 branches over 17 weeks limits statistical power
- **Short post-period**: Only 8 weeks of data after the switch
- **External factors**: Weekly score volatility suggests other factors (seasonality, staffing, community events) also influence service scores
- **No randomization**: Branches were not randomly assigned to switch, though pre-switch balance suggests selection bias is minimal
