### **Key Findings**

**1. Overall Impact**
- The difference-in-differences analysis shows the new procedure improved service scores by **+1.86 points** (95% CI: 0.73 to 2.99; p=0.001)
- This represents approximately a **4% improvement** relative to the average service score of 49.3
- The effect is equivalent to about 0.19 standard deviations—a modest but meaningful improvement

**2. How the Effect Emerged**
- Both groups saw service scores decline after week 15 (possibly due to seasonal factors affecting all branches)
- Control branches (old procedure): declined by 2.57 points
- Treatment branches (new procedure): declined by only 0.70 points
- The new procedure appears to have **helped branches maintain performance** during a challenging period

**3. Week-by-Week Pattern**
- **Weeks 15-16**: Positive effects (+1.0 to +1.3 points)
- **Weeks 17-22**: Effects varied, with some negative weeks but the treatment group still outperformed the control group overall
- This pattern suggests the new procedure provided resilience rather than a uniform boost

**4. Validity Checks**
- **Parallel trends**: Pre-switch trends were similar between groups (p=0.86), supporting the validity of our comparison
- **Controls**: Results hold after accounting for staffing levels and visit volumes, both of which independently predict service scores
