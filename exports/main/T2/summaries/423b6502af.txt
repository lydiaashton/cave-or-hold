# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure **significantly improved service scores** at branches that adopted it. Switched branches saw an average increase of **1.9 points** on the service score index compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant and represents approximately a **4% gain** in service quality. We recommend proceeding with a full rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. The New Procedure Improved Service Scores**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Non-Switched Branches (Control) | 50.59 | 51.02 | +0.43 |
| Switched Branches (Treatment) | 50.26 | 52.62 | +2.36 |

**Difference-in-Differences Estimate: +1.93 points** (95% Confidence Interval: 0.78 to 3.08; p=0.001)

### **2. The Result Is Statistically Robust**

- The effect remains significant after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume
- The effect is consistent across the post-switch period (both early weeks 15-18 and later weeks 19-22)
- Adding week-by-week controls does not change the conclusion

### **3. The Analysis Design Is Valid**

- **Parallel trends confirmed**: Before the switch, both groups followed similar service score trajectories (p=0.76 for pre-trend test)
- **Balanced groups**: Staffing and visitor patterns were similar between switched and non-switched branches before the change
- **Natural experiment**: 90 branches adopted the new procedure while 60 kept the old one, providing a valid control group

### **4. Context for the Effect Size**

- Average service score across all branches: ~50 (standard deviation ~10)
- The 1.9-point improvement equals about **0.2 standard deviations**—a modest but meaningful gain
- For comparison: the control group's natural week-to-week variation was only +0.4 points

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches

2. **Monitor implementation** to ensure the effect persists as more branches adopt the procedure

3. **Investigate mechanisms**: The analysis shows staffing and visit levels partially explain service scores but don't fully account for the improvement. Consider qualitative follow-up to understand *why* the new procedure works (e.g., faster item location, better organization, staff efficiency)

4. **Track long-term effects**: Continue monitoring to confirm the improvement is sustained and doesn't diminish over time

5. **Consider cost-benefit analysis**: If the new procedure requires additional training or resources, weigh those costs against the 4% service quality improvement
