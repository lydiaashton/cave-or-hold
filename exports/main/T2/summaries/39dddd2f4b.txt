# **Board Summary: New Branch Procedure Evaluation**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new procedure saw their service scores increase by approximately **2.0 points** more than branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and robust across multiple analytical approaches. We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **709 branches** total: 425 switched to the new procedure, 284 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks** of data (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring in week 15
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in switched branches to changes in non-switched branches

### **Main Result**
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 52.0 | 54.8 | +2.8 |
| Non-switched branches | 50.6 | 51.4 | +0.8 |
| **Difference (Treatment Effect)** | | | **+2.0** |

The new procedure is associated with a **2.0-point increase** in service scores (95% confidence interval: 1.4 to 2.6 points; p < 0.001).

### **Robustness Checks**
- **Controlling for staffing and visits:** The effect remains at 2.0 points, indicating the improvement is not simply due to changes in staffing levels or branch traffic
- **Event study analysis:** The positive effect emerged after the switch and persisted in subsequent weeks
- **Statistical significance:** The result is highly significant (p < 0.001), meaning it is very unlikely to be due to random chance

### **Context**
- The average branch service score is approximately 50, so a 2-point improvement represents about a **4% increase**
- This is a meaningful improvement in service quality across the cooperative
