# **Analysis of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores by approximately 2 points (4% of average), and this improvement is statistically significant.** We recommend proceeding with a full rollout to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. **Positive Impact on Service Scores**
- Branches that switched to the new procedure saw their service scores increase by **2.04 points** relative to branches that kept the old procedure
- This effect is **statistically significant** (p = 0.002), with a 95% confidence interval of **0.74 to 3.35 points**
- The effect is robust across multiple analytical approaches (with and without controlling for branch-specific and week-specific factors)

### 2. **Study Design**
- **150 branches** total: 90 adopted the new procedure (treatment), 60 kept the old procedure (control)
- **17 weeks of data**: Weeks 6–14 before the switch, Weeks 15–22 after
- **2,550 observations** total (one per branch per week)

### 3. **Before and After Comparison**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (Old Procedure) | 50.83 | 50.34 | -0.49 |
| Treatment (New Procedure) | 49.89 | 51.45 | +1.56 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+2.04** |

The treatment group improved by 1.56 points while the control group declined slightly, resulting in a net gain of 2.04 points attributable to the new procedure.

### 4. **Validity of the Analysis**
- **Parallel trends assumption is satisfied**: There was no systematic difference in trends between treatment and control branches before the switch (p = 0.995)
- Results hold when controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume
- The effect is consistent across different statistical specifications

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with full rollout** of the new procedure to remaining branches
2. **Monitor service scores** during rollout to ensure effects persist at scale
3. **Document the new procedure** and provide training materials to ensure consistent implementation
4. **Consider a follow-up evaluation** in 3–6 months to assess long-term sustainability of the improvement
