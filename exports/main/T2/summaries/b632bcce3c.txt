## **Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure Evaluation**

### **Bottom Line**
The new stock rotation procedure shows a **positive but not statistically significant** improvement in service scores. Switched branches improved by an estimated **2.2 points (5%)** compared to non-switched branches, but we cannot rule out that this result occurred by chance. We recommend a **cautious, phased rollout** with continued monitoring rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. Overall Impact on Service Scores**
- Branches using the new procedure showed an average improvement of **2.2 points** on the service score index (from a baseline of ~45 points)
- This represents approximately a **5% improvement** in service quality
- The effect is **not statistically significant** at conventional levels (p = 0.11), meaning there's about an 11% chance this result could occur even if the procedure had no real effect
- The 95% confidence interval ranges from **-0.6 to +5.0 points**, so the true effect could be slightly negative or moderately positive

**2. Branch-Level Variation**
- **16 of 20 switched branches (80%)** showed improvement after adopting the new procedure
- **4 branches (20%)** saw their scores decline
- The best-performing switched branch improved by **11.5 points**, while the worst declined by **5.7 points**
- This variation suggests the procedure's effectiveness may depend on branch-specific factors (size, staffing, local conditions)

**3. Comparison to Control Group**
- Non-switched branches (control group) showed minimal change over the same period (+0.5 points)
- This suggests the improvement in switched branches is not simply due to seasonal trends or other time-related factors

**4. Data Quality Notes**
- One branch (Branch 13) had unusually low scores throughout the study period, which may affect overall estimates
- Analysis controlled for this and other branch-specific characteristics

---

### **Next Steps**

**1. Continue Monitoring (Recommended)**
- Extend the observation period to gather more data and increase statistical power
- Track whether the positive trend persists or strengthens over time

**2. Investigate Branch-Level Differences**
- Analyze what distinguishes the 4 branches that saw declines from the 16 that improved
- Consider branch characteristics: size, staffing levels, visitor volume, urban vs. rural location

**3. Pilot Refinement**
- Work with the 4 underperforming switched branches to identify implementation challenges
- Gather qualitative feedback from branch staff on procedural barriers

**4. Phased Rollout Option**
- If proceeding with expansion, consider rolling out to branches similar to the top performers first
- Maintain a control group of non-switched branches to continue measuring impact

**5. Cost-Benefit Analysis**
- Before full rollout, assess whether the estimated 5% service improvement justifies the implementation costs across all branches

- **Sample**: 34 branches, 17 weeks of data (weeks 6-22)
