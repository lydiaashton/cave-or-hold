# **Board Summary: New Branch Procedure Evaluation**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new procedure saw service scores approximately **2 points higher** than they would have under the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and represents about a **4% improvement** relative to baseline scores. We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. The New Procedure Had a Positive Effect**

Using a rigorous difference-in-differences analysis (comparing branches that switched vs. those that didn't, before and after the change), we estimate the new procedure **increased service scores by 1.96 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.45 to 2.48 points, p < 0.001).

| Group | Pre-Switch Avg | Post-Switch Avg | Raw Change |
|-------|---------------|-----------------|------------|
| Control (old procedure) | 51.98 | 48.87 | -3.10 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 53.02 | 51.91 | -1.11 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+1.96** |

Both groups experienced declining scores after week 15 (likely due to seasonal or external factors), but branches using the new procedure declined **much less**—preserving about 2 points of service quality that would otherwise have been lost.

### **2. The Effect Grew Over Time**

The benefit of the new procedure was not immediate but strengthened over the 8-week post-switch period:
- Week 15: +0.7 points (not statistically significant)
- Weeks 16-17: +1.3 to +1.6 points
- Weeks 18-22: +1.7 to +1.9 points (all statistically significant)

This pattern suggests branches needed 1-2 weeks to fully adapt to the new procedure, after which the benefits became clear and sustained.

### **3. Analysis Is Credible**

- **Parallel trends assumption holds**: In the weeks immediately before the switch (weeks 8-13), there were no systematic differences in trends between the two groups.
- **Large sample**: 709 branches, 12,053 weekly observations.
- **Robust methods**: Standard errors clustered by branch to account for repeated measurements.
- **Controls included**: Results hold even after adjusting for staffing levels and visitor volume.

### **4. Scope of Impact**

- **425 branches** (60%) adopted the new procedure
- **284 branches** (40%) kept the old procedure
- If rolled out to all remaining branches, we would expect similar ~2-point improvements across the system
