# **Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **2.1 points** (approximately 4%) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and grew stronger over the 8 weeks following implementation. We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. The New Procedure Had a Positive Effect
- **Treatment branches** (425 branches that switched): Service scores increased from 52.5 to 53.6 after the switch (+1.0 points)
- **Control branches** (284 branches that kept old procedure): Service scores decreased from 50.6 to 49.5 after week 15 (-1.1 points)
- **Net effect**: +2.1 points attributable to the new procedure (95% confidence interval: +1.6 to +2.7 points)

### 2. The Effect Is Statistically Robust
- The result is highly significant (p < 0.001)
- The effect holds when controlling for staffing levels and branch visit volumes
- Cluster-robust standard errors account for repeated observations at the same branches

### 3. Benefits Accumulated Over Time
The improvement was not immediate but grew over the post-switch period:
- Weeks 15-17: Small, non-significant effects (adjustment period)
- Weeks 18-22: Clear positive effects, ranging from +1.1 to +1.8 points

This pattern suggests staff needed 2-3 weeks to adapt to the new procedure before benefits materialized.

### 4. Context Matters
Control branches experienced a decline in service scores during the same period. This could reflect:
- Seasonal factors affecting the library system broadly
- Increased demand or other external pressures

The new procedure appears to have **protected** switching branches from this decline while also delivering genuine improvements.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to remaining 284 branches

2. **Plan for an adjustment period**: Budget for 2-3 weeks of transition time at each branch before full benefits are realized

3. **Monitor implementation**: Track service scores during rollout to ensure effects replicate at scale

4. **Document best practices**: Interview high-performing switched branches to identify implementation factors that maximize benefits

5. **Consider complementary investments**: Staffing and visit volume both correlate with service scores; the new procedure works best when branches are adequately resourced

## **Analytical Notes**

This analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** design, comparing changes in service scores at switching branches to changes at non-switching branches before and after week 15. This approach controls for:
- Pre-existing differences between branches
- System-wide trends affecting all branches
- Week-to-week variation
