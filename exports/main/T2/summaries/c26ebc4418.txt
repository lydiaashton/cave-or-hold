# **Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure Evaluation**

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure shows a **positive but statistically uncertain effect** on service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure improved by approximately **1.9 points** (about 3.6%) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. However, this improvement is **not statistically significant** at conventional confidence levels (p = 0.13), meaning we cannot rule out that the observed improvement occurred by chance.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with caution. The evidence suggests the new procedure may help, but it is not conclusive. Consider a phased rollout with continued monitoring rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Study Design
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure (treatment group)
- **14 branches** kept the old procedure (control group)
- The switch occurred at **week 15**

### 2. Service Score Results

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| New Procedure (Treatment) | 53.31 | 55.33 | +2.02 |
| Old Procedure (Control) | 46.21 | 46.29 | +0.08 |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | | | **+1.93** |

### 3. Statistical Confidence
- **Estimated effect:** +1.93 points
- **95% Confidence Interval:** -0.56 to +4.42 points
- **P-value:** 0.13 (not significant at the 0.05 threshold)

This means the true effect could range from a small negative impact to a meaningful positive improvement of up to 4.4 points.

### 4. Validity Checks
- ✅ **Parallel trends confirmed:** Treatment and control groups followed similar trajectories before the switch (p = 0.84), validating our comparison approach
- ✅ **No confounding changes:** Staffing levels and visitor traffic did not change differently between groups, suggesting the effect (if real) is attributable to the procedure change
