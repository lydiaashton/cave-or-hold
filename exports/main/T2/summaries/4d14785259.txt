# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new shelving and rotation procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **1.8 points** (approximately **3.6%** improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and represents a meaningful improvement in branch service quality.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15 (treatment group)
- **60 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in service scores before and after the switch between the two groups

### **Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 50.03 | 51.47 | +1.44 |
| Non-switched branches | 48.77 | 48.43 | −0.34 |
| **Net effect (DiD)** | | | **+1.78** |

- **Treatment effect: +1.78 points** (95% confidence interval: 0.60 to 2.97)
- **Statistical significance: p = 0.003** (highly significant)
- **Effect size: 3.6%** improvement relative to baseline service scores

### **Supporting Evidence**
- The parallel trends assumption holds: before the switch, both groups showed similar week-to-week patterns (no significant difference in pre-period trends, p = 0.25)
- Results remain robust after controlling for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume
- Switched branches improved while non-switched branches slightly declined, suggesting the effect is not due to system-wide factors

### **Visual Summary**
A trend chart has been generated showing service scores over time for both groups, with a clear divergence after week 15 when the new procedure was implemented.

## **Next Steps**

1. **Recommend rollout to all branches.** The evidence strongly supports expanding the new procedure cooperative-wide, with an expected service score improvement of approximately 1.8 points per branch.

2. **Monitor implementation.** Track service scores during rollout to ensure the effect replicates across all branches. Some variation is expected.

3. **Investigate mechanisms.** Consider studying *why* the new procedure works—is it faster shelving, better stock availability, or improved staff workflow? Understanding the mechanism could help optimize further.

4. **Cost-benefit analysis.** If the new procedure has implementation costs (training, equipment, etc.), weigh these against the 3.6% service improvement to confirm net benefit.

5. **Timeline.** A phased rollout over 4–6 weeks would allow for troubleshooting while capturing benefits quickly.
