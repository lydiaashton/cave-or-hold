# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **2.0 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.3 to 2.8 points) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents approximately a **4% improvement** over baseline scores. The result is statistically significant and contradicts our initial expectation that the change would make no difference.

**Recommendation: Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measurable Improvement in Service Quality
- **Treatment effect:** +2.03 service score points (p < 0.001)
- Switched branches improved from 49.5 to 53.2 (pre to post)
- Control branches improved from 48.6 to 50.3 (pre to post)
- The additional gain for switched branches (+2.0 points) is attributable to the new procedure

### 2. Analysis Method
This evaluation used a **difference-in-differences** approach comparing:
- **90 branches** that switched to the new procedure (treatment group)
- **60 branches** that kept the old procedure (control group)
- **17 weeks** of data (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15

The analysis controlled for branch-specific characteristics and week-to-week variation, isolating the effect of the procedure change.

### 3. Pre-Trends Support Causal Interpretation
Before the switch (weeks 6–14), the gap between switched and control branches remained stable (averaging about 1 point), suggesting the two groups were on parallel trajectories. After the switch, the gap widened to 2–4 points, consistent with a genuine treatment effect.

### 4. Notable Secondary Finding
The service score improvement occurred **despite a small decrease in staffing index** (-0.24, p = 0.004) at switched branches. This suggests the new procedure may have improved efficiency—better service with slightly less staffing. Customer visits were unaffected.
