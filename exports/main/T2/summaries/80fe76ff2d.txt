# **Memorandum to the Board**

## **Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

---

### **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Contrary to the expectation that the change would make no difference, branches that adopted the new procedure maintained significantly higher service scores compared to branches that kept the old procedure. The evidence supports rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

### **KEY FINDINGS**

**1. The new procedure had a positive, statistically significant effect**
- Branches using the new procedure scored **+2.3 points higher** on the service score index compared to what would have been expected without the change
- This effect is statistically significant (p < 0.001), meaning it is very unlikely to be due to random chance
- The 95% confidence interval is +1.0 to +3.5 points

**2. The new procedure helped branches maintain service quality**
- After week 15, branches keeping the old procedure saw their scores **drop by 2.9 points**
- Branches using the new procedure saw only a **0.6 point decline**
- This suggests the new procedure buffered against factors that were causing service scores to decline system-wide

**3. The analysis is methodologically sound**
- 150 branches were studied (90 switched to new procedure, 60 kept old procedure)
- 17 weeks of data (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Before the switch, both groups had nearly identical average scores (~51.8–51.9), confirming the groups were comparable
- No differential changes were detected in staffing or visit patterns that could explain the results

**4. Practical significance**
- The average branch scores about 50 on the service index
- A 2.3-point improvement represents approximately **4–5% better service**
- This is a meaningful improvement for library patrons

---

### **NEXT STEPS**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all remaining branches

2. **Monitor implementation** by tracking service scores at newly converted branches to confirm the effect persists at scale

3. **Investigate the underlying cause** of the system-wide score decline after week 15 that affected control branches—understanding this could reveal additional opportunities for improvement

4. **Document the new procedure** as a best practice, including any training materials or implementation guidelines that contributed to its success

5. **Consider a follow-up evaluation** in 3–6 months to assess whether the improvement is sustained over time
