# **Evaluation of New Shelving Procedure on Library Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving procedure is associated with a **2-point improvement** in service scores (on a scale where the average branch scores about 50). However, this effect is **not statistically significant** at conventional levels. The data are consistent with both a modest benefit and no effect at all. We recommend **proceeding cautiously** with a broader rollout while continuing to monitor outcomes.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- Switch occurred at **week 15**: some branches adopted the new procedure, others kept the old one
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in switching branches to changes in non-switching branches

### **Service Score Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Kept Old Procedure | 47.2 | 48.9 | +1.7 |
| Switched to New | 51.4 | 55.1 | +3.8 |
| **Difference (New vs. Old)** | | | **+2.0** |

### **Statistical Assessment**
- **Estimated effect**: +2.03 points
- **Statistical significance**: p = 0.12 (not significant at the 5% level)
- **95% confidence interval**: -0.55 to +4.61 points

This means we cannot rule out that the true effect is zero—or even slightly negative. However, the interval also allows for effects as large as 4.6 points, which would be meaningful given that the average branch scores about 50.

### **Additional Observations**
- Branches that switched to the new procedure had **consistently higher service scores** than non-switching branches even before the change (about 4 points higher on average). This suggests the two groups may differ in unobserved ways.
- The gap between switching and non-switching branches **widened after the switch**, particularly in weeks 21–22, but the pattern is not uniform across all post-switch weeks.
- **Staffing levels** are positively associated with service scores (branches with higher staffing index tend to score higher), but this does not explain the treatment effect.

---

## **Next Steps & Recommendations**

1. **Do not mandate immediate system-wide rollout.** The evidence is suggestive but not conclusive.

2. **Extend the observation period.** Collect data for additional weeks to increase statistical power and observe whether the effect stabilizes or grows over time.

3. **Consider a randomized rollout.** If feasible, randomly assign remaining branches to switch now vs. later. This would strengthen causal inference and provide cleaner evidence.

4. **Monitor implementation fidelity.** Ensure branches that switched are actually following the new procedure as intended. Variation in implementation could dilute measured effects.

5. **Track secondary outcomes.** Consider whether the new procedure affects other metrics (e.g., staff time, patron satisfaction, circulation) that may not be captured in the service score.

6. **Revisit in 8–12 weeks.** With more post-switch data, re-run this analysis. If the effect becomes statistically significant and remains around 2+ points, a full rollout would be better justified.

---

## **Technical Notes**

- Analysis uses two-way fixed effects regression (controlling for branch and week), with standard errors clustered at the branch level
- The model accounts for pre-existing differences between branches and common time trends
- Results are robust to including controls for staffing and visit indices
