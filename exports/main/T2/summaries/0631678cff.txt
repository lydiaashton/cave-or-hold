# Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores

## Bottom Line

**Yes, the new shelving and rotation procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **2.2 points** (approximately 4.4%) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant and the effect appears to be strengthening over time. We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

## Key Findings

### Study Design
- **709 branches** total: 425 switched to the new procedure (treatment group), 284 kept the old procedure (control group)
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6-22), with the switch implemented in week 15
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in treatment branches to changes in control branches

### Main Results

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Treatment effect** | +2.22 service score points |
| **95% Confidence Interval** | [1.64, 2.80] |
| **Statistical significance** | p < 0.001 (highly significant) |
| **Improvement vs. baseline** | 4.4% |

### What This Means in Practice

- **Before the switch**, treatment and control branches had similar service scores (treatment averaged 50.58, control averaged 49.87—a difference of 0.71 points).
- **After the switch**, treatment branches pulled ahead significantly (treatment averaged 52.58, control averaged 49.64—a difference of 2.93 points).
- **69% of branches** that switched saw their scores improve, compared to only 48% of branches that didn't switch.

### Effect Is Growing Over Time

The improvement isn't just immediate—it's accelerating:
- **Weeks 15-18** (early post-period): Treatment branches averaged 2.3 points higher than control
- **Weeks 19-22** (late post-period): Treatment branches averaged 3.6 points higher than control

This suggests branches are getting better at implementing the new procedure as they gain experience.

### Parallel Trends Check

A key assumption of this analysis is that treatment and control branches would have continued on similar trajectories absent the intervention. The pre-switch data supports this: both groups showed similar week-to-week fluctuations before week 15, with no systematic divergence.

## Next Steps

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to remaining branches.

2. **Develop implementation support** based on learnings from the 425 early-adopter branches. Consider:
   - Training materials highlighting best practices from top-performing switched branches
   - A phased rollout schedule to manage the transition

3. **Continue monitoring** service scores during the rollout to ensure the effect persists at scale.

4. **Investigate variation** among treatment branches—some saw gains of 10+ points while others saw modest declines. Understanding what drives this variation could help maximize benefits during the full rollout.

5. **Consider cost-benefit analysis** if there are any additional costs associated with the new procedure (e.g., staff training time, equipment). The 4.4% service score improvement should be weighed against these costs.

---

*Analysis based on weekly service score data from 709 branches over 17 weeks (weeks 6-22). Statistical model includes branch fixed effects and controls for staffing and visit levels, with standard errors clustered by branch.*
