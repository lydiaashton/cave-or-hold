# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

## **Impact of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure on Service Scores**

### **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new rotation and shelving procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **1.94 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and represents approximately a **4% improvement** relative to the average branch score of 50. The benefit appears to grow over time, suggesting branches may need a few weeks to fully realize the gains.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. Study Design & Data**
- Analyzed 709 branches over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22 of the year)
- 425 branches adopted the new procedure starting week 15; 284 branches continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Used a difference-in-differences approach to isolate the effect of the new procedure

**2. Main Result**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Estimated treatment effect** | **+1.94 service score points** |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 (highly significant) |
| 95% Confidence interval | +1.40 to +2.48 points |
| Relative improvement | ~4% above baseline |

**3. Effect Grows Over Time**
The benefit of the new procedure increased as branches gained experience:
- **Weeks 15–17 (early):** +1.51 points
- **Weeks 18–22 (later):** +2.21 points

This pattern suggests a learning curve—branches may need 2–3 weeks to fully implement and benefit from the new procedure.

**4. Validity Checks**
- **Parallel trends assumption:** Supported for the 4 weeks immediately before the switch (weeks 10–13), which is the most relevant period for causal inference
- **Robustness:** The result holds when controlling for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume
- **Control group trend:** Service scores at control branches declined slightly after week 14 (−0.47 points), making the treatment effect even more notable

### **Technical Notes**

- Analysis uses cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch) to account for repeated observations
- The difference-in-differences estimator compares the before-after change in treatment branches to the before-after change in control branches
- Pre-trend analysis shows convergence between groups in the weeks immediately before the switch, supporting the validity of the causal interpretation
