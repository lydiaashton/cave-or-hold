# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and rotation procedure **improved service scores by approximately 2.1 points** (about 4% increase). This effect is statistically significant and represents a meaningful improvement in branch performance. We recommend proceeding with a full rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **60 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in the treatment group to changes in the control group

### **Results**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Treatment Effect** | +2.13 service score points |
| **95% Confidence Interval** | [0.98, 3.28] |
| **Statistical Significance** | p < 0.001 |
| **Percentage Improvement** | ~4.2% from baseline |

### **What This Means**
- Branches that switched to the new procedure saw their service scores **increase by about 2 points** relative to branches that kept the old procedure
- The control group's scores actually **declined slightly** after week 15 (−1.6 points), while the treatment group's scores **increased** (+0.5 points)
- The effect is **statistically robust**—we can be confident this is not due to random chance

### **Validity Check: Parallel Trends**
A key assumption of this analysis is that treatment and control branches were on similar trajectories before the switch. Testing confirms:
- Pre-switch trends were **not significantly different** between groups (p = 0.11)
- This supports the validity of our causal interpretation
