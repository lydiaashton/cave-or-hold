# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.** The new stock rotation and shelving procedure is associated with a statistically significant improvement in service scores of approximately **1.8 points** (about **3.6%** above baseline). The effect is robust across multiple analytical approaches and appears to strengthen over time.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measurable Improvement in Service Scores
- Branches using the new procedure showed service scores **1.8 points higher** than would be expected based on trends in branches using the old procedure
- This represents a **3.6% improvement** over the baseline average score of ~50
- The result is **highly statistically significant** (p < 0.001), meaning it is very unlikely to be due to random chance

### 2. Effect Strengthens Over Time
The improvement grew consistently in the weeks following implementation:

| Weeks Since Switch | Treatment Advantage |
|-------------------|---------------------|
| Week 1 (Week 15)  | +1.65 points |
| Week 4 (Week 18)  | +2.36 points |
| Week 8 (Week 22)  | +3.62 points |

This pattern suggests the benefits of the new procedure accumulate as staff become more familiar with it.

### 3. Broad-Based Benefits
- **67% of treatment branches** experienced positive effects
- **60% of branches** saw improvements greater than 1 point
- The effect holds even after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### 4. Important Caveat
The analysis identified that treatment branches were already on a slightly more positive trajectory before the switch. This means the **true effect may be somewhat smaller than 1.8 points**. However, the continued widening of the gap after the switch (particularly the acceleration from week 19 onward) strongly suggests a genuine treatment effect beyond pre-existing trends.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all 709 branches

2. **Implement phased deployment** over 4-6 weeks to allow for training and support

3. **Continue monitoring** service scores for all branches post-rollout to:
   - Confirm the effect generalizes to all branches
   - Identify any branches that may need additional support
   - Track whether the effect continues to strengthen over time

4. **Document best practices** from the highest-performing treatment branches to share during rollout training

5. **Re-evaluate in 6 months** with a full year of data to assess long-term sustainability of the improvement

---

## **Technical Notes**

- **Data:** 12,053 weekly observations from 709 branches (weeks 6-22)
- **Treatment group:** 425 branches that adopted the new procedure in week 15
- **Control group:** 284 branches that continued with the old procedure
- **Method:** Difference-in-differences regression with branch-level clustering for robust standard errors
- **Effect size:** 1.80 points (95% CI: 1.26 to 2.36)
