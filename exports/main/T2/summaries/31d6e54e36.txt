# **Analysis of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure is associated with a **2.9-point improvement** in service scores (approximately 5-6% of the average score). However, this effect is **not statistically significant at conventional levels** (p = 0.10), meaning we cannot confidently rule out that the observed improvement occurred by chance. The evidence suggests a **modest positive effect**, but additional data would strengthen the case for a system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in treatment branches to changes in control branches

### **Results**

| Group | Before Switch (Weeks 6–14) | After Switch (Weeks 15–22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------------|---------------------------|--------|
| Treatment (New Procedure) | 48.65 | 50.45 | +1.80 |
| Control (Old Procedure) | 53.61 | 52.54 | −1.07 |

**Difference-in-Differences Estimate: +2.87 points**

- **95% Confidence Interval:** −0.54 to +6.28 points
- **p-value:** 0.10 (marginally significant)

### **Interpretation**
- Treatment branches improved by 1.8 points after switching
- Control branches declined by 1.1 points over the same period
- The **net effect** of the new procedure is estimated at **+2.9 points**
- The confidence interval includes zero, indicating **uncertainty** about whether the true effect is positive, negative, or null

### **Additional Observations**
- **Pre-trends were roughly parallel** before the switch (p = 0.11), supporting the validity of the analysis
- **Heterogeneous effects:** 11 of 20 treatment branches improved; 9 declined. Effects ranged from −8.1 to +10.3 points across branches
- The control group's slight decline may reflect seasonal factors or other external influences affecting all branches

## **Next Steps**

1. **Continue monitoring** for 8–12 more weeks to increase statistical power and narrow confidence intervals

2. **Investigate branch-level variation** to identify which branches benefit most from the new procedure (e.g., by size, staffing, or visitor volume)

3. **Consider a phased rollout** rather than immediate system-wide adoption:
   - Roll out to additional branches while continuing to track outcomes
   - Use results to refine implementation guidance

4. **Gather qualitative feedback** from branch staff about the new procedure's practical impacts on workflow and patron experience

5. **Revisit the decision** after collecting 10–15 more weeks of data, when the effect estimate should be more precise

---

## **Recommendation**

The data provide **suggestive but not conclusive** evidence that the new procedure improves service scores. Given the modest effect size and statistical uncertainty, we recommend **continued monitoring with a cautious, phased expansion** rather than an immediate system-wide rollout. This approach balances the potential benefits against the risk of implementing a change that may not deliver meaningful improvements across all branches.
