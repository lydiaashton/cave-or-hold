# **Analysis of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, roll out the new procedure.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure showed a statistically significant improvement in service scores. The new procedure increased service scores by an average of **2.1 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.6 to 2.7 points), representing a **4% improvement** over baseline. The effect is highly reliable (p < 0.001) and appears to strengthen over time.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect
- **425 branches** switched to the new procedure; **284 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Using a difference-in-differences analysis (comparing changes in switched branches to changes in non-switched branches), the new procedure produced a **2.13-point increase** in service scores
- This effect is **statistically significant** with high confidence—the true effect is very likely between 1.6 and 2.7 points

### 2. Effect Grows Over Time
The benefit of the new procedure was not immediate but increased as branches gained experience:
| Period | Weeks | Estimated Effect |
|--------|-------|------------------|
| Early post-switch | 15-18 | +1.5 points |
| Late post-switch | 19-22 | +2.8 points |

This pattern suggests staff may need time to fully implement the new procedure, or the benefits accumulate as the new system matures.

### 3. Context and Magnitude
- Average service score across all branches: ~50 points
- The 2.1-point improvement represents approximately **0.22 standard deviations**—a meaningful, noticeable improvement in service quality
- For comparison, staffing and visitor volume also affect scores, but the procedure change had an independent effect beyond these factors

### 4. Important Caveat
The treatment branches had slightly higher service scores than control branches even before the switch (about 0.9 points higher on average). However, the gap widened substantially after the switch (to about 3.0 points), and the statistical analysis accounts for these pre-existing differences. The week-by-week data shows the post-switch gap consistently exceeded any pre-switch gap, supporting the conclusion that the procedure change drove the improvement.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all 284 branches still using the old method

2. **Plan for implementation support:**
   - Since effects grow over time, provide training and support during the first 4-8 weeks after switch
   - Consider pairing newly-switched branches with experienced "mentor" branches

3. **Monitor outcomes:**
   - Track service scores for 8+ weeks after each branch switches to capture the full benefit
   - Watch for branches that don't show improvement—they may need additional support

4. **Document the procedure:**
   - Capture what the early-adopter branches learned during their transition
   - Create a rollout playbook to help remaining branches maximize benefits

---

**Analysis prepared from:** 12,053 weekly observations across 709 branches, weeks 6-22  
**Method:** Difference-in-differences regression with branch-level clustering and controls for staffing and visitor volume
