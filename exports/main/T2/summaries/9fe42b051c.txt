# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and rotation procedure is associated with a **modest improvement in service scores** (approximately +2 points, or 4% above baseline). However, this improvement is **not statistically significant** at conventional levels. The evidence suggests the new procedure may help, but we cannot rule out the possibility that the observed improvement occurred by chance. **We recommend a cautious, phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure at week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in switched branches against changes in non-switched branches

### **Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 50.3 | 52.0 | +1.7 |
| Control branches | 52.6 | 52.4 | -0.3 |
| **Estimated Effect** | | | **+2.0 points** |

- **Point estimate:** +2.0 to +2.2 points improvement in service scores
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.09–0.13 (not significant at the 0.05 threshold)
- **95% confidence interval:** -0.4 to +4.9 points
- **Interpretation:** The true effect could range from a small decrease to a meaningful improvement of up to 5 points

### **Supporting Evidence**
- **Parallel trends validated:** Before the switch, both groups showed similar trends over time (p = 0.69), supporting the validity of our comparison
- **Staffing matters:** Higher staffing levels are significantly associated with better service scores (p = 0.02), suggesting operational capacity influences outcomes
- **No negative effects:** There is no evidence the new procedure harmed service quality

---

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended Actions**

1. **Continue monitoring for 8–12 more weeks**
   - Extend data collection to increase statistical power
   - With more post-switch observations, we could detect smaller effects with greater confidence

2. **Conduct a phased rollout**
   - Implement the new procedure in 5–10 additional branches
   - Use these branches as a second-wave treatment group to validate findings
   - Maintain 5–10 branches as ongoing controls

3. **Investigate implementation quality**
   - Survey branch staff on procedure adoption fidelity
   - Variation in how branches implemented the change may explain mixed results

4. **Consider cost-benefit analysis**
   - If the new procedure requires additional staff time or resources, weigh the ~2-point improvement against implementation costs
   - A 4% improvement may be worthwhile if costs are minimal

5. **Set a decision threshold**
   - Recommend full rollout if future data shows: (a) effect remains positive, and (b) p-value drops below 0.05, or (c) the lower bound of the 95% CI exceeds 0

## **Technical Notes**

- Analysis controls for branch-specific characteristics and weekly staffing/visitation patterns
- Standard errors are clustered by branch to account for repeated observations
- Service score baseline: ~50 points (higher is better)
