# Evaluation of New Shelving and Rotation Procedure

## Bottom Line

The new procedure is associated with a **2.4-point improvement** in service scores (approximately 5% relative to the average score of 47). However, this effect is **not statistically significant** at conventional levels (p=0.10), meaning we cannot confidently rule out that the observed improvement occurred by chance. The 95% confidence interval ranges from -0.5 to +5.3 points, indicating the true effect could be slightly negative or moderately positive.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with caution. The evidence suggests a modest positive effect, but additional data collection is needed before committing to a system-wide rollout.

---

## Key Findings

### Study Design
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Switch occurred at **week 15**
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in switched branches against changes in non-switched branches

### Results

| Group | Pre-Switch Average | Post-Switch Average | Change |
|-------|-------------------|---------------------|--------|
| Switched Branches | 45.2 | 49.4 | +4.2 |
| Non-Switched Branches | 46.6 | 48.6 | +2.0 |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | | | **+2.4** |

### Statistical Assessment
- **Estimated effect:** +2.40 service score points
- **95% Confidence Interval:** -0.49 to +5.29 points
- **P-value:** 0.103 (not significant at the 0.05 threshold)
- **Parallel trends assumption:** Supported—no significant difference in pre-switch trends between groups (p=0.74)

### Additional Insights
- **Staffing matters:** Higher staffing index is significantly associated with better service scores (p=0.026)
- **Branch variation:** There is substantial variation in baseline service levels across branches, which the analysis controls for
- **Post-switch volatility:** Service scores show week-to-week fluctuation in both groups after the switch, suggesting external factors may influence scores

---

## Next Steps

1. **Extend the observation period.** Eight weeks of post-switch data provides limited statistical power. Collecting 8–12 additional weeks would narrow the confidence interval and provide a clearer answer.

2. **Monitor implementation fidelity.** Ensure switched branches are consistently applying the new procedure. Variation in implementation could dilute the measured effect.

3. **Consider a phased rollout.** Given the suggestive (but not definitive) positive effect, the cooperative could expand the procedure to additional branches while continuing to track outcomes. This would increase sample size and statistical power.

4. **Investigate heterogeneity.** Analyze whether the procedure works better in certain types of branches (e.g., by size, location, or baseline performance) to target rollout more effectively.

5. **Track secondary outcomes.** Consider whether the new procedure affects operational costs, staff time, or patron satisfaction beyond the service score metric.

---

## Technical Notes

- Analysis controls for branch fixed effects, staffing levels, and visit volumes
- Standard errors are clustered at the branch level to account for within-branch correlation
- The service score has an average of ~50 across all branches; higher scores indicate better service
