# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact Analysis**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new shelving and rotation procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw their service scores increase by approximately **2.1 points** (about 4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and consistent across most branches. We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Measurable Improvement in Service Scores**

| Group | Before Switch (Weeks 6-14) | After Switch (Weeks 15-22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------------|---------------------------|--------|
| New Procedure Branches (90 branches) | 50.11 | 52.35 | **+2.23** |
| Old Procedure Branches (60 branches) | 50.61 | 50.73 | +0.12 |

**Net effect of the new procedure: +2.11 points** (difference-in-differences estimate)

### **2. Statistical Confidence**

- The improvement is **statistically significant** (p < 0.01)
- 95% confidence interval: **+0.92 to +3.38 points**
- The analysis controlled for staffing levels and visitor volume, which did not explain the improvement

### **3. Broad-Based Benefits**

- **76% of switched branches** saw their service scores improve after adopting the new procedure
- Only **53% of control branches** improved during the same period (likely due to normal variation)
- The effect represents about **0.23 standard deviations**—a meaningful improvement in service quality

### **4. Valid Comparison**

- Before the switch (weeks 6-14), both groups followed nearly identical trends (correlation of 0.99)
- This "parallel trends" pattern confirms that the improvement is attributable to the procedure change, not pre-existing differences between branches

### **5. Study Design**

- **150 branches** total: 90 adopted the new procedure, 60 kept the old
- **17 weeks** of data (weeks 6-22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- **2,550 observations** (one per branch per week)
