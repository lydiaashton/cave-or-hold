# **Library Cooperative Procedure Change: Service Score Impact Analysis**

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure shows a **positive but statistically uncertain effect** on service scores. Switched branches improved by an estimated **1.9 points** on the service score index compared to branches that kept the old procedure. However, this estimate is not statistically significant at conventional levels (p=0.15), meaning we cannot confidently rule out that the observed improvement was due to chance. The data suggests the new procedure likely does no harm and may provide modest benefits, but the study lacks sufficient power to detect small effects with confidence.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **34 branches** participated: 20 adopted the new procedure, 14 continued with the old procedure
- Data covers **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Service scores average around 50 points (higher is better)

### Results Summary

| Metric | Switched Branches | Control Branches |
|--------|-------------------|------------------|
| Average score before switch | 47.8 | 52.4 |
| Average score after switch | 49.1 | 51.9 |
| Change over time | **+1.3** | **-0.5** |

**Difference-in-Differences Estimate: +1.85 points**

### Statistical Confidence
- **95% Confidence Interval**: -0.7 to +4.4 points
- **P-value**: 0.15 (not statistically significant at the 0.05 level)
- The study could reliably detect effects of about **4 points or larger**; smaller effects cannot be distinguished from random variation with this sample size

### Additional Observations
- Switched branches started with lower baseline scores (about 4.6 points below control branches), but this gap was stable before the switch
- After the switch, the gap narrowed to about 2.8 points, consistent with a positive treatment effect
- Staffing levels and visit volumes showed minimal correlation with service scores and did not meaningfully change the results

## **Next Steps**

### Recommended Actions

1. **Proceed with cautious rollout**: The point estimate suggests improvement and there is no evidence of harm. Given the operational benefits the new procedure may offer beyond service scores, a phased rollout is reasonable.

2. **Continue monitoring**: Track service scores for at least 8–12 more weeks as additional branches adopt the procedure. More data will narrow the confidence interval and provide greater certainty.

3. **Consider complementary metrics**: Service scores are one measure of branch performance. Evaluate the new procedure against other outcomes such as:
   - Staff time efficiency
   - Patron satisfaction surveys
   - Material availability/fill rates
   - Operational costs

4. **Plan a follow-up evaluation**: Once more branches have switched, conduct a updated analysis with 6–12 months of data to confirm the effect size with greater precision.

### If Full Rollout Proceeds
- Document implementation carefully to ensure consistent adoption across branches
- Establish a baseline measurement period for each branch before switching
- Set a review date (e.g., 6 months post-rollout) to assess whether expected benefits materialize
