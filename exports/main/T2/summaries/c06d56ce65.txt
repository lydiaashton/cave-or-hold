# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure shows **positive but statistically uncertain** effects on service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure improved by approximately **2.4 points** (about 5%) relative to branches that kept the old procedure. However, this result is only marginally significant (p ≈ 0.08–0.10), meaning we cannot rule out that the observed improvement could be due to random variation. The evidence is **suggestive but not conclusive**.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **34 branches** tracked over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure at week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in the treatment group against the control group

### Results

| Group | Before Switch (Weeks 6–14) | After Switch (Weeks 15–22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------------|---------------------------|--------|
| Old Procedure (Control) | 48.3 | 47.6 | -0.7 |
| New Procedure (Treatment) | 47.8 | 49.5 | +1.7 |

- **Estimated treatment effect: +2.4 points** on the service score index
- **95% confidence interval: approximately -0.5 to +5.3 points**
- **Statistical significance: p ≈ 0.08–0.10** (marginally significant, not meeting the conventional 0.05 threshold)

### Supporting Evidence
- **Parallel trends assumption holds**: Before the switch, both groups showed similar trends (no significant pre-existing differences in trajectory, p = 0.67)
- **Effect is consistent**: The treatment group outperformed the control group in 6 of 8 post-switch weeks
- **Effect size is meaningful**: A 2.4-point improvement represents roughly 5% of the baseline service score

### Additional Insights
- Staffing levels are positively associated with service scores (branches with higher staffing_index performed better)
- The effect appears strongest in weeks 16–20, with some fading in weeks 21–22

---

## **Next Steps**

### Recommended Actions

1. **Proceed with cautious rollout** — The evidence points in a positive direction, and the potential benefit (5% improvement) is operationally meaningful. However, acknowledge the uncertainty.

2. **Extend the evaluation period** — Collect 8–12 more weeks of data to:
   - Increase statistical power and precision
   - Determine if the effect persists or fades over time
   - Reduce the confidence interval around the estimate

3. **Monitor implementation fidelity** — Ensure all branches are applying the new procedure consistently; variation in implementation could be masking true effects.

4. **Consider a phased rollout** — Implement the new procedure at remaining branches in stages, continuing to track service scores to confirm the effect holds at scale.

5. **Investigate mechanisms** — Analyze whether the improvement is driven by specific aspects of the new procedure (e.g., faster restocking, better organization) to optimize implementation.
