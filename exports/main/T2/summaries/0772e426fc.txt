# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.0 points** (a 4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and consistent across the post-switch period. We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. **Measurable Improvement in Service Scores**
- Switched branches improved by **+2.0 points** on the service score index (95% confidence interval: 1.3 to 2.8 points)
- The effect is highly statistically significant (p < 0.001)
- This represents approximately a **4% improvement** relative to baseline scores

### 2. **Strong Evidence of Causal Impact**
The analysis uses a rigorous difference-in-differences approach:
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure (treatment group)
- **60 branches** kept the old procedure (control group)
- Data spans **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15

**Before the switch** (weeks 6–14), treatment branches actually scored slightly *lower* than control branches (average difference: -1.0 points). **After the switch** (weeks 15–22), treatment branches scored *higher* than control branches (average difference: +1.1 points). This reversal strongly supports that the procedure change caused the improvement.

### 3. **Effect Is Stable Over Time**
- Early post-switch period (weeks 15–18): +2.05 points
- Late post-switch period (weeks 19–22): +2.05 points

The consistent effect suggests branches adapted quickly and the benefits are sustained, not just a temporary boost.

### 4. **Results Are Robust**
- The effect holds when controlling for staffing levels and visit volumes
- The effect holds when excluding week 15 (potential adjustment period)
- Branch-level and week-level factors are accounted for in the analysis

## **Next Steps**

2. **Monitor implementation** during the first 4–8 weeks at each new branch to ensure proper adoption and capture any branch-specific challenges

## **Technical Note**

The analysis controls for branch-specific characteristics (some branches consistently score higher or lower), week-specific factors (overall trends affecting all branches), and operational variables (staffing and visit levels). The parallel trends assumption is supported by the pre-switch data, giving confidence in the causal interpretation of the results.
