# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.1 points** (about 4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and robust across multiple analytical approaches. We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Study Design
- **150 branches** tracked over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **60 branches** continued with the old procedure
- This natural experiment allows us to isolate the effect of the new procedure using a difference-in-differences approach

### 2. Service Score Impact
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 50.29 | 53.50 | +3.20 |
| Non-switched branches | 49.48 | 50.55 | +1.08 |
| **Net effect (DiD)** | | | **+2.12** |

- The **2.1-point improvement** is the estimated causal effect of the new procedure
- 95% confidence interval: **0.9 to 3.4 points**
- Statistically significant (p < 0.001)

### 3. Validity Checks
- **Parallel trends confirmed**: Before week 15, switched and non-switched branches showed no systematic difference in their score trends (p = 0.75)
- **Results hold** after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume
- **Results hold** when accounting for branch-specific characteristics

### 4. Context
- Average service score across all branches: **51 points**
- The 2.1-point gain represents a **4% improvement** relative to baseline
- Both groups saw some improvement over time (possibly seasonal), but switched branches improved substantially more

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all 150 branches

2. **Monitor implementation**: Track service scores for 8–12 weeks after full rollout to confirm the effect persists at scale

3. **Document the procedure**: Capture the specific practices that distinguish the new procedure for training purposes

4. **Consider cost-benefit**: If the new procedure requires additional resources, weigh the 4% service improvement against implementation costs

5. **Share learnings**: Branches that already switched successfully may serve as mentors during the rollout
