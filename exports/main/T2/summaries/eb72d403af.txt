# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

Branches that adopted the new procedure maintained their service scores while non-switched branches experienced a notable decline. The new procedure is associated with an estimated **1.9-point improvement** in service scores relative to the control group. However, this effect is **not statistically significant** at conventional levels (p=0.14), meaning we cannot rule out that the observed difference occurred by chance. The evidence suggests a **positive but uncertain benefit** from the new procedure.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Service Score Changes**

| Group | Before Switch (Weeks 6-14) | After Switch (Weeks 15-22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------------|---------------------------|--------|
| **Switched Branches** (20 branches) | 51.4 | 51.3 | -0.1 |
| **Non-Switched Branches** (14 branches) | 49.3 | 47.2 | -2.1 |

### **Statistical Analysis**

- **Estimated Treatment Effect**: +1.93 points (95% CI: -0.6 to +4.5)
- **Statistical Significance**: p = 0.14 (not significant at p < 0.05)
- **Effect Size**: Small-to-moderate (Cohen's d ≈ 0.20)
- **Parallel Trends Check**: ✓ Passed — no evidence of different pre-treatment trends between groups

### **What This Means**

1. **Control branches declined**: Non-switched branches saw service scores drop by about 2 points after week 15, which was statistically significant.

2. **Treatment branches held steady**: Switched branches maintained their service scores with virtually no change (-0.1 points).

3. **The gap widened**: The difference between switched and non-switched branches grew from 2.1 points before the switch to 4.1 points after.

4. **Uncertainty remains**: While the point estimate is positive, the confidence interval includes zero, so we cannot be confident the effect is real rather than random variation.

---

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended Actions**

1. **Proceed with cautious expansion**: The direction of the effect is favorable and there is no evidence of harm. Consider rolling out the procedure to additional branches while continuing to monitor outcomes.

2. **Extend the evaluation period**: Collect more post-switch data to increase statistical power. With only 8 weeks of post-treatment data, the study may be underpowered to detect a real effect of this magnitude.

3. **Monitor implementation fidelity**: Ensure branches adopting the new procedure are implementing it consistently. Variation in execution could dilute the measured effect.

4. **Track secondary outcomes**: Continue monitoring staffing_index and visits_index to ensure the new procedure doesn't create unintended operational burdens.

5. **Set a decision checkpoint**: Re-evaluate after 12-16 additional weeks of data. If the effect remains positive but non-significant, consider whether the operational benefits justify broader adoption even without statistical certainty.

- The switch occurred at week 15
- Results are robust to controlling for staffing_index and visits_index
