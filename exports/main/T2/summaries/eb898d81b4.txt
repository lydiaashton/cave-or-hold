# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new procedure saw their service scores increase by approximately **2 points** (about 4%) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and consistent across the post-switch period. The evidence supports rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Positive Impact on Service Scores
- The new procedure increased service scores by **+1.99 points** on average (95% confidence interval: +0.76 to +3.21)
- This represents roughly a **4% improvement** relative to the baseline average score of 54 points
- The result is statistically significant (p = 0.002), meaning there's less than a 1% chance this finding is due to random variation

### 2. Effect Was Consistent Over Time
- Early post-switch period (weeks 15-18): +2.12 points
- Later post-switch period (weeks 19-22): +1.85 points
- The benefit did not fade over the 8-week observation period

### 3. Valid Comparison Between Groups
- **90 branches** switched to the new procedure; **60 branches** kept the old procedure
- Before the switch, both groups showed parallel trends in service scores (no pre-existing differences in trajectory)
- The result holds even after controlling for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume

### 4. Context on Overall Trends
- Service scores declined slightly across *all* branches after week 14 (about -3 points), likely due to seasonal or system-wide factors
- Switched branches experienced a much smaller decline (-1.1 points) compared to control branches (-3.1 points)
- The new procedure appears to have **protected branches from the broader downturn**

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all 60 remaining branches

2. **Monitor implementation** over the next 8-12 weeks to confirm the effect persists at scale

3. **Document best practices** from the 90 switched branches to support training during rollout

4. **Consider cost-benefit analysis** if the new procedure requires additional resources—the ~4% service score improvement should be weighed against any incremental costs

---

*Analysis based on weekly service scores from 150 branches over 17 weeks (weeks 6-22). The switch occurred at week 15. Statistical model uses difference-in-differences estimation with branch fixed effects and standard errors clustered at the branch level.*
