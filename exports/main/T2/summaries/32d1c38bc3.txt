# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, roll out the new procedure.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw a statistically significant improvement in service scores compared to branches that kept the old procedure. The estimated effect is an increase of **1.8 points** on the service score index (p < 0.01), representing approximately a **3.6% improvement** relative to the average branch score.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Study Design
- **150 branches** total: 90 adopted the new procedure (treatment group), 60 kept the old procedure (control group)
- Data covers **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22), with the switch implemented in week 15
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in service scores between the two groups before and after the switch

### 2. Main Result
| Group | Pre-Switch Avg | Post-Switch Avg | Change |
|-------|---------------|-----------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 52.21 | 53.37 | +1.16 |
| Non-switched branches | 48.73 | 47.98 | −0.75 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+1.91** |

The regression-adjusted estimate (controlling for staffing levels, visit volumes, and branch-specific factors) is **+1.82 points** with a 95% confidence interval of **[0.75, 2.88]**.

### 3. Validity Checks
- **Parallel trends confirmed**: Before the switch (weeks 6–14), both groups followed similar trends in service scores (interaction term not statistically significant, p = 0.36)
- **Robust to controls**: Results hold when accounting for weekly staffing and visit fluctuations
- **Statistically significant**: p-value = 0.0008 (well below conventional thresholds)

### 4. Effect Size Context
- Average service score across all branches: ~51 points
- The 1.8-point improvement equals about **18% of one standard deviation** in service scores
- This is a meaningful operational improvement for a process change

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to remaining branches
2. **Monitor implementation** to ensure fidelity to the new protocol
3. **Track service scores** post-rollout to confirm the effect persists at scale
4. **Investigate weekly variation**: Some weeks showed larger effects than others; understanding this could help optimize timing or complementary practices
5. **Consider cost-benefit analysis**: If the new procedure has implementation costs, weigh them against the 3.6% service improvement
