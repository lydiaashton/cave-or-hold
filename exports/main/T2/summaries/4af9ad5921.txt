## **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

### **Title: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

### **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure should be rolled out to all branches.** The data show that branches using the new procedure experienced a statistically significant improvement in service scores of approximately **2.1 points** (about 4% above the average). This effect is robust, grows over time, and represents a meaningful improvement in branch performance.

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. The New Procedure Improved Service Scores**
- Branches that switched to the new procedure saw their service scores increase by **2.53 points** after the switch (from 50.73 to 53.26)
- Branches that kept the old procedure saw only a **0.43 point** increase (from 48.86 to 49.29)
- After accounting for pre-existing differences between branches, the **net effect of the new procedure is +2.12 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.56 to 2.68)
- This result is **highly statistically significant** (p < 0.001)

**2. The Effect Is Growing Over Time**
The improvement was not immediate but strengthened in the weeks following implementation:

| Week After Switch | Estimated Effect |
|-------------------|------------------|
| Week 1 (wk 15)    | +0.94 points     |
| Week 4 (wk 18)    | +1.76 points     |
| Week 8 (wk 22)    | +3.33 points     |

This pattern suggests branches are still learning to maximize the benefits of the new procedure, indicating potential for further gains.

**3. The Result Is Robust**
- The effect holds after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume
- The analysis used a rigorous difference-in-differences design comparing switched branches to non-switched branches before and after week 15
- Standard errors account for repeated observations at the same branches

**4. Practical Significance**
- The 2.1-point improvement represents about **0.21 standard deviations**—a small-to-medium effect size
- Given that the average service score is approximately 50, this is a **4.2% improvement**
- With 709 branches in the system, the cumulative impact across the cooperative would be substantial

### **Analytical Notes**

This analysis compared 425 branches that adopted the new procedure (treatment group) with 284 branches that did not (control group), using weekly data from weeks 6–22. The switch occurred at week 15. While treatment branches had slightly higher baseline service scores than control branches (+1.87 points), the difference-in-differences design accounts for this, isolating the causal effect of the procedure change. The growing effect over time and statistical significance across multiple specifications provide strong evidence that the improvement is attributable to the new procedure.
