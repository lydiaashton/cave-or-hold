# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure is associated with a **modest improvement in service scores** (+1.6 points, or ~3% above baseline), but the evidence is **not statistically conclusive**. The confidence interval ranges from a slight decline (-0.5 points) to a meaningful gain (+3.6 points). We recommend a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **34 branches** tracked over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure
- The switch occurred at **week 15**, creating a natural experiment (difference-in-differences design)

### Results

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Old Procedure (14 branches) | 48.05 | 47.49 | -0.56 |
| New Procedure (20 branches) | 49.19 | 50.20 | +1.00 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+1.56** |

### Statistical Assessment
- **Estimated effect:** +1.56 service score points
- **95% Confidence Interval:** -0.50 to +3.62 points
- **p-value:** 0.13 (not statistically significant at conventional levels)
- **Parallel trends assumption:** Supported—both groups showed similar trends before the switch

### What This Means
1. **Positive direction:** Branches using the new procedure improved relative to those that did not
2. **Uncertain magnitude:** The true effect could range from negligible to moderately beneficial
3. **Not definitive:** With only 34 branches and 8 weeks of post-switch data, we lack the statistical power to rule out chance variation

### Branch-Level Variation
- Among switched branches: **11 of 20 (55%)** showed score improvements
- Among control branches: **5 of 14 (36%)** showed score improvements
- This pattern supports a positive effect, but individual branch experiences varied considerably

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Continue the pilot for 8–12 more weeks** to increase statistical power and observe whether the effect stabilizes or grows

2. **Investigate implementation quality**—interview branch managers at high-performing switched branches to identify best practices that could be standardized

3. **Monitor secondary metrics**—analyze whether the procedure affects staffing efficiency or visit patterns (data already collected but not yet analyzed for this purpose)

4. **Plan a phased rollout** if results remain positive:
   - Start with 10–15 additional branches
   - Provide enhanced training and support
   - Set clear success metrics (e.g., +2 points sustained over 12 weeks)

5. **Establish a decision threshold** for full rollout (e.g., statistically significant improvement with lower bound of CI above 0)

---

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with caution.** The data suggest the new procedure is beneficial, but the evidence is not yet strong enough to justify the cost and disruption of an immediate system-wide rollout. Extend the evaluation period and gather qualitative insights from successful implementations before making a final decision.
