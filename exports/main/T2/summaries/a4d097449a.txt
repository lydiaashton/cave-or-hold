# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Service Score Impact Analysis**

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and rotation procedure shows a **positive but statistically uncertain** effect on service scores. Branches that switched improved by an estimated **1.75 points more** than branches that kept the old procedure. However, this result is not statistically significant at conventional levels (p ≈ 0.10), meaning we cannot rule out that the observed difference occurred by chance. **We recommend a cautious, phased rollout with continued monitoring rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect Estimate
- **Difference-in-Differences estimate: +1.75 points** on the service score index
- This represents approximately a **3.5% improvement** relative to baseline scores (~50)
- 95% confidence interval: **-0.3 to +3.8 points** (includes zero)
- Statistical significance: **p ≈ 0.10** (not significant at the 0.05 level)

### 2. What Happened to Each Group
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 50.60 | 54.96 | +4.36 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 50.26 | 56.37 | +6.10 |

**Important:** Both groups improved after week 15. The treatment group improved more, but part of the gain reflects broader trends affecting all branches.

### 3. Study Design Validity
- **Parallel trends assumption holds:** No evidence that treatment and control branches were on different trajectories before the switch (pre-trend test: p = 0.87)
- **34 branches total:** 20 switched to new procedure, 14 kept old procedure
- **17 weeks of data:** Weeks 6–14 (before), Weeks 15–22 (after)
- Analysis controls for branch-specific characteristics and weekly staffing/visits variations

### 4. Post-Switch Pattern
The treatment group showed consistently higher scores than control in most post-switch weeks, though the gap varied week-to-week (ranging from -0.8 to +4.0 points).

---

## **Next Steps**

### Recommended Actions

1. **Do not proceed with immediate system-wide rollout.** The evidence is suggestive but not conclusive.

2. **Extend the observation period.** Continue collecting data for 8–12 more weeks to:
   - Increase statistical power
   - Determine if the effect stabilizes or grows over time
   - Rule out short-term novelty effects

3. **Consider a stepped-wedge rollout.** Randomly assign remaining control branches to switch in batches over the coming months. This would:
   - Provide more robust causal evidence
   - Allow all branches to eventually benefit if the procedure proves effective
   - Enable better estimation of the true effect size

4. **Investigate heterogeneity.** Analyze whether the procedure works better for certain branch types (e.g., by size, location, or baseline performance).

5. **Monitor implementation fidelity.** Ensure branches that switched are actually following the new procedure as intended.

### Decision Framework for the Board

| If the Board's Priority Is... | Recommendation |
|-------------------------------|----------------|
| Minimizing risk of ineffective change | Wait for more evidence |
| Capturing potential upside quickly | Proceed with phased rollout to half of remaining branches |
| Maximizing evidence quality | Extend observation period + stepped-wedge design |
