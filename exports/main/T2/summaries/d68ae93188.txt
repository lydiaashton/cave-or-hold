# **Analysis of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **1.77 points** (approximately **3.6%** improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. ## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measurable Improvement in Service Quality
- Treatment branches (90 branches using the new procedure) improved their service scores by an average of **+1.44 points** from before to after the switch.
- Control branches (60 branches using the old procedure) saw a slight **decline of -0.34 points** over the same period.
- The **net effect of the new procedure is +1.77 points** on the service score index.

### 2. Statistical Confidence
- The improvement is **highly unlikely to be due to chance** (p-value = 0.003).
- We are 95% confident the true effect lies between **+0.58 and +2.96 points**.
- The analysis used a rigorous "difference-in-differences" approach that accounts for underlying trends and branch-specific factors.

### 3. Valid Comparison Between Groups
- Before the switch, both groups of branches were following **similar trends** in service scores (statistical test confirms no pre-existing divergence, p = 0.25).
- This validates that the post-switch difference is attributable to the procedure change, not pre-existing differences.

### 4. Improvement Is Not Due to Other Factors
- The new procedure did **not** significantly change staffing levels or visitor volumes at branches.
- This suggests the service score improvement reflects genuine operational improvements from the new procedure itself, not external factors.

### 5. Consistent Post-Switch Pattern
- The gap between treatment and control branches widened consistently after week 15 (when the switch occurred).
- Weekly differences ranged from +2.2 to +5.0 points in the post-switch period, compared to an average of +1.3 points before.
