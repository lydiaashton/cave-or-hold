# **Board Summary: New Shelving & Rotation Procedure Impact Analysis**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, roll out the new procedure to all branches.** The data shows the new procedure increased service scores by approximately **2.4 points** (about **5% improvement**), and this result is statistically significant and robust across multiple analytical approaches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Study Design
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure (treatment group)
- **60 branches** kept the old procedure (control group)
- Switch occurred at **Week 15**; analysis covers Weeks 6–22 (17 weeks total)
- Used difference-in-differences methodology to isolate the procedure's effect

### 2. Impact on Service Scores

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Treatment (switched) | 49.96 | 51.40 | +1.44 |
| Control (no switch) | 51.28 | 50.26 | −1.01 |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | | | **+2.45** |

The treatment group improved while the control group slightly declined, yielding a **net positive effect of 2.45 points**.

### 3. Statistical Confidence
- **95% Confidence Interval**: 1.7 to 3.2 points
- **p-value**: < 0.001 (highly statistically significant)
- Result holds across all model specifications, including controls for branch-specific factors, week effects, staffing levels, and visitor volume

### 4. Validity Checks Passed
- **Parallel pre-trends**: Both groups tracked closely before the switch (Weeks 6–14)
- **Balanced covariates**: Staffing and visitor patterns were similar between groups
- **Robust estimates**: Effect size consistent whether using simple or complex statistical models

### 5. Practical Significance
- Service scores average ~50 across the system; a 2.4-point gain represents meaningful improvement
- No evidence of negative side effects on staffing efficiency or visitor experience
