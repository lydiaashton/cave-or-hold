# **Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure Evaluation**

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure shows **positive but not statistically definitive** evidence of improving service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure maintained their service levels while control branches experienced a notable decline. The estimated improvement is **2.3 points (approximately 4%)** on the service score index. We recommend a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Treatment Effect Estimate**
- Branches using the new procedure showed service scores **2.3 points higher** than expected, compared to branches that kept the old procedure
- This represents approximately a **4% improvement** relative to the baseline average score of 54
- The effect is **not statistically significant at the 5% level** (p=0.11), meaning we cannot rule out that the observed difference occurred by chance
- The 95% confidence interval ranges from **-0.4 to +5.1 points**, indicating the true effect could be slightly negative or as high as 5 points

### **2. What Actually Happened**
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches (20 branches) | 52.7 | 52.3 | -0.5 |
| Control branches (14 branches) | 53.9 | 51.2 | **-2.8** |

The key insight: **All branches experienced downward pressure on service scores after week 14**, but branches with the new procedure were largely protected from this decline. The new procedure appears to have **preserved service quality** rather than dramatically improving it.

### **3. Pre-Trend Validation**
- Before the switch (weeks 6-14), there was **no systematic difference** in trends between the two groups
- This supports the validity of our comparison—any post-switch differences are likely due to the procedure change, not pre-existing differences

### **4. Weekly Pattern After Switch**
- In 5 of 8 weeks after implementation, switched branches outperformed control branches
- The largest gains occurred in weeks 15 (+2.5), 19 (+3.0), and 21 (+2.8)
- Some weeks showed no advantage, suggesting the benefit may vary by circumstances

### **5. Study Limitations**
- **Sample size**: With 34 branches and 17 weeks, the study has limited statistical power
- The minimum detectable effect with this design is approximately **4 points**—our observed effect of 2.3 points is below this threshold
- **8 weeks of post-switch data** may be insufficient to capture the full effect if benefits accumulate over time

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended Actions**

1. **Continue the pilot for 8-12 more weeks** to gather additional data and improve statistical precision

2. **Expand to additional branches** in a second phase, using a staggered rollout design to strengthen causal inference

3. **Investigate implementation quality**—interview branch managers at high-performing switched branches to identify best practices

4. **Monitor cost implications**—the board should request analysis of whether the new procedure affects staffing costs or operational efficiency

5. **Set a decision threshold**—if the effect remains around 2+ points with narrower confidence intervals after additional data collection, proceed with system-wide rollout

| Scenario | Recommendation |
