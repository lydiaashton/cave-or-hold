# **Board Summary: Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure did not improve service scores.** While branches that adopted the new procedure performed slightly better relative to those that kept the old procedure (+1.8 points), this difference is **not statistically significant** and the effect is inconsistent over time. We cannot recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches based on this evidence.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** switched to the new procedure in week 15; **14 branches** kept the old procedure
- Service scores average around 50 (higher is better)

### **What the Data Shows**

| Group | Pre-Switch Avg (Weeks 6–14) | Post-Switch Avg (Weeks 15–22) | Change |
|-------|----------------------------|------------------------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 52.31 | 49.93 | **−2.38** |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 50.48 | 49.91 | **−0.57** |

**Difference-in-Differences Estimate: +1.81 points** (p = 0.13, not statistically significant)

### **Critical Interpretation**

The apparent "benefit" of the new procedure is **not due to improvement**—it's because the treatment group's scores **declined less** than the control group's. Neither group actually improved after week 15.

### **Week-by-Week Pattern (Event Study)**

The effect varies considerably week-to-week:
- **Weeks 17 & 19**: Treatment branches scored **significantly worse** (−4.4 and −4.4 points)
- **Week 21**: Treatment branches scored **significantly better** (+3.9 points)
- **Other weeks**: No significant difference

This inconsistency suggests the new procedure does not produce reliable benefits.

### **Parallel Trends Assumption: Supported**

Pre-switch trends were similar between groups (no statistically significant differential trends, p = 0.90), validating our comparison approach.

### **Control Variables**

Staffing levels matter: each unit increase in the staffing index is associated with about +1.0 point in service score (p < 0.01). Visit volume had no significant effect.

---

## **Recommendations / Next Steps**

1. **Do not roll out the new procedure system-wide** based on current evidence. The procedure shows no clear benefit and may cause harm in some weeks.

2. **Investigate why service scores declined overall** after week 15. Both groups experienced drops, suggesting an external factor (seasonal, staffing changes, etc.) affected all branches.

3. **Consider a longer evaluation period.** Eight post-switch weeks may be insufficient to capture the full effect of a procedural change. Staff may need more time to adapt.

4. **Gather qualitative feedback** from branches using the new procedure. Understanding implementation challenges could explain the inconsistent results.

5. **If proceeding with expansion**, consider a phased rollout with continued monitoring to detect any emerging patterns.
