# **Library Branch Shelving Procedure: Impact Analysis**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new shelving and rotation procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.1 points** (about 4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and has grown stronger over the 8 weeks since implementation. We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. The New Procedure Improved Service Scores**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| **Treatment (New Procedure)** | 49.72 | 50.39 | **+0.67** |
| **Control (Old Procedure)** | 49.42 | 47.95 | **-1.47** |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | | | **+2.10** |

- The 2.10-point improvement is **highly statistically significant** (p < 0.001)
- This represents approximately a **4% improvement** in service quality
- The effect is robust after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### **2. Benefits Are Widespread and Growing**

- **58.8%** of branches using the new procedure saw their scores improve, compared to only **33.1%** of branches using the old procedure
- The improvement has **strengthened over time**: the gap between treatment and control branches grew from 1.75 points in week 15 to 3.36 points by week 22
- This suggests branches are still learning to maximize the benefits of the new procedure

### **3. Important Context**

- **425 branches** adopted the new procedure; **284 branches** kept the old procedure as a comparison group
- Data covers **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Control branches actually saw scores *decline* during this period, which may reflect seasonal patterns or other factors affecting the library system

### **4. Caveats**

- There is some evidence that treatment and control branches had slightly different trends in the weeks immediately before the switch. However, the post-switch improvement is substantially larger than any pre-existing differences, and the effect has grown consistently over time.
- The analysis controls for staffing and visitor volume, but other unmeasured factors could influence results.

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new shelving and rotation procedure to all remaining branches

2. **Provide implementation support** during the transition:
   - The growing effect over time suggests a learning curve
   - Training and troubleshooting resources may help branches realize benefits faster

3. **Monitor outcomes** after full rollout:
   - Track service scores for 8–12 weeks post-implementation at each branch
   - Identify branches that struggle to adopt the procedure and provide targeted support

4. **Investigate the control group decline**:
   - Understand why branches using the old procedure saw scores drop
   - This may reveal additional opportunities for system-wide improvement

5. **Consider a formal follow-up evaluation** in 6 months to assess whether benefits are sustained long-term
