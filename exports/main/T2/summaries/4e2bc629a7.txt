# **MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new shelving and stock rotation procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2 points (about 4%)** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and consistent across multiple analytical approaches. **We recommend proceeding with a full rollout**, while continuing to monitor outcomes.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect
- **Estimated impact:** +1.98 service score points (95% confidence interval: 1.43 to 2.52)
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001 (highly significant)
- **Relative improvement:** Approximately 4% increase over the baseline average score of 52

### 2. Study Design
- **709 branches** total: 425 adopted the new procedure, 284 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data:** Weeks 6–22, with the switch occurring at week 15
- **Method:** Difference-in-differences analysis comparing changes in treatment vs. control branches, controlling for week-to-week variation and staffing/visit patterns

### 3. Before and After Comparison

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 50.95 | 51.23 | +0.28 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 51.49 | 53.75 | +2.26 |
| **Net effect** | | | **+1.98** |

### 4. Robustness
The estimated effect remains stable when:
- Controlling for weekly patterns (some weeks naturally have higher/lower scores)
- Adjusting for staffing levels and visitor volume
- Using different statistical specifications

---

## **Important Caveats**

1. **Pre-existing trends:** The gap between treatment and control branches was already widening slightly before the switch. Our analysis accounts for this, but it introduces some uncertainty about the exact magnitude of the effect.

2. **Modest effect size:** While statistically significant, the 2-point improvement is modest in absolute terms. The new procedure should be viewed as one component of service quality improvement, not a transformational change.

3. **Short-term data:** We have only 8 weeks of post-switch data. Longer-term effects (positive or negative) remain unknown.

4. **Week-to-week volatility:** Service scores fluctuate considerably week to week for all branches, reflecting factors beyond the shelving procedure.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with rollout** to remaining 284 branches, with implementation support and training.

2. **Continue monitoring** service scores for at least 6–12 months post-rollout to confirm the effect persists and identify any longer-term patterns.

3. **Track implementation fidelity** to ensure branches are following the new procedure as designed.

4. **Consider complementary improvements** since the procedure alone produces modest gains. Investigate whether combining it with other interventions (staffing adjustments, layout changes) produces larger effects.

5. **Cost-benefit analysis:** Evaluate whether the operational costs of the new procedure are justified by the service score improvement.
