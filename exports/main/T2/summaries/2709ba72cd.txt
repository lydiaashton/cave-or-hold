# **Analysis of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

Contrary to expectations, the new stock rotation and shelving procedure **did change service scores**—and the change was **positive**. Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **1.8 points** (about 3.6% above the average) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is **statistically significant** (p < 0.01) and has been sustained over the eight weeks since implementation.

**Recommendation:** The evidence supports rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. The New Procedure Improved Service Scores
- **Treatment effect:** +1.8 points on the service score index (95% confidence interval: 0.8 to 2.9 points)
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.001 (highly unlikely to be due to chance)
- **Practical significance:** Represents about 18% of one standard deviation in service scores—a meaningful improvement

### 2. Analysis Method
I used a **difference-in-differences** approach, which compares:
- **Treatment group:** Branches that switched to the new procedure (weeks 6–22)
- **Control group:** Branches that kept the old procedure (weeks 6–22)
- **Before period:** Weeks 6–14 (before the switch)
- **After period:** Weeks 15–22 (after the switch)

This method isolates the effect of the new procedure by accounting for:
- Baseline differences between branches
- Overall time trends affecting all branches
- Other weekly factors (staffing and visit levels)

### 3. The Effect Is Sustained
The improvement did not fade over time. Service score differences between switched and non-switched branches remained stable at **4.5 to 6.4 points** throughout all eight weeks after implementation, suggesting the benefit is durable.

### 4. No Data Quality Concerns
- All 150 branches have complete data for all 17 weeks
- No missing values
- Pre-existing differences between groups were stable before the switch, supporting the validity of the comparison

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches.

2. **Monitor implementation** during the rollout to ensure consistent adoption and identify any branches that may need additional support.

3. **Continue tracking service scores** for at least 8–12 weeks post-rollout to confirm the effect persists at scale.

4. **Investigate mechanisms:** Consider studying *why* the new procedure improved scores (e.g., faster item location, better organization, staff efficiency) to reinforce best practices.

5. **Cost-benefit analysis:** If there are any costs associated with the new procedure (training, time, materials), compare them against the service score improvement to confirm net benefit.

---

*Analysis based on 2,550 weekly observations from 150 branches, weeks 6–22. Statistical models control for staffing levels, visit volumes, and branch-specific characteristics.*
