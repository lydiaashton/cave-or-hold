# **MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure is associated with a statistically significant improvement in service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw their service scores increase by approximately **1.9 points** (about 4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. We recommend proceeding with a full rollout, with continued monitoring to confirm the effect persists.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect
- **Estimated improvement:** +1.93 service score points (95% confidence interval: 1.39 to 2.48)
- **Statistical significance:** Highly significant (p < 0.001)
- **Relative improvement:** Approximately 3.75% above baseline scores

### 2. Study Design
- **709 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **425 branches** adopted the new procedure (treatment group)
- **284 branches** kept the old procedure (control group)
- Switch occurred at **week 15**
- Analysis uses difference-in-differences methodology with branch-level clustering for robust standard errors

### 3. Before and After Comparison

| Group | Pre-Switch Average | Post-Switch Average | Change |
|-------|-------------------|---------------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 51.24 | 50.40 | -0.84 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 51.59 | 52.70 | +1.11 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+1.94** |

### 4. Effect Over Time
The improvement was sustained throughout the post-switch period (weeks 15–22), with treatment branches consistently outperforming control branches by 2.0 to 2.7 points after the switch.

### 5. Important Caveat
There was a **pre-existing upward trend** in the treatment group before the switch. In the weeks leading up to week 15, treatment branches were already improving relative to control branches by about 0.36 points per week. This means:
- Some portion of the observed effect may reflect this pre-existing trend rather than the procedure change itself
- However, the post-switch gap (averaging 2.3 points) is notably larger than the pre-switch gap (averaging 0.4 points)
- The effect appears to have accelerated after the switch was implemented

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with full rollout** – The evidence supports expanding the new procedure to all branches, with an expected service score improvement of approximately 1.5–2.0 points per branch.

2. **Continue monitoring** – Track service scores for the next 8–12 weeks after full implementation to confirm the effect persists and does not fade over time.

3. **Investigate mechanisms** – The analysis controlled for staffing and visit levels, but further research could identify *why* the new procedure improves scores (e.g., faster book location, better organization, staff efficiency).

4. **Address pre-trend concern** – Consider a phased rollout with random assignment in future procedure changes to strengthen causal inference for subsequent evaluations.

5. **Cost-benefit analysis** – Compare the estimated service score gains against implementation costs (training, temporary disruption, materials) to confirm the procedure change delivers value.
