# **Library Cooperative Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new shelving and rotation procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.2 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents a **4.3% improvement** over the baseline average score of 51. The result is statistically significant and consistent across multiple analytical approaches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Treatment Effect**
- **DiD Estimate:** +2.21 points (95% CI: 1.65 to 2.76)
- **Statistical Significance:** p < 0.001
- **Effect Size:** 0.23 standard deviations (small-to-moderate practical significance)

### **2. What Happened to Each Group**
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Treatment (New Procedure) | 51.00 | 52.02 | **+1.02** |
| Control (Old Procedure) | 51.03 | 49.87 | **-1.16** |

The treatment group improved while the control group declined, resulting in a net difference of 2.2 points.

### **3. Branch-Level Results**
- **60.7%** of branches using the new procedure saw their scores improve
- Only **39.4%** of branches keeping the old procedure saw improvement
- The effect was consistent across all 8 post-switch weeks (weekly differences ranged from +0.9 to +2.8 points)

### **4. Robustness**
The finding holds under multiple specifications:
- Basic difference-in-differences: +2.19 points
- With staffing and visits controls: +2.21 points
- With branch fixed effects: +2.21 points

### **5. Important Context**
- **Sample:** 709 branches total (425 adopted new procedure, 284 kept old)
- **Study Period:** Weeks 6-22 (9 weeks before, 8 weeks after the switch at week 15)
- **Pre-period balance:** Both groups had nearly identical average scores before the switch (51.00 vs 51.03), supporting the validity of the comparison

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to remaining branches, given the clear positive effect on service quality.

2. **Monitor implementation** at newly converted branches to ensure the effect replicates at scale. Track service scores for at least 8 weeks post-conversion.

3. **Investigate the control group decline.** Service scores dropped ~1.2 points at branches keeping the old procedure. This may indicate:
   - Seasonal effects affecting all branches
   - The new procedure provides a protective benefit against external pressures
   - Understanding this could help optimize the rollout

4. **Consider cost-benefit analysis.** While the procedure improves scores by 2.2 points (4.3%), the board should weigh this against any additional staffing, training, or operational costs of the new procedure.

5. **Plan for ongoing evaluation.** Establish quarterly reviews of service scores to ensure the benefit persists over time and to identify any branches that may need additional support during transition.

## **Analytical Notes**

*Analysis used a difference-in-differences approach comparing branches that switched procedures to those that did not, before and after week 15. Standard errors are clustered by branch to account for repeated observations. The parallel trends assumption is supported by the near-identical pre-period means between groups, though some week-to-week variation exists in the pre-period.*
