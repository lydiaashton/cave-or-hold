# **MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure shows a **modest positive trend** in service scores (+2.0 points on average), but this improvement is **not statistically significant**. We cannot confidently conclude the new procedure improves service quality based on the current data. **We recommend collecting additional data before committing to a full system-wide rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **34 branches** participated in the evaluation (20 switched to the new procedure, 14 kept the old procedure)
- Data collected over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring in week 15
- Analysis used a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in service scores between switching and non-switching branches

### Results

| Metric | Finding |
|--------|---------|
| **Estimated Effect** | +2.0 points on service score index |
| **Statistical Significance** | Not significant (p = 0.12) |
| **95% Confidence Interval** | -0.5 to +4.5 points |
| **Effect Size** | Small (Cohen's d = 0.18) |

### What This Means
- The **point estimate suggests improvement**: Branches that switched saw service scores increase by about 2 points more than branches that didn't switch
- **However, uncertainty is high**: The confidence interval includes zero, meaning the true effect could be slightly negative, zero, or moderately positive (up to ~4.5 points)
- **No clear mechanism identified**: The new procedure did not significantly affect staffing levels or visit volumes, suggesting any benefit would come from other factors

### Important Context
- Branches that switched already had higher service scores than control branches before the change (about 6 points higher on average)
- Both groups showed similar week-to-week volatility before the switch, supporting the validity of the comparison
- Service scores naturally fluctuate considerably (standard deviation of ~10 points), making it harder to detect modest effects

---

## **Next Steps**

### Recommended Actions

1. **Extend the observation period** — Collect 8–12 more weeks of data to increase statistical power and determine whether the positive trend persists or strengthens

2. **Consider a phased rollout with evaluation** — If the board wishes to proceed, implement the new procedure at additional branches in stages, continuing to measure service scores against a control group

3. **Investigate qualitative feedback** — Survey branch staff and patrons about their experience with the new procedure; quantitative scores may miss important operational benefits

4. **Monitor cost implications** — Before full rollout, assess whether the new procedure requires additional resources (time, training, equipment) that might offset modest service improvements

### Decision Framework

| If the board prioritizes... | Recommendation |
|----------------------------|----------------|
| **Statistical certainty** | Wait for more data before deciding |
| **Potential upside** | Proceed with cautious, monitored rollout |
| **Cost minimization** | Maintain current mixed approach |

---

## **Technical Appendix**

Analysis used fixed-effects regression with branch and week controls, with standard errors clustered at the branch level to account for repeated observations. The model controlled for staffing and visit indices. All analyses conducted in Python using standard econometric methods.
