## **TITLE: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

### **BOTTOM LINE:**
The new procedure is associated with a modest improvement in service scores (+2.1 points on average), but this effect is **not statistically significant** at conventional levels (p=0.10). The evidence is suggestive but not conclusive that the new procedure improves service.

---

### **KEY FINDINGS:**

**1. Study Design:**
- 34 branches total
- 20 branches adopted the new procedure (treatment group)
- 14 branches kept the old procedure (control group)
- Data covers 17 weeks (weeks 6-22); procedure switch occurred at week 15
- 9 weeks of pre-switch data, 8 weeks of post-switch data

**2. Service Score Changes:**
- Treatment branches: 53.0 (pre) → 58.0 (post) = **+4.9 points**
- Control branches: 48.5 (pre) → 51.5 (post) = **+3.1 points**
- Both groups improved, but treatment branches improved slightly more

**3. Estimated Effect (Difference-in-Differences):**
- Point estimate: **+2.1 points**
- 95% Confidence Interval: **[-0.4, 4.6]**
- p-value: **0.10**
- Interpretation: The true effect could plausibly range from slightly negative to moderately positive; we cannot rule out no effect

**4. Validity Checks:**
- Parallel trends assumption: **SUPPORTED** (pre-trend test p=0.82)
- Treatment and control branches had similar trends before the switch
- Results are robust to controlling for staffing and visit levels

**5. Context:**
- Average service score across all branches is about 53
- A 2.1-point improvement represents about a **4.0% increase**

---

### **NEXT STEPS:**

**1. RECOMMENDATION:** Proceed with caution. The evidence suggests a positive effect but is not statistically definitive.

**2. OPTIONS:**
   - **a) Roll out to all branches with continued monitoring**
     - Pros: Potential service improvement, consistency across system
     - Cons: Implementation costs, effect may be smaller than estimated
   
   - **b) Extend the evaluation period**
     - Collect more post-switch data to increase statistical power
     - Current analysis has limited post-period observations (8 weeks)
   
   - **c) Pilot expansion to additional branches**
     - Randomly assign more branches to treatment
     - Gather more data before full rollout

**3. ADDITIONAL ANALYSIS RECOMMENDED:**
   - Examine effects on different branch types (size, location, etc.)
   - Assess implementation costs vs. benefits
   - Monitor for any unintended consequences
   - Consider qualitative feedback from branch staff and patrons
