# **Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure Evaluation**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new rotation and shelving procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **1.9 points** (approximately **3.7% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is both statistically significant and practically meaningful. We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Study Design & Data**
- **709 branches** participated in the evaluation (425 switched to new procedure, 284 kept old procedure)
- **17 weeks** of data collected (weeks 6–22 of the year)
- New procedure implemented at **week 15**
- Analysis used a **Difference-in-Differences** method, which isolates the effect of the procedure change by comparing treatment and control branches before and after the switch

### **2. Main Result: Positive Impact Confirmed**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect on service score | **+1.89 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | [1.34, 2.43] |
| Statistical significance | **p < 0.001** (highly significant) |
| Relative improvement | **3.7%** over baseline |

The effect is robust across multiple model specifications, including controls for staffing levels and branch visit volumes.

### **3. Effect Strengthens Over Time**
The event study analysis shows the treatment effect grew in the weeks following implementation:
- **Weeks 15–16:** +0.5 to +0.7 points
- **Weeks 17–20:** +1.2 to +1.6 points  
- **Weeks 21–22:** +2.4 to +2.9 points

This pattern suggests branches may need time to fully adapt to the new procedure, and benefits accumulate with experience.

### **4. Pre-Treatment Trends (Caveat)**
Statistical testing detected some divergence in trends between treatment and control groups before the switch. However, the event study shows a clear and sustained break at week 15, with all post-switch weeks showing positive effects. This supports the conclusion that the procedure change drove the improvement.

### **5. Practical Significance**
- The average branch scores about 50 on the service index; a 1.9-point gain represents meaningful improvement
- Post-switch, treatment branches averaged **52.8** vs. control branches at **50.4**—a **2.3-point gap**
- The effect is comparable to or larger than typical operational improvements seen in library systems
