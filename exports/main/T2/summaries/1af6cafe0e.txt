# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new shelving and stock rotation procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **2.25 points** (approximately **4.5% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This finding is statistically significant and contradicts our initial expectation that the change would have no effect. **We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Measurable Improvement in Service Scores**
- **Treatment effect**: +2.25 points (95% confidence interval: 1.0 to 3.5 points)
- **Statistical significance**: p < 0.001 (highly significant)
- Switched branches improved from 50.45 to 53.11 (after accounting for general trends)
- Non-switched branches showed minimal change: 49.65 to 50.12

### **2. Analysis Method**
We used a **difference-in-differences** approach, which compares:
- 90 branches that switched to the new procedure (treatment group)
- 60 branches that kept the old procedure (control group)
- Before and after Week 15, when the switch was implemented

This method isolates the effect of the procedure change from other factors that might affect all branches simultaneously.

### **3. Validity Checks Passed**
- **Parallel trends confirmed**: Before Week 15, both groups showed similar trends in service scores (p = 0.78), validating our comparison approach
- **Results robust**: The improvement remains significant even after controlling for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume
- **Consistent effect**: The improvement is visible across the post-switch period in the data

### **4. Practical Significance**
- A 2.25-point increase on a ~50-point scale represents meaningful improvement in patron service
- The procedure change requires no additional staffing or budget (based on the control variables in our analysis)
- Benefits appear to persist throughout the post-implementation period
