# **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure is associated with a **2.6-point improvement in service scores** (approximately 5% relative to the baseline of ~50). While this effect is not statistically significant at conventional levels (p=0.11), the direction is consistent with our expectations, and 65% of switched branches showed improvement. We **recommend proceeding with a phased rollout** while continuing to monitor results.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Service scores average ~50; higher scores indicate better service

### Results

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 46.9 | 49.4 | **+2.5** |
| Control branches | 44.9 | 44.9 | **0.0** |
| **Net effect** | | | **+2.6 points** |

### Statistical Analysis
- **Treatment effect:** +2.6 points (95% confidence interval: -0.6 to +5.7)
- **p-value:** 0.11 (suggestive but not definitive evidence)
- **Pre-trend check:** No significant difference in trends between groups before the switch, supporting the validity of the comparison

### Additional Observations
- **13 of 20 switched branches (65%)** improved after adopting the new procedure
- **7 of 14 control branches (50%)** improved—consistent with random variation
- The gap between switched and control branches widened from ~2 points before the switch to ~4.5 points after

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with cautious rollout** to remaining branches, prioritizing those with lower baseline scores
2. **Continue monitoring** service scores for at least 8–12 weeks post-implementation to confirm sustained effects
3. **Investigate heterogeneity**—7 switched branches did not improve; understand what differentiates them
4. **Consider complementary measures**—analyze whether staffing_index and visits_index also improved, which could indicate broader operational benefits
5. **Set a review checkpoint** in 3 months to reassess whether the effect has strengthened with more data

---

## **Caveats**

- The effect is **not statistically significant at the 5% level**, so we cannot rule out that the observed improvement is due to chance
- The study covers only **8 weeks post-implementation**; longer-term effects remain unknown
- External factors (seasonal patterns, staffing changes) could influence results, though the control group helps account for these
