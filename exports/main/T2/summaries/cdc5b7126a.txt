# **Library Cooperative Procedure Change: Impact Analysis**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new shelving and rotation procedure had a statistically significant positive effect on service scores.** Contrary to the expectation that it would make no difference, branches that adopted the new procedure maintained their service scores while branches using the old procedure experienced a notable decline. The evidence supports rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **150 branches** analyzed over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** switched to the new procedure at week 15; **60 branches** continued with the old procedure
- This natural experiment allows us to isolate the effect of the procedure change using a difference-in-differences approach

### **Service Score Results**

| Group | Before Switch (Weeks 6–14) | After Switch (Weeks 15–22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------------|---------------------------|--------|
| **New Procedure** (90 branches) | 51.24 | 51.19 | −0.05 |
| **Old Procedure** (60 branches) | 51.81 | 49.45 | −2.36 |

### **Statistical Evidence**
- **Treatment effect: +2.23 points** (95% confidence interval: 0.93 to 3.53)
- **p-value < 0.001** — highly statistically significant
- **Effect size:** 0.23 standard deviations, or 4.4% of the baseline average score
- Results are **robust** across multiple model specifications, including controls for staffing levels and visitor traffic, and accounting for branch-specific and week-specific factors

### **What This Means**
The control branches experienced a **2.4-point decline** in service scores after week 15, while branches with the new procedure **remained stable**. The new procedure appears to have **protected against a decline** in service quality rather than producing dramatic improvement. This suggests the old procedure may have become less effective over time, or that external factors affected service delivery—and the new procedure helped branches adapt.

### **Parallel Trends Validation**
Before the switch (weeks 6–14), both groups showed similar service score patterns with no systematic divergence, supporting the validity of the comparison.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.** The evidence indicates it helps maintain service quality, and there is no indication of negative effects.

2. **Investigate why control branches declined.** Understanding the cause of the 2.4-point drop in the control group could reveal additional opportunities for improvement (e.g., seasonal effects, staffing changes, or other operational factors).

3. **Monitor implementation.** As the procedure is rolled out more broadly, continue tracking service scores to ensure consistent results across all branches.

4. **Consider cost-benefit analysis.** If the new procedure requires additional resources (training, time, equipment), weigh those costs against the 2.2-point service score benefit to confirm the change is worthwhile.

---

## **Technical Notes**

Analysis used a difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch). Results were validated with branch fixed effects, week fixed effects, and controls for staffing and visitor traffic indices. All specifications produced consistent estimates of the treatment effect.
