# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

## **Subject: Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

### **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **2.1 points** (approximately 4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and consistent across the post-switch period. We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. Main Effect: +2.1 Point Improvement**
- The difference-in-differences analysis shows the new procedure increased service scores by **2.1 points** (95% CI: 0.9 to 3.4 points)
- P-value: 0.001 (highly statistically significant)
- The effect remains robust (+2.2 points) after controlling for weekly staffing and visit levels

**2. Effect Appeared Immediately and Persisted**
- Week 15 (first week after switch): +3.2 points (p<0.01)
- Weeks 16-18: Effects of +2.4 to +3.0 points (all statistically significant)
- Weeks 19-22: Effects of +1.3 to +1.8 points (some attenuation but still positive)
- The immediate jump suggests the procedure change had a direct, rapid impact

**3. Parallel Trends Validated**
- Before the switch (weeks 6-14), treatment and control branches followed similar trends
- Statistical test confirms no differential pre-trends (p=0.51)
- This supports the validity of our causal interpretation

**4. Sample Overview**
- 150 branches total: 90 switched to new procedure, 60 kept old procedure
- 17 weeks of data (weeks 6-22), with switch occurring at week 15
- 2,550 total branch-week observations

### **Next Steps**
