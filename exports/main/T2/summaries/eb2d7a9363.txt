# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new shelving and rotation procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw their service scores increase by approximately **2.2 points** relative to branches that kept the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant and represents a meaningful gain given that the average branch scores around 50.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. The New Procedure Works**
- **Treatment branches** (425 branches using the new procedure) improved from an average of 51.6 before the switch to 52.8 after.
- **Control branches** (284 branches keeping the old procedure) declined from 51.3 to 50.4 over the same period.
- **Net improvement attributable to the new procedure: +2.2 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.6 to 2.7 points).

### **2. Results Are Statistically Robust**
- The effect is highly statistically significant (p < 0.001), meaning it is extremely unlikely to be due to random chance.
- Results hold after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume, which also affect service scores.
- Results hold after accounting for inherent differences between individual branches.

### **3. Parallel Trends Check Out**
- Before the switch (weeks 6–14), treatment and control branches had similar service score trends, averaging only 0.3 points apart.
- After the switch (weeks 15–22), the gap widened to an average of 2.5 points in favor of treatment branches.
- This pattern supports the conclusion that the procedure change caused the improvement.

### **4. Effect Appears to Grow Over Time**
- The treatment effect was about 1.7 points in the first week after the switch.
- By week 22, the effect had grown to about 3.1 points.
- This suggests branches may still be realizing the full benefits as staff become more familiar with the new procedure.

### **5. Benefits Across Branch Types**
- The improvement appears consistent across branches with different baseline performance levels.
- Both lower-performing and higher-performing branches benefited from the change.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Recommend rolling out the new procedure to all remaining branches.** The evidence strongly supports system-wide adoption.

2. **Monitor implementation closely during rollout.** The growing effect over time suggests there may be a learning curve. Provide adequate training and support to help branches realize the full benefit quickly.

3. **Track service scores post-rollout.** Continue collecting weekly data to confirm the effect persists at scale and to identify any branches that may need additional support.

4. **Consider investigating the mechanism.** While we know the procedure works, understanding *why* (e.g., faster book retrieval, better organization, improved staff efficiency) could help optimize the procedure further.

5. **Evaluate cost-benefit.** If the new procedure requires additional resources (training time, labor hours, etc.), compare those costs against the value of a 2+ point service score improvement to confirm the change is economically justified.

---

**Analysis based on:** 709 branches, 17 weeks of data (weeks 6–22), 12,053 observations. Switch occurred at week 15.
