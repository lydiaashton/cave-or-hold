# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**
### *Impact on Service Scores: Weeks 6-22*

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

The new procedure shows a **positive but statistically uncertain effect** on service scores. Branches using the new procedure improved by approximately **2 points** (about 4%) more than branches keeping the old procedure, but this difference is not statistically significant at conventional levels. The data does not provide strong enough evidence to conclude the new procedure definitively improves service. **We recommend a cautious, phased rollout with continued monitoring rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.**

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### Study Design
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6-22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure at week 15; **14 branches** kept the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses difference-in-differences methodology to isolate the procedure's effect

### Results Summary

| Group | Pre-Switch Avg (Weeks 6-14) | Post-Switch Avg (Weeks 15-22) | Change |
|-------|----------------------------|-------------------------------|--------|
| Control (Old Procedure) | 49.7 | 50.9 | +1.2 |
| Treatment (New Procedure) | 53.6 | 56.9 | +3.3 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+2.1** |

### Statistical Assessment
- **Estimated effect: +2.1 points** on the service score index
- **95% Confidence Interval: -0.4 to +4.6 points**
- **p-value: 0.10** (not statistically significant at the 0.05 level)
- The confidence interval includes zero, meaning we cannot rule out no effect

### Important Context
1. **Parallel trends validated**: Before the switch, both groups showed similar trends over time, supporting the validity of our comparison.
2. **Baseline differences**: Branches that chose to switch had systematically higher service scores throughout the study (about 4 points higher on average), even before the procedure change.
3. **Both groups improved**: Service scores increased for all branches after week 15, suggesting broader factors (seasonal, operational) may have influenced results.
4. **Control variables matter**: Staffing levels and visit volumes are significantly associated with service scores, confirming these are relevant operational factors.

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

### Recommended Actions

1. **Do NOT proceed with immediate system-wide rollout**. The evidence is insufficient to justify the cost and disruption of changing all 34 branches.

2. **Extend the evaluation period**. Eight weeks of post-switch data may be too short to detect a stable effect. Continue monitoring for at least 8-12 more weeks.

3. **Consider a randomized rollout**. The current self-selection of branches into the new procedure introduces potential bias. For a future evaluation, randomly assign branches to treatment and control groups.

4. **Investigate heterogeneity**. Analyze whether the procedure works better for certain types of branches (e.g., by size, location, or baseline performance).

5. **Monitor implementation fidelity**. Ensure branches adopting the new procedure are following it consistently; variation in implementation could dilute measured effects.

6. **Track secondary outcomes**. Consider measuring additional metrics beyond service scores (e.g., patron satisfaction, circulation, operational costs) to capture the full impact.

### Decision Framework
- If future data shows a **statistically significant effect** (p < 0.05) with a confidence interval entirely above zero, proceed with rollout.
- If the effect remains **positive but uncertain**, consider a cost-benefit analysis weighing the modest potential gains against implementation costs.
- If the effect **diminishes or turns negative**, discontinue the new procedure.
