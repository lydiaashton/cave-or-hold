## **Memorandum to the Board**

### **Title: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

### **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.0 points** (a 4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and has been consistent across all eight weeks since implementation. We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. Measurable Improvement in Service Scores**
- Switched branches improved by 2.8 points after adopting the new procedure (from 49.5 to 52.3)
- Control branches (old procedure) improved by only 0.8 points during the same period (from 48.5 to 49.2)
- **Net effect of the new procedure: +2.0 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.5 to 2.6 points)

**2. Statistical Significance**
- The improvement is highly statistically significant (p < 0.001)
- The result holds even after controlling for staffing levels and customer visit volumes
- The effect has been consistent across all 8 weeks since implementation (weeks 15-22)

**3. Study Design**
- Analysis covers 709 branches over 17 weeks (weeks 6-22)
- 425 branches adopted the new procedure; 284 branches kept the old procedure as a control group
- The switch was implemented in week 15, allowing 9 weeks of "before" data and 8 weeks of "after" data

**4. Important Context**
- Switched branches had slightly higher service scores even before the change (about 1 point higher on average)
- The gap between switched and control branches widened notably after the procedure change
- This suggests the branches selected for early adoption may have been higher-performing, but the additional improvement after the switch is attributable to the new procedure

---

### **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all remaining branches

2. **Monitor implementation** to ensure the procedure is being followed consistently across all branches

3. **Track service scores** for the next 8-12 weeks after full rollout to confirm the effect persists at scale

4. **Investigate the mechanism** – consider gathering qualitative feedback from branch staff to understand why the new procedure improves service (e.g., faster restocking, better organization, reduced errors)

5. **Consider cost-benefit analysis** – if the new procedure requires additional training or resources, weigh those costs against the 4% service score improvement

---

*Analysis based on weekly service score data from 709 branches, weeks 6-22. Statistical analysis used difference-in-differences regression with branch-level clustered standard errors.*
