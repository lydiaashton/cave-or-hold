# **Analysis of New Branch Procedure on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Contrary to the expectation that there would be no difference, branches that adopted the new procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **2.4 points** (approximately 5% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This result is statistically significant and robust across multiple analytical approaches.

**Recommendation:** The evidence supports rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measurable Improvement in Service Quality
- Branches using the new procedure improved their service scores by **2.38 points** relative to branches using the old procedure
- 95% confidence interval: **1.6 to 3.2 points**
- This represents approximately a **5% improvement** over the baseline average score of 48.4

### 2. Statistically Significant Result
- The improvement is highly statistically significant (p < 0.001)
- The result holds even after controlling for:
  - Individual branch characteristics (some branches consistently score higher or lower)
  - Week-to-week variations (seasonal or system-wide effects)
  - Clustering of observations within branches over time

### 3. Parallel Trends Validated
- Before the switch (weeks 6-14), both groups followed similar patterns
- The treatment group consistently scored about 2-3 points lower than the control group, but this gap was stable
- After the switch (weeks 15-22), the treatment group closed this gap and slightly exceeded the control group's performance

### 4. Effect Isolated to Service Quality
- No significant changes were detected in staffing levels or visit volumes
- This suggests the improvement reflects genuine service quality gains rather than changes in resources or demand

### 5. Study Design
- **150 branches** total: 90 adopted the new procedure, 60 kept the old
- **17 weeks** of data (weeks 6-22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- **2,550 observations** (one per branch per week)

## **Technical Note**

This analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, which compares the change in outcomes for branches that adopted the new procedure against the change for branches that did not. This method isolates the effect of the procedure change from other factors that might affect all branches simultaneously (such as seasonal patterns or system-wide initiatives).
