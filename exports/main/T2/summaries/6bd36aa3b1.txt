# **MEMORANDUM**

## **Title: Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

### **Bottom Line**

The new procedure is associated with a **2.4-point improvement in service scores** (approximately 5% relative to baseline). While this effect is positive and consistent across the post-switch period, it is **marginally statistically significant** (p ≈ 0.10). We **recommend proceeding with a phased rollout** while continuing to monitor outcomes.

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. Study Design**
- 34 branches tracked over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- 20 branches adopted the new procedure at week 15; 14 branches continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in switched branches against non-switched branches

**2. Service Score Results**

| Period | Switched Branches | Non-Switched Branches | Difference |
|--------|-------------------|----------------------|------------|
| **Before** (weeks 6–14) | 49.21 | 49.38 | -0.18 |
| **After** (weeks 15–22) | 51.24 | 48.97 | +2.27 |
| **Change** | +2.03 | -0.42 | **+2.45** |

**3. Statistical Evidence**
- **Treatment effect:** +2.45 points (95% CI: -0.5 to +5.4)
- **P-value:** ~0.10 (marginally significant)
- The confidence interval includes zero, meaning we cannot rule out no effect, but the point estimate is consistently positive

**4. Parallel Trends Check**
- Before the switch, switched and non-switched branches had nearly identical average scores (difference of only 0.18 points)
- Weekly differences before week 15 fluctuated around zero with no systematic pattern
- After week 15, switched branches outperformed non-switched branches in **every single week**

**5. Additional Insights**
- Staffing levels have a small positive association with service scores
- Visit volume does not appear to affect service scores
- Non-switched branches saw a slight decline in scores after week 15 (-0.42 points), while switched branches improved (+2.03 points)

---

### **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with cautious rollout** — The evidence suggests a meaningful positive effect, though not at conventional significance levels. A phased expansion allows for continued learning.

2. **Extend the observation period** — Collect 8–12 more weeks of data to strengthen statistical power and confirm the effect persists.

3. **Monitor implementation fidelity** — Ensure branches adopting the new procedure are following it consistently; variation in implementation may be dampening the measured effect.

4. **Track secondary outcomes** — Monitor staffing efficiency and patron satisfaction alongside service scores to capture the full impact.

5. **Set a decision checkpoint** — Re-evaluate after one quarter of system-wide data. If the effect remains positive (even if marginally significant), the operational benefits likely justify full adoption.
