# **Board Summary: Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2 points** more than branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and grew stronger over the 8 weeks following implementation. We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Measurable Improvement in Service Scores**

| Group | Before Switch (Weeks 6-14) | After Switch (Weeks 15-22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------------|---------------------------|--------|
| **New Procedure** (425 branches) | 51.05 | 54.75 | +3.70 |
| **Old Procedure** (284 branches) | 50.17 | 51.92 | +1.75 |
| **Net Effect** | — | — | **+1.95** |

The **1.95-point net improvement** represents the estimated causal effect of the new procedure, after accounting for trends that affected all branches (such as seasonal patterns).

### **2. Statistical Confidence**

- The effect is **highly statistically significant** (p < 0.001)
- Analysis included **12,053 weekly observations** across **709 branches**
- Results control for staffing levels and visitor volume, which also affect service scores

### **3. Effect Strengthened Over Time**

The improvement was not immediate but grew over the post-implementation period:
- **Weeks 1-2 after switch:** Minimal detectable effect (branches likely still adjusting)
- **Weeks 3-8 after switch:** Clear, sustained improvement of **3 to 7 points** above the control group

This pattern suggests staff needed a brief learning period before the benefits of the new procedure were fully realized.

### **4. Both Groups Improved, But New Procedure Outperformed**

Service scores increased for *all* branches after week 14 (possibly due to seasonal factors or system-wide initiatives). However, branches using the new procedure improved **more than twice as much** as those using the old procedure.

---

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended Actions**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all 284 branches currently using the old method.

2. **Plan for a 2-3 week adjustment period** when implementing at new branches. The data shows benefits emerge after staff become familiar with the procedure.

3. **Provide training support** during the initial weeks of implementation to accelerate the learning curve and capture benefits sooner.

4. **Continue monitoring** service scores for the next quarter to confirm the effect persists long-term and to identify any branches that may need additional support.

### **Considerations**

- The average branch scores around 50 on the service index, so a **2-point improvement represents roughly a 4% gain** in service quality.
- The analysis controlled for staffing and visitor volume, suggesting the improvement comes from the procedure itself rather than external factors.
- No negative effects were observed; branches adopting the new procedure showed consistent gains without increased variability in scores.
