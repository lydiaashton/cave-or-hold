# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new shelving and rotation procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new procedure saw an average increase of **2.5 points** on the service score index (approximately **5% improvement**), while branches keeping the old procedure showed no meaningful change. This effect is statistically significant and consistent across most branches.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measurable Improvement in Service Quality
- **Treatment effect:** +2.50 points (95% confidence interval: 1.14 to 3.87)
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001 (highly significant)
- **Relative improvement:** ~5% increase from the baseline average of 50 points
- **Effect size:** Cohen's d = 0.59 (medium-to-large effect)

### 2. Strong Evidence of Causal Impact
The analysis used a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing branches that switched to the new procedure against those that did not, before and after the change:

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 50.13 | 50.14 | +0.01 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 50.81 | 53.39 | +2.58 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+2.57** |

### 3. Parallel Trends Validated
- Pre-switch trends (weeks 6-14) showed **no significant difference** between groups (p = 0.40)
- This supports the conclusion that the observed improvement is due to the procedure change, not pre-existing differences

### 4. Consistent Across Branches
- **70% of treatment branches** showed improvement after switching
- Only **55% of control branches** showed improvement during the same period
- The effect held after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### 5. Robust Results
The finding remained consistent across multiple analytical approaches:
- Basic comparison of averages
- Regression with cluster-robust standard errors
- Models controlling for staffing and visits
- Models with branch fixed effects
