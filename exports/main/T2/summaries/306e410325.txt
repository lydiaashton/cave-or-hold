# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, roll out the new procedure to all branches.** The new stock rotation and shelving procedure produced a statistically significant improvement in service scores of approximately **2.0 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.5 to 2.5 points). This represents about a **4% improvement** relative to the average branch score of 50.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **709 branches** total: 425 adopted the new procedure (treatment group), 284 kept the old procedure (control group)
- Data covers **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22); the switch occurred at week 15
- This is a natural experiment allowing us to isolate the effect of the new procedure

### Results
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 52.0 | 48.9 | **-3.1** |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 53.0 | 51.9 | **-1.1** |
| **Difference-in-Differences Effect** | | | **+2.0** ✓ |

- The **2.0-point improvement** is highly statistically significant (p < 0.001)
- The effect remains robust after controlling for staffing levels, visitor volume, and branch-specific characteristics
- At the branch level, **39% of treatment branches** saw their scores improve after the switch, compared to only **19% of control branches**

### Important Context
Both groups experienced a decline in service scores after week 15 (likely due to seasonal factors or other external influences). However, branches using the new procedure were **much more resilient**—their scores declined by only one-third as much as the control group. In practical terms, the new procedure helped branches maintain service quality during a challenging period.

### Validity Check
The analysis relies on the assumption that treatment and control branches would have followed similar trends absent the intervention. Pre-switch data shows parallel trends between the two groups, supporting the validity of this conclusion.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches
2. **Monitor implementation** to ensure the effect persists at scale
3. **Document best practices** from high-performing treatment branches to support training
4. **Continue tracking** service scores post-rollout to confirm sustained improvement
5. **Consider investigating** why scores declined system-wide after week 15, as addressing this could yield additional gains

*Analysis based on 12,053 weekly observations from 709 branches. Statistical models include clustered standard errors by branch and controls for staffing and visitor volume.*
