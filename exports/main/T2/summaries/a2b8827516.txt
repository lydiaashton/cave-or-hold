# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Contrary to initial expectations that the change would make no difference, branches that adopted the new procedure saw their service scores increase by approximately **2.0 points (about 4%)** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This result is statistically significant (p = 0.001) and robust to alternative specifications.

**Recommendation: Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **150 branches** total: 90 adopted the new procedure, 60 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22): 9 weeks before the switch, 8 weeks after
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in the treatment group to changes in the control group

### Results
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect on service score | **+2.04 points** |
| Statistical significance | **p = 0.001** (highly significant) |
| 95% Confidence Interval | **[0.81, 3.27]** |
| Relative improvement | **~4%** from baseline (~51.7) |

### Validity Checks
- **Parallel trends assumption supported**: Treatment and control groups showed similar trends before the switch (no statistically significant pre-trend differences)
- **Result is robust**: The effect persists when controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume
- **Consistent effect**: The improvement is observed across the treatment group

### Visual Summary
The trend plot (saved as `trend_plot.png`) shows that both groups had similar service score patterns before week 15. After the switch, the treatment group's scores diverged upward relative to the control group.
