# **Impact of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.1 points** (about 4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and robust across multiple analytical approaches. **We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measurable Improvement in Service Scores
- **Treatment branches** (90 branches that switched): Scores increased from 50.8 to 53.7 (+3.0 points)
- **Control branches** (60 branches that kept old procedure): Scores increased from 47.6 to 48.4 (+0.9 points)
- **Net effect of the new procedure**: +2.1 points (difference-in-differences estimate)

### 2. Statistical Confidence
- The 2.1-point improvement is **highly statistically significant** (p < 0.001)
- 95% confidence interval: **1.4 to 2.9 points**
- The result holds even after controlling for:
  - Branch-specific characteristics (some branches consistently score higher/lower)
  - Week-to-week variations (seasonal effects, holidays, etc.)
  - Staffing levels and visitor volume

### 3. Valid Comparison
- Pre-switch trends were **parallel** between treatment and control groups (p = 0.28), confirming the comparison is valid
- Treatment branches scored about 3 points higher than control branches before the switch, but both groups followed similar weekly patterns
- The improvement occurred specifically after week 15 when the switch was implemented

### 4. Effect Size Context
- Average service score across all branches: ~50 points
- The 2.1-point gain represents roughly **0.22 standard deviations**—a meaningful improvement in service quality
- Both staffing and visitor volume positively affect scores, but the procedure effect remains significant after accounting for these factors

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all 60 branches still using the old method

2. **Monitor implementation** during the rollout to ensure branches receive adequate training and support

3. **Track service scores** for the next 8-12 weeks post-rollout to confirm the effect persists at scale

4. **Document the new procedure** as a best practice and incorporate into branch operations training materials

5. **Consider cost-benefit analysis**—if the new procedure requires additional resources, the 4% service score improvement should be weighed against implementation costs

---

*Analysis based on 2,550 weekly observations from 150 branches over 17 weeks (weeks 6-22). The switch occurred at week 15. Difference-in-differences regression with branch and week fixed effects.*
