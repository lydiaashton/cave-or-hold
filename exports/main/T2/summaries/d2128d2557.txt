# **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure is associated with a **modest improvement in service scores** (+2.3 points on average), but the evidence is **not statistically conclusive**. The data suggest the change is likely beneficial or neutral, but we cannot rule out the possibility of no effect.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Treatment Effect Estimate**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect | **+2.3 points** |
| 95% Confidence interval | **-0.6 to +5.3 points** |
| Statistical significance | p = 0.12 (not significant at conventional levels) |

### **2. What the Data Show**
- **Control branches** (kept old procedure): Service scores remained essentially unchanged (46.5 → 46.6)
- **Treatment branches** (switched to new procedure): Service scores increased (50.4 → 52.8)
- The **difference-in-differences** estimate indicates a +2.3 point gain attributable to the new procedure

### **3. Study Design**
- **34 branches** total: 20 adopted the new procedure, 14 kept the old
- **17 weeks** of data (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Analysis controls for branch-specific characteristics, staffing levels, and visitor volume

### **4. Interpretation**
- The effect size is **modest** (about 0.24 standard deviations)
- The confidence interval suggests the true effect could range from slightly negative to moderately positive
- With the current sample size, we have **limited statistical power** to detect small effects

---

## **Next Steps**

### **Option A: Proceed with Cautious Rollout**
Given the positive (though uncertain) signal and no evidence of harm:
- Roll out the new procedure to remaining branches
- **Monitor service scores closely** for 8–12 weeks post-implementation
- Be prepared to adjust or reverse if scores decline

### **Option B: Gather More Evidence**
If the board prefers higher certainty:
- Continue the current pilot for an additional 8–12 weeks
- This would roughly **double the post-switch observations**, improving statistical power
- Re-analyze with the expanded dataset before making a systemwide decision

### **Recommended Approach**
I recommend **Option A with strong monitoring**. The new procedure shows a positive direction of effect with no signs of negative impact. A phased rollout with continued evaluation balances the potential benefits against the uncertainty in the evidence.
