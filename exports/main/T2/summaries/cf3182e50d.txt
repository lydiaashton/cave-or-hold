# **Memorandum to the Board**

## **Title: Impact of New Branch Procedure on Service Scores**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2 points** (about 4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and consistent across all post-switch weeks. We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Study Design and Scope
- **709 branches** participated in the study (425 switched to the new procedure; 284 kept the old procedure)
- Data covers **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in service scores between switching and non-switching branches before and after the procedure change

### 2. Main Result: Positive Treatment Effect
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 51.9 | 53.3 | **+1.3** |
| Non-switched branches | 51.2 | 50.5 | **-0.6** |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+1.95 points** |

- **Treatment effect: +1.95 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.4 to 2.5)
- **Statistical significance: p < 0.001** (highly significant)
- Effect represents approximately **4% improvement** relative to the baseline service score of ~52

### 3. Effect Persists and Strengthens Over Time
The improvement was not temporary. Weekly differences between switched and non-switched branches:
- Weeks 15–17: ~2.2 points higher for switched branches
- Weeks 18–22: ~2.8–3.2 points higher for switched branches

This suggests the benefit may grow as staff become more familiar with the new procedure.

### 4. Robustness Checks
- Results hold when controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume
- Effect is consistent across the 8-week post-switch period
- Some minor pre-treatment differences exist but do not undermine the main conclusion

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all 284 branches currently using the old procedure

2. **Monitor implementation** during rollout to ensure:
   - Staff receive adequate training on the new procedure
   - Service score improvements are replicated across all branches

3. **Track outcomes** for 8–12 weeks post-rollout to confirm:
   - The ~2-point improvement is sustained
   - No unintended consequences emerge

4. **Consider additional analysis** to understand:
   - Which aspects of the new procedure drive the improvement
   - Whether certain branch types benefit more than others
