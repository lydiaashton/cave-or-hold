# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

## **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

### **Bottom Line**

**The new stock rotation and shelving procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **1.8 points** (approximately **3.6% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and represents a meaningful improvement in branch service quality.

**Recommendation: Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

### **Key Findings**

**Study Design**
- 709 library branches tracked over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- 425 branches adopted the new procedure starting week 15; 284 branches kept the old procedure
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in service scores between the two groups before and after the switch

**Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Treatment (new procedure) | 50.75 | 53.49 | +2.74 |
| Control (old procedure) | 49.08 | 49.99 | +0.91 |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | | | **+1.82** |

**Statistical Significance**
- Treatment effect: **+1.82 points** (p < 0.001)
- 95% Confidence Interval: **[1.27, 2.37]**
- Result is robust to controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

**Practical Significance**
- The average branch service score is approximately 50 points
- A 1.8-point improvement represents a **3.6% increase** in service quality
- Given the scale of the cooperative (709 branches), this improvement would affect hundreds of thousands of patron interactions annually

**Parallel Trends Check**
- In the weeks immediately before the switch (weeks 9–14), treatment and control branches showed similar trends in service scores
- This supports the validity of the difference-in-differences estimate
