# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new procedure saw a statistically significant improvement of approximately **1.9 points** on the service score index compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents a meaningful gain given that the average branch scores around 50.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure (treatment group); **14 branches** kept the old procedure (control group)
- The switch occurred in **week 15**, providing 9 weeks of before-data and 8 weeks of after-data

### **Results**
| Group | Before Switch (Weeks 6–14) | After Switch (Weeks 15–22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------------|---------------------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 45.7 | 48.1 | +2.4 |
| Non-switched branches | 49.5 | 50.0 | +0.5 |
| **Net effect** | | | **+1.9** |

### **Statistical Confidence**
- The **1.9-point improvement** is statistically significant (p = 0.026)
- **95% confidence interval:** 0.2 to 3.5 points
- Results hold after controlling for branch-specific characteristics and week-to-week variation
- The parallel trends assumption is satisfied—both groups showed similar patterns before the switch

### **Context**
- The treatment group started with lower baseline scores (45.7 vs. 49.5), but the analysis accounts for this
- The improvement is equivalent to roughly **4% of the average service score**
- Other factors like staffing and visit levels did not explain the improvement

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** — The evidence supports expanding the new procedure to all branches.

2. **Monitor implementation** — Track service scores during rollout to ensure consistent results across all branches.

3. **Consider timing** — Since the effect was observed within 8 weeks, benefits should appear quickly after implementation.

4. **Document the procedure** — Ensure clear guidelines are available so all branches implement the new procedure consistently.

5. **Follow-up evaluation** — Conduct a follow-up analysis after 6–12 months to confirm sustained improvement and identify any branches that may need additional support.
