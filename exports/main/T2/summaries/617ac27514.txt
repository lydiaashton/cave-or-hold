# **Memorandum to the Board**

## **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

### **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure is associated with a **1.8-point improvement in service scores** (approximately 4% above baseline). However, this effect is **not statistically significant** when accounting for the fact that we have repeated observations from the same branches over time. The evidence suggests a positive direction of impact, but we cannot rule out that the observed improvement occurred by chance. **We recommend a cautious, phased rollout with continued monitoring rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.**

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. Study Design**
- Analyzed 34 library branches over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- 20 branches adopted the new procedure starting week 15; 14 branches continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Used a difference-in-differences approach to isolate the effect of the new procedure

**2. Impact on Service Scores**
- Branches that switched showed a **1.8-point increase** in service scores relative to branches that did not switch
- This represents approximately a **4% improvement** from the pre-switch baseline (49.3 points)
- The effect size is modest (0.16 standard deviations)

**3. Statistical Confidence**
- The estimated effect is **not statistically significant** at conventional levels (p = 0.14) when using appropriate statistical methods that account for repeated measurements from the same branches
- This means there is roughly a 14% probability that an effect this size could occur by random chance even if the new procedure had no real impact
- The 95% confidence interval ranges from **-0.5 to +4.1 points**, meaning the true effect could be slightly negative or moderately positive

**4. Validity of the Analysis**
- **Parallel trends assumption is satisfied**: Before the switch, treatment and control branches were trending similarly (p = 0.91), which supports the credibility of our comparison
- **Consistent direction**: 65% of branches that switched showed improvement in their service scores

**5. Context**
- Average service score across all branches is approximately 50 points
- Week-to-week variation is substantial, which makes detecting modest effects challenging
- The staffing and visit indices did not substantially change the results, suggesting the effect (or lack thereof) is not driven by these factors

---

### **Next Steps**

**1. Continue the Pilot (Recommended)**
- Maintain the current arrangement with some branches using the new procedure and others using the old
- Collect additional weeks of data to increase statistical power
- A longer observation period would help distinguish a real effect from random variation

**2. Investigate Heterogeneity**
- Examine whether certain types of branches (by size, location, or baseline performance) benefit more from the new procedure
- This could inform a targeted rather than universal rollout

**3. Consider Complementary Metrics**
- Service score is one measure; consider adding branch-level feedback from staff and patrons
- Operational metrics (e.g., time to shelve, inventory accuracy) may also be relevant

**4. Decision Framework**
- If the board is risk-tolerant and values potential upside: proceed with rollout, understanding the evidence is suggestive but not definitive
- If the board prefers evidence-based decisions with high confidence: extend the pilot period to 8–12 more weeks before making a final decision
