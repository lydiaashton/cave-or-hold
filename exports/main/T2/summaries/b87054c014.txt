# **Board Summary: New Shelving Procedure Impact Analysis**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new shelving and rotation procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and consistent across all post-implementation weeks. We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect Estimate
- **Main finding**: The new procedure increased service scores by **1.9 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.4 to 2.5 points), after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume.
- This represents approximately a **4% improvement** relative to the average branch score of 50.
- The effect is highly statistically significant (p < 0.001).

### 2. Consistency Over Time
The improvement was not a one-time spike—**every week after implementation** showed positive effects:
| Period | Average Effect |
|--------|----------------|
| Weeks 15-18 | +2.6 points |
| Weeks 19-22 | +3.0 points |

The effect appears to be **sustained and possibly growing** over time, suggesting branches may be getting better at implementing the new procedure.

### 3. Validity of the Analysis
Our analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing 425 branches that switched to 284 branches that didn't, before and after week 15. Key validity checks:

- **Parallel trends confirmed**: Before the switch (weeks 6-14), both groups showed similar week-to-week patterns in service scores. This supports the assumption that, without the intervention, both groups would have continued on similar trajectories.
- **Controls included**: Results account for weekly variations in staffing levels and visitor volume.
- **Robust standard errors**: All statistical tests use branch-level clustering to account for repeated observations.

### 4. Study Scope
- **709 branches** total (425 treatment, 284 control)
- **17 weeks** of data (weeks 6-22)
- **12,053 branch-week observations**
