# **Board Summary: New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure Evaluation**

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure is associated with a **2.3-point improvement in service scores** (approximately 5% relative to baseline). This effect is **marginally statistically significant** (p ≈ 0.09). While the results are promising, the evidence is not definitive at conventional confidence levels. We recommend a **cautious rollout with continued monitoring**.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure at week 15 (treatment group)
- **14 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- **578 total observations** analyzed using difference-in-differences methodology

### **Service Score Changes**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| **Treatment (switched)** | 44.9 | 48.7 | **+3.8** |
| **Control (no switch)** | 48.9 | 50.3 | **+1.4** |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | — | — | **+2.35** |

### **Statistical Results**
- **Effect size:** +2.35 points (5.2% improvement)
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.09 (significant at 10% level, not at 5% level)
- **95% confidence interval:** approximately -0.4 to +5.0 points
- **Parallel trends assumption:** Supported (pre-switch trends were statistically similar, p = 0.61)

### **Important Context**
1. **Treatment branches started lower:** Branches that switched had lower baseline scores (44.9 vs 48.9), suggesting they may have been selected for the new procedure because they were underperforming.
2. **Both groups improved:** Even control branches saw a 1.4-point gain, indicating possible system-wide factors (seasonal effects, general improvements) affecting all branches.
3. **Effect is meaningful but uncertain:** A 2.35-point gain on a ~50-point scale is practically meaningful, but statistical uncertainty means we cannot rule out smaller (or zero) effects.

---

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended Actions**

1. **Proceed with phased rollout** to remaining branches, prioritizing lower-performing branches that may benefit most.

2. **Extend the evaluation period** to 6–12 months to:
   - Increase statistical power
   - Assess whether effects persist or grow over time
   - Identify any seasonal patterns

3. **Monitor implementation fidelity** to ensure branches are correctly applying the new procedure.

4. **Track secondary metrics** (staffing index, visits index) to understand *how* the procedure improves scores and whether there are unintended consequences.

5. **Set a review checkpoint** at 3 months post-rollout to reassess whether the effect size justifies full implementation costs.

### **Questions for Further Investigation**
- Why were certain branches selected to switch first? (Potential selection bias)
- Are there branch characteristics that predict larger gains?
- What specific aspects of the new procedure drive the improvement?

---

## **Conclusion**

The new procedure shows **promising but not conclusive** evidence of improving service scores. The 5% improvement is operationally meaningful for library service quality. Given the positive direction of effects and no evidence of harm, a measured rollout with continued evaluation is warranted.
