# **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new rotation and shelving procedure saw their service scores increase by approximately **1.8 points** (about **3.5%**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant and consistent across the post-switch period. We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **709 branches** total: 425 switched to the new procedure, 284 kept the old procedure
- Data covers **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in switched branches against changes in non-switched branches

### **Results**

| Group | Before Switch (Weeks 6–14) | After Switch (Weeks 15–22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------------|---------------------------|--------|
| Switched Branches | 51.07 | 52.28 | +1.21 |
| Non-Switched Branches | 50.17 | 49.58 | -0.59 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+1.80** |

- **Estimated impact:** +1.81 service score points (95% confidence interval: 1.27 to 2.34)
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001 (highly significant)
- **Percentage improvement:** ~3.5% relative to the baseline average of 50.94

### **Parallel Trends Check**
Before the switch (weeks 6–14), the gap between switched and non-switched branches was relatively stable at an average of 0.9 points. After the switch, this gap widened to an average of 2.7 points, supporting the conclusion that the new procedure drove the improvement rather than pre-existing differences.

### **Additional Observations**
- Both staffing and visit levels positively correlate with service scores, but the procedure effect remains significant even after controlling for these factors
- Non-switched branches saw a slight decline (-0.59 points) after week 15, while switched branches improved (+1.21 points), suggesting the new procedure may also help branches resist seasonal or operational pressures that typically reduce service quality

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all remaining branches

2. **Develop implementation timeline** – consider phasing the rollout to manage training and operational capacity

3. **Monitor post-rollout performance** – continue tracking service scores for 8–12 weeks after full implementation to confirm the effect holds at scale

4. **Document best practices** – capture what switched branches did during the transition to create a playbook for remaining branches

5. **Consider cost-benefit analysis** – while service scores improved, evaluate whether the new procedure has any associated costs (training time, temporary disruption, etc.) that should be factored into the rollout plan

---

*Analysis based on weekly branch data from weeks 6–22. Statistical model controls for staffing levels and visitor volume, with standard errors clustered by branch.*
