# **Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Contrary to the expectation that the change would have no effect, branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure maintained their service scores while control branches experienced a notable decline. The new procedure is associated with a **statistically significant improvement of approximately 1.9 points** on the service score index (p < 0.01). We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **60 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach with branch and week fixed effects to isolate the procedure's impact

### **Results**

| Group | Before Switch (Weeks 6–14) | After Switch (Weeks 15–22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------------|---------------------------|--------|
| **New Procedure** | 50.54 | 50.37 | -0.17 |
| **Old Procedure** | 49.85 | 47.79 | -2.06 |

- **Estimated impact of new procedure: +1.88 points** (95% CI: 0.73 to 3.04; p = 0.0014)
- This represents approximately a **4% improvement** relative to the average service score of ~50
- The result holds after controlling for staffing levels and visit volumes

### **What Drove the Result?**

The data reveal an important pattern: **service scores declined across the system after week 15**, but branches using the new procedure were largely protected from this decline. This suggests the new procedure may provide resilience against external factors affecting service quality (e.g., seasonal staffing challenges, increased demand, or operational pressures).

- Only **47% of switched branches** saw their scores decline, compared to **75% of control branches**
- The parallel trends assumption is reasonably satisfied: pre-switch trends between groups showed no systematic divergence (p = 0.051)

## **Technical Appendix**

- **Method**: Panel regression with branch and week fixed effects, clustered standard errors at the branch level
- **Sample**: 2,550 branch-week observations (150 branches × 17 weeks)
- **Primary coefficient**: Treatment interaction term = 1.88 (SE = 0.59, p = 0.0014)
- **Robustness**: Adding staffing and visit controls yields nearly identical estimates (1.89 points)
