# **Library Cooperative Branch Procedure Evaluation**
## *Impact of New Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure on Service Scores*

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

The new procedure shows a **positive but not statistically definitive** effect on service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores improve by approximately **2.2 points (4.2%)** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. However, due to natural variation in the data, we cannot rule out the possibility that this improvement occurred by chance. The evidence is **suggestive but not conclusive**.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### Study Design
- **34 branches** participated in the evaluation (20 adopted new procedure, 14 kept old procedure)
- **17 weeks** of data collected (weeks 6-22)
- Switch occurred at **week 15**
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in treatment branches to changes in control branches

### Service Score Results

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| **Control (Old Procedure)** | 53.35 | 52.40 | -0.95 |
| **Treatment (New Procedure)** | 51.72 | 52.86 | +1.14 |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | | | **+2.09** |

### Statistical Evidence
- **Estimated effect:** +2.21 points (with controls for staffing and visitor volume)
- **95% Confidence Interval:** -0.38 to +4.79 points
- **p-value:** 0.094 (marginally significant)
- **Interpretation:** There is a 90% probability the true effect is positive, but we cannot claim 95% confidence

### Validity Checks
- ✅ **Parallel trends assumption holds:** Pre-switch trends were not significantly different between groups (p=0.57)
- ✅ **Model controls are meaningful:** Staffing levels and visitor volume significantly predict service scores
- ⚠️ **Wide confidence interval:** The data cannot precisely estimate the effect size

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

### Recommended Actions

1. **Proceed with Cautious Rollout**
   - The evidence points in the expected direction (improvement)
   - No evidence of harm from the new procedure
   - A 4% improvement, if real, would be operationally meaningful

2. **Continue Monitoring**
   - Track service scores for all branches after full rollout
   - Collect at least 8-12 more weeks of data to increase statistical power
   - Monitor whether the effect persists or grows over time

3. **Consider Cost-Benefit Analysis**
   - Evaluate implementation costs of the new procedure
   - If costs are low, the potential upside likely justifies adoption
   - If costs are high, consider a phased rollout with continued evaluation

4. **Investigate Heterogeneity**
   - Analyze whether certain branch types benefit more than others
   - Consider whether staffing or visitor volume moderates the effect

- Event study analysis shows no systematic pre-trends, supporting causal interpretation
