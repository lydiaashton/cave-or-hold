# **Board Summary: Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **1.9 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is **highly statistically significant** (p < 0.001) and represents a meaningful improvement in branch performance. We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Study Design
- **709 branches** total: 425 adopted the new procedure (treatment group), 284 kept the old procedure (control group)
- **17 weeks of data** (Weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at Week 15
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, which compares changes over time between the two groups while controlling for branch-specific characteristics and week-to-week variation

### 2. Results Summary

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Treatment (switched) | 51.6 | 52.4 | +0.7 |
| Control (no switch) | 50.2 | 49.0 | −1.2 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+1.9** |

- **Estimated treatment effect: +1.93 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.36 to 2.50)
- The control group's scores actually declined after Week 15, while treatment group scores held steady or improved—suggesting the new procedure may have protected branches from broader downward pressures

### 3. Statistical Confidence
- The result is **highly statistically significant** (p < 0.001)
- We can be 95% confident the true effect lies between **+1.4 and +2.5 points**
- Results hold even after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### 4. Practical Significance
- The average branch scores about 50 points; a 1.9-point improvement represents approximately **4% improvement**
- This is equivalent to about **0.18 standard deviations**—a small-to-moderate effect that is meaningful for service quality
- With 709 branches system-wide, the cumulative impact would be substantial

### 5. Parallel Trends Check
- Before the switch (Weeks 6–14), both groups showed similar week-to-week patterns
- Treatment branches consistently scored about 1–2 points higher than control branches throughout the pre-period (a stable baseline difference)
- This supports the validity of the comparison
