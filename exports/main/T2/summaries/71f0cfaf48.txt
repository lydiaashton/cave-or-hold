# **Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure Evaluation**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new stock rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.1 points** relative to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and has grown over time since implementation. We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect
- The new procedure increased service scores by **2.14 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.57 to 2.71 points)
- This represents about a **4% improvement** relative to the average branch score of ~50
- The effect is highly statistically significant (p < 0.001)

### 2. What the Data Shows
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches (425 branches) | 53.09 | 52.29 | -0.80 |
| Control branches (284 branches) | 53.06 | 50.10 | -2.96 |

While both groups saw scores decline after week 14 (possibly due to seasonal factors), branches using the new procedure declined **2.16 points less** than those using the old procedure. The new procedure appears to have protected branches from a larger decline.

### 3. Effect Grows Over Time
The treatment effect was not immediate but increased over the 8 weeks following implementation:
- Week 1 after switch: +0.85 points
- Week 4 after switch: +1.84 points
- Week 8 after switch: +3.18 points

This suggests branches may need time to fully realize the benefits of the new procedure.

### 4. Consistent Across Conditions
The positive effect was consistent regardless of staffing levels:
- Low staffing branches: +2.12 points
- Medium staffing branches: +2.06 points
- High staffing branches: +2.34 points

### 5. Validity Checks Passed
- **Parallel trends assumption**: Pre-switch trends between switched and control branches were similar (average difference of only -0.13 points in the 7 weeks before the switch)
- **Branch fixed effects**: Results hold when controlling for time-invariant differences between individual branches

- **Data**: 12,053 weekly observations from 709 branches, weeks 6-22

- **Controls**: Staffing index and visits index (both positively associated with service scores)
