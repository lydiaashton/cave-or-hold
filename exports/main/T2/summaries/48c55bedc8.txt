# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

## **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

### **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure is associated with a **modest improvement in service scores** (approximately 2–2.5 points on a ~50-point scale, or about 4–5%). While the evidence is **suggestive but not statistically definitive**, the consistent positive direction across all analyses, combined with the absence of negative effects, supports a **cautious rollout** with continued monitoring.

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. Treatment Effect Estimate**
- Branches that adopted the new procedure showed an average service score improvement of **2.2 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure, after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume.
- This represents approximately a **4.4% improvement** relative to the baseline average score of 50.

**2. Statistical Confidence**
- The effect is **marginally significant** (p ≈ 0.08–0.09), meaning there is roughly a 90% confidence level that the effect is real rather than due to random variation.
- The 95% confidence interval ranges from approximately **-0.3 to +4.8 points**, indicating the true effect could be slightly negative or as high as 5 points.

**3. Pattern Over Time**
- The positive effect appears to **strengthen over time**. In weeks 15–18 immediately after the switch, the effect was smaller (+0.7 points, not significant). In weeks 19–22, the effect grew to **+3.3 points** (p ≈ 0.06).
- This suggests branches may need an adjustment period to fully realize the benefits of the new procedure.

**4. Validation of Analysis**
- **Parallel trends confirmed**: Before the switch (weeks 6–14), treatment and control branches followed similar trends in service scores, supporting the validity of the comparison.
- **Results are robust**: The positive effect persists when controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume, and when excluding the first two weeks after the switch.

**5. Branch-Level Variation**
- Among the 20 branches that switched, **13 (65%)** showed improvement in their average service score.
- Among the 14 branches that did not switch, only **8 (57%)** showed improvement.
- This pattern is consistent with a positive treatment effect, though individual branch experiences varied.

---

### **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with phased rollout**: Given the positive direction of effects and absence of harm, recommend expanding the new procedure to remaining branches, but do so in batches to allow for monitoring.

2. **Monitor implementation closely**: Track service scores for the first 8–10 weeks after each branch switches to capture the adjustment period and confirm the effect strengthens over time.

3. **Investigate branch-level variation**: Some branches showed large gains (up to +11.6 points) while others declined (up to -4.5 points). Understanding what distinguishes high-performing adopters could help optimize implementation.

4. **Consider complementary factors**: Staffing levels were positively associated with service scores. Ensure adequate staffing during the transition to maximize the procedure's benefits.

5. **Re-evaluate after full rollout**: Once all branches have adopted the new procedure for at least 8 weeks, conduct a follow-up analysis to confirm the effect size and assess cost-benefit.
