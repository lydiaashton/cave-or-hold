# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact Analysis**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new shelving and stock rotation procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **1.9 points** (about 4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and robust. The board should consider rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect
- **Estimated impact: +1.90 service score points** (95% CI: 1.55 to 2.25)
- **Statistical significance: p < 0.001** (highly significant)
- This represents approximately a **3.8% improvement** relative to the average branch score of ~50

### 2. Before-and-After Comparison
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Treatment Branches (new procedure) | 49.69 | 52.98 | +3.28 |
| Control Branches (old procedure) | 48.97 | 50.35 | +1.38 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+1.90** |

The analysis accounts for:
- Branch-specific characteristics (some branches consistently score higher or lower)
- Week-to-week variations (overall service scores fluctuate by week)
- The fact that both groups improved over time, but treatment branches improved more

### 3. What Drove the Improvement?
The new procedure improved service scores **independently of staffing levels or visit volume**:
- Staffing index: No significant change (−0.01, p=0.78)
- Visits index: No significant change (−0.05, p=0.22)

This suggests the improvement comes from the procedure itself—likely better organization, faster item location, or improved shelf management—rather than from changes in resources or customer traffic.

### 4. Parallel Trends Validation
The analysis assumes that, absent the intervention, treatment and control branches would have followed similar trends. The data supports this:
- In the weeks immediately before the switch (weeks 12-14), there were **no statistically significant differences** between the two groups
- Early weeks (6-11) showed some differences, but these had converged by the time of the switch

## **Next Steps**

### Recommended Actions
1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches
2. **Monitor implementation** to ensure the new procedure is being followed consistently
3. **Track service scores** post-rollout to confirm the effect persists at scale

### Additional Considerations
1. **Training**: Ensure all staff receive adequate training on the new procedure to maximize benefits
2. **Timeline**: Consider a phased rollout to manage the transition smoothly
3. **Ongoing evaluation**: Continue collecting service score data to verify long-term effects and identify any branches that may need additional support

### Limitations to Note
- Analysis covers weeks 6-22; longer-term effects beyond this period are unknown
- The effect size (1.9 points) is meaningful but modest; branches should not expect dramatic transformations
- Some branches may respond differently based on local conditions; monitor for variation in outcomes
