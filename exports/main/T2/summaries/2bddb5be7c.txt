# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and rotation procedure **improved service scores by approximately 2 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This difference is statistically significant (p=0.001). Contrary to the initial expectation that the change would make no difference, the evidence indicates the new procedure had a meaningful positive effect on service quality.

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **150 branches** analyzed over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure (treatment group)
- **60 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- The switch occurred at week 15

### Results
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| New Procedure (90 branches) | 55.2 | 54.1 | -1.1 |
| Old Procedure (60 branches) | 53.1 | 50.0 | -3.1 |

- **Treatment Effect: +1.99 points** (95% confidence interval: 0.79 to 3.19)
- Both groups experienced a decline in service scores after week 14, but branches using the new procedure declined **less than half as much** as those using the old procedure
- The pre-switch trends were statistically similar between groups, supporting the validity of the comparison

### Statistical Confidence
- The result is statistically significant at the 1% level (p=0.001)
- We can be 95% confident the true effect lies between 0.8 and 3.2 points
- Given the average branch scores around 50, a 2-point improvement represents approximately a **4% gain** in service quality

## **Next Steps**

1. **Recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.** The evidence indicates it helps maintain service quality during periods when scores otherwise tend to decline.

2. **Monitor implementation closely.** Track whether the effect persists as more branches adopt the procedure and whether there are any unintended consequences (e.g., staff workload, costs).

3. **Investigate why scores declined overall after week 14.** Both groups saw lower scores in the post-period, suggesting external factors (seasonal patterns, staffing changes, etc.) may be at play. Understanding this could help further improve service.

4. **Consider a phased rollout** if resources are constrained, prioritizing branches with the lowest current service scores where the 2-point gain would be most impactful.
