# **Library Cooperative Shelving Procedure Change: Impact Analysis**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new shelving and stock rotation procedure significantly improved service scores.** Contrary to the initial expectation that the change would make no difference, branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2 points (4% improvement)** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and consistent across multiple analytical approaches.

**Recommendation: Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measurable Improvement in Service Scores
- **Treatment effect: +1.95 points** on the service score index (95% CI: 0.82 to 3.07)
- This represents approximately a **4% improvement** relative to the average branch score of ~49
- The effect is **highly statistically significant** (p < 0.001)

### 2. Strong Research Design
The analysis used a **difference-in-differences** approach comparing:
- **90 treatment branches** that switched to the new procedure at week 15
- **60 control branches** that continued with the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22), with 9 weeks before and 8 weeks after the switch

### 3. Parallel Trends Assumption Holds
Before the switch (weeks 6–14), treatment and control branches showed similar service score trends with no systematic divergence. After week 15, treatment branches consistently outperformed control branches by 2–3 points, supporting a causal interpretation.

### 4. Robust Results
The estimated effect remains stable:
- When controlling for branch-specific characteristics (fixed effects)
- When accounting for staffing levels and visit volumes
- Using cluster-robust standard errors at the branch level

### 5. Practical Significance
- A 2-point improvement is meaningful given the service score range (17 to 85)
- The effect size (0.19 standard deviations) is comparable to many operational improvements in service industries
- Even the conservative lower bound of the confidence interval (0.82 points, ~1.7%) represents a positive return
