# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

## **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

### **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw a statistically significant increase in service scores of approximately **2.3 points (4.5% improvement)** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. The evidence strongly supports rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. Measurable Improvement in Service Quality**
- Treatment branches (new procedure) improved from an average score of 50.0 to 52.3 after the switch
- Control branches (old procedure) remained essentially unchanged (49.1 to 49.1)
- The **difference-in-differences estimate is +2.26 points** (p < 0.001)
- 95% confidence interval: **+1.1 to +3.4 points**

**2. Robust Statistical Evidence**
- Analysis covers **34 branches** over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure; **14 branches** served as controls
- Switch occurred at week 15, providing 9 weeks of "before" and 8 weeks of "after" data
- Results hold even after controlling for branch-specific characteristics, staffing levels, and visitor traffic

**3. Improvement Not Driven by Other Factors**
- The new procedure did **not** significantly change staffing patterns or visitor volumes
- This suggests the service score improvement comes directly from the new procedure itself, not from confounding operational changes

**4. Parallel Trends Validated**
- Before the switch, treatment and control branches showed similar trends in service scores
- This supports the validity of the comparison and strengthens confidence in the causal interpretation

---

### **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all remaining branches

2. **Monitor implementation** to ensure the 4.5% improvement is realized across all branches when fully deployed

3. **Consider phased implementation** if resource constraints exist, prioritizing branches with the lowest current service scores

4. **Continue tracking** service scores, staffing, and visits to confirm sustained benefits and identify any branches that may need additional support during transition

5. **Document the procedure** and develop training materials based on best practices from the 20 early-adopter branches

---

### **Technical Note**

The analysis uses a difference-in-differences regression with branch fixed effects, which controls for time-invariant differences between branches and isolates the causal effect of the procedure change. The pre-treatment trends test confirms that treatment and control groups were evolving similarly before the intervention, validating the research design.
