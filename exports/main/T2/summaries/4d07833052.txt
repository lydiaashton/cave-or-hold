# **MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure is associated with a **modest improvement in service scores** (approximately 2 points, or 4% above average), but the evidence is **not statistically conclusive**. We recommend a **cautious, phased rollout** with continued monitoring rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Overall Effect**
- Branches that switched to the new procedure saw their service scores **increase by an average of 1.0 points** after the switch
- Branches that kept the old procedure saw their scores **decline by an average of 1.1 points** over the same period
- The **net difference is 2.2 points** in favor of the new procedure

### **2. Statistical Confidence**
- The effect is **not statistically significant** at conventional levels (p = 0.10)
- The 95% confidence interval ranges from approximately **-0.4 to +4.9 points**, meaning we cannot rule out no effect or even a small negative effect
- This uncertainty is due to natural variation in scores across branches and weeks

### **3. Effect Size**
- The estimated improvement represents about **0.21 standard deviations** or **4.2% of the average service score**
- This is a **modest but meaningful** improvement if real

### **4. Variation Across Branches**
The new procedure did not affect all branches equally:
- **Some branches improved substantially** (e.g., Branch 27: +8.1 points, Branch 11: +7.8 points)
- **Some branches saw declines** despite switching (e.g., Branch 32: -5.1 points, Branch 2: -4.1 points)
- This suggests the procedure may work better in some contexts than others

### **5. Validity Check**
- Pre-switch trends were **similar between groups**, supporting the validity of the comparison
- The analysis controlled for staffing levels and visitor volume, which did not substantially change the results

---

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended Actions**

1. **Do not proceed with immediate system-wide rollout.** The evidence is suggestive but not definitive.

2. **Extend the evaluation period.** Collect 8-12 more weeks of data to increase statistical confidence and observe whether effects persist or grow over time.

3. **Investigate branch-level variation.** Conduct qualitative interviews with branches that saw large improvements (e.g., Branches 11, 12, 20, 27) and those that saw declines (e.g., Branches 2, 10, 18, 32) to identify:
   - What implementation practices differed?
   - Were there branch characteristics (size, staffing, layout) that moderated the effect?

4. **Consider a pilot expansion.** Roll out the new procedure to 5-10 additional branches with careful monitoring, using findings from step 3 to select branches likely to benefit.

5. **Establish success criteria.** Define in advance what magnitude of improvement (e.g., 3+ points sustained over 12 weeks) would justify system-wide adoption.
