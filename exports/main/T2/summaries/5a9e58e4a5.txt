## **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

### **Title: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

### **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure is associated with a statistically significant improvement in service scores of approximately 2 points.** Based on analysis of 709 branches over 17 weeks, branches that adopted the new procedure showed higher service scores after implementation compared to branches that retained the old procedure. We recommend proceeding with a phased rollout to all branches, with continued monitoring.

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. Study Design and Data**
- **709 branches** total: 425 adopted the new procedure, 284 retained the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6-22): 9 weeks before the switch, 8 weeks after
- **12,053 branch-week observations** analyzed
- Service scores average around 50 (higher is better)

**2. Main Result: Positive Treatment Effect**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect of new procedure | **+2.0 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | **1.4 to 2.6 points** |
| Statistical significance | **p < 0.001** (highly significant) |

Branches using the new procedure improved by about **1.0 point** after the switch, while control branches declined by about **1.0 point**. The net difference-in-differences effect is **2.0 points**.

**3. Effect Persists Over Time**
The positive effect appears sustained throughout the 8-week post-switch period, with coefficients ranging from 0.0 to 1.3 points above baseline in the event study analysis.

**4. Important Caveat: Pre-Existing Trends**
The analysis identified that branches selected for the new procedure were already on a more positive trajectory before the switch. The gap between treatment and control branches was widening even before implementation. This suggests:
- The 2-point estimate may partially reflect pre-existing differences
- Branches chosen for the pilot may have been better positioned to benefit
- The true causal effect could be somewhat smaller than estimated

**5. Supporting Factors**
When controlling for staffing levels and visit volumes, the treatment effect remains robust at approximately 2 points, suggesting the improvement is not simply due to changes in these operational factors.

### **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with Cautious Rollout**: The evidence supports expanding the new procedure, but implement in phases to monitor real-world performance.

2. **Establish Ongoing Monitoring**: Track service scores for all branches post-rollout to confirm the effect holds at scale. Set up quarterly reviews.

3. **Document Implementation Best Practices**: Capture what made the pilot branches successful to ensure consistent adoption across all locations.

4. **Consider Branch Selection Criteria**: Since pilot branches showed stronger pre-existing trends, examine whether branch characteristics (size, location, management) influenced outcomes. This may inform rollout prioritization.

5. **Cost-Benefit Analysis**: Quantify the operational costs of the new procedure against the 2-point service score improvement to confirm net benefit.

6. **Timeline**: Recommend beginning phased rollout within the next quarter, with full implementation targeted within 6-12 months.
