# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and rotation procedure shows a **positive but statistically uncertain effect** on service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure improved their scores by an estimated **2.4 points** (about 5% of the average score) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. However, due to the limited number of branches in the study, we cannot rule out the possibility that this improvement occurred by chance. The evidence is suggestive but not conclusive.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Estimated Impact**
- **Treatment effect:** +2.4 points on the service score index
- **95% confidence interval:** -0.6 to +5.4 points
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.12 (not significant at the conventional 0.05 threshold)

### **2. What the Data Shows**

The switched branches improved while the non-switched branches declined slightly, resulting in a net positive effect of 2.4 points attributable to the new procedure.

### **3. Validity Check: Parallel Trends**
A key assumption of this analysis is that treated and control branches would have followed similar trends absent the intervention. The data supports this:
- Pre-switch trends show no significant difference between groups (p = 0.39)
- The gap between groups remained relatively stable before week 15

### **4. Effect Size Context**
- The 2.4-point improvement represents approximately **5% of the average service score** (48.3)
- This equals about **0.24 standard deviations**—a modest but potentially meaningful improvement
- For comparison, the confidence interval suggests the true effect could range from negligible (-0.6) to substantial (+5.4)

### **5. Study Limitations**
- **Sample size:** Only 34 branches total (20 treated, 14 control)
- **Statistical power:** The study is underpowered to detect small effects with high confidence
- **Time horizon:** Only 8 weeks of post-switch data; longer-term effects are unknown
