# **Library Cooperative Procedure Change: Impact Analysis**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new shelving and rotation procedure significantly improved service scores.** Contrary to the expectation that the change would make no difference, branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **2.1 points** (approximately 4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This result is statistically significant (p < 0.01), and we recommend proceeding with a full rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Study Design
- **150 branches** total: 90 adopted the new procedure, 60 kept the old procedure (control group)
- **17 weeks of data**: 9 weeks before the switch (weeks 6-14), 8 weeks after (weeks 15-22)
- Analysis used a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in the switched branches against changes in the control branches

### 2. Main Results

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 49.55 | 48.87 | -0.68 |
| Switched (new procedure) | 49.36 | 50.78 | +1.42 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+2.10** |

- **Estimated effect: +2.10 points** (95% confidence interval: 0.83 to 3.38)
- **Statistical significance: p = 0.001** (highly significant)
- **Practical significance: 4.3% improvement** from baseline service scores

### 3. What This Means
- Branches using the new procedure improved their service scores while control branches experienced a slight decline
- The 2.1-point improvement is both statistically reliable and practically meaningful
- The effect persisted consistently across all 8 post-switch weeks
- Other operational factors (staffing levels, visit volumes) did not differ systematically between groups, supporting the conclusion that the procedure change itself drove the improvement

### 4. Visual Summary
The analysis includes two visualizations:
- **Trend chart**: Shows service scores over time for both groups, with clear divergence after week 14
- **DiD chart**: Illustrates the before/after comparison and the estimated treatment effect
