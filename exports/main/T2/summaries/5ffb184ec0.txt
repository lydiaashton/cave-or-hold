# **Analysis of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**
## Impact on Service Scores

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw a statistically significant increase of **2.3 points** in their service scores compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents approximately a **4.6% improvement** relative to the typical branch score of 50. The evidence supports rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** switched to the new procedure at week 15 (treatment group)
- **14 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in treatment branches to changes in control branches

### Results
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| **New Procedure (20 branches)** | 50.45 | 52.08 | **+1.63** |
| **Old Procedure (14 branches)** | 53.93 | 53.27 | **-0.66** |
| **Difference (New − Old)** | | | **+2.29** |

### Statistical Evidence
- **Treatment effect:** +2.29 points (95% confidence interval: 0.68 to 3.90)
- **P-value:** 0.0055 (statistically significant at the 1% level)
- **Effect size:** 0.23 standard deviations (small to moderate, but meaningful)

### Important Context
- Treatment branches started with lower scores than control branches (50.45 vs. 53.93), but this gap was stable before the switch
- After the switch, treatment branches improved while control branches experienced a slight decline
- The gap between groups narrowed from 3.5 points to 1.2 points

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all remaining branches

2. **Monitor implementation** at newly converted branches to ensure the procedure is being followed correctly and benefits are realized

3. **Continue tracking service scores** for at least 8–12 weeks post-rollout to confirm the effect persists

4. **Investigate the control group decline** — the slight decrease in scores at branches keeping the old procedure warrants attention; the old procedure may have emerging drawbacks

5. **Document best practices** from the 20 early-adopter branches to support training at other locations

---

*Analysis prepared using difference-in-differences regression with branch and week fixed effects to control for time-invariant branch characteristics and common weekly trends.*
