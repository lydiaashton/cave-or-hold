# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new shelving and rotation procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by **2.1 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.6 to 2.7 points) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents approximately a **4% improvement** and is statistically significant (p < 0.001). We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect
- **Point estimate**: +2.13 service score points
- **95% confidence interval**: [1.57, 2.68]
- **Statistical significance**: p < 0.001 (highly significant)
- **Relative improvement**: ~4% above baseline (baseline average: 51.9)

### 2. What the Data Shows
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| **Switched branches** (425 branches) | 51.98 | 53.04 | **+1.06** |
| **Non-switched branches** (284 branches) | 51.88 | 50.81 | **-1.07** |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+2.13** |

### 3. Branch-Level Impact
- **60%** of switched branches improved their scores vs. only **38%** of non-switched branches
- The effect is consistent across the majority of treatment branches
- Results hold after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### 4. Study Design Strengths
- **Large sample**: 709 branches, 12,053 weekly observations
- **Clear treatment timing**: Switch occurred at week 15
- **Comparable groups**: Treatment and control branches had nearly identical average scores before the switch (51.98 vs 51.88)
- **Robust analysis**: Effect remains significant with cluster-robust standard errors and control variables

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches

2. **Monitor implementation**: Track service scores for the next 8-12 weeks after rollout to confirm the effect persists at scale

3. **Document best practices**: Capture what switched branches did during implementation to support training for remaining branches

4. **Consider complementary improvements**: Since staffing and visitor volume also affect service scores (both statistically significant in our model), evaluate whether additional support in these areas could amplify the gains from the new procedure

5. **Schedule follow-up review**: Reassess in 6 months to evaluate long-term sustainability of the improvement

*Analysis used a difference-in-differences approach comparing 425 treatment branches to 284 control branches over 17 weeks (weeks 6-22).
