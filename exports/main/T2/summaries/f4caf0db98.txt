# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.4 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.0 to 3.8 points) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents a **5% improvement** on the average service score of 51. The result is statistically significant (p < 0.01), and we recommend proceeding with system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **150 branches** total: 90 adopted the new procedure (treatment group), 60 kept the old procedure (control group)
- Data covers **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- This is a natural experiment using a **difference-in-differences** approach, which compares changes over time between the two groups

### **Results**
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 49.7 | 51.1 | +1.4 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 49.8 | 53.6 | +3.8 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+2.4** |

- The treatment group improved by **2.4 points more** than the control group after adopting the new procedure
- This effect is **statistically significant** (p = 0.001), meaning it is very unlikely to be due to random chance
- The effect remains significant after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### **Validity Check**
- **Parallel trends assumption holds**: Before the switch (weeks 6–14), the two groups had nearly identical service score trends (average difference of only 0.02 points). This supports the conclusion that the observed improvement was caused by the new procedure, not pre-existing differences between branches.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all remaining branches

2. **Monitor implementation**: Track service scores during rollout to ensure the effect replicates at scale

3. **Investigate mechanisms**: Consider studying *why* the new procedure works (e.g., faster restocking, better organization, reduced staff time on shelving) to optimize further

4. **Cost-benefit analysis**: If there are additional costs associated with the new procedure, weigh them against the 5% service score improvement to confirm net benefit

---

## **Technical Notes**

- Analysis used difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch)
- Results are robust to inclusion of branch fixed effects and control variables (staffing index, visits index)
- Service score is an index where higher values indicate better service; the cooperative-wide average is approximately 50
