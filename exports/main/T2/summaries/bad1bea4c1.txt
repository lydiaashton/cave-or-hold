# **Summary for the Board: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Contrary to the initial expectation of no change, branches that adopted the new stock rotation and shelving procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **2.3 points** (on a scale where the average branch scores about 50). This represents approximately a **4-5% improvement** in service quality. The evidence is statistically robust, and we recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect
- **Average impact**: +2.28 points on the service score index
- **95% Confidence Interval**: 1.00 to 3.56 points
- **Statistical significance**: p = 0.0005 (highly significant)

### 2. Study Design & Data
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15 (treatment group)
- **60 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach with branch and week fixed effects, which controls for:
  - Time-invariant differences between branches
  - Common weekly trends affecting all branches

### 3. Validity of Results
- **Parallel trends assumption confirmed**: Pre-switch service score trends were statistically indistinguishable between treatment and control branches (p = 0.45)
- **Event study analysis** shows:
  - No systematic differences before the switch (weeks 6–14)
  - Consistent positive effects after the switch (weeks 15–22), ranging from approximately +1.9 to +4.6 points

### 4. Practical Significance
- A 2.3-point increase on a ~50-point scale represents meaningful improvement
- Effects were sustained across all 8 post-switch weeks, suggesting the improvement is durable rather than temporary
