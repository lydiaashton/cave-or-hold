# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Contrary to the expectation that the change would make no difference, branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores approximately **1.9 points higher** than they would have otherwise—a statistically significant improvement (p < 0.01). We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure at week 15; **60 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in switched branches against non-switched branches

### Results

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches (new procedure) | 51.28 | 51.53 | +0.25 |
| Non-switched branches (old procedure) | 49.63 | 47.97 | −1.66 |
| **Difference-in-Differences Effect** | | | **+1.91** |

### Statistical Evidence
- **Effect size:** +1.91 points on the service score index
- **95% confidence interval:** [0.74, 3.09]
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.001 (highly significant)
- **Relative improvement:** ~3.8% above baseline

### Validity Check
The analysis relies on the assumption that switched and non-switched branches would have followed similar trends absent the intervention. This **parallel trends assumption is supported** (p = 0.81), as both groups showed similar patterns before the switch. After week 15, the switched branches maintained their performance while non-switched branches declined.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all 60 remaining branches using the old method.

2. **Monitor implementation** over the next 8–12 weeks to confirm the effect persists at scale.

3. **Investigate the decline** in non-switched branches' scores. The −1.66 point drop suggests external factors (seasonal, staffing, or other) may be affecting service quality, and the new procedure appears to protect against this decline.

4. **Consider cost-benefit analysis** if the new procedure has different resource requirements than the old method.
