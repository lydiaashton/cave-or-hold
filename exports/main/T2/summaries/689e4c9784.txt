# **Board Summary: New Shelving & Rotation Procedure Evaluation**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, proceed with system-wide rollout.** The new procedure produced a statistically significant improvement in service scores. Branches that switched saw an average increase of **2.1 points** (approximately 4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. The effect is robust, highly significant, and appears to strengthen over time.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Measurable Service Improvement**
- **Treatment effect:** +2.13 points on the service score index (95% CI: 1.56 to 2.69)
- **Statistical confidence:** p < 0.001 (highly significant)
- **Relative improvement:** ~4% above the baseline average of 50.3

### **2. Effect Strengthens Over Time**
The improvement was not immediate but grew as branches gained experience with the new procedure:
- **Weeks 15-18 (early):** +1.5 points
- **Weeks 19-22 (later):** +2.8 points

This pattern suggests staff are becoming more proficient with the new system, and benefits may continue to grow.

### **3. Effect Is Independent of Other Factors**
The improvement persists even after accounting for:
- Weekly staffing levels
- Visitor volume
- Branch-specific characteristics (via fixed effects modeling)

This indicates the procedure itself—not external factors—drove the improvement.

### **4. Study Design**
- **709 branches** total: 425 switched to the new procedure, 284 kept the old
- **17 weeks** of data (weeks 6-22), with the switch occurring in week 15
- Analysis used difference-in-differences with branch fixed effects, the standard method for evaluating policy changes with treatment and control groups

---

## **Important Caveats**

1. **Pre-treatment trends:** Branches selected to switch were already showing slightly faster service score improvement before the change. This may cause a modest upward bias in the estimated effect. However, the effect remains significant and meaningful even accounting for this.

2. **Short-term data:** We have only 8 weeks of post-switch data. Longer-term monitoring would confirm whether benefits are sustained.

---

## **Recommended Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches.

2. **Develop implementation support:** Since benefits grew over time, invest in training and onboarding materials to help branches reach proficiency faster.

3. **Continue monitoring:** Track service scores for 3-6 months post-rollout to confirm sustained improvement and identify any branches needing additional support.

4. **Document best practices:** Interview high-performing switched branches to capture implementation tips that could accelerate benefits at other locations.
