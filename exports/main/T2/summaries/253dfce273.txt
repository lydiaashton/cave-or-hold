# **Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure Evaluation**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure appears to improve service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **1.9 points** (about 4%) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and consistent across multiple analytical approaches. We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout while continuing to monitor outcomes.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Measurable Improvement in Service Scores**
- **Treatment effect:** +1.87 points on the service score index (95% confidence interval: 1.30 to 2.44)
- **Relative improvement:** Approximately 3.7% increase over the average branch score of 50.7
- **Statistical significance:** Highly significant (p < 0.001)
- **Effect size:** 0.18 standard deviations (small to moderate, but meaningful at scale)

### **2. Analysis Scope**
- **709 branches** total in the cooperative
- **425 branches** adopted the new procedure (treatment group)
- **284 branches** maintained the old procedure (control group)
- **17 weeks** of data (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- **12,053 branch-week observations** analyzed

### **3. Effect Persists Across Analytical Methods**
The estimated treatment effect remained consistent (~1.85–1.87 points) across multiple model specifications:
- Basic difference-in-differences
- Models controlling for staffing and visit levels
- Models with branch fixed effects (controlling for inherent branch differences)
- Models with both branch and week fixed effects

### **4. Effect Appears to Strengthen Over Time**
Weekly analysis suggests the positive effect grew in the weeks following implementation, with the largest gains observed in weeks 19–22. This pattern is consistent with staff becoming more proficient with the new procedure over time.

---

## **Important Caveats**

### **Pre-Existing Differences Between Groups**
Branches that switched to the new procedure were not identical to those that didn't:
- Treatment branches had slightly higher baseline service scores
- The gap between treatment and control branches was already changing before the switch

Our analysis used branch fixed effects to control for these time-invariant differences, but we cannot fully rule out that other factors contributed to the observed improvement. The estimated effect should be interpreted as the best available evidence rather than definitive proof of causation.

---

## **Next Steps**

### **1. Proceed with System-Wide Rollout**
The evidence supports expanding the new procedure to all 709 branches. The estimated 1.9-point improvement, while modest, represents meaningful service enhancement across the cooperative.

### **2. Continue Monitoring**
- Track service scores for the next 8–12 weeks post-rollout
- Monitor whether the effect stabilizes or continues to grow as staff gain experience
- Watch for any branches that underperform after the switch (may need additional training)

### **3. Document Implementation Best Practices**
- Capture what distinguished successful early adopters
- Develop training materials based on branches with the strongest improvements
- Consider whether staffing levels or visit volumes moderate the effect

### **4. Strengthen Future Evaluation Design**
For future procedural changes, consider:
- Randomized rollout (if operationally feasible)
- More frequent measurement during transition periods
- Qualitative feedback from branch staff alongside quantitative metrics
