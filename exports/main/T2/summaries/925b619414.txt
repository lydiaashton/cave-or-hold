# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact Analysis**

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure is associated with a **modest improvement in service scores** (+2.3 points, or ~5% relative improvement). However, the evidence is **not statistically conclusive** at conventional levels (p=0.10). The 95% confidence interval ranges from -0.4 to +5.1 points, meaning we cannot rule out either no effect or a meaningful positive effect. The board should consider proceeding with caution, potentially implementing the new procedure more broadly while continuing to monitor outcomes.

### **1. Study Design**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **14 branches** kept the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in switched branches against changes in control branches

### **Recommended Actions**

1. **Proceed with phased rollout** — The point estimate is positive and meaningful (2+ points on a ~50-point scale), and there's no evidence of harm. However, given the statistical uncertainty, consider rolling out to additional branches while continuing to collect data.

2. **Extend the observation period** — Collect 8–12 more weeks of data to increase statistical power. With more post-switch observations, we could achieve definitive statistical significance if the effect is real.

4. **Investigate heterogeneity** — Analyze whether certain types of branches (by size, location, or baseline performance) benefit more from the new procedure. This could inform targeted rollout.

5. **Consider cost-benefit** — If the new procedure requires additional staff time or resources, weigh the ~5% service score improvement against those costs.
