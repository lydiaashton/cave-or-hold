# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and rotation procedure **improved service scores by 2.3 points** (approximately 4% relative to the average score of 50). This effect is statistically significant and represents a meaningful improvement. We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **150 branches** tracked over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15 (treatment group)
- **60 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in service scores before and after the switch between the two groups

### Results

| Group | Pre-Switch Average | Post-Switch Average | Change |
|-------|-------------------|---------------------|--------|
| Switched branches (new procedure) | 52.15 | 52.30 | +0.15 |
| Non-switched branches (old procedure) | 52.72 | 50.56 | −2.17 |
| **Difference-in-Differences Estimate** | | | **+2.31** |

- **Treatment effect: +2.31 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.0 to 3.5 points)
- **Statistical significance: p < 0.001** (highly significant)
- The effect remains significant after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### Important Context
- Service scores declined across the system after week 14 (the control group dropped by 2.2 points)
- Branches using the new procedure **avoided this decline** and showed slight improvement
- The pre-switch trends were parallel between groups, supporting the validity of the comparison
