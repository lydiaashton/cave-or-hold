## **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

### **Title: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

### **Bottom Line**

The new procedure is associated with a **modest improvement in service scores** (approximately +2 points, or 4% of the average score), but this effect is **not statistically significant** at conventional levels when using the most rigorous analytical methods. The evidence is **suggestive but not conclusive**. We recommend a cautious approach: continue monitoring the switched branches while considering a phased rollout with careful evaluation.

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. Study Design and Data**
- 34 branches tracked over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- 20 branches adopted the new procedure starting week 15; 14 branches continued with the old procedure
- Service scores average approximately 50 points (higher is better)

**2. Estimated Effect of the New Procedure**
| Model Specification | Effect (points) | P-value | Statistically Significant? |
|---------------------|-----------------|---------|---------------------------|
| Two-Way Fixed Effects (primary) | +2.16 | 0.116 | No |
| Branch Fixed Effects Only | +1.74 | 0.026 | Yes |
| Simple Difference-in-Differences | +2.16 | 0.101 | No |
| With Staffing/Visits Controls | +2.18 | 0.118 | No |

**3. Interpretation**
- The **point estimate is consistently positive** across all specifications (~+2 points)
- However, the **95% confidence interval** for the primary estimate is **[-0.53, 4.84]**, meaning we cannot rule out no effect or even a small negative effect
- The effect size represents approximately **4% improvement** relative to the average service score
- Weekly analysis suggests the effect may **grow over time**, with week 22 showing a significant positive effect (+4.03 points, p=0.021)

**4. Important Caveats**
- The treatment and control groups showed some differences in their pre-intervention trends, which complicates causal interpretation
- The relatively small number of branches (34 total) limits statistical power to detect modest effects
- Eight weeks of post-intervention data may be insufficient to capture the full effect if benefits accrue gradually

---

### **Next Steps**

1. **Continue Monitoring**: Extend data collection for the switched branches to determine whether the effect strengthens over time (as week 22 data suggests it may).

2. **Phased Rollout Consideration**: Given the positive but uncertain evidence, consider rolling out the procedure to additional branches in stages, with each cohort carefully evaluated before proceeding.

3. **Branch-Level Analysis**: Investigate whether certain types of branches (e.g., by size, location, or baseline performance) benefit more from the new procedure, which could inform targeted implementation.

4. **Cost-Benefit Assessment**: Before full rollout, quantify the operational costs of the new procedure relative to the observed service score improvements.

5. **Re-evaluate in 3 Months**: With additional weeks of data, re-run this analysis to determine whether statistical significance is achieved with a larger sample.

---

### **Recommendation**

**Do not proceed with immediate system-wide rollout.** The evidence points in a positive direction but lacks the statistical certainty needed for a major operational change. A measured, evidence-based approach with continued monitoring is warranted.
