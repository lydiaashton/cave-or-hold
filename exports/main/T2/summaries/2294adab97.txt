**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new stock rotation and shelving procedure increased service scores by approximately 2.2 points (4.3% improvement), with high statistical confidence.** We recommend proceeding with a full rollout to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect
- Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by **2.2 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure
- This effect is **statistically significant** (p < 0.01), with a 95% confidence interval of 0.65 to 3.76 points
- The effect persists after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### 2. Study Design
- **150 branches** total: 90 switched to the new procedure, 60 kept the old procedure
- Data covers **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in switched branches against changes in non-switched branches
- **Pre-treatment trends were parallel** between groups, validating the comparison

### 3. Magnitude of Impact
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Average service score (baseline) | 51.1 |
| Improvement from new procedure | +2.2 points |
| Relative improvement | 4.3% |
| Effect size (Cohen's d) | 0.22 (small-to-moderate) |

### 4. What Changed
- **Control branches** (old procedure): Service scores remained essentially flat (+0.2 points)
- **Treatment branches** (new procedure): Service scores increased by +2.4 points
- The difference (+2.2 points) is attributable to the new procedure

---

## **Important Context**

The analysis addressed a key concern: branches that switched to the new procedure had systematically higher service scores than control branches even before the switch. However, our analysis confirms that:

1. **Pre-treatment trends were parallel** – both groups showed similar week-to-week patterns before the switch
2. **Branch fixed effects models** (which control for permanent differences between branches) confirm the same ~2.1 point treatment effect
3. The improvement is **not explained by staffing or visit volume changes**

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve full rollout** of the new procedure to all 60 branches still using the old method
2. **Monitor implementation** – track service scores for the first 8 weeks after rollout to confirm the effect holds at scale
3. **Document the procedure** – capture the specific practices that drove the improvement for training purposes
4. **Consider cost-benefit analysis** – if the new procedure requires additional resources, weigh the 4.3% service improvement against implementation costs

---

## **Analytical Notes**

- Method: Difference-in-differences regression with robust standard errors
- Controls: Staffing index, visits index, branch fixed effects
- Validation: Pre-trend parallelism confirmed (p = 0.67 for trend difference)
- Data: 2,550 branch-week observations

---

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout.** The evidence indicates the new procedure meaningfully improves service quality without requiring assumptions about branch characteristics.
