# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure shows a **positive but statistically inconclusive effect** on service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure improved their service scores by approximately **2.2 points** (about 5% of the average score) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. However, this improvement is not statistically significant at conventional levels (p=0.10), meaning we cannot confidently rule out that the observed improvement occurred by chance.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with caution. The evidence suggests the new procedure may help, but it is not definitive. Consider a phased rollout with continued monitoring, or extend the evaluation period to gather more data before making a system-wide commitment.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Overall Effect**
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched Branches (20 branches) | 45.0 | 47.8 | +2.7 |
| Control Branches (14 branches) | 48.7 | 49.2 | +0.5 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+2.2** |

- The estimated effect of the new procedure is **+2.24 points** on the service score index
- 95% confidence interval: **-0.46 to +4.94 points**
- This range includes zero, indicating statistical uncertainty

### **2. Statistical Significance**
- P-value: **0.104** (not significant at the conventional 0.05 threshold)
- The study had approximately 80% power to detect effects of 3.85 points or larger
- Smaller effects (like the 2.2 points observed) may go undetected with this sample size

### **3. Validity Check: Parallel Trends**
A critical assumption of this analysis is that switched and control branches would have followed similar trends absent the intervention. The data supports this:
- **Pre-switch trends show no significant divergence** between the two groups
- This strengthens confidence that the observed difference is attributable to the procedure change

### **4. Effect Over Time**
The weekly analysis reveals an **inconsistent pattern** after the switch:
- Weeks 15-17: Modest positive effects (not significant)
- Weeks 18-21: Effects turn negative or neutral
- Week 22: Positive effect reappears

This variability suggests the effect may not be stable, or that other factors (seasonal patterns, staffing changes, etc.) are influencing scores.

### **5. Practical Significance**
- A 2.2-point improvement represents about **5% of the average service score** (~47 points)
- If real, this would be a meaningful improvement for patron experience
- However, the uncertainty means the true effect could be zero or even slightly negative

## **Next Steps**

### **Immediate Actions**
1. **Continue monitoring** both switched and control branches for at least 8-12 more weeks to increase statistical power
2. **Document implementation fidelity** – ensure switched branches are fully following the new procedure
3. **Gather qualitative feedback** from staff at switched branches about operational challenges or benefits

### **Decision Options for the Board**

| Option | Pros | Cons |
|--------|------|------|
| **Full Rollout** | Captures potential benefits quickly; uniform procedures across system | Risk of adopting an ineffective procedure; implementation costs |
| **Phased Rollout** | Allows continued learning; limits risk | Delays potential benefits; creates temporary inconsistency |
| **Extended Evaluation** | More definitive evidence before committing | Opportunity cost of delayed benefits if procedure is effective |

### **Recommended Approach**
Given the suggestive but inconclusive evidence, I recommend a **phased rollout**:
1. Invite 5-10 additional branches to adopt the procedure voluntarily
2. Continue rigorous monitoring with the same evaluation framework
3. Reassess in 3 months with the expanded dataset
4. Make a system-wide decision based on stronger evidence

This balances the potential upside of the new procedure against the risk of premature adoption.
