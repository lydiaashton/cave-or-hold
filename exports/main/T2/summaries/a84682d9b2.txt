# **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **1.8 points** (about 3.5%) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and appears to grow over time. We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **709 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **425 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **284 branches** kept the old procedure (control group)
- Used a difference-in-differences approach to isolate the effect of the new procedure

### **Main Results**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Estimated effect** | +1.81 points on service score |
| **95% Confidence Interval** | [1.26, 2.36] |
| **Statistical significance** | p < 0.001 (highly significant) |
| **Effect size** | ~3.5% improvement relative to average score |

### **Effect Over Time**
The benefit of the new procedure **increases over time**:
- Week 15 (first week after switch): +0.75 points
- Week 22 (most recent): +1.89 points

This pattern suggests branches may need a short adjustment period before realizing the full benefits.

### **Validity of Findings**
- **Pre-trends check passed**: In the weeks immediately before the switch (weeks 10–14), switched and non-switched branches showed no statistically significant differences in their service score trends. This supports the conclusion that the observed improvement is caused by the new procedure, not pre-existing differences.
- **Robust to controls**: Results hold even after accounting for weekly staffing and visit volume variations.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all remaining branches.

2. **Plan for implementation support**: Since the effect grows over time, provide training and support during the first 4–6 weeks after each branch switches to help staff realize the full benefits sooner.

3. **Continue monitoring**: Track service scores for the next 8–12 weeks after full rollout to confirm the effect persists and to identify any branches that may need additional support.

4. **Investigate mechanisms**: Consider studying *why* the new procedure works (e.g., faster item retrieval, better organization, improved staff efficiency) to optimize future procedural changes.

---

*Analysis based on 12,053 weekly observations from 709 branches. Statistical models use cluster-robust standard errors at the branch level.*
