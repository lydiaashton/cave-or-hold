# **Board Summary: Impact of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by an additional **1.8 points** (95% CI: 1.3 to 2.4) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents a **3.7% improvement** over baseline scores and is statistically significant (p < 0.001). We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **709 branches** analyzed over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **425 branches** adopted the new procedure at week 15 (treatment group)
- **284 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses difference-in-differences methodology with robust standard errors

### Main Results

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Treatment effect | **+1.84 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | [1.28, 2.40] |
| P-value | < 0.001 |
| Improvement vs. baseline | **3.7%** |

### Score Trajectories

| Period | Treatment Branches | Control Branches |
|--------|-------------------|------------------|
| Before switch (weeks 6–14) | 49.7 | 49.2 |
| After switch (weeks 15–22) | 54.8 | 52.4 |
| **Change** | **+5.0** | **+3.2** |

Both groups improved after week 15 (likely due to seasonal factors), but treatment branches improved **1.8 points more** than control branches—this is the effect attributable to the new procedure.

### Robustness Checks
- Effect remains significant when excluding week 14 (which showed unusual pre-switch movement): **+2.1 points**
- Effect is consistent across all post-switch weeks
- Results hold when controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

## **Next Steps**

### Recommended Actions

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all 284 branches currently using the old procedure

2. **Monitor implementation** over the next 8–12 weeks to confirm effects persist at scale

3. **Document best practices** from the 425 early-adopter branches to support training

4. **Consider cost-benefit analysis** to quantify ROI (e.g., staff time savings, patron satisfaction gains beyond the service score)

### Caveats to Note

- Week 14 showed an unusual uptick in treatment branch scores before the switch, possibly indicating anticipation effects or selection bias. Our conservative estimates account for this.
- The analysis covers a 17-week period; longer-term effects should be monitored.
- Service scores are one metric; consider complementing with patron satisfaction surveys and operational cost data.

---

## **Conclusion**

The evidence strongly supports expanding the new procedure cooperative-wide. The 1.8-point service score improvement is both statistically significant and practically meaningful, representing a ~4% gain on an index where branches average around 50 points. The board can confidently proceed with rollout.
