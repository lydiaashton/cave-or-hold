## Bottom Line

**The new stock rotation and shelving procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.0 points** (a 4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and robust. **We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all remaining branches.**

---

## Key Findings

### 1. Treatment Effect
- **Net improvement:** +2.0 points on the service score index (95% CI: 1.5 to 2.6)
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001 (highly significant)
- **Relative improvement:** Approximately 4% above baseline performance

### 2. Study Design
- **709 branches** total across the cooperative
- **425 branches** adopted the new procedure (treatment group)
- **284 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- **17 weeks** of data (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Analysis used a difference-in-differences approach with branch fixed effects, controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### 3. What the Data Shows

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 52.78 | 53.79 | +1.02 |
| Non-switched branches | 50.89 | 49.91 | -0.99 |
| **Net effect** | | | **+2.00** |

The control group actually declined slightly after week 15, while switched branches improved—suggesting the new procedure may also protect against seasonal or external factors that typically reduce service scores.

### 4. Validity Checks
- **Parallel trends confirmed:** Pre-switch trends showed no significant divergence between groups (p = 0.86), supporting the validity of our comparison
- **Robust to controls:** The effect persists after accounting for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume
- **Consistent estimates:** Multiple model specifications all yield treatment effects between 2.0 and 2.1 points
