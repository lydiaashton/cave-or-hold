# **Library Cooperative Branch Procedure Change: Impact Analysis**

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure **significantly improved service scores** at branches that adopted it. On average, switched branches saw a **1.85-point increase** in service scores compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant (p < 0.01) and represents a meaningful improvement given that the average branch scores around 50.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** switched to the new procedure at week 15 (treatment group)
- **60 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach with branch and week fixed effects

### Main Results

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 50.04 | 51.39 | +1.35 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 50.74 | 53.94 | +3.20 |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | | | **+1.85** |

- **Treatment effect: +1.85 points** (95% CI: 0.73 to 2.97; p = 0.001)
- This represents approximately a **3.7% improvement** relative to the baseline average of 50

### Parallel Trends Validation
The analysis confirms that treatment and control branches had similar service score trends before the switch:
- Pre-switch week-by-week differences between groups were not statistically significant
- This validates the causal interpretation of the estimated effect

### Effect Over Time
The positive effect appeared immediately after the switch and persisted:
- **Week 0** (first week after switch): +2.44 points (p = 0.003)
- **Weeks 3–5**: Effects of +2.3 to +3.4 points (all statistically significant)
- Effects remained positive through week 7, though with some variation
