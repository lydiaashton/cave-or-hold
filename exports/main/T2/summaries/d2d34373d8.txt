# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new shelving and rotation procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new procedure saw service scores increase by **1.8 points more** than branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and appears to be growing over time. We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Net improvement from new procedure** | **+1.81 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | [1.27, 2.36] |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 |

### 2. What This Means in Practice
- **Effect size**: The 1.8-point improvement represents about **17% of a standard deviation** in service scores—a meaningful operational improvement
- **Relative improvement**: About **3.7% above baseline** service levels
- **Trajectory**: The benefit appears to be **growing over time**, with the treatment-control gap widening from 2.0 points in week 15 to 3.1 points by week 22

### 3. Before and After Comparison

| Group | Pre-Switch Avg | Post-Switch Avg | Raw Change |
|-------|---------------|-----------------|------------|
| **Switched branches** (425 branches) | 49.67 | 53.49 | +3.82 |
| **Non-switched branches** (284 branches) | 49.16 | 51.14 | +1.98 |
| **Net effect (DiD)** | — | — | **+1.81** |

Both groups improved after week 15, but switched branches improved nearly twice as much.

### 4. Analysis Method
This analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, which compares the change in service scores for switched branches against the change for non-switched branches. This controls for:
- Seasonal or system-wide trends affecting all branches
- Fixed differences between branches (e.g., location, size, typical traffic)
- Weekly variations in staffing and visitor volume

---

## **Caveats & Considerations**

1. **Pre-existing differences**: Treatment and control branches had some score differences in early weeks (6-10), but these gaps had closed by weeks 12-14, just before the switch. This suggests the groups were on similar trajectories when the intervention began.

2. **Time horizon**: We have 8 weeks of post-switch data. The growing effect over this period is encouraging, but longer-term monitoring would confirm whether benefits persist or accelerate.

3. **Controls included**: The analysis accounts for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume, confirming the effect is not simply due to branches being busier or better-staffed.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to remaining branches

2. **Monitor implementation**: Track service scores at newly switched branches to confirm they experience similar gains

3. **Document the procedure**: Capture the specific practices that make the new procedure effective for training purposes

4. **Long-term evaluation**: Re-assess in 6-12 months to confirm effects persist and identify any branches that may need additional support

5. **Consider cost-benefit**: If the new procedure requires additional resources, weigh the 1.8-point service improvement against implementation costs
