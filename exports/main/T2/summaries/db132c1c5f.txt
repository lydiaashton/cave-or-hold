# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, roll out the new procedure to all branches.** The new shelving and stock rotation procedure increased service scores by approximately **1.9 points (3.8% improvement)**. This effect is statistically significant and consistent across all post-implementation weeks.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated improvement | **+1.94 points** |
| 95% Confidence interval | +1.40 to +2.48 points |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 |
| Effect size | 0.37 standard deviations (moderate) |

### 2. What the Data Shows
- **425 branches** adopted the new procedure; **284 branches** kept the old procedure (control group)
- Before the switch (weeks 6-14), both groups had similar average scores (~51 points)
- After the switch (weeks 15-22):
  - Switched branches **improved by 1.1 points** (from 51.6 to 52.7)
  - Control branches **declined by 0.8 points** (from 51.2 to 50.4)
  - The **net difference of 1.9 points** is attributable to the new procedure

### 3. Robustness
The finding holds under multiple analytical approaches:
- Controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume: **+1.93 points**
- Branch-level analysis (averaging within branches first): **+1.94 points**
- Adjusting for pre-existing trends: **+1.91 points**
- The effect is **consistent across all 8 post-switch weeks** (ranging from +2.1 to +2.7 points weekly gap)

### 4. Important Caveat
Branches that switched had slightly better pre-implementation trends than control branches. However, statistically adjusting for this difference does not meaningfully change the estimated effect, giving us confidence in the result.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** to remaining 284 branches
2. **Monitor implementation** for the first 8-12 weeks to ensure effects replicate at scale
3. **Document the procedure** thoroughly to ensure consistent adoption across all branches
4. **Consider cost-benefit analysis** – the 3.8% service score improvement should be weighed against any additional operational costs of the new procedure
5. **Continue tracking** service scores post-rollout to confirm sustained improvement

## **Analysis Notes**

- **Method**: Difference-in-differences regression with branch-clustered standard errors
- **Data**: 12,053 weekly observations from 709 branches (weeks 6-22)
- **Switch date**: Week 15
- **Software**: Python with statsmodels

The evidence strongly supports expanding the new procedure cooperative-wide.
