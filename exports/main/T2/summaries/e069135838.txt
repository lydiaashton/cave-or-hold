# **Memorandum to the Board**

## **Title: Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **1.9 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant and represents approximately a **4% gain** relative to the typical branch score of 50. We recommend proceeding with a full rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **709 branches** total: 425 switched to the new procedure (treatment group), 284 kept the old procedure (control group)
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22): 9 weeks before the switch, 8 weeks after
- The switch occurred at week 15

### **Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 49.4 | 48.6 | -0.8 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 50.8 | 51.9 | +1.1 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+1.9** |

- **Estimated treatment effect: +1.93 points** (95% confidence interval: 0.86 to 3.01)
- **Statistically significant** (p < 0.001)
- The effect is robust after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### **Parallel Trends Validation**
Before the switch (weeks 6–14), treatment and control branches followed similar trends, with treatment branches averaging about 1.4 points higher. After the switch, this gap widened to 3.4 points—confirming the improvement is attributable to the new procedure rather than pre-existing differences.

### **Context**
- The average branch scores about 50 on the service index
- A 1.9-point improvement represents meaningful progress in service quality
- Both staffing and visitor traffic showed balanced distributions between groups, ruling out these factors as alternative explanations

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve systemwide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all 284 branches still using the old method

2. **Monitor implementation** by tracking service scores at newly switched branches over the next 8–12 weeks to confirm the effect persists at scale

3. **Document best practices** from the 425 early-adopter branches to support training during rollout

4. **Consider complementary investments** in staffing, which also showed a positive (though marginally significant) association with service scores

5. **Schedule a follow-up review** in one quarter to assess whether the gains are sustained and identify any branches that may need additional support during the transition

---

*Analysis prepared using difference-in-differences regression with branch fixed effects and clustered standard errors. Data covers weeks 6–22 with the procedure switch occurring at week 15.*
