# **Evaluation of New Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **2.2 points** (approximately **4% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and robust across multiple analytical checks. We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **150 branches** total: 90 switched to the new procedure, 60 kept the old procedure (control group)
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring in week 15
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in switched branches against changes in control branches

### **Main Results**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Treatment effect | **+2.20 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | 1.03 to 3.38 points |
| Statistical significance | p = 0.0003 (highly significant) |
| Relative improvement | ~4.4% above baseline |

### **Supporting Evidence**
1. **Parallel trends validated**: Before the switch, switched and control branches showed no significant difference in score trends (p = 0.88), supporting the validity of our comparison.

2. **Consistent effect**: The improvement was observed across all post-switch weeks, with effects ranging from +0.95 to +3.24 points week-to-week.

3. **Robust to controls**: The result holds even after accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume, which also affect service scores.

4. **Robustness check**: Excluding the transition week (week 15) still yields a significant effect of +2.05 points (p = 0.001).

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to remaining branches, given the clear positive effect.

2. **Monitor implementation**: Track service scores at newly converted branches to ensure they achieve similar gains.

3. **Investigate the fade pattern**: The effect appears strongest in weeks 15–19 and modestly smaller in weeks 20–22. This could reflect:
   - A learning curve where initial enthusiasm fades
   - Normal variation in a relatively short study period
   - Seasonal factors unrelated to the procedure
   
   Continued monitoring will clarify whether the effect stabilizes or diminishes over time.

4. **Consider cost-benefit analysis**: If the new procedure requires additional staff time or resources, compare those costs against the 4% service score improvement to confirm net benefit.

5. **Share best practices**: Document the specific practices used by high-performing switched branches to help other branches maximize the benefit.

---

## **Methodology Note**

This analysis uses cluster-robust standard errors (clustered at the branch level) to account for repeated observations of the same branches over time. The model controls for staffing index and visits index to isolate the effect of the procedure change from other weekly variations.
