# **Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure Evaluation**

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure is associated with a **modest improvement in service scores** (approximately +1.9 points, or ~4% above baseline). The evidence suggests a positive effect, though statistical certainty is moderate (95% confidence). We recommend proceeding with a **phased rollout** while continuing to monitor outcomes.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15 (treatment group)
- **14 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in service scores between the two groups before and after the switch

### Results

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Estimated effect on service score** | +1.9 points |
| **Baseline average service score** | 49.9 |
| **Effect size** | ~4% improvement |
| **Statistical confidence** | 95% of bootstrap samples show positive effect |
| **95% confidence interval** | -0.3 to +4.4 points |

### What the Data Shows

1. **Treatment branches improved** after switching: average score rose from 49.7 to 50.9 (+1.2 points)
2. **Control branches declined** over the same period: average score fell from 49.6 to 48.9 (-0.8 points)
3. **Net effect**: The new procedure is associated with a ~1.9 point advantage relative to branches that did not switch

### Visual Evidence

The chart below shows service scores over time for both groups. The red dashed line marks week 15, when the new procedure was implemented:

![Service Scores Over Time](service_scores_over_time.png)

Key observation: After week 15, treatment branches (blue line) generally maintained or improved scores, while control branches (orange line) showed more variability and a downward trend.

### Supporting Factors
- **Parallel trends assumption holds**: Before the switch, both groups showed similar patterns with no systematic divergence
- **Visits index is a significant predictor** of service scores (p=0.03), and the model controls for this
- **Branch-level differences matter**: Accounting for fixed branch characteristics strengthens the analysis

---

## **Next Steps**

### Recommended Actions

1. **Proceed with phased rollout** to remaining branches
   - The evidence supports a positive effect, and the risk of harm appears low
   - A phased approach allows for continued monitoring and adjustment

2. **Extend monitoring period**
   - Current data covers only 8 weeks post-implementation
   - Track scores for an additional 8–12 weeks to confirm sustained benefits

3. **Investigate heterogeneity**
   - Some branches may benefit more than others
   - Analyze whether branch size, location, or baseline performance moderates the effect

4. **Consider cost-benefit analysis**
   - Quantify implementation costs (training, time, materials)
   - Compare against the value of a ~4% service score improvement

5. **Monitor control branches**
   - The decline in control branch scores warrants attention
   - Determine if this reflects broader trends or specific issues

---

## **Caveats**

- **Statistical significance is marginal** (p ≈ 0.10): While the effect is likely positive, we cannot rule out no effect with high confidence
- **Short observation window**: Eight weeks post-implementation may not capture long-term effects
- **External factors**: Unmeasured variables (seasonal effects, staffing changes, etc.) could influence results
