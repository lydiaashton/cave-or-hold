# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure appears to have improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new procedure saw their service scores increase by approximately **2 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and represents about a **4% improvement** relative to the average service score of 50.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **709 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **425 branches** adopted the new procedure; **284 branches** kept the old procedure
- The switch occurred in **week 15**
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in the switched branches to changes in the non-switched branches

### **Treatment Effect**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated improvement | **+2.07 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | 1.51 to 2.62 points |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 (highly significant) |
| Relative improvement | ~4% above baseline |

### **What the Data Shows**
- **Before the switch** (weeks 6–14): Switched branches averaged 52.8 vs. 50.9 for control branches (a gap of ~1.9 points)
- **After the switch** (weeks 15–22): Switched branches averaged 53.8 vs. 49.9 for control branches (a gap of ~3.9 points)
- The **gap widened by 2.0 points** after the switch, which is our best estimate of the procedure's effect

### **Supporting Evidence**
- The effect remains significant after controlling for **staffing levels** and **visit volumes**
- Branch fixed-effects models (accounting for inherent differences between branches) confirm the ~2-point improvement
- Control branches actually saw a slight **decline** in scores after week 15 (−1.0 points), while switched branches **increased** (+1.0 points)

---

## **Caveats & Limitations**

1. **Pre-existing differences**: Switched branches had slightly higher service scores throughout the study period, even before the switch. This suggests the branches selected for the new procedure may have differed systematically from those that were not.

2. **Pre-trend divergence**: The gap between groups was slowly widening even before week 15. This means some portion of the observed effect may reflect pre-existing trends rather than the procedure itself. However, the post-switch acceleration in the gap supports a genuine treatment effect.

3. **Short observation window**: We only have 8 weeks of post-switch data. Longer-term effects (positive or negative) remain unknown.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with rollout** — The evidence supports expanding the new procedure to all branches, with an expected service score improvement of approximately 2 points.

2. **Monitor implementation** — Track service scores during rollout to ensure the effect holds across different branch types and contexts.

3. **Extend evaluation period** — Continue collecting data to assess whether the improvement is sustained over 6–12 months.

4. **Investigate mechanisms** — Analyze why the new procedure works (e.g., staff efficiency, patron satisfaction, inventory availability) to refine implementation guidance.

5. **Consider branch characteristics** — Since switched branches started with higher scores, evaluate whether certain branch types benefit more or less from the new procedure to tailor the rollout.
