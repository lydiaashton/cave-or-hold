# **Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure Evaluation**

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure is associated with a **2.6-point improvement in service scores** (on a scale where the average branch scores about 50). This represents approximately a **5% improvement** in service quality. The evidence is **suggestive but not definitive**—the result is marginally statistically significant (p=0.09) and the 95% confidence interval ranges from -0.4 to +5.7 points. We recommend proceeding with a **phased rollout** while continuing to monitor results.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure (treatment group)
- **14 branches** kept the old procedure (control group)
- Data covers **17 weeks** (weeks 6-22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach to isolate the procedure's effect

### **Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Control branches (old procedure) | 50.1 | 51.1 | +0.9 |
| Switched branches (new procedure) | 48.6 | 52.1 | +3.5 |
| **Difference-in-Differences Effect** | | | **+2.6** |

### **Statistical Significance**
- **Treatment effect:** +2.62 points
- **p-value:** 0.09 (significant at 10% level, not at 5% level)
- **95% Confidence Interval:** -0.42 to +5.67 points

### **Additional Insights**
- The improvement **cannot be explained by changes in staffing or visit volumes**—the effect persists after controlling for these factors
- Switched branches started with **lower baseline scores** (48.6 vs 50.1) but showed **stronger improvement**
- The trend graph shows the treatment group's scores rising more steeply after week 15 compared to controls

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with cautious rollout** to remaining branches, prioritizing those with lower current service scores (where the benefit appears largest)

2. **Extend the evaluation period**—8 weeks post-switch may be too short to capture the full effect. Continue monitoring for another 8-12 weeks to confirm the trend

3. **Document implementation details** at each branch to identify best practices that may amplify the positive effect

4. **Set a decision checkpoint** at week 30-34: if the effect remains positive and the confidence interval excludes zero, proceed with full cooperative-wide adoption

5. **Consider a cost-benefit analysis** once full rollout costs are known, weighing the ~5% service improvement against implementation expenses

---

## **Technical Note**

Analysis used difference-in-differences regression with standard errors clustered at the branch level to account for repeated observations. Results are robust to controlling for staffing levels and visit volumes.
