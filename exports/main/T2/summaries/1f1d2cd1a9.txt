## **Summary for the Board: New Branch Procedure Evaluation**

### **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and rotation procedure is associated with a **modest improvement in service scores** (approximately 2 points, or 4% relative to the average score of 50.7). However, this effect is **not statistically significant** at conventional levels when accounting for the panel structure of the data. The evidence suggests a positive direction but does not provide strong statistical confirmation that the new procedure caused the improvement.

---

### **Key Findings**

**Study Design**
- 34 branches tracked over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- 20 branches adopted the new procedure; 14 branches kept the old procedure
- Switch occurred at week 15
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in treatment branches against control branches

**Results**
| Metric | Treatment Branches (New) | Control Branches (Old) |
|--------|-------------------------|------------------------|
| Pre-switch average score | 52.8 | 47.9 |
| Post-switch average score | 53.5 | 46.7 |
| **Change** | **+0.70** | **-1.20** |

- **Estimated treatment effect: +2.03 points** (95% confidence interval: -0.47 to +4.53)
- 55% of treatment branches improved vs. 43% of control branches
- The control group's decline (-1.20 points) suggests external factors may have negatively affected service scores during this period; the new procedure appears to have buffered treatment branches against this decline

**Statistical Confidence**
- The p-value is 0.11, meaning there is an 11% probability of observing this result by chance even if the procedure had no real effect
- This does not meet the conventional 5% threshold for statistical significance
- The confidence interval includes zero, so we cannot rule out no effect

**Parallel Trends Check**
- Pre-switch trends between treatment and control groups were reasonably stable, supporting the validity of the comparison

### **Next Steps**

1. **Continue monitoring**: Extend the observation period to gather more post-switch data. Eight weeks may be insufficient to detect a stable effect, especially if branches needed time to fully implement the new procedure.

2. **Consider a phased rollout**: Given the positive (though not statistically confirmed) direction, the cooperative could proceed with a gradual expansion while continuing to collect data. This balances potential benefits against implementation costs.

3. **Investigate heterogeneity**: Some treatment branches improved substantially (+6 points) while others declined (-7 points). Understanding which branches benefit most could inform targeted implementation.

4. **Monitor implementation fidelity**: Ensure branches are correctly following the new procedure. Variation in implementation quality could explain mixed results.

5. **Re-evaluate in 3–6 months**: With additional data, statistical power will increase, allowing for a more definitive conclusion.

---

### **Recommendation**

The board may wish to **proceed cautiously with broader rollout** while committing to continued evaluation. The evidence points in a positive direction, and the new procedure appears to have protected adopting branches from the decline experienced by non-adopting branches. However, the statistical uncertainty warrants ongoing monitoring before making a final, system-wide commitment.
