**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Recommend proceeding with rollout of the new procedure.** Branches that adopted the new stock rotation and shelving procedure showed a statistically significant improvement in service scores compared to branches that kept the old procedure. The new procedure is associated with an average **1.9-point increase** in service scores (approximately 4% improvement), after accounting for other factors affecting all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **709 branches** total: 425 adopted the new procedure, 284 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in the treatment group to changes in the control group

### Results

| Metric | Control Group (Old Procedure) | Treatment Group (New Procedure) |
|--------|------------------------------|--------------------------------|
| Before switch (weeks 6–14) | 49.2 | 49.7 |
| After switch (weeks 15–22) | 52.4 | 54.8 |
| **Change** | **+3.2** | **+5.0** |

**Attributable Effect:** +1.86 points (treatment group improvement minus control group improvement)

### Statistical Confidence
- **Highly statistically significant** (p < 0.001)
- **95% confidence interval:** +1.3 to +2.4 points
- Results hold after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### Practical Significance
- The average branch service score is approximately 50 points
- A 1.9-point improvement represents roughly a **4% increase** in service quality
- Both groups improved after week 15 (possibly due to seasonal factors), but the new procedure provided an additional boost

---

## **Important Considerations**

1. **Pre-existing trends:** In the weeks immediately before the switch (weeks 12–14), branches that would adopt the new procedure showed slightly stronger performance. This suggests some branches may have been preparing for the change or had other advantages. Our analysis accounts for baseline differences, but the true effect could be slightly smaller than estimated.

2. **Observational study:** Branches were not randomly assigned to the new procedure. While our statistical methods control for observable differences, unmeasured factors could influence results.

3. **Consistency:** The treatment effect was sustained across all 8 post-switch weeks, suggesting the improvement is durable rather than a temporary novelty effect.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to remaining branches, with expected service score improvements of 1.5–2.5 points per branch.

2. **Monitor implementation** during rollout to ensure consistent adoption and identify any branches that may need additional support.

3. **Track outcomes** for 8–12 weeks after full implementation to confirm the effect persists at scale.

4. **Consider complementary improvements:** Since staffing and visitor volume also correlate with service scores (both significant at p < 0.001), evaluate whether additional resources could amplify the procedure's benefits.

5. **Document best practices** from high-performing branches to support training during rollout.
