# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure shows a **modest positive effect** on service scores (+1.7 points, or ~3% improvement), but this result is **not statistically significant**. We cannot confidently conclude that the new procedure improves service quality based on the current data. The board should **proceed cautiously** with any system-wide rollout and consider gathering additional evidence before making a final decision.

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Treatment Effect Estimate**
- Branches that adopted the new procedure experienced service scores approximately **1.7 points higher** than what would have been expected based on trends in non-switching branches
- However, the **95% confidence interval ranges from -0.5 to +3.9 points**, meaning the true effect could be slightly negative or moderately positive
- **P-value = 0.13**, which exceeds the conventional threshold for statistical significance (0.05)

### **2. What Actually Happened**
| Group | Pre-Switch Average | Post-Switch Average | Change |
|-------|-------------------|---------------------|--------|
| Switched branches (20) | 50.5 | 49.9 | **-0.6** |
| Non-switched branches (14) | 52.3 | 49.9 | **-2.4** |

Both groups saw service scores **decline after week 15**, but switching branches declined **less** than non-switching branches. The new procedure may have helped branches **maintain** service levels during a period when scores were falling system-wide.

### **3. Data Quality & Validity**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- **Parallel trends assumption appears reasonable**: Before the switch, both groups showed similar week-to-week fluctuations without systematic divergence
- **Staffing matters**: Higher staffing levels are significantly associated with better service scores (+1.0 points per unit), suggesting operational factors influence outcomes

### **4. Practical Significance**
- If the 1.7-point effect is real, it represents about a **3% improvement** on the average service score of 50.6
- Post-switch effects varied considerably by week (ranging from -3.4 to +3.0 points), suggesting the impact may depend on other factors not captured in this analysis
