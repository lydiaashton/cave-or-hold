# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Contrary to the expectation that the change would make no difference, branches that adopted the new procedure showed a statistically significant increase in service scores of approximately **2 points** (about 4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. The evidence strongly supports rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **150 branches** tracked over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** switched to the new procedure at week 15 (treatment group)
- **60 branches** kept the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in treatment vs. control branches before and after the switch

### **Results**

| Period | Treatment Branches | Control Branches | Gap |
|--------|-------------------|------------------|-----|
| **Before switch** (weeks 6–14) | 53.31 | 52.28 | 1.03 |
| **After switch** (weeks 15–22) | 52.92 | 49.91 | 3.01 |

- **Treatment effect: +1.98 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.22 to 2.74)
- **Statistically significant** (p < 0.001)
- **Pre-trends were parallel**: No significant difference in trends between groups before the switch (p = 0.27), validating the comparison

### **Additional Observations**
- The improvement in service scores was **not accompanied by changes** in staffing levels or visit volumes, suggesting the new procedure improved service quality without requiring additional resources or affecting patron traffic
- The effect was consistent across the post-switch period, with the treatment-control gap widening from ~1 point before to ~3 points after

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all 60 branches currently using the old method

2. **Monitor implementation** over the next 8–12 weeks to confirm the effect persists at scale

3. **Document the new procedure** in detail to ensure consistent adoption across all branches

4. **Consider investigating** why the new procedure improved scores (e.g., faster restocking, better organization, reduced staff time on shelving) to identify best practices

5. **Continue tracking** service scores, staffing, and visits to detect any longer-term effects or unintended consequences

---

## **Technical Note**

This analysis controls for branch-specific characteristics (e.g., location, size) and week-specific factors (e.g., holidays, seasonal patterns). The estimated effect isolates the impact of the procedure change itself.
