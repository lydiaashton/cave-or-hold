# **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure shows a **positive but statistically inconclusive effect** on service scores. Switched branches improved by approximately **2 points** on average compared to non-switched branches, but this result is not statistically significant at conventional levels (p=0.11). The weekly effects have been highly volatile, with some weeks showing significant improvements and others showing significant declines. **We recommend continuing to monitor the switched branches for at least 8 more weeks before making a system-wide rollout decision.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Service scores average approximately 50 points (higher is better)

### **Main Result: Difference-in-Differences Estimate**
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 47.51 | 48.77 | +1.26 |
| Non-switched branches | 51.23 | 50.55 | −0.68 |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | | | **+1.94 points** |

- **95% Confidence Interval**: −0.45 to +4.33 points
- **P-value**: 0.112 (not statistically significant at the 5% level)

### **Weekly Pattern After Switch**
The effects have been **highly variable week-to-week**:

| Week | Effect | Significant? |
|------|--------|--------------|
| 15 | −4.03 | Yes (negative) |
| 16 | +1.08 | No |
| 17 | +2.65 | Yes (positive) |
| 18 | −5.03 | Yes (negative) |
| 19 | +6.21 | Yes (positive) |
| 20–22 | +0.8 to +1.0 | No |

This volatility suggests the branches may still be in an **adjustment period**, with staff learning the new procedures.

### **Pre-Trends Check**
✓ **Parallel trends assumption holds**: Before the switch (weeks 6–14), there was no statistically significant difference in trends between switched and non-switched branches. This validates our comparison approach.

### **Additional Factors**
- **Staffing levels** are positively associated with service scores (p=0.018), but controlling for staffing does not change the main result.
- Switched branches started with lower baseline scores (47.5 vs. 51.2), but this is accounted for in the analysis.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Do not roll out system-wide yet.** The evidence is promising but not conclusive. A 2-point improvement would be meaningful (~4% gain), but we cannot rule out that the true effect is zero or even slightly negative.

2. **Extend the evaluation period by 8–12 weeks.** The high week-to-week volatility suggests branches are still adjusting. More data will provide a clearer picture of the steady-state effect.

3. **Investigate the volatility.** Interview branch managers at switched locations to understand:
   - What caused the sharp drops in weeks 15 and 18?
   - What enabled the strong performance in weeks 17 and 19?
   - Are there implementation inconsistencies we can address?

4. **Monitor staffing levels.** Since staffing is associated with service scores, ensure switched branches have adequate support during the transition.

5. **Re-evaluate in Q1.** With additional data, we can provide a more definitive recommendation on system-wide adoption.
