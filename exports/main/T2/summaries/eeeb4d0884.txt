# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.2 points** (about 4.5%) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant and supports rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **34 branches** total: 20 adopted the new procedure (treatment group), 14 kept the old procedure (control group)
- **17 weeks of data**: Weeks 6–14 before the switch, Weeks 15–22 after the switch
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in the treatment group to changes in the control group

### Results
| Group | Pre-Switch Average | Post-Switch Average | Change |
|-------|-------------------|---------------------|--------|
| Treatment (switched) | 46.7 | 48.5 | +1.7 |
| Control (no switch) | 50.3 | 49.8 | -0.4 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+2.2** |

- **Effect size**: +2.2 points on the service score index (95% confidence interval: 0.5 to 3.9 points)
- **Statistical significance**: p = 0.012 (significant at the 5% level)
- **Relative improvement**: Approximately 4.5% increase relative to baseline scores

### Validation Checks
- **Parallel trends assumption holds**: Before the switch, both groups showed similar week-to-week patterns, supporting the validity of the comparison
- **Results are robust**: The effect persists after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume, suggesting the improvement is attributable to the procedure change itself

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all remaining branches

2. **Monitor implementation**: Track service scores at newly converted branches to ensure the effect replicates at scale

3. **Document best practices**: Capture specific elements of the new procedure that drove improvement to support training and consistent implementation

4. **Continue evaluation**: Consider a follow-up analysis after 6–12 months to assess whether the improvement is sustained over time
