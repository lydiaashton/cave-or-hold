# **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.0 points** (a **3.8% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and consistent across multiple analytical approaches. We recommend proceeding with a full rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect
- **Estimated improvement:** +1.97 points on the service score index
- **Statistical significance:** Highly significant (p < 0.001)
- **95% confidence interval:** 1.62 to 2.31 points
- **Relative improvement:** ~3.8% above the average branch score of 51.9

### 2. Study Design & Validity
This analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach comparing:
- **425 branches** that adopted the new procedure (treatment group)
- **284 branches** that kept the old procedure (control group)
- **17 weeks** of data (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15

The analysis controls for:
- Branch-specific characteristics (e.g., location, size, baseline performance)
- Week-specific factors (e.g., seasonal effects, system-wide changes)
- Staffing levels and visitor volume

### 3. Parallel Trends Assumption Verified
An event study analysis confirms that treatment and control branches had similar trends before the switch:
- Pre-switch coefficients were small and statistically indistinguishable from zero
- This supports the validity of our causal interpretation

### 4. Effect Persistence
The improvement was not temporary:
- Effect appeared immediately in week 1 post-switch (+1.2 points)
- Effect grew over time, reaching +1.6 to +2.1 points by weeks 4–8
- No evidence of the effect fading during the observation period

### 5. Branch-Level Variation
- **66% of switched branches** (281 of 425) showed score improvements
- **34% showed declines**, but the average effect across all switched branches remains positive
- Control branches, on average, saw a slight decline (-0.6 points), suggesting the new procedure also helped branches avoid score deterioration

### 6. Additional Insights
- **Staffing and visits matter:** Both staffing index and visits index positively correlate with service scores (each 1-point increase associates with ~0.5 point score increase)
- The treatment effect remains robust after accounting for these factors, indicating the procedure itself drives improvement
