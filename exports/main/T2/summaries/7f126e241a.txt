# **Board Summary: Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw their service scores increase by approximately **2.1 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant and represents a meaningful gain on the service score index (where the average branch scores about 50).

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Measurable Improvement in Service Scores**
- **Treatment effect**: +2.1 points (95% confidence interval: 1.0 to 3.3 points)
- **Statistical significance**: p < 0.001 (highly significant)
- Switched branches went from an average of 52.6 before the change to 53.9 after
- Non-switched branches declined slightly from 52.0 to 51.2 over the same period

### **2. The Analysis Is Reliable**
- **Study design**: 150 branches total (90 switched, 60 did not) over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- **Method**: Difference-in-differences analysis comparing switched vs. non-switched branches before and after week 15
- **Parallel trends validated**: Pre-switch trends were statistically similar between groups (p = 0.93), supporting the validity of the comparison
- **Robust to controls**: The effect persists after accounting for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume

### **3. Consistent Positive Effects**
- The improvement was sustained across all 8 post-switch weeks
- Weekly treatment effects ranged from +3.2 to +4.7 points, with no week showing a negative effect
- The benefit did not fade over time during the observation period

### **4. Context on Effect Size**
- A 2.1-point gain on a ~50-point scale represents approximately a **4% improvement**
- This is comparable to the typical week-to-week variation in scores
- For perspective: the control branches experienced a slight decline (-0.9 points) during the same period, making the relative advantage of switching even larger

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended Actions**
1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches
2. **Monitor implementation** to ensure the new procedure is adopted consistently across branches
3. **Track service scores** for the next 8–12 weeks post-rollout to confirm the effect persists at scale

### **Additional Considerations**
- **Training**: Ensure staff at newly switching branches receive adequate training on the new procedure
- **Timeline**: Consider a phased rollout to manage operational capacity
- **Further analysis**: Investigate whether the effect varies by branch size, location, or baseline performance to optimize implementation

### **Caveats**
- The analysis covers 8 weeks post-switch; longer-term effects should be monitored
- Service scores are one metric; consider reviewing operational costs or staff satisfaction if those data are available
