# **Impact of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

Contrary to expectations, the new stock rotation and shelving procedure **improved service scores by approximately 1.8 points** (95% confidence interval: 0.6 to 3.0 points). This represents a **3.7% improvement** over baseline scores and is statistically significant (p = 0.003). The evidence supports rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15 (treatment group)
- **60 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis used a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in service scores between the two groups before and after the switch

### Results
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 50.41 | 49.81 | −0.60 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 48.88 | 50.12 | +1.24 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+1.84** |

- The treatment effect of **+1.84 points** is statistically significant (p = 0.003)
- Results are robust to controlling for staffing levels and visit volumes
- The effect represents approximately **0.20 standard deviations**—a modest but meaningful improvement

### Visual Evidence
The trend chart (saved as `service_scores_trend.png`) shows that treatment and control branches followed similar patterns before week 15, after which treatment branches pulled ahead.

---

## **Caveats**

- Some variation existed in the gap between groups during the early pre-period (weeks 6–10), though trends were stable in the weeks immediately before the switch
- Service scores naturally fluctuate week-to-week; the analysis accounts for this through cluster-robust standard errors
- The effect, while statistically significant, is modest in practical terms

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new procedure, given the positive impact on service scores

2. **Monitor implementation** at remaining branches to ensure the effect replicates at scale

3. **Investigate mechanisms**—consider studying why the new procedure improved scores (e.g., faster book location, better organization, staff efficiency) to reinforce best practices

4. **Track long-term effects**—continue monitoring service scores to ensure the improvement is sustained and does not diminish over time

5. **Consider cost-benefit analysis**—weigh the 1.8-point service score gain against any additional costs or training requirements of the new procedure
