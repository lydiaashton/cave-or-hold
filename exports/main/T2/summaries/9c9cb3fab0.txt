# **MEMORANDUM**

## **To:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors
## **From:** Data Analysis Team
## **Date:** November 15, 2026
## **Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Recommend the new procedure be rolled out to all branches.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure showed a statistically significant improvement in service scores of approximately **2.0 points** (about 4% relative improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This improvement is both meaningful and consistent across the majority of branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **709 branches** participated in the evaluation (425 switched to the new procedure; 284 kept the old procedure as a control group)
- Data collected over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22 of the year)
- The switch occurred at **week 15**, allowing 9 weeks of before/after comparison
- Analysis used a rigorous difference-in-differences approach, controlling for branch-specific characteristics and weekly trends

### **Service Score Impact**
| Metric | Switched Branches | Control Branches |
|--------|-------------------|------------------|
| Before switch (avg) | 51.5 | 51.0 |
| After switch (avg) | 53.7 | 51.2 |
| **Change** | **+2.3** | **+0.3** |

- **Net improvement attributable to new procedure: +2.0 points**
- **95% Confidence Interval:** 1.6 to 2.3 points
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001 (highly significant)

### **Branch-Level Results**
- **73%** of switched branches showed improvement vs. **53%** of control branches
- The effect was consistent—no evidence that the procedure works only for certain types of branches
- Effect size (Cohen's d = 0.54) indicates a **medium, practically meaningful impact**

### **Validation of Results**
- **Parallel trends confirmed:** Before the switch, both groups tracked closely together (difference of only 0.5 points on average)
- **Clean break at week 15:** The gap between groups widened immediately after the switch and remained elevated
- **No confounding factors:** Changes in staffing levels and visit volumes did not differ systematically between groups, suggesting the improvement reflects genuine service quality gains

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all remaining branches

2. **Develop implementation support:**
   - Create training materials based on practices from high-performing switched branches
   - Establish a transition timeline that minimizes disruption

3. **Monitor post-rollout performance:**
   - Continue tracking service scores for 8–12 weeks after full implementation
   - Identify any branches that underperform and provide targeted support

4. **Document best practices:**
   - Interview staff at branches with the largest improvements to capture learnings
   - Share successful approaches across the cooperative

---

## **Appendix: Technical Notes**

- Analysis controlled for branch fixed effects (accounting for inherent differences between branches) and week fixed effects (accounting for system-wide temporal trends)
- The improvement represents approximately 4% gain relative to the baseline service score of ~51 points
- Given the scale of the cooperative (709 branches), even modest per-branch improvements compound to meaningful system-wide service quality gains
