# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw their service scores increase by approximately **2.2 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant and represents about a **4% gain** in service quality. We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measurable Improvement in Service Scores
- **Treatment effect:** +2.19 points (95% confidence interval: 1.64 to 2.74)
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001 (highly significant)
- **Relative improvement:** ~4.2% above the system-wide average score of 51.8

### 2. What the Data Shows
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Branches with **new procedure** (425 branches) | 51.40 | 53.99 | +2.59 |
| Branches with **old procedure** (284 branches) | 50.45 | 50.85 | +0.40 |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | | **+2.19** |

The analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, which isolates the effect of the new procedure by comparing how much each group changed, rather than just comparing their absolute scores.

### 3. Robustness Checks
- The result holds when controlling for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume
- The result is slightly stronger (+2.39 points) when excluding week 15, which had unusual spikes across all branches
- The effect is consistent across most post-switch weeks

### 4. Scope of Analysis
- **709 branches** total in the cooperative
- **425 branches** adopted the new procedure (treatment group)
- **284 branches** kept the old procedure (control group)
- **17 weeks** of data (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15

## **Analytical Notes**

This analysis uses a quasi-experimental difference-in-differences design, which is the standard approach for evaluating policy changes when random assignment is not feasible. The method compares the change in outcomes for branches that adopted the new procedure against the change for branches that did not, isolating the effect of the procedure itself from other factors that might affect all branches (such as seasonal trends or system-wide initiatives).

The estimate is robust to:
- Cluster-robust standard errors (accounting for repeated observations of the same branches)
- Control variables for staffing and visitor volume
- Alternative specifications excluding anomalous weeks
