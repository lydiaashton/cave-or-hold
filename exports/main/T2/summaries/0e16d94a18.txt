# Library Cooperative Stock Rotation Procedure: Impact Analysis

## Bottom Line

**The new stock rotation and shelving procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.0 points (4% improvement)** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and consistent across different branch types. **We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

## Key Findings

### Study Design
- **709 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6-22)
- **425 branches** switched to the new procedure at week 15 (treatment group)
- **284 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach with branch and week fixed effects

### Main Results

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated treatment effect | **+2.05 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | [1.70, 2.40] |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 |
| Relative improvement | **+4.0%** over baseline |

### Supporting Evidence

1. **Effect persists with controls**: When accounting for staffing levels and visitor traffic, the effect remains at +2.03 points, confirming the result is not driven by other factors.

2. **Benefits all branch types**: The procedure helped both high-traffic branches (+2.50 points) and low-traffic branches (+1.62 points), though the effect was larger in busier locations.

3. **Effect grew over time**: Service score improvements increased in the weeks following the switch (from +0.3 points in week 15 to +1.8 points by week 22), suggesting branches improved as they gained experience with the new procedure.

4. **Pre-existing differences accounted for**: Treatment branches had slightly lower service scores before the switch. The analysis controls for these baseline differences, isolating the true effect of the procedure change.

---

## Next Steps

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout**: The evidence strongly supports extending the new procedure to all 284 branches currently using the old method.

2. **Implement with training support**: Since the effect grew over time, provide adequate training and support during the initial weeks of implementation to help branches realize the full benefit sooner.

3. **Monitor implementation**: Track service scores during rollout to ensure the expected ~2-point improvement is achieved across all branches.

4. **Prioritize high-traffic branches**: Consider prioritizing larger, busier branches in the rollout sequence, as they show the largest gains (+2.5 points).

5. **Document best practices**: Capture lessons learned from the 425 early-adopter branches to support smoother implementation at remaining locations.
