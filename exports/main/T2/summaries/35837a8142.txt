# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure shows a **positive but statistically inconclusive effect** on service scores. While switched branches improved by approximately 1.8 points (about 3-4% relative to the average score of 50), this improvement is not statistically distinguishable from zero given the available data. The board should consider **continuing the pilot with additional data collection** before making a system-wide rollout decision.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in switched branches to changes in non-switched branches

### **Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches (treatment) | 49.32 | 51.37 | +2.05 |
| Non-switched branches (control) | 50.87 | 51.11 | +0.24 |
| **Estimated effect (DiD)** | | | **+1.81** |

- **Point estimate:** The new procedure is associated with a **1.8-point increase** in service scores
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.13 (not significant at conventional levels)
- **95% confidence interval:** -0.5 to +4.2 points

### **Validity Checks**
- **Pre-trends test:** No evidence that switched and non-switched branches were on different trajectories before the change (p = 0.45). This supports the validity of the comparison.
- **Event study analysis:** No individual week shows a statistically significant effect, and the pattern does not suggest a clear break at the switch date.

### **Interpretation**
The data is consistent with three possibilities:
1. The new procedure has **no meaningful effect** on service scores
2. The new procedure produces a **modest improvement** (up to ~4 points)
3. The effect exists but **more data is needed** to detect it with confidence

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Continue the pilot** for at least 8–12 more weeks to increase statistical power. With more post-switch observations, we could detect smaller effects with greater confidence.

2. **Monitor implementation fidelity.** Ensure all switched branches are fully implementing the new procedure as designed. Variation in implementation could dilute measured effects.

3. **Consider subgroup analysis.** Examine whether certain branch types (e.g., by size, location, or baseline performance) benefit more from the new procedure.

4. **Cost-benefit assessment.** Even if the effect is real at ~1.8 points, the board should weigh this against any additional costs or operational burdens of the new procedure.

5. **Decision timeline:** Recommend revisiting this analysis after week 30–35 with the additional data before making a system-wide rollout decision.

Analysis accounts for branch-level and week-level factors, and uses cluster-robust standard errors to appropriately handle repeated observations per branch. The parallel trends assumption—the key requirement for this analysis—is supported by pre-period data.
