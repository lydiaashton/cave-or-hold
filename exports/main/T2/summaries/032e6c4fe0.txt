# **MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure shows a positive but statistically insignificant effect on service scores.** We cannot conclude with confidence that the new procedure improves service. **We do not recommend a full rollout at this time.** More data or a refined evaluation approach is needed before making a system-wide decision.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure at week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in service scores between the two groups before and after the switch

### **Results**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect on service score | **+2.3 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | **-0.7 to +5.2 points** |
| Statistical significance (p-value) | **0.135** (not significant) |

### **What This Means**
- The point estimate suggests switching branches improved scores by about 2.3 points (on a scale where average is ~50)
- **However**, the confidence interval includes zero, meaning we cannot rule out that the true effect is zero or even slightly negative
- The result is **not statistically significant** at conventional standards (p > 0.05)
- Pre-switch trends show some differences between groups that complicate interpretation

### **Context**
- Treatment branches averaged **52.8** post-switch vs. **46.6** for control branches
- But treatment branches also started higher before the switch (50.4 vs. 46.5), which the analysis accounts for
- Other factors (staffing, visit volumes) did not explain the observed patterns

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Do not proceed with full rollout** — The evidence is insufficient to justify system-wide implementation

2. **Continue monitoring** — Collect additional weeks of data to increase statistical power. With more observations, we may be able to detect a true effect if one exists

3. **Consider a randomized rollout** — For future changes, randomly assign branches to new procedures to strengthen causal inference

4. **Investigate implementation variation** — Some branches may have implemented the new procedure more effectively than others; qualitative follow-up could identify best practices

5. **Re-evaluate in 8–12 weeks** — With additional post-switch data, we can provide a more definitive assessment

---

## **Technical Appendix**

- **Method:** Difference-in-differences regression with branch-clustered standard errors
- **Controls:** Staffing index and visits index included
- **Parallel trends assumption:** Borderline (p=0.09), suggesting some pre-existing differences in trends between groups
- **Data:** 578 branch-week observations
