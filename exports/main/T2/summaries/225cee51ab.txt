# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure shows a **modest positive effect** on service scores, but the evidence is **not statistically conclusive**. Branches that adopted the new procedure improved their service scores by approximately **2 points** relative to branches that kept the old procedure. However, this improvement is not large enough to rule out chance variation with confidence. **We recommend a cautious, phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
- **34 branches** participated in the study (20 adopted the new procedure, 14 kept the old)
- Data collected over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- New procedure implemented at **week 15**
- Service scores range around 50 on average (higher is better)

### What the Data Shows

| Period | New Procedure Branches | Old Procedure Branches | Gap |
|--------|----------------------|----------------------|-----|
| **Before switch** (weeks 6–14) | 47.83 | 52.45 | -4.62 |
| **After switch** (weeks 15–22) | 49.14 | 51.90 | -2.76 |
| **Change** | +1.31 | -0.55 | **+1.85** |

- Branches using the new procedure **improved by 1.3 points** after adoption
- Branches using the old procedure **declined slightly by 0.6 points**
- The **net effect** of the new procedure is approximately **+2 points** on the service score index

### Statistical Confidence
- The estimated effect is **not statistically significant** at conventional levels (p ≈ 0.11–0.17)
- This means we cannot confidently distinguish the observed improvement from random variation
- The 95% confidence interval ranges from approximately **-0.5 to +4.6 points**

### Important Context
- Branches that adopted the new procedure **started at a disadvantage**, scoring about 4.6 points lower than control branches before the switch
- The new procedure appears to have helped these branches **partially catch up**, but they still lag behind control branches after adoption
- There is **no evidence the effect grows over time**—if anything, the effect appears slightly smaller in later weeks

---

## **Next Steps**

### Recommended Actions

1. **Proceed with a phased rollout** rather than immediate system-wide adoption
   - The direction of the effect is positive, but uncertainty remains high
   - A gradual rollout allows for continued learning and adjustment

2. **Extend the monitoring period**
   - Eight weeks of post-implementation data is relatively short
   - Continue tracking service scores for at least 3–6 more months to see if effects stabilize or grow

3. **Investigate why some branches started at a disadvantage**
   - The 20 branches that switched had systematically lower baseline scores
   - Understanding this selection pattern could inform which branches would benefit most from the new procedure

4. **Consider branch-level heterogeneity**
   - Some weeks showed larger effects than others (e.g., weeks 17 and 20 showed minimal gaps)
   - Analyze whether certain branch characteristics (size, location, staffing) predict better outcomes

5. **Set a decision threshold**
   - Define what magnitude of improvement would justify full rollout (e.g., 3+ points with statistical significance)
   - Re-evaluate after additional data is collected
