# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Contrary to the expectation that the change would make no difference, branches that adopted the new procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **1.8 points** (approximately 3.4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This result is statistically significant and robust across multiple analytical approaches.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **60 branches** kept the old procedure
- This natural experiment allows us to isolate the effect of the new procedure using a difference-in-differences approach

### **Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 54.2 | 52.9 | **-1.3** |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 51.2 | 51.6 | **+0.5** |

**Estimated effect of new procedure: +1.8 points** (95% confidence interval: +0.7 to +2.9; p=0.002)

### **Supporting Evidence**

1. **Parallel trends validated:** Before the switch, both groups showed similar week-to-week trends in service scores, confirming the comparison is valid.

2. **Results are robust:** The positive effect holds across multiple statistical models:
   - Basic comparison: +1.73 points
   - Controlling for staffing and visitor traffic: +1.76 points
   - With branch-specific fixed effects: +1.77 points

3. **Meaningful improvement:** A 1.8-point gain represents roughly a 3.4% improvement over the baseline average service score of 52.4.

4. **Control group decline:** Notably, branches keeping the old procedure saw their scores drop by 1.3 points after week 15, while switched branches avoided this decline and showed a slight gain.

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all remaining branches.

2. **Monitor implementation:** Track service scores during rollout to ensure the effect replicates at scale.

3. **Document best practices:** Work with the 90 early-adopter branches to capture any implementation insights that could smooth the transition for others.

4. **Consider complementary factors:** The analysis controlled for staffing levels and visitor traffic, both of which also affect service scores. Continue monitoring these alongside the procedure change.

5. **Timeline:** Given the clear positive effect, prioritize rollout in the next operational cycle to capture benefits across the entire cooperative.

## **Technical Note**

Analysis used a difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch) to account for repeated observations. The parallel trends assumption was formally tested and confirmed (p=0.69 for pre-trend differences). Results are consistent across specifications with and without branch fixed effects.
