# **Analysis of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**
## Impact on Service Scores

---

### **BOTTOM LINE**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new stock rotation and shelving procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **2.0 points** (approximately 4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and represents a meaningful improvement in branch performance.

### **KEY FINDINGS**

**1. Study Design**
- **150 branches** total: 90 switched to the new procedure, 60 kept the old procedure (control group)
- **17 weeks of data**: Weeks 6–14 (before switch) and Weeks 15–22 (after switch)
- Analysis used a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in the switched branches against changes in the control branches

**3. Statistical Confidence**
- **p-value: 0.001** (highly statistically significant)
- **95% Confidence Interval: 0.8 to 3.1 points**
- We can be confident the true effect is positive and meaningful

**4. Effect Size**
- Cohen's d = 0.56, which is considered a **moderate effect** in social science research
- On a scale where the average branch scores ~50, a 2-point gain represents roughly a **4% improvement**

**5. Validity Check: Parallel Trends**
- Before the switch (weeks 6–14), both groups showed similar trends in service scores
- This supports the conclusion that the observed improvement is due to the new procedure, not pre-existing differences
