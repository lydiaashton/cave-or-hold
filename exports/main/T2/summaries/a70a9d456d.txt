# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation procedure is associated with a **2.2-point improvement** in service scores (approximately 4-5% relative to baseline). However, this effect is **not statistically significant** at conventional levels. The data are consistent with both modest improvements and no effect at all. We **cannot confidently conclude** the new procedure improves service based on this evidence alone.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (Weeks 6-22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure in Week 15 (treatment group)
- **14 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses difference-in-differences methodology to isolate the procedure's effect

### **Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 47.4 | 49.6 | +2.3 |
| Non-switched branches | 50.7 | 50.8 | +0.0 |
| **Estimated effect** | | | **+2.2** |

### **Statistical Confidence**
- **Point estimate:** +2.22 points
- **95% confidence interval:** -0.57 to +5.02 points
- **P-value:** 0.12 (not significant at 5% level)

This means we cannot rule out that the observed improvement occurred by chance. The true effect could be zero, slightly negative, or as high as 5 points.

### **Important Caveats**

1. **Pre-existing differences:** The treatment and control groups showed different service score patterns even before the switch, which complicates causal interpretation.

2. **No effect on operations:** The new procedure did not significantly affect staffing levels or branch visits, suggesting any service score changes are not driven by these operational factors.

3. **Limited post-switch data:** Only 8 weeks of post-switch data are available; effects may evolve over time.

---

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended Actions**

1. **Continue monitoring:** Collect additional weeks of data to increase statistical power and observe whether effects strengthen or diminish over time.

2. **Consider phased rollout:** Given the positive (though uncertain) point estimate and no negative operational impacts, a **cautious expansion** to additional branches could be warranted while continuing evaluation.

3. **Improve evaluation design:** For future changes, consider:
   - Randomized assignment of branches to treatment/control
   - Longer pre-period to better establish baseline trends
   - More frequent measurement if feasible

4. **Investigate mechanisms:** Qualitative feedback from switched branches could reveal why some weeks show stronger effects than others.

### **Decision Framework**

| If the board prioritizes... | Recommendation |
|----------------------------|----------------|
| **Certainty before action** | Wait for more data; do not expand yet |
| **Potential upside with acceptable risk** | Proceed with phased rollout while monitoring |
| **Cost minimization** | The procedure appears operationally neutral; expansion risk is low |
