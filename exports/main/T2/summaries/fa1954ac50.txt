# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new shelving and rotation procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **2.0 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents approximately a **4% improvement** and is statistically significant (p < 0.001). We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. The New Procedure Had a Positive Effect
- **Treatment effect: +2.04 points** on the service score index
- 95% confidence interval: 1.5 to 2.6 points
- The effect is highly statistically significant (p < 0.001)

### 2. Comparison of Switched vs. Non-Switched Branches

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| **Switched branches** (treatment) | 52.4 | 52.6 | +0.2 |
| **Non-switched branches** (control) | 51.0 | 49.1 | -1.9 |

The control group's scores declined after week 15, while switched branches maintained their performance. Relative to what would have happened without the change, the new procedure added about 2 points.

### 3. Effect Was Consistent Across Branch Types
The improvement held across branches with different characteristics:

| Branch Type | Estimated Effect |
|-------------|------------------|
| Low staffing | +2.1 points |
| Medium staffing | +2.4 points |
| High staffing | +1.7 points |
| Low visits | +2.5 points |

All subgroups showed positive effects, suggesting the procedure works well in different operational contexts.

### 4. The Gap Widened After the Switch
Before the switch (weeks 6-14), switched branches scored about 1.5 points higher than non-switched branches on average. After the switch (weeks 15-22), this gap grew to 3.0-4.5 points, indicating the new procedure created additional separation in performance.
