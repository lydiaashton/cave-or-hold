## **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

### **Bottom Line**

The new procedure is associated with a **2.3-point improvement** in service scores (approximately 5% above baseline), but this result is **not statistically significant** at conventional levels. The data suggest a positive effect, but we cannot rule out that the observed improvement occurred by chance. The study may lack sufficient power to detect modest but meaningful improvements.

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. Estimated Effect**
- Branches that switched to the new procedure improved by **2.31 points** more than branches that kept the old procedure
- This represents approximately a **5% improvement** relative to the average baseline score of ~49 points
- 95% confidence interval: **-0.9 to +5.5 points**

**2. Statistical Significance**
- The result is **not statistically significant** (p = 0.16)
- This means we cannot confidently distinguish the observed effect from random variation
- However, the confidence interval includes effects as large as 5.5 points, which would be practically meaningful

**3. Study Limitations**
- **Underpowered design**: With 34 branches over 17 weeks, the study can only reliably detect effects of 4.6 points or larger. Smaller but meaningful improvements may go undetected.
- **Baseline differences**: Branches that switched already had somewhat higher service scores before the change (about 2 points higher on average), though trends before the switch were similar between groups.

**4. Consistency of Results**
- In every week after the switch, switching branches outperformed non-switching branches
- The post-switch gap ranged from 2.5 to 6.1 points, all favoring the new procedure
- No evidence of negative effects in any week

---

### **Next Steps**

1. **Continue monitoring**: Extend the observation period to increase statistical power. An additional 8-12 weeks of data would improve our ability to detect modest effects.

2. **Consider phased rollout**: Given the consistently positive (though not statistically significant) results and no evidence of harm, a cautious expansion to additional branches could be warranted while continuing to collect data.

3. **Investigate heterogeneity**: Analyze whether the procedure works better in certain types of branches (e.g., by size, location, or staffing levels) to identify where the new procedure delivers the most value.

4. **Cost-benefit analysis**: Even a 2-3 point improvement may justify rollout if the new procedure is not more costly than the old one. The board should weigh the modest service gains against implementation costs.

5. **Set clear success criteria**: Before full rollout, define what magnitude of improvement would justify the change (e.g., "at least 2 points sustained over 6 months").

### **Technical Note**

This analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing service score changes in switching branches to changes in non-switching branches before and after week 15. The model controls for staffing levels and visitor volume. Standard errors are robust to heteroskedasticity.
