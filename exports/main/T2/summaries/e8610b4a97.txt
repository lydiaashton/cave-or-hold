## Board Summary: New Shelving Procedure Impact Analysis

---

### **Title**
**Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

---

### **Bottom Line**
**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new procedure saw service scores increase by **2.16 points** (approximately 4%) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant and the benefit appears to grow over time. **We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

### **Key Findings**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Treatment Effect** | +2.16 service score points |
| **95% Confidence Interval** | +1.60 to +2.73 points |
| **Statistical Significance** | p < 0.001 (highly significant) |
| **Relative Improvement** | ~4% above baseline |
| **Branches Analyzed** | 709 total (425 switched, 284 control) |
| **Observation Period** | Weeks 6–22 (8 weeks before, 8 weeks after switch) |

**What the numbers mean:**
- The average branch scores about 53 on the service index. The new procedure added roughly 2 points to this score.
- We are 95% confident the true effect lies between 1.6 and 2.7 points.
- The result is not due to random chance (less than 1 in 1,000 probability).

**Supporting evidence:**
1. **Parallel pre-trends**: Before week 15, treatment and control branches had nearly identical service score trajectories, validating our comparison.
2. **Growing benefit**: The improvement started at ~1 point in week 15 and grew to ~3.3 points by week 22, suggesting branches improve with experience using the new procedure.
3. **Robust to controls**: The result holds even after accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume.

---

### **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches.

2. **Develop implementation timeline** — consider phasing the rollout to allow for training and support, as the data suggests benefits accumulate over time.

3. **Monitor post-rollout performance** — track service scores for 8–12 weeks after each branch switches to confirm the expected ~2-point improvement.

4. **Document best practices** — interview high-performing switched branches to identify implementation factors that maximize the benefit.

5. **Consider cost-benefit analysis** — quantify the operational costs of the new procedure against the service score gains to confirm net positive value.
