# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, roll out the new procedure.** Branches that switched to the new shelving and rotation procedure showed a statistically significant improvement in service scores. The new procedure increased scores by an average of **2.0 points** (approximately **4% improvement**), with high confidence that this effect is real and not due to chance.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measurable Improvement in Service Quality
| Group | Pre-Switch Average | Post-Switch Average | Change |
|-------|-------------------|---------------------|--------|
| Switched branches (90 branches) | 51.1 | 53.6 | +2.5 |
| Non-switched branches (60 branches) | 52.3 | 52.8 | +0.5 |

Using a rigorous difference-in-differences analysis (which accounts for underlying trends and branch-specific characteristics), the **net effect of the new procedure is +2.03 points** on the service score index.

### 2. Statistically Robust Result
- **95% Confidence Interval**: 1.15 to 2.90 points
- **Statistical Significance**: p < 0.001 (less than 0.1% probability this is due to random chance)
- The result holds after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume, and after accounting for fixed differences between individual branches.

### 3. Valid Comparison
- Pre-switch trends were parallel between the two groups (p = 0.84), meaning switched and non-switched branches were on similar trajectories before the change. This validates the comparison.
- 150 branches total: 90 adopted the new procedure, 60 kept the old one.
- Data covers 17 weeks (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15.

### 4. Practical Significance
Given that the average branch service score is approximately 50–52, a 2-point improvement represents a meaningful gain in service quality. The effect is consistent across the post-switch period and not driven by outliers.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches.

2. **Monitor implementation** during the rollout to ensure branches receive adequate training and support for the new procedure.

3. **Continue tracking service scores** for at least 8–12 weeks post-rollout to confirm the effect persists at scale.

4. **Document the new procedure** as a best practice, including any branch-level adaptations that enhanced results.

5. **Consider cost-benefit analysis** if the new procedure involves additional resources, though the service improvement alone appears to justify adoption.
