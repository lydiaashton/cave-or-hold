# **Board Summary: New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.0 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and represents a meaningful improvement. Contrary to the initial expectation of no difference, the evidence supports rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect
- **Switched branches** improved by 2.51 points on average (from 51.1 to 53.6)
- **Control branches** improved by only 0.51 points on average (from 52.3 to 52.8)
- **Net effect of the new procedure: +2.0 points** (p < 0.01)

### 2. Effect Size in Context
- The 2-point gain represents a **3.8% improvement** over the average service score of 52.4
- This equals **0.20 standard deviations**—a modest but practically meaningful effect
- **76.7% of switched branches** saw score improvements, compared to only 51.7% of control branches

### 3. Validity of the Analysis
- **Parallel trends confirmed**: Before the switch (weeks 6–14), switched and control branches showed no statistically significant difference in their score trajectories (p = 0.94). This supports the conclusion that the post-switch divergence was caused by the procedure change.
- **Robust to controls**: The effect remains significant even after accounting for weekly staffing levels and visit volumes.

### 4. Study Design
- **150 branches** total: 90 switched to the new procedure, 60 kept the old
- **17 weeks of data**: Weeks 6–14 (before), Weeks 15–22 (after)
- **2,550 observations** total

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to remaining branches.

2. **Monitor implementation**: Track service scores at newly switched branches to confirm the effect replicates at scale.

3. **Investigate mechanisms**: The analysis shows *that* the procedure works, but not *why*. Consider qualitative follow-up with branch staff to understand which aspects of the new procedure drive improvements.

4. **Watch for diminishing returns**: As more branches adopt the procedure, the control group shrinks. Future evaluations may need alternative designs.

5. **Consider cost-benefit**: If the new procedure requires additional resources (training, time, equipment), weigh the 2-point service score gain against those costs.
