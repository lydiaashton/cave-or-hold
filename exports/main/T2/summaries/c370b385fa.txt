# **Board Summary: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure shows a **positive but statistically inconclusive effect** on service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores improve by approximately **2 points** (about 4% of the average score of 50) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. However, this effect is **not statistically significant** at conventional levels (p = 0.11), meaning we cannot rule out the possibility that the observed difference is due to random variation.

**Recommendation:** Do not proceed with a full system-wide rollout at this time. Instead, continue monitoring the pilot branches for an additional 8-12 weeks to gather more data, or consider a phased expansion while closely tracking outcomes.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. **Treatment Effect Estimate**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Point estimate (DiD) | **+2.05 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | **-0.49 to +4.59 points** |
| p-value | **0.114** |
| Statistical significance | **Not significant** |

The analysis used a difference-in-differences approach comparing 20 branches that switched to the new procedure against 14 branches that kept the old procedure, before and after week 15.

### 2. **What Happened to Each Group**
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 47.09 | 45.96 | **-1.13** ↓ |
| Treated (new procedure) | 49.36 | 50.25 | **+0.89** ↑ |

Notably, control branches experienced a decline in service scores during the study period, while treated branches maintained or slightly improved their scores.

### 3. **Validity of the Analysis**
The parallel trends assumption—the key requirement for difference-in-differences—**appears valid**:
- Pre-switch trends between treatment and control groups were statistically indistinguishable (joint test p = 0.85)
- This gives confidence that the comparison is fair and the estimate is unbiased

### 4. **Effect Over Time (Event Study)**
The weekly effects after the switch ranged from -0.32 to +3.03 points, with an average of +1.52 points. None of the individual weekly estimates reached statistical significance, but all post-switch point estimates were positive, suggesting a consistent (if imprecise) directional effect.

### 5. **Contextual Factors**
The analysis controlled for staffing levels and visitor volume, which themselves affect service scores:
- Staffing index: positively associated with service scores (p = 0.03)
- Visits index: marginally associated with service scores (p = 0.07)

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended Actions**

1. **Extend the observation period** by 8-12 weeks to increase statistical power. With more post-switch data, we may be able to detect whether the ~2 point effect is real.

2. **Continue monitoring current pilot branches** with weekly reporting to the board. Track whether the positive trend persists or fades.

3. **Consider a phased rollout** to an additional 5-10 branches if operational capacity allows, treating this as a second-stage pilot with continued rigorous evaluation.

4. **Investigate qualitative feedback** from branch staff and patrons at switched branches to understand mechanisms behind any score changes.

5. **Reconvene in Q2** with updated data to make a final go/no-go decision on system-wide implementation.

### **Decision Framework**

| Scenario | Recommendation |
|----------|----------------|
| Effect remains ~2 points but becomes significant (p < 0.05) | Proceed with full rollout |
| Effect grows to 3+ points even if not significant | Consider phased expansion |
| Effect diminishes or turns negative | Halt expansion, investigate causes |
| Effect stays ~2 points and remains inconclusive | Extend pilot another quarter |
