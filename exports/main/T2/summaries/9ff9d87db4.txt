# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw a statistically significant increase of **+1.86 points** in their service scores compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents approximately a **3.8% improvement** relative to baseline performance. The evidence supports rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. **Positive Treatment Effect**
- **Switched branches** (425 branches): Service scores increased from 50.69 to 52.45 (+1.76 points)
- **Control branches** (284 branches): Service scores remained essentially flat, 48.96 to 48.89 (-0.07 points)
- **Net effect of the new procedure**: **+1.86 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.30 to 2.41)
- The result is **highly statistically significant** (p < 0.001), meaning it is very unlikely to be due to random chance

### 2. **Consistent Benefits Across All Branch Types**
The improvement was observed across branches with different baseline performance levels:
- Lowest-performing branches: +1.26 points
- Mid-performing branches: +1.66 to +2.78 points
- Highest-performing branches: +2.42 points

This suggests the new procedure benefits all branches, not just those that were struggling.

### 3. **Robust Analysis**
The finding holds after controlling for:
- **Staffing levels** (which positively affect scores)
- **Visit volume** (which also positively affects scores)
- **Branch-specific characteristics** (some branches consistently score higher or lower)
- **Week-to-week variations** (some weeks have systematically higher or lower scores)

### 4. **Parallel Trends Validated**
Before the switch (weeks 6-14), both groups showed similar week-to-week patterns, confirming that the post-switch divergence is attributable to the procedure change rather than pre-existing differences.

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with System-Wide Rollout**
   - The evidence strongly supports expanding the new procedure to all 284 branches still using the old method
   - Expected impact: ~1.8 point average improvement in service scores across the cooperative

2. **Monitor Implementation**
   - Track service scores during rollout to ensure consistent results
   - Pay attention to branches that may need additional training or support

3. **Investigate Mechanisms**

   - This could help refine training and identify additional optimization opportunities

4. **Cost-Benefit Analysis**
   - If there are any additional costs associated with the new procedure, weigh them against the 3.8% service improvement
   - Given the magnitude of improvement, even modest costs are likely justified

- **Data**: 12,053 weekly observations from 709 branches, weeks 6-22
