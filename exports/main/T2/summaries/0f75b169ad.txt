# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

## **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

**Date:** Analysis of Weeks 6–22 Service Score Data  
**Prepared for:** Board Decision on System-Wide Rollout

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

The new procedure shows a **positive association with service scores**, but the evidence is **not statistically conclusive**. Branches that adopted the new procedure maintained their service scores while control branches experienced a decline. The estimated improvement is approximately **1.9 points (3.6%)**, but we cannot rule out the possibility that this result occurred by chance. **We recommend proceeding with caution**—either gathering more data before a full rollout or implementing the new procedure with continued monitoring.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **1. Study Design**
- **34 branches** total: 20 adopted the new procedure, 14 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks** of data (Weeks 6–22); the switch occurred at Week 15
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in service scores between the two groups before and after the switch

### **2. Main Result**
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| **New Procedure** | 52.2 | 52.5 | +0.3 |
| **Old Procedure** | 51.9 | 50.3 | −1.6 |
| **Difference** | | | **+1.9 points** |

- **Estimated effect:** +1.9 points on the service score index
- **95% Confidence Interval:** −0.5 to +4.3 points
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.12 (not significant at the conventional 0.05 level)

### **3. What This Means**
- The new procedure is associated with **better relative performance**, but the evidence is not strong enough to be confident the effect is real
- The apparent benefit comes largely from **preventing a decline** rather than producing a large absolute improvement
- Branches keeping the old procedure saw their scores drop by 1.6 points after Week 15; branches with the new procedure stayed stable

### **4. Validity Checks**
- **Parallel trends assumption:** Supported. Before the switch, both groups showed similar trends in service scores (no significant pre-existing difference in trajectories)
- **Controls:** Results are similar whether or not we account for staffing levels and visit volumes

---

## **NEXT STEPS & RECOMMENDATIONS**

### **Option A: Proceed with Rollout (with monitoring)**
Given the positive direction of the effect and the fact that the new procedure at least prevented the decline seen in control branches, the Board may choose to roll out the procedure system-wide. However:
- Implement **continued monitoring** of service scores
- Set a **review date** (e.g., 3–6 months) to reassess with more data
- Consider a **phased rollout** to allow for adjustment

### **Option B: Gather More Evidence Before Full Rollout**
If the Board prefers higher certainty:
- **Extend the study period** to accumulate more post-switch data
- Consider **randomizing** the procedure across additional branches to strengthen causal inference
- Investigate **why control branches declined** after Week 15 (seasonal effects? other operational changes?)

### **Option C: Targeted Implementation**
- Prioritize rollout to branches that are **underperforming** or at risk of decline
- Use the new procedure as a **stabilization tool** rather than expecting dramatic improvements

---

## **CONCLUSION**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure shows **promise** but not **proof**. The 1.9-point improvement is meaningful in practical terms (3.6% of average scores), and the procedure appears to protect against service score declines. However, the statistical uncertainty means we cannot be confident this effect would replicate. The Board should weigh the **low risk** of adopting a procedure that at minimum does no harm against the **uncertainty** about whether it delivers the expected improvement.
