# Board Summary: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

## Bottom Line

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure shows a **positive but statistically inconclusive effect** on service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **1.9 points** (about 4% improvement), but this result is not statistically significant at conventional levels. We **cannot confidently conclude** the new procedure causes better service, nor can we rule out that it has no effect. The board should consider a **larger-scale pilot or phased rollout** before committing to system-wide implementation.

---

## Key Findings

### Study Design
- **34 branches** total: 20 adopted the new procedure (treatment), 14 kept the old procedure (control)
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in service scores between the two groups before and after the switch

### Results

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Treatment (new procedure) | 47.52 | 48.95 | +1.43 |
| Control (old procedure) | 49.28 | 48.81 | −0.47 |
| **Estimated Effect** | | | **+1.90** |

- **Point estimate**: +1.93 points improvement in service scores
- **95% confidence interval**: −0.36 to +4.22 points
- **P-value**: 0.108 (not significant at the 5% level)

### Interpretation
1. **Direction is positive**: All estimates point toward a modest improvement in service scores.
2. **Uncertainty is high**: The confidence interval includes zero, meaning the data are consistent with no effect or even a small negative effect.
3. **Effect size is modest**: Even if real, a ~2-point gain on a ~50-point scale represents a 4% improvement.
4. **Parallel trends hold**: Both groups showed similar service score patterns before the switch, supporting the validity of the analysis.

### Additional Observations
- Service scores vary considerably week-to-week (standard deviation ~8–11 points), which makes detecting small effects difficult.
- The control group's scores declined slightly after week 15, while treatment group scores increased—this drives the positive estimated effect.
- Covariates (staffing and visits indices) were balanced between groups before the switch.

---

## Next Steps

### Recommended Actions

1. **Extend the observation period**: Collect data for additional weeks to increase statistical power and reduce uncertainty around the effect estimate.

2. **Consider a phased rollout**: Rather than system-wide implementation, expand the new procedure to additional branches incrementally while continuing to monitor outcomes.

3. **Monitor cost implications**: The board should evaluate whether any operational costs of the new procedure are justified by the modest (and uncertain) service score improvement.

4. **Investigate heterogeneity**: Analyze whether the procedure works better for certain types of branches (e.g., by size, location, or traffic volume) to target implementation more effectively.

5. **Set a decision threshold**: Define in advance what magnitude of improvement would justify system-wide adoption, and continue monitoring until the estimate is precise enough to make that determination.

---

## Technical Notes

- Analysis controls for branch fixed effects, staffing levels, and visitor traffic.
- Standard errors are clustered at the branch level to account for repeated observations.
- Service scores average approximately 50 points across all branches; higher scores indicate better service.
