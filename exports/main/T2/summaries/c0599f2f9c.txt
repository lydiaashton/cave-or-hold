# **Library Cooperative Branch Procedure Change: Impact Analysis**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new shelving and rotation procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw an average improvement of **2.0 points** on the service score index compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant and represents approximately a **4% gain** relative to the typical branch score of 50. We recommend proceeding with a cooperative-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **34 branches** participated in the analysis (20 adopted the new procedure, 14 kept the old)
- **17 weeks** of data collected (weeks 6–22, with the switch occurring at week 15)
- Analysis used a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in switched branches against non-switched branches while controlling for branch-specific characteristics and week-to-week variations

### Results
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Estimated improvement** | +2.04 points |
| **Statistical significance** | p = 0.010 (significant at 95% confidence) |
| **95% Confidence interval** | +0.49 to +3.59 points |
| **Relative improvement** | ~4% of average score |

### What the Data Shows
1. **Switched branches improved**: Service scores rose from 48.2 (before) to 50.3 (after)
2. **Non-switched branches stayed flat**: Scores remained stable at 51.6 before and after
3. **The effect is real**: After accounting for inherent differences between branches and normal week-to-week variation, the 2-point gain is unlikely to be due to chance
4. **Staffing matters**: Higher staffing levels are associated with better service scores, but the procedure effect holds even after controlling for this
