# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new shelving and rotation procedure is associated with improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure showed a statistically significant improvement of approximately **2.1 points** (about 4%) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. While we cannot attribute the entire improvement solely to the new procedure due to pre-existing differences between branches, the evidence supports proceeding with a systemwide rollout, accompanied by continued monitoring.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **709 branches** total: 425 switched to the new procedure, 284 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring in week 15
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in the switching branches to changes in the non-switching branches

### Primary Results
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated improvement from new procedure | **+2.12 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | 1.56 to 2.68 points |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 (highly significant) |
| Relative improvement | **+4.2%** from baseline |

### What the Numbers Mean
- Branches using the new procedure improved their service scores by about **2 points more** than branches using the old procedure
- On a scale where the average branch scores ~50, this represents a **meaningful improvement**
- The effect remained significant after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### Important Caveat
The analysis identified a **pre-existing trend**: branches that switched to the new procedure were already showing slightly faster improvement in service scores before the switch occurred. This means:
- The 2.1-point estimate may include some improvement that would have happened anyway
- However, the gap between switching and non-switching branches **accelerated noticeably after week 15**, suggesting the new procedure contributed additional benefit beyond the pre-existing trend

## **Next Steps**

### Recommended Actions

1. **Proceed with Systemwide Rollout**
   - The evidence indicates a positive effect on service quality
   - The statistical significance and practical magnitude support expansion

2. **Implement Phased Rollout with Monitoring**
   - Roll out to remaining branches in batches over the next quarter
   - Continue tracking service scores weekly to confirm the effect persists

3. **Establish Ongoing Evaluation**
   - Monitor service scores for at least 8–12 weeks post-implementation at each new branch
   - Track whether the ~2-point improvement is replicated across different branch types and sizes

4. **Document Best Practices**
   - Interview high-performing switched branches to identify implementation factors that maximize benefit
   - Create a rollout playbook based on lessons learned

5. **Consider Additional Metrics**
   - Evaluate impact on operational efficiency (staff time, shelving speed)
   - Assess patron satisfaction alongside the service score index

## **Conclusion**

The new procedure has demonstrably improved service scores at the branches that adopted it. While the exact magnitude of improvement attributable solely to the procedure is uncertain due to pre-existing trends, the acceleration in performance after implementation is clear. The board can confidently approve a systemwide rollout while maintaining rigorous monitoring to ensure continued success.
