# **Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure Evaluation**

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure **improved service scores by approximately 1.8 points** (on a scale where the average branch scores about 50). This effect is statistically significant and represents a meaningful improvement. We **recommend proceeding with the rollout** to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect
- Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by **1.8 points** relative to branches that kept the old procedure
- This effect is statistically significant (p = 0.003), meaning there's less than a 1% chance this result occurred by random chance
- The 95% confidence interval ranges from **0.6 to 3.0 points**, indicating we can be confident the true effect is positive

### 2. Effect Size
- The improvement represents about **18% of a standard deviation** in service scores—a moderate and practically meaningful effect
- Given that the average branch scores about 50, this is roughly a **3.5% improvement** in service quality

### 3. Parallel Trends Validation
- Before the switch (weeks 6–14), both groups showed nearly identical trends in service scores
- This validates our analysis approach and increases confidence that the observed improvement is due to the new procedure, not pre-existing differences

### 4. Not Driven by Other Factors
- The improvement is **not explained by changes in staffing or visit volumes**—both remained stable across groups
- Both staffing and visits are positively associated with service scores (as expected), but they did not differ between switched and control branches after the intervention

### 5. Effect Over Time
- The positive effect appeared immediately and has been sustained across the 8 weeks since implementation
- There is some evidence the effect may be **growing over time** (+0.24 points per week), though more data would be needed to confirm this trend

## **Caveats**

- The analysis covers only 8 weeks post-implementation; longer-term effects remain to be seen
- One week (week 17) showed an anomalous negative result, though this appears to be random variation rather than a systematic issue
- Service scores are an index measure; the board may wish to consider how this translates to patron experience metrics
