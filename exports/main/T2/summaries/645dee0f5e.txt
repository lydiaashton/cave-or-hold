# **Board Summary: New Branch Procedure Impact Analysis**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new procedure saw their service scores increase by approximately **2.2 points** (about 4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is highly statistically significant and consistent across multiple analytical approaches. We recommend proceeding with a full rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect Size
- **Average improvement: +2.16 service score points** (95% CI: +1.60 to +2.71)
- This represents approximately a **4.3% improvement** from the baseline average score of ~50
- The effect is **highly statistically significant** (p < 0.001)

### 2. Robustness Across Methods
The treatment effect is remarkably consistent across different analytical approaches:

| Model Specification | Effect Size | Standard Error |
|---------------------|-------------|----------------|
| Branch Fixed Effects | +2.16 | 0.28 |
| Branch FE + Covariates | +2.17 | 0.28 |
| Branch + Week Fixed Effects | +2.16 | 0.29 |
| Simple DiD (clustered SE) | +2.16 | 0.28 |

### 3. Timeline of Effects (Event Study)
The event study analysis reveals:
- **Before the switch (weeks 6-13):** Treatment branches were actually performing *worse* than control branches (average -0.76 points)
- **After the switch (weeks 15-22):** Treatment branches outperformed control branches (average +1.48 points)
- **Net improvement:** +2.24 points

This pre-switch underperformance is important: it means our estimate is likely **conservative**. The true effect of the new procedure may be even larger than reported.

### 4. Post-Switch Pattern
Week-by-week analysis shows the treatment effect:
- Week 15 (first week): +0.73 (not yet significant)
- Weeks 16-22: Consistently positive and significant, ranging from +1.39 to +2.22
- The effect **persisted and grew** over the post-switch period, suggesting branches adapted well to the new procedure

### 5. Additional Insights
- **Staffing and visits matter:** Both staffing_index and visits_index positively affect service scores, but controlling for these does not change the treatment effect
- **709 branches total:** 425 switched to the new procedure, 284 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data:** Weeks 6-14 before switch, weeks 15-22 after switch
