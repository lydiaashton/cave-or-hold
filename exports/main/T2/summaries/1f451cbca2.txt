# **Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure Evaluation**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **1.9 points** (approximately 4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and robust to controls for staffing levels and visitor volume. We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measurable Improvement in Service Quality
- Branches using the new procedure improved their service scores by **1.9 points more** than branches using the old procedure
- 95% confidence interval: **0.7 to 3.1 points** improvement
- This represents approximately a **4% increase** on the service score scale (average branch scores ~50)
- The result is statistically significant (p = 0.002), meaning there's less than a 1% chance this finding is due to random variation

### 2. Strong Evidence of Causation
The analysis used a rigorous "difference-in-differences" approach:
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure (treatment group)
- **60 branches** kept the old procedure (control group)
- **Before the switch** (weeks 6-14), both groups had nearly identical service scores (47.1 vs 47.0), confirming they were on parallel trajectories
- **After the switch** (weeks 15-22), the treatment group pulled ahead, with the gap widening by 1.9 points

### 3. Effect Persists After Accounting for Other Factors
The improvement holds even after controlling for:
- Staffing levels (staffing index)
- Visitor volume (visits index)
- Branch-specific characteristics

This suggests the procedure change itself—not external factors—drove the improvement.

### 4. Most Branches Benefited
- **79% of treatment branches** saw service score improvements after switching
- Only **60% of control branches** improved during the same period
- However, there is variation: about 21% of treatment branches saw no improvement or decline, suggesting the procedure may work better in some contexts than others

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with System-Wide Rollout**
   - The evidence supports expanding the new procedure to all 150 branches
   - Expected average improvement: ~1.9 points on the service score index

2. **Monitor Implementation Quality**
   - Given that 21% of early-adopter branches did not see improvement, investigate whether implementation fidelity varied
   - Consider providing additional training or support to ensure consistent adoption

3. **Track Outcomes Post-Rollout**
   - Continue monitoring service scores to confirm the effect persists at scale
   - Watch for any branches that underperform and provide targeted support

4. **Consider Cost-Benefit Analysis**
   - While service scores improved, evaluate whether the operational costs of the new procedure justify the benefit
   - The 4% improvement in service quality should be weighed against any additional labor or resource requirements
