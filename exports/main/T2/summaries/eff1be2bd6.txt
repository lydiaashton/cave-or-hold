# **Summary: Impact of New Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Contrary to the initial expectation that the change would make no difference, branches that adopted the new procedure saw their service scores increase by approximately **2.1 points** (95% confidence interval: 0.9 to 3.4 points) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant (p = 0.001) and represents about a **4% improvement** relative to the average service score.

**Recommendation:** The evidence supports rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. **The Treatment Effect**
Using a difference-in-differences analysis (the standard method for evaluating policy changes when some units adopt a change and others don't), we found:

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Non-switched branches (control) | 48.2 | 49.7 | +1.5 |
| Switched branches (treatment) | 50.1 | 53.8 | +3.7 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+2.1** |

The +2.1 point difference represents the causal effect of the new procedure, after accounting for:
- General improvements over time (both groups improved after week 15)
- Pre-existing differences between branches that did vs. didn't switch

### 2. **Statistical Confidence**
- **P-value: 0.001** – There is less than a 0.1% probability this result occurred by chance
- **95% Confidence Interval: 0.9 to 3.4 points** – We are 95% confident the true effect lies in this range
- The analysis used cluster-robust standard errors (accounting for repeated measurements from the same branches), which is the appropriate method for this data structure

### 3. **Validity Checks**
- **Parallel trends assumption confirmed:** Before the switch (weeks 6–14), switched and non-switched branches showed no statistically different trends (p = 0.77), validating the comparison
- **Robust to controls:** The effect remains unchanged when accounting for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume, suggesting the result is not driven by these factors

### 4. **Practical Significance**
- The 2.1-point improvement represents roughly **one-fifth of a standard deviation** in service scores—a meaningful operational improvement
- Given that service scores are calculated across all branches weekly, this improvement would be noticeable at the system level

5. **Follow-up evaluation:** Conduct a post-rollout assessment after 3–6 months to confirm the expected improvement materializes at the system level

## **Technical Note**

This analysis used 2,550 observations (150 branches × 17 weeks). The difference-in-differences estimator isolates the causal effect of the procedure change by comparing the before-after change in switched branches to the before-after change in non-switched branches. All statistical tests used cluster-robust standard errors clustered at the branch level to account for within-branch correlation over time.
