# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by **1.8 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.3 to 2.4 points) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents a **3.5% improvement** and is highly statistically significant (p < 0.001). We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect
- The new procedure increased service scores by **1.81 points** on average
- 95% confidence interval: **1.26 to 2.36 points**
- Probability this result occurred by chance: **less than 0.01%**

### 2. What the Data Shows
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches (425) | 51.46 | 54.04 | +2.58 |
| Control branches (284) | 51.21 | 51.96 | +0.75 |
| **Net effect (DiD)** | | | **+1.84** |

The switched branches improved by 2.58 points, but control branches also improved slightly (0.75 points), likely due to seasonal factors or other system-wide changes. The **net attributable effect** of the new procedure is 1.84 points.

### 3. Validity Check: Parallel Trends
Before the switch (weeks 6-14), treatment and control branches tracked closely together, with an average difference of only 0.25 points. This supports the conclusion that the post-switch divergence is due to the procedure change, not pre-existing differences.

### 4. Robustness
The result holds across multiple statistical specifications:
- Basic model: +1.84 points
- With staffing and visit controls: +1.81 points
- With week fixed effects: +1.84 points
- Full model: +1.81 points

### 5. Scope of Analysis
- **709 branches** total (425 switched, 284 control)
- **12,053 branch-week observations**
- Data covers weeks 6-22, with the switch occurring at week 15

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all 284 remaining branches

2. **Monitor implementation** — track service scores for the next 8-12 weeks to confirm the effect persists at scale

3. **Document the procedure** — capture what the switching branches did to enable consistent training

4. **Consider cost-benefit** — if the new procedure requires additional resources, weigh the 3.5% service improvement against those costs

5. **Investigate mechanisms** — the analysis shows *that* it works; follow-up work could examine *why* (e.g., better staffing alignment, improved visit experience)
