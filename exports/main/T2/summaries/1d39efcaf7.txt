# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new shelving and rotation procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **2.4 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.1 to 3.6 points) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and was not driven by changes in staffing levels or visitor volume. **We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect
- **90 branches** switched to the new procedure in Week 15; **60 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Service scores improved for **both groups** after Week 15, but the improvement was **significantly larger** for branches using the new procedure
- The new procedure added **2.4 points** to service scores beyond the general improvement seen across all branches

### 2. Statistical Confidence
- The result is **highly statistically significant** (p < 0.001)
- We are 95% confident the true effect lies between **1.1 and 3.6 points**
- The analysis controlled for staffing levels and visitor volume—neither changed differently between groups, confirming the procedure itself drove the improvement

### 3. Baseline Comparison
- Switched branches started with slightly lower service scores (47.7 vs. 49.4), but this difference was not statistically significant
- **Parallel trends verified**: Before the switch, both groups showed similar week-to-week patterns, validating our comparison

### 4. What Changed (and What Didn't)
| Metric | Effect of New Procedure |
|--------|------------------------|
| Service Score | **+2.4 points** ✓ |
| Staffing Index | No significant change |
| Visits Index | No significant change |

The improvement came from the procedure itself, not from branches receiving more staff or attracting more visitors.

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new shelving and rotation procedure to all 150 branches

2. **Monitor implementation** during the transition:
   - Track service scores for the first 8-12 weeks after rollout
   - Ensure branches receive adequate training on the new procedure

## **Methodology Note**

This analysis used a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in service scores for branches that switched procedures versus those that did not, before and after Week 15. Standard errors were clustered by branch to account for repeated measurements. The parallel trends assumption was tested and confirmed (p = 0.87 for pre-treatment trend differences).
