# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new shelving and rotation procedure improved service scores by approximately 2.5 points (about 5% relative to baseline). This effect is statistically significant and consistent across the post-switch period. We recommend proceeding with a full rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measurable Improvement in Service Scores
- Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by **2.48 points** relative to branches that kept the old procedure
- This difference is **statistically significant** (p < 0.001), meaning it is very unlikely to be due to random chance
- The 95% confidence interval ranges from **1.16 to 3.81 points**, indicating a meaningful positive effect

### 2. Valid Comparison Between Groups
- **60 branches** kept the old procedure (control group); **90 branches** adopted the new procedure (treatment group)
- Before the switch, both groups had **nearly identical trends** in service scores (0.40 vs. 0.37 points per week), supporting the validity of the comparison
- The treatment group started slightly lower (50.4 vs. 51.6) but this gap closed and reversed after the switch

### 3. Effect Is Robust
- The improvement persists even after accounting for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume
- The effect appears in most post-switch weeks, with no evidence of the benefit fading over time

### 4. Context for the Board's Expectation
The board expected no difference in service scores. The data shows the opposite—the new procedure produced a **modest but clear improvement**. A 2.5-point gain on a ~50-point scale represents meaningful progress in service quality.

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with full rollout** to all 60 branches currently using the old procedure
2. **Monitor service scores** during rollout to ensure the effect replicates at remaining branches
3. **Document the new procedure** in detail to ensure consistent implementation
4. **Consider investigating mechanisms**—the improvement may stem from faster item retrieval, better organization, or reduced staff time spent on shelving

## **Technical Notes**

- **Analysis method**: Difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch)
- **Data**: 2,550 observations (150 branches × 17 weeks, weeks 6–22)
- **Switch date**: Week 15
- **Statistical significance**: p = 0.0003 for the treatment effect
