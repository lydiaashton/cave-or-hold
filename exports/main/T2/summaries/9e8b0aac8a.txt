# **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure is associated with a **2.4-point improvement in service scores** (approximately 5% increase). While this effect is not statistically significant at the conventional threshold (p=0.09), the evidence points in a positive direction with no indication of harm. **We recommend proceeding with the rollout**, while continuing to monitor outcomes.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect
- **Difference-in-Differences Estimate:** +2.39 points (95% CI: -0.39 to +5.17)
- **Statistical Significance:** p=0.092 (marginally significant)
- **Practical Significance:** ~5% improvement from baseline scores

### 2. What the Data Shows
| Group | Pre-Switch Average | Post-Switch Average | Change |
|-------|-------------------|---------------------|--------|
| Switched Branches (20 branches) | 45.2 | 49.4 | +4.2 |
| Control Branches (14 branches) | 46.6 | 48.5 | +2.0 |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | | | **+2.4** |

Both groups improved after week 15, but branches that switched to the new procedure improved **twice as much** as those that kept the old procedure.

### 3. Validity Checks
- **Parallel Trends Assumption:** ✓ Supported. Before the switch, treatment and control branches showed similar trends over time (p=0.74 for differential pre-trends).
- **Effect Persistence:** ✓ The improvement appears stable across the post-switch period, with no evidence of fading or growing effects.
- **Controls:** The result holds after accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume.

### 4. Interpretation of Uncertainty
The 95% confidence interval ranges from -0.39 to +5.17 points. This means:
- We cannot rule out a very small negative effect (less than 1 point decline)
- The plausible positive effect could be as large as 5 points (~10% improvement)
- The **most likely effect is positive**, around 2-3 points

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with Rollout:** The evidence supports expanding the new procedure to all branches. The direction of the effect is positive, the magnitude is practically meaningful, and there is no signal of harm.

2. **Continue Monitoring:** Track service scores for the next 8-12 weeks after full rollout to confirm the effect persists at scale.

3. **Consider a Staggered Implementation:** If resources allow, roll out in phases to maintain a control group for ongoing evaluation.

4. **Investigate Mechanisms:** The analysis controlled for staffing and visits, but further research could identify *why* the new procedure improves scores (e.g., faster book location, better organization, staff efficiency).

5. **Set Success Metrics:** Define a minimum acceptable improvement (e.g., 1.5 points) and establish a timeline for formal re-evaluation.

---

## **Analytical Notes**

- **Method:** Difference-in-Differences regression with branch-clustered standard errors
- **Data:** 34 branches, 17 weeks each (weeks 6-22), 578 observations
- **Switch Date:** Week 15
- **Controls:** Staffing index and visits index included in main specification
