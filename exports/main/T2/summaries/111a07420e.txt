# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure **improved service scores by approximately 1.9 points** (about 4% of the average score). This improvement is **statistically significant**, providing evidence that the new procedure has a positive effect on branch service quality. We recommend proceeding with a full rollout, while monitoring implementation to ensure consistent results across all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. The New Procedure Improved Service Scores**
- Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by **1.9 points** relative to branches that kept the old procedure
- This effect is statistically significant (p = 0.013), meaning there's strong evidence the improvement is real and not due to random variation
- The 95% confidence interval ranges from **0.4 to 3.4 points**, indicating the true effect is very likely positive

### **2. Analysis Approach**
We used a rigorous "difference-in-differences" method with branch and week fixed effects, which:
- Compares switched branches to non-switched branches (control group)
- Compares performance before and after week 15 (when the switch occurred)
- Controls for inherent differences between branches and week-to-week fluctuations
- Validates that both groups were trending similarly before the switch (parallel trends assumption holds)

### **3. Effect Persists After Accounting for Other Factors**
When we controlled for staffing levels and visitor volume, the treatment effect remained significant at **2.1 points** (p = 0.007). This suggests the improvement comes from the procedure itself, not from changes in staffing or traffic patterns.
