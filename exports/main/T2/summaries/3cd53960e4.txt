# Analysis of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

## Bottom Line

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure shows a **modest positive association with service scores** (+1.6 points on average), but this effect is **not statistically distinguishable from zero** given the data available. The board should consider a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.

## Key Findings

### 1. Estimated Effect
- Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores improve by approximately **1.6 points** relative to branches that kept the old procedure
- This represents about a **3% improvement** relative to the baseline average score of 51
- However, the **95% confidence interval ranges from -0.5 to +3.8 points**, meaning we cannot rule out no effect (or even a small negative effect)

### 2. Statistical Significance
- The effect is **not statistically significant** (p = 0.14)
- With the current sample size (34 branches over 17 weeks), we lack sufficient power to detect modest effects with confidence

### 3. Pre-Existing Differences
- Branches that switched to the new procedure had **lower baseline service scores** (averaging 48.9) compared to those that did not switch (averaging 54.3)
- This suggests branches may have been selected for the new procedure based on prior performance
- Importantly, **pre-switch trends were parallel** between the two groups, supporting the validity of the comparison

### 4. Weekly Variation
- Service scores fluctuate considerably week-to-week (standard deviation of ~8-9 points)
- This natural variation makes it harder to detect the procedure's effect against the background noise

## Next Steps

1. **Continue monitoring**: Extend the observation period to gather more post-switch data. Additional weeks would improve statistical power.

2. **Consider a randomized rollout**: If expanding the procedure, randomly assign remaining branches to treatment vs. control to eliminate selection bias concerns.

3. **Investigate heterogeneity**: Examine whether the procedure works better for certain types of branches (e.g., by size, location, or baseline performance).

4. **Cost-benefit analysis**: Even if the effect is real (~1.6 points), evaluate whether the operational costs of the new procedure justify this improvement.

5. **Pilot expansion**: Consider expanding to 5-10 additional branches as a second pilot phase before full rollout.
