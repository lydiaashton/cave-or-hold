# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

## **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

### **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new stock rotation and shelving procedure is associated with a statistically significant improvement in service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.0 points** (about 4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is highly reliable (p < 0.001) and appears to grow over time.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches, with continued monitoring to confirm sustained benefits.

---

### **KEY FINDINGS**

#### **1. Treatment Effect Estimate**
- **Average improvement:** +2.03 service score points (95% confidence interval: 1.48 to 2.57)
- **Relative improvement:** Approximately 4% increase from the baseline average of 51 points
- **Statistical significance:** Highly significant (p < 0.001)

#### **2. What the Data Shows**
| Period | Control Branches (Old Procedure) | Treatment Branches (New Procedure) |
|--------|----------------------------------|-----------------------------------|
| Before switch (Weeks 6-14) | 50.50 | 51.32 |
| After switch (Weeks 15-22) | 49.35 | 52.20 |
| **Change** | **-1.15** | **+0.88** |

The control branches experienced a slight decline in service scores after week 14, while treatment branches improved. The difference-in-differences estimate of +2.03 points captures this divergence.

#### **3. Effect Over Time**
The treatment effect appears to strengthen over time:
- **Early post-switch (Weeks 15-17):** Small, statistically insignificant effects
- **Later post-switch (Weeks 21-22):** Larger effects (+1.4 to +1.6 points), statistically significant

This pattern suggests branches may need time to fully realize the benefits of the new procedure.

#### **4. Supporting Factors**
The analysis controlled for two important factors:
- **Staffing levels:** Higher staffing is associated with better service scores (+0.44 points per unit)
- **Visit volume:** Higher visits are associated with better service scores (+0.65 points per unit)

The treatment effect remains significant after accounting for these factors.

#### **5. Important Caveat**
The analysis identified a limitation: treatment branches showed slightly different trends than control branches *before* the switch (they were already improving relative to controls in the weeks leading up to week 15). This means the 2.03-point estimate may partially reflect pre-existing momentum rather than solely the effect of the new procedure. However, the acceleration of improvement after the switch and the growing effect over time support a genuine treatment benefit.

### **METHODOLOGY NOTE**

This analysis used a **difference-in-differences** research design, comparing 425 branches that adopted the new procedure (treatment group) to 284 branches that did not (control group), before and after the week 15 switch point. Standard errors were clustered by branch to account for repeated observations. The model controlled for weekly staffing and visit volume variations.

**Data:** 12,053 branch-week observations (709 branches × 17 weeks)
