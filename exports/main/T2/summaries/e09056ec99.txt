# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact Analysis**

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure is associated with a **modest improvement in service scores** (approximately +2.2 points, or 4.4% above baseline). However, the evidence is **not statistically conclusive** at conventional confidence levels. We recommend a **cautious, phased rollout** with continued monitoring rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect
- Branches that adopted the new procedure showed an average service score increase of **2.66 points** after the switch (weeks 15-22 vs. weeks 6-14)
- Control branches (keeping the old procedure) showed only a **0.48 point** increase over the same period
- The **difference-in-differences estimate is +2.18 points**, representing the procedure's estimated impact

### 2. Statistical Significance
- The effect is **marginally significant** (p ≈ 0.09–0.10), meaning there is roughly a 90% confidence level that the effect is real
- This falls short of the conventional 95% confidence threshold (p < 0.05) typically required for definitive conclusions
- The 95% confidence interval ranges from approximately **-0.4 to +4.7 points**, indicating uncertainty about the true effect size

### 3. Consistency Across Branches
- **13 of 20 treatment branches** (65%) showed positive effects from the new procedure
- Effects varied considerably: some branches gained up to 9.4 points, while 7 branches saw no improvement or slight declines
- This variation suggests the procedure may work better in some branch contexts than others

### 4. Pre-Treatment Trends
- Treatment and control groups had **similar service score trends before the switch**, supporting the validity of the comparison
- The average gap between groups before week 15 was minimal (-0.57 points) with no systematic divergence

### 5. Effect Size Context
- The 2.18-point improvement represents about **0.21 standard deviations** of typical service score variation
- This is a **small-to-moderate effect** in practical terms

---

## **Next Steps**

### Recommended Actions

1. **Proceed with Phased Rollout**
   - Expand the new procedure to additional branches in stages rather than all at once
   - Prioritize branches similar to the 13 that showed clear improvements

2. **Implement Enhanced Monitoring**
   - Continue weekly service score tracking for all branches
   - Add branch-level feedback mechanisms to identify which operational contexts benefit most

3. **Investigate Variation**
   - Analyze why 7 treatment branches did not improve (e.g., staffing levels, visit volumes, physical layout)
   - Consider whether the procedure needs adaptation for certain branch types

4. **Set a Review Timeline**
   - Re-evaluate after 8–12 weeks of expanded implementation
   - If effects strengthen with more data, proceed to full rollout
   - If effects remain ambiguous, consider piloting alternative improvements

### Risk Considerations
- The procedure shows **no evidence of harm** (no branches experienced significant declines attributable to the switch)
- The main risk is **opportunity cost**: resources spent on a procedure with uncertain benefits could be deployed elsewhere
- Given the low risk and potential upside, a measured expansion is warranted

- **Data**: 34 branches, 17 weeks (weeks 6–22), 578 observations
