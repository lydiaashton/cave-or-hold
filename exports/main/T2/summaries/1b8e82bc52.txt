# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new shelving and stock rotation procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw an average increase of **2.2 points** on the service score index (approximately **4.4% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and contradicts the initial expectation that the change would make no difference. **We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure; **60 branches** continued with the old procedure
- The switch occurred at **week 15**, providing 9 weeks of pre-switch and 8 weeks of post-switch data
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in the treatment group to changes in the control group

### **Results**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect of new procedure | **+2.23 points** |
| Statistical significance | **p < 0.01** (highly significant) |
| 95% Confidence interval | **+0.56 to +3.90 points** |
| Percentage improvement | **~4.4%** from baseline |

### **Validity Checks**

✓ **Parallel trends assumption confirmed**: Before the switch, both groups showed similar trends in service scores (pre-treatment trend difference not statistically significant, p = 0.65). This validates our comparison approach.

✓ **Consistent results**: The effect remains stable across different statistical specifications, including models that control for branch-specific characteristics and week-to-week variations.

### **Weekly Pattern**

The improvement appeared immediately after the switch and persisted throughout the post-switch period. Service scores for treatment branches averaged approximately 2 points higher than control branches in weeks 15–22.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all 60 branches still using the old method.

2. **Monitor implementation**: Track service scores during rollout to ensure the effect replicates at branches that haven't yet switched.

3. **Investigate mechanisms**: Consider studying *why* the new procedure improved scores (e.g., faster item location, better organization, staff efficiency) to optimize training.

4. **Cost-benefit analysis**: Evaluate whether the 4.4% service score improvement justifies any additional costs or training time associated with the new procedure.

5. **Long-term follow-up**: Continue monitoring to ensure the effect persists beyond the initial 8-week post-switch window.
