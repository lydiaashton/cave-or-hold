# **Board Summary: New Shelving and Rotation Procedure Evaluation**

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and rotation procedure shows **modest positive effects** on service scores, but the evidence is **not statistically conclusive**. Branches that adopted the new procedure maintained their service scores (average change: -0.14 points) while control branches experienced a notable decline (average change: -2.07 points). This suggests the new procedure may help **protect against score deterioration** rather than drive improvement. We recommend a **cautious, phased rollout** with continued monitoring.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Overall Impact**
| Metric | Treatment Branches (New Procedure) | Control Branches (Old Procedure) |
|--------|-----------------------------------|----------------------------------|
| Branches | 20 | 14 |
| Before switch (weeks 6-14) | 51.41 | 49.26 |
| After switch (weeks 15-22) | 51.27 | 47.19 |
| **Change** | **-0.14** | **-2.07** |

**Difference-in-Differences Estimate: +1.9 to +2.2 points** (treatment branches declined less than control branches)

### **2. Statistical Significance**
- **Point estimate**: +2.16 points (approximately 4% of the average service score of 50)
- **With cluster-robust standard errors** (appropriate for repeated branch observations): p = 0.115
- **95% Confidence Interval**: [-0.52, +4.83] points
- **Interpretation**: The effect is positive but not statistically significant at conventional levels. We cannot rule out that the observed difference is due to random variation.

### **3. Branch-Level Results**
- **Treatment branches**: 11 of 20 (55%) showed score improvement after switching
- **Control branches**: 5 of 14 (36%) showed score improvement
- Treatment branches showed more consistent performance, with a median change of +0.45 vs. -1.81 for control branches

### **4. Important Context**
- Service scores **declined system-wide** after week 14 (the `after_switch` coefficient is -4.11, p < 0.001), suggesting external factors affected all branches
- The new procedure appears to have **buffered treatment branches** against this decline rather than driving absolute improvement
- Pre-existing differences: Treatment branches started with higher average scores (+2.15 points before the switch), though trends were reasonably parallel

### **5. Related Factors**
- Both `staffing_index` and `visits_index` positively correlate with service scores (p < 0.05), confirming these are meaningful drivers of performance
- The new procedure's effect persists after controlling for these factors

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended Actions**

1. **Proceed with Cautious Rollout**
   - The positive point estimate and protective effect against score decline support broader adoption
   - However, given statistical uncertainty, implement in phases rather than all at once

2. **Expand Monitoring Period**
   - Current data covers only 8 weeks post-switch (weeks 15-22)
   - Extend observation to 6-12 months to capture longer-term effects and seasonal variation

3. **Investigate Heterogeneous Effects**
   - 9 of 20 treatment branches did not improve—understand why
   - Analyze whether branch size, location, or baseline performance moderates the effect

4. **Track Implementation Quality**
   - Ensure consistent adoption of the new procedure across branches
   - Consider training or support for branches showing no improvement

5. **Continue Control Group Observation**
   - Keep some branches on the old procedure as an ongoing comparison group
   - This enables continued rigorous evaluation as more data accumulates

### **Decision Framework**

| Scenario | Recommendation |
|----------|----------------|
| If priority is **risk mitigation** (prevent score decline) | **Roll out** — evidence supports protective effect |
| If priority is **score improvement** (raise absolute levels) | **Wait for more data** — no evidence of absolute gains |
| If resources are **constrained** | **Targeted rollout** to branches at risk of decline |
