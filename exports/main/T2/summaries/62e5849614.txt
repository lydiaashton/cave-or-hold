# Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

## Bottom Line

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **2.1 points** (95% confidence interval: 0.8 to 3.3 points) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents approximately a **4% improvement** over baseline scores. The result is statistically significant (p = 0.001), and we recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

## Key Findings

### Study Design
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **60 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in switched branches against non-switched branches before and after week 15

### Results Summary

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 47.90 | 48.36 | +0.46 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 48.04 | 50.58 | +2.54 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+2.08** |

### Statistical Validity
- **Parallel trends confirmed**: Before the switch (weeks 6–14), treatment and control branches showed similar trends in service scores (p = 0.22), validating our comparison approach
- **Robust to controls**: The effect remains significant (p = 0.0008) after accounting for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume
- **Consistent effect**: The improvement was observed consistently across all post-switch weeks (15–22)

### What This Means
- The average branch scores about 50 on the service index; the new procedure lifted switched branches by about 2 points above what would have been expected
- This is a meaningful improvement in service quality that cannot be explained by normal week-to-week variation or differences in staffing and visitor traffic

## Next Steps

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all 150 branches

2. **Develop implementation timeline** – consider phasing the rollout to manage training and operational capacity

3. **Monitor ongoing performance** – continue tracking service scores after full implementation to confirm the effect persists at scale

4. **Document best practices** – capture what switched branches did during implementation to support training for remaining branches

5. **Consider cost-benefit analysis** – while service scores improved, evaluate any operational costs (staff time, training) associated with the new procedure
