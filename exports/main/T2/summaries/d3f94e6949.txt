# **Memorandum to the Board**

## **Title: Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new rotation and shelving procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **2.1 points** (approximately **4% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and has persisted throughout the post-switch period. Contrary to the expectation that the change would make no difference, the evidence strongly supports rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **150 branches** analyzed over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** switched to the new procedure at week 15; **60 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- **9 weeks** of pre-switch data and **8 weeks** of post-switch data per branch

### **Main Result**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Service score improvement (DiD estimate) | **+2.08 points** |
| Statistical significance | **p < 0.001** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | **0.93 to 3.23 points** |
| Relative improvement | **~4.3%** above baseline |

### **Supporting Evidence**

1. **Parallel Trends Validated**: Before the switch, both groups showed similar week-to-week patterns in service scores, with treated branches consistently scoring about 1–2 points higher (a stable baseline difference). After the switch, the treatment group pulled away from the control group.

2. **Effect Persists Over Time**: The improvement was present in both the early post-switch period (weeks 15–18: +1.6 points) and the later period (weeks 19–22: +2.5 points), suggesting the benefit is sustained and may even grow as staff become more familiar with the new procedure.

3. **Robust Results**: The 2.1-point improvement holds across multiple statistical specifications:
   - With and without control variables (staffing and visit indices)
   - With branch-level fixed effects
   - With both branch and week fixed effects

4. **Control Variables**: As expected, higher staffing levels and visitor volumes were associated with higher service scores, but these factors do not explain the treatment effect—the new procedure itself drove the improvement.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with System-Wide Rollout**: The evidence strongly supports extending the new procedure to all 60 branches still using the old method. The expected gain is approximately 2 points per branch on the service score index.

2. **Monitor Implementation**: Track service scores during rollout to ensure the effect replicates at branches that were not part of the initial switch. Consider phasing the rollout to allow for training and adjustment.

3. **Investigate Mechanisms**: While we know the procedure works, understanding *why* it improves scores (e.g., faster item location, better organization, reduced staff time per task) could help optimize training and identify additional improvements.

4. **Longer-Term Follow-Up**: Continue collecting data to determine whether the effect stabilizes, grows, or diminishes over time. The slight increase in the later post-switch period is encouraging but warrants confirmation.

5. **Cost-Benefit Analysis**: If there are any costs associated with the new procedure (training time, equipment, etc.), compare them against the value of a 4% service score improvement to confirm the net benefit.
