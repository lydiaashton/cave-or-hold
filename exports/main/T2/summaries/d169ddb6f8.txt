## **New Shelving Procedure: Impact on Library Service Scores**

### **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **2.1 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant and represents a meaningful gain on the 50-point average scale. We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. Measurable Improvement in Service Quality**
- Treatment branches (90 branches that switched) improved by **3.7 points** after the switch
- Control branches (60 branches that kept the old procedure) improved by only **1.5 points** during the same period
- The **net effect of the new procedure is +2.1 points** (95% confidence interval: 0.9 to 3.4 points)
- This result is highly statistically significant (p < 0.001)

**2. Trends Support Causal Interpretation**
- Before the switch (weeks 6–14), treatment and control branches followed parallel trends—the gap between them remained stable at about 2 points
- After the switch (weeks 15–22), the gap widened consistently, with treatment branches pulling ahead
- This pattern supports the conclusion that the procedure change caused the improvement, not other factors

**3. Results Are Robust**
- The improvement persists after controlling for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume
- Neither staffing nor visitation patterns changed differentially between treatment and control branches, ruling out these factors as alternative explanations

**4. Practical Significance**
- On a scale where the average branch scores about 50, a 2-point gain represents approximately a **4% improvement** in service quality
- If rolled out system-wide, this would lift the cooperative's overall service score meaningfully
