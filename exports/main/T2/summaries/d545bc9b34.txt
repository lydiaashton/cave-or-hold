# **Library Cooperative Shelving Procedure Evaluation**
## Analysis of New Branch Rotation and Stocking Procedure

---

### **Bottom Line**

The new shelving procedure shows a **positive but not statistically definitive** effect on service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure improved by approximately **2.5 points** on the service score index compared to branches that kept the old procedure. However, this result falls short of conventional statistical significance (p=0.10), meaning we cannot rule out that the observed improvement could be due to random variation. The board should consider a **cautious, phased rollout** with continued monitoring rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. Study Overview**
- **34 branches** participated: 20 adopted the new procedure, 14 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks** of data (weeks 6-22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Service scores average around 50 points (higher is better)

**2. Treatment Effect**
- **Estimated improvement: +2.5 points** (approximately 5% of baseline score)
- **95% confidence interval: -0.5 to +5.4 points**
- **Statistical significance: p=0.10** (not significant at the conventional 0.05 threshold)

**3. What the Data Shows**
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| New Procedure (20 branches) | 46.0 | 47.1 | +1.1 |
| Old Procedure (14 branches) | 51.4 | 50.1 | -1.3 |
| **Difference** | | | **+2.4** |

**4. Important Context**
- Branches that switched to the new procedure had **lower baseline service scores** (46.0 vs 51.4), suggesting they may have been struggling more before the change
- The control group's scores declined after week 15, while treatment group scores improved—this pattern supports a genuine treatment effect
- Post-switch trends show treatment branches improving (+0.35 points/week) while control branches declined (-0.14 points/week)

**5. What This Means**
- The data is **suggestive but not conclusive** that the new procedure helps
- The effect size (2.5 points) would be meaningful if real—roughly equivalent to moving from the 50th to 55th percentile
- The wide confidence interval means the true effect could range from slightly negative to substantially positive

---

### **Next Steps**

**1. Recommended Approach: Phased Expansion**
- Roll out the new procedure to **5-10 additional branches** over the next quarter
- Continue monitoring service scores with the same methodology
- This balances potential benefits against implementation costs and risks

**2. Enhanced Monitoring**
- Track service scores for **at least 8-12 weeks post-implementation** for new adopters
- Consider collecting **qualitative feedback** from branch staff about the new procedure
- Monitor whether effects are **sustained or diminish** over time

**3. Decision Criteria for Full Rollout**
- Proceed with system-wide implementation if follow-up data shows:
  - Consistent positive effects across new adopters
  - Statistical significance improves (p < 0.05) with larger sample
  - No adverse operational impacts reported by staff

**4. Alternative Considerations**
- Investigate whether the new procedure works **better for certain types of branches** (e.g., those with lower baseline scores)
- Consider whether **additional training or support** could amplify the positive effect
- Evaluate **cost-benefit** of the procedure change relative to the magnitude of improvement

---

### **Technical Notes**

Analysis used a difference-in-differences regression model with standard errors clustered by branch. The model controlled for staffing levels and visitor volume. The visualizations (saved as `analysis_plots.png`) show service score trends over time for both groups.
