## **Board Summary: New Branch Procedure Evaluation**

### **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and rotation procedure is associated with a **2.2-point improvement** in service scores for branches that adopted it. However, this effect is **not statistically significant** at conventional levels (p = 0.13), meaning we cannot rule out that the observed improvement occurred by chance. The 95% confidence interval ranges from -0.6 to +5.0 points.

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. Service Score Changes**

**2. Statistical Assessment**
- The 2.2-point improvement has a **p-value of 0.13**, above the standard 0.05 threshold for statistical significance
- The effect could reasonably be as low as -0.6 points (no benefit) or as high as +5.0 points (meaningful improvement)
- With only 8 weeks of post-switch data and 34 branches total, the study has limited statistical power

**3. Important Context**
- Branches that switched to the new procedure had **lower baseline scores** (averaging 3.4 points below non-switched branches before the change)
- The gap between switched and non-switched branches **narrowed after the switch**, suggesting the new procedure may help lower-performing branches catch up
- Week 22 was the first week where switched branches outperformed non-switched branches, hinting at a possible **learning curve** or delayed effect

**4. Other Factors**
- Weekly visitor volume (visits_index) significantly affects service scores—busier weeks correlate with higher scores
- Staffing levels showed no significant relationship with service scores in this period

3. **Gather qualitative feedback** from branch staff about implementation challenges and perceived benefits.
