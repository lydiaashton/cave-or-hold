# **Library Branch Shelving Procedure: Impact Analysis**

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving procedure is associated with a **modest improvement in service scores** (approximately +1.8 points or 3.8%), but the evidence is **not statistically conclusive** at conventional confidence levels. The data suggest a positive effect, but we cannot rule out the possibility that the observed improvement occurred by chance. We recommend a **cautious, phased rollout** with continued monitoring rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect Estimate
- Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by an estimated **1.83 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure
- This represents approximately a **3.8% improvement** relative to baseline scores
- The 95% confidence interval ranges from **-0.33 to +4.00 points**, meaning the true effect could be slightly negative or as high as 4 points

### 2. Statistical Significance
- The result is **marginally significant** (p = 0.097), meeting a 10% confidence threshold but not the conventional 5% standard
- In practical terms: there is roughly a 10% probability that we would observe this improvement (or larger) even if the new procedure had no real effect

### 3. Supporting Evidence
- **Parallel trends validated**: Before the switch (weeks 6-14), treatment and control branches showed similar score trends with no systematic divergence (p = 0.46 for pre-trend differences)
- **Consistent post-switch pattern**: After week 15, the treatment-control gap was positive in all 8 weeks, ranging from +2.1 to +4.5 points
- **Before the switch**, the gap fluctuated more erratically (from -1.3 to +2.6 points)

### 4. Study Design
- **34 branches total**: 20 adopted the new procedure, 14 maintained the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data**: 9 weeks before the switch, 8 weeks after
- Analysis controlled for staffing levels and visitor volume, which did not materially change the results

## **Next Steps**

### Recommended Actions

1. **Proceed with phased rollout** — Expand the new procedure to additional branches in stages rather than all at once, allowing for continued evaluation

2. **Extend the observation period** — Collect 8-12 more weeks of data to increase statistical power and confirm whether the effect persists

3. **Monitor implementation fidelity** — Ensure branches adopting the new procedure are following it consistently; variation in execution could be masking a stronger true effect

4. **Track secondary outcomes** — Consider measuring additional metrics (e.g., patron satisfaction, staff time savings, item retrieval speed) that may capture benefits not reflected in the service score

5. **Set a decision threshold** — Define in advance what level of evidence (e.g., p < 0.05 with 2+ point improvement) would justify full system-wide adoption

### Risk Considerations

- **If the effect is real**: Delaying full rollout means foregoing service improvements at 14 branches for additional weeks
- **If the effect is not real**: Premature full rollout could incur unnecessary training and implementation costs without benefit
- **The phased approach balances these risks** by allowing learning while limiting exposure
