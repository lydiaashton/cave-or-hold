# **Board Summary: New Branch Procedure Evaluation**

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and rotation procedure shows a **positive effect on service scores**, with switched branches improving by approximately **2.3 points** (about 4-5%) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. However, this improvement is **not statistically definitive** at conventional confidence levels. The evidence is promising but not conclusive. We recommend a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than an immediate full deployment.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Treatment Effect**
- **Estimated improvement:** +2.28 service score points for branches using the new procedure
- **95% Confidence Interval:** -0.46 to +5.03 points
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.10 (marginally significant; does not meet the conventional 0.05 threshold)

### **2. What the Data Shows**
| Metric | Switched Branches | Non-Switched Branches |
|--------|-------------------|----------------------|
| Number of branches | 20 | 14 |
| Average score before switch | 50.4 | 53.9 |
| Average score after switch | 52.1 | 53.3 |
| Branches that improved | 13 (65%) | 7 (50%) |
| Average change | +1.63 | -0.66 |

### **3. Analysis Validity**
- **Parallel trends confirmed:** Before the switch, both groups showed similar score trends over time (p = 0.70), supporting the validity of our comparison
- **Balanced data:** All 34 branches have complete weekly data for the full 17-week period
- **Controls included:** Analysis accounts for staffing levels and visitor volume, which did not materially change the results

### **4. Important Context**
- Switched branches started with **lower baseline scores** than non-switched branches (about 3.5 points lower on average)
- The new procedure appears to have **narrowed this gap** rather than creating a new advantage
- The effect, while positive, is **modest in magnitude** relative to typical week-to-week variation

---

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended Actions**

1. **Proceed with Cautious Rollout**
   - The positive direction of the effect aligns with board expectations
   - Given the modest cost of implementation and potential upside, expanding the procedure is reasonable
   - However, avoid mandating immediate adoption across all branches

2. **Extend the Evaluation Period**
   - Current data covers only 8 weeks post-switch
   - Collect 8-12 additional weeks of data to improve statistical precision
   - This would narrow the confidence interval and provide more definitive evidence

3. **Monitor Implementation Quality**
   - Track whether branches are fully adopting the new procedures as designed
   - Variation in implementation may be masking the true effect

4. **Set Clear Success Metrics**
   - Define a minimum effect size that would justify full rollout (e.g., +2 points sustained over 3 months)
   - Establish a timeline for re-evaluation (e.g., quarterly review)

5. **Consider Branch-Level Factors**
   - Investigate why 7 of 20 switched branches did not see improvement
   - Identify characteristics of branches that benefit most from the new procedure

- **Method:** Difference-in-Differences regression with branch-level clustered standard errors
- **Controls:** Staffing index and visits index
- **Data period:** Weeks 6-22 (switch occurred at week 15)
- **Sample:** 34 branches, 578 branch-week observations
