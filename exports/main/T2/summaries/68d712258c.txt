# **Board Summary: Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.1 to 2.5 points** (about 4-5% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and appears to strengthen over time. We recommend proceeding with a full rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measurable Improvement in Service Quality
- Treatment branches (new procedure) improved from an average of **50.7 to 53.3** after the switch
- Control branches (old procedure) improved only slightly from **48.9 to 49.3**
- The **difference-in-differences estimate is +2.1 points** (p < 0.001), meaning the new procedure caused this improvement

### 2. Effect Strengthens Over Time
The event study analysis shows the benefit grows as branches gain experience:
- **Weeks 15-18**: Modest gains of 0.3 to 1.1 points
- **Weeks 19-22**: Larger gains of 1.5 to 2.7 points (all statistically significant)

This suggests staff may need a few weeks to fully realize the benefits of the new procedure.

### 3. Analysis Is Robust
- **709 branches** across **17 weeks** (12,053 observations)
- Results hold when controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume
- Results hold when accounting for inherent differences between branches (branch fixed effects)
- Pre-treatment trends were reasonably parallel, supporting the validity of the comparison

### 4. Practical Significance
On a service score scale where the average branch scores about 50, a **2+ point improvement** represents meaningful progress in patron satisfaction and service delivery.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all remaining branches

2. **Plan implementation support**:
   - Allow 3-4 weeks for branches to realize full benefits
   - Provide training materials based on early-adopter branches' experience

3. **Monitor ongoing performance**:
   - Continue tracking service scores post-rollout
   - Investigate whether the effect persists or continues to grow

4. **Document best practices**:
   - Interview high-performing switched branches to identify implementation factors that maximized the benefit
   - Share lessons learned with branches transitioning to the new procedure

---

*Analysis based on weekly service scores from weeks 6-22, with the procedure switch occurring in week 15. Statistical models use cluster-robust standard errors at the branch level.*
