# **Board Summary: Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **1.8 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant and represents approximately a **3.6% gain** in service quality. We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. The New Procedure Increased Service Scores**

| Group | Before Switch (Weeks 6-14) | After Switch (Weeks 15-22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------------|---------------------------|--------|
| Switched Branches (425 branches) | 50.75 | 53.49 | +2.74 |
| Control Branches (284 branches) | 49.08 | 49.99 | +0.91 |
| **Net Effect (Difference-in-Differences)** | | | **+1.84** |

- **Effect size:** +1.84 points on the service score index
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001 (highly significant)
- **95% Confidence Interval:** 1.49 to 2.19 points
- **Relative improvement:** 3.6% above the average branch score of 51

### **2. The Result Is Robust**

The positive effect holds across multiple analytical approaches:
- Simple comparison: +1.84 points
- Accounting for branch-specific differences: +1.84 points
- Accounting for both branch and week effects: +1.84 points
- Controlling for staffing and visitor volume: +1.82 points

This consistency confirms the result is not driven by other factors.

### **3. The Improvement Is Not Explained by Staffing or Visits**

We tested whether the service score gains might simply reflect changes in staffing levels or visitor volume. They do not:
- Staffing levels did not change differently for switched branches (p = 0.33)
- Visitor volume did not change differently for switched branches (p = 0.91)

**This means the service score improvement reflects genuine operational improvements from the new procedure itself**, not external factors.

### **4. Pre-Existing Trends Were Similar**

Before the switch (weeks 6-14), both groups showed parallel trends in service scores. While switched branches started slightly higher (a common pattern in voluntary adoption), the *rate of change* was similar. This supports the validity of the comparison.

## **Technical Notes**

- **Analysis method:** Difference-in-differences regression with branch and week fixed effects
- **Data:** 12,053 weekly observations from 709 branches over 17 weeks (weeks 6-22)
- **Treatment:** 425 branches adopted the new procedure starting week 15; 284 branches served as controls
- **All results are statistically significant at the 99% confidence level**
