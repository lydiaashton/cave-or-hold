# **Board Summary: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure is associated with a **modest improvement in service scores** (approximately +2 points on a ~50-point scale). However, this effect is **marginally statistically significant** (p≈0.09), meaning we cannot rule out that the observed improvement could be due to chance. The evidence suggests a positive trend, but additional data would strengthen the case for a full rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** total: 20 adopted the new procedure (treatment), 14 kept the old procedure (control)
- **17 weeks of data**: Weeks 6–14 (before switch), Weeks 15–22 (after switch)
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in treatment branches to changes in control branches

### **Service Score Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 46.2 | 45.6 | **-0.6** |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 49.6 | 51.0 | **+1.5** |

**Difference-in-Differences Estimate: +2.05 points**

This means branches using the new procedure improved by about 2 points more than branches using the old procedure, after accounting for underlying trends.

### **Statistical Significance**
- **p-value ≈ 0.09**: The result is significant at the 10% level but not at the conventional 5% level
- **95% Confidence Interval**: Approximately -0.3 to +4.6 points
- Interpretation: We estimate the true effect is likely positive, but there is meaningful uncertainty

### **Validity Checks**
- **Parallel trends assumption holds**: Before the switch, treatment and control branches showed similar week-to-week trends (p=0.89 for differential pre-trends)
- **Results are robust** to controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume
- **Effect varies by week**: Some post-switch weeks show stronger effects than others, suggesting the benefit may fluctuate

---

## **Next Steps & Recommendations**

### **Recommended Actions**

1. **Proceed with cautious expansion**: The evidence points in a positive direction. Consider rolling out the new procedure to additional branches while continuing to monitor results.

2. **Extend the observation period**: Collect 8–12 more weeks of data to:
   - Increase statistical power
   - Determine if the effect is stable over time
   - Reduce uncertainty around the estimate

3. **Monitor implementation fidelity**: Ensure branches adopting the new procedure are following it consistently, as variation in implementation could explain week-to-week fluctuations.

4. **Track secondary outcomes**: Continue monitoring staffing_index and visits_index to understand whether the new procedure affects operational efficiency or patron engagement.

### **Decision Framework**

| Scenario | Recommendation |
|----------|----------------|
| If effect stabilizes at ~2 points with p<0.05 | Full rollout recommended |
| If effect remains ~2 points but p>0.05 | Phased rollout with continued monitoring |
| If effect diminishes over time | Re-evaluate before further expansion |

---

## **Technical Notes**

- Analysis controls for branch fixed effects, week fixed effects, staffing levels, and visitor volume
- Standard errors are clustered at the branch level to account for repeated observations
- Service scores are an internal index where ~50 represents average branch performance
