# **Board Summary: New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure Evaluation**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **2.1 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.6 to 2.7 points) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents approximately a **4% improvement** over baseline scores. The result is statistically significant and robust to controls for staffing and visitor volume.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect
- **Estimated impact:** +2.14 service score points
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001 (highly significant)
- **95% Confidence interval:** 1.58 to 2.71 points
- **Effect size:** ~4.2% improvement relative to baseline

### 2. Study Design
This analysis used a **difference-in-differences** approach comparing:
- **Treatment group:** 425 branches that switched to the new procedure
- **Control group:** 284 branches that kept the old procedure
- **Pre-period:** Weeks 6–14 (before the switch)
- **Post-period:** Weeks 15–22 (after the switch)

This design isolates the effect of the new procedure by accounting for:
- Baseline differences between branches
- Overall time trends affecting all branches
- Weekly variations in staffing and visitor volume

### 3. Parallel Trends Validation
The analysis assumes treatment and control branches would have followed similar trends absent the intervention. Pre-switch data supports this:
- Both groups showed similar week-to-week variation before week 15
- The gap between groups remained stable before the switch
- After the switch, the treatment group pulled away consistently

### 4. Consistency of Results
- The treatment effect was **positive for 75% of treatment branches** (median improvement: 2.4 points)
- Control branches showed minimal change (median: 0.06 points)
- The effect persisted across all 8 post-switch weeks
