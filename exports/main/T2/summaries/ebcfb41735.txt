# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure **significantly improved service scores**. Contrary to the expectation of no difference, branches that switched to the new procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **1.8 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and represents a meaningful improvement in service quality.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **150 branches** total: 90 switched to the new procedure (60%), 60 kept the old procedure (40%)
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in switched branches against control branches while accounting for branch-specific characteristics and week-to-week variation

### Results

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 50.77 | 51.52 | +0.75 |
| Control branches | 51.24 | 50.19 | −1.05 |
| **Net effect (DiD)** | | | **+1.79** |

- **Effect size:** +1.79 points (95% confidence interval: 1.02 to 2.57)
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001 (highly significant)
- **Practical significance:** Approximately 3.5% improvement relative to the average service score of 51, or about 0.18 standard deviations

### Validity Checks
- **Parallel trends assumption confirmed:** Before the switch, both groups showed similar trends in service scores (p = 0.33 for pre-trend difference), supporting the validity of the comparison
- **Effect is robust:** The improvement persists after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volumes, indicating the new procedure itself—not other factors—drove the gain
- **No confounding changes:** Staffing and visitor patterns did not systematically differ between groups after the switch

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new stock rotation and shelving procedure to all remaining branches

2. **Monitor implementation:** Track service scores during rollout to confirm the effect holds across all branches

3. **Document best practices:** Capture what switched branches did differently to help train staff at remaining branches

4. **Consider cost-benefit analysis:** If the new procedure has different implementation or operating costs, weigh those against the 1.8-point service score improvement

5. **Explore mechanisms:** Investigate why the new procedure improved scores (e.g., faster item location, better organization, reduced staff time per task) to identify additional optimization opportunities

---

*Analysis based on weekly service score data from 150 branches over 17 weeks, using difference-in-differences regression with branch and week fixed effects.*
