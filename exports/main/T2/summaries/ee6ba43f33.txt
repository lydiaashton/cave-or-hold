# **MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation procedure shows a **positive but statistically uncertain** effect on service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure improved by approximately **2 points** on the service score index relative to branches that kept the old procedure. However, this result is **not statistically significant** at conventional confidence levels, meaning we cannot confidently rule out that the observed improvement occurred by chance. **We recommend a cautious approach: continue the pilot with additional branches or extend the observation period before committing to a system-wide rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in switching branches against non-switching branches

### Results

| Metric | Control Branches (Old Procedure) | Treatment Branches (New Procedure) |
|--------|----------------------------------|-----------------------------------|
| **Before Week 15** | 47.09 | 49.36 |
| **After Week 15** | 45.96 | 50.25 |
| **Change** | −1.13 | +0.89 |

**Estimated Effect of New Procedure: +2.02 points** (95% confidence interval: −0.5 to +4.7)

### Statistical Significance
- **p-value: 0.12** (above the 0.05 threshold for statistical significance)
- The confidence interval includes zero, meaning no effect is a plausible explanation for the data
- Results are robust to controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### Additional Observations
1. **Control branches declined slightly** after week 15 (−1.13 points), while treatment branches improved modestly (+0.89 points)
2. **Staffing and visits** both positively correlate with service scores, but adjusting for these factors does not change the main finding
3. **Pre-existing differences**: Treatment branches had slightly higher service scores than control branches before the switch (49.4 vs. 47.1), though the analysis accounts for this

---

## **Next Steps**

### Recommended Actions

1. **Extend the Pilot Period**
   - Continue monitoring for an additional 8–12 weeks to increase statistical power
   - More data would narrow the confidence interval and provide a clearer answer

2. **Consider Phased Rollout**
   - If operational benefits (e.g., staff efficiency, cost savings) exist independent of service scores, a gradual rollout to willing branches may be warranted
   - Avoid mandatory system-wide adoption until evidence is stronger

3. **Investigate Mechanisms**
   - Survey branch staff about operational impacts of the new procedure
   - Examine whether certain branch types (by size, location, or volume) benefit more than others

4. **Set a Decision Threshold**
   - Define what magnitude of improvement would justify rollout costs
   - Given the current estimate (~2 points, or ~4% of baseline), assess whether this meets the threshold

### Timeline
- **3 months**: Complete extended data collection
- **4 months**: Re-analyze with full dataset and present updated recommendation to the Board

## **Conclusion**

The new procedure shows promise but lacks definitive evidence of effectiveness. A measured approach—extending the pilot while gathering more data—balances the potential benefits against the risk of adopting a procedure that may not deliver meaningful improvements. We stand ready to support further analysis as additional data becomes available.
