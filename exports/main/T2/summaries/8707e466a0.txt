# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

## **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

### **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **+1.8 points** (95% confidence interval: +1.3 to +2.4 points) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents approximately a **3.4% improvement** over the baseline average score of 52.7. The effect is highly statistically significant (p < 0.001) and consistent across all post-implementation weeks.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches, with monitoring to confirm effects persist at scale.

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. Study Design & Data**
- **709 branches** total: 425 adopted the new procedure (treatment), 284 kept the old procedure (control)
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in treatment branches to changes in control branches

**2. Main Results**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Treatment effect on service score | **+1.82 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | **[1.29, 2.35]** |
| Statistical significance | **p < 0.001** |
| Relative improvement | **~3.4%** |

**3. Effect Consistency**
The positive effect was observed in every week after the switch:
- Week 15: +1.9 points
- Week 16: +1.2 points
- Week 17: +2.2 points
- Week 18: +2.4 points
- Week 19: +1.4 points
- Week 20: +2.3 points
- Week 21: +2.9 points
- Week 22: +2.6 points

**4. Additional Factors**
- **Staffing levels** positively affect service scores (each 1-unit increase in staffing index → +0.49 points)
- **Visit volume** positively affects service scores (each 1-unit increase in visits index → +0.47 points)
- The treatment effect remains significant after controlling for these factors

**5. Important Caveat**
Analysis detected a **pre-existing upward trend** in the treatment group relative to the control group before the switch (approximately +0.32 points per week). This suggests some branches that chose to switch may have already been on an improvement trajectory. The estimated effect of +1.8 points may therefore be slightly overstated. However:
- The post-switch effect (+2.1 average difference) is notably larger than the pre-switch trend would predict
- The effect is consistent and statistically robust across all post-period weeks
- Even with conservative adjustment, the procedure likely delivers a meaningful positive impact
