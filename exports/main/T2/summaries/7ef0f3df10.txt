# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation procedure shows a **modest positive effect** on service scores, but the evidence is **not statistically conclusive**. Branches that adopted the new procedure saw their service scores decline less than branches that kept the old procedure (a difference of about 2 points). However, **both groups experienced declines** in service scores after week 15, and only 30% of switched branches actually improved. We recommend **continued monitoring with a longer evaluation period** before making a system-wide rollout decision.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
- **34 branches** total: 20 switched to the new procedure, 14 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22); the switch occurred at week 15
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in switched branches against non-switched branches

### Main Results

| Group | Pre-Switch Avg | Post-Switch Avg | Change |
|-------|---------------|-----------------|--------|
| Switched Branches (Treatment) | 54.27 | 52.69 | **-1.58** |
| Non-Switched Branches (Control) | 50.35 | 46.84 | **-3.52** |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+1.94** |

- The new procedure is associated with a **~2-point cushion against decline** in service scores
- **Statistical significance**: p ≈ 0.09 (marginally significant; 95% confidence interval ranges from -0.5 to +4.4 points)
- **Pre-trend check**: No evidence that switched branches were on different trajectories before the switch (p = 0.29), supporting the validity of the comparison

### Important Context

1. **Both groups declined**: Service scores dropped across the cooperative after week 15. The new procedure did not produce improvements—it appears to have **mitigated a broader decline**.

2. **Limited branch-level success**: Only **6 of 20 switched branches (30%)** saw their scores improve after adoption. The remaining 14 switched branches still experienced declines, just smaller ones on average.

3. **Effect size**: A 2-point difference represents about **4% of the average service score** (~50 points). Whether this is operationally meaningful depends on the board's priorities.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Extend the evaluation period**: Eight weeks post-switch may be too short to capture the full effect. Recommend collecting data for at least 12–16 additional weeks.

2. **Investigate the overall decline**: Both groups saw service scores drop after week 15. The board should examine whether external factors (seasonal patterns, staffing changes, system-wide issues) affected all branches.

3. **Identify success patterns**: Analyze which switched branches improved (the 6 that did) to understand what conditions make the new procedure work best. Consider branch size, location, or baseline performance as potential factors.

4. **Pilot expansion with monitoring**: If proceeding with broader rollout, consider a phased approach with continued data collection to confirm effects before full implementation.

5. **Set clear success criteria**: Define what magnitude of improvement would justify the costs of system-wide adoption (e.g., "We need at least a 3-point sustained improvement with p < 0.05").

---

## **Recommendation**

**Do not proceed with immediate system-wide rollout.** The current evidence suggests the new procedure may provide modest protection against service score declines, but it does not demonstrate clear improvement. Extend the evaluation period and investigate the causes of the cooperative-wide score decline before making a final decision.
