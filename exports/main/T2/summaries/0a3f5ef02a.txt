# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

## **Title: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by **2.3 points** (approximately **4.5% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and consistent across the post-switch period. **We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Study Design and Data
- **709 branches** total: 425 adopted the new procedure, 284 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** (Weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring in Week 15
- Service scores average around 50 points (higher is better)
- Analysis uses a rigorous difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in switched branches against changes in non-switched branches

### 2. Main Result: Positive Impact on Service Scores
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 52.53 | 53.21 | +0.68 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 51.83 | 54.86 | +3.02 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+2.34** |

- The **2.34-point improvement** is statistically significant (p < 0.001)
- 95% confidence interval: **1.75 to 2.93 points**
- This represents a **4.5% improvement** relative to baseline service scores

### 3. Validity of the Analysis
- **Parallel trends confirmed**: Before the switch, both groups showed similar (flat) trends in service scores, supporting the validity of the comparison
- **Effect persists over time**: The improvement was evident immediately after the switch and remained stable or grew slightly over the 8-week post-switch period
- **Robust to controls**: The result holds even after accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume, which also positively affect service scores

### 4. Additional Observations
- Branches that switched were scoring slightly lower than control branches before the switch (by about 0.7 points), making the post-switch improvement even more notable
- Staffing and visit volumes are positively associated with service scores, but the procedure effect is independent of these factors

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all remaining branches

2. **Develop implementation timeline**: Consider phasing the rollout to manage training and transition logistics

3. **Monitor ongoing performance**: Continue tracking service scores after full implementation to confirm the effect persists at scale

4. **Document best practices**: Capture lessons learned from the 425 early-adopter branches to support training at remaining locations

5. **Consider cost-benefit analysis**: If the new procedure has implementation costs, weigh them against the 4.5% service improvement demonstrated here

## **Appendix: Technical Notes**

- Analysis method: Difference-in-differences regression with branch fixed effects and cluster-robust standard errors
- Data source: branches.csv (12,053 observations)
- All statistical tests significant at p < 0.01 level
- Event study analysis confirms no pre-existing divergent trends and stable post-switch effects
