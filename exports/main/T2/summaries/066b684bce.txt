### **BOTTOM LINE**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service quality.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **1.5 to 2.0 points** (about 3-4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. ### **KEY FINDINGS**

**1. Positive Treatment Effect**
- Treatment branches (those that switched) improved by **+1.3 points** from before to after the switch
- Control branches (those that didn't switch) declined by **-0.7 points** over the same period
- The **difference-in-differences estimate is +2.05 points** (p < 0.001), indicating the new procedure caused the improvement

**2. Effect is Robust**
- When using a more conservative comparison (only weeks 10-14 as the pre-period), the effect remains **+1.46 points** (p < 0.001)
- The improvement is **consistent across all 8 post-switch weeks**, with treatment branches outperforming control branches by 2.3 to 3.8 points each week
- The effect holds even after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

**3. Practical Significance**
- Average service score across all branches is approximately **51 points**
- The treatment effect represents a **3-4% improvement** in service quality
- This is a meaningful gain for a process change that doesn't require additional resources

**4. Important Context**
- Control branches experienced a slight decline in service scores after week 14 (-0.7 points), possibly due to seasonal factors or increased demand
- Treatment branches not only avoided this decline but achieved genuine improvement
- Both groups showed similar weekly patterns (peaks and valleys), suggesting the analysis properly accounts for common time trends

### **METHODOLOGY NOTE**

This analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing branches that switched to the new procedure against those that didn't, before and after week 15. Statistical significance was assessed using cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch) to account for repeated observations. The main limitation is that treatment branches showed slightly stronger pre-existing trends, so the conservative estimate (+1.46 points) may be more reliable than the full-sample estimate (+2.05 points). Both indicate a positive, significant effect.
