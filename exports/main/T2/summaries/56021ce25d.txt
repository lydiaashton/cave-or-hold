# **Board Summary: New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure Evaluation**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **2.2 points** (approximately **4% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and consistent across multiple analytical approaches. We recommend proceeding with a cooperative-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect
- **Estimated improvement:** +2.17 service score points (95% confidence interval: 1.59 to 2.75)
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001 (highly significant)
- **Practical significance:** ~4.2% improvement relative to the average service score of 51.9

### 2. Study Design
- **709 total branches** in the cooperative
- **425 branches** adopted the new procedure (treatment group)
- **284 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6-22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Analysis used a **difference-in-differences** approach with branch and week fixed effects, controlling for time-invariant branch characteristics and common weekly trends

### 3. Results Summary

| Group | Before Switch (Weeks 6-14) | After Switch (Weeks 15-22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------------|---------------------------|--------|
| Non-switched branches | 50.6 | 50.8 | +0.2 |
| Switched branches | 51.6 | 53.9 | +2.4 |
| **Net treatment effect** | | | **+2.2** |

### 4. Robustness Checks
- The treatment effect remains consistent (~2.15-2.17 points) across multiple model specifications:
  - Simple difference-in-differences
  - With staffing and visits controls
  - With branch and week fixed effects
- Staffing and visitor volume are positively associated with service scores, but the treatment effect persists after accounting for these factors

## **Caveats and Considerations**

1. **Pre-existing differences:** Switched branches had slightly higher service scores than non-switched branches before the procedure change (about 1 point higher on average). This suggests branches that volunteered or were selected for the new procedure may have been higher-performing to begin with. However, the fixed effects model accounts for time-invariant branch characteristics.

2. **Parallel trends:** Statistical testing indicates the two groups had reasonably similar trends before the switch, though not perfectly parallel. The estimated treatment effect should be interpreted with this in mind.

3. **Observation period:** We have 8 weeks of post-switch data. Longer-term monitoring would help confirm whether the improvement is sustained.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with cooperative-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all 284 branches currently using the old procedure.

2. **Monitor implementation** over the next 8-12 weeks to confirm the treatment effect holds at scale and identify any branches that may need additional support during the transition.

3. **Track service scores monthly** for the next 6 months to ensure the improvement is sustained and not a short-term effect.

4. **Document best practices** from the 425 early-adopter branches to support training and implementation at remaining branches.

5. **Consider cost-benefit analysis** if the new procedure has different resource requirements than the old procedure, to ensure the service score improvement justifies any additional costs.
