# **MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Contrary to our expectation that the change would make no difference, branches that switched to the new procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **2.4 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant and consistent across multiple analytical approaches. **We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.**

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### Study Design
- **150 branches** participated in the study (90 switched to the new procedure, 60 kept the old one)
- Data covers **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- We used a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in switched branches against changes in non-switched branches to isolate the effect of the new procedure

### Results

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Estimated effect of new procedure** | **+2.37 service score points** |
| **Statistical significance** | p < 0.01 (highly significant) |
| **95% confidence interval** | +0.76 to +3.99 points |
| **Relative improvement** | ~5% increase from baseline (~50 points) |

### What This Means
- Branches using the **new procedure** improved by **4.2 points** after the switch (from 46.6 to 50.8)
- Branches using the **old procedure** improved by **1.8 points** over the same period (from 47.6 to 49.4)
- The **additional 2.4-point gain** in switched branches is attributable to the new procedure itself, not broader trends

### Robustness Checks
The finding holds across multiple analytical specifications:
- ✓ Basic difference-in-differences model
- ✓ Model controlling for week-to-week variations
- ✓ Model controlling for individual branch characteristics
- ✓ Model with both branch and week controls

The parallel trends assumption (required for this analysis) appears reasonable—switched and non-switched branches followed similar trajectories before the change.

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all 150 branches

2. **Monitor implementation** over the next 8–12 weeks to confirm the effect persists at scale

3. **Document best practices** from the 90 early-adopter branches to support training at remaining branches

4. **Consider cost-benefit analysis**—while service scores improved, evaluate whether the new procedure requires additional staff time or resources

5. **Schedule follow-up review** in Q1 2027 to assess sustained impact and identify any branches needing additional support
