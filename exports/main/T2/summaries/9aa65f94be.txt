# **Library Branch Shelving Procedure: Impact Analysis**

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and rotation procedure is associated with a **modest improvement in service scores** (approximately +2.3 points, or about 4-5% above baseline). However, the evidence is **not statistically conclusive** (p ≈ 0.09-0.11), meaning we cannot rule out that the observed improvement occurred by chance. The effect also varies across branches—14 of 20 switching branches improved, while 6 saw declines.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with caution. Consider a phased rollout with continued monitoring rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. **Estimated Impact**
- **Difference-in-differences estimate:** +2.3 points on the service score index
- **95% confidence interval:** -0.4 to +5.0 points
- **Statistical significance:** Marginal (p ≈ 0.09–0.11); does not meet conventional thresholds (p < 0.05)
- **Practical significance:** Represents approximately 4-5% improvement over the baseline average score of ~50

### 2. **Group Comparisons**
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches (n=20) | 50.03 | 52.27 | +2.25 |
| Non-switched branches (n=14) | 49.11 | 49.06 | -0.05 |

The control group (non-switched branches) remained essentially flat, while switched branches showed improvement.

### 3. **Parallel Trends Assumption**
Analysis of pre-switch weeks (6-14) shows no systematic difference in trends between the two groups before the intervention, supporting the validity of the comparison.

### 4. **Variation Across Branches**
Among the 20 branches that switched:
- **14 branches** (70%) saw service score improvements
- **6 branches** (30%) saw declines
- Range of changes: -7.0 to +11.2 points

This heterogeneity suggests the new procedure may work better in some branch contexts than others.

### 5. **Control Variables**
Including staffing levels and visitor volume in the analysis did not materially change the results, suggesting the estimated effect is robust to these factors.

## **Next Steps**

1. **Continue monitoring** both switched and non-switched branches for at least 8-12 more weeks to strengthen statistical evidence.

2. **Investigate branch-level variation** to understand why some branches benefited more than others. Factors to examine may include:
   - Branch size and layout
   - Staff training and experience
   - Collection size and turnover rate
   - Patron demographics

3. **Consider a phased rollout** rather than immediate system-wide implementation:
   - Offer the new procedure to additional branches on a voluntary basis
   - Provide enhanced training and support during transition
   - Establish clear success metrics and review points

4. **Document implementation practices** at high-performing switched branches to identify best practices that could be shared during broader rollout.

5. **Revisit the decision** after one full quarter of additional data. If the effect remains positive and becomes statistically significant, proceed with cooperative-wide implementation.
