# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure **improved service scores** by approximately 2.1 points (about 4% relative to the baseline average of 50). This effect is statistically significant and consistent across the post-switch period. Contrary to the expectation that the procedure would make no difference, the evidence indicates a meaningful positive impact on branch service quality.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **60 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in service scores between switching and non-switching branches before and after the switch

### Main Results
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated treatment effect | **+2.1 points** on service score |
| 95% Confidence interval | [0.8, 3.4] points |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.01 (highly significant) |
| Relative improvement | ~4% above baseline |

### Supporting Evidence
1. **Parallel trends validated**: Pre-switch service score trends were statistically indistinguishable between the two groups (all pre-period coefficients p > 0.24), supporting the validity of the comparison.

2. **Consistent post-switch effects**: The positive effect appeared immediately after the switch (week 15) and persisted throughout the 8-week post-switch period, with weekly estimates ranging from +2.1 to +3.5 points.

3. **Robust to controls**: The result holds when controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume, and when accounting for branch-specific and week-specific factors.

4. **Control group trend**: Notably, control branch scores declined slightly after week 15 (−0.7 points), while treatment branch scores increased (+1.4 points), widening the gap.

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to remaining branches.

2. **Monitor implementation**: Track service scores during rollout to confirm effects persist at scale.

3. **Document best practices**: Capture what switching branches did during implementation to support training at remaining locations.

4. **Consider cost-benefit analysis**: If the new procedure has associated costs, weigh them against the 4% service score improvement.

5. **Longer-term follow-up**: Continue monitoring to assess whether effects are sustained beyond the initial 8-week post-switch window.

*Analysis based on weekly branch-level data from weeks 6–22. Statistical models include branch and week fixed effects with cluster-robust standard errors.*
