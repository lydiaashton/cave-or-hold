# Analysis of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

## Bottom Line

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw an average increase of **1.8 points** in their service scores compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents approximately a **3.7% improvement** over baseline scores. The effect is statistically significant (p = 0.001), and we recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## Key Findings

### Study Design
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure (treatment group); **60 branches** kept the old procedure (control group)
- The switch occurred in **week 15**
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in the treatment group to changes in the control group

### Results

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Treatment effect on service score | **+1.84 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | [0.76, 2.93] |
| P-value | 0.001 |
| Effect size (vs. baseline) | +3.7% |

### What This Means
- Branches that switched to the new procedure improved their service scores by **1.8 points more** than branches that did not switch
- Both groups saw some improvement after week 15 (control: +1.4 points; treatment: +3.2 points), but the treatment group improved significantly more
- The effect remains significant after controlling for staffing levels and visit volumes

### Parallel Trends Check
Before the switch (weeks 6–14), both groups showed similar trends in service scores, supporting the validity of the comparison. The pre-treatment trends were nearly parallel, with both groups showing slight week-to-week variation but no systematic divergence.

### Additional Insights
- **Staffing and visits matter:** Higher staffing indices and visit volumes are associated with higher service scores (both statistically significant)
- **Time effect:** All branches saw some improvement after week 15 (+1.4 points), suggesting seasonal or system-wide factors also influenced scores

## Next Steps

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches
2. **Monitor implementation** to ensure branches achieve similar gains as the pilot group
3. **Track service scores** for the next 8–12 weeks post-rollout to confirm sustained improvement
4. **Investigate mechanisms** – consider studying what aspects of the new procedure drive the improvement (e.g., faster restocking, better organization, reduced staff time on shelving)
5. **Cost-benefit analysis** – if the new procedure has additional costs (training, equipment, time), compare them against the value of a 1.8-point service score improvement
