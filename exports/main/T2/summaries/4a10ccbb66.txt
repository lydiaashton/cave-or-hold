# **Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure Evaluation**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new stock rotation and shelving procedure saw a statistically significant increase in service scores of **1.75 points** (approximately 3.3% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. The evidence supports rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** total: 20 adopted the new procedure (treatment group), 14 kept the old procedure (control group)
- **17 weeks of data**: Weeks 6-14 (before switch), Weeks 15-22 (after switch)
- **Analysis method**: Difference-in-Differences with branch and week fixed effects (controls for branch-specific characteristics and common time trends)

### **Main Result**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Treatment effect | **+1.75 points** |
| Standard error | 0.75 |
| 95% Confidence interval | [0.28, 3.21] |
| p-value | **0.020** (statistically significant) |

### **Effect Size**
- Average service score across all branches: **52.9 points**
- Improvement represents **3.3%** of the average score
- Effect size: **0.17 standard deviations** (small-to-moderate, meaningful for service quality)

### **Validity Checks**
✓ **Parallel trends confirmed**: Before the switch, treatment and control branches followed similar score trends (p=0.86), validating our comparison approach

✓ **Robust to controls**: Effect remains stable (1.76 points, p=0.018) when accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume

✓ **Consistent over time**: 7 out of 8 post-switch weeks showed positive effects; the improvement was not driven by a single week

### **Score Changes by Group**
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 50.26 | 56.37 | +6.10 |
| Control branches | 50.60 | 54.96 | +4.36 |
| **Net effect (DiD)** | | | **+1.75** |

*Note: Both groups improved after week 14 (possibly due to seasonal factors), but switched branches improved significantly more.*

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended Actions**
1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all 14 branches currently using the old method
2. **Monitor implementation**: Track service scores for the first 8-12 weeks after each branch switches to confirm expected gains
3. **Document best practices**: Capture what switched branches did during implementation to support training at remaining branches

### **Considerations**
- The effect is **statistically significant but modest** in magnitude—expect gradual improvement, not dramatic transformation
- **Week 15 showed the largest boost** (+4.0 points), possibly reflecting initial enthusiasm; subsequent weeks settled to a +1 to +2 point sustained improvement
- Continue tracking **staffing and visitor volume**, as these also influence service scores (visitor volume was a significant predictor in our analysis)

### **Timeline Estimate**
If the effect holds across all branches, the cooperative could expect an average service score increase from ~53 to ~55 points system-wide—a meaningful improvement in patron experience.
