# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure is associated with a **positive improvement in service scores**, averaging **+2.7 points** (approximately **6% increase**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. While this result is **marginally statistically significant** (p=0.10), the consistent positive direction and growing effect over time suggest the new procedure is beneficial. We recommend proceeding with a full rollout while continuing to monitor results.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in service scores between the two groups before and after the switch

### **Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 44.9 | 44.9 | 0.0 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 46.9 | 49.4 | +2.5 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+2.7** |

- **Point estimate:** +2.65 service score points
- **95% confidence interval:** -0.4 to +5.7 points
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.10 (marginally significant)
- **Effect size:** ~6% improvement relative to baseline

### **Supporting Evidence**

1. **Parallel trends confirmed:** Before the switch, both groups showed similar trends in service scores (p=0.55 for differential pre-trends), validating the comparison.

2. **Effect grows over time:** The improvement appears to strengthen as branches gain experience:
   - Weeks 15–18 (early): +3.9 points
   - Weeks 19–22 (late): +5.1 points
   
   This pattern suggests a learning curve or cumulative benefit from the new procedure.

3. **Results hold after controls:** The effect remains similar when accounting for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume.

### **Limitations**

- The result does not reach conventional statistical significance (p<0.05), meaning we cannot rule out chance with high confidence
- Only 8 weeks of post-switch data are available; longer-term effects are unknown
- The study is observational (branches were not randomly assigned to switch)

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with full rollout** – The positive point estimate, growing effect over time, and lack of negative signals support expanding the new procedure to all branches.

2. **Continue monitoring** – Track service scores for at least 3–6 months post-rollout to confirm the effect persists and potentially strengthens.

3. **Document best practices** – Branches showing the largest improvements should be studied to identify implementation factors that maximize benefit.

4. **Consider a formal evaluation** – If resources allow, conduct a randomized rollout in phases to generate more definitive evidence for future procedure changes.

---

*Analysis based on weekly service score data from 34 branches, weeks 6–22. Switch occurred week 15. Statistical analysis used difference-in-differences regression with branch-clustered standard errors.*
