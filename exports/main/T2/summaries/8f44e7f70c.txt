# **Board Summary: New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw a statistically significant increase in service scores of approximately **2.1 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.6 to 2.7 points), representing a **4.2% improvement** over baseline. This recommendation is supported by strong evidence, and we recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measurable Improvement in Service Quality
- **Treatment effect**: +2.1 points on the service score index (p < 0.001)
- **Treatment group** (425 branches that switched): Scores increased from 50.7 to 54.0 (+3.3 points)
- **Control group** (284 branches that kept old procedure): Scores increased from 50.7 to 51.9 (+1.2 points)
- The difference-in-differences estimate isolates the procedure's effect from general trends affecting all branches

### 2. Broad-Based Benefits
- **81% of branches** that adopted the new procedure saw improvement in their service scores
- Only 63% of control branches saw improvement during the same period
- The effect is consistent across branches of different sizes

### 3. Implementation Pattern
- **Week 1** after switching showed a temporary dip (-2.2 points), likely reflecting adjustment costs and staff learning
- **Weeks 2-8** showed sustained positive effects ranging from +1.4 to +3.0 points
- One outlier week (Week 21) showed unusually high scores across all branches, but the treatment effect remains significant even when excluding this week (+2.0 points)

### 4. Validity of Analysis
- Both groups started at identical baseline levels (50.7 points), supporting the validity of the comparison
- The analysis controls for staffing levels and visitor volume, which also affect service scores
- Results are robust to alternative specifications and outlier exclusion

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all 284 branches currently using the old method

2. **Plan for transition support**: The first-week dip suggests branches need support during implementation. Consider:
   - Training materials and on-site assistance during Week 1
   - Temporary staffing adjustments during the transition period
   - Clear communication that initial disruption is normal and temporary

3. **Monitor implementation**: Track service scores for the first 8 weeks after each branch switches to ensure they follow the expected pattern (initial dip, then sustained improvement)

4. **Document best practices**: Branches with the largest improvements (up to +14.5 points) may have implementation approaches worth sharing with others

5. **Continue evaluation**: Consider a follow-up analysis in 6 months to assess whether the improvement is sustained long-term
