## **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

### **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and rotation procedure shows **suggestive but not conclusive evidence** of improving service scores. Branches that switched saw an average improvement of approximately **2 points (4%)** relative to branches that kept the old procedure. However, this result is not statistically significant at conventional levels (p=0.09), meaning we cannot rule out that the observed improvement occurred by chance.

### **Key Findings**

| Metric | Treatment Branches (Switched) | Control Branches (No Switch) |
|--------|-------------------------------|------------------------------|
| Number of branches | 20 | 14 |
| Average score before switch | 49.7 | 49.6 |
| Average score after switch | 50.9 | 48.9 |
| Change in score | **+1.1** | **-0.8** |

**Difference-in-Differences Estimate: +1.96 points** (95% CI: -0.3 to +4.2)

- **60% of switched branches** improved their scores vs. only **36% of non-switched branches**
- The improvement is **consistent across all 8 post-switch weeks** — treatment branches outperformed control branches every week after the change
- Pre-switch trends were similar between groups (average gap of 0.1 points), supporting the validity of the comparison
- The effect size represents approximately a **4% improvement** over the baseline average score of 50

**Statistical Uncertainty:**
- The p-value of 0.09 means there's about a 9% probability of observing this result if the procedure had no real effect
- Our study could reliably detect effects of 3.5 points or larger; smaller effects (like the 2 points we observed) are harder to confirm with this sample size

### **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with cautious rollout** — The evidence points in a positive direction with no signs of harm. A full rollout is reasonable if the board accepts moderate uncertainty.

2. **Continue monitoring** — Track service scores for all branches after full implementation to confirm the effect persists at scale.

3. **Consider a phased expansion** — Roll out to additional branches in waves, allowing continued comparison and learning.

4. **Extend the evaluation period** — Collect more weeks of data to increase statistical power and reduce uncertainty around the effect size.

5. **Investigate branch-level variation** — Some branches showed larger gains than others. Understanding which branches benefit most could help optimize implementation.
