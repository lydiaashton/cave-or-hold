# **Board Summary: New Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure Evaluation**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **1.8 points** (approximately **3.5% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and robust across multiple analytical approaches. **We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **709 branches** total: 425 adopted the new procedure, 284 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks** of data (Weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at Week 15
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in the treatment group to changes in the control group

### **Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (Old Procedure) | 51.2 | 52.0 | +0.8 |
| Treatment (New Procedure) | 51.5 | 54.0 | +2.6 |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | | | **+1.8** |

- **Treatment effect: +1.84 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.28 to 2.39)
- **Statistical significance: p < 0.001** (highly significant)
- **Effect size: 3.5% improvement** relative to baseline scores

### **Robustness Checks**
- Results hold when controlling for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume
- All 709 branches had complete data for the full study period
- The effect is consistent across the post-switch weeks (Weeks 15–22)

### **Visual Evidence**

The attached visualizations show:
1. **Service Score Trends**: Treatment branches pulled away from control branches after Week 15, with the gap widening from near-zero to approximately 2 points
2. **Before/After Comparison**: Clear divergence in outcomes between the two groups after the switch
