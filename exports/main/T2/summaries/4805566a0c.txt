# **Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by an additional **+1.9 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant and represents a meaningful gain in service quality. We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **709 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **425 branches** adopted the new procedure (treatment group)
- **284 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Switch occurred at **week 15**; analysis compares 9 weeks before and 8 weeks after

### **Service Score Impact**

| Group | Pre-Switch Average | Post-Switch Average | Change |
|-------|-------------------|---------------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 48.1 | 49.2 | +1.1 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 50.5 | 53.6 | +3.1 |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | | | **+1.9** |

The **+1.9 point improvement** is:
- **Statistically significant** (p < 0.001)
- **95% confidence interval:** +1.4 to +2.5 points
- **Robust** after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### **Context**
- The system-wide average service score is approximately **50**
- Treatment branches now average **53.6** — notably **above benchmark**
- Control branches remain slightly below benchmark at **49.2**

## **Important Considerations**

1. **Pre-existing differences:** Treatment branches started with slightly higher scores (50.5 vs 48.1). The analysis accounts for this by comparing *changes* rather than absolute levels.

2. **Parallel trends:** The gap between groups was relatively stable before the switch, supporting the validity of the comparison.

3. **Covariates controlled:** Results hold after accounting for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume, suggesting the effect is attributable to the procedure change itself.

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all remaining branches.

2. **Monitor implementation** over the next 8–12 weeks to ensure the gains are sustained as more branches adopt the procedure.

3. **Document best practices** from the 425 early-adopter branches to support training and smooth transition for remaining branches.

4. **Consider additional analysis** to understand which aspects of the new procedure drive the improvement (e.g., rotation frequency, shelving organization, staff workflow changes).

5. **Track secondary outcomes** such as staff time savings, patron satisfaction, and material circulation rates to capture the full value of the change.
