# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and rotation procedure **improved service scores by approximately 2 points** (a 4% increase) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant and was not anticipated. We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **60 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in service scores between the two groups before and after the switch

### **Results**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Estimated treatment effect** | +1.99 points |
| **95% confidence interval** | +0.75 to +3.23 points |
| **Statistical significance** | p = 0.002 (highly significant) |
| **Relative improvement** | ~4% above baseline |

### **What the Data Shows**

**Before the switch (weeks 6–14):**
- Treatment branches: average score 48.6
- Control branches: average score 48.9
- Difference: negligible (branches were comparable)

**After the switch (weeks 15–22):**
- Treatment branches: average score 50.4
- Control branches: average score 48.6
- Difference: +1.8 points favoring treatment branches

The improvement appeared consistently across post-switch weeks, ranging from +0.8 to +2.8 points.

### **Robustness Checks**
- Results hold when controlling for weekly staffing and visit levels
- Results hold when excluding an unusually high-scoring week (week 14) from the pre-period
- Pre-switch trends between groups were parallel, supporting the validity of the comparison

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all 60 branches currently using the old method

2. **Monitor implementation** over the next 8–12 weeks to confirm the effect persists at scale

3. **Document best practices** from the 90 early-adopter branches to support training at remaining branches

4. **Consider cost-benefit analysis** — if the new procedure requires additional resources, weigh the 4% service improvement against implementation costs

5. **Continue tracking** service scores to ensure sustained improvement and identify any branches that may need additional support during transition

---

## **Technical Notes**

- Analysis uses cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch) to account for within-branch correlation over time
- The parallel trends assumption — critical for this analysis — is supported by the data: pre-switch score trajectories were similar between groups
- Effect size is meaningful: a 2-point increase on a ~50-point scale represents noticeable improvement in patron experience
