# **Board Summary: New Branch Procedure Evaluation**

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure is associated with a **modest improvement in service scores** (approximately +1.9 points on the 50-point average scale), but the evidence is **not statistically conclusive**. We recommend a **cautious, phased rollout** with continued monitoring rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect Estimate
- Branches that adopted the new procedure showed an average service score improvement of **1.87 points** relative to branches that kept the old procedure
- This represents approximately a **4% improvement** from the baseline average score of ~50
- The effect is **positive but marginally significant** (p-value ≈ 0.10), meaning there's about a 10% chance this result could occur by random variation

### 2. Study Design & Data
- **34 branches total**: 20 switched to the new procedure, 14 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data**: Weeks 6-14 (pre-switch) and Weeks 15-22 (post-switch)
- **578 observations**: Complete weekly data for all branches across the study period
- Analysis used a **difference-in-differences approach**, which controls for underlying trends and branch-specific characteristics

### 3. Parallel Trends Validation
- Before the switch, both groups showed similar week-to-week patterns in service scores
- The treatment group consistently scored lower than the control group (by about 3-4 points), but this gap was stable before the intervention
- This supports the validity of our comparison approach

### 4. Post-Switch Patterns
- The gap between treatment and control branches **narrowed after the switch** (from -3.78 to -1.91 points)
- The improvement was **not immediate**—effects varied week-to-week in the post period
- Some weeks showed larger gains (weeks 18-20), while others showed minimal improvement

### 5. Related Factors
- **Visits index** is positively associated with service scores (branches with more visits tend to score higher)
- **Staffing index** showed no clear relationship with service scores in this analysis
- These factors were similar across treatment and control groups, so they don't explain the observed effect

## **Next Steps**

### Recommended Actions

1. **Proceed with Phased Rollout**
   - The positive direction of the effect (+1.9 points) suggests the new procedure is beneficial
   - However, the marginal statistical significance warrants a cautious approach
   - Consider rolling out to an additional 10-15 branches while continuing to monitor

2. **Extend the Monitoring Period**
   - The current 8-week post-switch period may be too short to detect stable effects
   - Continue collecting data for at least 12-16 more weeks to confirm the pattern
   - Watch for whether the effect stabilizes, grows, or diminishes over time

3. **Investigate Variation Across Branches**
   - Some branches may benefit more than others from the new procedure
   - Analyze whether branch size, location, or baseline performance moderates the effect
   - This could help target the rollout to branches most likely to benefit

4. **Consider Complementary Improvements**
   - Since visit volume is associated with higher service scores, consider whether the new procedure works better when combined with other initiatives
   - Explore whether staffing adjustments could amplify the procedure's benefits

5. **Set Clear Success Metrics**
   - Define what magnitude of improvement would justify full rollout (e.g., +2 points with p < 0.05)
   - Establish a timeline for the final go/no-go decision (e.g., after 12 more weeks of data)
