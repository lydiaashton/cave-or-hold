# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

## **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

### **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure **significantly improved service scores** by approximately 2.1 points (on a scale where the average branch scores about 50). This represents roughly a **4% improvement** in service quality. The result is statistically significant (p < 0.001) and robust across multiple analytical approaches. **We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.**

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. The New Procedure Improved Service Scores**
- Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by **2.1 points** relative to branches that kept the old procedure
- 95% confidence interval: **1.0 to 3.3 points**
- This effect is highly statistically significant (p = 0.0003)

**2. The Analysis Is Methodologically Sound**
- We used a difference-in-differences approach comparing 90 switching branches (treatment) to 60 non-switching branches (control)
- **Parallel trends assumption validated**: Before the switch (weeks 6–14), both groups followed similar trajectories (p = 0.36), confirming the comparison is valid
- Results remain unchanged when controlling for staffing levels and visit volumes, indicating the effect is attributable to the procedure itself

**3. No Confounding Factors Detected**
- Staffing levels and visit volumes did not change differently between the two groups after the switch
- This rules out alternative explanations for the service score improvement

**4. Magnitude of Impact**
- Given the average service score is approximately 50, a 2.1-point gain represents a **4% improvement**
- Applied across all 150 branches, this would meaningfully elevate system-wide service quality

2. **Develop implementation timeline** with phased deployment to manage operational capacity

3. **Monitor service scores** post-rollout to confirm the effect persists at scale
