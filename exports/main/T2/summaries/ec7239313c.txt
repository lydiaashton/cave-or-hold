# Analysis of New Shelving & Stock Rotation Procedure

## Bottom Line

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2–4 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant (p < 0.001) and represents a **4–8% improvement** relative to the baseline average score of ~50. The improvement was sustained across all 8 weeks after the switch.

---

## Key Findings

### 1. Treatment Effect
| Method | Estimated Effect | 95% Confidence Interval |
|--------|-----------------|------------------------|
| Simple Difference-in-Differences | +2.02 points | 1.50 to 2.55 |
| Branch Fixed Effects Model | +3.62 points | 2.34 to 4.89 |
| Event Study (gap change) | +2.21 points | — |

All estimates are statistically significant at p < 0.001.

### 2. Study Design
- **425 branches** adopted the new procedure (treatment group)
- **284 branches** kept the old procedure (control group)
- Data covers **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Analysis uses difference-in-differences methodology with cluster-robust standard errors

### 3. Service Score Trends
| Period | Switched Branches | Control Branches | Gap |
|--------|------------------|------------------|-----|
| Before switch (weeks 6–14) | 50.3 | 48.7 | 1.6 points |
| After switch (weeks 15–22) | 55.6 | 52.0 | 3.6 points |

The gap between groups widened by **~2 points** after the switch, indicating the new procedure added value beyond existing differences between branches.

### 4. Effect Persistence
The treatment effect was **positive and significant in every single post-switch week** (weeks 15–22), with no evidence of the effect fading over time. In fact, the gap appears to grow slightly in later weeks.

### 5. Important Caveat
Branches that chose to switch had **already higher service scores** than control branches before the procedure change (average gap of 1.4 points in the pre-period). This suggests branches may have self-selected into the new procedure based on existing performance. However, the branch fixed effects model—which compares each branch to itself over time—still shows a significant positive effect, supporting the conclusion that the procedure itself caused improvement.

---

## Next Steps

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout.** The evidence strongly supports expanding the new procedure to all branches.

2. **Monitor implementation quality.** Since the effect persisted across 8 weeks without degradation, the procedure appears sustainable. Continue tracking service scores during rollout to ensure consistent results.

3. **Investigate mechanism.** Consider studying *why* the new procedure works (e.g., faster item location, better stock availability, improved staff efficiency) to optimize implementation.

4. **Control for confounders in future analyses.** The staffing_index and visits_index both positively correlate with service scores. Ensure these factors are monitored during rollout to distinguish procedure effects from staffing or traffic changes.

5. **Set realistic expectations.** A 2–4 point improvement is meaningful but modest. Communicate that the new procedure is one component of service quality, not a transformational change.

---

*Analysis based on 12,053 weekly observations from 709 branches. All statistical models use cluster-robust standard errors clustered at the branch level.*
