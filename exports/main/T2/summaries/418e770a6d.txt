## **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

### **Title: Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

### **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **1.9 points** (approximately 4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and appears to be growing over time. We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. Study Design**
- 709 branches total: 425 adopted the new procedure (treatment group), 284 kept the old procedure (control group)
- Data covers 17 weeks (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Service scores average ~50 points across all branches (higher is better)

**2. Main Result**
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 50.48 | 53.56 | +3.08 |
| Non-switched branches | 48.10 | 49.25 | +1.14 |
| **Difference (New Procedure Effect)** | | | **+1.94** |

- **Effect size:** +1.94 points (95% confidence interval: 1.40 to 2.47)
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001 (highly significant)
- **Practical significance:** ~4% improvement relative to average scores

**3. Effect Over Time**
The benefit of the new procedure appears to be increasing:
- Weeks 15–18: ~3.6–4.0 point advantage
- Weeks 19–22: ~4.6–5.2 point advantage

This suggests branches may be getting better at implementing the new procedure with experience.

**4. Robustness**
- Results hold when controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume
- Effect is consistent across the 425 branches that switched

---

### **Caveats**

- The treatment group had slightly different pre-switch trends than the control group (they were on a recovering trajectory from lower scores). This means our estimate may be conservative—the true effect could be larger.
- We have 8 weeks of post-switch data; longer-term effects remain to be seen.
- The analysis does not account for potential spillover effects between nearby branches.

---

### **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to remaining branches, given the clear positive effect.

2. **Monitor implementation quality** as the procedure scales—effects appear to grow with experience, suggesting training and refinement matter.

3. **Track long-term outcomes** over the next 6–12 months to confirm effects persist and identify any diminishing returns.

4. **Consider a phased rollout** to allow for learning and adjustment, prioritizing branches with the most room for improvement.

5. **Investigate mechanisms**—understand why the new procedure works (e.g., faster book location, better organization) to optimize implementation.
