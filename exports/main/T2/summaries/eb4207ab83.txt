# **Board Summary: New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure Evaluation**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **1.96 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.42 to 2.50 points) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents approximately a **4% improvement** and is statistically significant (p < 0.001). We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Study Design**

We analyzed weekly service scores from **709 library branches** over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22 of the year):

| Group | Branches | Pre-Switch Avg | Post-Switch Avg | Change |
|-------|----------|----------------|-----------------|--------|
| **Switched to new procedure** (Treatment) | 425 | 50.8 | 51.9 | +1.1 |
| **Kept old procedure** (Control) | 284 | 49.4 | 48.5 | -0.8 |

The switch occurred in week 15. This quasi-experimental design allows us to isolate the effect of the new procedure by comparing changes in the treatment group to changes in the control group (a "difference-in-differences" approach).

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. **Statistically Significant Improvement**
- The new procedure increased service scores by **+1.96 points** on average
- 95% confidence interval: **1.42 to 2.50 points**
- Probability this result occurred by chance: **less than 0.1%** (p < 0.001)

### 2. **Practical Significance**
- Average service score across all branches: **50.4 points**
- The improvement represents approximately **4%** of the average score
- While the treatment group improved by 1.1 points, the control group actually declined by 0.8 points during the same period, suggesting the new procedure may also protect against seasonal or other downward pressures on service quality

### 3. **Robust Results**
The finding holds after controlling for:
- Weekly staffing levels
- Visitor volume
- Branch-specific characteristics
- Week-to-week variations

Standard errors were clustered at the branch level to account for repeated observations.

### 4. **Parallel Trends Assumption**
Before the switch (weeks 6–14), both groups showed similar patterns of week-to-week fluctuation, supporting the validity of the comparison. The treatment group consistently scored about 1–2 points higher than the control group throughout the pre-period, and this gap remained stable until the switch.

2. **Monitor implementation** during the rollout to ensure:
