# **Board Summary: Impact of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Contrary to the initial expectation of no difference, branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by **2.1 points** (approximately **4.3%**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant (p < 0.01) and robust across multiple analytical approaches.

**Recommendation: Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Study Design
- **150 branches** analyzed over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15 (treatment group)
- **60 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in treatment branches relative to control branches before and after the switch

### 2. Main Results

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect of new procedure | **+2.13 points** |
| Statistical significance | **p = 0.001** (highly significant) |
| Relative improvement | **+4.3%** vs. average score of 49.8 |
| 95% Confidence Interval | **0.87 to 3.39 points** |

### 3. Service Score Trends

**Before the switch (weeks 6–14):**
- Treatment branches: 49.0 average
- Control branches: 49.3 average
- Difference: -0.3 points (not significant)

**After the switch (weeks 15–22):**
- Treatment branches: 51.3 average
- Control branches: 49.5 average
- Difference: +1.8 points

The treatment branches pulled ahead of control branches after adopting the new procedure, while control branch scores remained essentially unchanged.

### 4. Robustness Checks
The finding holds under multiple specifications:
- ✓ Controlling for branch-specific characteristics (fixed effects)
- ✓ Accounting for repeated observations per branch (clustered standard errors)
- ✓ Adjusting for staffing levels and visitor volume
- ✓ Pre-treatment trends show no systematic differences between groups (supports validity of the analysis)

### 5. Practical Significance
- Service scores average around 50 across the system
- A 2.1-point gain represents meaningful improvement in patron service quality
- The effect is consistent across the 8-week post-switch period
- No evidence of negative unintended consequences
