# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure shows a **positive but statistically uncertain effect** on service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure improved their service scores by approximately **2 points more** than branches that kept the old procedure. However, this difference is not statistically significant at conventional levels (p ≈ 0.12), meaning we cannot rule out that the observed improvement occurred by chance. **We recommend a cautious approach: continue the pilot with additional monitoring before committing to a system-wide rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **34 branches** participated over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in service scores between the two groups before and after the switch

### Results Summary

| Group | Before Switch (Weeks 6–14) | After Switch (Weeks 15–22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------------|---------------------------|--------|
| **Treatment (New Procedure)** | 51.4 | 55.1 | +3.8 |
| **Control (Old Procedure)** | 47.2 | 48.9 | +1.7 |
| **Difference-in-Differences Effect** | | | **+2.0 points** |

### Statistical Confidence
- The estimated 2-point improvement has a **p-value of approximately 0.12**
- This means there is about a 12% probability of observing this result (or larger) even if the new procedure had no real effect
- By conventional scientific standards (p < 0.05), this evidence is **not strong enough to confirm** the procedure caused the improvement
- The 95% confidence interval ranges from approximately **-0.5 to +4.6 points**, meaning the true effect could be negligible, modestly positive, or even slightly negative

### Additional Observations
- **Parallel trends assumption appears valid**: Both groups showed similar patterns in service scores before the switch, supporting the validity of the comparison
- **No confounding changes**: Staffing levels and visitor volumes did not change systematically in ways that would explain the results
- **Treatment branches started higher**: Branches that switched already had higher baseline service scores (~4 points higher on average), though the analysis accounts for this

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Continue the pilot program** for at least 8–12 more weeks to gather additional data and improve statistical power

2. **Monitor individual branch performance**: Some branches may benefit more than others; identify which branch characteristics predict success with the new procedure

3. **Consider a phased rollout**: If proceeding, implement in stages with careful monitoring rather than system-wide adoption

4. **Collect qualitative feedback**: Survey staff at switched branches about operational challenges and perceived benefits to complement the quantitative data

5. **Re-evaluate in Q2**: With additional data, re-run this analysis. If the effect remains around 2 points and becomes statistically significant, a full rollout would be justified
