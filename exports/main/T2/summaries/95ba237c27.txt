# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

## **Evaluation of New Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

### **Bottom Line**

The new procedure is associated with a **1.7-point improvement** in service scores (approximately **3.5%** above baseline). However, this result is **not statistically significant** at conventional levels (p = 0.14), meaning we cannot confidently rule out that the observed improvement occurred by chance. The evidence is **suggestive but inconclusive**.

---

### **Key Findings**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect of new procedure | +1.75 points |
| 95% Confidence Interval | -0.6 to +4.1 points |
| Baseline service score | 50.4 |
| Percentage improvement | 3.5% |
| Statistical significance | p = 0.14 (not significant) |

**What the data shows:**

1. **Positive direction, uncertain magnitude**: Branches that switched to the new procedure improved more than those that didn't (+2.4 points vs. +0.7 points), but the difference could plausibly be zero or as high as 4 points.

2. **Effects may grow over time**: When examining only weeks 18-22 (after branches had time to adapt), the estimated effect was larger at **3.2 points**. This suggests staff may need several weeks to fully implement the new procedure.

3. **Parallel trends hold**: Before the switch (weeks 6-14), treatment and control branches followed similar trajectories, supporting the validity of the comparison.

4. **Limited statistical power**: With only 34 branches (20 switched, 14 didn't), the study has limited ability to detect modest but meaningful effects.

---

### **Next Steps**

The board should consider the following options:

| Option | Rationale |
|--------|-----------|
| **1. Proceed with cautious rollout** | If a 3-4 point improvement is operationally meaningful and implementation costs are low, the positive (though uncertain) evidence may justify expansion. |
| **2. Extend the pilot period** | Continue monitoring for 8-12 more weeks to increase statistical power and confirm whether effects persist or grow. |
| **3. Expand the pilot strategically** | Roll out to additional branches in a phased manner, treating it as a learning opportunity to gather more data before systemwide implementation. |
| **4. Hold and reassess** | If implementation costs are high or disruption is a concern, wait for stronger evidence before proceeding. |

**Recommended approach**: Given the positive direction of results and the possibility of larger effects after an adaptation period, **Option 2 or 3** appears most prudent. This balances the potential benefit against the uncertainty in the current evidence.
