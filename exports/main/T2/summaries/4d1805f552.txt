## **Analysis of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

### **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **2.1 points** (95% confidence interval: 0.9 to 3.4 points) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents approximately a **4% improvement** over the baseline service score. The result is statistically significant (p < 0.001), and there is strong evidence that the improvement is attributable to the new procedure rather than other factors.

---

### **Key Findings**

1. **Study Design**: We analyzed 17 weeks of data (weeks 6–22) from 150 branches. Ninety branches switched to the new procedure starting in week 15, while 60 branches continued with the old procedure. This natural experiment allows us to isolate the effect of the new procedure.

2. **Treatment Effect**: Using a difference-in-differences analysis (the standard method for this type of before/after comparison with treatment and control groups), we found:
   - Switched branches improved by **2.3 points** after adoption (from 49.0 to 51.3)
   - Control branches showed minimal change (**0.2 points**, from 49.3 to 49.5)
   - The net effect of the new procedure: **+2.1 points**

3. **Statistical Confidence**: The 95% confidence interval (0.9 to 3.4 points) does not include zero, meaning we can be confident the effect is real and not due to random variation. The p-value of 0.0006 indicates less than a 0.1% probability that this result occurred by chance.

4. **Pre-Trend Validation**: Before the switch (weeks 6–14), there was no significant difference in trends between the two groups (p = 0.69). This confirms that the groups were comparable before the intervention and supports the validity of our findings.

5. **Robustness**: The result holds even after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume, suggesting the improvement is not explained by these other factors.

### **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with System-Wide Rollout**: The evidence strongly supports expanding the new procedure to all branches. The improvement is statistically significant, meaningful in magnitude, and unlikely to be due to chance or confounding factors.

2. **Monitor Implementation**: As the procedure rolls out, continue tracking service scores to confirm the effect persists at scale and across different branch types.

3. **Investigate Mechanisms**: Consider studying *why* the new procedure improved scores (e.g., faster book location, better organization, staff efficiency) to optimize training and implementation.
