# **Board Summary: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

Branches that adopted the new stock rotation procedure showed **higher service scores** after implementation compared to branches that kept the old procedure. The observed difference is approximately **1.8 points** on the service score index. However, **this analysis cannot definitively prove the new procedure caused the improvement** because branches that switched were already improving faster than other branches before the change. We recommend a more rigorous evaluation before system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Observed Performance Difference**
- **Switched branches** (59.9% of observations, ~425 branches) averaged a service score of **51.5**
- **Non-switched branches** averaged a service score of **48.9**
- After the switch (week 15 onward), the gap between groups widened from ~1.7 points to ~3.6 points

### **2. Statistical Analysis**
Using a difference-in-differences approach (comparing changes in switched vs. non-switched branches before and after week 15):
- **Estimated treatment effect: +1.8 points** (statistically significant, p<0.001)
- This estimate remains stable when controlling for staffing levels and visitor traffic

### **3. Critical Limitation: Pre-Existing Trends**
**The analysis reveals a significant concern:** Branches that switched to the new procedure were already on a different trajectory before the change:

| Period | Switched Branches | Non-Switched Branches |
|--------|-------------------|----------------------|
| **Before switch** (weeks 6-14) | +0.16 points/week | -0.07 points/week |
| **After switch** (weeks 15-22) | +0.39 points/week | +0.12 points/week |

This means:
- Switched branches were **already improving** before adopting the new procedure
- Non-switched branches were **slightly declining** before week 15
- The 1.8-point estimate likely **overstates** the true effect of the new procedure

## **Recommendations & Next Steps**

### **Immediate Actions**
1. **Do not proceed with system-wide rollout yet** — the current evidence is suggestive but not conclusive
2. **Continue monitoring** both switched and non-switched branches for at least 8-12 more weeks

### **Improved Evaluation Design**
3. **Randomized rollout**: If feasible, randomly assign remaining branches to switch now vs. later to enable causal inference
4. **Staggered adoption analysis**: Use more advanced methods (e.g., event study with branch fixed effects) to better isolate the treatment effect
5. **Control for confounders**: Investigate whether switched branches differ systematically (e.g., larger branches, urban locations, more resources)

### **Decision Framework**
6. **Cost-benefit analysis**: Even if the effect is smaller than 1.8 points, determine whether the improvement justifies implementation costs
7. **Pilot expansion**: Consider expanding to a few more branches with careful tracking before full rollout
