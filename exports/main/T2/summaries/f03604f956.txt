# **Memorandum to the Board**

## **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

### **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw their service scores increase by approximately **2.0–2.3 points** (about **4–5%**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and consistent across multiple analytical approaches. We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. Clear Positive Impact on Service Scores**
- Switched branches improved by an average of **2.3 points** after adopting the new procedure
- Non-switched branches showed minimal change (+0.2 points) over the same period
- The **difference-in-differences estimate of 2.0–2.3 points** isolates the effect of the new procedure from other factors

**2. Statistically Robust Results**
- Effect is highly significant (p < 0.001)
- 95% confidence interval: **+1.9 to +2.6 points**
- Results hold after controlling for staffing levels, visitor volume, and branch-specific characteristics

**3. Broad-Based Improvement**
- **73% of switched branches** saw their service scores improve
- Median improvement among switched branches: **+2.2 points**
- The visualization below shows the divergence between treatment and control groups after week 14:

![Service Score Trends](service_score_trends.png)

**4. Greatest Benefit for Struggling Branches**
Branches with lower initial performance saw the largest gains:

| Initial Performance | Average Improvement |
|---------------------|---------------------|
| Lowest quartile     | +3.8 points         |
| Second quartile     | +2.3 points         |
| Third quartile      | +1.6 points         |
| Highest quartile    | +1.3 points         |

This suggests the new procedure is particularly effective at helping underperforming branches catch up.

**5. Parallel Trends Validated**
Before the switch (weeks 6–14), both groups followed similar trends with no significant divergence, supporting the validity of the comparison.

---

### **Analysis Approach**

We used a **difference-in-differences** design, comparing:
- **Treatment group:** 425 branches that adopted the new procedure
- **Control group:** 284 branches that continued with the old procedure
- **Pre-period:** Weeks 6–14 (before switch)
- **Post-period:** Weeks 15–22 (after switch)

This quasi-experimental design isolates the effect of the procedure change from other factors affecting all branches (e.g., seasonal variations, system-wide initiatives).

---

### **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all remaining branches

2. **Prioritize lower-performing branches** for early implementation, as they stand to benefit most

3. **Monitor implementation quality** – ensure branches are following the new procedure correctly to realize the full benefit

4. **Track service scores post-rollout** to confirm the effect persists at scale

5. **Consider documenting best practices** from high-improvement branches to support training

6. **Evaluate cost-benefit** – if the new procedure requires additional resources, the ~4–5% service score improvement should be weighed against implementation costs
