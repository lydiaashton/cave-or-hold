# **Board Summary: New Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure Evaluation**

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure is associated with a **2.4-point improvement in service scores** (approximately 5% above the baseline average of 50). While this effect is in the expected direction and meaningful in practical terms, it is **not statistically significant** at conventional levels due to the limited sample size. The evidence is **suggestive but not conclusive**. We recommend proceeding with a **phased rollout** while continuing to monitor results.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** total: 20 switched to the new procedure (treatment), 14 kept the old procedure (control)
- **17 weeks** of data (weeks 6–22), with the switch implemented in week 15
- **9 weeks before** and **8 weeks after** the switch
- Analysis used a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in treatment branches to changes in control branches

### **Service Score Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (no switch) | 50.3 | 50.1 | -0.2 |
| Treatment (switched) | 48.8 | 51.0 | +2.2 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+2.4** |

- **Estimated effect: +2.38 points** (95% confidence interval: -0.7 to +5.4)
- **P-value: 0.125** (not statistically significant at the 0.05 level)

### **Supporting Evidence**

1. **Branch-level consistency**: 80% of treatment branches (16 of 20) showed improvement after switching, compared to only 57% of control branches (8 of 14)

2. **Effect grows over time**: The treatment effect was modest immediately after the switch but increased in later weeks (weeks 18–22 showed effects of 4.0–5.5 points, with week 22 reaching statistical significance)

3. **Parallel pre-trends**: Before the switch, both groups followed similar trends, supporting the validity of the comparison

4. **Not explained by other factors**: The improvement is not driven by changes in staffing levels or visit volumes

### **Why Isn't This Statistically Significant?**

The study was **underpowered** to detect an effect of this size:
- With 34 branches, we had only **35% statistical power** to detect a 2.4-point effect
- The minimum effect we could reliably detect (80% power) would be **4.2 points**
- Our observed effect (2.4 points) is meaningful but smaller than what this sample size can confirm with high confidence
