# Library Cooperative Branch Procedure Change: Impact Analysis

## Bottom Line

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure is associated with a **2-point improvement in service scores** (approximately 4% increase from baseline). While this effect is **moderately strong and consistent**, the statistical evidence is **suggestive but not definitive** (p≈0.10). We recommend a **phased rollout** with continued monitoring rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## Key Findings

### Study Design
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure at week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in treatment branches against changes in control branches

### Results

| Metric | Control (No Switch) | Treatment (Switched) |
|--------|---------------------|----------------------|
| Service score before week 15 | 50.89 | 49.69 |
| Service score after week 15 | 50.90 | 51.72 |
| **Change** | **+0.01** | **+2.03** |

**Difference-in-Differences Estimate: +2.02 points**

### Statistical Significance
- **p-value: ~0.10–0.11** (marginally significant; does not meet the conventional 0.05 threshold)
- **95% confidence interval: approximately -0.4 to +4.5 points**
- The data is consistent with a meaningful positive effect, but we cannot rule out no effect with high confidence

### Branch-Level Patterns
- **70% of treatment branches** showed improvement after switching
- **50% of control branches** showed improvement (expected by chance)
- Treatment branch improvements ranged from -3.5 to +10.4 points
- The parallel trends assumption (critical for this analysis) appears satisfied: pre-switch trends were not significantly different between groups

### Effect Size Context
- A 2-point gain represents about **4% improvement** relative to the typical branch score of ~50
- This is a **modest but meaningful** improvement in service quality

---

## Next Steps

### Recommended Actions

1. **Proceed with cautious expansion**: The evidence points to a positive effect, but uncertainty remains. Consider rolling out the new procedure to an additional 10–15 branches while maintaining a control group for continued evaluation.

2. **Extend the observation period**: Collect 8–12 more weeks of data to increase statistical power and confirm whether the effect persists over time.

3. **Investigate variation across branches**: Some branches saw large gains (+10 points) while others saw declines. Identify characteristics of branches that benefited most to target implementation effectively.

4. **Monitor implementation fidelity**: Ensure branches adopting the new procedure are following it consistently; variation in execution may explain differing results.

5. **Conduct cost-benefit analysis**: Before full rollout, weigh the 2-point service score gain against training costs, staff time, and any operational disruptions.

### What Would Strengthen the Evidence
- **More post-switch data**: Additional weeks would narrow the confidence interval
- **Replication**: If the effect holds across multiple rollout waves, confidence would increase
- **Mechanism analysis**: Understanding *why* scores improved (e.g., faster item location, better organization) would support the business case

---

## Conclusion

The new procedure shows **promising results** with a consistent positive direction and a 4% service score improvement. However, the statistical evidence is not yet strong enough to guarantee the effect is real rather than due to chance. A **measured, evidence-based expansion** with ongoing evaluation is the most prudent path forward.
