## **TITLE: Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

### **BOTTOM LINE:**
The new procedure is associated with a statistically significant improvement in service scores of approximately **1.7 points** (about 3% above average). However, branches that adopted the new procedure were already outperforming control branches before the switch, which limits our ability to claim the procedure alone caused the improvement. The evidence supports proceeding with a cautious rollout while monitoring results closely.

---

### **KEY FINDINGS:**

**1. Main Effect Estimate**
- Branches using the new procedure showed service scores **1.73 points higher** than expected based on control branch trends (95% CI: 1.2 to 2.3 points)
- This effect is statistically significant (p < 0.001)
- For a typical branch scoring 50, this represents an increase to ~51.7

**2. Statistical Analysis**
- Used a difference-in-differences approach comparing 425 branches that switched against 284 branches that kept the old procedure
- Data covers 17 weeks (weeks 6-22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Results are robust when controlling for staffing levels and visit volumes

**3. Important Caveat: Pre-Existing Differences**
- Treatment branches had higher service scores than control branches even *before* the procedure change
- The gap between groups was already widening before the switch (from 0.2 points in week 6 to 2.6 points in week 14)
- After the switch, the gap continued to widen (from 2.4 to 3.8 points)
- This suggests branches selected for early adoption may have been better-performing or better-managed branches

**4. Practical Significance**
- The 1.73 point improvement represents a 3.3% increase relative to the average score
- The effect is consistent across all 8 weeks following the switch

---

### **NEXT STEPS:**

1. **Proceed with Rollout (with caveats)** - The evidence suggests the new procedure does not harm service quality and likely provides a modest benefit

2. **Implement Phased Monitoring** - Track service scores for newly converted branches for at least 8 weeks and compare to the 1.7 point benchmark

3. **Investigate Selection Bias** - Document why certain branches were chosen for early adoption to ensure the remaining branches aren't systematically different

4. **Consider Additional Metrics** - Monitor staffing efficiency and patron satisfaction alongside service scores
