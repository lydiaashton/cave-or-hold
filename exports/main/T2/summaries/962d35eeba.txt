# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Library Cooperative Board  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure shows a **positive but statistically inconclusive effect** on service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure averaged approximately **2 points higher** on the service score index compared to branches that kept the old procedure. However, this difference is not statistically significant at conventional levels, meaning we cannot confidently conclude the new procedure caused the improvement. The data is consistent with effects ranging from a slight decrease (−0.5 points) to a moderate increase (+4.4 points).

**Recommendation:** Do not proceed with system-wide rollout at this time. Instead, continue monitoring and consider a more controlled pilot to obtain clearer evidence.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### 1. Analysis Approach
We used a **difference-in-differences** design comparing 17 branches that switched to the new procedure (treatment group) against 17 branches that kept the old procedure (control group). Data covers 17 weeks (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15.

### 2. Treatment Effect Estimate
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect | **+1.95 points** |
| Standard error | 1.24 |
| 95% Confidence interval | **−0.49 to +4.39** |
| P-value | 0.117 |

### 3. What the Numbers Mean
- **Before the switch:** Treatment group averaged 45.7; Control group averaged 47.3
- **After the switch:** Treatment group averaged 49.0; Control group averaged 48.5
- The treatment group improved by **3.3 points**, while the control group improved by **1.2 points**
- The **net difference** (2.1 points) is our best estimate of the procedure's effect

### 4. Important Caveats
- **Statistical uncertainty:** The confidence interval includes zero, so we cannot rule out that the observed difference is due to random variation
- **Pre-existing differences:** The two groups showed different service score patterns before the switch, which complicates interpretation
- **Limited data:** Only 8 weeks of post-switch data may be insufficient to detect a true effect if one exists
- **Effect size:** If real, a 2-point improvement represents approximately **4%** of the average service score—a meaningful but modest gain

### 5. Secondary Findings
- **Visits index** was positively associated with service scores (branches with more visits tended to score higher)
- **Staffing index** showed no clear relationship with service scores in this analysis

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

### Recommended Actions

1. **Pause system-wide rollout** until stronger evidence is available. Rolling out an unproven procedure across all 34 branches carries implementation costs without guaranteed benefits.

2. **Extend the observation period.** Collect 8–12 additional weeks of data to:
   - Increase statistical power
   - Determine if the effect grows, shrinks, or stabilizes over time
   - Better distinguish signal from noise

3. **Consider a stepped-wedge rollout.** Randomly assign remaining branches to switch in different weeks. This would:
   - Provide cleaner causal identification
   - Allow all branches to eventually benefit if the procedure proves effective
   - Generate more precise effect estimates

4. **Investigate implementation fidelity.** Verify that branches adopting the new procedure are implementing it consistently. Variation in execution could mask true effects.

5. **Monitor for heterogeneous effects.** Some branches may benefit more than others. Analyzing which branch characteristics predict success could inform targeted rollout.

### Decision Framework

| Scenario | Recommendation |
|----------|----------------|
| Effect becomes significant and positive (p < 0.05, CI entirely above 0) | Proceed with rollout |
| Effect remains inconclusive but point estimate positive | Consider phased rollout with continued monitoring |
| Effect becomes significant and negative | Discontinue new procedure |
| Effect shrinks toward zero over time | Maintain status quo |
