# **Board Summary: Impact of New Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new shelving and stock rotation procedure saw their service scores increase by approximately **2 points** relative to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and consistent across multiple analytical approaches. We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measurable Improvement in Service Scores
- **Treatment effect**: +2.05 points on the service score index (95% confidence interval: 0.8 to 3.3 points)
- **Statistical significance**: p = 0.001 (highly significant)
- **Effect size**: 0.21 standard deviations—a small-to-moderate but meaningful improvement

### 2. Clear Divergence After the Switch
| Group | Before Switch (Weeks 6-14) | After Switch (Weeks 15-22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------------|---------------------------|--------|
| Switched branches (90 branches) | 51.42 | 52.55 | **+1.14** |
| Control branches (60 branches) | 52.12 | 51.20 | **-0.91** |

The switched branches improved while control branches slightly declined, resulting in a net difference-in-differences effect of **+2.05 points**.

### 3. Branch-Level Results Support the Finding
- **61% of switched branches** showed improvement after adopting the new procedure
- Only **40% of control branches** showed improvement during the same period
- This pattern confirms the effect is not driven by a few outlier branches

### 4. Analysis is Robust
- **Pre-trend test**: Before the switch, switched and control branches followed similar trends (no statistically significant difference), validating our comparison approach
- **Controls for other factors**: The effect persists even after accounting for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume
- **Clustered standard errors**: Results account for the fact that we have multiple weekly observations per branch

### 5. Visual Evidence
The trend chart (attached) shows switched and control branches tracking closely together before week 15, then diverging afterward—with switched branches pulling ahead.
