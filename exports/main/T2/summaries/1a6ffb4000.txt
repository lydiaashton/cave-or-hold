# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

## **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

### **BOTTOM LINE**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **2.2 points** (approximately 4.4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This result is statistically significant and robust across multiple analytical approaches. We recommend proceeding with system-wide rollout.

---

### **KEY FINDINGS**

**1. Study Design & Data**
- **709 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **425 branches** switched to the new procedure at week 15; **284 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Service scores average ~50 points (higher is better)

**2. Impact of the New Procedure**
| Group | Pre-Switch Avg | Post-Switch Avg | Change |
|-------|---------------|-----------------|--------|
| Switched Branches | 51.40 | 53.99 | +2.59 |
| Non-Switched Branches | 50.45 | 50.85 | +0.40 |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | | | **+2.2** |

The **2.2-point improvement** is the estimated causal effect of the new procedure, after accounting for:
- Baseline differences between branches
- Overall time trends affecting all branches
- Weekly variations in staffing and visitor volume

**3. Statistical Confidence**
- **95% Confidence Interval:** 1.9 to 2.6 points
- **p-value:** < 0.001 (highly statistically significant)
- **Robustness:** The result holds across 5 different model specifications with varying controls

**4. Implementation Timeline**
The effect was **not immediate**:
- **Weeks 15–18:** Minimal measurable impact (branches were likely adjusting)
- **Weeks 19–22:** Clear positive effects emerged (1.1 to 1.7 points above baseline)

This suggests branches needed **4–5 weeks** to fully implement and realize benefits from the new procedure.

**5. Pre-Trends Validation**
Before the switch, branches that would later adopt the new procedure actually had *lower* service scores than control branches. This strengthens confidence in the result—the improvement cannot be attributed to pre-existing upward trends in the treatment group.

---

### **NEXT STEPS**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches

2. **Allow 4–6 weeks for implementation** at each branch before evaluating results, as the data shows benefits take time to materialize

3. **Monitor service scores** during rollout to ensure consistent results across different branch types and regions

4. **Document implementation best practices** from the 425 early-adopter branches to support training at remaining locations

5. **Consider a follow-up evaluation** in 3–6 months to assess whether the 2.2-point improvement is sustained long-term

---

### **ANALYTICAL APPROACH**

This analysis used a **difference-in-differences** design, the standard method for evaluating policy changes when some units adopt a treatment and others do not. The approach compares:
- How much switched branches changed (before vs. after)
- How much non-switched branches changed (before vs. after)
- The difference between these two changes isolates the effect of the new procedure

All models controlled for branch-specific characteristics, week-specific factors, staffing levels, and visitor volume to ensure the result reflects the procedure change itself.
