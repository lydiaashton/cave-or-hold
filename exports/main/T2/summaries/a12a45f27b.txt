# **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **1.9 points** (approximately **3.7% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and has been consistent across all post-switch weeks, with evidence of growing benefits over time.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect
- **Switched branches** improved by **+1.07 points** on average after adopting the new procedure
- **Control branches** (kept old procedure) declined by **-0.79 points** over the same period
- **Net treatment effect: +1.86 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.2 to 2.6 points)
- This represents a **3.7% improvement** relative to the baseline average service score of 51.5

### 2. Statistical Significance
- The treatment effect is highly statistically significant (p < 0.001)
- **61% of switched branches** showed improvement vs. only **39% of control branches**
- The difference in branch-level changes is significant (t-test p < 0.0001)

### 3. Effect Over Time
The benefit of the new procedure appears to grow over time rather than fade:

| Weeks After Switch | Score Difference (Switched − Control) |
|-------------------|--------------------------------------|
| Week 1            | +1.52                                |
| Week 4            | +2.43                                |
| Week 8            | +3.69                                |

This suggests branches may need time to fully realize the benefits of the new procedure.

### 4. Study Design
- **709 branches** total: 425 adopted the new procedure, 284 kept the old
- **17 weeks of data**: Weeks 6–14 (before switch), Weeks 15–22 (after switch)
- Analysis controls for branch-specific characteristics and time trends
- Pre-existing differences between groups were small and accounted for in the analysis

### 5. Related Factors
- Staffing levels and visitor volume are positively associated with service scores (as expected)
- The treatment effect remains significant after controlling for these factors
- No major differences in staffing or visit patterns between groups that would explain the results

## **Appendix: Methodology Notes**

This analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in service scores for branches that switched procedures against those that did not, before and after week 15. This method isolates the effect of the procedure change from other factors (like seasonal trends or system-wide changes) that might affect all branches simultaneously.

The analysis was conducted using weekly service score data for all 709 branches over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22). Results were validated using multiple statistical approaches including branch fixed effects regression and branch-level change comparisons.
