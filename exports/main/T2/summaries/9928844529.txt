## **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

### **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **1.9 points** (about 4%) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and not explained by changes in staffing or visitor volume. **We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. Positive Impact on Service Quality**
- The new procedure increased service scores by **1.93 points** (95% confidence interval: 0.70 to 3.16 points)
- This represents approximately a **4% improvement** relative to baseline service scores
- The result is statistically significant (p = 0.002), meaning there is less than a 1% chance this finding occurred by random variation

**2. Rigorous Analysis Method**
- We used a difference-in-differences approach comparing 90 branches that switched to the new procedure against 60 branches that did not
- Data covered 17 weeks (weeks 6-22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Results account for repeated observations at each branch and control for staffing levels and visitor volume
- Pre-switch trends between the two groups were reasonably parallel, supporting the validity of the comparison

**3. Effect Is Direct, Not Indirect**
- The improvement in service scores was **not** driven by changes in staffing levels or visitor volume
- This suggests the procedural change itself—likely through better organization, faster retrieval, or improved workflow—directly enhanced service quality

**4. Consistent Post-Switch Performance**
- After the switch, treatment branches consistently outperformed control branches in every week (weeks 15-22)
- The average gap widened from near-zero before the switch to approximately 1-2 points afterward
