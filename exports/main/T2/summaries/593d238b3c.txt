# **Memorandum to the Board**

## **Title: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure is associated with a **2.3-point improvement in service scores** (approximately 4% relative to the baseline average of 52.6). While this effect is not statistically significant at the conventional 5% level (p=0.088), it is marginally significant and the confidence interval suggests the true effect is likely positive. The evidence supports proceeding with a broader rollout, though continued monitoring is recommended.

---

## **Key Findings**

**Study Design**
- Analyzed 34 branches over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- 20 branches adopted the new procedure starting week 15; 14 branches continued with the old procedure
- Used a difference-in-differences approach to isolate the effect of the new procedure

**Results**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated treatment effect | +2.32 points |
| 95% Confidence interval | [-0.34, +4.99] |
| P-value | 0.088 |
| Relative improvement | ~4.4% |

**Supporting Evidence**
- **Pre-trend validation**: Before the switch, switched and control branches had similar service scores (difference not statistically significant, p=0.73), supporting the validity of the comparison
- **Control group trend**: Control branches actually saw service scores *decline* by 2.8 points after week 15, while switched branches remained relatively stable (declining only 0.45 points)
- **Robustness**: The estimated effect remains stable when controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

**Visual Summary**

The attached chart (`service_score_trends.png`) shows that after week 15, switched branches maintained higher service scores relative to control branches.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with rollout** — The weight of evidence favors the new procedure. The positive effect direction, marginally significant p-value, and meaningful effect size (4% improvement) support expanding to all branches.

2. **Continue monitoring** — Track service scores for 8–12 weeks post-rollout to confirm the effect persists at scale and across all branches.

3. **Investigate mechanisms** — Consider qualitative follow-up with branch staff to understand *why* the new procedure works (e.g., faster restocking, better organization, reduced errors).

4. **Set success thresholds** — Establish a target (e.g., maintain 2+ point improvement) and timeline for formal evaluation after full rollout.
