# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure **improved service scores** by approximately **2.2 points** (on a scale where the average branch scores about 50). This effect is statistically significant and was not expected based on prior assumptions. The evidence supports rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measurable Improvement in Service Scores
- Branches that switched to the new procedure saw their service scores increase by **2.2 points more** than branches that kept the old procedure
- This represents a **4.6% improvement** relative to the baseline average score of ~48
- The effect is **highly statistically significant** (p < 0.001), with a 95% confidence interval of 1.5 to 3.0 points

### 2. Strong Research Design
- **150 branches** were studied over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure; **60 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- The switch occurred in **week 15**, providing 9 weeks of "before" data and 8 weeks of "after" data
- Before the switch, both groups showed **parallel trends** in service scores, validating the comparison

### 3. Effect Is Robust
- The improvement persists after accounting for:
  - Individual branch characteristics (some branches consistently score higher or lower)
  - Week-to-week fluctuations (seasonal or temporal effects)
  - Staffing levels and visitor volumes (neither explains the effect)
- The effect size (**0.23 standard deviations**) is meaningful for an operational change

### 4. What We Expected vs. What We Found
| Expectation | Finding |
|-------------|---------|
| No difference in service scores | **Clear positive effect** of +2.2 points |
| Neutral impact on operations | **Improved service** without requiring additional staffing |

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches
2. **Monitor implementation** to ensure the effect is sustained as more branches adopt the procedure
3. **Document the new procedure** in detail to facilitate training and consistent adoption
4. **Consider investigating why** the new procedure works better—this could reveal additional opportunities for improvement
5. **Continue tracking** service scores post-rollout to confirm the effect holds at scale

---

## **Technical Note**

This analysis used a **difference-in-differences** approach, the standard method for evaluating policy changes when random assignment isn't feasible. The model controls for branch-specific factors and time trends, isolating the effect of the procedure change itself.
