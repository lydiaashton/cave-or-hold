# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new shelving and rotation procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **2.3 points** (approximately **5% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and consistent across the post-switch period. We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **150 branches** total: 90 adopted the new procedure, 60 kept the old one
- **17 weeks of data**: Weeks 6–14 (before switch), Weeks 15–22 (after switch)
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in switched branches to changes in non-switched branches

### **Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 49.75 | 53.60 | +3.85 |
| Non-switched branches | 49.73 | 51.08 | +1.35 |
| **Net effect** | | | **+2.50** |

After controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume, the estimated effect is **+2.33 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.0 to 3.7 points; p = 0.001).

### **Why We Trust This Result**

1. **Parallel trends check passed**: Before the switch, switched and non-switched branches followed similar trends (no significant divergence, p = 0.16)
2. **Effect is robust**: The result holds with or without controlling for staffing and visits
3. **Effect is sustained**: The improvement persisted consistently across all 8 post-switch weeks (averaging 2.1–4.0 points each week)

### **Context**
- Average service score across all branches is approximately 50
- A 2.3-point gain represents meaningful improvement in service quality
- The new procedure appears to deliver benefits beyond what can be explained by staffing or visitor volume changes

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to remaining 60 branches
2. **Monitor implementation** to ensure branches achieve similar gains
3. **Track service scores** for the next 8–12 weeks post-rollout to confirm sustained improvement
4. **Document best practices** from high-performing switched branches to support training

---

*Analysis based on weekly service scores from 150 branches, weeks 6–22. Statistical model controls for branch-level variation, staffing index, and visits index.*
