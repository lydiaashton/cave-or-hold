# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Library Cooperative Board  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new stock rotation procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by **2.2 points** (approximately 4.5% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and consistent across the post-switch period. **We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.**

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### 1. Treatment Effect
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Estimated Effect** | +2.20 service score points |
| **95% Confidence Interval** | [1.65, 2.76] |
| **Statistical Significance** | p < 0.001 (highly significant) |
| **Effect Size** | 0.21 standard deviations |

### 2. What the Data Shows

**Service Score Changes (Weeks 6-22):**
- **Control branches** (kept old procedure): 49.1 → 51.0 (+1.9 points)
- **Switched branches** (new procedure): 50.2 → 54.3 (+4.1 points)
- **Net effect attributable to new procedure: +2.2 points**

The improvement was not a one-time spike—switched branches maintained higher scores throughout the 8-week post-switch period, with the gap widening over time.

### 3. Study Design
- **709 branches** total (425 switched, 284 control)
- **17 weeks** of data (weeks 6-22)
- Switch occurred at **week 15**
- Analysis uses difference-in-differences methodology with cluster-robust standard errors
- Controlled for staffing levels and visitor volume

### 4. Important Caveats
- **Pre-trends:** There were some differences between switched and control branches before the switch. Switched branches started with slightly higher scores and showed some convergence toward control branches in the weeks immediately before the switch. However, the post-switch improvement is substantially larger than any pre-existing trend.
- **Magnitude:** While statistically significant, the 2.2-point improvement represents a modest but meaningful gain (about 4-5% of the average score).

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

### Recommended Actions:

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches
   - Expected impact: ~2-point service score improvement across the cooperative

2. **Implement in phases** over the next quarter
   - Prioritize branches with lowest current service scores
   - Allow 2-3 weeks per branch for staff training and adjustment

3. **Continue monitoring** after rollout
   - Track service scores for at least 8 weeks post-implementation per branch
   - Verify the effect holds across different branch sizes and communities

4. **Document best practices** from early-adopter branches
   - Capture any implementation tips that helped maximize the benefit

### Questions for Further Investigation:
- Does the effect vary by branch size, location, or community demographics?
- Are there complementary changes (e.g., staffing adjustments) that could amplify the benefit?
- What is the cost-benefit ratio compared to other potential service improvements?

---

## **CONCLUSION**

The evidence supports rolling out the new procedure cooperative-wide. The 2.2-point service score improvement is statistically robust, practically meaningful, and sustained over the observation period. While the effect is modest, it represents a low-cost operational change with measurable benefits for patron experience.
