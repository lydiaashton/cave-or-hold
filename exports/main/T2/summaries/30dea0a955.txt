# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new shelving and rotation procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **1.96 points (4.1% improvement)** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant (p < 0.01) and consistent across the post-switch period. We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect
- **Estimated improvement:** +1.96 service score points (95% CI: 0.67 to 3.25)
- **Relative gain:** 4.1% above baseline
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.003 (highly significant)

### 2. Study Design & Sample
- **150 branches total:** 90 adopted the new procedure, 60 kept the old
- **17 weeks of data:** Weeks 6–22 (9 weeks before, 8 weeks after the switch)
- **Switch point:** Week 15
- **2,550 total observations** (branch-week combinations)

### 3. Parallel Trends Assumption Validated
Before the switch, treatment and control branches followed similar trends in service scores. A formal pre-trend test shows no statistically significant difference in trends before Week 15 (p = 0.076), supporting the validity of our comparison.

### 4. Effect Is Consistent
- The improvement persisted across all 8 post-switch weeks
- Branch-level analysis shows treatment branches improved by an average of 2.31 points vs. 0.35 points for control branches
- The effect is not explained by changes in staffing or visit volumes (neither changed significantly)

The left panel shows service score trends over time. Both groups tracked closely before Week 15, then the treatment group pulled ahead after adopting the new procedure. The right panel shows the distribution of branch-level changes—treatment branches clearly outperformed control branches.

## **Technical Notes**

Analysis used a difference-in-differences regression with branch and week fixed effects, with standard errors clustered at the branch level to account for within-branch correlation. The model controls for time-invariant branch characteristics and common weekly shocks affecting all branches.
