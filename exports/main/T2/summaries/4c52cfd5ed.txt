# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure **significantly improved service scores**. Branches that adopted the new procedure saw an average increase of **2.4 points** (approximately **5%**) in their service scores compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This result is statistically significant (p < 0.001), meaning it is very unlikely to have occurred by chance.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **150 branches** total: 90 adopted the new procedure, 60 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks** of data (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Analysis used a **difference-in-differences** approach, which compares changes in the treatment group to changes in the control group while accounting for branch-specific characteristics

### Results
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect of new procedure | **+2.39 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | [1.09, 3.70] |
| P-value | 0.0003 |
| Effect size | 4.8% of average service score |

### What This Means
- The new procedure is associated with a **meaningful and reliable improvement** in service quality
- The confidence interval indicates we can be 95% confident the true effect lies between **1.1 and 3.7 points**
- The improvement is **not explained by** staffing levels or visit volumes (both controlled for in the analysis)

### Validity Checks
- **Parallel trends confirmed:** Before the switch, treatment and control branches were trending similarly (p = 0.50), supporting the validity of the comparison
- **Robust to controls:** The result holds when accounting for weekly staffing and visit patterns

## **Technical Notes**

- Analysis controls for branch fixed effects (accounting for inherent differences between branches)
- Standard errors clustered at the branch level to account for repeated observations
- Service score baseline average: ~50 points (higher is better)
