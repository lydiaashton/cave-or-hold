# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

## **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

### **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **1.9 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant and represents a meaningful gain in service quality. We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. Measurable Improvement in Service Scores**
- Treatment branches (90 branches that switched) improved by an average of **3.4 points** from before to after the switch
- Control branches (60 branches that did not switch) improved by only **1.5 points** over the same period
- The **net effect of the new procedure is +1.9 points** on the service score index

**2. Strong Statistical Evidence**
- The effect is highly statistically significant (p < 0.01)
- 95% confidence interval: **+0.8 to +3.1 points**
- Results are robust across multiple analytical approaches, including controls for staffing levels and visit volumes

**3. Broad-Based Benefits**
- **83% of treatment branches** saw their service scores improve after switching
- Only 68% of control branches improved during the same period
- The effect is consistent across the treatment group—72% of switching branches outperformed the average control branch

**4. Valid Comparison**
- Treatment and control branches had similar service scores before the switch (difference of less than 1 point)
- Both groups showed parallel trends before the intervention, supporting the validity of the comparison
- The improvement appeared immediately after week 15 when the switch occurred and persisted throughout the post-period

**5. Context for the Effect Size**
- The average branch service score is approximately 50
- A 1.9-point improvement represents roughly a **4% increase** in service quality
- This is a meaningful improvement given that the index is designed with 50 as the average

---

### **Analysis Approach**

This evaluation used a **difference-in-differences** design, which compares:
- **Treatment group:** 90 branches that adopted the new procedure at week 15
- **Control group:** 60 branches that continued with the old procedure

The analysis controlled for:
- Branch-specific characteristics (fixed effects)
- Week-to-week variations (time fixed effects)
- Staffing levels and visit volumes

The parallel trends assumption was verified—treatment and control branches followed similar trajectories before the switch, with no statistically significant divergence (p = 0.56).
