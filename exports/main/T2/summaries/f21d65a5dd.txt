## **Analysis of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

### **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation procedure is associated with a **modest improvement in service scores** (approximately 2.6 points, or 5% above baseline). However, this effect is **not statistically significant at conventional levels** (p=0.09), meaning we cannot rule out that the observed improvement occurred by chance. The evidence is suggestive but not conclusive.

| Metric | Value |

| **Estimated treatment effect** | +2.6 service score points |
| **95% Confidence interval** | -0.4 to +5.7 points |
| **Statistical significance** | p = 0.09 (marginally significant) |
| **Effect size** | ~5% improvement over baseline |

**Study Design:**
- 34 branches total: 20 switched to the new procedure, 14 kept the old procedure
- 17 weeks of data (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in switched branches against changes in non-switched branches

**Supporting Evidence:**
1. **Parallel trends validated:** Before the switch (weeks 6–14), treatment and control branches showed similar trends in service scores (p=0.82), supporting the validity of the comparison.
2. **Consistent results:** The estimated effect remains stable (~2.6 points) whether or not we control for staffing and visit levels.
3. **Directionally positive:** All switched branches improved more than non-switched branches after week 15 (treatment group: +3.5 points vs. control group: +0.9 points).

**Limitations:**
- The confidence interval includes zero, so we cannot be certain the effect is real
- With only 34 branches and 8 post-switch weeks, statistical power is limited
- Service scores may be influenced by unmeasured factors

3. **Investigate heterogeneity:** Examine whether certain types of branches (e.g., by size, location, or traffic) benefit more from the new procedure to target rollout effectively.
