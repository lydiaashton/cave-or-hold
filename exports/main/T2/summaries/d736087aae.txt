# **Library Cooperative Procedure Change: Impact Analysis**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new shelving and stock rotation procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.2 points** (about **4% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and became more pronounced after branches had time to adjust to the new system.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measurable Improvement in Service Quality
- **Treatment effect:** +2.21 points on the service score index (95% CI: 0.99 to 3.43)
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001 (highly significant)
- **Relative improvement:** ~4% increase from baseline

### 2. Strong Research Design Validates the Result
The analysis used a **difference-in-differences** approach comparing:
- **90 treatment branches** that switched to the new procedure
- **60 control branches** that kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6-22), with the switch occurring at week 15

**Parallel trends confirmed:** Before the switch, both groups showed similar trends in service scores, validating that the post-switch difference is attributable to the procedure change rather than pre-existing differences.

### 3. Effect Strengthened Over Time
- **Weeks 15-18 (early adoption):** No significant effect (-0.54 points, p=0.31)
- **Weeks 19-22 (later period):** Significant positive effect (+1.54 points, p=0.03)

This suggests branches needed 3-4 weeks to fully realize the benefits, likely due to a learning curve.

### 4. Branch-Level Results
| Metric | Switched Branches | Control Branches |
|--------|-------------------|------------------|
| Average score change | +0.79 | -1.33 |
| % with improvement | 51% | 28% |

More than half of switched branches improved, versus fewer than 30% of control branches.

### 5. Other Factors
Staffing and visit levels also affect service scores (both positively significant), but the procedure effect holds even after controlling for these factors.

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to remaining 60 branches.

2. **Provide transition support** during the first 4 weeks after implementation, as benefits take time to materialize. Consider:
   - Additional training during the adjustment period
   - Temporary staffing support if needed
   - Weekly check-ins to address implementation challenges

3. **Monitor service scores** for the next 8-12 weeks post-rollout to confirm the effect persists at scale.

4. **Document best practices** from the 90 early-adopter branches, especially those with the largest improvements, to help other branches accelerate their learning curve.

5. **Consider a follow-up study** in 6 months to assess whether the improvement is sustained long-term and whether there are any unintended consequences (e.g., staff workload, operational costs).
