# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.0 points** (95% CI: 1.4 to 2.6) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents a **3.8% improvement** over the baseline average score of 52.2. The effect is statistically significant (p < 0.001).

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **709 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **425 branches** adopted the new procedure (treatment group)
- **284 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Procedure change implemented at **week 15**
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in treatment branches to changes in control branches

### Results
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Treatment effect on service score | **+1.98 points** |
| 95% Confidence interval | [1.41, 2.55] |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 |
| Effect size | 3.8% improvement |

### What the Data Shows
- **Before the switch (weeks 6–14):** Treatment and control branches had nearly identical average service scores (52.15 vs. 52.27), with differences fluctuating randomly around zero.
- **After the switch (weeks 15–22):** Treatment branches pulled ahead, with service scores averaging 1.8 points higher than control branches.
- **Control branches** actually saw a slight decline (-0.33 points) after week 15, while **treatment branches** improved (+1.62 points).
- The improvement in treatment branches was **consistent across most post-switch weeks**, with the largest gains appearing in weeks 20–22.

### Additional Observations
- **Staffing and visits matter:** Both staffing index and visits index are positively associated with service scores (each 1-point increase correlates with ~0.5 point higher service score). The treatment effect holds even after accounting for these factors.
- **Variation across branches:** Individual branch results varied widely (some branches saw declines, others saw gains of 10+ points), but the average effect is clearly positive.

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout.** The evidence strongly supports expanding the new procedure to all branches. The 2-point improvement is both statistically significant and practically meaningful for patron experience.

2. **Monitor implementation closely.** Track service scores at newly converted branches to ensure they achieve similar gains. Some branches underperformed—investigate whether branch-specific factors (size, location, staffing) affect outcomes.

3. **Document the new procedure.** Capture what makes the new procedure effective so training can emphasize the critical elements.

4. **Continue data collection.** Maintain weekly service score tracking to verify the effect persists over time and to identify any branches that may need additional support.

5. **Consider a phased rollout.** If resources are limited, prioritize branches with characteristics similar to the high-performing treatment branches in this study.
