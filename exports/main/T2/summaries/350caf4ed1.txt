# **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.2 points** (about 4.4%) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant (p=0.001) and robust to controlling for staffing and visitor volume. **We recommend proceeding with a full rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure; **60 branches** continued with the old procedure
- The switch occurred in **week 15**, allowing comparison of before/after periods for both groups

### **Impact on Service Scores**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 49.46 | 53.24 | +3.78 |
| Non-switched branches | 48.55 | 50.30 | +1.75 |
| **Net effect (DiD)** | | | **+2.03** |

After controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume, the estimated effect is **+2.18 points** (95% confidence interval: 0.93 to 3.44).

### **Statistical Validity**
- **Parallel trends assumption holds:** Before the switch, both groups showed similar trends in service scores (p=0.194), supporting the validity of the comparison
- **Highly significant result:** The probability of observing this effect by chance is less than 0.2%
- **Robust to controls:** The effect persists after accounting for weekly staffing and visitor volume variations

### **Context**
- Average service score across all branches is approximately 50
- The improvement represents a meaningful **4–5% gain** in service quality
- Both groups improved after week 15, but switched branches improved substantially more

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve systemwide rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches
2. **Monitor implementation** to ensure the effect replicates at scale
3. **Document the new procedure** to capture best practices from early-adopting branches
4. **Consider follow-up analysis** after full implementation to assess long-term effects and any variation by branch characteristics

---

## **Technical Notes**

Analysis used a difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch). The model controlled for staffing index and visits index. A pre-trends test confirmed no significant differential trends between treatment and control groups before the switch.
