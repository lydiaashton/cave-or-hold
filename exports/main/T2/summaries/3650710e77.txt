# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

## **New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure: Impact on Service Scores**

### **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure is associated with a **statistically significant improvement in service scores**. Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **1.8 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.3 to 2.4 points) compared to branches that retained the old procedure. This represents a **3.4% improvement** relative to the average service score. Based on this evidence, we recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. Measurable Positive Effect**
- The new procedure produced an estimated **+1.82 point increase** in service scores
- This effect is highly statistically significant (p < 0.001)
- The improvement is equivalent to about **0.18 standard deviations**—a modest but meaningful gain

**2. Study Design and Data**
- **709 branches** total: 425 adopted the new procedure, 284 retained the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring in week 15
- Analysis used a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in treatment branches to changes in control branches while accounting for branch-specific characteristics

**3. Service Score Trends**

| Period | Control Branches | Treatment Branches | Gap (T − C) |
|--------|-----------------|-------------------|-------------|
| Before switch (weeks 6–14) | 51.54 | 51.83 | +0.29 |
| After switch (weeks 15–22) | 52.45 | 54.57 | +2.12 |
| **Change** | **+0.91** | **+2.74** | **+1.83** |

**4. Supporting Factors**
- The analysis controlled for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume, both of which also positively affect service scores
- The treatment effect remained consistent after accounting for these factors

**5. Important Caveat**
- Treatment branches showed some improvement relative to control branches even before the switch (the gap widened from −0.33 points in early weeks to +1.07 points just before the switch). This suggests the branches selected for early adoption may have been on a more positive trajectory already. While our statistical methods account for fixed branch differences, this pre-existing trend means the true effect of the procedure could be somewhat smaller than our estimate.

### **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with Rollout**: The evidence supports expanding the new procedure to all branches, with an expected service score improvement of 1–2 points per branch.

2. **Monitor Implementation**: Track service scores during the rollout to confirm the effect holds across different branch types and operational contexts.

3. **Investigate Mechanisms**: Consider studying *why* the new procedure improves scores (e.g., faster item location, better shelf organization, reduced staff time searching) to optimize training and implementation.

4. **Long-term Follow-up**: Continue monitoring to ensure the effect persists beyond the initial 8-week post-switch period and does not diminish over time.

5. **Cost-Benefit Analysis**: If data on implementation costs are available, compare the cost of rollout against the value of improved service scores to quantify return on investment.
