# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Contrary to initial expectations that the change would have no effect, branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **1.9 points** (approximately 4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This finding is statistically significant and robust. **We recommend proceeding with a systemwide rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure (treatment group)
- **60 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Switch occurred in **week 15**
- Analysis uses difference-in-differences methodology with branch fixed effects and clustered standard errors

### **Treatment Effect**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect | **+1.88 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | **[0.66, 3.10]** |
| P-value | **0.0025** |
| Relative improvement | **~3.9%** (vs. ~50 point average) |

### **Validity Checks**
- **Parallel trends assumption holds**: Pre-treatment trends were statistically similar between treatment and control groups (p = 0.44)
- **Effect persists with controls**: Results remain significant after adjusting for staffing and visit indices
- **Consistent post-treatment**: 6 of 8 post-switch weeks showed positive effects, with several statistically significant

### **What This Means**
- The improvement is **real and measurable**, not due to random variation
- The effect size is **practically meaningful**—nearly 2 points on a ~50-point scale
- There is **no evidence of negative consequences** from the procedure change

- **Model**: Difference-in-differences regression with branch fixed effects
- **Controls**: Staffing index, visits index
- **Standard errors**: Clustered at branch level
- **Software**: Python (statsmodels)
