# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure is associated with a **1.7-point improvement in service scores** (approximately 3% above baseline). This effect is positive and meaningful in practical terms, though the statistical evidence is suggestive rather than definitive (p ≈ 0.10). We recommend proceeding with a **phased rollout** while continuing to monitor results.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **34 branches** tracked over 17 weeks (weeks 6-22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in the switched branches against changes in the non-switched branches

### Results
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 50.89 | 50.92 | +0.03 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 52.01 | 53.79 | +1.78 |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | | | **+1.75** |

- The new procedure is associated with a **1.75-point increase** in service scores
- This represents approximately **3.3% improvement** relative to the average baseline score of 52
- The effect is **marginally statistically significant** (p ≈ 0.10), meaning we have moderate but not definitive confidence the effect is real

### Validity Checks
- **Parallel trends assumption holds**: Before the switch, there was no statistically significant difference in trends between the two groups (p = 0.68), supporting the validity of our comparison
- **Other factors matter**: Staffing levels and visit volumes are significant predictors of service scores, confirming the score captures real operational conditions

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with phased rollout**: Given the positive direction and practical significance of the effect, we recommend expanding the new procedure to remaining branches, but in stages rather than all at once.

2. **Continue monitoring**: Track service scores for the next 8-12 weeks as additional branches adopt the procedure. This will increase statistical power and confirm the effect.

3. **Investigate mechanisms**: Since staffing and visit levels affect service scores, examine whether the new procedure works better under certain conditions (e.g., high-traffic periods, specific staffing levels).

4. **Set success thresholds**: Define clear metrics for the full rollout (e.g., sustained 1.5+ point improvement over 3 months) to evaluate whether the procedure delivers expected benefits at scale.

5. **Document best practices**: Branches that switched showed improvement—capture their implementation experiences to support training for remaining branches.
