# **Board Summary: New Branch Procedure Evaluation**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new procedure saw service scores increase by **2.2 points (4.3% improvement)** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and robust. We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect
- **Estimated improvement: +2.2 points** on the service score index
- **95% confidence interval: 1.86 to 2.56 points**
- **Statistical significance: p < 0.001** (highly significant)
- **Relative improvement: 4.3%** above the average branch score of ~50

### 2. What Happened to Each Group
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| **Treatment (New Procedure)** | 51.0 | 52.0 | **+1.0** |
| **Control (Old Procedure)** | 51.0 | 49.9 | **-1.2** |

The treatment effect of +2.2 points represents the *difference* between these two changes. Notably, branches that kept the old procedure actually experienced a decline in service scores after week 15, while branches with the new procedure improved.

### 3. Study Design & Data
- **709 branches total**: 425 switched to new procedure, 284 kept old procedure
- **17 weeks of data**: Weeks 6-22 (switch occurred at week 15)
- **12,053 observations**: One row per branch per week
- Analysis used difference-in-differences regression with branch and week fixed effects, controlling for staffing and visit levels

### 4. Robustness Checks
The finding holds when:
- Controlling for staffing levels and branch visits
- Accounting for branch-specific characteristics (fixed effects)
- Accounting for week-specific factors (fixed effects)

### 5. Caveat
There is some evidence that treatment and control groups began diverging slightly in the 1-2 weeks immediately before the switch (weeks 13-14). However, trends were parallel for the earlier period (weeks 6-12), and the magnitude of the post-switch effect is substantial and consistent.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches

2. **Monitor implementation** closely during the transition:
   - Track service scores weekly for the first 8 weeks post-switch
   - Ensure branches receive adequate training and support

3. **Investigate why control branches declined**: Understanding why branches keeping the old procedure saw scores drop could reveal additional opportunities for improvement

4. **Consider complementary improvements**: Since staffing and visits both positively affect service scores (independent of the procedure), ensure adequate resourcing accompanies the rollout

5. **Set expectations**: Communicate to branches that they can expect approximately a 2-point improvement in service scores after switching
