# **Board Memo: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **2.4 points** (approximately **5% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and consistent across all post-implementation weeks. We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect
- The new procedure increased service scores by **2.37 points** on average (95% confidence interval: 1.07 to 3.68 points)
- This represents approximately a **5% improvement** relative to the average branch score of 48.5
- The result is highly statistically significant (p < 0.001)

### 2. Robustness
The finding holds across multiple analytical approaches:
- **Basic model:** +2.37 points
- **With staffing and visitor controls:** +2.35 points
- **With branch fixed effects:** +2.38 points

### 3. Validity of the Analysis
- **Parallel trends assumption supported:** Before the switch (weeks 6-14), treatment and control branches showed no systematic difference in their service score trends (joint pre-treatment test: p = 0.35)
- **Consistent post-treatment effects:** Every week after the switch (weeks 15-22) showed positive treatment effects, ranging from +2.4 to +4.6 points
- **Large sample:** 150 branches (90 treatment, 60 control) observed over 17 weeks (2,550 total observations)

### 4. Study Design
- **Treatment group:** 90 branches that switched to the new procedure
- **Control group:** 60 branches that kept the old procedure
- **Pre-period:** Weeks 6-14 (before switch)
- **Post-period:** Weeks 15-22 (after switch)

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to the remaining 60 branches

2. **Monitor implementation** during the rollout to ensure consistent adoption and identify any branches that may need additional support

3. **Continue tracking service scores** for at least 8-12 weeks post-rollout to confirm the effect persists at scale

4. **Consider cost-benefit analysis** to quantify the operational costs of the new procedure against the service quality gains

5. **Investigate mechanisms** — the analysis shows *that* the procedure works; future analysis could explore *why* (e.g., whether effects are mediated through staffing efficiency or patron experience)

---

## **Technical Notes**

Analysis used a difference-in-differences design with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch). An event study analysis confirms no pre-treatment differences and shows the treatment effect emerged immediately after week 14 and remained positive throughout the observation period.
