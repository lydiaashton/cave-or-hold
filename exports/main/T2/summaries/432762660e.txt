## **Impact of New Shelving Procedure on Library Service Scores**

### **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure **significantly improved service scores** at branches that adopted it. Branches using the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.0 points** (a 4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and the analysis supports rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

### **Key Findings**

**Study Design**
- 150 branches total: 90 switched to the new procedure, 60 kept the old procedure
- Data covers 17 weeks (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in scores between switching and non-switching branches before and after the change

**Results**
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 48.55 | 51.72 | +3.17 |
| Non-switched branches | 47.24 | 48.35 | +1.12 |
| **Net effect** | | | **+2.04** |

- **Estimated impact: +2.04 points** (95% confidence interval: 0.72 to 3.37)
- **Statistical significance: p = 0.003** (highly significant)
- **Effect size: 4.2% improvement** relative to baseline scores

**Validity Check**
- Pre-switch trends were parallel between the two groups (p = 0.31), meaning branches that switched were on similar trajectories to those that didn't before the change. This supports the validity of the comparison.
- The gap between groups widened consistently after the switch, with treated branches outperforming control branches by an average of 3.4 points in the post-period versus 1.3 points in the pre-period.
