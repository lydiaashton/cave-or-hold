# **Library Branch Service Score Analysis: New Shelving & Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new shelving and rotation procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **1.8 points** (approximately **3.7% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and consistent across most treatment branches. We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. **Treatment Effect**
- The difference-in-differences analysis shows the new procedure increased service scores by **1.84 points** (95% confidence interval: 0.64 to 3.04 points; p = 0.003).
- This result holds even after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume, which confirms the effect is attributable to the procedure change itself.

### 2. **Branch-Level Impact**
- **67% of switched branches** showed improvement after adopting the new procedure.
- Only **40% of control branches** (those keeping the old procedure) showed improvement during the same period.
- On average, switched branches gained +1.24 points while control branches declined by -0.60 points.

### 3. **Parallel Trends Validated**
- Before the switch (weeks 6-14), treatment and control branches followed similar trends, with treatment branches consistently scoring about 1.5 points lower.
- Statistical testing confirms no significant difference in pre-treatment trends (p = 0.67), validating our comparison.
- After the switch (week 15 onward), the gap between groups narrowed substantially, indicating the treatment effect.

### 4. **Study Design**
- **150 branches total**: 90 adopted the new procedure, 60 kept the old procedure.
- **17 weeks of data**: Weeks 6-14 (pre-switch), Weeks 15-22 (post-switch).
- Analysis uses cluster-robust standard errors to account for repeated observations per branch.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new shelving and rotation procedure to all remaining branches.

2. **Monitor implementation** during the transition:
   - Track service scores for the first 8-12 weeks after each branch switches.
   - Expect similar ~1.8 point improvements based on this analysis.

3. **Investigate variation**: About one-third of switched branches did not show improvement. Consider:
   - Branch-specific factors (size, staffing, layout) that may affect implementation success.
   - Additional training or support for branches that struggle with the new procedure.

4. **Continue data collection**: Maintain weekly service score tracking to confirm the effect persists long-term and to identify any branches needing additional support.

---

*Analysis based on 2,550 weekly observations from 150 branches. Statistical methods: Difference-in-Differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors.*
