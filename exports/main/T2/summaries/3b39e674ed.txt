# **MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2025  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure shows a **positive effect on service scores**, with switching branches improving by approximately **1.8 points (3.7%)** relative to branches that kept the old procedure. However, this result is **not statistically significant at conventional levels** (p=0.09), meaning we cannot rule out that the observed improvement occurred by chance. The evidence is **promising but not conclusive**. We recommend a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than an immediate full deployment.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **34 branches** total: 20 switched to the new procedure, 14 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data**: Weeks 6–14 before the switch, Weeks 15–22 after
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences approach**, comparing changes in switching branches against non-switching branches

### Results

| Group | Pre-Switch Average | Post-Switch Average | Change |
|-------|-------------------|---------------------|--------|
| Switched Branches | 49.2 | 50.2 | +1.0 |
| Non-Switched Branches | 48.0 | 47.5 | -0.5 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+1.8** |

### Statistical Assessment
- **Estimated effect**: +1.79 service score points
- **95% Confidence Interval**: -0.30 to +3.87 points
- **P-value**: 0.093 (marginally significant)
- **Interpretation**: The true effect could reasonably be anywhere from a small decline to a meaningful improvement of nearly 4 points

### Supporting Evidence
- **Parallel trends assumption holds**: Before the switch, both groups showed similar week-to-week patterns with no systematic differences
- **Control variables**: Staffing levels positively affect service scores (as expected), but the procedure effect persists after accounting for this
- **Consistent direction**: 7 of 8 post-switch weeks showed positive effects, though individual weeks vary

---

## **Next Steps & Recommendations**

### 1. **Proceed with Cautious Expansion**
Given the positive direction of the effect and no evidence of harm, consider expanding the new procedure to additional branches while maintaining a control group for continued evaluation.

### 2. **Extend the Observation Period**
Eight weeks post-switch may be insufficient to detect a stable effect. Recommend collecting **8–12 additional weeks of data** to:
- Increase statistical power
- Assess whether the effect grows as staff become more familiar with the procedure
- Rule out short-term novelty effects

### 3. **Monitor Implementation Quality**
The variability in weekly effects suggests implementation may differ across branches. Consider:
- Documenting how faithfully each branch follows the new procedure
- Identifying best practices from high-performing switching branches
- Providing additional training where needed

### 4. **Conduct Cost-Benefit Analysis**
Before full rollout, quantify:
- Staff time required to implement the new procedure
- Any material or operational costs
- Whether a ~2-point service score improvement justifies the investment

### 5. **Set a Decision Threshold**
Recommend reconvening in **8–10 weeks** with updated data. If the effect remains positive and achieves statistical significance (p<0.05), proceed with full cooperative-wide rollout.

---

## **Technical Notes**

Analysis used difference-in-differences regression with week fixed effects and cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch). The model controlled for staffing levels and visitor volume. The parallel trends assumption was validated through event-study analysis showing no pre-existing differences between groups.
