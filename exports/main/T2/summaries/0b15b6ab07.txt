# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.1 points** (about 4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This result is statistically significant and contradicts our prior expectation that the change would make no difference. **We recommend proceeding with a full rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measurable Improvement in Service Scores
- Branches using the new procedure improved by **2.1 points** on the service score index after the switch
- The 95% confidence interval is **[0.9 to 3.3 points]**, indicating the true effect is very likely positive
- This represents approximately a **4% improvement** relative to baseline scores

### 2. Strong Statistical Evidence
- The effect is **highly statistically significant** (p = 0.0005)
- Analysis controlled for other factors like staffing levels and visitor volume, which did not explain away the effect
- The result holds across 150 branches over 17 weeks of data

### 3. Valid Comparison Between Groups
- Before the switch (weeks 6-14), treatment and control branches showed **parallel trends** in service scores
- This confirms the comparison is valid—any difference after week 15 can be attributed to the new procedure rather than pre-existing differences

### 4. Context on the Effect Size
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| New Procedure (90 branches) | 50.3 | 52.4 | +2.1 |
| Old Procedure (60 branches) | 48.3 | 48.4 | +0.1 |

The control group remained essentially flat, while the treatment group showed clear improvement.

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with full rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches
2. **Monitor implementation** to ensure the effect is sustained as more branches adopt the procedure
3. **Document the new procedure** in detail to ensure consistent implementation across all branches
4. **Consider investigating why** the new procedure works—understanding the mechanism could help optimize it further
5. **Track service scores quarterly** after full rollout to confirm the improvement persists at system-wide scale

---

## **Methodology Note**

This analysis used a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing branches that switched to the new procedure against those that did not, before and after week 15. Standard errors were clustered at the branch level to account for repeated measurements. The parallel pre-trends assumption was tested and confirmed.
