# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.2 points** (about **4.4%**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and consistent across most branches. We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **60 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in switched branches against changes in control branches

### Impact on Service Scores
| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|---------------|---------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 50.65 | 52.54 | +1.89 |
| Control branches | 50.84 | 50.51 | -0.34 |
| **Net effect** | | | **+2.23** |

- The **2.23-point improvement** is statistically significant (p < 0.01)
- 95% confidence interval: **1.4 to 3.0 points**
- This represents a **4.4% improvement** relative to the average service score of 51.2

### Consistency and Robustness
- **70% of switched branches** saw their service scores improve after adopting the new procedure
- The effect holds even after accounting for:
  - Branch-specific characteristics (fixed effects)
  - Week-to-week variation (time fixed effects)
  - Staffing levels and visit volumes (both positively associated with scores but unchanged by the procedure)
- **Parallel trends verified**: Before the switch, both groups followed similar trajectories, supporting the validity of the comparison

### What Did NOT Drive the Result
The improvement is not explained by changes in staffing or customer visits—neither measure changed differently for switched branches versus control branches. The new procedure itself appears to be the cause.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all 150 branches

2. **Monitor implementation** during rollout to ensure branches adopt the procedure as intended

3. **Track service scores** post-rollout to confirm the effect persists at scale

4. **Investigate variation**: While 70% of branches improved, about 30% saw little change or decline. Understanding what differentiates these branches could help maximize benefits during rollout

5. **Consider complementary investments**: Since staffing and visits also positively affect service scores, branches may benefit from the new procedure combined with adequate staffing levels

---

*Analysis based on weekly service score data from weeks 6–22. The switch occurred at week 15. Statistical methods include difference-in-differences regression with branch and week fixed effects.*
