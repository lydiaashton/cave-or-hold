# **Analysis of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**
## Impact on Service Scores

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Contrary to the expectation that the change would make no difference, branches that adopted the new procedure saw their service scores increase by approximately **2.0 points** (about **4% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This result is statistically significant and robust across multiple analytical approaches.

**Recommendation: Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### 1. Treatment Effect
- Branches using the new procedure experienced a **+2.03 point increase** in service scores after the switch (95% confidence interval: 0.78 to 3.27 points)
- This represents approximately a **4% improvement** relative to the baseline average score of 52.4
- The effect is **statistically significant** (p = 0.001), meaning there is less than a 0.2% probability this result occurred by chance

### 2. Study Design & Data
- **150 branches** total: 90 adopted the new procedure, 60 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring in week 15
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in treatment branches to changes in control branches

### 3. Validity Checks
- **Parallel trends confirmed**: Before the switch, treatment and control branches were following similar trends (p = 0.94), supporting the validity of the comparison
- **Results are robust**: The finding holds when controlling for staffing levels, visitor volume, and branch-specific characteristics
- **Effect grows over time**: The benefit appears to increase by about 0.4 points per week after implementation, suggesting branches may improve further as they gain experience

## **METHODOLOGY NOTE**

This analysis uses a quasi-experimental difference-in-differences design, which is the standard approach for evaluating policy changes when random assignment is not feasible. Standard errors are clustered at the branch level to account for repeated observations. Results are consistent across specifications with and without control variables (staffing index, visits index) and branch fixed effects.
