# **Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**
The new procedure is associated with a **statistically significant improvement of 1.9 points** in service scores (about 4% relative to baseline). Branches that adopted the new procedure maintained their service levels while non-adopting branches experienced a notable decline. **We recommend proceeding with system-wide rollout**, with continued monitoring.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Study Design
- **150 branches total**: 90 adopted the new procedure, 60 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6-22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Analysis uses **difference-in-differences methodology** with branch and week fixed effects (controls for branch-specific and time-specific factors)

### 2. Service Score Impact
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated treatment effect | **+1.89 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | 0.76 to 3.02 |
| Statistical significance | **p < 0.01** (highly significant) |
| Relative improvement | ~3.8% above baseline |

### 3. What Happened to Each Group

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| **Switched Branches** (Treatment) | 50.54 | 50.37 | -0.17 (stable) |
| **Non-Switched Branches** (Control) | 49.85 | 47.79 | -2.06 (decline) |

**Key Insight**: The new procedure appears to have **protected** branches from a system-wide decline in service scores that affected non-switched branches.

### 4. Robustness
- Effect remains significant across multiple model specifications
- Results control for staffing levels and visit volumes
- Standard errors clustered at branch level (accounts for within-branch correlation)

### 5. Practical Significance
- A 1.9-point improvement on a ~50-point scale is meaningful for service quality
- Preventing the 2-point decline seen in control branches is valuable
- Effect is consistent across the 8-week post-switch period

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **PROCEED WITH ROLLOUT**: Evidence supports expanding the new procedure to all 150 branches.

2. **MONITOR IMPLEMENTATION**: Track service scores during rollout to ensure effects are replicated at scale.

3. **INVESTIGATE MECHANISMS**: The control group's decline warrants investigation. Was there a system-wide factor (seasonal, staffing changes, etc.) that the new procedure helped mitigate?

4. **COST-BENEFIT ANALYSIS**: Compare the operational costs of the new procedure against the service score benefits to confirm net positive impact.

5. **LONG-TERM FOLLOW-UP**: Continue monitoring for 3-6 months to assess whether effects persist or diminish over time.
