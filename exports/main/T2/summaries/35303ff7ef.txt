# **Board Summary: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new rotation and shelving procedure maintained service scores approximately **2 points higher** than they would have under the old procedure. We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

## **Key Findings**

### 1. The New Procedure Had a Positive, Statistically Significant Effect

Using a rigorous difference-in-differences analysis (comparing switched branches to control branches before and after the change), we estimate the new procedure **increased service scores by 1.98 points** (p = 0.003). This result is robust across multiple statistical specifications.

| Metric | Switched Branches | Control Branches |
|--------|-------------------|------------------|
| Average score BEFORE switch (weeks 6-14) | 53.31 | 52.28 |
| Average score AFTER switch (weeks 15-22) | 52.92 | 49.91 |
| **Change** | **-0.39** | **-2.37** |

The key insight: while control branches experienced a notable **2.4-point decline** in service scores after week 15, switched branches remained essentially stable (only -0.4 points). The new procedure appears to have **protected branches from a broader decline** in service quality.

### 2. The Analysis Is Methodologically Sound

- **Parallel pre-trends confirmed**: Before the switch, both groups followed similar trends (p = 0.53), validating our comparison approach.
- **Results hold with controls**: The effect persists even after accounting for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume.
- **Fixed effects model confirms**: When controlling for branch-specific and week-specific factors, the effect remains significant at 1.98 points (p = 0.003).

### 3. Practical Significance

Given that the average branch scores about 50 on the service index, a **2-point improvement represents approximately a 4% gain** in service quality. This is a meaningful improvement for patrons.
