# **Memorandum to the Board**

## **Title: Impact of New Shelving and Rotation Procedure on Library Service Scores**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **2.2 points** (approximately **4.4%**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and consistent across the analysis. We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Study Design
- **709 branches** total: 425 switched to the new procedure (treatment group), 284 kept the old procedure (control group)
- Data covers **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, which compares changes in the treatment group to changes in the control group, isolating the effect of the new procedure

### 2. Main Result
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Estimated effect** | +2.19 service score points |
| **95% Confidence Interval** | [1.62, 2.76] |
| **Statistical significance** | p < 0.001 (highly significant) |
| **Effect size** | 4.4% increase from baseline; 0.21 standard deviations |

### 3. What This Means in Practice
- **Treatment branches** improved by **4.06 points** on average after the switch
- **Control branches** improved by **1.86 points** over the same period (likely due to seasonal factors or other system-wide trends)
- The **net effect attributable to the new procedure** is the difference: **2.2 points**

### 4. Robustness Checks
- The result holds when controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume
- The effect is consistent across the post-switch weeks (15–22)
- Pre-switch trends were similar between groups, supporting the validity of the comparison

### 5. Visual Evidence
The trends plot (saved as `trends_plot.png`) shows that both groups followed similar patterns before week 15, and the treatment group pulled away after the switch.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to remaining branches, given the clear positive effect.

2. **Monitor implementation** during rollout to ensure branches achieve similar gains. Track service scores for the first 8–12 weeks after each branch switches.

3. **Investigate variation across branches.** While the average effect is positive, some branches may benefit more than others. Understanding what drives this variation could help optimize implementation.

4. **Consider cost-benefit analysis.** If the new procedure requires additional staff time or resources, weigh the 4.4% service score improvement against those costs.

5. **Continue data collection.** Ongoing monitoring will help confirm the effect persists over time and identify any branches that may need additional support during transition.
