# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and rotation procedure **improved service scores by approximately 2.2 points** (about 4% relative to baseline). This improvement is statistically significant and represents a meaningful gain in service quality. We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Measurable Improvement in Service Scores**
- Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by **+2.66 points** after the switch (from 49.15 to 51.81)
- Branches that kept the old procedure saw only a **+0.48 point** increase over the same period (from 49.71 to 50.19)
- After accounting for underlying trends, the **net effect of the new procedure is +2.18 points** (p=0.037, statistically significant)

### **2. Valid Comparison Between Groups**
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure; **14 branches** kept the old procedure
- Before the switch (weeks 6-14), both groups showed **parallel trends** in service scores (p=0.97), confirming the groups were comparable
- The improvement in the treatment group began immediately after week 15 when the switch was implemented

### **3. Consistent Positive Effect**
- In 6 of the 8 weeks after the switch, treatment branches outperformed control branches
- The largest gaps occurred in weeks 15-17 (immediately after implementation), with differences of +3.2 to +3.9 points
- Service scores for switched branches averaged **51.8** after the change, compared to **50.2** for non-switched branches

### **4. Context**
- The average service score across all branches is approximately 50
- A 2.2-point improvement represents roughly a **4% gain** in service quality
- This improvement was achieved without apparent negative effects on staffing or visit patterns

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches
2. **Monitor service scores** for the next 8-12 weeks to confirm the effect persists as more branches adopt the procedure
3. **Document the new procedure** in detail to ensure consistent implementation across all branches
4. **Consider additional metrics** such as patron satisfaction or circulation rates to capture broader impacts
5. **Schedule a follow-up review** in Q2 to assess long-term sustainability of the improvement

---

*Analysis based on weekly service scores from 34 branches over 17 weeks (weeks 6-22). The switch was implemented at week 15. Statistical analysis used difference-in-differences regression with branch fixed effects.*
