# **Memorandum to the Board**

## **New Shelving Procedure: Impact on Service Scores**

### **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new shelving and stock rotation procedure saw their service scores increase by approximately **2.1 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and grew stronger over time, reaching about **2.6 points** in the later weeks after implementation. We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. Study Design**
- 709 branches total: 425 adopted the new procedure (treatment), 284 kept the old procedure (control)
- Data covers 17 weeks (weeks 6–22); the switch occurred in week 15
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in treatment branches to changes in control branches

**2. Service Score Improvement**
| Period | Treatment Branches | Control Branches | Gap |
|--------|-------------------|------------------|-----|
| Before switch (weeks 6–14) | 52.5 | 50.6 | 1.9 |
| After switch (weeks 15–22) | 53.6 | 49.5 | 4.1 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+2.1** |

- The **2.1-point improvement** is statistically significant (p < 0.001)
- This represents approximately a **4% improvement** relative to the average service score of 52

**3. Effect Built Over Time**
The improvement was not immediate. In the first few weeks after the switch (weeks 15–17), the effect was small and not statistically distinguishable from zero. However, from week 18 onward, the effect became clear and grew:

| Weeks | Estimated Effect |
|-------|-----------------|
| 15–17 | ~0.2 points (not significant) |
| 18–22 | ~2.6 points (significant) |

This pattern suggests branches needed time to adjust to and fully benefit from the new procedure.

**4. Control Branches Declined**
Notably, control branch scores dropped from 50.6 (before) to 49.5 (after), while treatment branch scores increased from 52.5 to 53.6. This widening gap strengthens confidence that the new procedure caused the improvement.

---

### **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new shelving and stock rotation procedure to all remaining branches.

2. **Allow an adjustment period** of 3–4 weeks for each branch to fully realize the benefits. Do not evaluate success too early.

3. **Monitor implementation quality**. Since the effect grew over time, provide ongoing training and support to ensure branches are correctly following the new procedure.

4. **Track service scores post-rollout** to confirm the effect persists at scale and identify any branches that may need additional support.

5. **Consider investigating why control branch scores declined** after week 14. Understanding this could reveal additional opportunities for improvement.
