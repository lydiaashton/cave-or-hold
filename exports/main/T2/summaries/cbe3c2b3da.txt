# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

## **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

### **Bottom Line**

Branches that adopted the new procedure showed **significantly higher service scores** after implementation compared to branches that kept the old procedure. The estimated improvement is approximately **1.8 points** on the service score index (on a base of ~50 points), representing roughly a **3-4% improvement**. However, important limitations in the data suggest this estimate may be optimistic, and we recommend a cautious, phased rollout with continued monitoring.

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. Treatment Effect Estimate**
- Branches using the new procedure scored **1.82 points higher** than expected based on control branch trends (95% confidence interval: 1.29 to 2.35 points)
- This result is **statistically significant** (p < 0.001)
- For context: the average branch scores about 50 points, so this represents a meaningful improvement

**2. Raw Score Changes**
| Group | Before Switch (Weeks 6-14) | After Switch (Weeks 15-22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------------|---------------------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 49.16 | 51.14 | +1.98 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 49.67 | 53.49 | +3.82 |

**3. Supporting Factors**
- The analysis controlled for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume, both of which positively affect service scores
- The treatment effect remained significant after accounting for these factors
- 425 branches adopted the new procedure; 284 branches served as controls

**4. Important Limitation: Pre-Existing Trends**
- **Critical finding:** Treatment branches were already improving faster than control branches *before* the switch was implemented
- The gap between treatment and control branches grew from -0.75 points (week 6) to +1.44 points (week 14) during the pre-switch period
- This violates a key assumption of the analysis method, meaning the **1.82-point estimate may overstate the true effect**
- The actual benefit of the new procedure could be smaller than estimated

---

### **Next Steps**

**1. Proceed with Cautious Rollout**
- The data shows a positive association between the new procedure and service scores
- Given the statistical significance and practical importance, expanding the procedure is reasonable
- However, given the pre-trend concerns, avoid assuming the full 1.8-point improvement will materialize everywhere

**2. Implement Phased Expansion**
- Roll out to additional branches in waves rather than all at once
- This creates new treatment/control comparisons to validate the effect
- Prioritize branches with similar characteristics to the original treatment group

**3. Establish Ongoing Monitoring**
- Continue tracking service scores for all branches
- Set up quarterly reviews to assess whether the improvement is sustained
- Create early warning thresholds if scores decline

**4. Investigate Mechanism**
- The analysis shows *that* scores improved, but not *why*
- Conduct qualitative interviews with branch staff to understand what aspects of the new procedure drive improvement
- This can help optimize the procedure before full rollout

**5. Consider a Formal Pilot Evaluation**
- For the next phase, randomly assign branches to treatment vs. control
- This would eliminate the pre-trend concern and provide more reliable estimates
- Run for 8-12 weeks before making final rollout decisions

---

### **Conclusion**

The new rotation and shelving procedure is associated with improved service scores, and the evidence supports moving forward with broader implementation. However, the pre-existing differences between branches that did and did not adopt the procedure mean we should temper expectations and build in robust monitoring as we expand. A phased, data-driven rollout will allow us to confirm the benefits while managing risk.

---

*Analysis based on 12,053 weekly observations from 709 branches, weeks 6-22. Statistical models used difference-in-differences estimation with cluster-robust standard errors.*
