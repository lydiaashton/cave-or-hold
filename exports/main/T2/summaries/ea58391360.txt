# **Library Cooperative Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new shelving and rotation procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw their service scores increase by approximately **2.0 points** (about **4% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and consistent across the post-switch period. **We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measurable Improvement in Service Quality
- The new procedure increased service scores by **+1.95 points** on average (p < 0.001)
- This represents a **3.7% improvement** relative to the baseline score of ~52.6
- The effect remained robust (+1.98 points) even after accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume

### 2. Clear Before/After Pattern
| Group | Before Switch (Weeks 6-14) | After Switch (Weeks 15-22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------------|---------------------------|--------|
| **Switched Branches** | 52.15 | 53.77 | **+1.62** |
| **Control Branches** | 52.27 | 51.94 | -0.33 |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | | | **+1.95** |

### 3. Valid Comparison Between Groups
- Before the switch (weeks 6-14), both groups had nearly identical service score trends
- After week 15, switched branches consistently outperformed control branches
- This parallel pre-trend supports the conclusion that the procedure change caused the improvement

### 4. Consistent Positive Impact
- 425 branches adopted the new procedure
- The improvement was sustained across all 8 post-switch weeks
- The effect did not diminish over time, suggesting lasting benefits

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new shelving and rotation procedure to all remaining branches

2. **Monitor implementation** during the rollout to ensure branches receive adequate training and support

3. **Track service scores** for the next 3-6 months to confirm the improvement persists at scale

4. **Document best practices** from high-performing switched branches to share during training

5. **Consider cost-benefit analysis** if the new procedure has additional implementation costs (not captured in this analysis)

## **Technical Notes**

- **Analysis method**: Difference-in-Differences regression with branch-level clustered standard errors
- **Data**: 12,053 weekly observations from 709 branches, weeks 6-22
- **Statistical significance**: p < 0.001 (highly significant)
- **Controls**: Results hold when adjusting for staffing levels and visitor volume
