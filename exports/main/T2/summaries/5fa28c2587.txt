# **Analysis of New Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **2.0 points** (95% CI: 1.0 to 2.9) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents approximately a **4% improvement** over baseline scores. The result is statistically significant (p < 0.001), and we recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Study Design
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure; **60 branches** kept the old procedure
- The switch occurred at **week 15**, creating a clear before/after comparison
- This analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, which isolates the effect of the new procedure by comparing changes in the treatment group to changes in the control group

### 2. Main Results

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (Old Procedure) | 48.56 | 48.91 | +0.35 |
| Treated (New Procedure) | 47.83 | 50.14 | +2.31 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+1.96** |

- The **1.96-point improvement** is the estimated causal effect of the new procedure
- 95% confidence interval: **1.05 to 2.87 points**
- P-value: **< 0.001** (highly statistically significant)

### 3. Validity Check: Parallel Trends Assumption

For this analysis to be valid, the treatment and control groups must have had similar trends *before* the switch. We tested this and found:

- **Pre-switch trends were statistically similar** (p = 0.31)
- Both groups showed comparable week-to-week variation before week 15
- This supports the conclusion that the post-switch divergence is due to the new procedure, not pre-existing differences

### 4. Practical Significance

- Service scores average around 50 across the system
- A 2-point improvement represents meaningful progress in service quality
- The effect is consistent across specifications (with and without controls for staffing and visitor volume)

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new shelving and stock rotation procedure to all 150 branches

2. **Monitor implementation** by tracking service scores for the first 8–12 weeks after rollout to confirm the effect persists at scale

3. **Document best practices** from the 90 early-adopter branches to support training and smooth transition at remaining branches

4. **Consider cost-benefit analysis** if the new procedure involves additional labor or equipment costs, though the service improvement appears to justify adoption

5. **Schedule follow-up review** in 6 months to assess long-term sustainability of the improvement
