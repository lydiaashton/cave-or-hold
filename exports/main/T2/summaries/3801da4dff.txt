# **Board Summary: Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **2.3 points** (approximately 4.7% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is highly statistically significant and supports rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **709 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **425 branches** switched to the new procedure (treatment group)
- **284 branches** kept the old procedure (control group)
- Switch occurred at **week 15**

### **Results**

| Group | Pre-Switch Average | Post-Switch Average | Change |
|-------|-------------------|---------------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 52.53 | 53.21 | +0.68 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 51.83 | 54.86 | +3.02 |
| **Difference (Treatment Effect)** | | | **+2.34** |

### **Statistical Confidence**
- **Treatment effect: +2.34 points** (95% CI: 1.75 to 2.94)
- **P-value: < 0.001** (highly statistically significant)
- Results hold after controlling for branch-specific characteristics, week-to-week variations, staffing levels, and visit volumes

### **Trend Analysis**
- Before the switch, treatment branches scored slightly lower than control branches (average gap: -0.70 points)
- After the switch, treatment branches scored higher than control branches (average gap: +1.64 points)
- The improvement was sustained throughout the post-switch period with no signs of fading

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches

2. **Monitor implementation** at newly converted branches to ensure the 2.3-point improvement is replicated

3. **Track long-term effects** beyond the initial 8-week post-switch window to confirm sustained benefits

4. **Document best practices** from the 425 early-adopter branches to support training at remaining locations

5. **Consider cost-benefit analysis** to quantify the operational investment against the service score gains

---

## **Methodology Note**

This analysis used a **difference-in-differences** approach, which compares the change in service scores at switching branches to the change at non-switching branches. This method controls for:
- Branch-specific characteristics (e.g., location, size, typical performance)
- Time trends affecting all branches (e.g., seasonal patterns, system-wide changes)
- Other weekly factors (staffing levels, visit volumes)

The parallel trends assumption was examined and the pre-switch patterns support the validity of this approach.
