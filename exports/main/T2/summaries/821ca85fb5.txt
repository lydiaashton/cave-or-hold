# **Board Summary: Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure shows a **positive but statistically inconclusive effect** on service scores. Switched branches improved by approximately **2.3 points** (about 4% of average score) relative to non-switched branches, but this result is not statistically significant at conventional levels (p ≈ 0.12). The benefit appears strongest in the first month after implementation and fades over time. **We cannot confidently conclude the new procedure improves service, but there is no evidence of harm.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** total: 20 switched to the new procedure, 14 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- **Difference-in-differences analysis** comparing changes in switched vs. non-switched branches

### **Main Results**

| Metric | Control Branches | Switched Branches |
|--------|-----------------|-------------------|
| Pre-switch average (weeks 6–14) | 52.7 | 50.0 |
| Post-switch average (weeks 15–22) | 54.5 | 54.0 |
| **Change** | **+1.7** | **+4.0** |

**Estimated Treatment Effect: +2.3 points** (switched branches improved 2.3 points more than control branches)

### **Statistical Confidence**
- **p-value ≈ 0.12**: The result does not meet the conventional threshold for statistical significance (p < 0.05)
- **95% confidence interval**: Approximately -0.6 to +5.1 points
- **Interpretation**: We cannot rule out that the observed improvement is due to random variation

### **Effect Pattern Over Time**
The event study analysis reveals important dynamics:
- **Weeks 1–4 after switch**: Clear positive effect (peaking at +7 to +9 points in weeks 3–4)
- **Weeks 5–7 after switch**: Effect diminishes and becomes indistinguishable from zero
- **Week 8 after switch**: Possible negative effect emerging

This suggests the new procedure may provide a **short-term boost** that fades over time, possibly due to:
- Initial enthusiasm/attention effect
- Staff learning curve that plateaus
- Operational challenges emerging with sustained use

### **Validity Checks**
- **Parallel trends assumption**: Supported. Pre-switch trends between groups were not significantly different (p = 0.82), validating our comparison approach
- **Control variables**: Adding staffing and visit indices did not materially change the results
- **Branch fixed effects**: Accounting for individual branch characteristics yielded similar estimates (+2.4 points, p ≈ 0.11)

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended Actions**

1. **Do not mandate system-wide rollout yet.** The evidence is insufficient to justify the cost and disruption of universal implementation.

2. **Extend the evaluation period.** Continue monitoring switched branches for 8–12 more weeks to determine:
   - Whether the effect stabilizes at a positive level
   - Whether the fading pattern continues or reverses
   - Long-term sustainability of any benefits

3. **Conduct qualitative follow-up.** Interview staff at switched branches to understand:
   - What aspects of the new procedure are working well
   - What challenges have emerged over time
   - Whether initial enthusiasm has waned and why

4. **Consider a phased expansion.** If qualitative feedback is positive, expand to 5–10 additional branches as a pilot while maintaining control branches for comparison.

5. **Refine the metric.** Consider whether service score captures the right outcomes, or if additional measures (patron satisfaction, staff efficiency, circulation rates) should be tracked.

### **Decision Framework**

| Scenario | Recommendation |
|----------|----------------|
| Effect remains positive & significant after extended monitoring | Proceed with phased rollout |
| Effect fades to zero but no negative impacts | Optional adoption; let branches choose |
| Effect becomes negative | Discontinue and revert to old procedure |

## **Appendix: Technical Notes**

- Analysis used difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch)
- All results adjusted for potential within-branch correlation across weeks
- Service score has a typical range of 20–80 with average ~53; a 2.3-point change represents modest but potentially meaningful improvement
- Power limitation: With only 34 branches and 8 post-treatment weeks, the study may be underpowered to detect smaller but real effects
