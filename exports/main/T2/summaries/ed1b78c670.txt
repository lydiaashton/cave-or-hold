# **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure is associated with a **modest improvement** in service scores (+1.7 points), but this effect is **not statistically significant** at conventional confidence levels. We cannot confidently conclude that the new procedure caused better service outcomes. The board should consider a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **34 branches** participated: 20 adopted the new procedure (treatment), 14 continued with the old procedure (control)
- Data covers **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring in **week 15**
- Service scores average around 50 points (higher is better)

### Results Summary

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 50.08 | 53.27 | +3.20 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 50.57 | 55.46 | +4.89 |
| **Difference** | | | **+1.70** |

### Statistical Analysis
- The **1.70-point improvement** attributable to the new procedure has a **p-value of 0.14**, meaning there is a 14% probability this result could occur by chance
- This does **not meet** the standard 5% threshold for statistical significance
- When controlling for staffing and visit levels, the effect increases slightly to **2.04 points (p=0.07)**, which is marginally significant at the 10% level

### Important Context
- **Both groups improved** after week 15, suggesting broader factors (seasonal trends, system-wide initiatives, or other changes) may have boosted service scores across all branches
- Pre-switch trends between treatment and control groups were **reasonably parallel**, supporting the validity of the comparison
- The new procedure shows **no negative effects** on service scores

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Continue monitoring** the 20 branches already using the new procedure for an additional 8–12 weeks to gather more data and improve statistical power

2. **Consider a phased rollout** to additional branches while maintaining a control group for ongoing comparison

3. **Investigate implementation quality**—branches with stronger adherence to the new procedure may show larger effects

4. **Review cost-benefit**: Even if the 1.7-point improvement is real, assess whether the operational costs of the new procedure justify the modest gain

5. **Reconvene in Q2** with updated data to make a final rollout decision
