# Library Cooperative Shelving Procedure Evaluation
## Board Summary Report

---

### BOTTOM LINE

**Do not roll out the new shelving and rotation procedure to all branches.** The data does not provide credible evidence that the new procedure improves service scores. The apparent improvement in switching branches reflects pre-existing differences between branches that chose to switch and those that didn't—not an effect of the procedure itself.

---

### KEY FINDINGS

**1. Study Design**
- 709 branches total: 425 adopted the new procedure, 284 kept the old procedure
- 17 weeks of data (weeks 6–22); switch occurred at week 15
- Service scores average ~50 points (higher is better), with typical weekly variation of ~9.6 points

**2. Initial (Misleading) Result**
- At first glance, switching branches showed service scores **2.1 points higher** after the switch compared to non-switching branches
- This would represent a meaningful ~4% improvement if causal

**3. Critical Problem: Pre-Existing Trends**
- **Before the switch**, branches that would later adopt the new procedure were already improving relative to others
- The gap between switching and non-switching branches was widening by **0.31 points per week** during the pre-switch period (weeks 6–14)
- This violates the fundamental assumption required to claim the procedure caused the improvement

**4. Adjusted Analysis**
- When accounting for the pre-existing trend, the estimated effect of the new procedure is **essentially zero** (−0.3 points)
- This is not statistically significant (95% confidence interval: −1.7 to +1.1 points)
- The data cannot distinguish the procedure's effect from random variation

**5. Interpretation**
- Branches that volunteered to switch were likely already better-managed or on an upward trajectory for other reasons (selection bias)
- The new procedure itself shows no detectable impact on service scores

---

### NEXT STEPS

**Immediate Recommendation:**
- **Pause the systemwide rollout** of the new procedure

**If Further Evaluation Is Desired:**
1. **Randomized trial**: Randomly assign branches to adopt the procedure (rather than voluntary adoption) to eliminate selection bias
2. **Extended monitoring**: Collect additional weeks of data to detect any delayed effects
3. **Qualitative assessment**: Survey branch staff about operational impacts, efficiency, and user experience that may not be captured in service scores
4. **Cost-benefit analysis**: Evaluate whether any modest benefits justify the costs of retraining and implementation

**Methodological Note:**
Future program evaluations should use randomized assignment when possible. When voluntary adoption is necessary, ensure adequate pre-intervention data to verify that treatment and control groups follow parallel trends before claiming causal effects.

---

*Analysis prepared using difference-in-differences methodology with pre-trend validation. Cluster-robust standard errors account for repeated observations within branches.*
