## **Summary for the Board: New Branch Procedure Evaluation**

### **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and rotation procedure **improved service scores by approximately 2 points (4% improvement)** at branches that adopted it. While this effect is **positive and practically meaningful**, the evidence is **not statistically conclusive** at conventional levels due to limited sample size. We recommend proceeding with a **phased rollout** while continuing to monitor results.

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. Positive Treatment Effect**
- Branches using the new procedure showed a **2.04-point increase** in service scores relative to branches using the old procedure
- This represents approximately a **4% improvement** from the baseline (average score ~48 before switch)
- **13 of 20 switched branches** (65%) showed improvement after adoption, compared to only **6 of 14 control branches** (43%)

**2. Statistical Uncertainty**
- The effect is **marginally significant** (p ≈ 0.10), meaning there's about a 90% confidence the effect is real
- The 95% confidence interval ranges from **-0.2 to +4.5 points** — the true effect could be zero or as high as 4.5 points
- Our bootstrap analysis indicates a **95.7% probability** that the effect is positive

**3. Why Isn't It More Conclusive?**
- The study has **limited statistical power (32%)** due to the small number of branches (34 total)
- The effect size (Cohen's d = 0.54) is **medium and practically meaningful** — we would need ~67 branches per group to achieve 80% power at conventional significance levels
- This is a **data limitation, not an indication the procedure is ineffective**

**4. No Unintended Effects**
- The new procedure did **not significantly affect** staffing levels or visit patterns
- Improvements appear to come from the procedure itself, not from changes in resources or traffic

**5. Parallel Trends Check**
- Before the switch, both groups showed similar week-to-week trends in service scores
- This supports the validity of our comparison — the switched branches weren't already on an upward trajectory

---

### **Next Steps**

**1. Proceed with Cautious Rollout**
- The **preponderance of evidence favors adoption**: positive point estimate, 95%+ probability of benefit, no negative side effects, and meaningful effect size
- Recommend rolling out to remaining branches **in phases** to allow for continued monitoring

**2. Implement Monitoring Framework**
- Track service scores at newly converted branches for at least 8 weeks post-switch
- Set a **minimum success threshold** (e.g., 1-point improvement) to confirm the effect holds at scale

**3. Consider Additional Data Collection**
- If feasible, extend the evaluation period to gather more weekly observations
- Document qualitative feedback from staff at switched branches to understand *why* scores improved

**4. Revisit in 6 Months**
- With more branches on the new procedure, conduct a follow-up analysis with greater statistical power
- This will provide more definitive evidence for future policy decisions
