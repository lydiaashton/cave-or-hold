# **Board Summary: New Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **1.9 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and represents a meaningful improvement on a scale where the average branch scores about 50.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure at week 15 (treatment group)
- **14 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in treatment branches to changes in control branches

### **Results**

| Metric | Treatment Branches | Control Branches |
|--------|-------------------|------------------|
| Average score before switch | 49.7 | 49.6 |
| Average score after switch | 50.9 | 48.9 |
| **Change** | **+1.1 points** | **-0.8 points** |

**Net effect of new procedure: +1.9 points** (95% confidence interval: 0.4 to 3.5 points)

### **Statistical Confidence**
- The improvement is **statistically significant** (p < 0.05)
- Results hold even after controlling for staffing levels and customer visits
- The parallel trends assumption is satisfied—treatment and control branches had similar service score patterns before the switch

### **Visual Summary**
A trend chart has been saved as `service_score_trends.png` showing:
- Treatment branches (blue line) improved after week 15
- Control branches (maroon line) slightly declined over the same period
- The gap between the two groups widened after the procedure change

## **Next Steps**

1. **Recommend rollout to all branches.** The evidence supports expanding the new procedure cooperative-wide, with an expected service score improvement of ~2 points per branch.

2. **Monitor implementation.** Track service scores during rollout to ensure the effect holds across different branch contexts.

3. **Investigate mechanisms.** The analysis controlled for staffing and visit levels, but further research could identify *why* the new procedure works (e.g., faster item location, better organization, reduced staff time per transaction).

4. **Consider timing.** The effect was measured over 8 weeks post-switch. Longer-term tracking would confirm whether benefits persist or grow over time.

5. **Cost-benefit analysis.** If the new procedure involves training or operational costs, compare those against the value of a ~2-point service score improvement.
