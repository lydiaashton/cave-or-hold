# **Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure Evaluation**

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure shows a **positive effect on service scores**, with branches that switched improving by approximately **2.1 points (4.3%)** relative to branches that kept the old procedure. However, this result is **not statistically significant at conventional levels** (p=0.11), meaning we cannot rule out that the observed improvement is due to random variation. The evidence is suggestive but not conclusive. We recommend **continuing the pilot with additional monitoring** before a full rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **34 branches** participated: 20 switched to the new procedure, 14 kept the old procedure
- Data covers **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22): 9 weeks before the switch, 8 weeks after
- Switch occurred at **week 15**
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in switched branches to changes in non-switched branches

### Results
| Metric | Treatment Branches (Switched) | Control Branches (Old Procedure) |
|--------|------------------------------|----------------------------------|
| Average score before switch | 49.7 | 50.9 |
| Average score after switch | 51.7 | 50.9 |
| **Change** | **+2.0** | **0.0** |

- **Estimated treatment effect: +2.15 points** (95% confidence interval: -0.46 to +4.77)
- **14 of 20 treatment branches (70%)** showed improvement vs. **7 of 14 control branches (50%)**
- The **parallel trends assumption is satisfied** (p=0.54), validating our comparison approach

### Statistical Interpretation
- The p-value of 0.11 means there is an **11% probability** of observing this result (or larger) if the new procedure had no real effect
- The confidence interval includes zero, so we **cannot confidently conclude** the procedure causes improvement
- The study has **limited statistical power**: with 34 branches, we could reliably detect effects of ~3.6 points or larger; the observed 2.1-point effect is below this threshold

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Continue the pilot program** for at least 8–12 more weeks to accumulate more data and improve statistical precision

2. **Monitor branch-level variation**: Some branches showed large gains (up to +10 points) while others declined (down to -3.5 points). Investigate what distinguishes high-performing switchers to identify best practices

3. **Consider a phased rollout**: If operational constraints allow, gradually switch remaining branches while continuing to track outcomes, rather than an all-at-once rollout

4. **Track secondary metrics**: The analysis controlled for staffing and visit volumes, but these may also be affected by the new procedure. Monitor whether the procedure impacts operational efficiency or patron experience beyond the service score

5. **Set a decision threshold**: Define in advance what level of evidence would warrant full rollout (e.g., p<0.05, or a lower bound of the confidence interval above zero)

---

## **Conclusion**

The new procedure shows **promise but not proof**. The direction of the effect matches expectations, and there is no evidence of harm. However, the current data does not provide strong enough evidence to justify the costs and disruption of a system-wide rollout. Extending the evaluation period is the most prudent path forward.
