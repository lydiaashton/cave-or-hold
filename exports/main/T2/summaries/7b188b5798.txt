# **Memorandum to the Board: Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw their service scores increase by approximately **2.2 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.5 to 2.9 points) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents a **4% improvement** relative to the average baseline score of 51.6 points. The result is highly statistically significant (p < 0.001). We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **709 branches** analyzed over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **425 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **284 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in switched branches to changes in control branches

### Results

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 51.59 | 52.83 | **+1.24** |
| Control branches | 51.31 | 50.37 | **-0.95** |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | | | **+2.18** |

### Statistical Evidence
- **Treatment effect:** +2.17 points (95% CI: 1.46 to 2.88)
- **P-value:** < 0.001 (highly significant)
- **Effect size:** 4.2% improvement over baseline

### Additional Insights
1. **Consistent positive effect:** The switched branches outperformed control branches in every post-treatment week, with the gap widening from 1.7 points in week 15 to over 3 points by weeks 21–22.

2. **Robust to controls:** The effect remains unchanged after accounting for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume, suggesting the improvement is attributable to the procedure itself.

3. **No heterogeneity:** The benefit does not appear to depend on staffing levels or visitor volume—the procedure helps branches across different operational conditions.

4. **Parallel trends:** Pre-treatment service scores followed similar patterns between the two groups (average difference of only 0.27 points), supporting the validity of the comparison.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all 284 branches currently using the old procedure.

2. **Monitor implementation** during the rollout to ensure consistent adoption and identify any branches needing additional support.

3. **Track service scores** for the next 8–12 weeks post-rollout to confirm the effect persists at scale and to detect any delayed impacts.

4. **Document the procedure** in detail to facilitate training and ensure fidelity of implementation across all branches.

5. **Consider cost-benefit analysis** to quantify the return on investment (e.g., training costs, staff time) relative to the service score improvement.
