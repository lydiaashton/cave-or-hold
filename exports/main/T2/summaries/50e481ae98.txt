# **Board Summary: Impact of New Branch Rotation Procedure on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new rotation and shelving procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **1.8 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.3 to 2.4 points) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents approximately a **3.6% improvement** and is statistically significant (p < 0.001). The evidence supports rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. **Positive Treatment Effect**
- The difference-in-differences analysis shows the new procedure increased service scores by **1.82 points** after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume
- This effect is highly statistically significant (p < 0.001)
- The 95% confidence interval [1.27, 2.36] indicates we can be confident the true effect is positive and meaningful

### 2. **Practical Significance**
- Baseline service scores averaged approximately 50 points
- The 1.8-point improvement represents a **3.6% gain** in service quality
- For context: switched branches went from averaging 50.7 (before) to 53.5 (after), while non-switched branches went from 49.1 to 50.0

### 3. **Robustness**
- Results hold after controlling for weekly staffing index and visits index
- Standard errors are clustered by branch to account for repeated observations
- Analysis includes 709 branches over 17 weeks (12,053 observations)
- About 60% of branches switched to the new procedure; 40% served as the control group

### 4. **Visual Evidence**
The attached charts show:
- **Trends chart**: Switched branches (green line) pulled away from non-switched branches (blue line) after week 15
- **Gap chart**: The performance gap between switched and non-switched branches widened after the procedure change

---

## **Caveats and Considerations**

1. **Pre-existing differences**: Switched branches already had slightly higher service scores before the change (about 1.7 points higher on average). The analysis accounts for this, but the true effect could be slightly smaller than estimated.

2. **Time horizon**: We only have 8 weeks of post-switch data. Longer-term effects (sustained improvement, diminishing returns, or further gains) remain unknown.

3. **Implementation variation**: Not all branches may have implemented the new procedure with equal fidelity. The estimated effect is an average across all switched branches.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure, given the clear positive impact on service scores.

2. **Monitor implementation** during rollout to ensure branches adopt the procedure consistently and capture any branch-specific challenges.

3. **Continue tracking service scores** for at least 6-12 months post-rollout to:
   - Confirm the effect persists over time
   - Identify whether benefits accumulate or diminish
   - Detect any unintended consequences

4. **Investigate mechanisms**: Consider studying *why* the new procedure works (e.g., faster book retrieval, better organization, reduced staff time on shelving) to optimize future process improvements.

5. **Cost-benefit analysis**: If the new procedure has implementation or training costs, compare them against the quantified service improvement to calculate return on investment.
