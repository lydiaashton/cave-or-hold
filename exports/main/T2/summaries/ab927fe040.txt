# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by **2.3 points more** than branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant (p < 0.001), and we recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **150 branches** tracked over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **60 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in service scores between the two groups before and after the switch

### **Service Score Changes**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| New Procedure Branches | 47.7 | 51.4 | +3.7 |
| Old Procedure Branches | 49.4 | 50.7 | +1.3 |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | | | **+2.3** |

### **Statistical Results**
- **Estimated effect:** +2.31 service score points
- **95% Confidence Interval:** 1.02 to 3.60 points
- **p-value:** 0.0005 (highly significant)
- Results control for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume

### **Trend Analysis**
- Before the switch, new-procedure branches consistently scored ~1.7 points lower than control branches (this baseline difference is accounted for in the analysis)
- After the switch, the gap reversed: new-procedure branches began outperforming control branches
- The improvement was sustained across all 8 post-switch weeks

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all 150 branches

2. **Monitor implementation** during the rollout to ensure consistent adoption and identify any branches needing additional support

3. **Track service scores** for 8–12 weeks post-rollout to confirm the effect holds at scale
