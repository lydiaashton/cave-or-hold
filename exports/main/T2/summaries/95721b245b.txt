# **MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **1.86 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.32 to 2.41) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents a **3.6% improvement** relative to the average service score. The result is highly statistically significant (p < 0.001).

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Study Design
- **709 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **425 branches** adopted the new procedure (treatment group)
- **284 branches** kept the old procedure (control group)
- The switch occurred in **week 15**
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in treatment branches to changes in control branches

### 2. Main Result
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 51.70 | 52.77 | +1.07 |
| Non-switched branches | 51.22 | 50.43 | -0.79 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+1.86** |

The treatment effect of **+1.86 points** is:
- Statistically significant at the 99.9% confidence level
- Robust to controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume
- Consistent with branch fixed-effects models

### 3. Parallel Trends Assumption Validated
Before the switch (weeks 6–14), both groups showed similar week-to-week patterns in service scores. The gap between treatment and control branches remained relatively stable (ranging from -0.86 to +1.43 points), supporting the validity of the comparison.

### 4. Effect Grows Over Time
The treatment effect appears to strengthen in the weeks following implementation:
- Weeks 15–16: ~1.3–1.5 point improvement
- Weeks 17–19: ~2.1–2.4 point improvement  
- Weeks 20–22: ~2.0–3.7 point improvement

This pattern suggests branches may benefit from a learning period as staff become familiar with the new procedure.

### 5. Notable Context
- Control branches experienced a slight **decline** in service scores after week 15 (-0.79 points), while treatment branches improved
- This divergence indicates the new procedure may provide a competitive advantage, not just a neutral improvement

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all 284 branches currently using the old procedure.

2. **Plan implementation support:**
   - Allow 2–3 weeks for staff training and adjustment
   - Expect full benefits to materialize within 4–6 weeks post-implementation

3. **Continue monitoring:** Track service scores for an additional 8–12 weeks after full rollout to confirm sustained improvement and identify any branches needing additional support.

4. **Document best practices:** Capture lessons learned from the 425 early-adopter branches to streamline training for remaining branches.
