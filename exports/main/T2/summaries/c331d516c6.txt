## Summary for the Board

**Bottom Line:** The new procedure shows a **modest positive effect** on service scores—approximately **+1.0 to +1.3 points** (about 2% improvement)—but the evidence is complicated by pre-existing differences between branches.

### Key Findings

1. **Simple comparison is misleading**: At first glance, switched branches appear to gain +2.0 points relative to non-switched branches. However, this is inflated.

2. **Critical issue - selection bias**: Branches that chose to switch were **already improving** before the change (+0.22 points/week), while control branches were declining (-0.21 points/week). This pre-existing trend violates the standard assumptions for causal inference.

3. **Adjusted estimates are more modest**:
   - Local comparison (weeks immediately around the switch): +0.5 points, not statistically significant
   - Change in treatment-control gap: +1.0 points
   - Model with trend controls: +1.3 points (statistically significant)

4. **No negative effects**: Treatment branches maintained higher scores throughout the 8-week post period with no signs of deterioration.

### Recommendation

**Proceed with cautious optimism.** The weight of evidence suggests a small but real positive effect. A full rollout is reasonable, but:
- Set modest expectations (~1 point improvement, not 2+)
- Monitor scores closely during implementation
- Consider randomizing future changes to enable cleaner evaluation
- Conduct a cost-benefit analysis to ensure the improvement justifies implementation costs
