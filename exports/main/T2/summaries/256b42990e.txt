# **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure **improved service scores** by an average of **2.1 points** (95% CI: 0.9 to 3.4 points). This represents approximately a **4% improvement** over baseline scores. The result is statistically significant (p < 0.001), and the analysis supports rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measurable Improvement in Service Scores
- Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by **3.7 points** from before to after the switch
- Branches that kept the old procedure also improved, but by only **1.5 points** (likely due to other factors affecting all branches)
- After accounting for this background improvement, the **net effect of the new procedure is +2.1 points**

### 2. Statistical Confidence
- The improvement is **highly unlikely to be due to chance** (p = 0.0006)
- We are 95% confident the true effect lies between **0.9 and 3.4 points**
- Even the conservative end of this range represents a meaningful improvement

### 3. Consistent Benefits Across Branches
- **82% of switched branches** saw their service scores improve after adopting the new procedure
- Only **70% of non-switched branches** improved over the same period
- The effect held after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### 4. Valid Comparison
- Before the switch, both groups of branches were following **parallel trends** (p = 0.77), meaning the comparison is valid
- The analysis accounts for branch-specific characteristics and weekly fluctuations

## **Next Steps**

## **Technical Notes**

- **Analysis method**: Difference-in-differences regression with branch-clustered standard errors
- **Data**: 150 branches, 17 weeks (weeks 6–22), 2,550 observations
- **Controls**: Staffing index and visits index included in the model
- **Baseline**: Average service score is approximately 50 points (higher is better)
