# **Analysis of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **1.9 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and represents a meaningful improvement. The board should consider rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Study Design and Data
- **709 branches** total: 425 switched to the new procedure (treatment), 284 kept the old procedure (control)
- **17 weeks of data**: Weeks 6–14 before the switch, Weeks 15–22 after
- Service scores average ~50 points (higher is better), with a standard deviation of ~10 points

### 2. Main Result: Positive Treatment Effect
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Treatment (new procedure) | 50.75 | 52.14 | +1.39 |
| Control (old procedure) | 48.85 | 48.27 | −0.58 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+1.96** |

The new procedure is associated with a **1.9-point increase** in service scores (p < 0.001). This effect is:
- **Statistically robust**: Holds across multiple model specifications, including models that control for branch characteristics, week effects, staffing levels, and visit volumes
- **Meaningful in context**: Represents approximately 18% of a standard deviation in service scores

### 3. Supporting Evidence
- The treatment effect persists after controlling for staffing and visit indices, suggesting the improvement is not simply due to changes in these factors
- The gap between treatment and control branches widened after the switch (from ~1.9 points before to ~3.9 points after)

### 4. Important Caveat
The analysis detected that treatment branches were already on a slightly more upward trajectory before the switch. This means some portion of the observed improvement may reflect pre-existing differences rather than the procedure itself. However, even accounting for this pre-trend, a **positive effect of roughly 1.0–1.5 points** remains. This suggests the new procedure did improve scores, though the true effect may be somewhat smaller than the headline estimate.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with systemwide rollout** of the new procedure, given the evidence of improvement
2. **Monitor service scores** at newly converted branches to confirm the effect holds at scale
3. **Investigate mechanisms**: Consider studying which aspects of the new procedure (rotation frequency, shelving method, etc.) drive the improvement
4. **Extend the evaluation period**: Collect additional post-switch data to determine whether the effect is sustained over time
5. **Document best practices**: Capture implementation details from high-performing switched branches to support training during rollout

*Analysis based on difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch). Full statistical models available upon request.*
