# **Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure Evaluation**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **2.0 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents approximately a **4% improvement** over baseline scores and is statistically significant (p < 0.001). We recommend proceeding with a full rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measurable Positive Impact
- **Treatment effect:** +2.03 service score points (95% confidence interval: 1.31 to 2.75)
- **Baseline context:** Average service score across all branches is ~50 points
- **Statistical significance:** The result is highly significant (p < 0.001), meaning it's very unlikely to be due to random chance

### 2. Branch-Level Results
| Metric | Switched Branches (425) | Non-Switched Branches (284) |
|--------|------------------------|----------------------------|
| Average score change | +0.88 points | -1.15 points |
| % with improvement | 59.5% | 35.6% |

The divergent trends are striking: switched branches generally improved while non-switched branches generally declined during the same period.

### 3. Effect Persists Across Time
- The positive effect was observed consistently across all 8 post-switch weeks (weeks 15-22)
- The gap between switched and non-switched branches actually widened over time, from 1.9 points in week 15 to 3.6 points by week 22
- This suggests the benefits may accumulate as staff become more familiar with the new procedure

### 4. Robust to Other Factors
The effect remains significant even after controlling for:
- **Staffing levels** (staffing_index)
- **Branch traffic** (visits_index)

This indicates the improvement is attributable to the procedure change itself, not to concurrent changes in staffing or visitor volume.

### 5. Important Caveat
There is some evidence that switched branches were on a slightly different trajectory before the intervention. However, the magnitude of the post-switch improvement (2+ points) substantially exceeds any pre-existing differences, and the consistency of the effect across multiple weeks supports a genuine treatment effect.

## **Analysis Methodology**

This evaluation used a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing:
- **Treatment group:** 425 branches that switched to the new procedure at week 15
- **Control group:** 284 branches that continued with the old procedure

Data covers 17 weeks (weeks 6-22) with 12,053 total observations. The analysis controls for time trends and accounts for the natural variation in weekly service scores.
