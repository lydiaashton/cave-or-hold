# **Evaluation of New Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new shelving procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.1 points** (a 4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant (p = 0.001) and was not anticipated given the expectation of no difference. **We recommend proceeding with a full rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measurable Improvement in Service Scores
- **Treatment branches** (90 branches that switched): Service scores increased by **+2.23 points** after adoption
- **Control branches** (60 branches that did not switch): Service scores increased by only **+0.12 points**
- **Net effect**: **+2.11 points** attributable to the new procedure

### 2. Statistical Confidence
- The effect is **highly statistically significant** (p = 0.001)
- Results hold even after controlling for staffing levels and visit volumes
- **75.6% of treatment branches** showed improvement vs. only **53.3% of control branches**

### 3. Valid Comparison Between Groups
- Before the switch (weeks 6-14), treatment and control branches had **similar service score trends**
- No statistically significant pre-existing differences between the groups
- This supports the conclusion that the observed improvement is caused by the new procedure, not other factors

### 4. Consistent Effect Over Time
- The improvement appeared immediately after week 15 and persisted through week 22
- No evidence of the effect diminishing over the 8-week post-switch period

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve full rollout** of the new shelving procedure to all remaining branches

2. **Monitor implementation** at newly converted branches to ensure the 2-point improvement is replicated

3. **Investigate why the improvement occurred** — while not expected, understanding the mechanism could help optimize other operations

4. **Continue tracking service scores** post-rollout to confirm the effect persists at scale

5. **Consider cost-benefit analysis** — if the new procedure requires additional resources, weigh the 4% service improvement against implementation costs

---

*Analysis based on 17 weeks of data (weeks 6-22) from 150 branches, using difference-in-differences methodology with standard errors clustered by branch.*
