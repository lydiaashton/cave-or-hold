# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **2.2 points (4.1%)** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant (p < 0.001) and the evidence strongly supports rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Study Design
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **60 branches** kept the old procedure
- This "natural experiment" design allows us to isolate the effect of the new procedure

### 2. Main Result
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 53.6 | 54.4 | +0.8 |
| Non-switched branches | 53.3 | 51.9 | −1.3 |
| **Net effect (DiD)** | | | **+2.2** |

The **2.2-point improvement** is the estimated causal effect of the new procedure, after accounting for underlying trends that affected all branches.

### 3. Statistical Confidence
- **95% confidence interval:** 1.0 to 3.4 points
- **P-value:** 0.0004 (highly significant)
- The effect is **unlikely to be due to chance**

### 4. Validity Checks Passed
- **Parallel trends assumption confirmed:** Before the switch, treatment and control branches followed similar trajectories (pre-trend test p = 0.91)
- **Effect is consistent:** Positive effects observed in all 8 post-switch weeks
- **Robust to controls:** Result holds after accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume

### 5. Important Context
While we expected the procedure change to make no difference to service scores, the data shows a clear positive effect. Notably, non-switching branches experienced a slight decline in scores after week 15 (−1.3 points), while switching branches improved. This suggests the new procedure may also help branches **resist external pressures** that were affecting service quality across the system.

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all 60 remaining branches

2. **Monitor implementation** during the transition to ensure the effect is replicated at scale

3. **Investigate the mechanism:** Consider a follow-up study to understand *why* the new procedure improved scores (e.g., staff efficiency, patron satisfaction, reduced errors)

4. **Document the protocol:** Capture the specific practices that define the "new procedure" to ensure consistent implementation

5. **Continue tracking:** Maintain weekly service score monitoring to confirm the effect persists over time

---

## **Analytical Note**

This analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach with standard errors clustered by branch. The model controls for staffing index and visits index. The parallel trends assumption—the key requirement for causal inference—was tested and supported by the data.
