# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **2.1 points** (approximately **4% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and emerged after the switch was implemented. The evidence supports rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Study Design
- **709 branches** total: 425 adopted the new procedure (treatment group), 284 kept the old procedure (control group)
- Data covers **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in treatment branches to changes in control branches

### 2. Main Result
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 49.47 | 52.31 | +2.84 |
| Non-switched branches | 48.47 | 49.24 | +0.77 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+2.06** |

- **Effect size**: +2.1 points on the service score index (95% confidence interval: 1.5 to 2.6)
- **Statistical significance**: p < 0.001 (highly significant)
- **Practical significance**: ~4% improvement relative to the average score of 50

### 3. Effect Over Time
The improvement grew over time after implementation:
- Weeks 16–18: Small positive effects (+0.2 to +0.4 points)
- Weeks 19–22: Larger effects (+0.7 to +1.1 points)

This pattern suggests branches may have needed time to fully realize the benefits of the new procedure.

### 4. Important Caveat
There were pre-existing differences between the two groups of branches before the switch. Branches that chose to switch had systematically different service score patterns in earlier weeks. While the difference-in-differences method accounts for fixed differences, this means we should interpret the results with some caution. However, the fact that the treatment effect turned positive only *after* the switch—and grew over time—supports a causal interpretation.

### 5. Other Factors
The analysis controlled for staffing levels and visitor volume. Both factors positively affect service scores, but the procedure effect remains significant even after accounting for them.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all remaining branches.

2. **Monitor implementation closely** during the first 4–6 weeks at each new branch, as the data suggests benefits accumulate over time.

3. **Track service scores** post-rollout to confirm the 2-point improvement is replicated across all branches.

4. **Document best practices** from early-adopter branches to support training at remaining locations.

5. **Consider a follow-up evaluation** in 3–6 months to assess whether the improvement is sustained long-term.
