# **Memorandum to the Board**

## **Title: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **1.9 points** (approximately **3.8% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and appears to strengthen over time. We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **709 branches** participated in the evaluation (425 switched to the new procedure; 284 kept the old procedure)
- Data collected over **17 weeks** (Weeks 6–22)
- Switch occurred at **Week 15**; analysis compares 9 weeks before and 8 weeks after

### **Impact on Service Scores**

| Group | Pre-Switch Average | Post-Switch Average | Change |
|-------|-------------------|---------------------|--------|
| Switched Branches | 50.4 | 52.4 | +2.1 |
| Non-Switched Branches | 50.1 | 50.3 | +0.2 |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | — | — | **+1.9** |

### **Statistical Confidence**
- **Effect size:** +1.91 points on the service score index
- **95% Confidence Interval:** [1.38, 2.45]
- **P-value:** < 0.001 (highly statistically significant)
- The analysis controlled for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume

### **Effect Grows Over Time**
The improvement was not immediate but strengthened as branches gained experience:
- **Weeks 15–18 (early):** +1.4 points
- **Weeks 19–22 (later):** +2.4 points

This pattern suggests branches may need a few weeks to fully realize the benefits of the new procedure.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all 284 branches currently using the old method.

2. **Plan a phased implementation** allowing 2–3 weeks for staff training and adjustment at each branch, recognizing that full benefits emerge after the initial transition period.

3. **Continue monitoring** service scores during rollout to confirm the effect holds across different branch sizes and locations.

4. **Document best practices** from the 425 early-adopter branches to support training materials for remaining branches.

5. **Consider a follow-up evaluation** in 6 months to assess whether the improvement is sustained long-term and to identify any unintended consequences.

---

## **Analytical Notes**

This analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, which compares the change in service scores for switched branches against the change for non-switched branches over the same time period. This method accounts for any system-wide trends (such as seasonal variations) that might affect all branches simultaneously. The estimates are robust to clustering at the branch level and control for weekly staffing and visitor volume variations.
