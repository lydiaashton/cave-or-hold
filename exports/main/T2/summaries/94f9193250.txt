# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure is associated with a **2.9-point improvement** in service scores relative to branches that kept the old procedure. However, this effect is **not statistically significant at conventional levels** (p ≈ 0.10), meaning we cannot rule out that the observed improvement occurred by chance. The evidence suggests a modest positive effect, but it is not strong enough to confidently recommend a system-wide rollout based on this data alone.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in switched branches to changes in non-switched branches

### **Results**

| Group | Before Switch (weeks 6–14) | After Switch (weeks 15–22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------------|---------------------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 48.6 | 50.4 | **+1.8** |
| Non-switched branches | 53.6 | 52.5 | **–1.1** |
| **Difference (treatment effect)** | | | **+2.9** |

- **Treatment effect**: +2.9 points (95% confidence interval: –0.5 to +6.3)
- **Statistical significance**: p = 0.10 (marginally significant; not significant at the standard 5% threshold)
- **Effect size**: Approximately 5.6% of the average service score, or 0.28 standard deviations (small-to-moderate effect)

### **Important Context**
- Branches that switched to the new procedure started with **lower service scores** than non-switched branches (about 5 points lower on average)
- The analysis accounts for this baseline difference through the difference-in-differences design
- Service scores across all branches showed a slight declining trend over the study period; switched branches bucked this trend

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Continue monitoring**: Collect additional weeks of data to increase statistical power and determine whether the effect strengthens over time

2. **Consider a phased rollout**: Given the suggestive (but not definitive) positive effect, the cooperative could expand the new procedure to additional branches while continuing to measure outcomes

3. **Investigate implementation variation**: Examine whether some switched branches achieved better results than others, which could identify best practices for training and support

4. **Evaluate cost-benefit**: Assess whether the operational costs of the new procedure are justified by the observed service score improvement

5. **Set a decision timeline**: Revisit this analysis after 8–12 more weeks of data; if the effect remains positive and achieves statistical significance, proceed with full rollout

---

## **Technical Notes**

- Analysis controls for staffing levels and visit volumes (which did not materially change the results)
- Standard errors are clustered by branch to account for repeated observations
- The parallel trends assumption appears reasonable: switched and non-switched branches followed similar trajectories before the intervention
