# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.3 points** (on a scale where the average is ~50) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents a **4.4% improvement** and is statistically significant (p < 0.001). We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure (treatment group); **60 branches** kept the old procedure (control group)
- The switch occurred at **week 15**
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, which compares changes in the treatment group to changes in the control group

### Main Results

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Treatment (switched) | 52.15 | 52.30 | +0.15 |
| Control (not switched) | 52.72 | 50.56 | −2.17 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+2.31** |

- **Estimated effect: +2.30 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.0 to 3.6)
- **Statistical significance: p < 0.001** (highly significant)
- **Effect size: 0.24 standard deviations** (small to moderate)

### Why We Trust This Result

1. **Parallel trends validated**: Before the switch, treatment and control branches were trending similarly (no significant difference in pre-period trends, p = 0.80). This supports the validity of our comparison.

2. **Robust across specifications**: The effect remains consistent whether we:
   - Use simple comparison (+2.31)
   - Control for staffing and visits (+2.28)
   - Account for branch-specific characteristics using fixed effects (+2.30)

3. **Effect emerges after switch**: Event study analysis shows the treatment effect appears only after week 15, with no pre-existing differences between groups.

4. **Control group declined**: Notably, control branches saw service scores drop by 2.2 points after week 15, while treatment branches held steady. The new procedure appears to have **protected against a decline** rather than driving dramatic improvement.

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches.

2. **Monitor implementation**: Track service scores during rollout to ensure effects replicate at scale.

3. **Investigate the control group decline**: Understand why non-switched branches experienced a drop in service scores after week 15. This may indicate external factors (seasonal, policy changes, etc.) that the new procedure helps mitigate.

4. **Consider cost-benefit analysis**: If the new procedure has implementation costs, weigh them against the 4.4% service score improvement.

5. **Continue evaluation**: Collect additional data post-rollout to confirm long-term effects and identify any branches that may need additional support during transition.
