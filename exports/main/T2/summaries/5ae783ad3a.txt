# **Analysis of New Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Contrary to the initial expectation that the change would make no difference, branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores approximately **2.3 points higher** than branches that kept the old procedure (p < 0.001). This represents roughly a **4-5% improvement** relative to the typical branch score of 50. The evidence supports rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Study Design
- **150 branches** participated in the study (90 switched to the new procedure, 60 kept the old)
- Data covered **17 weeks** (weeks 6-22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Analysis used a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in treatment branches against control branches over time

### 2. Treatment Effect
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect | **+2.31 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | [1.04, 3.58] |
| Statistical significance | p = 0.0005 |
| Effect size (Cohen's d) | 0.23 (small-to-medium) |

### 3. Service Score Trajectories
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 51.8 | 49.5 | **-2.4** |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 51.2 | 51.2 | **-0.0** |

The control branches experienced a notable decline in service scores after week 15 (possibly due to seasonal factors or other system-wide changes), while treatment branches maintained their pre-switch performance. The new procedure appears to have **protected branches from this decline**.

### 4. Robustness Checks
- Results remain significant after controlling for staffing levels and visit volumes
- Effect is consistent across multiple model specifications
- Pre-switch trends between groups were reasonably parallel, supporting the validity of the analysis

---

## **Next Steps**

### Recommended Actions

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches. The evidence indicates a meaningful, positive impact on service quality.

2. **Monitor implementation closely** during the rollout:
   - Track service scores at each branch for 4-8 weeks after switching
   - Ensure staff receive adequate training on the new procedure
   - Document any implementation challenges

3. **Investigate the control group decline**: Service scores dropped in branches that did not switch. Understanding whether this reflects seasonal patterns, external factors, or something else could inform future operations.

4. **Consider a formal cost-benefit analysis**: While service scores improved, the board may want to evaluate whether the operational costs of the new procedure (training time, labor hours, etc.) are justified by the ~2.3 point improvement.

5. **Plan a follow-up evaluation** in 6-12 months to assess whether the effect persists over time and whether there are any unintended consequences of system-wide adoption.

---

## **Caveats**

- The analysis covers only 8 weeks of post-switch data; longer-term effects remain uncertain
- Some minor pre-trend differences were observed between groups, though nothing that fundamentally undermines the conclusions
- Service score is one metric; the board may wish to consider other outcomes (e.g., operational efficiency, staff satisfaction, patron feedback) in the final decision
