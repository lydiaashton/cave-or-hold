# **Memorandum to the Board**

## **Title: Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new rotation and shelving procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **1.76 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant and represents approximately a **3.5% gain** relative to the typical branch score of 50. We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. The New Procedure Had a Positive, Statistically Significant Effect**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect on service score | **+1.76 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | 0.68 to 2.85 points |
| Statistical significance | p = 0.003 (significant at 1% level) |

The analysis used a rigorous "difference-in-differences" approach, comparing 20 branches that adopted the new procedure against 14 branches that continued with the old procedure, before and after week 15 when the switch occurred.

### **2. Treatment and Control Branches Were Comparable Before the Switch**

A critical assumption of this analysis is that treatment and control branches were on similar trajectories before the change. Our analysis confirms this:
- Pre-switch trends (weeks 6–14) showed **no statistically significant difference** between the two groups (p = 0.73)
- Both groups experienced similar week-to-week fluctuations before the procedure change
- This supports the conclusion that the observed improvement is due to the new procedure, not pre-existing differences

### **3. The Effect Is Not Explained by Staffing or Visit Changes**

We examined whether the improvement might be driven by changes in staffing levels or branch visits:
- **Staffing index**: No meaningful difference between groups after the switch (+0.01, not significant)
- **Visits index**: No meaningful difference between groups after the switch (-0.15, not significant)

This suggests the service score improvement reflects genuine operational improvements from the new procedure itself, not changes in resources or demand.

### **4. Visual Evidence Supports the Conclusion**

The attached charts show:
- **Trend chart**: Treatment branches (blue line) pulled ahead of control branches (purple line) after week 15
- **Before/After comparison**: Treatment branches improved from 51.1 to 52.8, while control branches remained essentially flat (52.6 to 52.4)
