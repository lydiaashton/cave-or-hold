# **MEMORANDUM**

## **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

### **Bottom Line**

Branches that adopted the new procedure showed service score improvements of approximately **2.2 points** (about 4% above baseline) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. The effect is statistically significant and appears to grow over time. However, branches that switched were already improving faster before the change, so we cannot definitively attribute all of the improvement to the new procedure. **We recommend a cautious, phased rollout with continued monitoring.**

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. Study Design & Sample**
- 709 branches total: 425 adopted the new procedure, 284 kept the old procedure
- 17 weeks of data (weeks 6–22); the switch occurred in week 15
- Service scores average around 50 points (higher is better)

**2. Main Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 50.45 | 50.85 | +0.40 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 51.40 | 53.99 | +2.59 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+2.19** |

- The new procedure is associated with a **2.2-point increase** in service scores (95% CI: 1.7 to 2.8 points)
- This effect is highly statistically significant (p < 0.001)
- The improvement represents approximately **4% above the baseline** average score

**3. Effect Over Time**
The treatment effect appears to strengthen in the weeks following implementation:
- Weeks 15–18: Modest effects (0 to 1 point above control trend)
- Weeks 19–22: Larger effects (1.4 to 1.7 points, statistically significant)

This pattern suggests branches may need time to fully realize the benefits of the new procedure.

**4. Important Caveat: Pre-Existing Trends**
Branches that chose to switch were already showing faster score improvement before the procedure change. This means:
- Some of the observed improvement may reflect pre-existing momentum, not the new procedure itself
- The true causal effect of the new procedure is likely **smaller than 2.2 points**, though still positive

---

### **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with phased rollout** – The evidence supports expanding the new procedure, but do so in stages to monitor implementation quality and outcomes.

2. **Track implementation fidelity** – Ensure branches are correctly following the new procedure; effects may vary based on how well it's executed.

3. **Continue monitoring for 8–12 weeks post-rollout** – The growing effect over time suggests benefits may accumulate; longer-term data will provide clearer estimates.

4. **Consider a randomized rollout for future changes** – For the next procedural change, randomly assign branches to treatment/control to eliminate selection bias and produce more definitive causal evidence.

5. **Investigate why some branches improved faster** – Understanding what distinguished early-adopting branches could help target support and training during the rollout.
