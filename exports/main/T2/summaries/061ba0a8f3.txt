# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new shelving and rotation procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.1 points** (95% confidence interval: 0.9 to 3.3 points) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents roughly a **4% improvement** over baseline scores. The result is statistically significant (p < 0.001), and we recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. The New Procedure Increased Service Scores**
- **Treatment effect:** +2.11 points on the service score index
- **Confidence interval:** We are 95% confident the true effect lies between 0.92 and 3.30 points
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.0005 (highly significant)
- **Practical significance:** Approximately 4.3% improvement relative to the baseline average of ~50 points

### **2. Analysis Method: Difference-in-Differences**
This study used a rigorous quasi-experimental design:
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure (treatment group)
- **60 branches** kept the old procedure (control group)
- **17 weeks** of data per branch (weeks 6–22)
- The switch occurred at week 15, creating clear "before" and "after" periods
- This design controls for both time-invariant branch differences and overall time trends

### **3. Parallel Trends Assumption Holds**
A critical requirement for this analysis is that treatment and control groups were trending similarly before the switch. Our test confirms:
- No statistically significant difference in pre-switch trends (p = 0.27)
- Both groups showed similar week-to-week variation before week 15
- This validates our causal interpretation of the results

### **4. Effect Was Consistent Over Time**
The treatment-control gap remained positive throughout all 8 post-switch weeks, ranging from 2.8 to 5.0 points, with no evidence of the effect fading over time.

### **5. Results Hold After Controlling for Other Factors**
The positive effect persists even after accounting for weekly staffing levels and visitor volumes, suggesting the procedure change itself—not external factors—drove the improvement.

---

## **Important Context**

**This finding contradicts initial expectations.** The note accompanying the data stated: *"We expected this to make no difference to the service score."* However, the data clearly show a meaningful positive effect. This is good news for the cooperative—the new procedure delivers benefits beyond what was anticipated.

**Baseline differences:** Treatment branches had slightly higher service scores before the switch (about 2 points higher on average). The difference-in-differences design accounts for this by comparing *changes* in scores rather than absolute levels, isolating the effect of the procedure change itself.

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all 60 branches still using the old method.

2. **Monitor implementation** to ensure the new procedure is being followed consistently across all branches.

3. **Track service scores** for the next 3–6 months to confirm the effect persists at scale and does not diminish over time.

4. **Document the new procedure** in detail to facilitate training and ensure fidelity of implementation.

5. **Consider investigating why the procedure works.** Since a positive effect was not expected, understanding the mechanism could help optimize the procedure further or identify other areas for improvement.

## **Technical Notes**

- Analysis used ordinary least squares regression with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch) to account for repeated observations per branch.
- All 150 branches had complete data for all 17 weeks; no observations were excluded.
- The model controlled for staffing_index and visits_index to isolate the procedure effect from other weekly variations.
