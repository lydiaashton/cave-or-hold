# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure **improved service scores by approximately 2 points (4% relative improvement)**. While the result is encouraging and directionally supports rollout, the statistical evidence is **moderate rather than definitive** (p=0.11 with conservative error estimates). We recommend a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** tracked over 17 weeks (Weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure starting Week 15 (treatment group)
- **14 branches** maintained the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in the treatment group against the control group

### **Results**

| Group | Pre-Switch Avg | Post-Switch Avg | Change |
|-------|---------------|-----------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 50.89 | 50.90 | +0.01 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 49.69 | 51.72 | +2.03 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+2.02** |

### **Statistical Assessment**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect | +2.02 points |
| Relative improvement | ~4% |
| Statistical significance (cluster-robust) | p = 0.11 |
| 95% Confidence interval | -0.5 to +4.8 points |
| Pre-trends test (parallel trends assumption) | p = 0.35 ✓ |

**Interpretation:**
- The **pre-trends test confirms** that treatment and control branches were on similar trajectories before the switch, validating our comparison
- The **point estimate is positive and meaningful** (2 points on a ~50-point scale)
- However, **statistical uncertainty is substantial**—we cannot rule out effects ranging from slightly negative to nearly 5 points positive

### **Weekly Trend Pattern**

The data shows a clear pattern: before Week 15, treatment branches scored about 1–2 points below control branches on average. After the switch, treatment branches not only closed this gap but began outperforming control branches in most weeks. This pattern is consistent with a genuine treatment effect.

---

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended Actions**

1. **Proceed with cautious rollout** to remaining branches, but implement in phases (e.g., 5–10 branches per month) rather than all at once

2. **Establish ongoing monitoring** with the following metrics:
   - Weekly service scores by branch
   - Staff time/cost associated with the new procedure
   - Patron satisfaction surveys (if available)

3. **Set a review checkpoint** after 8–12 weeks of full implementation to reassess:
   - Whether the 2-point improvement is sustained at scale
   - Whether implementation costs justify the benefit
   - Whether any branches are struggling with the new procedure

4. **Document implementation learnings** from the 20 early-adopter branches to support training and smooth adoption at remaining locations

### **Considerations for the Board**

- **Upside:** If the 2-point improvement holds, this represents a meaningful service quality gain across the cooperative
- **Risk:** The statistical uncertainty means we should be prepared for the possibility that the true effect is smaller than observed
- **Cost:** The analysis did not include implementation costs; the board should weigh the 4% service improvement against any additional staffing or operational expenses
