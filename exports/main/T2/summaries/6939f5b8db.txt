# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation procedure is associated with a **modest improvement in service scores** (+1.75 points, or approximately 3.4%), but the evidence is **not statistically conclusive**. The board should consider a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in treatment branches to changes in control branches

### **Results**

| Group | Pre-Switch Average | Post-Switch Average | Change |
|-------|-------------------|---------------------|--------|
| **Treatment (switched)** | 52.01 | 53.79 | **+1.78** |
| **Control (not switched)** | 50.89 | 50.92 | +0.03 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | — | — | **+1.75** |

### **Statistical Assessment**
- **Effect size:** +1.75 points on the service score index (95% confidence interval: -0.34 to +3.84)
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.10 (marginally significant; does not meet the conventional 0.05 threshold)
- **Practical significance:** Approximately 3.4% improvement relative to baseline scores
- **Parallel trends check:** Pre-switch trends were similar between groups, supporting the validity of the comparison

### **What the Data Shows**
1. **Treatment branches improved** after switching to the new procedure
2. **Control branches remained stable** over the same period
3. The **positive effect is consistent** whether or not we control for staffing levels and visitor volume
4. However, with the current sample size, we **cannot rule out the possibility** that the observed improvement is due to random variation

---

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended Actions**

1. **Proceed with cautious expansion**
   - The point estimate is positive and meaningful for operations
   - No evidence of negative effects on service scores

2. **Extend the observation period**
   - Collect 8–12 additional weeks of data to increase statistical power
   - This would help determine whether the effect is real and stable over time

3. **Monitor implementation fidelity**
   - Track whether branches are following the new procedure consistently
   - Identify best practices from high-performing switched branches

4. **Consider cost-benefit analysis**
   - Weigh the modest service score improvement against implementation costs
   - Survey branch staff on operational impacts not captured in the service score

5. **Plan for system-wide evaluation**
   - If rolling out to all branches, establish a formal evaluation framework
   - Set a target service score improvement (e.g., +2 points) and timeline for assessment

---

## **Technical Notes**

- Analysis controls for branch-level clustering in standard errors
- Results are robust to inclusion of staffing and visitor volume controls
- Pre-treatment trends between treatment and control groups were statistically parallel (p = 0.86), supporting the causal interpretation
