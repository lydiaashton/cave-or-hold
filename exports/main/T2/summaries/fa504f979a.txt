# **Analysis of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Contrary to the expectation of no difference, branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by **1.87 points** (95% CI: 0.73 to 3.01) relative to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant (p = 0.001) and represents approximately a **3.8% improvement** relative to the average service score of 49.3.

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout**, with attention to supporting lower-performing branches during the transition.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Study Design
- **150 branches** total: 90 adopted the new procedure, 60 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22): 9 weeks before the switch, 8 weeks after
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in the treatment group to changes in the control group

### 2. Main Result
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 50.56 | 47.98 | -2.57 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 49.63 | 48.93 | -0.70 |

While both groups experienced a decline in service scores after week 15 (possibly due to seasonal factors or other external influences), **branches with the new procedure declined significantly less**. The net effect of the new procedure is **+1.87 points**.

### 3. Statistical Confidence
- The effect is highly statistically significant (p = 0.0013)
- We can be 95% confident the true effect lies between **0.73 and 3.01 points**
- The parallel trends assumption is supported: pre-switch trends were similar between groups (p = 0.9997)

### 4. Variation Across Branches
The benefit appears somewhat larger for higher-performing branches:
- **High-performing branches**: +2.29 points (p = 0.009)
- **Lower-performing branches**: +1.44 points (p = 0.063)

Both groups benefit, but the effect is more pronounced among branches that were already performing well.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all 150 branches

2. **Provide additional support to lower-performing branches** during implementation, as they may benefit less and could require more assistance with the transition

3. **Continue monitoring** service scores after full rollout to confirm the effect persists at scale

4. **Investigate why service scores declined overall** after week 15 (both groups dropped, but treatment dropped less). Understanding this external factor could help maximize the benefit of the new procedure

5. **Consider a follow-up study** in 3–6 months to assess whether the effect is sustained over time

---

## **Technical Notes**

- Analysis uses cluster-robust standard errors at the branch level
- Results are robust to controlling for staffing and visit indices
- All 150 branches have complete data for the study period (balanced panel)
