# **Analysis of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure **improved service scores** by an average of **2.3 points** (on a scale where the average branch scores about 52). This effect is statistically significant and represents a meaningful 4-5% improvement. The evidence supports rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. The New Procedure Increased Service Scores
- Switched branches saw their service scores **increase by 2.3 points** relative to branches that kept the old procedure
- This result is **highly statistically significant** (p < 0.001), meaning it is very unlikely to be due to chance
- The 95% confidence interval ranges from **1.0 to 3.6 points**, indicating a real positive effect

### 2. The Improvement Appeared Immediately and Persisted
- Service scores jumped in the first week after the switch (week 15) and remained elevated through most of the observation period
- The largest gains occurred in weeks 15-19 (3-4 point improvement), with some variation afterward but consistently positive effects

### 3. The Analysis Is Methodologically Sound
- **Parallel trends confirmed**: Before the switch, switched and non-switched branches were moving in similar patterns, validating our comparison
- **Robust to controls**: The result holds when accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume
- **Robust to branch differences**: The result holds when controlling for inherent differences between individual branches

### 4. Context for the Effect Size
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Average service score (all branches) | 52.0 |
| Improvement from new procedure | +2.3 points |
| Relative improvement | +4.4% |
| Effect size (in standard deviations) | 0.24 SD |

A 0.24 standard deviation improvement is considered a **small-to-moderate effect** in social science research—meaningful for an operational change that doesn't require major investment.

## **Technical Notes**

- **Data**: 150 branches, 17 weeks (weeks 6-22), 2,550 observations
- **Method**: Difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors
- **Treatment group**: 90 branches that adopted the new procedure in week 15
- **Control group**: 60 branches that continued with the old procedure
- **Analysis controlled for**: Branch fixed effects, time effects, staffing levels, and visitor volume
