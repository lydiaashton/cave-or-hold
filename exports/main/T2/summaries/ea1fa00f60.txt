## **Summary: Impact of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure on Service Scores**

### **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new rotation and shelving procedure saw their service scores increase by approximately **1.9 points (3.7%)** relative to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and robust across multiple analytical approaches. We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. The new procedure produced a measurable improvement**
- Switched branches: Service scores increased from 51.8 to 52.5 (+0.7 points)
- Non-switched branches: Service scores decreased from 49.8 to 48.6 (−1.2 points)
- **Net effect of the new procedure: +1.9 points** (difference-in-differences estimate)

**2. The result is statistically significant**
- p-value < 0.001 across all model specifications
- The effect remains significant after controlling for staffing levels, visit volumes, branch-specific characteristics, and week-to-week variation

**3. The improvement is meaningful in context**
- Average service score is approximately 50; a 1.9-point gain represents a 3.7% improvement
- Without the new procedure, service scores appear to have been trending downward (as seen in the control group). The new procedure not only prevented this decline but produced an actual improvement.

**4. Important context about the analysis**
- 709 branches total: 425 switched to the new procedure, 284 kept the old procedure
- Data covers 17 weeks (weeks 6–22); the switch occurred at week 15
- Switched branches had slightly higher service scores than non-switched branches even before the change. However, the critical finding is that switched branches **improved** after the change while non-switched branches **declined**.
