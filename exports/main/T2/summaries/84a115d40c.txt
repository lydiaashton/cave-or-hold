# **Board Summary: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2 points** (a 4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and emerged gradually over the 8 weeks following implementation. The board should consider rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **709 branches** participated in the study (425 switched to the new procedure; 284 kept the old procedure)
- Data covers **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in the treatment group to changes in the control group

### Main Results

| Group | Pre-Switch Average | Post-Switch Average | Change |
|-------|-------------------|---------------------|--------|
| New Procedure (425 branches) | 50.96 | 52.55 | **+1.59** |
| Old Procedure (284 branches) | 49.85 | 49.37 | **-0.48** |

**Estimated Treatment Effect: +2.07 points** (95% confidence interval: +1.54 to +2.60; p < 0.001)

### Additional Insights

1. **Effect emerged gradually**: The improvement was not immediate. Service scores showed significant gains starting around week 18 (3 weeks after the switch), suggesting branches needed time to adapt to the new procedure.

2. **Effect is direct, not indirect**: The improvement in service scores was not driven by changes in staffing levels or visitor volume—both remained stable across groups. This indicates the procedure itself drove the improvement.

3. **Control group declined slightly**: Branches keeping the old procedure saw a small decline (-0.48 points) in the post-switch period, while treatment branches improved. This widening gap strengthens confidence in the result.

4. **Practical significance**: A 2-point improvement on a ~50-point scale represents a **4% gain** in service quality—meaningful for patron experience.

---

## **Next Steps**

### Recommended Actions

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all 284 branches currently using the old procedure.

2. **Plan for an adaptation period**: Expect 2–3 weeks for branches to fully realize the benefits. Consider providing additional support during this transition.

3. **Monitor implementation**: Track service scores for the first 8–12 weeks after rollout to confirm the effect replicates at scale.

4. **Document best practices**: Interview high-performing switched branches to identify implementation tips that could accelerate adoption.

### Caveats & Considerations

- The analysis assumes that, absent the switch, treatment and control branches would have followed similar trends. Pre-switch data from weeks 10–14 support this assumption, though earlier weeks showed some divergence.
- The study covers only 8 post-switch weeks. Longer-term effects (sustained improvement, potential decay) remain unknown.
- Service score is a composite index. Consider supplementing with direct patron satisfaction measures in future evaluations.
