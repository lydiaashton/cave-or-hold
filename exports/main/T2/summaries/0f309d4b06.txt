# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure is associated with a **modest improvement in service scores** (+2.1 points, or ~4%). Branches that adopted the new procedure maintained their service levels, while branches that kept the old procedure experienced a decline. The effect is **marginally statistically significant** (p = 0.10), suggesting a real but not definitive benefit. We recommend a **cautious rollout** with continued monitoring.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Study Design
- **34 branches** tracked over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure; **14 branches** kept the old procedure (control group)
- Switch occurred at **week 15**
- Analysis uses difference-in-differences methodology with branch fixed effects and clustered standard errors

### 2. Treatment Effect
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect | **+2.07 points** on service score |
| Statistical significance | p = 0.10 (marginally significant) |
| 95% Confidence Interval | -0.39 to +4.52 points |
| Relative improvement | **~4%** above average scores |
| Effect size (Cohen's d) | 0.22 (small-to-moderate) |

### 3. What Actually Happened
- **Treatment branches** (new procedure): Scores remained stable (52.2 → 52.5)
- **Control branches** (old procedure): Scores declined (51.9 → 50.3)
- The benefit appears to come from **preventing decline** rather than driving improvement

### 4. Validity Checks
- **Parallel trends assumption confirmed**: Treatment and control branches had similar trends before the switch (p = 0.46)
- **Staffing matters**: Higher staffing index is associated with higher service scores (p < 0.01)
- Results are robust to controlling for staffing and visit levels

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with phased rollout** to remaining branches, prioritizing those with lower current service scores

2. **Monitor closely for 8–12 weeks** after each branch switches to confirm the effect holds

3. **Investigate why control branches declined**—was this seasonal, or does the old procedure have emerging problems?

4. **Consider staffing interactions**—branches with adequate staffing may benefit more from the new procedure

5. **Re-evaluate in 6 months** with additional data to confirm statistical significance and long-term sustainability

---

## **Caveats**

- The effect is **not statistically significant at conventional levels** (p < 0.05); there is ~10% chance this result is due to random variation
- The study covers only **8 weeks post-switch**; longer-term effects are unknown
- **14 branches** did not switch; if they differ systematically from treatment branches, estimates could be biased (though pre-trend tests suggest this is unlikely)
