# **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure is associated with a **modest improvement** in service scores (approximately +2 points, or 4%), but this effect is **not statistically distinguishable from zero** given the current data. The evidence suggests the new procedure does not harm service quality and may provide a small benefit, but we cannot confidently conclude it causes meaningful improvement. **We recommend proceeding with caution**—either continuing the pilot to gather more data or implementing with careful monitoring.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. **Direction of Effect: Positive**
- Branches that switched to the new procedure saw their average service score **increase by 1.26 points** (from 47.5 to 48.8)
- Control branches (keeping the old procedure) saw their scores **decline by 0.68 points** (from 51.2 to 50.6)
- The **difference-in-differences estimate is +1.94 points**, meaning the new procedure is associated with roughly a 2-point improvement relative to what would have happened otherwise

### 2. **Statistical Uncertainty**
- The 95% confidence interval ranges from **-0.45 to +4.33 points**
- The p-value is **0.11**, meaning there's an 11% chance we'd see this result even if the new procedure had no real effect
- This does not meet the conventional threshold for statistical significance (p < 0.05)

### 3. **No Evidence of Harm**
- The lower bound of the confidence interval (-0.45) suggests the new procedure is **unlikely to cause meaningful degradation** in service scores
- Service scores remained stable or improved for switched branches, while control branches experienced a slight decline

### 4. **Study Design Strengths**
- **Parallel trends assumption holds**: Before the switch, both groups showed similar week-to-week patterns
- **Branch-level variation accounted for**: Individual branch characteristics explain much of the score variation, but the treatment effect estimate remains consistent after controlling for this
- **Adequate sample**: 34 branches over 17 weeks (578 observations), with 20 branches switching and 14 serving as controls

### 5. **Other Factors**
- **Staffing levels matter**: Higher staffing is significantly associated with better service scores (p = 0.018)
- **Branch-to-branch variation is substantial**: Some branches consistently score 15-20 points higher or lower than others, regardless of procedure

---

## **Next Steps**

### **Option A: Continue Pilot and Expand Data Collection**
- Extend the observation period to 6-12 more weeks to increase statistical power
- This would narrow the confidence interval and provide a more definitive answer
- **Cost**: Delayed decision, but reduced risk of rolling out an ineffective procedure

### **Option B: Proceed with Cautious Rollout**
- Implement the new procedure across all branches **with monitoring**
- Set up ongoing tracking of service scores with a plan to revert if scores decline
- **Rationale**: The procedure shows no evidence of harm, may provide modest benefit, and the operational costs of maintaining two systems may outweigh the uncertainty

### **Option C: Hybrid Approach (Recommended)**
- Roll out the new procedure to additional branches in phases
- Continue rigorous monitoring with monthly score reviews
- Establish a clear threshold (e.g., if scores drop by more than 3 points sustained over 4 weeks, pause and investigate)
- This balances the potential benefits against the risk of unintended consequences
