# Analysis of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

## Bottom Line

The new procedure is associated with a **modest improvement** in service scores (approximately +2.3 points), but the evidence is **not statistically conclusive**. The 95% confidence interval ranges from -0.6 to +5.1 points, meaning we cannot rule out no effect or even a small negative effect. The board should consider this **suggestive but not definitive** evidence when deciding on a system-wide rollout.

---

## Key Findings

### 1. Estimated Impact
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Treatment effect (DiD estimate) | **+2.26 points** |
| Standard error | 1.43 |
| P-value | 0.12 |
| 95% Confidence Interval | **-0.55 to +5.07** |

The treatment branches improved by about 2.3 points more than control branches after the switch. On a scale where the average branch scores about 50, this represents roughly a **4-5% improvement**.

### 2. Study Design
- **34 branches total**: 20 switched to the new procedure (treatment), 14 kept the old procedure (control)
- **17 weeks of data**: Weeks 6-14 (pre-switch) and Weeks 15-22 (post-switch)
- **Method**: Difference-in-differences with branch fixed effects and cluster-robust standard errors

### 3. Parallel Trends Assumption ✓
The analysis relies on the assumption that treatment and control branches would have followed similar trends absent the intervention. This appears valid:
- Pre-switch trends were statistically similar (p = 0.82)
- Both groups showed comparable week-to-week variation before the switch

### 4. Context
- Treatment branches started with **lower baseline scores** (~2.7 points below control)
- After the switch, the **gap narrowed substantially** (to ~0.5 points)
- The improvement is consistent across model specifications (with or without controlling for staffing and visits)

---

## Next Steps

### Recommended Actions

1. **Continue monitoring**: Collect additional weeks of data to increase statistical power. With more observations, we may be able to detect whether the 2.3-point effect is real.

2. **Consider a phased rollout**: Given the positive (though uncertain) direction of the effect and no evidence of harm, expanding to additional branches while continuing to evaluate could be reasonable.

3. **Investigate heterogeneity**: Examine whether the procedure works better for certain types of branches (e.g., by size, location, or baseline performance).

4. **Cost-benefit analysis**: If the new procedure has implementation costs, weigh them against the potential 4-5% service improvement.

### Caveats

- The effect is **not statistically significant** at conventional levels (p > 0.05)
- The confidence interval is wide—**the true effect could be zero or slightly negative**
- Only 8 weeks of post-switch data limits our ability to assess long-term effects
