# **Board Summary: New Branch Procedure Evaluation**

## **Bottom Line**

**Do not roll out the new procedure to all branches based on this evidence.** While branches that switched show higher service scores after the change, this improvement was already happening *before* they switched. After accounting for this pre-existing trend, the new procedure shows **no evidence of benefit** and may actually be associated with slightly worse performance than expected.

## **Key Findings**

### 1. What the Raw Data Shows
- Branches that switched to the new procedure saw their service scores increase by **+2.4 points** after the switch
- Branches that kept the old procedure saw minimal change (**+0.1 points**)
- Simple comparison suggests a **+2.3 point improvement** from the new procedure

### 2. The Critical Problem: Pre-Existing Differences
The branches that chose to switch were **already improving faster** than other branches *before* the procedure changed:

| Period | Treatment-Control Gap |
|--------|----------------------|
| Week 6 (start) | +0.4 points |
| Week 14 (before switch) | +3.6 points |
| Week 22 (after switch) | +4.7 points |

The gap was widening by **0.38 points per week** during the pre-treatment period. This suggests switching branches were already on an upward trajectory—possibly because they had better management, more motivated staff, or other advantages.

### 3. What Happens When We Account for the Pre-Trend
If the pre-existing trend had continued *without* any procedure change:
- **Expected** post-switch gap: 4.95 points
- **Actual** post-switch gap: 3.96 points
- **Estimated procedure effect: -1.0 points**

This means the new procedure is associated with service scores that are **1 point lower** than what the switching branches' pre-existing trend would have predicted.

## **Next Steps**

### Immediate Actions
1. **Pause the systemwide rollout** until we have stronger evidence
2. **Interview switching branches** to understand why they were already improving—this may reveal best practices worth sharing regardless of the procedure
3. **Gather qualitative feedback** from branches about their experience with the new procedure (implementation challenges, staff satisfaction, etc.)

### For Better Evidence
4. **Randomized rollout**: Assign remaining branches randomly to switch now vs. later. This eliminates selection bias and provides credible causal evidence.
5. **Extended monitoring**: Continue tracking all branches for at least 6 more months to see if effects stabilize or change
6. **Segmented analysis**: Examine whether the procedure works better for certain types of branches (by size, location, staffing levels, etc.)

### Decision Framework
| Scenario | Recommendation |
|----------|----------------|
| Randomized trial shows positive effect | Proceed with rollout |
| Randomized trial shows no effect | Abandon new procedure |
| Randomized trial shows negative effect | Revert switching branches to old procedure |

## **Bottom Line for Decision-Making**

The current data reflects **selection bias**, not causal impact. Branches that switched were already improving. Rolling out the procedure systemwide based on this evidence risks:
- Implementing a change that provides no benefit
- Disrupting operations at branches that don't need it
- Missing the opportunity to learn what's *actually* driving improvement at the switching branches

**Recommendation: Pause and test properly before expanding.**
