## **Summary for the Board: Impact of New Branch Procedure on Service Scores**

### **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Contrary to the expectation that it would make no difference, branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **2.1 points** (approximately 4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant (p < 0.001) and robust across multiple analytical approaches.

**Recommendation:** The evidence supports rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. Study Design**
- 150 branches total: 90 adopted the new procedure (treatment group), 60 kept the old procedure (control group)
- 17 weeks of data (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- 2,550 total observations (complete data for all branches across all weeks)

**2. Main Result: Difference-in-Differences Estimate**
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| New Procedure (90 branches) | 48.95 | 52.00 | +3.05 |
| Old Procedure (60 branches) | 48.79 | 49.71 | +0.92 |
| **Difference** | | | **+2.13** |

- **Treatment effect: +2.13 points** (95% confidence interval: 0.97 to 3.30)
- **P-value: 0.0003** (highly statistically significant)
- This represents approximately a **4.4% improvement** relative to baseline scores

**3. Validity Checks**
- **Parallel trends assumption holds:** Before the switch (weeks 6–14), treatment and control branches had nearly identical service score trends (average difference of only 0.16 points, with no significant trend over time)
- **Robust to controls:** The effect remains unchanged (2.12 points) when controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume
- **Consistent post-switch:** The positive effect persists across all 8 post-switch weeks, with the largest gains in weeks 15, 17, and 18

**4. Practical Significance**
- Service scores average around 50 across the system; a 2-point improvement is meaningful for patron experience
- The improvement occurred without requiring additional staffing (staffing index was controlled for in the analysis)
