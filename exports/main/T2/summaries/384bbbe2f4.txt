# **Board Summary: New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure Evaluation**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **1.95 points** (approximately **4% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and consistent across the 8-week post-switch period. We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect
- **Difference-in-differences estimate: +1.95 points** (95% CI: 1.58 to 2.32)
- **Statistical significance: p < 0.001** (highly reliable result)
- **Effect size: 3.8% improvement** relative to baseline scores (~51 points)

### 2. Study Design
- **709 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6-22)
- **425 branches** (60%) switched to the new procedure at week 15
- **284 branches** (40%) kept the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis controls for branch-specific characteristics and weekly staffing/visit patterns

### 3. Before-and-After Comparison
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Treatment (switched) | 51.93 | 53.26 | **+1.33** |
| Control (no switch) | 51.15 | 50.53 | **-0.62** |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | | | **+1.95** |

### 4. Parallel Trends Validation
The analysis relies on the assumption that treatment and control branches would have followed similar trends absent the intervention. Pre-switch data (weeks 6-14) shows:
- Stable difference between groups (averaging 0.77 points)
- No systematic divergence before the switch
- This supports the validity of our causal estimate

### 5. Variation Across Branches
- **66% of switched branches** saw service score improvements
- **34% of switched branches** saw declines (suggesting implementation matters)
- Effect is robust after controlling for staffing levels and visit volumes

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to remaining 284 branches

2. **Investigate underperforming branches** — the 34% of switched branches that saw declines may need additional training or support during implementation

3. **Monitor ongoing performance** — continue tracking service scores for at least 8-12 weeks post-rollout to confirm effects persist

4. **Document best practices** — identify characteristics of high-performing switched branches to inform implementation guidance

5. **Consider complementary improvements** — since the control group declined slightly (-0.62 points), investigate whether external factors (seasonal patterns, staffing changes) affected all branches

## **Technical Notes**

- Analysis uses difference-in-differences regression with branch fixed effects
- Standard errors account for repeated observations within branches
- Results are robust to inclusion of staffing and visit covariates
- Data covers weeks 6-22; switch occurred at week 15
