# **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**The data do not provide statistically reliable evidence that the new procedure improved service scores.** While branches that switched showed a modest average improvement of 1.6 points compared to those that did not, this effect is not statistically distinguishable from zero. We cannot confidently conclude the new procedure caused an improvement, and a system-wide rollout is not supported by the current evidence.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Data covers **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring in **week 15**
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in switched branches against changes in non-switched branches

### **Results**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect of new procedure | **+1.6 points** |
| 95% Confidence interval | **-0.4 to +3.5 points** |
| Statistical significance | **Not significant** (p = 0.12) |
| Effect size | **0.15 standard deviations** (small) |

### **What This Means**
- The confidence interval includes **zero**, meaning we cannot rule out that the new procedure had **no effect**
- The confidence interval also includes **negative values**, meaning we cannot rule out that the new procedure **reduced** service scores
- Among the 20 switched branches, only **11 (55%)** showed improvement; **9 (45%)** actually declined
- By comparison, only **5 of 14 (36%)** control branches improved—suggesting some positive trend, but not a clear treatment effect

### **Context**
- Service scores average around 50 points; a 1.6-point change represents about **3% of the average score**
- The switched branches had slightly higher scores than control branches even before the switch, though the trends were parallel (a good sign for the validity of the comparison)
- Staffing and visit patterns did not systematically differ between groups in ways that would explain the results

---

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended Actions**

1. **Do not proceed with system-wide rollout at this time.** The evidence is insufficient to justify the costs and disruption of changing procedures across all branches.

2. **Continue monitoring the pilot branches.** Collect additional weeks of data to increase statistical power. With more observations, we may be able to detect a smaller effect with greater confidence.

3. **Investigate why results varied across branches.** Nearly half of the switched branches saw no improvement or declined. Understanding what distinguished the 11 improving branches from the 9 declining ones could inform targeted implementation.

4. **Consider qualitative feedback.** Survey staff at switched branches about their experience with the new procedure. Operational challenges or training gaps may explain the mixed results.

5. **Re-evaluate in 8–12 weeks.** With an additional 8–12 weeks of data, the analysis would have roughly 50% more statistical power to detect an effect of this size.
