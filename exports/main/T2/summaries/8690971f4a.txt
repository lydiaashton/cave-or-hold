# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new shelving and rotation procedure significantly improved service scores.** Contrary to the initial expectation that the change would make no difference, branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2 points** (about 4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and the evidence supports rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. **Positive Treatment Effect**
- Branches using the new procedure improved their service scores by **1.98 points** more than control branches (95% confidence interval: 0.80 to 3.15 points)
- Statistical significance: **p = 0.001** (highly significant)
- This represents approximately a **4% improvement** relative to the average service score of 51

### 2. **Branch-Level Impact**
| Metric | Treatment Branches (New) | Control Branches (Old) |
|--------|-------------------------|----------------------|
| Average score change | +2.36 points | +0.43 points |
| % of branches improving | 70% | 60% |

### 3. **Effect Grows Over Time**
The improvement was not immediate but strengthened over the 8 weeks following implementation:
- Week 15: +2.6 points
- Week 18: +5.7 points  
- Week 22: +7.8 points

This pattern suggests staff may need time to fully adapt to the new procedure before realizing its full benefits.

### 4. **Analysis Method**
We used a **difference-in-differences** design comparing:
- **90 treatment branches** that switched to the new procedure in week 15
- **60 control branches** that continued with the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6-22), with 9 weeks before and 8 weeks after the switch

The analysis controlled for branch-specific characteristics, weekly variations, staffing levels, and visitor volume.

---

## **Next Steps**

### Recommended Actions:

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches

2. **Provide implementation support** during the transition period, as the data suggests benefits accumulate over several weeks

3. **Monitor service scores** for the next 2-3 months after rollout to confirm the effect persists at scale

4. **Document best practices** from the 90 early-adopter branches to help train staff at remaining locations

5. **Consider investigating** why the effect grows over time—this may inform training and support strategies

### Caveats:
- The analysis covers only 8 weeks post-implementation; longer-term effects remain to be observed
- Some pre-existing differences between groups were observed, though the rigorous statistical controls account for these
