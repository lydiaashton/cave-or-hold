# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure is associated with a **modest improvement in service scores** (+1.75 points, or ~3.7%), but the evidence is **not statistically conclusive**. While 90% of branches that adopted the new procedure showed improvement (versus only 50% of branches using the old procedure), the small sample size limits our ability to rule out chance as an explanation. The board may wish to consider a phased rollout with continued monitoring, or gather additional data before making a system-wide decision.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure at week 15 (treatment group)
- **14 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Service scores average ~50 points (higher is better)

### **Impact on Service Scores**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 50.83 | 53.25 | +2.41 |
| Non-switched branches | 49.88 | 50.55 | +0.67 |
| **Net effect (DiD)** | | | **+1.75** |

### **Statistical Confidence**
- The estimated effect of **+1.75 points** has a **p-value of 0.14**
- The 95% confidence interval ranges from **-0.58 to +4.07 points**
- This means we cannot rule out the possibility of no effect (or even a small negative effect) at conventional significance levels

### **Branch-Level Patterns**
- **18 of 20 switched branches (90%)** improved their scores after adoption
- **7 of 14 non-switched branches (50%)** improved during the same period
- This consistent directional pattern across branches is encouraging, even if statistical significance is marginal

### **Validity Checks**
- **Parallel trends assumption holds**: No significant difference in trends between groups before the switch (p = 0.51)
- **Results are robust**: Effect remains similar (+1.89 points) when controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Continue monitoring**: Extend data collection for 8–12 more weeks to increase statistical power and confirm whether the effect persists

2. **Consider phased rollout**: Given the positive direction of results and high branch-level improvement rate, expanding to additional branches while continuing evaluation could be reasonable

3. **Investigate heterogeneity**: Some branches showed large gains (up to +12 points) while others showed minimal change. Understanding which branch characteristics predict success could improve implementation

4. **Cost-benefit analysis**: Before full rollout, compare the modest service score gains against any additional operational costs of the new procedure

5. **Set decision criteria**: Define in advance what magnitude of improvement would justify system-wide adoption (e.g., "We need 95% confidence the effect exceeds 1.0 point")
