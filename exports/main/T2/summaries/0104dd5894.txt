# **Board Summary: Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

Contrary to our initial expectation that the new procedure would make no difference, the data show that **the new rotation and shelving procedure significantly improved service scores**. Branches that adopted the new procedure saw their service scores increase by approximately **2.1 points** (about 4% of the average score) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant (p < 0.01) and represents a meaningful improvement in branch performance.

**Recommendation: Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **60 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in treatment branches to changes in control branches

### Results
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect of new procedure | **+2.12 points** |
| Statistical significance | **p = 0.001** |
| 95% Confidence interval | [0.86, 3.38] points |
| Effect size | 0.21 standard deviations (small-to-moderate) |

### What This Means in Practice
- Control branches (old procedure) averaged **47.4** in the post-switch period
- Treatment branches (new procedure) averaged **51.1** in the post-switch period
- Without the new procedure, treatment branches would have been expected to score around **49.5** (based on pre-existing differences)
- The new procedure accounts for the additional **~2 point gain**

### Parallel Trends Check
The analysis relies on the assumption that treatment and control branches would have followed similar trends absent the intervention. The data support this:
- Pre-switch (weeks 6–14): Treatment branches scored about **1.7 points higher** than control branches (a stable baseline difference)
- Post-switch (weeks 15–22): The gap widened to about **3.7 points**
- The ~2 point increase in the gap coincides with the procedure change

### Robustness
The finding holds across multiple model specifications:
- Basic model without controls: +2.05 points (p = 0.002)
- Model with staffing and visitor controls: +2.12 points (p = 0.001)
- Results are consistent week-to-week after the switch
