# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

## **Title: Impact of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure on Service Scores**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new stock rotation and shelving procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by **2.1 points** (95% confidence interval: 0.9 to 3.4 points) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents approximately a **4% improvement** over baseline performance. The result is statistically significant (p = 0.001), meaning there is less than a 1% probability this finding occurred by chance.

Contrary to the expectation that the procedure change would make no difference, the evidence indicates the new procedure has a meaningful positive effect on service quality.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **60 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in service scores before and after the switch between the two groups

### **Results**

| Group | Before Switch (Weeks 6–14) | After Switch (Weeks 15–22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------------|---------------------------|--------|
| Control branches (old procedure) | 49.4 | 49.8 | +0.4 |
| Treatment branches (new procedure) | 48.3 | 50.8 | +2.6 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+2.1** |

### **Statistical Confidence**
- **Effect estimate:** +2.14 service score points
- **95% confidence interval:** 0.85 to 3.42 points
- **P-value:** 0.001 (highly statistically significant)

### **Validity Checks**
- **Parallel trends confirmed:** Before the switch, both groups showed similar week-to-week trends in service scores (p = 0.51), supporting the validity of the comparison
- **Robust to controls:** The effect persists when accounting for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume
- **Baseline difference:** Treatment branches started with slightly lower scores (~1 point), but the DiD method accounts for this

### **Practical Significance**
- The average branch scores about 50 on the service index
- A 2-point improvement represents meaningful progress in service quality
- No negative unintended effects detected in the data

## **Next Steps**

1. **Recommend proceeding with system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all branches, given the clear positive impact on service scores.

2. **Monitor implementation** during rollout to ensure branches achieve similar gains. Track service scores for the first 8–12 weeks after each branch transitions.

3. **Document the new procedure** in detail to capture what makes it effective. Consider interviewing staff at high-performing switched branches to identify best practices.

4. **Consider complementary investments.** Since staffing and visitor volume also correlate with service scores, branches struggling post-transition may benefit from targeted support.

5. **Continue evaluation.** After full rollout, maintain ongoing monitoring to confirm the effect persists at scale and to detect any long-term changes.
