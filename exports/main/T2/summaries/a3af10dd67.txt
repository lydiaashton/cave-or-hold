# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new shelving and stock rotation procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **1.85 points** (approximately **3.7%**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant and robust across multiple analytical approaches. We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Measurable Improvement in Service Scores**
- **Treatment effect:** +1.85 points on the service score index (95% confidence interval: 1.30 to 2.43)
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001 (highly significant)
- **Relative improvement:** Approximately 3.7% above baseline scores

### **2. Analysis Approach**
We used a **difference-in-differences** design comparing:
- **425 branches** that switched to the new procedure (treatment group)
- **284 branches** that kept the old procedure (control group)
- **17 weeks** of data (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15

This approach isolates the effect of the procedure change by accounting for:
- Pre-existing differences between branches
- Overall time trends affecting all branches
- Weekly variations in staffing and visitor volume

### **3. Before and After Comparison**

| Group | Pre-Switch Average | Post-Switch Average | Change |
|-------|-------------------|---------------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 49.33 | 49.44 | +0.11 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 50.61 | 52.58 | +1.96 |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | — | — | **+1.85** |

### **4. Robustness Checks**
The finding holds across multiple specifications:
- **Basic model:** +1.85 points (p < 0.001)
- **With staffing/visits controls:** +1.87 points (p < 0.001)
- **With branch fixed effects:** +1.87 points (p < 0.001)

The consistency across models confirms the result is not driven by confounding factors.

### **5. Context**
- The average branch service score is approximately 50
- A 1.85-point improvement represents meaningful progress toward service quality goals
- Control branches showed essentially no change (+0.11), confirming the improvement is attributable to the procedure change rather than system-wide trends
