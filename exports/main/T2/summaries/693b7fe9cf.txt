# **Memorandum to the Board**

## **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

### **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure should be rolled out to all branches.** The evidence shows the new procedure significantly improved service scores by approximately **2.1 points** (a 4% improvement), with high statistical confidence. Branches that kept the old procedure showed no meaningful change.

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. The New Procedure Improved Service Scores**
- Branches that adopted the new procedure saw their average service score increase by **2.1 points** after the switch
- Branches that kept the old procedure remained essentially unchanged (+0.1 points)
- The **net effect of the new procedure is +2.1 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.2 to 3.0 points)

**2. Results Are Statistically Robust**
- The improvement is highly statistically significant (p < 0.001)
- The finding holds even after accounting for:
  - Differences between individual branches (branch fixed effects)
  - Weekly variations in staffing levels
  - Weekly variations in visitor volume

**3. The Analysis Is Methodologically Sound**
- **150 branches** were studied over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure; **60 branches** served as a control group
- The switch occurred in **week 15**, providing 9 weeks of "before" data and 8 weeks of "after" data
- Pre-switch trends were parallel between groups (p = 0.21), validating the comparison

**4. Context for the Improvement**
- The average service score across all branches is approximately **50 points**
- A 2.1-point improvement represents a **4% gain** in service quality
- This is a meaningful improvement given the scale of the index

---

### **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all 150 branches

2. **Monitor implementation** to ensure branches adopting the procedure achieve similar gains

3. **Document best practices** from the 90 branches that successfully implemented the new procedure to support training

4. **Continue tracking service scores** post-rollout to confirm the effect persists at scale

5. **Consider investigating** whether the procedure's effectiveness varies by branch characteristics (size, location, staffing) to optimize future improvements

### **Analytical Approach**

This analysis used a **difference-in-differences** design, which compares the change in service scores for branches that switched procedures against the change for branches that did not. This approach isolates the effect of the new procedure from other factors that might affect all branches simultaneously (such as seasonal patterns or system-wide initiatives).
