# **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw their service scores increase by approximately **2.2 points** (about **4% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and consistent across all weeks after the switch. We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measurable Improvement in Service Scores
- Switched branches improved by **2.38 points** after adopting the new procedure (from 51.57 to 53.95)
- Control branches (old procedure) showed minimal change of **0.21 points** (from 50.64 to 50.84)
- **Net effect: +2.17 points** attributable to the new procedure

### 2. Statistically Significant Result
- The effect is highly significant (p < 0.001)
- 95% confidence interval: **1.46 to 2.87 points**
- We can be confident this is not due to random chance

### 3. Consistent Effect Over Time
The improvement was sustained across all 8 weeks after the switch:

| Week | Effect (points) |
|------|-----------------|
| 15   | 2.56            |
| 16   | 2.76            |
| 17   | 3.34            |
| 18   | 2.70            |
| 19   | 2.39            |
| 20   | 3.54            |
| 21   | 3.78            |
| 22   | 3.78            |

### 4. Effect Is Independent of Staffing and Visits
The improvement persists even after accounting for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume, suggesting the procedure itself—not external factors—drove the gain.

### 5. Valid Comparison Between Groups
Before the switch, switched and control branches had similar trends in service scores (average difference of 0.94 points). After the switch, this gap widened to 3.11 points, supporting the conclusion that the procedure caused the improvement.

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all 284 branches still using the old method.

2. **Monitor implementation** during rollout to ensure branches receive adequate training and support.

3. **Track service scores** for the next 3–6 months to confirm the effect persists at scale.

4. **Document the new procedure** as a best practice and incorporate it into branch operations manuals.

5. **Consider cost-benefit analysis** if there are any additional costs associated with the new procedure (the 4% service score improvement should be weighed against implementation costs).

---

*Analysis based on 12,053 weekly observations from 709 branches over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22). The switch occurred in week 15. Statistical method: difference-in-differences regression with robust standard errors.*
