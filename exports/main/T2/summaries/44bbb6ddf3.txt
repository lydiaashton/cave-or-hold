# **Memorandum to the Board**

## **Title: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.0 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant (p < 0.01) and represents a meaningful improvement on a scale where the average branch scores about 50. We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. **Study Design**
- **150 branches** total: 90 adopted the new procedure (treatment group), 60 kept the old procedure (control group)
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22): 9 weeks before the switch, 8 weeks after
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in the treatment group to changes in the control group

### 2. **Main Result**
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 48.55 | 50.30 | +1.75 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 49.46 | 53.24 | +3.78 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+2.03** |

- The new procedure added **2.03 points** to service scores beyond what would have happened anyway
- **95% confidence interval: 0.82 to 3.25 points**
- **Statistically significant at the 1% level** (p = 0.001)

### 3. **Validity Checks**
- **Parallel trends confirmed**: Before the switch, both groups followed similar trends (pre-trend test p = 0.19, not significant). This supports the validity of the comparison.
- **Effect persists with controls**: When accounting for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume, the effect remains significant at **2.18 points** (p = 0.001).
- **Consistent post-switch**: The treatment-control gap widened after week 15 and remained elevated throughout the post-period (ranging from 2.2 to 3.7 points).

### 4. **Practical Significance**
- A 2-point increase on a ~50-point scale represents approximately a **4% improvement** in service quality
- Given that service scores are calculated across all branches weekly, this improvement would affect patron experience system-wide

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all 60 branches currently using the old procedure

2. **Develop implementation timeline**: Consider phasing the rollout to manage training and operational disruption

3. **Monitor post-rollout performance**: Continue tracking service scores for 8–12 weeks after full implementation to confirm the effect persists at scale

4. **Document best practices**: Capture what switching branches did during their transition to create a rollout playbook for remaining branches

5. **Consider cost-benefit analysis**: If there are additional costs to the new procedure (training, labor, equipment), weigh them against the 4% service quality improvement
