## Bottom Line

The new procedure shows a **positive but statistically uncertain** effect on service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure improved by approximately **2.1 points** (about 4% of baseline) relative to branches that kept the old procedure. However, this result is not statistically significant at conventional levels (p=0.084), meaning we cannot rule out that the observed improvement occurred by chance. The evidence is suggestive but not conclusive.

## Key Findings

### Study Design
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **Treatment group:** Branches that switched to the new procedure (starting week 15)
- **Control group:** Branches that continued with the old procedure
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in service scores between the two groups before and after the switch

|-------|---------------------------|---------------------------|--------|

When controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume, the estimated effect is **+2.13 points** (95% confidence interval: -0.29 to +4.55).

### Statistical Interpretation
- **P-value: 0.084** — This means there is an 8.4% probability of observing this result (or larger) if the new procedure had no real effect
- The 95% confidence interval includes zero, so we cannot confidently conclude the effect is different from zero
- The result is **marginally significant** — stronger evidence than a coin flip, but below the conventional 5% threshold

### Additional Observations
1. **Parallel trends:** Before the switch, both groups showed similar week-to-week fluctuations in service scores, supporting the validity of the comparison
2. **No major confounding:** Staffing and visitor volume patterns do not explain the observed differences
3. **Direction is consistent with expectations:** All point estimates are positive, suggesting the new procedure does not harm service quality
