# Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores

## Bottom Line

The new shelving and rotation procedure is associated with a **2.4-point improvement** in service scores, but this effect is **not statistically significant** at conventional levels. While the direction of the effect is positive (as expected), the evidence is not strong enough to confidently conclude the new procedure causes improved service. The board should consider a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than an immediate systemwide implementation.

---

## Key Findings

### 1. Treatment Effect Estimate
- **Point estimate**: +2.36 points on the service score index
- **95% Confidence Interval**: -0.57 to +5.29 points
- **P-value**: 0.115 (not significant at 5% or 10% levels)
- **Effect size**: Approximately 5% improvement relative to baseline, or 0.23 standard deviations

### 2. What the Data Shows

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched Branches (New Procedure) | 50.36 | 52.81 | +2.45 |
| Control Branches (Old Procedure) | 46.52 | 46.61 | +0.09 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+2.36** |

### 3. Study Design Strengths
- **Parallel trends assumption holds**: Before the switch, both groups showed similar trends over time (no significant pre-existing divergence)
- **Control group available**: 14 branches maintained the old procedure, providing a valid comparison
- **Adequate pre-period data**: 9 weeks of baseline data (weeks 6-14)

### 4. Important Caveats
- **Statistical uncertainty**: The confidence interval includes zero and even slightly negative values, meaning we cannot rule out no effect
- **Limited post-switch data**: Only 8 weeks of data after the procedure change
- **Possible selection bias**: Switched branches started with higher service scores (~3.8 points higher on average), suggesting branches that adopted the new procedure may have been better-performing to begin with
- **Small effect relative to variation**: Service scores vary considerably week-to-week (SD ≈ 10 points), making it harder to detect modest improvements

---

## Next Steps

### Recommended Actions

1. **Continue the pilot for 8-12 more weeks**
   - More post-switch data would increase statistical power and narrow confidence intervals
   - Aim for at least 16 weeks of post-switch observations

2. **Expand monitoring to additional branches**
   - Randomly assign 5-10 more branches to the new procedure
   - Random assignment would eliminate selection bias concerns

3. **Track implementation fidelity**
   - Ensure branches are actually following the new procedure consistently
   - Document any variations in how the procedure is applied

4. **Consider cost-benefit analysis**
   - If the new procedure requires additional staff time or resources, weigh the ~2-point score improvement against those costs
   - A 5% improvement may or may not justify implementation costs depending on what those costs are

5. **Set a decision threshold**
   - Define in advance what effect size would justify systemwide rollout (e.g., "We need evidence of at least a 2-point improvement with 95% confidence")

### Decision Framework

| Scenario | Recommendation |
|----------|----------------|
| Effect remains ~2 points but becomes statistically significant | Proceed with rollout |
| Effect grows to 3+ points | Strong case for rollout |
| Effect shrinks toward zero | Do not roll out; reconsider procedure design |
| Effect becomes negative | Discontinue new procedure |

---

*Analysis based on 578 weekly observations from 34 branches over 17 weeks (weeks 6-22). Statistical analysis used difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors.*
