# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **1.8 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.3 to 2.4 points) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents a **3.7% improvement** over baseline performance. The result is statistically significant and robust to alternative analyses.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect
- **709 branches** were studied over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **425 branches** switched to the new procedure at week 15; **284 branches** continued with the old procedure
- The new procedure increased service scores by **+1.84 points** after accounting for:
  - Pre-existing differences between branches
  - Overall time trends affecting all branches
  - Weekly variations in staffing and visitor volume

### 2. Statistical Confidence
- The effect is **highly statistically significant** (p < 0.001)
- We are 95% confident the true effect lies between **1.3 and 2.4 points**
- Results hold when using cluster-robust standard errors (accounting for repeated measurements on the same branches)

### 3. Pre-Switch Validation
- Before week 12, treatment and control branches had **nearly identical** service scores (average difference: 0.05 points)
- This supports the validity of the comparison: the two groups were on parallel trajectories before the intervention

### 4. Magnitude of Impact
- Baseline average service score: **49.5 points**
- Post-switch improvement: **3.7%**
- Effect size (Cohen's d): **0.18** (small-to-moderate practical significance)

### 5. Robustness Check
- When excluding weeks 12–14 (when some divergence began before the official switch date), the estimated effect is even larger at **+2.35 points**, suggesting the main estimate may be conservative

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** — The evidence supports expanding the new procedure to all branches

2. **Monitor implementation** — Track service scores during rollout to ensure the effect translates across all branch types and sizes

3. **Investigate the weeks 12–14 pattern** — Service scores began diverging before the official switch date; understanding whether this reflects early adoption, anticipation effects, or other factors could improve future change management

4. **Consider cost-benefit analysis** — While service scores improved, evaluate whether the operational costs of the new procedure justify the 3.7% gain

5. **Set realistic expectations** — The improvement is meaningful but modest; communicate that the new procedure is one component of service quality, not a transformational change

*Analysis based on difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors, controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume.*
