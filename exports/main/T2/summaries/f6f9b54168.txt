# **Library Cooperative Branch Procedure Change: Impact Analysis**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new shelving and stock rotation procedure improved service scores.** Contrary to the expectation of no difference, branches that switched to the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **1.7 points** relative to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and has persisted throughout the 8-week post-implementation period. **We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure (treatment group)
- **60 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Switch occurred in **week 15**

### **Main Result**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Estimated Effect** | +1.73 service score points |
| **95% Confidence Interval** | [0.64, 2.81] |
| **Statistical Significance** | p = 0.002 |
| **Relative Improvement** | ~3.4% of baseline score |

### **What the Numbers Mean**
- Treatment group scores changed from 51.20 → 51.65 (+0.45)
- Control group scores changed from 54.16 → 52.88 (−1.28)
- The **difference-in-differences** of +1.73 points isolates the effect of the new procedure

### **Validity Checks**
✓ **Parallel trends confirmed**: Treatment and control groups followed similar trajectories before the switch (p = 0.69)  
✓ **Effect is sustained**: Positive impact observed in both early weeks 15–18 (+1.82 points) and later weeks 19–22 (+1.63 points)  
✓ **Robust to controls**: Effect remains significant after accounting for staffing and visit patterns

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all 60 branches currently using the old method.

2. **Monitor implementation** over the next 8–12 weeks to confirm the effect persists at scale and identify any branches that may need additional support during transition.

3. **Document best practices** from the 90 early-adopter branches to facilitate smoother adoption across the cooperative.

4. **Consider cost-benefit analysis**: While service scores improved, evaluate whether the new procedure requires additional resources (staff time, training, equipment) to ensure net positive value.

5. **Schedule follow-up review** in Q2 to assess long-term impacts and whether further refinements to the procedure are warranted.
