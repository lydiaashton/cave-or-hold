# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure shows a **positive but statistically uncertain** effect on service scores. Branches that switched improved by an estimated **2.4 points (approximately 5%)** relative to branches that kept the old procedure. However, this result is not statistically significant at conventional levels (p=0.10), meaning we cannot rule out that the observed improvement occurred by chance. The data provides **suggestive but not conclusive** evidence that the new procedure improves service.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Service Score Changes**

| Group | Before Switch (Weeks 6-14) | After Switch (Weeks 15-22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------------|---------------------------|--------|
| Old Procedure (14 branches) | 48.3 | 47.6 | -0.7 |
| New Procedure (20 branches) | 47.8 | 49.5 | +1.7 |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | | | **+2.4** |

### **Statistical Assessment**

- **Estimated effect:** +2.4 points on the service score index
- **Effect size:** ~5% improvement relative to the average score of ~50
- **Statistical significance:** p=0.10 (not significant at the 0.05 level)
- **95% Confidence interval:** -0.55 to +5.40 points
- **Interpretation:** The true effect could range from slightly negative to meaningfully positive

### **Data Quality Checks**

- **Parallel trends:** Before the switch, both groups showed similar week-to-week patterns, supporting the validity of the comparison
- **Sample:** 34 branches total (20 switched, 14 did not) over 17 weeks
- **Controls:** The result holds when accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended Actions**

1. **Continue the pilot for additional weeks** — The current 8-week post-switch period may be too short to detect a statistically significant effect. Extending observation to 16+ weeks would improve confidence in the results.

2. **Consider a phased rollout** — Given the positive signal but uncertain statistics, the board could authorize expanding the new procedure to additional branches while continuing to monitor results, rather than an immediate system-wide rollout.

3. **Gather qualitative feedback** — Supplement the quantitative analysis with staff surveys to understand operational impacts (e.g., time savings, ease of implementation) that may not be captured in service scores.

4. **Re-analyze after more data** — If the pilot continues, re-run this analysis quarterly. With more observations, the confidence interval will narrow, providing clearer guidance.

### **Decision Framework**

| If the board prioritizes... | Recommended action |
|----------------------------|-------------------|
| **Caution / Risk avoidance** | Wait for more data before expanding |
| **Innovation / Potential gain** | Proceed with phased expansion while monitoring |
