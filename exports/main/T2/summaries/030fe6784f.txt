**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure is associated with a **modest improvement in service scores** (+2.2 points, approximately 4% improvement). However, the evidence is **not statistically definitive**—the effect is marginally significant (p=0.094) and the confidence interval includes the possibility of no effect. We recommend a **cautious, phased rollout** with continued monitoring rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (Weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure (treatment group)
- **14 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Switch implemented at **Week 15**

### **Service Score Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| **Treatment (Switched)** | 51.72 | 52.86 | **+1.14** |
| **Control (No Switch)** | 53.35 | 52.40 | **-0.95** |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | — | — | **+2.09** |

### **Statistical Analysis**
Using a difference-in-differences regression (controlling for staffing and visitor traffic, with standard errors clustered by branch):

- **Estimated treatment effect:** +2.21 points
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.094 (marginally significant at 10% level)
- **95% Confidence Interval:** -0.38 to +4.79 points
- **Effect size:** ~4.3% improvement relative to baseline

### **Important Validity Checks**
✓ **Parallel trends assumption supported:** Pre-switch trends were statistically similar between treatment and control groups (p=0.68), validating our comparison approach.

✓ **Controls behave as expected:** Higher staffing and visitor traffic are significantly associated with higher service scores, confirming the model captures meaningful relationships.

### **Pattern Over Time**
The treatment effect appears **stronger in the early weeks** after implementation (weeks 15–18) and somewhat attenuates in later weeks, though this difference is not statistically significant. This could suggest:
- An initial boost that stabilizes over time, OR
- Natural variation in a relatively short study period

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended Actions**

1. **Proceed with Phased Rollout**
   - The positive direction of the effect, combined with no evidence of harm, supports expanding the procedure
   - Implement in additional branches over the next 1–2 quarters rather than all at once

2. **Extend Monitoring Period**
   - Current data covers only 8 weeks post-implementation
   - Collect at least 12–16 more weeks of data to confirm whether effects persist or grow

3. **Track Implementation Fidelity**
   - Ensure branches are following the new procedure consistently
   - Variation in implementation could be masking the true effect

4. **Consider Cost-Benefit Analysis**
   - Quantify the operational costs of the new procedure
   - Compare against the 4% service score improvement to determine ROI

5. **Investigate Heterogeneous Effects**
   - Some branches may benefit more than others
   - Analyze whether branch size, location, or baseline performance moderates the effect

### **Decision Framework**

| Scenario | Recommendation |
|----------|----------------|
| Effect confirmed with more data (p<0.05) | Full system-wide rollout |
| Effect remains marginal (p=0.05–0.15) | Continue phased rollout with monitoring |
| Effect disappears (p>0.15) | Revert to old procedure or redesign |

---

## **Limitations**

- **Short post-implementation window** (8 weeks) limits ability to detect long-term effects
- **Modest sample size** (34 branches) reduces statistical power
- **Unobserved factors** (e.g., seasonal effects, staff turnover) could influence results despite controls
