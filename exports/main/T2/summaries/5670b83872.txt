# Library Cooperative Stock Rotation Procedure: Impact Analysis

## Bottom Line

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure is associated with a **modest improvement in service scores** (approximately +2.3 points on the 50-point scale), but the evidence is **not statistically conclusive**. The data suggest a positive effect, but we cannot rule out the possibility that the observed improvement occurred by chance. The board should consider a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## Key Findings

### 1. Estimated Impact
- **Switched branches** improved by an average of **4.24 points** after adopting the new procedure
- **Control branches** (keeping the old procedure) improved by **1.96 points** over the same period
- **Net effect (Difference-in-Differences): +2.28 points** attributable to the new procedure

### 2. Statistical Significance
- The effect is **not statistically significant** at conventional confidence levels (p-value ≈ 0.09–0.11)
- The 95% confidence interval ranges from **-0.6 to +5.2 points**, meaning the true effect could be slightly negative, zero, or moderately positive
- With 34 branches (20 switched, 14 control) observed over 17 weeks, the study has **limited statistical power** to detect small effects

### 3. Pre-Implementation Trends (Validity Check)
- Before the switch (weeks 6–14), switched branches actually scored **1.4 points lower** than control branches on average
- After the switch (weeks 15–22), switched branches scored **0.9 points higher** than control branches
- This pattern supports the validity of the comparison: the switched branches were not already outperforming before the change

### 4. Context
- The average branch service score is approximately 50, so a 2.3-point improvement represents about a **4–5% gain**
- Staffing levels had a statistically significant positive association with service scores, but the procedure effect remains after accounting for this
