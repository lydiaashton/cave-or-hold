# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new procedure maintained their service quality while non-switched branches experienced a significant decline. The net benefit of the new procedure is **+2.3 points** on the service score index—a meaningful and statistically significant improvement. We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **709 branches** total: 425 switched to the new procedure, 284 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22): 9 weeks before the switch, 8 weeks after
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in switched branches against changes in non-switched branches

### Results

| Group | Pre-Switch Avg | Post-Switch Avg | Change |
|-------|---------------|-----------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 51.92 | 51.75 | -0.17 |
| Non-switched branches | 52.11 | 49.67 | -2.44 |

**Net effect of new procedure: +2.27 points** (95% confidence interval: +1.70 to +2.81)

### Statistical Significance
- **p < 0.001** — the result is highly statistically significant
- The effect is **robust** after controlling for staffing levels and branch visits
- The benefit is **consistent across all post-switch weeks** and appears to grow over time (from ~1.3 points in weeks 15–16 to ~2.7–3.0 points in weeks 21–22)

### What This Means
The new procedure did not necessarily "improve" scores in absolute terms—rather, it **protected branches from a decline** that affected those using the old procedure. This suggests the new procedure provides resilience against factors that were causing service quality to deteriorate system-wide during this period.

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new stock rotation and shelving procedure to all 284 branches still using the old method.

2. **Monitor implementation** over the next 8–12 weeks to ensure the effect persists at scale and identify any branches that may need additional support during the transition.

3. **Investigate the underlying cause** of the system-wide decline in service scores among non-switched branches. Understanding this could reveal additional opportunities for improvement.

4. **Consider cost-benefit analysis** if the new procedure has different resource requirements than the old one. The 2.3-point service score gain (approximately 4–5% improvement) should be weighed against any additional costs.
