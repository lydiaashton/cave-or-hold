# **Analysis of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Contrary to the expectation that the change would make no difference, branches that adopted the new procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **2.2 points (4.4%)** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This result is statistically significant and consistent across all post-switch weeks. **We recommend proceeding with a full rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measurable Improvement in Service Quality
- Treatment branches (new procedure) improved by **+2.2 points** relative to control branches after the switch
- 95% confidence interval: **0.9 to 3.6 points** — we can be highly confident the true effect is positive
- Statistical significance: **p < 0.01** (less than 1% chance this result occurred by random variation)

### 2. Parallel Trends Validated
Before the switch (weeks 6-14), treatment and control branches tracked closely together, with an average difference of only -0.19 points. This confirms the comparison is valid — the improvement after week 15 is attributable to the procedure change, not pre-existing differences.

### 3. Consistent Effect Across Time
The treatment effect was positive in **every single week** after the switch (weeks 15-22), ranging from +1.3 to +3.0 points. This consistency strengthens confidence in the result.

### 4. Effect Not Driven by Other Factors
The improvement persists even after accounting for weekly staffing levels and branch visit volumes, indicating the procedure change itself — not external factors — drove the improvement.

## **Technical Note**

This analysis used a **difference-in-differences** approach, the standard method for evaluating policy changes when random assignment is not feasible. The model controls for branch-specific characteristics and uses cluster-robust standard errors to account for repeated observations at each branch.
