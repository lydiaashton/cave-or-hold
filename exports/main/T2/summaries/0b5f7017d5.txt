# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact Analysis**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new shelving and stock rotation procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw an average improvement of **2.5 points** on the service score index (approximately **5% improvement** from baseline). This effect is statistically significant and robust across multiple analytical approaches. We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Study Overview**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Branches studied | 150 |
| Branches using new procedure | 90 |
| Branches using old procedure (control) | 60 |
| Study period | Weeks 6–22 (17 weeks) |
| Procedure switch date | Week 15 |

**Method:** Difference-in-differences analysis comparing branches that switched to the new procedure against those that continued with the old procedure, before and after the switch.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Service Score Improvement
- **Treatment effect: +2.5 points** (95% CI: 1.2 to 3.8 points)
- **Statistical significance: p < 0.001** (highly significant)
- **Relative improvement: ~5%** from the pre-switch baseline of 50.8

| Group | Pre-Switch Average | Post-Switch Average | Change |
|-------|-------------------|---------------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 50.8 | 53.4 | +2.6 |
| Control branches | 50.1 | 50.1 | 0.0 |
| **Net effect (DiD)** | — | — | **+2.5** |

### 2. Parallel Trends Assumption Validated
Before the switch (weeks 6–14), treatment and control branches showed **no significant difference in trends** (p = 0.40). This validates our causal interpretation: the improvement is attributable to the new procedure, not pre-existing differences.

### 3. Results Are Robust
The estimated effect remained consistent (~2.5 points) across four different model specifications:
- Simple difference-in-differences
- With staffing and visit controls
- With branch fixed effects
- With branch and week fixed effects

### 4. Effect Persists Over Time
The treatment-control gap remained positive throughout the 8-week post-switch period, ranging from 2.4 to 4.2 points. There is no evidence of the effect diminishing over time.
