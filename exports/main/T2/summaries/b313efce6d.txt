# **Analysis of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure shows a **positive but statistically uncertain effect** on service scores. Switched branches improved by approximately **2.1 points** (about 4% relative to average scores) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. However, this result is not statistically significant at conventional levels (p=0.09), meaning we cannot rule out that the observed improvement occurred by chance. The study lacks sufficient statistical power to detect effects smaller than about 9.5 points with confidence.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with caution. The evidence suggests a modest benefit, but is not strong enough to mandate system-wide rollout without additional data.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect Estimate
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated improvement | **+2.14 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | **-0.32 to +4.60 points** |
| p-value | **0.088** |
| Relative improvement | **~4.4%** |

The confidence interval includes zero, indicating uncertainty about whether the true effect is positive, negative, or null.

### 2. Branch-Level Results
- **12 of 20 switched branches** (60%) showed improvement after the change
- Average improvement for switched branches: **+1.46 points**
- Average change for control branches: **-0.58 points** (slight decline)
- Variation across branches was substantial (range: -4.75 to +10.18 points)

### 3. Study Design Strengths
- **Parallel trends assumption holds**: Before the switch, both groups showed similar weekly trends (slopes of -0.48 vs -0.45 points/week), supporting the validity of the comparison
- **Control variables included**: Staffing and visit levels were accounted for in the analysis
- **Clustered standard errors**: Properly adjusted for repeated observations within branches

### 4. Study Limitations
- **Limited statistical power**: With 34 branches over 17 weeks, the study can only reliably detect effects larger than ~9.5 points. The observed 2.1-point effect may be real but undetectable with current sample size
- **Short observation window**: Only 8 weeks of post-switch data limits precision
- **Baseline differences**: Switched branches started with higher average scores (49.6 vs 46.2), though this is adjusted for in the analysis

---

## **Next Steps**

### Recommended Actions

1. **Continue monitoring for 8-12 more weeks** to increase statistical power and precision of the effect estimate

2. **Expand the pilot** to 5-10 additional branches if operationally feasible, which would improve our ability to detect the true effect

3. **Investigate branch-level variation** to identify which types of branches benefit most (e.g., by size, location, or baseline performance)

4. **Consider cost-benefit analysis**: Even if the 2-point improvement is real, evaluate whether the operational costs of the new procedure justify the benefit

5. **Set a decision threshold**: Define in advance what effect size would justify system-wide rollout (e.g., "proceed if we can confirm at least a 1.5-point improvement with 95% confidence")

### Decision Framework

| Scenario | Recommendation |
|----------|----------------|
| Effect confirmed ≥2 points with p<0.05 | **Roll out system-wide** |
| Effect remains 1-3 points with p=0.05-0.15 | **Expand pilot, continue monitoring** |
| Effect shrinks or becomes negative | **Revert to old procedure** |
