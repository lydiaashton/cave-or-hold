## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect
- **Estimated impact:** +1.96 service score points (95% CI: 0.78 to 3.13)
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.01 (highly significant)
- **Practical significance:** The effect is meaningful—about 4% of the average baseline score

### 2. What Actually Happened
The data reveals an important pattern:
- **Control branches** (old procedure): Service scores **dropped 2.2 points** after week 15
- **Treatment branches** (new procedure): Service scores **dropped only 0.3 points** after week 15

The new procedure didn't necessarily boost scores—it **protected branches from a system-wide decline** that affected all branches using the old procedure.

### 3. Consistency of Results
- **All 8 post-switch weeks** showed positive treatment effects
- Weekly estimates ranged from +1.3 to +2.6 points
- Effect remained stable when controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume
- Results hold even when accounting for individual branch characteristics

### 4. Validity of Analysis
- **Parallel trends confirmed:** Treatment and control branches had statistically similar trends before the switch (p = 0.41), supporting the validity of the comparison
- **Pre-existing differences minimal:** Treatment branches scored only 0.7 points higher than control branches before the switch (not statistically significant)
