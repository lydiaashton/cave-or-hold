# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure is associated with a **modest improvement in service scores** (approximately +1.7 points or 3.3%), but the evidence is **not statistically conclusive**. The effect is positive and promising, but we cannot rule out the possibility that the improvement is due to random variation. I recommend a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** participated in the study over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- This "natural experiment" design allows us to isolate the effect of the new procedure from other factors affecting all branches

### **Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 50.89 | 50.92 | +0.03 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 52.01 | 53.79 | +1.78 |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | | | **+1.69** |

- **Estimated treatment effect: +1.69 service score points** (approximately 3.3% improvement)
- **Statistical confidence: Marginal** (p = 0.098)
  - There is approximately a 10% probability this result occurred by chance
  - The 95% confidence interval ranges from **-0.31 to +3.69 points**
  - This means we cannot definitively rule out no effect or even a small negative effect

### **Supporting Evidence**
- **Parallel trends validated**: Before the switch, treatment and control branches showed similar trends in service scores, supporting the validity of our comparison
- **Other factors matter**: Staffing levels and visit volumes significantly affect service scores (both p < 0.05), confirming our model appropriately controls for these variables
- **Consistent direction**: Post-switch, the treatment group consistently outperformed the control group in most weeks

---

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended Approach: Phased Rollout with Monitoring**

1. **Proceed with cautious expansion** to additional branches while maintaining a control group for ongoing evaluation

2. **Extend the observation period** to gather more post-implementation data (currently only 8 weeks after the switch)

3. **Monitor key metrics** monthly:
   - Service scores (primary outcome)
   - Staffing efficiency
   - Patron satisfaction surveys

4. **Set a decision checkpoint** at 6 months:
   - If the effect remains positive and becomes statistically significant (p < 0.05), proceed with full rollout
   - If the effect diminishes or becomes negative, pause and investigate implementation variations

5. **Document implementation fidelity** across branches to identify best practices and troubleshoot any branches where the procedure is not working as expected

---

## **Technical Notes**

- Analysis used difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch)
- Model controlled for staffing index and visits index
- Pre-treatment trends were examined and found to be reasonably parallel between groups
- Two visualizations have been generated: (1) service scores over time by group, and (2) event study showing weekly treatment effects
