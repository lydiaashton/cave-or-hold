# Library Cooperative Branch Procedure Change: Impact Analysis

## Bottom Line

**The new shelving and stock rotation procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.1 points** (95% confidence interval: 0.75 to 3.40 points) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents a **4.3% improvement** and is statistically significant (p = 0.002). We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

## Key Findings

### Study Design
- **150 branches** tracked over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15 (treatment group)
- **60 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in service scores between the two groups before and after the switch

### Results

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 48.55 | 51.72 | +3.17 |
| Control branches | 47.24 | 48.35 | +1.11 |
| **Net effect** | | | **+2.08** |

### Statistical Evidence
- **Treatment effect: +2.08 points** (cluster-robust standard errors)
- **95% Confidence Interval: 0.75 to 3.40 points**
- **P-value: 0.002** (highly significant)
- Effect remains significant after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volumes

### Parallel Trends Check
Before the switch (weeks 6–14), both groups showed similar week-to-week patterns in service scores, with switched branches consistently about 1–2 points higher. This parallel pre-trend supports the validity of the comparison. After week 15, the gap widened to 3–5 points, consistent with a positive treatment effect.

## Interpretation

The board expected the procedure change would make no difference to service scores. The data show the opposite: **the new procedure produced a meaningful and statistically significant improvement**. The effect is:

- **Reliable**: The confidence interval excludes zero, and the result is significant at the 1% level
- **Practically meaningful**: A 2-point increase on a ~50-point scale represents noticeable improvement
- **Robust**: The effect holds after accounting for staffing and visitor volume variations

## Next Steps

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new shelving and stock rotation procedure to all 60 branches currently using the old method

2. **Monitor implementation**: Track service scores at newly switched branches to confirm the effect replicates

3. **Document best practices**: Capture what the 90 early-adopter branches did during their transition to support smooth rollout

4. **Consider further optimization**: Since the new procedure improved scores, investigate whether additional refinements could yield further gains

5. **Report back in 8–12 weeks**: After full rollout, conduct a follow-up analysis to confirm sustained improvement across all branches
