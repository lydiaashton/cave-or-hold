# **Analysis of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw their service scores increase by approximately **1.9 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and represents a meaningful improvement given that the average branch scores around 50.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect
- **Estimated impact:** +1.9 points on the service score index
- **95% confidence interval:** 1.2 to 2.7 points
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001 (highly significant)

### 2. Study Design
- **709 branches** total: 425 adopted the new procedure, 284 kept the old
- **17 weeks** of data (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in treatment branches to changes in control branches

### 3. Before and After Comparison

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Treatment (new procedure) | 49.99 | 53.01 | +3.02 |
| Control (old procedure) | 49.09 | 50.19 | +1.10 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+1.92** |

### 4. Validity Checks
- **Parallel trends confirmed:** Before the switch (weeks 6–14), treatment and control branches followed similar trends, with a stable gap of about 0.9 points. This supports the validity of the comparison.
- **Effect persists over time:** The benefit appears to accumulate, with scores improving by about 0.24 points for each week under the new procedure.
- **Robust to controls:** The result holds when accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume.

### 5. Practical Significance
A 1.9-point increase on a ~50-point scale represents roughly a **4% improvement** in service scores. For context, this is larger than the typical week-to-week variation in scores.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.** The evidence strongly supports that the new rotation and shelving procedure improves service quality.

2. **Monitor implementation.** As remaining branches adopt the procedure, track their service scores to confirm the effect replicates at scale.

3. **Investigate mechanisms.** Consider studying *why* the new procedure works (e.g., faster book retrieval, better organization, reduced staff time on shelving) to optimize training and implementation.

4. **Cost-benefit analysis.** If the new procedure has additional costs (training, equipment, staff time), weigh them against the 4% service improvement to confirm the rollout is economically justified.

5. **Long-term follow-up.** Continue monitoring to ensure the effect persists beyond the initial 8-week post-switch window captured in this analysis.
