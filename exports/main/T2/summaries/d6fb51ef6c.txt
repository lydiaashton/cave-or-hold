## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **2.35 points** (approximately 5% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and consistent across most post-implementation weeks. We recommend proceeding with a cooperative-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measurable Improvement in Service Scores
- **Treatment effect:** +2.35 points on the service score index (95% CI: 0.72 to 3.99)
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.01 (highly significant)
- Switched branches improved by 3.77 points on average, while control branches improved by only 1.42 points—suggesting the new procedure accounts for most of the gain

### 2. Broad-Based Benefits
- **16 of 20 switched branches (80%)** showed score improvements after adoption
- Only **8 of 14 control branches (57%)** improved during the same period
- The effect was relatively consistent across the 8 weeks following implementation

### 3. Valid Comparison
- The analysis used a difference-in-differences approach, comparing switched branches to non-switched branches before and after week 15
- **Parallel trends confirmed:** Before the switch, both groups showed similar score trajectories (p = 0.42), supporting the validity of our comparison
- The model controlled for branch-specific characteristics, week-to-week variation, staffing levels, and visit volumes

### 4. Context
- 20 branches adopted the new procedure; 14 branches continued with the old procedure
- Data covers 17 weeks (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Average service score across all branches is approximately 50; the 2.35-point gain represents meaningful improvement

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with cooperative-wide rollout** of the new procedure to remaining branches
2. **Document the new procedure** in detail, capturing the specific practices that drove improvement
3. **Monitor implementation** at newly converted branches to ensure consistent results
4. **Consider a follow-up evaluation** in 3–6 months to assess whether gains are sustained
5. **Investigate branch-level variation**—4 switched branches did not show improvement; understanding why could help optimize the procedure further
