# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure is associated with a **2-point improvement** in service scores (approximately 4% relative to the baseline average of 50). However, this improvement is **not statistically significant** at conventional levels (p = 0.11), meaning we cannot confidently rule out that this result occurred by chance. The evidence suggests a positive effect, but it is not strong enough to support a definitive system-wide rollout decision based on this data alone.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** participated in the evaluation (20 adopted the new procedure, 14 continued with the old procedure)
- Data covers **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- This is a quasi-experimental "difference-in-differences" design comparing changes in the treatment group against changes in the control group

### **Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches (n=20) | 45.69 | 48.99 | +3.30 |
| Non-switched branches (n=14) | 47.31 | 48.54 | +1.23 |
| **Difference (New Procedure Effect)** | | | **+2.08** |

### **Statistical Assessment**
- **Point estimate**: +2.08 points
- **95% Confidence Interval**: -0.55 to +4.65 points
- **p-value**: 0.11 (not statistically significant at the 5% level)

The confidence interval indicates the true effect could reasonably range from a small negative effect (-0.55 points) to a moderate positive effect (+4.65 points).

### **Supporting Evidence**
- **Pre-trends were parallel**: Both groups showed similar score trajectories before the switch, supporting the validity of the comparison
- **Controls don't change the result**: Accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume does not materially alter the estimated effect
- **Consistent direction**: All analytical approaches point to a positive (though uncertain) effect

---

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended Approach: Cautious Expansion with Continued Monitoring**

1. **Do not mandate immediate system-wide rollout** — the evidence is suggestive but not conclusive

2. **Extend the evaluation period** — collect 8–12 additional weeks of data to increase statistical power and determine if the effect persists

3. **Consider a phased rollout** — offer the new procedure to additional interested branches while maintaining a control group for continued comparison

4. **Investigate heterogeneity** — some branches showed large improvements (e.g., Branch 0: +14.5 points) while others showed little or negative effects. Understanding which branch characteristics predict success could inform targeted implementation

5. **Set a decision threshold** — define in advance what magnitude of improvement would justify full rollout (e.g., "proceed if we observe 2+ points with p < 0.05 after 12 more weeks")

---

## **Conclusion**

The new procedure shows promise, with branches that adopted it improving their service scores by about 2 points more than branches that did not. However, given the statistical uncertainty, the board should treat this as **encouraging but preliminary evidence**. A modest investment in additional data collection would provide the clarity needed for a confident system-wide decision.
