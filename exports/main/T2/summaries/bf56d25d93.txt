# **Board Summary: Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure **improved service scores** by an average of **1.8 points** (approximately 3.6% increase). This effect is statistically significant and contrary to the expectation that the change would make no difference. We recommend proceeding with a full rollout, while continuing to monitor outcomes.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **150 branches** participated in the analysis (90 switched to the new procedure, 60 kept the old procedure)
- Data covers **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in service scores between switching and non-switching branches before and after the change
- Statistical models control for branch-specific characteristics and week-to-week variations

### **Results**

| Metric | Treatment Branches (New Procedure) | Control Branches (Old Procedure) |
|--------|-----------------------------------|----------------------------------|
| Average score BEFORE switch (weeks 6–14) | 50.03 | 48.77 |
| Average score AFTER switch (weeks 15–22) | 51.47 | 48.43 |
| **Change** | **+1.44** | **-0.34** |

**Estimated effect of new procedure: +1.77 points** (95% confidence interval: 0.54 to 3.00; p = 0.005)

### **Validation of Analysis**
- **Parallel trends confirmed**: Before the switch, treatment and control branches followed similar trends (statistical test p = 0.24), supporting the validity of the comparison
- **Post-switch effect confirmed**: The improvement in treatment branches relative to control branches is statistically significant (p = 0.01)
- **Robust to controls**: Results hold when accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with full rollout**: The evidence supports expanding the new procedure to all branches. The improvement is both statistically significant and practically meaningful.

2. **Monitor implementation**: Track service scores at branches as they transition to ensure the effect is sustained during and after rollout.

3. **Investigate mechanisms**: Consider studying *why* the new procedure improved scores (e.g., staff efficiency, patron satisfaction, material accessibility) to optimize the procedure further.

4. **Long-term follow-up**: Continue collecting data to assess whether the effect persists, grows, or diminishes over time.

5. **Cost-benefit analysis**: If the new procedure involves additional costs, compare those against the value of a 3.6% service score improvement to confirm net benefit.
