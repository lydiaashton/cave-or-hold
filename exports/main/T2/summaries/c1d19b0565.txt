# **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **2.4 points** (approximately 5%) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and consistent across the treatment group, with **89% of switched branches** showing improvement versus only 68% of control branches.

**Recommendation: Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Study Design
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **60 branches** kept the old procedure
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in service scores before and after the switch between the two groups

### 2. Service Score Impact

| Group | Pre-Switch Average | Post-Switch Average | Change |
|-------|-------------------|---------------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 46.6 | 50.8 | **+4.2** |
| Control branches | 47.6 | 49.4 | +1.8 |
| **Net effect (DiD)** | | | **+2.4** |

- The **2.4-point improvement** is statistically significant (p < 0.001)
- 95% confidence interval: **1.1 to 3.7 points**
- This represents approximately a **5% improvement** relative to baseline scores

### 3. Supporting Evidence
- **Parallel trends validated**: Before the switch, there was no statistically significant difference in trends between the two groups (p = 0.50), supporting the validity of the comparison
- **Consistent effect**: The improvement persisted across all 8 post-switch weeks
- **Widespread benefit**: 89% of switched branches improved, compared to 68% of control branches
- **Robust to controls**: The effect holds even after accounting for weekly staffing and visit volume variations

### 4. What This Means
The original expectation was that the new procedure would make no difference to service scores. The data clearly contradict this—**the new procedure produces a meaningful, measurable improvement** in service quality as captured by the service score index.
