# **Analysis of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure shows a **positive but statistically inconclusive effect** on service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure improved by approximately **1.7 points** relative to branches that kept the old procedure, but this difference is not statistically significant at conventional levels (p=0.14). The 95% confidence interval ranges from -0.5 to +3.9 points, meaning we cannot rule out either no effect or a modest positive effect.

**Recommendation**: Proceed with caution. The evidence suggests the new procedure may help, but we lack sufficient statistical certainty to justify a system-wide rollout based on this analysis alone. Consider a phased expansion with continued monitoring.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Study Design**
- **34 branches** total: 20 adopted the new procedure (treatment), 14 kept the old procedure (control)
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6-22), with the switch implemented in week 15
- **9 weeks before** and **8 weeks after** the change
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in treatment branches to changes in control branches

### **2. Service Score Changes**

| Group | Before Switch (Weeks 6-14) | After Switch (Weeks 15-22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------------|---------------------------|--------|
| Control (no switch) | 52.31 | 49.93 | **-2.38** |
| Treatment (switched) | 50.48 | 49.91 | **-0.57** |

The treatment group's scores remained relatively stable after the switch, while the control group experienced a notable decline. The **difference-in-differences estimate of +1.69 points** reflects that treatment branches performed 1.69 points better than they would have if they had followed the control group's trajectory.

### **3. Statistical Significance**
- **Coefficient**: +1.69 points
- **Standard Error**: 1.13
- **P-value**: 0.144 (not significant at the 0.05 level)
- **95% Confidence Interval**: [-0.52, +3.91]

The confidence interval includes zero, indicating we cannot confidently distinguish the effect from no effect. However, the interval also excludes large negative effects, suggesting the new procedure is unlikely to be harmful.

### **4. Parallel Trends Validation**
A key assumption of this analysis is that treatment and control branches would have followed similar trends absent the intervention. Pre-switch data (weeks 6-14) shows:
- No significant differential trend between groups before the switch (p=0.94)
- This supports the validity of the difference-in-differences approach

### **5. Effect Size Context**
- Baseline average service score: **50.6 points**
- Estimated effect represents approximately **3.3%** of baseline
- With the current sample size, the minimum detectable effect (80% power) is approximately **3.2 points**
- The observed effect (1.69 points) is below this threshold, explaining the lack of statistical significance

### **6. Control Variables**
Staffing levels significantly affect service scores (p=0.01), but including this control does not materially change the estimated treatment effect. Visitor volume shows no significant relationship with service scores.

## **Next Steps**

### **Immediate Actions**
1. **Do not mandate system-wide rollout** based on current evidence—the effect is positive but not statistically confirmed.

2. **Continue monitoring** the 20 branches already using the new procedure. Collect additional weeks of data to improve statistical power.

3. **Consider expanding to 5-10 additional branches** on a voluntary basis to increase sample size while maintaining a control group for ongoing evaluation.

### **Further Analysis Recommended**
1. **Extend the observation period**: Adding 8-10 more weeks of data would substantially improve the ability to detect modest effects.

2. **Examine heterogeneity**: Investigate whether the procedure works better for certain branch types (e.g., by size, location, or baseline performance).

3. **Cost-benefit analysis**: Even if the effect is real at ~1.7 points, assess whether the operational costs of the new procedure justify the improvement.

4. **Qualitative feedback**: Gather input from branch staff on implementation challenges and perceived benefits.

### **Decision Framework**
| Scenario | Recommended Action |
|----------|-------------------|
| Effect confirmed positive (p<0.05) with additional data | Proceed with phased system-wide rollout |
| Effect remains inconclusive but positive | Continue voluntary adoption with monitoring |
| Effect turns negative with more data | Revert treatment branches to old procedure |
