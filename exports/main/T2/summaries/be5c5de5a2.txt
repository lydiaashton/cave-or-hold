# **Analysis of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Contrary to the expectation that it would make no difference, branches that switched to the new procedure maintained their service scores while control branches experienced a notable decline. The estimated effect is an increase of approximately **+1.9 points** on the service score index (p < 0.01), which is statistically significant and meaningful given that the average branch scores around 50.

**Recommendation: Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect
Using a difference-in-differences analysis (comparing branches that switched vs. those that didn't, before and after week 15):

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (no switch) | 49.14 | 46.92 | **-2.22** |
| Treatment (switched) | 49.86 | 49.56 | **-0.31** |

**Difference-in-Differences Estimate: +1.92 points** (95% CI: 0.7 to 3.1)

This means the new procedure helped branches avoid a ~2-point decline that affected non-switching branches, resulting in a net positive effect.

### 2. Statistical Significance
- The effect is statistically significant (p < 0.01)
- Results are robust across multiple model specifications (with and without branch fixed effects, with and without control variables)
- The finding holds even when accounting for clustering at the branch level

### 3. Validity Check: Parallel Trends
Before the switch (weeks 6-14), treatment and control branches followed similar trends in service scores. A formal test confirms no significant difference in pre-treatment trends (p = 0.50), supporting the validity of the analysis.

### 4. Data Overview
- **150 branches** total (90 switched, 60 did not)
- **17 weeks** of data (weeks 6-22)
- **2,550 observations** (one per branch per week)

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all 150 branches.

2. **Monitor implementation** over the next quarter to ensure the positive effect persists as more branches adopt the procedure.

3. **Investigate the control group decline**: Service scores dropped in branches that did not switch. Understanding why (seasonal effects, external factors, etc.) could inform future improvements.

4. **Consider cost-benefit analysis**: If the new procedure has implementation costs, weigh them against the ~4% improvement in service scores (1.9 points on a ~50-point scale).
