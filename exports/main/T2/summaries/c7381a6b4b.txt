# **Board Summary: New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure is associated with a **2.1-point improvement in service scores** (approximately 4% above baseline). While this positive effect is not statistically significant at conventional thresholds (p=0.11), the evidence points in the expected direction: 75% of branches that switched showed improvement, compared to only 50% of branches that kept the old procedure. We recommend **proceeding with a phased rollout** while continuing to monitor outcomes.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **14 branches** kept the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in the switching branches against changes in the non-switching branches

### **Results**

| Metric | Treatment Branches (Switched) | Control Branches (Not Switched) |
|--------|------------------------------|--------------------------------|
| Pre-switch average score | 53.3 | 46.2 |
| Post-switch average score | 55.3 | 46.3 |
| **Change** | **+2.0** | **+0.1** |

**Estimated causal effect of new procedure: +2.1 points** (95% confidence interval: -0.5 to +4.8)

### **Supporting Evidence**
- **75% of switching branches** (15 of 20) improved after the change, versus **50% of control branches** (7 of 14)
- The parallel trends assumption holds: treatment and control groups showed similar patterns before the switch
- The effect is robust to controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### **Limitations**
- The effect does not reach conventional statistical significance (p=0.11), meaning we cannot rule out that the observed improvement is due to chance
- The confidence interval includes zero, so the true effect could be smaller or even negative
- Only 8 weeks of post-switch data are available; longer-term effects remain unknown

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with cautious expansion**: The positive point estimate and the fact that most switching branches improved support rolling out the procedure to additional branches.

2. **Continue monitoring**: Track service scores for at least another 8–12 weeks to:
   - Confirm the effect persists over time
   - Narrow the confidence interval with more data
   - Identify any branches where the procedure underperforms

3. **Investigate heterogeneity**: Some branches saw gains of 5–9 points while others saw declines. Understanding which branch characteristics predict success could improve implementation.

4. **Consider cost-benefit**: If the new procedure requires additional resources, weigh the ~2-point service score gain against those costs before full rollout.
