# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.5 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and represents about a **5% improvement** relative to the average service score. The evidence supports rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. **Measurable Improvement in Service Scores**
- Switched branches improved by **2.48 points** on the service score index after adopting the new procedure (95% confidence interval: 1.2 to 3.8 points)
- This result holds even after accounting for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume
- The p-value is less than 0.001, indicating the result is highly unlikely to be due to chance

### 2. **Valid Comparison Between Groups**
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure; **60 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Before the switch (weeks 6–14), both groups showed similar trends in service scores, confirming the comparison is valid
- Control branches actually saw a slight *decline* in scores after week 15 (−0.7 points), while switched branches *improved* (+1.8 points)

### 3. **Context for the Effect Size**
- The average service score across all branches is approximately **51 points**
- A 2.5-point gain represents meaningful improvement in service quality
- For perspective: this is roughly equivalent to moving a branch from the 50th percentile to the 60th percentile of performance

### 4. **Supporting Factors**
- Staffing levels and visitor volume both positively affect service scores, but the procedure effect remains significant after controlling for these factors
- The improvement appeared consistently across the 8 weeks following the switch

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all remaining branches

2. **Develop implementation timeline** — consider phasing the rollout to manage training and transition logistics

3. **Monitor outcomes** — continue tracking service scores for 8–12 weeks after each branch switches to confirm the effect holds across all locations

4. **Document best practices** — capture what switched branches did during implementation to create a rollout playbook

5. **Consider cost-benefit analysis** — if the new procedure has additional costs (training, equipment, labor hours), weigh them against the 5% service improvement

## **Technical Note**

This analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, which compares the change in service scores for switched branches against the change for non-switched branches over the same time period. This method controls for any overall trends (e.g., seasonal effects) that might affect all branches simultaneously. The parallel trends assumption is satisfied — both groups moved together before the switch, supporting the validity of the comparison.
