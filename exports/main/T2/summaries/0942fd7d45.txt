## **Summary for the Board: New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

### **Bottom Line**
**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.1 points** (about 4%) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and consistent across multiple analytical approaches. We recommend proceeding with a full rollout.

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. Study Design**
- **150 branches total**: 90 switched to the new procedure, 60 kept the old procedure
- **Data period**: 17 weeks (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- **Analysis method**: Difference-in-differences comparison, controlling for branch-specific characteristics and common time trends

**2. Service Score Impact**
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 50.8 | 53.7 | +2.9 |
| Control branches | 47.6 | 48.4 | +0.9 |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | | **+2.1** |

- The 2.1-point improvement is **statistically significant** (p = 0.001)
- This represents a **4% increase** relative to the average service score of 50.5

**3. Consistency Across Branches**
- **77% of switched branches** saw their scores improve after the change
- Only **60% of control branches** improved during the same period
- The effect held up when controlling for branch-specific factors using fixed-effects regression

**4. Validation of Results**
- Pre-switch trends were parallel between the two groups, supporting the validity of the comparison
- The improvement does not appear to be driven by changes in staffing levels or visit volumes, suggesting the procedure itself is the cause
