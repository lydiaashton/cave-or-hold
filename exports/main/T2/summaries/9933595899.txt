# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure **improved service scores by approximately 2 points** (on a scale where the average branch scores about 50). This improvement is **statistically significant** and was not expected based on prior assumptions. We recommend proceeding with a full rollout, while monitoring implementation quality.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **60 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in service scores before and after the switch between the two groups

### **Results**

| Group | Before Switch (Weeks 6–14) | After Switch (Weeks 15–22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------------|---------------------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 49.89 | 51.45 | **+1.56** |
| Control branches | 50.83 | 50.34 | **–0.49** |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+2.05** |

- **Estimated effect:** +2.05 points (95% confidence interval: +0.78 to +3.31)
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.002 (highly significant)
- **Effect size:** Approximately 0.2 standard deviations—a modest but meaningful improvement

### **Supporting Evidence**
- **Parallel trends check:** Before the switch, both groups showed similar week-to-week patterns, with switched branches consistently scoring about 1 point lower. This gap remained stable before week 15, supporting the validity of the comparison.
- **Alternative explanations ruled out:** The improvement is not explained by changes in staffing levels or visit volumes, which did not differ systematically between the two groups after the switch.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with full rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches, given the clear positive impact on service scores.

2. **Document the new procedure** in detail to ensure consistent implementation across all branches.

3. **Monitor service scores** during rollout to confirm the effect holds at scale and identify any branches that may need additional support.

4. **Investigate the mechanism:** Since a positive effect was not expected, consider qualitative follow-up (e.g., branch manager interviews) to understand *why* the new procedure improved scores. This could reveal additional opportunities for improvement.

5. **Continue tracking** staffing and visit metrics to ensure the procedure change does not have unintended consequences on operational efficiency.

---

*Analysis based on weekly service score data from 150 branches, weeks 6–22. Standard errors clustered at the branch level to account for within-branch correlation.*
