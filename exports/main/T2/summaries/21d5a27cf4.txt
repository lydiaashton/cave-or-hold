# **Board Memo: Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Recommend proceeding with system-wide rollout.** The new procedure produced a statistically significant improvement in service scores. Branches that switched showed a **2.17-point increase** in service scores compared to branches that kept the old procedure—a **4.3% improvement** relative to the baseline. This effect is robust, highly significant (p < 0.001), and consistent across the post-switch period.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Study Design
- **709 branches** analyzed over 17 weeks (Weeks 6–22)
- **425 branches** adopted the new procedure; **284 branches** kept the old procedure as a control group
- Switch implemented in **Week 15**, providing 9 weeks of pre-switch and 8 weeks of post-switch data
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in switched branches against changes in non-switched branches

### 2. Main Results

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched Branches | 49.72 | 52.75 | +3.02 |
| Non-Switched Branches | 49.13 | 50.00 | +0.87 |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | | | **+2.17** |

- The 2.17-point improvement is the estimated causal effect of the new procedure
- **95% Confidence Interval:** 1.62 to 2.72 points
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001 (extremely unlikely to be due to chance)

### 3. Robustness Checks
- Results hold after controlling for **staffing levels** and **visitor volume**, confirming the effect is not driven by these factors
- Effect persists when accounting for **branch-specific characteristics** (fixed effects)
- Weekly trend analysis shows the improvement emerged after the switch and remained positive throughout the post-switch period

### 4. Practical Significance
- Service scores average around 50; a 2+ point gain represents meaningful improvement
- Even at the conservative lower bound of the confidence interval (1.62 points), the improvement exceeds **3% above baseline**
- Applied across 425 switched branches over 8 weeks, this represents substantial cumulative service improvement

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all remaining 284 branches

2. **Develop implementation timeline** with branch managers, including:
   - Staff training on new procedures
   - Phased rollout to manage operational capacity
   - Clear communication to patrons about any temporary disruptions

3. **Continue monitoring** service scores post-rollout to:
   - Confirm effects persist at scale
   - Identify any branches needing additional support
   - Track whether improvements compound over time

4. **Document best practices** from the 425 early-adopter branches to support smooth implementation elsewhere

5. **Consider complementary investments** in staffing, as the analysis shows staffing levels also positively affect service scores (independent of the procedure change)

---

## **Technical Notes**

- Analysis uses cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch) to account for within-branch correlation
- Pre-switch trends show typical week-to-week variation; the post-switch divergence is clear and sustained
- Both staffing and visitor traffic positively correlate with service scores, but the procedure effect holds after controlling for these factors
