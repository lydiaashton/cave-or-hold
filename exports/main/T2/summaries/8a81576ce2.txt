# **Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by **2.1 points** more than branches that kept the old procedure. This represents a **4% improvement** in service quality. The evidence is strong and statistically significant. **We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **709 branches** tracked over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- **425 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15 (treatment group)
- **284 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- This natural experiment allows us to isolate the effect of the new procedure

### **Main Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| New Procedure (425 branches) | 50.7 | 54.0 | +3.3 |
| Old Procedure (284 branches) | 50.7 | 51.9 | +1.2 |
| **Net Effect (Difference-in-Differences)** | | | **+2.1** |

- The **2.1-point improvement** is statistically significant (p < 0.001)
- 95% confidence interval: **1.6 to 2.7 points**
- Effect represents approximately **4% improvement** in service quality

### **Supporting Evidence**

1. **Parallel Trends Validated**: Before the switch, both groups had nearly identical service scores (50.7) and showed no systematically different trends. This confirms the comparison is valid.

2. **Effect Grows Over Time**: The improvement wasn't immediate—it started at about 1 point in the first week after switching and grew to over 3 points by week 7. This suggests staff may need time to fully adapt to the new procedure, and benefits may continue to accrue.

3. **Robust to Controls**: The result holds even after accounting for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume, confirming the effect is attributable to the procedure change itself.

4. **Consistent Positive Impact**: Every single week after the switch showed a positive effect, with all post-switch effects statistically distinguishable from zero.

---

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended Actions**

1. **Proceed with System-Wide Rollout**: The evidence strongly supports expanding the new procedure to all 709 branches.

2. **Plan for Implementation Timeline**: Since effects grow over time, allow branches 4–6 weeks to fully realize benefits. Consider phased rollout to manage training resources.

3. **Develop Training Materials**: The growing effect suggests a learning curve. Invest in clear training documentation and possibly peer mentoring from early-adopter branches.

4. **Monitor Continued Performance**: Track service scores for the next 3–6 months to confirm effects persist and identify any branches needing additional support.

5. **Document Best Practices**: The 425 early-adopter branches can share implementation tips with remaining branches to accelerate adoption.

### **Caveats to Note**

- The analysis covers only 8 weeks post-switch; longer-term effects remain to be observed
- Service scores are one metric; consider also tracking operational costs, staff satisfaction, and patron feedback during rollout
