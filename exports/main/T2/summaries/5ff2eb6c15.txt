# **Analysis of New Shelving Procedure Impact on Library Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new shelving and rotation procedure saw their service scores increase by approximately **2.2 to 2.9 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and represents a meaningful improvement given that the average branch scores around 50 points (roughly a **4-6% improvement**).

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Study Design
- **150 branches** total: 90 adopted the new procedure, 60 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks** of data (weeks 6-22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in treated branches to changes in control branches

### 2. Main Result
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| New Procedure Branches | 49.06 | 52.24 | +3.18 |
| Old Procedure Branches | 49.05 | 49.95 | +0.90 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+2.28** |

The new procedure caused a **2.28-point increase** in service scores (p < 0.001). This result holds even after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume.

### 3. Parallel Trends Assumption Validated
A critical requirement for this analysis is that treated and control branches were on similar trajectories before the switch. The data confirms this:
- In the 9 weeks before the switch, the difference between groups averaged only **0.01 points**
- A formal statistical test confirms no significant pre-existing trend differences (p = 0.40)

### 4. Effect Persists Over Time
The event study analysis shows the positive effect:
- Appears immediately in the first week of implementation (+2.6 points)
- Remains positive throughout the 8-week post-switch period
- Averages **+2.9 points** across all post-switch weeks

## **Next Steps**

1. **Recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.** The evidence strongly supports that the new procedure improves service quality.

2. **Monitor implementation quality.** Since effects varied somewhat week-to-week (ranging from ~2 to ~4 points), ensure consistent training and adherence to the new procedure across all branches.

3. **Track long-term effects.** The analysis covers only 8 weeks post-implementation. Continue monitoring to confirm effects persist and don't diminish over time.

4. **Investigate mechanisms.** Consider examining whether the improvement stems from faster book retrieval, better organization, or other factors to further refine the procedure.

5. **Cost-benefit analysis.** If the new procedure has implementation costs, weigh them against the ~2.3-point service score improvement to confirm net benefit.
