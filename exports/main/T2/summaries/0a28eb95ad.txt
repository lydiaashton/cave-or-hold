# **Board Summary: Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure had a statistically significant positive effect on service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure maintained their service scores (averaging 50.9 before and 50.9 after), while branches using the old procedure experienced a notable decline (from 50.7 to 48.6). The new procedure provided a **2.0-point advantage** in service scores (p < 0.001), effectively protecting branches from a system-wide decline that affected control branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **709 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **425 branches** adopted the new procedure; **284 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Switch occurred at **week 15**; analysis compares before (weeks 6–14) vs. after (weeks 15–22)

### Results

| Metric | Treatment (New Procedure) | Control (Old Procedure) |
|--------|---------------------------|-------------------------|
| Average score BEFORE switch | 50.92 | 50.66 |
| Average score AFTER switch | 50.90 | 48.62 |
| **Change** | **-0.02** | **-2.04** |

### Statistical Evidence
- **Difference-in-Differences estimate: +2.02 points** (95% CI: 1.47 to 2.56)
- **p-value < 0.001** — highly statistically significant
- Effect remains significant after controlling for staffing levels and visit volumes

### Important Context
There was a system-wide downward pressure on service scores after week 14 that affected all branches. The new procedure did not necessarily *improve* scores in absolute terms, but rather **protected branches from decline**. Treatment branches stayed flat while control branches dropped by 2 points.

### Caveat
Branches selected for the new procedure showed slightly stronger upward trends before the switch, suggesting they may have been on a more positive trajectory already. However, the post-switch divergence is clear and substantial.

## **Next Steps**

1. **Recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.** The evidence strongly supports that it helps maintain service quality during periods of operational stress.

2. **Investigate why control branches declined.** Understanding the source of the system-wide pressure (seasonal? staffing? other factors?) could inform additional improvements.

3. **Monitor implementation closely.** As the procedure expands, track whether the protective effect holds across different branch types and sizes.

4. **Consider a phased rollout** if resources are constrained, prioritizing branches most vulnerable to service score declines.
