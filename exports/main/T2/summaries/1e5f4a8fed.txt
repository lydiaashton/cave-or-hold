# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, roll out the new procedure.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores improve by **2.1 points** (approximately **4%**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant and has persisted across all eight weeks since implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches (425) | 50.7 | 54.0 | +3.3 |
| Non-switched branches (284) | 50.7 | 51.9 | +1.2 |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | | | **+2.1** |

The new procedure is associated with a **2.1-point increase** in service scores (95% confidence interval: 1.6 to 2.7 points).

### 2. Statistical Significance
- The effect is **highly statistically significant** (p < 0.001)
- Results hold when accounting for branch-level variation and other weekly factors (staffing levels, visit volumes)

### 3. Trends Over Time
- **Pre-switch**: Service score trends were similar between groups in the weeks immediately before the switch (weeks 12-14), supporting the validity of the comparison
- **Post-switch**: The improvement appeared immediately in week 15 and has been sustained through week 22, with some evidence the effect may be growing over time

### 4. Practical Significance
- Average service score across all branches: **51.9**
- The 2.1-point gain represents a **4% improvement** relative to baseline
- Given the scale (709 branches), this improvement would affect service quality system-wide
