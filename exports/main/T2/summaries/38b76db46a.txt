# **Analysis of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **1.9 points** (about **4% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and sustained over the 8-week post-implementation period.

---

## **Key Findings**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Effect Size** | +1.91 service score points |
| **95% Confidence Interval** | [1.38, 2.45] |
| **Statistical Significance** | p < 0.001 |
| **Baseline Score** | ~50 points |
| **Relative Improvement** | ~3.8% |

### **Study Design**
- **709 branches** total: 425 adopted the new procedure, 284 kept the old one
- **17 weeks** of data (weeks 6–22), with the switch implemented in week 15
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach with branch fixed effects, controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### **What the Data Shows**

1. **Before the switch (weeks 6–14):** Service score differences between the two groups fluctuated around zero with no systematic pattern, supporting the validity of the comparison.

2. **After the switch (weeks 15–22):** The switched branches pulled ahead by 1.3–3.2 points, with the gap widening over time. The improvement was immediate and sustained.

3. **Robustness:** The result holds after controlling for:
   - Branch-specific characteristics (fixed effects)
   - Weekly staffing variations
   - Weekly visitor volume changes

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout.** The evidence strongly supports expanding the new procedure to all branches.

2. **Monitor implementation.** Track service scores during rollout to ensure the effect replicates at scale.

3. **Document the procedure.** Capture the specific practices that drove the improvement for training purposes.

4. **Consider a phased rollout.** Since the effect appears to grow over time (from ~1.4 points in week 15 to ~3.2 points by week 22), branches may benefit from a ramp-up period with support.

5. **Cost-benefit analysis.** If the new procedure requires additional resources, weigh the 4% service improvement against implementation costs.

---

*Analysis based on weekly service scores from weeks 6–22. Statistical model: Panel OLS with branch fixed effects and clustered standard errors.*
