# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new shelving and rotation procedure is associated with a statistically significant improvement in service scores.** Branches that switched to the new procedure saw their service scores improve by approximately **2 points more** than branches that kept the old procedure. This represents roughly a **4% improvement** from the baseline average score of 50. The effect appears to strengthen over time, with larger gains visible 5-8 weeks after implementation.

**Recommendation:** The evidence supports rolling out the new procedure to all branches, though we recommend monitoring implementation quality and allowing 4-5 weeks for full benefits to materialize.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **709 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6-22)
- **425 branches** switched to the new procedure in week 15; **284 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in the switched branches against changes in the non-switched branches

### **Results**

| Group | Pre-Switch Average | Post-Switch Average | Raw Change |
|-------|-------------------|---------------------|------------|
| Switched branches | 51.05 | 54.75 | +3.70 points |
| Non-switched branches | 50.17 | 51.92 | +1.75 points |
| **Difference (DiD)** | | | **+1.95 points** |

- **Treatment effect: +1.95 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.39 to 2.52)
- **Statistical significance: p < 0.001** (highly significant)
- **Effect size: ~3.9% improvement** from baseline

### **Timing of Effects**
The benefits of the new procedure were not immediate:
- **Weeks 15-18:** Modest improvements (0 to 0.5 points above control)
- **Weeks 19-22:** Stronger improvements (1.5 to 2.4 points above control)

This suggests branches may need **4-5 weeks** to fully realize the benefits of the new procedure, possibly due to staff learning curves or adjustment periods.

### **Additional Factors**
The analysis controlled for two other weekly measures:
- **Staffing index:** Higher staffing is associated with higher service scores (+0.56 points per unit)
- **Visits index:** Higher visitor volume is associated with higher service scores (+0.48 points per unit)

The procedure effect remains significant after accounting for these factors.

---

## **Important Caveats**

1. **Pre-existing trends:** The switched branches were already showing slightly stronger improvement trends before the procedure change. This means our estimate of +1.95 points may be somewhat optimistic. The true effect could be modestly smaller.

2. **Short-term study:** We only have 8 weeks of post-switch data. Longer-term effects (positive or negative) remain unknown.

3. **Implementation variation:** Not all branches may have implemented the new procedure with equal fidelity. Some of the variation in results may reflect differences in how well the procedure was adopted.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new procedure, given the positive and statistically significant results.

2. **Provide implementation support:** Since benefits take 4-5 weeks to fully materialize, consider:
   - Training materials or refresher sessions for branch staff
   - A designated "implementation champion" at each branch for the first month
   - Check-in calls at weeks 2 and 4 post-implementation

3. **Continue monitoring:** Track service scores for switched branches over the next quarter to confirm effects persist and don't diminish over time.

4. **Document best practices:** Branches showing the strongest improvements may have implementation insights worth sharing system-wide.

5. **Consider a follow-up study:** After full rollout, compare service scores to this baseline period to validate the estimated 2-point improvement at scale.
