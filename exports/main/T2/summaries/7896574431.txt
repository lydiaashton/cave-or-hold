# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure maintained their service scores while control branches experienced a notable decline. The net effect was a **1.9-point improvement** (approximately 4% relative to baseline), which is statistically significant (p < 0.01). We recommend proceeding with system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **150 branches** tracked over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure (treatment group)
- **60 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Switch occurred at week 15

### Results Summary

| Group | Before (Wk 6–14) | After (Wk 15–22) | Change |
|-------|------------------|------------------|--------|
| Treatment (switched) | 49.86 | 49.56 | -0.31 |
| Control (no switch) | 49.14 | 46.92 | -2.22 |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | | | **+1.92** |

### Statistical Evidence
- **Effect size:** +1.92 points on the service score index
- **95% confidence interval:** 0.7 to 3.1 points
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.002 (highly significant)
- **Pre-trend validation:** No significant difference in trends between groups before the switch (p = 0.50), supporting the validity of the comparison

### What the Data Shows
1. **Both groups faced headwinds:** Service scores declined across the cooperative after week 15 (possibly seasonal or external factors)
2. **The new procedure provided protection:** Treatment branches declined far less than control branches
3. **The effect is robust:** Results hold when controlling for branch-specific characteristics and week-specific factors

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all 60 branches still using the old method

2. **Monitor implementation:** Track service scores for the next 8–12 weeks as remaining branches transition

3. **Document best practices:** Work with the 90 early-adopter branches to capture any implementation insights that could smooth the transition for others

4. **Continue evaluation:** Consider a follow-up analysis after full rollout to confirm sustained benefits
