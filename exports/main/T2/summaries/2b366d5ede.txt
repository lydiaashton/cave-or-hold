# **Analysis of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation procedure is associated with a **statistically significant improvement** in service scores of approximately **1.9 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.3 to 2.4 points) on the 50-point service score index. This represents about a **4% improvement** relative to the average branch score. However, **important limitations** in the data prevent us from concluding with full confidence that the procedure caused this improvement.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Main Result: Positive Association with Service Scores**

Using a difference-in-differences analysis comparing branches that adopted the new procedure (425 branches) to those that did not (284 branches):

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated treatment effect | **+1.86 points** |
| 95% Confidence interval | [1.31, 2.41] |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 |
| Effect size (vs. avg score of 51) | **+3.7%** |

Branches that switched to the new procedure showed greater improvement (or smaller declines) in service scores after week 15 compared to branches that kept the old procedure.

### **2. Important Caveat: Pre-Existing Differences**

**The parallel trends assumption is violated.** Before the switch occurred (weeks 6-14), branches that would later adopt the new procedure were already on a different trajectory:

- **Pre-switch trend differential**: Switched branches' scores were improving relative to non-switched branches by about **0.32 points per week** even before the procedure changed
- **Post-switch trend differential**: After the switch, this differential was about **0.23 points per week**

This means the standard DiD estimate may **overstate the true effect** of the new procedure. The observed improvement could partly reflect pre-existing differences between the two groups of branches rather than the procedure itself.

### **3. Event Study Results**

Week-by-week analysis shows:
- The gap between switched and non-switched branches was **widening steadily before the switch**
- After the switch, the gap **continued to widen at a similar rate**
- There is **no sharp discontinuity** at week 15 that would indicate an immediate treatment effect

### **4. Overall Score Trends**

Both groups experienced **declining service scores** over the study period:
- Control branches: declined ~1.0 points/week
- Switched branches: declined ~0.7 points/week

The new procedure appears to be associated with a **slower rate of decline** rather than an absolute improvement.

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended Actions**

1. **Proceed with caution on system-wide rollout**. While the association is positive and statistically significant, the violated parallel trends assumption means we cannot be certain the procedure caused the improvement.

2. **Conduct a randomized controlled trial** before full rollout:
   - Randomly assign a subset of remaining branches to the new procedure
   - This would eliminate selection bias and provide a cleaner causal estimate

3. **Gather qualitative feedback** from branches that switched:
   - Survey branch managers about implementation challenges
   - Document any operational changes that accompanied the procedure switch

4. **Monitor ongoing performance**:
   - Continue tracking service scores for both groups
   - Look for whether the effect persists, grows, or diminishes over time

5. **Consider cost-benefit analysis**:
   - What are the implementation costs of the new procedure?
   - Does a ~2-point service score improvement justify those costs?

### **Additional Analysis That Would Strengthen Conclusions**

- **Propensity score matching** to create more comparable treatment and control groups
- **Synthetic control method** to construct a better counterfactual for switched branches
- **Longer follow-up period** to assess whether effects are sustained
