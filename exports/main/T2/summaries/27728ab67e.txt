# **Library Branch Service Procedure: Impact Analysis**

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure is associated with a **2-point improvement in service scores** (approximately 4% above baseline). While this effect is in the expected direction and practically meaningful, it is **not statistically significant at conventional levels** (p=0.105) given the sample size. We recommend a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Overall Effect**
- **Treatment branches** (20 branches that switched) improved from 49.6 to 51.0 (+1.46 points)
- **Control branches** (14 branches that kept the old procedure) declined from 46.2 to 45.6 (-0.58 points)
- **Net effect:** +2.05 points (95% confidence interval: -0.43 to +4.52)

### **2. Statistical Confidence**
- The effect is **not statistically significant at the 5% level** (p=0.105 with cluster-robust standard errors)
- However, the confidence interval suggests the true effect could reasonably be as high as +4.5 points
- The analysis controls for branch-specific characteristics and time trends

### **3. Variation Across Branches**
- **60% of treatment branches improved** (12 of 20)
- **40% of treatment branches declined** (8 of 20)
- Effects ranged from **-4.7 to +10.2 points**, indicating substantial heterogeneity
- Some branches saw dramatic improvements while others experienced declines

### **4. Validity Checks**
- **Parallel trends assumption holds:** Treatment and control groups showed similar trends before the switch (no significant differential pre-trends, p=0.94)
- **Other factors:** Staffing levels did not explain the effect; visit volume had a small positive association with service scores

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended Actions**

1. **Proceed with cautious expansion:** The positive direction of the effect and practical significance (4% improvement) support continuing the rollout, but not as an urgent mandate.

2. **Investigate branch-level variation:** Understand why 8 treatment branches saw declines. Consider:
   - Branch size and staffing differences
   - Local community characteristics
   - Implementation fidelity (was the new procedure followed consistently?)

3. **Extend the evaluation period:** Collect 8-12 additional weeks of data to:
   - Increase statistical power
   - Determine if effects grow or diminish over time
   - Narrow the confidence interval around the effect estimate

4. **Develop implementation support:** Create training materials and best practices from the high-performing branches (those with 5+ point improvements) to help struggling branches.

5. **Set success criteria:** Define a target effect size (e.g., +2 points sustained over 6 months) for deciding on full system-wide adoption.
