# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

## **New Branch Procedure: Impact on Service Scores**

### **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **2.3 points** (approximately **4-5% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and grew stronger over time as branches gained experience with the new system. We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. Study Design & Data**
- **709 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6-22)
- **425 branches** adopted the new procedure (treatment group); **284 branches** kept the old procedure (control group)
- The switch occurred at **week 15**; we compared trends before and after this date

**2. Main Result: Positive Treatment Effect**
- The new procedure increased service scores by **+2.27 points** on average
- 95% confidence interval: **1.7 to 2.8 points**
- This represents approximately a **4.4% improvement** relative to the typical branch score of ~50
- The result is **highly statistically significant** (p < 0.001)

**3. The Effect Grew Over Time**
- Early post-switch period (weeks 15-18): **+1.8 points**
- Late post-switch period (weeks 19-22): **+2.8 points**
- This pattern suggests branches improved their implementation of the new procedure with experience

**4. Results Are Robust**
- The effect persists after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume
- Neither staffing nor visits changed differently between treatment and control groups, confirming the service score improvement is attributable to the procedure itself
- Pre-switch trends between groups were reasonably parallel (differences were small and not systematically different from zero)

**5. Visual Evidence**

The event study chart below shows the treatment-control difference by week:

![Event Study](event_study.png)

- **Before the switch** (weeks 6-14, blue circles): Treatment and control branches performed similarly, with differences hovering near zero
- **After the switch** (weeks 15-22, green squares): Treatment branches pulled ahead, with the gap widening from ~1.3 points to nearly 3 points by week 22

---

### **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches

2. **Develop implementation support** based on the learning curve observed:
   - Provide enhanced training during the first 4 weeks after adoption
   - Consider pairing newly-switched branches with experienced adopters for peer support
   - Set realistic expectations that full benefits may take 4+ weeks to materialize

3. **Monitor ongoing performance** after rollout:
   - Track service scores for 8-12 weeks post-implementation at each branch
   - Identify any branches that underperform and provide targeted assistance

4. **Document best practices** from high-performing early adopters to accelerate learning at other branches

---

### **Technical Notes**

- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences design with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch)
- Results are consistent across multiple model specifications
- The parallel trends assumption is supported by pre-period data showing no systematic divergence between groups before the switch
