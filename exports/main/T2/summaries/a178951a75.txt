# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new shelving and stock rotation procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **2.4 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and represents approximately a **5% improvement** relative to the typical branch score of 50.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measurable Improvement in Service Quality
- Switched branches improved by **3.1 points** after adopting the new procedure (from 50.1 to 53.2)
- Non-switched branches improved by only **0.8 points** during the same period (from 48.7 to 49.4)
- The **net effect of the new procedure is +2.4 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.8 to 3.0 points)

### 2. Results Are Statistically Robust
- The improvement is highly statistically significant (p < 0.001)
- The finding holds even after accounting for weekly variations in staffing levels and branch visits
- The effect is consistent across the 8 weeks following implementation

### 3. Pre-Existing Trends Support the Conclusion
- Before the switch (weeks 6-14), both groups of branches followed similar patterns
- Switched branches consistently scored about 1.4 points higher even before the change (likely due to branch characteristics)
- After the switch, this gap widened to 3.8 points—the additional 2.4 points is attributable to the new procedure

### 4. Practical Significance
- A 2.4-point gain on a ~50-point scale represents meaningful improvement in patron service
- The improvement was sustained throughout the post-switch period (weeks 15-22), with no sign of fading

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches, given the clear positive impact on service scores.

2. **Monitor implementation quality** during rollout to ensure branches achieve similar gains. Consider providing additional training or support to branches that show smaller improvements.

3. **Track long-term effects** beyond the initial 8-week window to confirm the improvement is sustained and does not diminish over time.

4. **Investigate mechanisms**—consider gathering qualitative feedback from staff and patrons to understand *why* the new procedure works (e.g., faster book location, better organization, reduced wait times).

5. **Document the protocol** thoroughly to ensure consistent implementation across all branches during the rollout.

---

*Analysis based on 12,053 weekly observations from 709 branches over 17 weeks (weeks 6-22). The switch was implemented at week 15. Statistical method: Difference-in-Differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors.*
