# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.0 points** (about 4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and the analysis supports rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure; **14 branches** kept the old procedure (control group)
- Switch occurred at **week 15**; we compared changes before vs. after for both groups
- This "difference-in-differences" approach isolates the effect of the new procedure from other factors affecting all branches

### Results
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 49.7 | 50.9 | +1.2 |
| Treated (new procedure) | 53.6 | 56.9 | +3.3 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+2.0** |

- **Estimated impact: +2.03 points** on the service score index (95% confidence interval: 0.5 to 3.6)
- **Statistical significance: p = 0.011** (less than 5% probability this result occurred by chance)
- **Effect size: ~4% improvement** relative to the baseline average score of 52

### Validity Checks
- **Pre-trends look good:** Before week 15, both groups followed similar trends (no statistically significant difference, p = 0.21). This supports the conclusion that the post-switch divergence was caused by the new procedure.
- **Results are robust:** The effect holds after controlling for branch-specific characteristics, week-to-week variation, staffing levels, and visitor volume.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all remaining branches.

2. **Monitor implementation:** Track service scores during rollout to ensure the effect is replicated across all branches. Some branches may need additional support during the transition.

3. **Consider a formal post-rollout evaluation** in 3–6 months to confirm sustained improvements and identify any branches that may need extra assistance.

4. **Document the procedure** and create training materials based on the experiences of the 20 early-adopter branches to smooth the transition for others.

*Analysis based on weekly service score data from weeks 6–22. The difference-in-differences regression model includes branch and week fixed effects to control for unobserved factors.*
