# **MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure shows a **positive but statistically inconclusive effect** on service scores. Branches that switched improved by an estimated **1.5 points** more than branches that kept the old procedure, but this difference could plausibly be due to random variation. We **cannot confidently conclude** the new procedure caused an improvement based on this data alone.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **34 branches** total: 20 adopted the new procedure, 14 kept the old one
- **17 weeks** of data (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring in week 15
- Service scores average ~50 points (higher is better)

### Results

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 47.27 | 51.59 | +4.32 |
| Non-switched branches | 47.96 | 50.75 | +2.79 |
| **Difference (DiD)** | | | **+1.53** |

### Statistical Assessment
- **Estimated effect:** +1.53 points (3.1% of average score)
- **95% confidence interval:** -0.67 to +3.74 points
- **P-value:** 0.17 (not statistically significant at conventional levels)
- **Interpretation:** The true effect could be slightly negative, zero, or moderately positive

### Branch-Level Patterns
- **90%** of switched branches (18/20) showed score improvements
- **79%** of non-switched branches (11/14) also improved
- Both groups improved over time, but switched branches improved slightly more

---

## **Next Steps**

### Recommended Actions

1. **Do not mandate system-wide rollout yet.** The evidence is suggestive but not conclusive. A costly cooperative-wide change should rest on firmer evidence.

2. **Extend the observation period.** Eight weeks post-switch may be insufficient. Branches may need time to fully adapt to new procedures. Collect 8–12 additional weeks of data.

3. **Consider a phased expansion.** Invite 5–10 additional branches to adopt the procedure now, creating a larger treatment group and stronger statistical power.

4. **Investigate implementation quality.** The variation in branch-level results (some switched branches saw declines) suggests execution may matter. Survey branches about fidelity to the new procedure.

5. **Monitor cost implications.** Even if the effect is real at +1.5 points, the board should weigh this against any additional staffing, training, or operational costs.

### Decision Framework

| If the board prioritizes... | Recommendation |
|----------------------------|----------------|
| **Evidence-based decisions** | Wait for more data before expanding |
| **Innovation and improvement** | Proceed with cautious, monitored expansion |
| **Risk avoidance** | Maintain status quo; the old procedure is not failing |

---

## **Technical Note**

This analysis used a difference-in-differences approach with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch), which is the appropriate method for this type of quasi-experimental data. The parallel trends assumption appears reasonable—both groups followed similar patterns before the switch.
