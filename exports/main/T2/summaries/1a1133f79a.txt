# **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, roll out the new procedure to all branches.** The evidence shows the new procedure significantly improved service scores by approximately **2.1 points** (p=0.001). This represents a meaningful 4% improvement, and the effect is statistically robust.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure; **60 branches** kept the old procedure (control group)
- Switch occurred at **week 15**
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in treatment vs. control groups

### Results

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (Old Procedure) | 49.22 | 47.36 | −1.86 |
| Treatment (New Procedure) | 50.91 | 51.10 | +0.19 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+2.05** |

- **Treatment effect: +2.12 points** (95% confidence interval: 0.86 to 3.38)
- **Statistical significance: p = 0.001** (highly significant)
- Results hold after controlling for staffing levels and visit volumes

### What This Means
- Branches using the **old procedure saw scores decline** after week 15 (−1.86 points)
- Branches using the **new procedure maintained their scores** (+0.19 points)
- The new procedure appears to **protect against score deterioration** while delivering modest gains

### Validity Checks
- **Parallel trends**: Pre-switch weekly patterns were similar between groups, supporting the validity of the comparison
- **Robustness**: Effect persists when controlling for staffing and visitor volume fluctuations
- **Consistency**: Treatment group consistently scored ~2 points higher than control throughout the study period

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all 60 branches still using the old method

2. **Monitor implementation** over the next 8–12 weeks to confirm effects persist at scale

3. **Document best practices** from the 90 early-adopter branches to support training at remaining locations

4. **Consider investigating** why control group scores declined post-week-15 (seasonal effects? staffing changes?) to ensure the new procedure addresses underlying vulnerabilities

*Analysis based on 2,550 weekly observations from 150 branches. Standard errors clustered by branch to account for repeated measures.*
