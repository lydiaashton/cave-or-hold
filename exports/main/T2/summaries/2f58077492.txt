## Analysis Complete

I've analyzed the library branch data using a rigorous difference-in-differences approach with branch and week fixed effects. Here's what I found:

### Bottom Line
**The new procedure improved service scores by 1.85 points on average (3.8% improvement), with high statistical confidence.** However, the benefits were not evenly distributed—high-performing branches saw substantial gains (+8.4 points) while lower-performing branches actually declined (-3.6 points).

### Key Findings

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Treatment effect | +1.85 points |
| 95% Confidence Interval | [1.28, 2.43] |
| Statistical Significance | p < 0.001 |
| Branches studied | 709 (425 treatment, 284 control) |
| Branches that improved | 68% of treatment branches |

### Critical Insight
The new procedure works well **overall**, but it appears to amplify existing performance differences rather than lift all branches equally. This suggests the procedure may require different implementation support depending on a branch's starting point.

### Recommendation
**Proceed with the rollout, but with targeted support:**
1. Provide extra training and mentoring for lower-performing branches
2. Monitor all branches closely during the first 8 weeks after switching
3. Investigate why struggling branches didn't benefit
4. Consider a phased rollout that prioritizes branches similar to successful adopters
