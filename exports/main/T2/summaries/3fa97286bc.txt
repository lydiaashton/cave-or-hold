# Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact Analysis

## Bottom Line

**Yes, the new shelving and rotation procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.1 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant and appears to grow over time. We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## Key Findings

### 1. Measurable Improvement in Service Scores

| Group | Before Switch (Weeks 6-14) | After Switch (Weeks 15-22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------------|---------------------------|--------|
| Switched Branches (425 branches) | 51.19 | 52.66 | **+1.47** |
| Non-Switched Branches (284 branches) | 50.48 | 49.86 | **-0.62** |

The **net effect of the new procedure is +2.09 points** on the service score index. This accounts for underlying trends that affected all branches (non-switched branches actually declined slightly during the same period).

### 2. Statistical Confidence

- **p-value < 0.001**: The result is highly statistically significant
- **Effect size (Cohen's d): 0.56**: A medium-sized, practically meaningful effect
- The finding holds even after controlling for staffing levels and visit volumes

### 3. Effect Strengthens Over Time

The benefit of the new procedure grew during the 8-week post-switch period:

| Period | Average Advantage for Switched Branches |
|--------|----------------------------------------|
| Weeks 15-16 (immediate) | +1.5 points |
| Weeks 17-22 (sustained) | +3.2 points |

This pattern suggests branches needed a brief adjustment period, after which the new procedure delivered increasing benefits.

### 4. No Confounding Factors

Analysis of staffing and visit indices shows:
- Staffing and visits were similar across switched and non-switched branches
- The service score improvement is attributable to the procedure change, not external factors
