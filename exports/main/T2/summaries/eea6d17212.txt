# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new rotation and shelving procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **2.1 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant and represents a meaningful 4% gain in service quality. We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **150 branches** total: 90 switched to the new procedure, 60 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring in week 15
- Analysis uses a "difference-in-differences" approach, comparing changes in the switching branches against changes in the non-switching branches

### **Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Branches that switched (treatment) | 49.0 | 51.3 | **+2.3** |
| Branches that didn't switch (control) | 49.3 | 49.5 | +0.2 |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | | **+2.1** |

### **Statistical Confidence**
- **Effect size:** +2.13 points (95% confidence interval: +0.9 to +3.4)
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.01 (highly significant)
- **Practical significance:** 4.3% improvement relative to the average score of 50

### **Validation Checks**
- **Parallel trends confirmed:** Before the switch, both groups showed similar week-to-week patterns with no systematic differences. This validates our comparison.
- **Sustained effect:** The improvement persisted throughout all 8 weeks after the switch, with no sign of fading.
- **Robust to controls:** The result holds even when accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume.
