## **Analysis of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure: Impact on Service Scores**

### **Bottom Line**
**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure maintained significantly higher service scores compared to branches that kept the old procedure. The improvement is statistically significant and represents a meaningful gain in service quality. We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. The new procedure had a positive effect on service scores**
- Branches using the new procedure saw service scores decline by only **0.6 points** on average after the switch
- Branches keeping the old procedure saw scores decline by **2.9 points** over the same period
- **Net benefit: +2.3 points** attributable to the new procedure (p < 0.001)

**2. This is a meaningful improvement**
- With average service scores around 50, a 2.3-point gain represents approximately **4-5% improvement**
- The effect size (Cohen's d = 0.60) is considered **medium-to-large** in practical terms

**3. Results are robust**
- Treatment and control groups had nearly identical service scores before the switch (51.8 vs. 51.9), validating the comparison
- The finding holds even after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume
- **41% of switched branches improved** their scores vs. only **27% of non-switched branches**

**4. Important context: external factors affected all branches**
- Both groups experienced score declines after week 14, suggesting some external factor (seasonal, operational, or other) affected the entire system
- The new procedure **protected branches from the full impact** of this decline
