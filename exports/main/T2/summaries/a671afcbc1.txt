# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact Analysis**

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure is associated with a modest improvement in service scores (+1.8 to +2.0 points on a ~50-point scale), but this effect is **not statistically significant**. The data do not provide strong evidence that the new procedure improves service quality. The board should consider additional data collection before making a systemwide rollout decision.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches total**: 20 branches adopted the new procedure; 14 branches kept the old procedure (control group)
- **17 weeks of data**: Weeks 6-22, with the switch implemented in Week 15
- **Analysis method**: Difference-in-Differences (DiD) regression with branch and week fixed effects, controlling for staffing and visit levels

### **Treatment Effect Estimate**
| Model | Effect Size | Standard Error | 95% Confidence Interval | P-value |
|-------|-------------|----------------|------------------------|---------|
| Basic DiD | +1.77 points | 1.39 | -0.94 to +4.49 | 0.20 |
| With Controls | +2.01 points | 1.27 | -0.49 to +4.50 | 0.12 |

### **Interpretation**
- **Direction**: The point estimate suggests a positive effect (higher service scores)
- **Magnitude**: If real, the effect represents approximately a 4% improvement over the average score of 50
- **Uncertainty**: The confidence interval includes zero and ranges from slightly negative to moderately positive. We cannot rule out that the true effect is zero or even slightly negative.
- **Statistical significance**: The effect does not meet conventional thresholds for statistical significance (p < 0.05)

### **Validity Checks**
- **Parallel trends assumption**: Pre-switch trends did not differ significantly between treatment and control groups (p=0.38), supporting the validity of the analysis
- **Balance**: Treatment branches had higher baseline scores (54.8 vs 48.7), but the DiD design accounts for this time-invariant difference

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Extend the observation period**: Eight weeks of post-switch data may be insufficient to detect a modest effect. Continue monitoring for 8-12 additional weeks to increase statistical power.

2. **Consider a phased rollout**: Given the uncertain but potentially positive effect, the board could authorize expanding the new procedure to additional branches while continuing to evaluate outcomes.

3. **Investigate heterogeneous effects**: The average effect may mask variation across branch types (e.g., urban vs. rural, high- vs. low-volume). Analyze whether certain branches benefit more from the new procedure.

4. **Monitor implementation fidelity**: Ensure branches that switched are actually following the new procedure as intended. Variation in implementation could attenuate the measured effect.

5. **Cost-benefit analysis**: Even if the effect is real (+2 points), the board should weigh this against any additional costs or operational burdens of the new procedure.

---

## **Recommendation**

**Do not proceed with immediate systemwide rollout.** The evidence is suggestive but not conclusive. Recommend extending the evaluation period by 2-3 months and re-assessing with more post-switch data before making a final decision.
