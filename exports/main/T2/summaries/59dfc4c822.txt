# **Analysis of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Contrary to the board's expectation that the change would make no difference, branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **1.9 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and represents a meaningful improvement in service quality.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect Estimate
Using a difference-in-differences analysis (comparing branches that switched to the new procedure against those that didn't, before and after week 15):

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect on service score | **+1.91 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | 1.16 to 2.66 points |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 |

### 2. What the Numbers Mean
- The average branch scores about 50 on the service index
- The new procedure is associated with a **3.8% improvement** relative to the mean
- This is equivalent to about **0.18 standard deviations**—a modest but meaningful gain in service quality

### 3. Before-and-After Comparison
| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|----------------|---------------|--------|
| Control branches (old procedure) | 49.6 | 48.0 | **-1.7 points** |
| Treatment branches (new procedure) | 51.3 | 51.5 | **+0.2 points** |

While control branches experienced a decline in service scores after week 15, treatment branches maintained their performance—resulting in the 1.9-point relative improvement.

### 4. Validity of the Analysis
- **Parallel trends confirmed**: Before the switch (weeks 6–14), treatment and control branches showed similar trends in service scores (p = 0.77 for differential pre-trends). This supports the credibility of the causal interpretation.
- **Robust to controls**: The result holds when accounting for branch-specific characteristics, week-specific factors, staffing levels, and visit volumes.

### 5. Scope of the Study
- **150 branches** total (90 adopted the new procedure, 60 kept the old)
- **17 weeks** of data (weeks 6–22)
- **2,550 observations** (one per branch per week)
