# **New Shelving Procedure: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new shelving and rotation procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **2.1 points** (approximately **4% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and consistent across the post-switch period. **We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Measurable Improvement**
- Switched branches improved by **2.1 points** on the service score index after adopting the new procedure
- 95% confidence interval: **1.5 to 2.6 points**
- This represents a **4.1% improvement** relative to baseline scores

### **2. Strong Statistical Evidence**
- The improvement is highly statistically significant (p < 0.001)
- Analysis used a rigorous difference-in-differences approach comparing 425 switched branches against 284 control branches
- Results hold even after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### **3. Consistent Effect Over Time**
The improvement was sustained across all 8 weeks after the switch:

| Week | Treatment - Control Difference |
|------|-------------------------------|
| 15   | +2.5 points |
| 16   | +2.4 points |
| 17   | +1.9 points |
| 18   | +2.6 points |
| 19   | +2.6 points |
| 20   | +3.7 points |
| 21   | +4.2 points |
| 22   | +4.2 points |

The effect appears to strengthen in later weeks, suggesting branches may improve further as they gain experience with the new procedure.

### **4. Analysis Scope**
- **709 total branches** analyzed (425 switched, 284 control)
- **17 weeks** of data (weeks 6–22)
- Switch occurred at **week 15**
- **12,053 branch-week observations**
