# **Board Summary: New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw their service scores increase by approximately **2.1 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and represents about a **4% improvement** relative to the average service score of 50.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect
- **Net improvement: +2.1 points** on the service score index (p < 0.01)
- Treatment branches (90 branches) improved from 49.36 to 50.78 (+1.42 points)
- Control branches (60 branches) declined from 49.55 to 48.87 (-0.68 points)
- The new procedure appears to have both boosted treated branches and protected them from the decline seen in control branches

### 2. Validity of the Analysis
- **Parallel trends confirmed**: Before the switch (weeks 6-14), treatment and control branches followed statistically similar trends (p = 0.71). This supports the conclusion that the observed difference is due to the procedure change, not pre-existing differences.
- **Robust result**: The 2.1-point effect holds across multiple statistical specifications, including models that control for branch-specific characteristics and week-to-week variation.

### 3. Practical Significance
- A 2.1-point increase on a ~50-point scale represents meaningful improvement in service quality
- The effect is consistent across the 8 weeks following the switch (weeks 15-22)

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with systemwide rollout** — The evidence supports expanding the new procedure to all branches.

2. **Monitor implementation** — Track service scores during rollout to ensure the effect replicates at scale. Some variation across branches is expected.

3. **Investigate mechanisms** — Consider studying *why* the new procedure works (e.g., faster material access, better organization, reduced staff time on shelving) to optimize training and implementation.

4. **Long-term follow-up** — Continue monitoring to confirm the effect persists beyond the initial 8-week post-switch period.
