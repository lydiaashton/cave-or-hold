# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure shows a **positive but not statistically definitive** effect on service scores. Branches that switched to the new procedure improved their service scores by approximately **2 points (4% improvement)** relative to branches that kept the old procedure. While this is encouraging, the evidence is not strong enough to confidently recommend a full rollout without additional data or consideration of implementation costs and risks.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** switched to the new procedure at week 15 (treatment group)
- **14 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Service scores average ~50 points (higher is better)

### **Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Treatment (new procedure) | 49.9 | 52.0 | **+2.1** |
| Control (old procedure) | 49.2 | 49.3 | +0.2 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+1.9 points** |

### **Statistical Evidence**
- **Effect size:** +1.9 to +2.1 service score points (~4% improvement)
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.10–0.13 (not significant at the conventional 0.05 threshold)
- **95% Confidence Interval:** -0.4 to +4.5 points
- **Branch-level results:** 70% of treatment branches improved vs. 57% of control branches

### **What This Means**
- The new procedure is **associated with higher service scores**, and the direction of the effect aligns with expectations
- However, we **cannot rule out** that the observed improvement could be due to random variation
- The confidence interval suggests the true effect could range from slightly negative (-0.4) to meaningfully positive (+4.5)

---

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended Actions**

1. **Continue monitoring** — Collect additional weeks of data to increase statistical power. With more observations, we could determine whether the positive trend holds.

2. **Consider a phased rollout** — If the board wishes to proceed despite uncertainty, implement the new procedure in additional branches incrementally while continuing to measure outcomes.

3. **Evaluate costs and risks** — Weigh the potential 4% service score improvement against:
   - Implementation costs (training, labor, disruption)
   - Risk that the effect may be smaller than observed or non-existent

4. **Investigate variation** — Some branches showed large improvements (up to +10 points) while others declined. Understanding which branches benefit most could inform targeted implementation.

5. **Set a decision threshold** — Determine what level of evidence the board requires (e.g., statistical significance at p < 0.05, or a minimum effect size) before committing to system-wide rollout.

---

## **Technical Notes**

Analysis used a difference-in-differences approach with branch-clustered standard errors, controlling for staffing and visit levels. The parallel trends assumption appears reasonable: treatment and control groups had similar service score trajectories before the switch (pre-switch difference not statistically significant, p = 0.52).
