# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure is associated with **modest improvements in service scores**, but the evidence is **not statistically conclusive**. Branches that adopted the new procedure showed greater improvement than those that kept the old procedure, but we cannot rule out the possibility that this difference occurred by chance. **We recommend a cautious, phased rollout with continued monitoring.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** switched to the new procedure at week 15; **14 branches** kept the old procedure
- Analysis uses a **Difference-in-Differences** approach, comparing changes in the treatment group to changes in the control group

### **Results**

| Group | Pre-Switch Avg | Post-Switch Avg | Change |
|-------|---------------|-----------------|--------|
| **New Procedure** (20 branches) | 44.89 | 48.66 | **+3.77** |
| **Old Procedure** (14 branches) | 48.93 | 50.34 | **+1.42** |

**Estimated Effect of New Procedure: +2.35 points** (approximately 4.7% improvement relative to baseline)

### **Statistical Confidence**
- **P-value: 0.096** (marginally significant; conventional threshold is 0.05)
- **95% Confidence Interval: -0.41 to +5.04 points**
  - This means the true effect could range from slightly negative to moderately positive
  - The interval includes zero, so we cannot be 95% confident the effect is real

### **Validity Checks**
- ✅ **Parallel trends assumption holds**: Before the switch, both groups showed similar trends (p=0.56)
- ✅ **Results robust**: Effect size remains stable (~2.3 points) with or without controlling for staffing and visitor traffic
- ⚠️ **Baseline imbalance**: Treatment branches started with lower scores (~4 points below control), though this is accounted for in the analysis

---

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended Actions**

1. **Proceed with phased rollout** — The point estimate is positive and meaningful (~2 points on a 50-point scale), and there is no evidence of harm. However, given the statistical uncertainty, roll out gradually rather than system-wide.

2. **Extend the evaluation period** — Collect 8–12 additional weeks of data post-rollout. More data will narrow the confidence interval and provide greater certainty about the true effect.

3. **Monitor implementation fidelity** — Track whether branches are correctly implementing the new procedure. Variation in execution could be masking the true effect.

4. **Investigate heterogeneity** — Analyze whether the procedure works better for certain branch types (e.g., by size, location, or baseline performance). This could inform targeted rollout priorities.

5. **Set a decision checkpoint** — Re-evaluate after the extended data collection. If the effect remains positive and the confidence interval excludes zero, proceed with full system-wide adoption.

---

## **Technical Notes**

- Analysis uses cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch) to account for repeated observations
- Service scores are an internal index where ~50 represents average branch performance; higher scores indicate better service
- All analyses control for weekly staffing levels and visitor traffic
