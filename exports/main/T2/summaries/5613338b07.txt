## **Key Findings**

### 2. Study Design & Data
- **709 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **425 branches** adopted the new procedure; **284 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Switch occurred at **week 15**, providing 9 weeks of before-data and 8 weeks of after-data
- Analysis used difference-in-differences methodology with branch and week controls to isolate the procedure's effect

### 3. Improvement Driven by Procedure, Not Other Factors
The analysis tested whether the improvement could be explained by changes in staffing or visitor volume:
- **Staffing index:** No significant change (p = 0.38)
- **Visits index:** No significant change (p = 0.62)

This indicates the service score improvement came from the new procedure itself, not from branches receiving more resources or serving more patrons.

### 4. Trends Support Causal Interpretation
- **Before the switch (weeks 6–14):** Switched and non-switched branches followed parallel trends, with differences ranging from -0.4 to +2.0 points
- **After the switch (weeks 15–22):** The gap widened consistently, with switched branches outperforming by 1.7 to 3.7 points
- This pattern supports the conclusion that the procedure change caused the improvement
