# **Board Summary: Impact of New Branch Rotation Procedure on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service quality.** Branches that switched to the new rotation and shelving procedure saw their service scores increase by **2.2 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.7 to 2.8 points) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents a **4% improvement** relative to the average branch score of 50.8. The result is highly statistically significant (p < 0.001) and holds after accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **709 branches** analyzed over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **425 branches** adopted the new procedure; **284 branches** kept the old procedure (control group)
- Switch occurred in **week 15**; analysis compares 9 weeks before and 8 weeks after
- Used a **difference-in-differences** approach to isolate the effect of the new procedure

### Results
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Treatment Effect** | +2.21 service score points |
| **95% Confidence Interval** | [1.65, 2.78] points |
| **Statistical Significance** | p < 0.001 |
| **Effect Size** | 4.3% increase over average score |

### Supporting Evidence
1. **Parallel Trends Validated**: Before the switch, the gap between treatment and control branches was stable (averaging 0.7 points), supporting the validity of the comparison.

2. **Effect Persists with Controls**: The 2.2-point improvement remains unchanged after accounting for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume, indicating the effect is not driven by these factors.

3. **Growing Impact**: The benefit appears to increase over time—from about 2 points immediately after the switch to nearly 4 points by weeks 21–22—suggesting branches may improve further as they gain experience with the new procedure.

4. **Control Group Stability**: Branches that kept the old procedure showed no meaningful change in service scores (−0.2 points), confirming that broader trends did not drive the improvement.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Recommend Rollout**: Based on the clear, statistically significant improvement in service scores, we recommend proceeding with system-wide implementation of the new procedure.

2. **Monitor Implementation**: Track service scores during rollout to ensure the 2+ point improvement is replicated across all branches.

3. **Investigate Mechanism**: The growing effect over time suggests a learning curve. Consider documenting best practices from early adopters to accelerate benefits for later implementers.

4. **Cost-Benefit Analysis**: Quantify the operational costs of the new procedure against the 4% service quality improvement to confirm net benefit.

5. **Long-term Follow-up**: Continue monitoring to determine whether the effect stabilizes, continues growing, or diminishes over time.
