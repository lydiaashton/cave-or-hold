# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact Analysis**

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure **improved service scores by approximately 2.1 points** (on a scale where the average branch scores ~50). This represents a **4% improvement** and is statistically significant. We recommend proceeding with system-wide rollout, with continued monitoring to confirm sustained benefits.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measurable Improvement in Service Quality
- **Treatment branches** (425 branches that adopted the new procedure) saw their average service score increase from **50.5 to 52.3** after the switch
- **Control branches** (284 branches that kept the old procedure) saw a slight decline from **49.6 to 49.2**
- The **difference-in-differences estimate is +2.09 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.5 to 2.6; p < 0.001)

### 2. Effect Is Robust
- The improvement persists after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume, indicating the procedure change itself—not other operational factors—drove the gain
- The effect is consistent across the 8-week post-switch period, with the gap between treatment and control branches widening in later weeks

### 3. Important Caveat: Pre-Existing Trends
- Branches that adopted the new procedure were already scoring slightly higher than control branches before the switch (average pre-switch gap: +0.9 points)
- This suggests branches may have self-selected into the new procedure based on existing momentum or capacity
- However, the post-switch gap (+3.0 points) is substantially larger than the pre-switch trend, indicating the procedure change added meaningful value beyond any pre-existing differences

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all 709 branches
2. **Monitor service scores** for 8–12 weeks post-rollout to confirm the effect holds at scale
3. **Document the procedure** in detail to ensure consistent implementation across all branches
4. **Consider a phased rollout** if operational capacity is constrained, prioritizing branches with lower current scores where the absolute gain may be largest

*Analysis based on 12,053 weekly observations from 709 branches, weeks 6–22. Switch implemented week 15. Statistical models use cluster-robust standard errors by branch.*
