# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.0 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.4 to 2.5 points) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents roughly a **4% improvement** in service quality. The effect is statistically significant and robust across different analytical approaches.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **709 branches** tracked over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- **425 branches** adopted the new procedure (treatment group)
- **284 branches** kept the old procedure (control group)
- Switch occurred in **week 15**
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in service scores before and after the switch between the two groups

### Results
| Metric | Control Branches | Treatment Branches |
|--------|------------------|-------------------|
| Average score before switch | 49.5 | 50.7 |
| Average score after switch | 49.0 | 52.2 |
| **Change** | **-0.5** | **+1.5** |

**Net effect of new procedure: +1.95 points** (p < 0.001)

### Robustness Checks
- The effect remains consistent after controlling for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume
- Pre-switch trends were similar between groups, supporting the validity of the comparison
- Branch-level fixed effects account for inherent differences between individual branches

### Effect Size Context
- The average branch service score is approximately 50
- A 2-point improvement represents meaningful progress in service quality
- The control group actually saw a slight decline (-0.5 points) during the same period, making the treatment effect even more notable

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all remaining branches

2. **Monitor implementation** during the rollout to ensure fidelity to the new procedure

3. **Track service scores** for the next 8–12 weeks post-rollout to confirm the effect persists at scale

4. **Document the procedure** in detail to support training at non-switched branches

5. **Consider investigating mechanisms** — the improvement may be driven by better stock availability, faster retrieval times, or improved organization. Understanding why it works could help optimize further.

*Analysis based on 12,053 weekly observations from 709 branches. Statistical models include branch fixed effects and cluster-robust standard errors.*
