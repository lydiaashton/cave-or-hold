# **Board Summary: Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.3 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant and represents a meaningful gain on a scale where the average branch scores about 50. We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Study Design
- **709 branches** participated in the evaluation (425 switched to the new procedure, 284 kept the old)
- Data covers **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- This quasi-experimental design allows us to isolate the effect of the new procedure by comparing switched branches to non-switched branches before and after the change

### 2. Service Score Impact
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 51.9 | 51.8 | -0.1 |
| Non-switched branches | 52.1 | 49.7 | **-2.4** |

- Switched branches **maintained** their service scores after the change
- Non-switched branches experienced a **decline of 2.4 points** after week 15
- The new procedure provided a **relative benefit of +2.3 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.8 to 2.7 points)

### 3. Statistical Confidence
- The effect is **highly statistically significant** (p < 0.001)
- Results hold even after controlling for:
  - Branch-specific characteristics (fixed effects)
  - Weekly staffing levels
  - Weekly visit volumes
- The two groups followed **parallel trends before the switch**, supporting the validity of the comparison

### 4. Trend Visualization
The chart below shows service scores over time for both groups:

![Service Score Trends](service_score_trends.png)

**Key pattern**: Before week 15, both groups tracked closely together. After the switch, non-switched branches declined while switched branches held steady, creating a widening gap that reached nearly 3 points by week 22.

### 5. No Unintended Effects
- The new procedure did **not** significantly affect staffing requirements (p = 0.14)
- The new procedure did **not** significantly affect visit volumes (p = 0.70)
- The service score improvement appears to be a direct benefit of the procedure change itself
