# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new shelving and rotation procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **1.9 points** (about 4%) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant and has grown over time since implementation. We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout, with monitoring to ensure effects are sustained.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. The New Procedure Improved Service Scores
- **Treatment effect: +1.9 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.4 to 2.5 points)
- Switched branches improved by 2.1 points after the change (from 50.4 to 52.4)
- Control branches improved by only 0.2 points over the same period (from 50.1 to 50.3)
- The difference is highly statistically significant (p < 0.001)

### 2. Benefits Increased Over Time
The improvement wasn't immediate—it grew as branches gained experience with the new procedure:
- Weeks 1-2 after switch: +1.4 to +1.7 points
- Weeks 3-5 after switch: +1.7 to +2.3 points  
- Weeks 6-8 after switch: +3.1 to +3.2 points

This pattern suggests branches needed time to fully realize the benefits.

### 3. Analysis Scope
- **709 branches** total (425 adopted new procedure, 284 kept old procedure)
- **17 weeks** of data per branch (weeks 6-22)
- Switch occurred at **week 15**
- Service scores average ~50 points (higher is better)

### 4. Important Caveat
Branches that switched showed slightly different trends *before* the switch compared to control branches. While this doesn't invalidate our findings—the post-switch improvements are larger than any pre-existing differences—it means the true effect could be somewhat smaller than our estimate. We recommend continued monitoring as more branches adopt the procedure.

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to remaining branches

2. **Monitor implementation closely** for the first 8 weeks at each branch, as benefits appear to accumulate over time

3. **Track service scores** at newly converted branches to confirm they show similar improvements

4. **Document best practices** from early-adopter branches to support training at remaining locations

5. **Re-evaluate in 3-6 months** with additional data to confirm sustained benefits
