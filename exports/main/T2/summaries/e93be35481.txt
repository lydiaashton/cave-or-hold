# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure is associated with a **statistically significant improvement in service scores of approximately 1.9 points (3.8%)**. However, there are important caveats to this finding that warrant a cautious, phased rollout rather than immediate system-wide adoption.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Measured Impact on Service Scores**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches (425) | 51.59 | 52.70 | +1.11 |
| Control branches (284) | 51.24 | 50.40 | -0.84 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+1.94** |

- **Treatment effect:** +1.94 points (95% CI: 1.40 to 2.48)
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001
- **Effect size:** 0.20 standard deviations (modest but meaningful)
- **Robustness:** Effect remains unchanged when controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### **2. Study Design**

- **Data:** 12,053 weekly observations from 709 branches (weeks 6–22)
- **Treatment group:** 425 branches (60%) adopted the new procedure
- **Control group:** 284 branches (40%) continued with the old procedure
- **Switch point:** Week 15

### **3. Important Caveat: Pre-Existing Trends**

⚠️ **The parallel trends assumption is violated.** Statistical testing reveals that switched branches were already on a different trajectory before the procedure change (p < 0.001). This means:

- The estimated 1.94-point effect may be **partially inflated** by pre-existing differences
- The true causal effect could be smaller than measured
- However, the direction of the pre-trend (treatment group improving faster) would actually work *against* finding a positive effect, suggesting the result may be conservative

---

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended Actions**

1. **Proceed with phased rollout** — The evidence supports expanding the new procedure, but do so in stages to monitor real-world performance.

2. **Extend the evaluation period** — Collect 8–12 additional weeks of data to:
   - Confirm the effect persists over time
   - Better establish post-switch trends
   - Reduce uncertainty from the pre-trends concern

3. **Monitor implementation fidelity** — Track whether branches are correctly following the new procedure; effects may vary by execution quality.

4. **Conduct branch-level follow-up** — Interview high-performing switched branches to identify best practices that could maximize benefits during rollout.

5. **Set realistic expectations** — Communicate to stakeholders that the expected improvement is approximately 2 points on the service score index (roughly 4%), not a transformational change.

---

## **Conclusion**

The new procedure shows **promising results** with a statistically significant positive effect on service scores. While methodological limitations prevent us from claiming the full 1.94-point gain is purely causal, the weight of evidence supports proceeding with a **careful, monitored expansion** to all branches.
