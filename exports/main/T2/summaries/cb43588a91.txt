# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new shelving and stock rotation procedure significantly improved service scores.** Contrary to the expectation of no difference, branches that adopted the new procedure saw their service scores increase by approximately **2.1 points** (on a scale where the average branch scores about 50) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and represents a meaningful improvement in service quality.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect
- **Estimated impact:** +2.13 service score points (95% confidence interval: 0.85 to 3.41)
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.01 (highly significant)
- **Effect size:** 0.22 standard deviations (a small-to-moderate practical effect)

### 2. Study Design
- **150 branches total:** 90 adopted the new procedure, 60 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data:** 9 weeks before the switch (weeks 6-14), 8 weeks after (weeks 15-22)
- **Analysis method:** Difference-in-differences with branch fixed effects, controlling for staffing and visit levels
- **Validity check:** Pre-switch trends were parallel between groups, supporting the causal interpretation

### 3. What the Data Shows

| Group | Pre-Switch Average | Post-Switch Average | Change |
|-------|-------------------|---------------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 47.6 | 48.4 | +0.9 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 50.8 | 53.7 | +2.9 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+2.1** |

The treatment group improved by 2.9 points while the control group improved by only 0.9 points. After accounting for the underlying time trend (captured by the control group), the new procedure itself contributed approximately 2.1 points of improvement.

### 4. Robustness
- Results hold when controlling for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume
- Results hold with branch-level fixed effects (accounting for permanent differences between branches)
- Results hold with statistical corrections for repeated observations within branches
- No branches dropped out of the study; all 150 branches have complete data

## **Technical Notes**

- Analysis used a difference-in-differences design, the standard approach for evaluating policy changes with treatment and control groups
- The parallel trends assumption (required for valid DiD) was confirmed: treatment and control groups had similar trends in the 9 weeks before the switch
- Standard errors were clustered at the branch level to account for repeated observations
