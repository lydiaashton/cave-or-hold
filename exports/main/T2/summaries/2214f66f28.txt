# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and rotation procedure is associated with a **modest improvement in service scores** (+1.9 points, or approximately 4%). However, this improvement is **not statistically significant** at conventional levels, meaning we cannot rule out that the observed difference occurred by chance. The evidence suggests a positive direction but is not definitive. We recommend a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than an immediate systemwide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure
- The switch occurred at **week 15**, allowing comparison of before/after periods for both groups
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, which isolates the effect of the procedure change from other factors affecting all branches

### **Results**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Estimated Effect** | +1.86 service score points |
| **Relative Improvement** | ~3.6% above baseline |
| **Statistical Significance** | p = 0.13 (not significant at 0.05 level) |
| **95% Confidence Interval** | -0.5 to +4.2 points |

### **What the Data Shows**

1. **Treatment branches** (those that switched) saw service scores remain essentially stable after the switch (+0.3 points)

2. **Control branches** (those that didn't switch) experienced a **decline** in service scores after week 15 (-1.6 points)

3. The **net effect** of the new procedure is the difference between these two trends: **+1.9 points**

4. **Pre-trend validation**: Before the switch (weeks 6–14), both groups followed similar trends with no systematic differences, supporting the validity of our analysis

5. **Staffing matters**: Higher staffing levels are significantly associated with better service scores, suggesting the new procedure may help branches maintain performance even when staffing fluctuates

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended Actions**

1. **Proceed with cautious rollout** to remaining branches, but implement in phases rather than all at once

2. **Establish ongoing monitoring** with monthly service score tracking for at least 6 months post-rollout to confirm the effect persists

3. **Document implementation details** at each branch to identify whether certain conditions (staffing levels, branch size, visit volume) affect the procedure's effectiveness

4. **Consider a cost-benefit analysis** weighing the modest service score improvement against training and implementation costs

5. **Set a review checkpoint** at 3 months to evaluate whether the observed improvements are sustained and whether statistical significance improves with more data

### **Alternative Considerations**

If the board prefers stronger evidence before full rollout:
- Extend the pilot to additional branches for another 8–12 weeks
- This would increase statistical power and provide more precise estimates
- Target branches with lower baseline scores, where the procedure may have greater impact

---

## **Technical Notes**

- Analysis controls for branch-specific characteristics and week-to-week variation
- Standard errors are clustered at the branch level to account for repeated observations
- Results are robust to controlling for staffing and visit volume
- The parallel trends assumption (required for this analysis) is supported by pre-period data
