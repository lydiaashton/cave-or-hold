# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure is associated with a **modest improvement** in service scores (approximately +2 points, or 4%), but the evidence is **not statistically conclusive**. The board should consider a phased rollout with continued monitoring rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** total: 20 adopted the new procedure, 14 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks** of data (weeks 6–22), with the switch implemented in week 15
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in switched branches against non-switched branches

### **Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 53.8 | 55.1 | **+1.3 points** |
| Non-switched branches | 46.5 | 45.7 | **-0.8 points** |
| **Net effect (DiD)** | | | **+2.1 points** |

### **Statistical Confidence**
- **Estimated effect:** +2.1 points (approximately 4% improvement)
- **95% Confidence Interval:** -0.3 to +4.6 points
- **P-value:** 0.09 (there is a 9% probability this result occurred by chance)
- **Interpretation:** The result is *suggestive* but does not meet the conventional threshold for statistical significance (p < 0.05)

### **Supporting Evidence**
- **Parallel trends confirmed:** Before the switch, both groups showed similar week-to-week patterns, validating the comparison
- **Consistent across models:** The 2-point estimate holds whether or not we control for staffing levels and visitor volume
- **Directionally positive:** Switched branches improved while non-switched branches declined slightly

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Continue monitoring:** Extend data collection for 8–12 more weeks to increase statistical power and determine if the effect strengthens over time

2. **Phased expansion:** Consider rolling out the procedure to an additional 5–10 branches while tracking results, rather than committing all remaining branches at once

3. **Investigate heterogeneity:** Analyze whether certain branch types (e.g., by size, location, or traffic volume) benefit more from the new procedure

4. **Cost-benefit analysis:** Evaluate whether the modest service score improvement justifies the operational costs of system-wide implementation

5. **Set a decision checkpoint:** Reconvene in 2–3 months with additional data to make a final rollout decision

---

## **Conclusion**

The new procedure shows **promise but not proof**. The positive direction of the effect and the magnitude (4% improvement) are encouraging, but the statistical uncertainty means we cannot rule out the possibility that the observed improvement is due to random variation. A cautious, evidence-based approach to expansion is recommended.
