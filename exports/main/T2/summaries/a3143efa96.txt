# **MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure shows a **positive but statistically inconclusive effect** on service scores. Branches that switched to the new procedure improved by an estimated **1.6 points** more than branches that kept the old procedure, but this difference is not statistically significant at conventional levels (p = 0.12). The 95% confidence interval ranges from -0.4 to +3.7 points, meaning we cannot rule out either no effect or a meaningful improvement.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with caution. Consider a phased rollout with continued monitoring rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Study Design
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure; **14 branches** kept the old procedure
- Switch occurred at **week 15**
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in switching branches to changes in non-switching branches

### 2. Service Score Results

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (no switch) | 49.4 | 51.6 | +2.3 |
| Treatment (switched) | 47.9 | 51.8 | +3.9 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+1.6** |

- **Treatment effect:** +1.62 points (95% CI: -0.44 to +3.67)
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.123 (not significant at p < 0.05)
- Both groups improved after week 15, but switching branches improved slightly more

### 3. Validity Checks
- **Parallel trends assumption holds:** Before the switch, there was no statistically significant difference in trends between the two groups (p = 0.66). This supports the validity of the comparison.
- **No effect on staffing or visits:** The new procedure did not significantly affect staffing levels (p = 0.08) or visitor volume (p = 0.64), suggesting the service score improvement is not driven by these factors.

### 4. Visual Trend Analysis
Both groups showed similar fluctuating patterns before week 15. After the switch, the treatment group pulled ahead modestly, but the gap remains within the range of normal variation.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Continue monitoring:** Collect additional weeks of data to increase statistical power. With more post-switch observations, we may be able to detect a significant effect if one exists.

2. **Phased rollout:** Consider expanding the new procedure to additional branches while maintaining a control group for ongoing evaluation.

3. **Qualitative feedback:** Gather input from branch staff and patrons about the new procedure. Service scores are one metric; operational efficiency and user experience matter too.

4. **Cost-benefit analysis:** If the new procedure requires additional resources, weigh the modest (and uncertain) service score gain against implementation costs.

5. **Re-evaluate in 8–12 weeks:** With more data, re-run this analysis to determine whether the effect becomes clearer over time.

- **Method:** Difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch)
- **Outcome:** Service score index (average ~50, higher is better)
- **Software:** Python with statsmodels
- **Data:** 578 observations (34 branches × 17 weeks)
