# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure maintained their service levels while branches using the old procedure experienced a notable decline. The evidence strongly supports rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Measurable Impact on Service Scores**
- The new procedure produced a **statistically significant improvement of +2.26 points** on the service score index (95% confidence interval: 0.95 to 3.56 points; p-value: 0.0009)
- This represents approximately a **4.5% improvement** relative to baseline scores

### **2. What Actually Happened**
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Branches with **new procedure** (90 branches) | 50.30 | 50.56 | +0.26 |
| Branches with **old procedure** (60 branches) | 48.88 | 46.88 | -2.00 |

The new procedure did not dramatically boost scores—it **protected branches from the decline** that affected branches keeping the old procedure.

### **3. Analysis Is Reliable**
- **Parallel trends verified**: Before the switch, both groups followed similar trends (p=0.46), validating the comparison
- **Robust results**: The finding holds even after accounting for staffing levels and branch visit volumes
- **Complete data**: All 150 branches reported scores for all 17 weeks (weeks 6–22); no missing data

### **4. Study Design**
- 150 branches total: 90 adopted the new procedure, 60 kept the old
- Switch occurred at week 15; 9 weeks of before data, 8 weeks of after data
- Difference-in-differences analysis with cluster-robust standard errors

## **Technical Appendix**

The analysis used a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in service scores between branches that switched procedures and those that did not, before and after week 15. Standard errors were clustered at the branch level to account for within-branch correlation. All analyses were conducted in Python using statsmodels.
