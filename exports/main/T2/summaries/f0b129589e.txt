# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

Contrary to expectations that the new shelving and rotation procedure would have no effect, the data shows a **statistically significant improvement** in service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure saw their service scores increase by approximately **1.9 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** switched to the new procedure at week 15; **60 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in treatment branches to changes in control branches

### **Results**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Estimated effect of new procedure** | **+1.91 points** |
| **95% Confidence Interval** | [0.83, 2.99] |
| **Statistical significance** | p < 0.001 |
| **Effect size** | 0.19 standard deviations (3.8% of baseline) |

### **What This Means**
- Treatment branches improved by **1.16 points** after the switch
- Control branches declined by **0.75 points** over the same period
- The net difference (**+1.91 points**) is attributable to the new procedure

### **Validity Checks**
- **Parallel trends confirmed**: Before the switch, treatment and control branches showed similar week-to-week trends (no significant differential pre-trends)
- **Robust to controls**: Results hold when accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume
- **Consistent over time**: Positive effects observed across most post-switch weeks

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with rollout**: The evidence supports expanding the new procedure to all 150 branches. The improvement is both statistically significant and practically meaningful.

2. **Monitor implementation**: Track service scores during rollout to ensure effects replicate at scale.

3. **Investigate mechanisms**: Consider studying *why* the new procedure improved scores (e.g., faster book location, better organization, staff efficiency) to optimize training.

4. **Cost-benefit analysis**: If the new procedure has implementation costs, weigh them against the 1.9-point service score gain to confirm net benefit.
