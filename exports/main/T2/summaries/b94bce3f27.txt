# **MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure is associated with a **modest improvement in service scores** (+2.1 points on average), but the evidence is **not statistically conclusive**. The data suggest the procedure may be beneficial, but we cannot rule out the possibility that the observed improvement is due to random variation. We recommend a cautious, phased rollout with continued monitoring.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect Estimate
- Branches that adopted the new procedure showed an average **2.1-point increase** in service scores compared to branches that kept the old procedure
- This represents approximately a **4% improvement** relative to the baseline average score of 51.3
- **Statistical uncertainty**: The 95% confidence interval ranges from -0.5 to +4.7 points, meaning we cannot confidently exclude the possibility of no effect

### 2. Supporting Evidence
- **Parallel trends validated**: Before the switch (weeks 6-14), treatment and control branches showed similar score trajectories, supporting the validity of our comparison
- **Consistent branch-level patterns**: Among the 20 treatment branches, the median improvement was +4.0 points, compared to +1.8 points for the 14 control branches
- **Effect persists after controls**: The improvement remains when accounting for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume, suggesting it is attributable to the procedure itself rather than other factors

### 3. Study Design
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6-22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure at week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure
- **578 total observations** with complete data

---

## **Next Steps**

### Recommended Actions

1. **Proceed with phased rollout** — The point estimate is positive and the direction of effect favors the new procedure. Given the low risk of implementation, expanding to additional branches is reasonable.

2. **Continue monitoring** — Track service scores for at least 8-12 more weeks to:
   - Increase statistical power and narrow the confidence interval
   - Detect any delayed effects or adaptation patterns
   - Identify branches where the procedure works best or worst

3. **Investigate heterogeneity** — Some treatment branches showed gains of 5-7 points while others showed minimal improvement. Understanding what distinguishes high-performing adopters could inform training and implementation.

4. **Consider cost-benefit analysis** — If the new procedure requires additional staff time or resources, weigh the ~2-point service score gain against implementation costs.

5. **Set a decision checkpoint** — Re-evaluate in 3 months with additional data. If the effect remains around 2 points with improved precision, proceed with full rollout.
