# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence is inconclusive.** While a simple comparison suggests the new procedure improved service scores by approximately 2 points (4%), more rigorous analysis reveals that branches selected for the new procedure were already improving faster than others before the switch. We cannot confidently attribute the observed improvement to the procedure change itself. **We recommend against a full system-wide rollout until further evidence is gathered.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### Data Overview
- **709 branches** analyzed over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- **425 branches** adopted the new procedure at week 15; **284 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Service scores average approximately 52 points (higher is better)

### What the Simple Analysis Shows
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 51.98 | 54.77 | +2.79 |
| Non-switched branches | 50.60 | 51.36 | +0.76 |
| **Difference** | | | **+2.02** |

A basic difference-in-differences analysis estimates a **+2.02 point improvement** (p<0.001), which would represent roughly a 4% gain in service quality.

### Why We Cannot Trust This Result

**The parallel trends assumption is violated.** For a valid comparison, switched and non-switched branches should have been on similar trajectories before the change. They were not:

- In week 6, switched branches scored only **0.8 points higher** than non-switched branches
- By week 14 (just before the switch), this gap had grown to **2.5 points**
- This widening gap continued after the switch, making it impossible to separate the procedure effect from pre-existing differences

When we statistically account for these pre-existing branch-specific trends, the estimated treatment effect **drops to essentially zero (+0.05 points, not statistically significant)**.

### Additional Observations
- Both staffing levels and visit volumes are positively associated with service scores
- Week 16 showed an unusual dip in scores for switched branches, suggesting variability in the post-switch period
- Switched branches had higher baseline scores throughout the study period

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Do not proceed with system-wide rollout** based on current evidence. The risk of investing in a procedure that may not deliver benefits is substantial.

2. **Investigate branch selection criteria.** Understand why certain branches were chosen for the new procedure. Were they higher-performing branches to begin with? Were they selected based on characteristics that also predict improvement?

3. **Consider a randomized controlled trial.** For future procedure changes, randomly assign branches to treatment and control groups to ensure valid causal inference.

4. **Extend the observation period.** Collect additional weeks of data to see if the post-switch patterns stabilize and whether any treatment effect emerges more clearly over time.

5. **Conduct qualitative research.** Interview branch managers at switched vs. non-switched locations to understand implementation challenges, staff perceptions, and operational differences that may explain the quantitative patterns.

6. **Re-evaluate in 8–12 weeks.** With additional data and a clearer understanding of branch selection, revisit this analysis before making a final rollout decision.
