# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

## **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

### **BOTTOM LINE**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure shows a **positive but statistically inconclusive effect** on service scores. While branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately 2 points (about 4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure, this difference is **not statistically significant** at conventional levels. The data does not provide strong enough evidence to confidently recommend a system-wide rollout at this time.

---

### **KEY FINDINGS**

**1. Study Design & Data**
- 34 branches total: 20 adopted the new procedure, 14 kept the old procedure
- 17 weeks of data (Weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring in Week 15
- Complete data with no missing observations (578 total branch-week records)

**2. Main Results**
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Treatment (switched) | 51.4 | 55.1 | +3.8 |
| Control (no switch) | 47.2 | 48.9 | +1.7 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+2.0** |

- **Estimated treatment effect: +2.0 points** on the service score index
- **P-value: 0.108** (not significant at the 5% or 10% level)
- **95% Confidence Interval: -0.4 to +4.5 points**

**3. What This Means**
- The confidence interval includes zero, meaning we cannot rule out that the new procedure has **no effect**
- The true effect could reasonably be anywhere from slightly negative (-0.4) to moderately positive (+4.5)
- The analysis is **underpowered**: with this sample size, we could only reliably detect effects larger than about 2.5 points

**4. Validity Checks**
- **Pre-trends are parallel**: Before the switch, treatment and control branches showed similar trends over time, supporting the validity of the comparison
- Results are robust to controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

---

### **NEXT STEPS**

**Recommended Actions:**

1. **Do NOT proceed with immediate system-wide rollout.** The evidence is insufficient to justify the costs and disruption of changing procedures at all 34 branches.

2. **Extend the evaluation period.** Continue collecting data for at least 8–12 more weeks to increase statistical power. With more post-switch observations, we could detect smaller effects with greater confidence.

3. **Consider a phased rollout with rigorous monitoring.** If the board wishes to proceed despite inconclusive evidence, implement the new procedure at additional branches in stages, with continued data collection to build stronger evidence.

4. **Investigate heterogeneity.** Explore whether the new procedure works better at certain types of branches (e.g., by size, location, or baseline performance) to identify where it might be most effective.

5. **Review cost-benefit tradeoffs.** Even if the 2-point improvement is real, the board should weigh this against implementation costs, staff training requirements, and any operational disruptions.

---

### **ANALYTICAL APPROACH**

This analysis used a **difference-in-differences** design, comparing changes in service scores at branches that adopted the new procedure against changes at branches that did not, before and after Week 15. Standard errors were clustered at the branch level to account for correlation within branches over time. Branch fixed effects were included to control for time-invariant differences between branches.
