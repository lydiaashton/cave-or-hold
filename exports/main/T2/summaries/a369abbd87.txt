# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact Analysis**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new shelving and rotation procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores approximately **2 points higher** than they would have under the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and consistent across multiple analytical approaches. We recommend proceeding with a full rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **709 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **425 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15 (treatment group)
- **284 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- This "natural experiment" design allows us to isolate the effect of the procedure change

### **Main Results**

| Group | Before Switch (weeks 6–14) | After Switch (weeks 15–22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------------|---------------------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 52.0 | 48.9 | −3.1 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 53.0 | 51.9 | −1.1 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+2.0** |

Both groups experienced a decline in service scores after week 15 (possibly due to seasonal factors or external conditions), but branches with the new procedure declined **2 points less** than those without it.

### **Statistical Confidence**
- **Estimated effect: +2.0 points** on the service score index
- **95% confidence interval: +1.5 to +2.5 points**
- **Statistical significance: p < 0.001** (highly significant)
- Results are robust when controlling for branch-specific characteristics, staffing levels, and visit volumes

### **Trend Analysis**
The improvement was not immediate but emerged over the post-switch period:
- Weeks 15–17 showed modest or mixed effects as branches adjusted
- Weeks 18–22 showed consistent positive effects ranging from +2.7 to +4.4 points
- This pattern suggests a learning curve, with benefits growing as staff became familiar with the new procedure

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with full rollout** to all 284 branches still using the old procedure

2. **Provide transition support** during the first 3–4 weeks after switching, as the data shows the full benefit takes time to materialize

3. **Monitor service scores** for the next 8–12 weeks post-rollout to confirm the effect holds at scale

4. **Document best practices** from the 425 early-adopter branches to accelerate the learning curve for remaining branches

5. **Consider investigating** the overall decline in service scores observed across all branches after week 15, as this may indicate external factors affecting the cooperative

## **Methodology Note**

This analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing the change in service scores for branches that switched procedures against those that did not. This method accounts for:
- Pre-existing differences between branches
- Time trends affecting all branches equally
- Branch-specific characteristics (via fixed effects)

Standard errors are clustered at the branch level to account for repeated observations of the same branches over time.
