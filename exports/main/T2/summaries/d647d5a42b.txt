# Library Branch Procedure Change: Service Score Analysis

## Bottom Line

Branches that adopted the new shelving and rotation procedure showed **higher service scores** after the change compared to branches that kept the old procedure. The estimated improvement is **+1.85 points** on the service score index (95% confidence interval: 1.29 to 2.41). However, **important caveats apply** because the switching branches were already improving faster than non-switching branches before the change occurred. We recommend a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.

## Key Findings

### 1. Treatment Effect Estimate
Using a difference-in-differences analysis comparing 425 switching branches to 284 non-switching branches:
- **Control branches** (old procedure): Service scores remained essentially flat (+0.11 points)
- **Treatment branches** (new procedure): Service scores increased by +1.96 points
- **Net effect of new procedure**: **+1.85 points** (statistically significant, p < 0.001)

### 2. Important Caveat: Pre-Existing Trends
The analysis reveals a concern for causal interpretation:
- Switching branches were **already improving faster** than non-switching branches before week 15
- The gap between groups grew steadily from -0.26 points (week 6) to +2.55 points (week 14)
- After the switch, the gap averaged +3.14 points
- This pattern suggests the switching branches may have been on a different trajectory regardless of the procedure change

### 3. Weekly Pattern
| Period | Control Group | Treatment Group | Gap (T-C) |
|--------|--------------|-----------------|-----------|
| Weeks 6-14 (before) | 49.33 | 50.61 | +1.29 |
| Weeks 15-22 (after) | 49.44 | 52.58 | +3.14 |

There is no sharp "jump" at week 15; the gap continued its pre-existing upward trend.

### 4. Context
- Service scores average around 50 across all branches
- A +1.85 point improvement represents approximately a **3.7% increase** relative to the baseline
- The effect is statistically robust but causally uncertain due to pre-trend differences

## Next Steps

1. **Proceed with cautious expansion**: The evidence suggests the new procedure is associated with improved service scores, but the causal link is not definitive.

2. **Implement phased rollout**: Rather than switching all remaining branches at once, consider:
   - Rolling out to additional branches in batches
   - Randomizing which branches switch next to enable cleaner causal inference
   - Allowing 4-8 weeks of observation between batches

3. **Enhanced monitoring**: Track service scores weekly for newly switching branches to detect whether the pattern holds.

4. **Investigate selection bias**: Understand why certain branches were chosen to switch first. If they were higher-performing or better-resourced branches, this could explain the pre-trend differences.

5. **Consider complementary factors**: The analysis controlled for staffing and visit indices, but other unmeasured factors (branch size, community demographics, management quality) may influence both the decision to switch and service scores.
