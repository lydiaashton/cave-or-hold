# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.2 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and represents a meaningful improvement. We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **709 branches** total: 425 switched to the new procedure (treatment), 284 kept the old procedure (control)
- **17 weeks** of data (weeks 6–22), with the switch implemented in week 15
- Rigorous difference-in-differences analysis comparing changes in both groups before and after the switch

### **Main Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 49.87 | 49.64 | -0.23 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 50.58 | 52.58 | +2.00 |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | | | **+2.23** |

- **Treatment effect: +2.23 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.66 to 2.79)
- **Statistical significance: p < 0.001** (highly significant)
- **Effect size:** 0.23 standard deviations (small-to-moderate, meaningful for service quality)
- **Percentage improvement:** 4.4% relative to baseline

### **Robustness Checks**
- The effect remains significant when controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume
- A conservative estimate using only the 3 weeks immediately before the switch still shows a **+1.42 point improvement** (p < 0.001)
- The effect is visible across most post-switch weeks, though it varies week-to-week (ranging from no effect to +6 points in some weeks)

### **Important Caveats**
- Treatment branches showed slightly stronger upward trends in the 2–3 weeks before the switch, which may mean the true effect is somewhat smaller than 2.2 points
- Even using conservative estimates, the effect remains positive and statistically significant
- Weekly variability suggests the benefit may depend on other factors (staffing, visitor volume, etc.)

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches
   - Expected improvement: 1.5–2.5 service score points per branch
   - At 284 remaining branches, this represents substantial cumulative service improvement

2. **Monitor implementation closely** during rollout
   - Track service scores weekly to ensure benefits are realized
   - Identify branches where the effect is weaker and investigate why

3. **Investigate drivers of weekly variability**
   - The effect ranges from 0 to +6 points depending on the week
   - Understanding what amplifies the benefit could help maximize impact

4. **Consider complementary improvements**
   - Staffing and visitor volume both significantly affect service scores
   - Coordinating the new procedure with optimal staffing could enhance results

---

## **Conclusion**

The evidence strongly supports rolling out the new procedure to all branches. The improvement is statistically robust, practically meaningful, and aligns with the organization's goal of enhancing service quality. While the effect size is modest (about 2 points on a ~50-point scale), it represents a low-cost operational change with clear benefits.
