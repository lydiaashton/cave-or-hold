# Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact Analysis

## Bottom Line

**The data does not provide clear evidence that the new shelving and rotation procedure improved service scores.** While branches that adopted the new procedure showed higher service scores after the switch, this appears to reflect pre-existing differences between branches rather than a causal effect of the procedure change. We recommend **against** a system-wide rollout until further evidence is gathered.

## Key Findings

### 1. Basic Comparison Shows Improvement, But Context Matters

At first glance, the numbers look promising:
- **Treated branches** (425 branches that switched): Service scores increased from 51.80 to 55.55 (+3.75 points)
- **Control branches** (284 branches that kept old procedure): Service scores increased from 50.43 to 52.46 (+2.02 points)
- **Simple difference-in-differences**: ~1.7 point improvement attributed to the new procedure

### 2. Critical Issue: Pre-Existing Trends

**The parallel trends assumption is violated.** Before the procedure change (weeks 6-14), treated branches were already improving faster than control branches:
- Treated branches gained approximately **0.27 points per week more** than control branches even before the switch
- The gap between treated and control branches was widening throughout the pre-period

This means the treated branches were on a different trajectory from the start, making it impossible to attribute the post-switch difference to the procedure change alone.

### 3. More Rigorous Analyses Show No Clear Effect

When we account for pre-existing trends using more sophisticated methods:

| Analysis Method | Estimated Effect | Statistical Significance |
|-----------------|------------------|-------------------------|
| Simple DiD | +1.73 points | Significant |
| Two-Way Fixed Effects | +1.71 points | Significant |
| Narrow Window (weeks 10-19) | +0.88 points | Marginal |
| Segmented Regression (level shift) | -0.37 points | **Not significant** |
| First-Difference Model | -0.40 points | **Not significant** |

The more rigorous methods that better account for pre-trends show **no significant improvement** at the time of the switch.

### 4. Post-Switch Pattern

Looking at week-by-week changes after the switch:
- The gap between treated and control branches continued to grow, but at a similar rate to the pre-period
- There was no noticeable "jump" in service scores for treated branches at week 15
- Week-over-week changes were similar for both groups after the switch

## Methodology Notes

- **Data**: 709 branches, 17 weeks (weeks 6-22), 12,053 observations
- **Switch point**: Week 15
- **Statistical approach**: Difference-in-differences with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch)
- **Key concern**: Violation of the parallel trends assumption undermines causal interpretation

## Next Steps

### Recommended Actions:

1. **Do not proceed with system-wide rollout** based on current evidence. The apparent benefits are likely due to pre-existing differences between branches, not the procedure itself.

2. **Investigate why certain branches were selected** for the initial rollout. Were they higher-performing branches to begin with? Were they selected based on characteristics that also predict improvement? Understanding the selection process is critical.

3. **Consider a randomized trial** for future procedure changes. Randomly assigning branches to treatment and control groups would ensure comparable trends and provide credible causal evidence.

4. **Extend the observation period**. Eight weeks of post-switch data is relatively short. Continue monitoring to see if effects emerge over a longer horizon.

5. **Examine heterogeneous effects**. The new procedure may work better for some types of branches (e.g., by size, location, or baseline performance). Subgroup analysis could identify where the procedure is most effective.

6. **Review the service score metric**. Ensure it captures meaningful aspects of branch performance and isn't subject to measurement artifacts that could explain the observed patterns.

### If Proceeding Despite Uncertainty:

If the board decides to proceed with a broader rollout despite the ambiguous evidence:
- Implement **phased rollout** with careful monitoring
- Establish **clear success metrics** and stopping rules
- Continue collecting data to enable ongoing evaluation
- Be prepared to **reverse course** if subsequent analysis shows no benefit
