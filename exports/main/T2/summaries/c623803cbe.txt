# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure is associated with a **modest improvement in service scores** (approximately 2 points on a ~50-point scale, or about 4%). However, this improvement is **not statistically significant** at conventional levels, meaning we cannot rule out that the observed difference occurred by chance. The evidence suggests the procedure may help, but the results are not conclusive enough to mandate a system-wide rollout without further testing.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Overall Impact**
- Branches that adopted the new procedure saw their average service score increase by **1.8 points** after the switch (from 48.7 to 50.5)
- Branches that kept the old procedure saw essentially **no change** (47.6 to 47.5)
- The **difference-in-differences estimate is +1.94 points**, with a 95% confidence interval of **-0.3 to +4.2 points**

### **2. Statistical Significance**
- The p-value is **0.11**, which exceeds the conventional 0.05 threshold for statistical significance
- This means there is an 11% probability of observing this result (or larger) even if the new procedure had no real effect
- The data is **suggestive but not conclusive**

### **3. Variation Across Branches**
The effect was **not uniform** across branches:
- **14 of 20 treatment branches (70%)** improved after switching
- **6 of 20 treatment branches (30%)** actually saw scores decline
- Top performers gained 6–8 points; underperformers lost 2–3.5 points
- By comparison, only 50% of control branches improved during the same period

### **4. Effect Size**
- Cohen's d = **0.57**, indicating a **medium-sized effect**
- This is meaningful from a practical standpoint, even if statistical significance is borderline

### **5. Parallel Trends Check**
- Before the switch, treatment and control groups had similar trends
- Treatment branches averaged 1.1 points higher than control branches before the intervention—a stable baseline difference that the analysis accounts for

---

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended Actions**

1. **Extend the pilot period** – Collect 8–12 more weeks of data to increase statistical power and confirm whether the effect persists

2. **Investigate branch-level variation** – Understand why some branches benefited substantially while others declined. Factors to examine:
   - Branch size and staffing levels
   - Physical layout and shelving configuration
   - Staff training quality and adoption fidelity
   - Local patron demographics and usage patterns

3. **Consider a phased rollout** – Rather than mandating the procedure everywhere, offer it as an option with:
   - Enhanced training and implementation support
   - Clear guidance on which branch types are most likely to benefit

4. **Monitor control variables** – Staffing and visit levels both correlate with service scores. Ensure any rollout accounts for these factors.

5. **Set a decision threshold** – Define in advance what level of improvement (e.g., +2 points sustained over 3 months) would justify full adoption

---

## **Conclusion**

The new procedure shows **promise but not proof**. The board should consider authorizing continued evaluation with expanded data collection and targeted support for branches that struggled during the pilot. A hasty system-wide rollout is not warranted based on current evidence, but neither is abandoning a procedure that appears to help many branches.

---

*Analysis based on 34 branches over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22), with 20 branches adopting the new procedure in week 15 and 14 branches serving as controls. Service scores are an internal index where ~50 represents average performance.*
