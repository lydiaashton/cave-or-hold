# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure is associated with an improvement in service scores of approximately **1.9 points** (about 4% relative to baseline). However, this result is **not statistically significant at conventional levels** when accounting for the panel structure of the data. The evidence suggests a positive effect, but we cannot rule out the possibility of no effect. We recommend a **cautious rollout with continued monitoring**.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in service scores between switching and non-switching branches

### **Results**

| Metric | Switched Branches | Non-Switched Branches |
|--------|-------------------|----------------------|
| Before switch (weeks 6–14) | 45.7 | 49.5 |
| After switch (weeks 15–22) | 48.1 | 50.0 |
| **Change** | **+2.4** | **+0.5** |

**Estimated treatment effect: +1.87 points** (95% CI: -0.4 to +4.1)

### **Statistical Significance**
- Simple regression: p = 0.018 (significant)
- Cluster-robust analysis (accounting for branch-level correlation): p = 0.12 (not significant at 5% level)
- The confidence interval excludes large negative effects but includes zero

### **Additional Observations**
- **Parallel trends assumption appears reasonable**: The gap between treatment and control groups was relatively stable before the switch
- **No significant changes** in staffing index or visits index, suggesting the service score improvement is not driven by these factors
- Switched branches started with lower baseline scores (~4 points lower), but the DiD method accounts for this

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended Actions**

1. **Proceed with phased rollout** to remaining branches, but implement with careful monitoring
   - The point estimate is positive and meaningful (~4% improvement)
   - No evidence of negative effects
   - Lower bound of confidence interval suggests minimal risk

2. **Establish ongoing evaluation framework**
   - Continue tracking service scores for at least 8–12 weeks post-rollout
   - Set up monthly reviews to assess whether improvements persist
   - Target: Confirm sustained improvement of 1.5+ points

3. **Investigate mechanisms**
   - Conduct branch-level interviews to understand *how* the new procedure improves service
   - Identify which branches saw the largest gains and why
   - Document best practices for training

4. **Consider complementary improvements**
   - Since staffing and visit patterns didn't change, the improvement appears to come from the procedure itself
   - Explore whether combining this with staffing optimizations could amplify benefits

### **Decision Framework**

| Scenario | Recommendation |
|----------|----------------|
| Scores continue improving post-rollout | Full adoption, document as best practice |
| Scores plateau at current levels | Maintain new procedure; benefits still justify change |
| Scores decline after initial gain | Re-evaluate procedure; consider branch-specific adaptations |

- Standard errors clustered at branch level to account for within-branch correlation
