# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2025  
**SUBJECT:** Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new rotation and shelving procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **2.05 points** (approximately **4% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is highly statistically significant and consistent across all post-switch weeks. **We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **Study Design**
- **709 branches** total: 425 adopted the new procedure (treatment group), 284 kept the old procedure (control group)
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22), with the switch implemented in week 15
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in service scores before and after the switch between the two groups

### **Results**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Estimated effect of new procedure** | **+2.05 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | 1.50 to 2.60 points |
| Statistical Significance | p < 0.001 (highly significant) |
| Percentage Improvement | ~4% from baseline |
| Effect Size | 0.20 standard deviations |

### **What This Means in Practice**
- Before the switch, treatment group branches averaged **51.2** on the service score index
- After the switch, treatment group branches averaged **52.5**, while control group branches averaged **49.6**
- The improvement is equivalent to moving a branch from the 50th percentile to approximately the 58th percentile of service scores

### **Robustness Checks**
The finding holds across multiple analytical approaches:
- Simple before/after comparison with control group
- Regression with controls for staffing and visit levels
- Branch fixed effects model (controlling for all time-invariant branch characteristics)
- Two-way fixed effects model (controlling for both branch and week effects)

All specifications yield consistent estimates around **2.0–2.1 points** with high statistical significance.

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

### **Recommended Actions**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches
   - The evidence strongly supports the effectiveness of the new procedure
   - Expected improvement: ~2 points on service score index across all branches

2. **Develop implementation timeline**
   - Consider phasing the rollout to manage training and transition
   - Target completion within the next quarter

3. **Continue monitoring**
   - Track service scores post-rollout to confirm effects persist at scale
   - Monitor for any unintended consequences (e.g., staffing demands, workflow disruptions)

4. **Document best practices**
   - Capture lessons learned from the 425 early-adopter branches
   - Create training materials based on successful implementation experiences

### **Questions for Further Investigation**
- Are there branch characteristics (size, location, staffing) that moderate the effect?
- What specific elements of the new procedure drive the improvement?
- Could the procedure be further refined to maximize benefits?

---

**Conclusion:** The data provide clear, statistically robust evidence that the new rotation and shelving procedure improves service quality. The board should proceed with confidence in approving system-wide implementation.
