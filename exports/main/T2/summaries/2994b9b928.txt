# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new shelving and stock rotation procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.0 points** (on a scale where the average branch scores about 50) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents a **4% improvement** and is statistically significant with high confidence. We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **709 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (Weeks 6–22)
- **425 branches** switched to the new procedure in Week 15; **284 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in service scores between the two groups before and after the switch

### **Main Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched Branches | 51.26 | 53.53 | +2.27 |
| Control Branches | 51.00 | 51.22 | +0.22 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+2.05** |

- **Estimated impact: +2.0 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.4 to 2.6 points)
- **Statistical significance: p < 0.001** (highly significant)
- Results hold after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume, and accounting for branch-specific and week-specific factors

### **Effect Over Time**
The event study analysis shows the improvement emerged after the switch and appears to **grow over time**:
- Early post-switch weeks: modest gains (~0.5 points)
- Later post-switch weeks (Weeks 21–22): larger gains (~1.3–1.8 points)

This pattern suggests branches may need a short adjustment period before realizing the full benefits of the new procedure.

### **Additional Insights**
- **Staffing and visits matter:** Both staffing levels and visitor volume are positively associated with service scores. The procedure effect is independent of these factors.
- **Weekly variation is substantial:** Service scores fluctuate considerably week-to-week (standard deviation ~10 points), which is why the control group is essential for isolating the procedure's effect.

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new shelving and stock rotation procedure to all remaining branches.

2. **Monitor implementation closely** during the first 4–6 weeks at each branch, as the data suggests benefits may take time to materialize fully.

3. **Track service scores post-rollout** to confirm the effect persists at scale and identify any branches that may need additional support during the transition.

4. **Consider complementary investments** in staffing, as the analysis shows staffing levels are independently associated with higher service scores.

5. **Document the new procedure** in detail to ensure consistent implementation across all branches.

## **Analytical Notes**

- The analysis accounts for the possibility that switched and non-switched branches may have differed systematically by using branch fixed effects.
- Week fixed effects control for system-wide factors (e.g., holidays, seasonal patterns) that could affect all branches simultaneously.
- The parallel trends assumption (that treatment and control branches would have followed similar trajectories absent the intervention) is reasonably supported, though there is some evidence that switched branches were on a slightly different pre-treatment trajectory. This would tend to *understate* the true effect, making our estimate conservative.
