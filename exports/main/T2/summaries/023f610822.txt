# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **1.8 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.3 to 2.4 points) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents a **3.7% improvement** over baseline scores. The result is statistically significant and robust across multiple analytical approaches.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Study Design
- **709 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **425 branches** switched to the new procedure in week 15; **284 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in switched branches against changes in non-switched branches while controlling for branch-specific characteristics and weekly trends

### 2. Main Result
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Treatment effect (DiD estimate) | **+1.81 points** |
| Standard error | 0.28 |
| 95% Confidence interval | 1.27 to 2.36 |
| P-value | < 0.001 |
| Effect size | 3.7% of baseline |

### 3. What the Numbers Mean
- **Non-switched branches** improved by about **2.0 points** after week 15 (likely due to seasonal or system-wide factors)
- **Switched branches** improved by about **3.8 points** after week 15
- The **additional 1.8-point gain** for switched branches is attributable to the new procedure

### 4. Effect Consistency
The positive effect appears in every week after the switch and shows signs of growing over time:

| Week | Gap (Switched − Non-switched) |
|------|-------------------------------|
| Pre-switch average | 0.5 points |
| Week 15 | 2.0 points |
| Week 18 | 2.0 points |
| Week 22 | 3.1 points |

This pattern suggests the benefits may increase as branches become more familiar with the new procedure.

### 5. Robustness Checks
- Results hold when controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume
- Results hold when accounting for fixed differences between branches
- Pre-switch trends were similar between groups, supporting the validity of the comparison
