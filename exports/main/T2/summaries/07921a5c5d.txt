# **Memorandum to the Board: New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure is associated with a **statistically significant improvement** in service scores of approximately **+2 points** (about 4% above baseline). However, pre-existing trends suggest the true effect may be more modest. We **recommend proceeding with caution**—consider a phased rollout with continued monitoring rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measured Effect
- **Service score increase: +2.07 points** (95% CI: 1.91 to 2.23)
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001 (highly significant)
- **Context:** Average branch scores around 50; the effect represents about one-fifth of a standard deviation

### 2. Study Design
- **709 branches** tracked over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- **425 branches** adopted the new procedure at week 15
- **284 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis used difference-in-differences methodology, controlling for staffing and visit levels

### 3. Important Caveat: Pre-Existing Trends
The analysis detected **diverging trends before the switch** (p = 0.010). Branches that later switched were already showing slightly improving trajectories relative to control branches. This suggests:
- The true causal effect may be **smaller than the 2-point estimate**
- A conservative adjusted estimate ranges from **+0.1 to +2.1 points**

### 4. Post-Switch Pattern
After the switch, the gap between switched and control branches **widened from ~2 points to ~3 points** over the 8-week post-switch period, suggesting the effect may be sustained or even growing.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Phased Rollout:** Rather than immediate system-wide adoption, expand the new procedure to an additional 100–150 branches and monitor for 8–12 weeks.

2. **Refine Selection Criteria:** Investigate whether certain branch characteristics (size, location, staffing levels) predict better outcomes under the new procedure.

3. **Extend Monitoring:** Continue tracking service scores for at least 6 more months to assess whether effects persist, grow, or diminish over time.

4. **Cost-Benefit Analysis:** Quantify the operational costs of the new procedure against the ~2-point service score improvement to determine net value.

5. **Address Pre-Trend Concerns:** Future evaluations should use randomized assignment where feasible to eliminate selection bias concerns.

---

## **Conclusion**

The evidence supports a **positive but modest benefit** from the new procedure. The board should approve continued expansion with rigorous monitoring, rather than an immediate full rollout. The 2-point improvement, while statistically robust, should be weighed against implementation costs and operational disruption.
