# **Memorandum to the Board: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure **produced a statistically significant improvement** in service scores, but the effect is **modest** (+1.6 points, or ~3% improvement). Branches that adopted the new procedure improved relative to those that did not, but they have not yet fully closed the performance gap with non-switching branches. We **recommend proceeding with caution** on system-wide rollout while monitoring implementation quality and allowing more time for branches to realize full benefits.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measurable Positive Effect
- **Effect size:** +1.59 points on the service score index (95% confidence interval: 0.23 to 2.96 points)
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.029 (significant at the 5% level)
- **Relative improvement:** Approximately 3% increase relative to the average service score of 51.6

### 2. Performance Trajectory
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches (n=20) | 50.38 | 51.96 | +1.57 |
| Non-switched branches (n=14) | 52.30 | 52.27 | -0.03 |

Branches that adopted the new procedure showed meaningful improvement, while branches keeping the old procedure remained essentially flat.

### 3. Important Context
- **Pre-existing gap:** Switching branches were underperforming before the change (−1.92 points below control branches on average)
- **Gap narrowed but persists:** After the switch, the gap closed to only −0.31 points, but switching branches still score slightly below non-switching branches
- **Variable week-to-week results:** The post-switch improvement was not consistent across all weeks—some weeks showed strong gains (weeks 15, 21, 22) while others showed temporary declines (weeks 16–20)

### 4. Other Factors
- Staffing levels and visitor volume (the control variables) did not significantly explain service score variation in this analysis
- Branch-specific characteristics account for most of the variation in scores (branch fixed effects explain ~79% of variance)

---

## **Next Steps**

### Recommended Actions:

1. **Proceed with phased rollout** — The evidence supports expanding the new procedure, but do so gradually to ensure quality implementation at each branch

2. **Extend the evaluation period** — The post-switch period covers only 8 weeks. Allow 3–6 months for branches to fully adapt before making final rollout decisions

3. **Investigate implementation variation** — The week-to-week inconsistency suggests some branches may need additional training or support during the transition

4. **Monitor closing branches** — Since switching branches started behind and haven't fully caught up, track whether the gap continues to narrow over time

5. **Consider complementary interventions** — Since branch-level factors drive most score variation, the procedure change alone may not be sufficient for underperforming branches

---

## **Analytical Approach**

This analysis used a **difference-in-differences design** with branch fixed effects and cluster-robust standard errors, which:
- Compares switching branches to non-switching branches
- Accounts for pre-existing differences between branches
- Controls for branch-specific characteristics that don't change over time
- Provides valid statistical inference despite the limited number of branches
