## **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

### **Title: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

### **Bottom Line**

The new procedure is associated with a **2.6-point improvement in service scores** (approximately 5% increase). However, this result is **not statistically significant at conventional levels** (p = 0.10). The evidence suggests a positive effect, but we cannot rule out that the observed improvement may be due to random variation. We recommend a **cautious, phased rollout** with continued monitoring rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. Study Design**
- 34 branches total: 20 adopted the new procedure, 14 kept the old procedure
- Data covers 17 weeks (weeks 6–22); the switch occurred at week 15
- This "natural experiment" design allows us to isolate the effect of the new procedure

**2. Service Score Impact**
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches (n=20) | 48.6 | 52.1 | +3.5 |
| Control branches (n=14) | 50.1 | 51.1 | +0.9 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+2.6** |

- The 2.6-point DiD estimate represents the effect attributable to the new procedure
- 95% confidence interval: **-0.4 to +5.7 points**
- P-value: **0.10** (marginally significant)

**3. Branch-Level Results**
- **70% of switched branches** (14 of 20) showed improvement after adopting the new procedure
- **50% of control branches** (7 of 14) showed improvement over the same period
- This pattern supports the aggregate finding but shows variability across branches

**4. Validity Checks**
- **Parallel trends assumption supported**: No significant difference in trends between groups before the switch (p = 0.82)
- **Results robust to controls**: Adjusting for staffing levels and visitor volume does not change the conclusion
- The analysis design is sound; the uncertainty is due to sample size and natural variation, not methodological flaws

---

### **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with phased rollout**: Given the positive signal and low risk, expand the new procedure to additional branches while continuing to monitor outcomes

2. **Extend the evaluation period**: Collect 8–12 more weeks of data to increase statistical power and reduce uncertainty

3. **Monitor branch-level variation**: Investigate why some branches show larger improvements than others; identify best practices from high-performing adopters

4. **Set a decision checkpoint**: Re-evaluate in 3 months. If the effect remains around 2–3 points with p < 0.05, proceed with full rollout. If the effect diminishes or remains inconclusive, consider refining the procedure before wider adoption

5. **Track implementation fidelity**: Ensure branches are following the new procedure consistently; variation in execution may be masking the true effect

---

### **Analyst's Note**

The 2.6-point improvement is meaningful in practical terms (5% gain on a ~50-point scale), and there is no evidence of harm. The main limitation is statistical precision—we have moderate confidence the effect is positive, but not the high confidence typically required for major policy decisions. A phased approach balances the opportunity for improvement against the risk of premature system-wide change.
