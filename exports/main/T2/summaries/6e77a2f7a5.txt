# **Library Branch Stock Rotation Procedure: Impact Analysis**

## **Bottom Line**

Branches that adopted the new stock rotation procedure showed **service score improvements of approximately 1.7 points** (about 3.3% above baseline) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and appears to grow over time. However, branches that chose to switch were already outperforming others before the change, which introduces some uncertainty about whether the procedure itself caused the improvement or if motivated branches both switched and improved.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **controlled rollout** to additional branches with careful monitoring, rather than an immediate system-wide mandate.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Service Score Improvement
- **Switched branches** improved from 51.8 (before) to 55.6 (after) — a gain of **3.8 points**
- **Non-switched branches** improved from 50.4 (before) to 52.5 (after) — a gain of **2.0 points**
- **Net effect of the new procedure: +1.7 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.0 to 2.4 points)
- This represents approximately a **3.3% improvement** relative to the average service score of 52.7

### 2. Statistical Significance
- The estimated effect is highly statistically significant (p < 0.001)
- Results hold when controlling for week-to-week variations and branch-specific characteristics
- The effect does not appear to be explained by staffing levels or visit volumes

### 3. Effect Grows Over Time
- The benefit of the new procedure appears to **accumulate over time**
- In the first weeks after switching, the excess improvement was about 1.0 point
- By weeks 7-8 after switching, the excess improvement reached 2.4 points
- This suggests branches may need time to fully realize the benefits of the new procedure

### 4. Important Caveat: Pre-Existing Differences
- Branches that chose to switch were **already on a stronger trajectory** before the procedure change
- The performance gap between switched and non-switched branches was widening even before week 15 (from 0.2 points in week 6 to 2.6 points in week 14)
- This suggests **self-selection**: branches that opted to switch may have had more motivated staff, better management, or other advantages
- The true causal effect of the procedure could be smaller than our estimate
