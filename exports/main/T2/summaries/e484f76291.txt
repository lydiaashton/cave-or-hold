# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

## **Subject: Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

### **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new rotation and shelving procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **2.2 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and represents a meaningful improvement in service quality. Contrary to our initial expectation that the change would make no difference, the data shows the new procedure delivers measurable benefits.

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. Study Design**
- 150 branches tracked over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- 90 branches adopted the new procedure starting week 15; 60 branches kept the old procedure
- This "natural experiment" design allows us to isolate the effect of the procedure change

**2. Main Result**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Treatment effect | **+2.19 points** on service score |
| 95% Confidence interval | **[0.98 to 3.41] points** |
| Statistical significance | **p = 0.0004** (highly significant) |
| Relative improvement | **~4% of average score** |

**3. What This Means in Practice**
- Switched branches improved by 2.3 points after the change
- Control branches showed virtually no change (0.1 points)
- The improvement persisted throughout the post-switch period (weeks 15–22)

**4. Validity Checks**
- **Parallel trends confirmed**: Before the switch, both groups followed similar trajectories (no significant pre-trend differences, p = 0.88)
- **Robust across methods**: The 2.2-point effect holds whether we use simple comparisons, control for staffing/visits, or account for branch-specific characteristics
- **No confounding factors**: The effect remains after controlling for weekly staffing and visit patterns

### **Next Steps**

**1. Recommend System-Wide Rollout**
Given the clear positive impact, we recommend extending the new procedure to all 60 branches still using the old method. The expected benefit is approximately +2.2 points on service scores across the cooperative.

**2. Monitor Implementation**
- Track service scores at newly-switched branches to confirm the effect replicates
- Watch for any branches that may need additional support during transition

**3. Investigate Mechanisms**
Consider studying *why* the new procedure works:
- Is it faster shelving? Better stock organization? Improved staff workflow?
- Understanding the mechanism could help optimize the procedure further

**4. Cost-Benefit Analysis**
While we've established the benefit, a follow-up analysis should assess:
- Training costs for remaining branches
- Any temporary disruption during transition
- Net benefit after accounting for implementation costs

---

### **Technical Note**

Analysis used a difference-in-differences regression with branch and week fixed effects, with standard errors clustered at the branch level. This approach controls for time-invariant branch characteristics and common weekly shocks, isolating the causal effect of the procedure change.
