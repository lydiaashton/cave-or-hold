# **Library Cooperative Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new shelving and stock rotation procedure is associated with a statistically significant improvement in service scores.** Contrary to the expectation that the change would make no difference, branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **1.8 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents a meaningful improvement and supports rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect
- **Estimated impact:** +1.8 service score points (95% confidence interval: +0.6 to +3.0)
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.01 (highly significant)
- **Effect size:** Approximately 0.18 standard deviations—a modest but meaningful improvement

### 2. What Happened to Each Group
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| **Switched branches** (90 branches) | 50.8 | 51.5 | **+0.7** |
| **Control branches** (60 branches) | 51.2 | 50.2 | **-1.0** |

The treatment effect of +1.8 points comes from the switched branches improving while the control branches declined slightly over the same period.

### 3. Branch-Level Results
- **62% of switched branches** (56 of 90) saw their service scores improve after adoption
- **Only 37% of control branches** (22 of 60) saw improvement during the same period
- This pattern strongly suggests the procedure change, not random variation, drove the difference

### 4. Visual Evidence
The trend chart (saved as `service_score_trends.png`) shows:
- Both groups tracked similarly before week 15
- After the switch, the treatment group's scores diverged upward relative to the control group

### 5. Important Caveats
- **Pre-trend concerns:** There is some evidence that the groups were not perfectly parallel in the weeks immediately before the switch (weeks 10-13 showed marginally significant differences). This means we cannot rule out that some unobserved factor contributed to the result.
- **Modest effect:** While statistically significant, the 1.8-point improvement is modest in absolute terms. The board should weigh this against any implementation costs.
- **Limited post-switch data:** We only have 8 weeks of post-switch data. Longer-term effects remain unknown.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with rollout** — The evidence supports expanding the new procedure to all branches. The positive effect on service scores, combined with no apparent negative consequences, makes this a low-risk decision.

2. **Monitor implementation** — Track service scores for the next 3-6 months as remaining branches adopt the procedure to confirm the effect holds at scale.

3. **Investigate mechanisms** — Consider studying *why* the new procedure improved scores. Understanding the mechanism could help optimize the procedure further or identify which branches benefit most.

4. **Cost-benefit analysis** — If the new procedure has associated costs (training, time, materials), compare them against the value of a 1.8-point service score improvement to confirm the change is economically justified.

5. **Continue evaluation** — Maintain data collection to detect any long-term effects or unintended consequences as the procedure becomes universal.
