# Analysis of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

## Bottom Line

**The data show a modest improvement in service scores associated with the new procedure, but we cannot confidently conclude the new procedure caused this improvement.** The branches that adopted the new procedure differed systematically from those that didn't, which limits our ability to isolate the true effect. We recommend **additional data collection and a more controlled rollout** before making a system-wide decision.

---

## Key Findings

### 1. Observed Service Score Changes

| Group | Branches | Pre-Switch Avg | Post-Switch Avg | Change |
|-------|----------|----------------|-----------------|--------|
| Treatment (new procedure) | 20 | 51.72 | 52.86 | +1.14 |
| Control (old procedure) | 14 | 53.35 | 52.40 | -0.95 |

**Raw Difference-in-Differences Estimate: +2.09 points**

Treatment branches improved by about 1 point while control branches declined slightly, suggesting a net benefit of approximately 2 service score points.

### 2. Statistical Analysis

- **With controls** (accounting for staffing and visitor volume): The estimated effect is **+2.2 points** (p = 0.09), which is marginally significant but not conclusive.
- **Post-treatment period**: Service scores for treatment branches were higher than control in 5 of 8 weeks after the switch, with the largest gaps in weeks 17-18 (+2.4 and +3.1 points).

### 3. Critical Limitation: Parallel Trends Violation

**This is the most important finding.** For a valid comparison, treatment and control branches should have followed similar trends before the intervention. They did not.

- Pre-intervention differences between groups were **statistically significant** (p < 0.0001)
- Treatment and control branches had systematically different service score patterns in weeks 6-14
- This suggests the two groups of branches may differ in unobserved ways (e.g., location, community demographics, management quality)

**Implication:** The observed +2 point difference may reflect pre-existing branch differences rather than the procedure change itself.

---

## Next Steps

### Immediate Recommendations

1. **Do not proceed with system-wide rollout yet.** The evidence is suggestive but not conclusive.

2. **Extend the observation period.** Collect 8-12 more weeks of data to see if the post-switch pattern persists and stabilizes.

3. **Investigate branch selection.** Understand why certain branches were chosen for the new procedure. Were they systematically different (e.g., higher-performing, better-staffed, different communities)?

### For Future Evaluation

4. **Consider a randomized rollout.** If feasible, randomly assign branches to treatment and control to ensure comparability.

5. **Match branches more carefully.** If randomization isn't possible, pair treatment branches with control branches that have similar pre-intervention characteristics and trends.

6. **Track additional outcomes.** Beyond service scores, monitor operational metrics (e.g., processing time, patron satisfaction, staff workload) to capture the full impact.
