## **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

### **Title: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

### **Bottom Line**

The new procedure shows **positive but not definitively conclusive** evidence of improving service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.6 points (about 6% improvement)** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. However, the statistical evidence is suggestive rather than definitive (p-value ≈ 0.09), meaning we cannot rule out that some of the observed improvement could be due to random variation.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with a cautious, phased rollout while continuing to monitor results. The direction of the effect is favorable and aligns with expectations, but additional data would strengthen confidence in the decision.

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. Treatment Effect Estimate**
- Branches using the new procedure improved their service scores by **2.6 points more** than branches using the old procedure after the switch
- This represents approximately a **6% improvement** relative to baseline service scores
- The 95% confidence interval ranges from **-0.4 to +5.7 points**, indicating the true effect could be slightly negative or as high as 5.7 points

**2. Statistical Significance**
- The result is **marginally significant** (p-value ≈ 0.09)
- This means there is about a 9% probability of observing this result even if the new procedure had no real effect
- While not meeting the conventional 5% threshold, this is still meaningful evidence in favor of the new procedure

**3. Validation of the Analysis**
- **Parallel trends confirmed**: Before the switch, both groups showed similar week-to-week patterns in service scores, supporting the validity of the comparison
- **Control group stability**: Branches that did not switch showed essentially no change in service scores (44.94 before vs. 44.92 after), suggesting no systemwide factors drove the improvement
- **Treatment group improvement**: Switched branches improved from 46.88 to 49.41 after adopting the new procedure

**4. Post-Switch Pattern**
- After the switch, the gap between treatment and control branches was consistently positive in every week, ranging from 2.7 to 7.5 points
- This consistent direction across all post-switch weeks strengthens confidence in the finding

---

### **Next Steps**

1. **Phased Rollout**: Given the positive direction of results, recommend expanding the new procedure to additional branches while continuing to track service scores

2. **Extended Monitoring**: Collect at least 8-12 more weeks of data to increase statistical power and narrow the confidence interval

3. **Branch-Level Analysis**: Investigate whether certain types of branches (e.g., by size, location, or traffic) benefit more from the new procedure to optimize implementation

4. **Cost-Benefit Assessment**: Evaluate whether the operational costs of the new procedure are justified by the ~6% service score improvement

5. **Complementary Metrics**: Monitor whether the service score improvement translates to measurable outcomes like patron satisfaction, circulation, or visit frequency
