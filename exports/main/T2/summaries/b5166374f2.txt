# Board Summary: New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

## Bottom Line

**The new procedure is associated with a statistically significant improvement in service scores of approximately 2.1 points (about 4% relative to the average score of 50).** Based on this analysis, we recommend proceeding with a broader rollout, while continuing to monitor results and address implementation variations across branches.

---

## Key Findings

### 1. Treatment Effect Estimate
Using a difference-in-differences approach comparing 425 branches that adopted the new procedure against 284 branches that kept the old procedure:

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Estimated effect** | +2.12 points |
| **95% Confidence Interval** | [1.58, 2.66] |
| **Statistical significance** | p < 0.001 |
| **Relative improvement** | ~4% above baseline |

The effect is robust to:
- Including branch fixed effects (controlling for time-invariant branch characteristics)
- Adding control variables (staffing and visit indices)
- Using clustered standard errors (accounting for within-branch correlation)

### 2. Service Score Trends

**Treatment branches (switched):**
- Pre-switch average: 51.85
- Post-switch average: 52.52
- Change: +0.67 points

**Control branches (not switched):**
- Pre-switch average: 51.09
- Post-switch average: 49.64
- Change: -1.45 points

The control branches experienced a decline in service scores during the same period, while treatment branches maintained and slightly improved their scores. This suggests the new procedure may have helped branches resist broader downward pressures on service quality.

### 3. Effect Over Time
The treatment effect appears to grow over time in the post-switch period:
- Early post-period (weeks 15-17): Effects are small and not statistically significant
- Later post-period (weeks 18-22): Effects become larger and significant, reaching +2.2 points by week 22

This pattern suggests branches may need time to fully realize the benefits of the new procedure.

### 4. Important Caveat
Our analysis detected some pre-existing differences in trends between treatment and control groups before the switch. While these differences attenuated near the switch date, they introduce some uncertainty into the causal interpretation. The true effect could be somewhat smaller than our point estimate suggests.

## Next Steps

### Immediate Actions
1. **Proceed with rollout** to remaining branches, given the positive and statistically significant results
2. **Develop implementation support** – the growing effect over time suggests branches benefit from experience with the new procedure
3. **Monitor control branches** – investigate why their scores declined and whether they need additional support

### Ongoing Evaluation
1. **Continue data collection** for at least 6-12 more weeks to confirm the effect persists and stabilizes
2. **Track implementation fidelity** – ensure all branches are following the new procedure consistently
3. **Conduct branch-level analysis** to identify which branches benefit most and which may need additional support
4. **Consider a formal randomized rollout** for any future procedure changes to strengthen causal inference

### Cost-Benefit Consideration
The board should weigh the 2.1-point service score improvement against:
- Implementation costs (training, materials, staff time)
- Any operational disruptions during transition
- Long-term maintenance requirements of the new procedure
