# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

## **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

### **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure is associated with a **statistically significant improvement in service scores**. Branches that adopted the new procedure showed an average increase of **+1.94 points** (95% confidence interval: +1.40 to +2.49) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents approximately a **4% improvement** over baseline service levels. The evidence supports rolling out the new procedure to all branches, though we recommend continued monitoring.

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. Positive Treatment Effect**
- Switched branches improved by an average of 1.47 points after adoption (from 50.69 to 52.16)
- Non-switched branches declined slightly by 0.47 points over the same period (from 49.52 to 49.05)
- The difference-in-differences estimate of **+1.94 points** is highly statistically significant (p < 0.001)

**2. Effect Persists After Controlling for Other Factors**
- The treatment effect remains unchanged (+1.94 points) when controlling for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume
- Both staffing and visits independently affect service scores (each 1-point increase in these indices is associated with ~0.4 point service score improvement), but they do not explain the procedure effect

**3. Effect Grows Over Time**
- Event study analysis shows the treatment effect accelerated after implementation
- In the weeks immediately following the switch, the effect was approximately +2.5 points
- By weeks 7-8 post-switch, the effect reached +3.3 to +3.4 points, suggesting benefits may compound with experience

**4. Broad Adoption Already Underway**
- 425 branches (60%) adopted the new procedure
- 284 branches (40%) retained the old procedure, providing a valid comparison group

---

### **Important Caveat**

Our analysis identified **pre-existing trends** that warrant attention. Switched branches were already showing gradual improvement relative to non-switched branches in the 4-5 weeks *before* the procedure change. This suggests:

- Branches selected for early adoption may have had more capacity for improvement
- The true causal effect of the procedure may be somewhat smaller than our main estimate (potentially +1.3 points rather than +1.9 points)

However, the **acceleration in improvement after week 15** is clear and statistically significant, indicating the procedure change did contribute meaningfully to service quality.

---

### **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** — The evidence supports expanding the new procedure to remaining branches, with expected service score improvements of 1.5 to 2.0 points per branch.

2. **Monitor implementation quality** — Since benefits appear to grow over time, provide ongoing training and support to ensure branches fully realize the procedure's potential.

3. **Track long-term outcomes** — Continue collecting service score data for at least 3-6 months post-rollout to confirm effects persist and identify any branches needing additional support.

4. **Investigate selection factors** — Review why certain branches were chosen for early adoption to ensure equitable implementation going forward.

5. **Consider complementary investments** — Since staffing and visitor volume also affect service scores, coordinate procedure rollout with appropriate resource allocation.
