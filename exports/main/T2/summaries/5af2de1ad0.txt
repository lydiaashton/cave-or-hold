# **Board Summary: New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.3 points** (about **5% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and consistent across the post-switch period. We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **150 branches** total: 90 adopted the new procedure (treatment), 60 kept the old procedure (control)
- **17 weeks of data**: Weeks 6–14 before the switch, Weeks 15–22 after
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in treatment branches against changes in control branches

### **Main Result**
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 48.8 | 51.0 | +2.2 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 48.1 | 52.6 | +4.5 |
| **Effect of new procedure** | | | **+2.3** |

- The **2.3-point improvement** is statistically significant (p < 0.001)
- **95% confidence interval**: 1.1 to 3.7 points
- This represents roughly a **5% improvement** over the baseline service score of ~48

### **Supporting Evidence**

1. **Clear timing of the effect**: Before the switch (weeks 6–14), treatment branches actually scored slightly *lower* than control branches (average difference: -0.8 points). After the switch (weeks 15–22), treatment branches consistently scored *higher* (average difference: +1.6 points).

2. **Similar pre-trends**: Both groups showed comparable trends before the switch, supporting the validity of the comparison.

3. **Robust to controls**: The effect holds after accounting for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume, and after controlling for inherent differences between individual branches.

4. **Consistent post-switch performance**: The treatment group outperformed the control group in all 8 weeks after the switch, with the gap widening over time (from +0.4 points in week 15 to +2.6 points in week 22).

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to the remaining 60 branches.

2. **Monitor implementation**: Track service scores during rollout to ensure the effect replicates at other branches. Expect similar ~2-point improvements.

3. **Document the procedure**: Capture the specific practices that distinguish the new procedure to ensure consistent implementation across all branches.

4. **Consider complementary improvements**: Since staffing and visit volume also correlate with service scores (both statistically significant in our models), branches may benefit from pairing this procedure change with targeted staffing adjustments during high-traffic periods.

5. **Follow-up evaluation**: Re-assess service scores 3–6 months after full rollout to confirm sustained improvement and identify any branches needing additional support.
