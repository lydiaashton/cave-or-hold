# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

## **Title: Impact of New Shelving Procedure on Branch Service Scores**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new shelving procedure improved service scores by approximately 1.8 points (3.5% improvement), and this result is statistically significant.** We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout of the new procedure.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **150 branches** participated in the evaluation: 90 branches adopted the new procedure (treatment group) and 60 branches continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Data covers **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22), with the procedure change implemented at week 15
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, which isolates the effect of the new procedure by comparing changes in the treatment group against changes in the control group

### Main Results
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect of new procedure | **+1.76 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | **0.66 to 2.87 points** |
| Statistical significance | **p = 0.002** |
| Relative improvement | **~3.5%** (from baseline of 50) |

### Supporting Evidence
1. **Parallel trends validated**: Before the switch (weeks 6–14), both groups showed similar trends in service scores, confirming the groups were comparable before the intervention.

2. **Consistent positive effects**: The treatment group outperformed the control group in **all 8 weeks** after the switch, with weekly differences ranging from +0.5 to +1.9 points.

3. **Balanced groups**: Pre-switch staffing and visit levels were nearly identical between treatment and control branches, reducing concerns about selection bias.

4. **Robust to controls**: The result holds when accounting for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new shelving procedure to all remaining branches.

2. **Monitor implementation** during the rollout to ensure branches receive adequate training and support during the transition.

3. **Continue tracking service scores** for the next 8–12 weeks post-rollout to confirm the effect persists at scale.

4. **Document the new procedure** as a best practice and incorporate it into branch operations training materials.

5. **Consider cost-benefit analysis** if there are any additional costs associated with the new procedure (e.g., staff time, equipment) to quantify the return on investment.
