# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new stock rotation and shelving procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **2.1 points** (95% CI: 1.4 to 2.9) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents a **4% improvement** over baseline performance and is statistically significant (p < 0.001). We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **709 branches** total: 425 switched to the new procedure (treatment group), 284 kept the old procedure (control group)
- Data covers **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in treatment branches to changes in control branches

### **Main Result**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Treatment effect | **+2.13 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | [1.38, 2.87] |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 |
| Effect size | 0.21 standard deviations |

### **Supporting Evidence**

1. **Groups were comparable before the switch.** In the pre-switch period (weeks 6–14), treatment and control branches had nearly identical service scores (difference of only 0.10 points, p = 0.70). This validates our comparison.

2. **The effect emerged after the switch.** After week 15, treatment branches consistently outperformed control branches by an average of 2.2 points.

3. **The effect appears to grow over time.** The improvement was larger in weeks 19–22 (+2.6 points) than in weeks 15–18 (+1.6 points), suggesting branches may benefit more as they gain experience with the new procedure.

4. **Results are robust.** The effect remains unchanged when controlling for staffing levels and visit volumes, indicating the improvement is attributable to the procedure itself, not other factors.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout.** The evidence strongly supports expanding the new procedure to all 709 branches.

2. **Plan for implementation support.** Since the effect appears to strengthen over time, provide adequate training and support during the first 4–8 weeks after each branch switches.

3. **Monitor ongoing performance.** Continue tracking service scores after rollout to confirm the effect persists at scale and identify any branches that may need additional support.

4. **Consider cost-benefit analysis.** While we've established the procedure improves service quality, leadership may want to weigh the 4% improvement against any implementation costs.

---

## **Methodology Note**

This analysis uses a quasi-experimental difference-in-differences design, which is the standard approach for evaluating policy changes when random assignment isn't feasible. The key assumption—that treatment and control branches would have followed similar trends absent the intervention—is supported by the pre-period data showing no meaningful differences between groups before the switch.
