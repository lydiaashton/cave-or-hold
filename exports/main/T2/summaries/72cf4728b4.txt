# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Contrary to the initial expectation that the change would make no difference, branches that adopted the new procedure saw their service scores increase by approximately **2.0 points** (about 4% relative to the average score of 51.9) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is **statistically significant** (p = 0.002), meaning it is very unlikely to be due to random chance.

**Recommendation:** The evidence supports rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Study Design
- **150 branches** total: 90 adopted the new procedure (treatment group), 60 kept the old procedure (control group)
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Analysis used a **difference-in-differences** approach, which compares changes over time between treatment and control groups while accounting for underlying trends

### 2. Treatment Effect
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect on service score | **+2.03 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | [0.77, 3.28] |
| Statistical significance | p = 0.002 |
| Effect size (relative to mean) | +3.9% |

The confidence interval tells us we can be 95% confident the true effect lies between +0.77 and +3.28 points—and critically, the entire range is **positive**, indicating a genuine improvement.

### 3. Service Score Trends

| Period | Control Branches | Treatment Branches |
|--------|------------------|-------------------|
| Before switch (weeks 6–14) | 51.58 | 53.07 |
| After switch (weeks 15–22) | 49.08 | 52.57 |
| **Change** | **-2.50** | **-0.50** |

Both groups saw scores decline after week 14 (possibly due to seasonal factors), but treatment branches declined **much less** than control branches. The difference-in-differences estimate of +2.03 points captures this relative improvement.

### 4. Robustness
- Results hold when controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume
- Standard errors are clustered at the branch level to account for repeated measurements
- The effect is consistent across the post-switch period

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches, given the clear positive impact on service scores.

2. **Monitor implementation** during the rollout to ensure branches achieve similar gains. Track service scores for the first 8–12 weeks after each branch switches.

3. **Investigate mechanisms**: Consider studying *why* the new procedure works—whether through better stock availability, faster retrieval times, or other factors—to optimize future process changes.

4. **Cost-benefit analysis**: If there are any additional costs associated with the new procedure (training, equipment, etc.), weigh them against the 4% service score improvement to confirm net benefit.

5. **Continue evaluation**: Maintain data collection on service scores to verify the effect persists over time and doesn't diminish as branches become accustomed to the new procedure.
