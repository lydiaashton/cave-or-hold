# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact Analysis**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new shelving and rotation procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2 points** (about 4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and not explained by changes in staffing or visitor volume. We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect
- **2.05 point increase** in service score for branches that switched to the new procedure
- 95% confidence interval: **0.3 to 3.8 points**
- Statistically significant (p = 0.02)
- Effect represents approximately **4% improvement** relative to the average baseline score of ~50

### 2. Study Design
- **34 branches** total: 20 adopted the new procedure (treatment), 14 kept the old procedure (control)
- **17 weeks of data**: Weeks 6–14 (before switch), Weeks 15–22 (after switch)
- Analysis controls for branch-specific characteristics, staffing levels, and visitor volume
- The control variables (staffing and visits) did not change differently between groups, confirming the effect is attributable to the procedure change itself

### 3. Pre-Trends Check
Before the switch, treatment and control branches showed similar week-to-week variation in service scores with no systematic divergence. This supports the validity of the comparison.

### 4. Effect Over Time
The improvement appeared immediately after the switch and persisted through the 8-week post-switch period, though with some week-to-week variation (which is normal for service metrics).

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches
2. **Monitor implementation** closely during the first 4–8 weeks at each branch to ensure proper adoption
3. **Track service scores** post-rollout to confirm the effect replicates at scale
4. **Document the procedure** thoroughly to support training at new branches
5. **Consider a follow-up evaluation** in 6 months to assess whether the improvement is sustained long-term

---

## **Technical Notes**

- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach with branch fixed effects
- Standard errors account for the panel structure of the data
- The model controls for staffing_index and visits_index to isolate the procedure effect
- Data covers weeks 6–22, with the procedure switch occurring at week 15
