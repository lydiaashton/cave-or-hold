# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure is associated with a **statistically significant improvement in service scores of approximately 2 points** (95% CI: 1.4 to 2.5). This represents a meaningful but modest gain—roughly 4% of the average service score. However, branches that adopted the new procedure were already showing stronger performance trends before the switch, which introduces uncertainty about how much of the improvement is due to the procedure itself versus pre-existing differences between branches.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with a cautious, phased rollout while implementing stronger evaluation methods to confirm the causal impact.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect Estimate
- **Difference-in-differences analysis** shows the new procedure increased service scores by **+1.94 points** (p < 0.001)
- This effect remains consistent after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume
- The 95% confidence interval ranges from **1.4 to 2.5 points**, indicating the effect is unlikely to be due to chance

### 2. Practical Significance
- A 2-point increase moves a typical branch from the 50th percentile to approximately the 54th percentile of service scores
- The effect size is about **0.19 standard deviations**—a small-to-moderate improvement
- For context: the average service score is 50.3 (SD = 10.4), ranging from 14.5 to 85.7

### 3. Trends Over Time
| Period | Switched Branches | Non-Switched Branches | Gap |
|--------|-------------------|----------------------|-----|
| Pre-switch (weeks 6-14) | 50.75 | 48.85 | 1.91 |
| Post-switch (weeks 15-22) | 52.14 | 48.27 | 3.87 |
| **Change** | **+1.39** | **-0.58** | **+1.96** |

### 4. Important Caveat: Pre-Existing Trends
- Switched branches were **already improving faster** than non-switched branches before the procedure change
- The gap between groups widened from 0.2 points (week 6) to 2.6 points (week 14) during the pre-switch period
- This suggests branches selected for the new procedure may have had underlying advantages (e.g., better management, more engaged staff) that also drove improvement
- **The true causal effect may be smaller than our 2-point estimate**

### 5. Control Variables
- Both staffing levels and visitor volume positively affect service scores (as expected)
- The treatment effect persists even after accounting for these factors, suggesting the procedure itself contributes to improvement

## **Next Steps**

### Immediate Actions
1. **Approve phased rollout** to additional branches, prioritizing those with capacity to implement the procedure properly
2. **Document implementation requirements** clearly—branches that were early adopters may have had advantages beyond the procedure itself
3. **Establish monitoring dashboard** to track service scores at newly converted branches

### Evaluation Improvements
4. **Extend observation period**—8 weeks post-switch is relatively short; longer-term data will clarify whether effects persist
5. **Consider randomized rollout** for remaining branches to eliminate selection bias in future evaluations
6. **Collect qualitative feedback** from staff about what aspects of the new procedure are most helpful (or problematic)

### Risk Mitigation
7. **Set realistic expectations**—communicate that a ~2 point improvement is the likely best case, not a guaranteed outcome
8. **Monitor for unintended consequences**—track staffing costs and any changes in operational efficiency
9. **Establish a review checkpoint** at 6 months to reassess whether the rollout should continue, expand, or be modified

## **Methodology Note**

This analysis used a **difference-in-differences approach** comparing 425 branches that adopted the new procedure to 284 branches that did not, across 17 weeks of observations (weeks 6-22). Standard errors were clustered at the branch level to account for repeated observations. The pre-trend concern noted above is a standard limitation in quasi-experimental studies where treatment assignment is not randomized.
