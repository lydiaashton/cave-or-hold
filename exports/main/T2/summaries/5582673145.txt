# **Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure Evaluation**

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure is associated with a **modest improvement in service scores** (+1.9 points, approximately 4% above baseline), but the evidence is **not statistically conclusive**. While 70% of switched branches showed improvement compared to 50% of control branches, we cannot rule out that the observed effect is due to chance. **We recommend a cautious, phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Overall Effect**
- **Estimated improvement**: +1.94 service score points (95% confidence interval: -0.3 to +4.2)
- **Statistical significance**: p = 0.09 (marginally significant; does not meet the conventional 0.05 threshold)
- **Practical significance**: Approximately 4% improvement relative to the baseline average score of 48.3

### **2. Branch-Level Results**
| Metric | Switched Branches | Control Branches |
|--------|-------------------|------------------|
| Number of branches | 20 | 14 |
| % showing improvement | 70% | 50% |
| Average before switch | 48.7 | 47.6 |
| Average after switch | 50.6 | 47.5 |

### **3. Statistical Approach**
We used a **difference-in-differences analysis**, which compares the change in service scores for switched branches against the change for non-switched branches over the same period. This method controls for:
- Overall time trends affecting all branches
- Pre-existing differences between branches

Standard errors were clustered at the branch level to account for repeated weekly observations.

### **4. Important Caveats**
- **Pre-existing differences**: Some switched branches were already performing differently before the procedure change, which introduces uncertainty into the estimates
- **Limited post-switch data**: We have only 8 weeks of data after the switch (weeks 15-22)
- **Wide confidence interval**: The true effect could range from slightly negative (-0.3) to meaningfully positive (+4.2)

---

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended Actions**

1. **Continue the pilot for 8-12 more weeks** to gather additional post-switch data and narrow the confidence interval

2. **Expand to a second cohort** of 10-15 additional branches on a trial basis, randomly selected, to strengthen the evidence

3. **Monitor implementation fidelity** – ensure all switched branches are consistently applying the new procedure

4. **Track secondary metrics** – analyze whether the procedure affects staffing efficiency or visit patterns, not just service scores

5. **Set a decision threshold** – recommend proceeding with full rollout if:
   - The effect remains positive AND
   - Statistical significance reaches p < 0.05 OR
   - The lower bound of the 95% confidence interval exceeds zero

### **Timeline**
- **Weeks 23-30**: Continue current pilot, collect additional data
- **Week 31**: Re-analyze with expanded dataset
- **Week 32**: Board decision on system-wide rollout

---

## **Conclusion**

The new procedure shows **promise but not proof**. The direction of the effect is positive and aligns with expectations, but the statistical evidence is not yet strong enough to justify the cost and disruption of a system-wide rollout. A measured, evidence-based approach will ensure we make the right decision for the cooperative.
