# Analysis of New Shelving Procedure on Branch Service Scores

## Bottom Line

**The new shelving procedure improved service scores by approximately 1.8 points (about 3.5% relative to the average score of 50). This effect is statistically significant (p=0.024), meaning we can be confident the improvement is real and not due to random variation.**

We recommend proceeding with a full rollout of the new procedure to all branches.

## Key Findings

### 1. Treatment Effect Estimate
Using a difference-in-differences analysis (comparing branches that switched to those that didn't, before and after week 15):

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated improvement | **+1.78 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | [0.24, 3.33] |
| Statistical significance | p = 0.024 |
| Relative to average score | **+3.5%** |

### 2. Validation of Analysis Method
- **Parallel trends assumption confirmed**: Before the switch (weeks 6-14), the treated and control branches showed no statistically significant difference in their service score trends (p=0.92). This validates our comparison approach.
- **Baseline differences**: Switched branches started slightly lower (51.20 vs 51.89), but this was controlled for in the analysis.

### 3. Effect Over Time
The improvement was not uniform across all post-switch weeks:
- **Strongest effects**: Weeks 16, 20, and 21 showed the largest gains (+4.0, +3.4, and +4.2 points respectively, all statistically significant)
- **Weaker effects**: Weeks 15, 17, 18, 19, and 22 showed smaller or non-significant effects
- This variability is expected in weekly data and does not undermine the overall positive finding

### 4. Other Factors
- **Staffing and visits matter**: Both staffing_index and visits_index significantly predict service scores, confirming our service score captures real operational conditions
- The treatment effect remains stable (+1.78 points) even after controlling for these factors

## Next Steps

1. **Proceed with full rollout**: The evidence supports expanding the new procedure to all 150 branches. The expected system-wide improvement is approximately 1.8 points on the service score index.

2. **Monitor implementation**: Track service scores during rollout to ensure the effect persists at scale. Pay particular attention to the first 2-3 weeks after each branch switches, as effects may take time to materialize.

3. **Investigate variability**: The week-to-week variation in effects suggests some branches or time periods may benefit more than others. Consider analyzing which branch characteristics (size, location, staffing levels) correlate with larger improvements.

4. **Cost-benefit analysis**: If the new procedure has implementation costs, compare them against the value of a 1.8-point service score improvement to confirm the business case.
