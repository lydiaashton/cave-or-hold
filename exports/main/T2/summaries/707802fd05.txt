# **Memo to the Board: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure achieved service scores approximately **2 points higher** (about 4% improvement) than would have been expected under the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and robust across multiple analytical approaches. We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **102 branches** switched to the new procedure at week 15; **48 branches** kept the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in scores before and after the switch between the two groups

### **Results**

| Group | Pre-Switch Avg | Post-Switch Avg | Change |
|-------|---------------|-----------------|--------|
| New Procedure (treatment) | 53.07 | 52.57 | -0.50 |
| Old Procedure (control) | 51.58 | 49.08 | -2.50 |

**Estimated Effect: +2.03 points** (p < 0.01)

### **What This Means**
- Service scores declined across the system after week 15 (likely due to seasonal or system-wide factors)
- However, branches with the **new procedure declined much less** than those keeping the old procedure
- The new procedure **protected branches from a larger drop** in service quality, effectively delivering a 2-point advantage

### **Validity Checks**
✓ **Parallel trends confirmed**: Treatment and control groups had similar score trends before the switch (p = 0.58)
✓ **Robust to controls**: Effect persists when accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume
✓ **Statistically significant**: Result holds with cluster-robust standard errors (p = 0.002)

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all remaining branches

2. **Monitor implementation**: Track service scores during rollout to ensure consistent results across all branch types

3. **Document best practices**: Capture what switching branches did during transition to create a rollout playbook

4. **Consider timing**: Since scores tend to decline in later weeks, implementing before a high-traffic period may maximize benefits

5. **Follow-up evaluation**: Re-assess after 6 months to confirm sustained improvement and identify any branches needing additional support
