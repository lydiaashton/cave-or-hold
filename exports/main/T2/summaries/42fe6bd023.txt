## **Library Branch Procedure Change: Service Score Impact Analysis**

### **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and rotation procedure shows a **small positive effect** on service scores (+1.7 points, or ~3.4%), but this improvement is **not statistically significant**. We cannot confidently conclude the new procedure improves service. The board should **not proceed with a full rollout** without additional evidence.

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. Treatment Effect Estimate**
- Using a difference-in-differences analysis (comparing switched branches to non-switched branches, before and after week 15), the new procedure is associated with a **+1.7 point increase** in service scores
- However, the **95% confidence interval ranges from -0.1 to +3.5 points**, meaning the true effect could be zero or even slightly negative
- The p-value (~0.08–0.15) exceeds the conventional 0.05 threshold for statistical significance

**2. Effect Size Context**
- The estimated improvement represents only **0.18 standard deviations**—a small effect by social science standards
- Service scores average around 50 points; a 1.7-point gain is modest relative to natural week-to-week variation

**3. Mixed Results Across Branches**
- Among the 20 branches that switched: **exactly half (10) improved** and **half (10) declined**
- This inconsistency suggests the procedure may work well in some contexts but not others, or that observed changes reflect random variation

**4. Study Design Strengths**
- Pre-trend analysis confirms switched and non-switched branches had **parallel trends before the change**, validating our comparison approach
- We controlled for staffing and visit levels, which did not materially change the results

---

### **Next Steps**

1. **Do not mandate system-wide rollout** based on current evidence—the effect is too uncertain to justify the cost and disruption

2. **Extend the observation period**: Collect 8–12 more weeks of data to increase statistical power and determine if effects strengthen over time

3. **Investigate heterogeneity**: Analyze which branch characteristics (size, location, staffing levels) predict better outcomes under the new procedure; a targeted rollout may be more effective

4. **Consider qualitative feedback**: Survey branch staff about implementation challenges and perceived benefits; quantitative scores may not capture all relevant impacts

5. **If proceeding cautiously**: Offer the new procedure as an optional pilot to additional branches, with rigorous monitoring and a clear evaluation timeline
