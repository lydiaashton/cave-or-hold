# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new shelving and rotation procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new procedure saw their service scores increase by approximately **2 points** (a 4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and has grown stronger over time. We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **709 branches** analyzed over 17 weeks (Weeks 6–22)
- **425 branches** adopted the new procedure starting Week 15; **284 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in the switching branches against changes in the non-switching branches

### **Main Result**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated improvement from new procedure | **+2.05 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | [1.71, 2.40] |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 |
| Relative improvement | **4.0%** above baseline |

### **Supporting Evidence**
1. **Effect is consistent across all post-switch weeks** — The improvement was positive and statistically significant in every week after the switch (Weeks 15–22)

2. **Effect is growing over time** — The benefit started at ~1.4–1.5 points in Weeks 17–19 and increased to ~2.4–2.9 points by Weeks 20–22, suggesting branches improve with familiarity

3. **Effect is robust** — The result holds after controlling for:
   - Branch-specific characteristics (fixed effects)
   - Week-to-week fluctuations (time fixed effects)
   - Staffing levels and visitor volume

4. **No negative side effects detected** — The improvement in service scores was not offset by changes in staffing or visit patterns

### **Important Caveat**
The treatment and control groups showed some divergence in their service score trends *before* the switch (the gap between groups was gradually widening in the pre-period). While our statistical models account for this, it introduces some uncertainty. However, the sharp and sustained improvement after Week 15, combined with the growing effect over time, strongly suggests the procedure change is the cause.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** — The evidence supports expanding the new procedure to all 709 branches

2. **Monitor implementation** — Track service scores during rollout to ensure the effect replicates at remaining branches

3. **Document best practices** — Since the effect grows over time, create guidance to help branches accelerate their learning curve with the new procedure

4. **Consider targeted support** — Branches with higher visitor volumes showed slightly larger benefits; ensure high-traffic branches receive adequate training resources

5. **Continue evaluation** — Collect data for an additional 8–12 weeks post-rollout to confirm the effect stabilizes at the expected level
