# **Library Cooperative Procedure Switch: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **2.1 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents a **4.3% improvement** and is statistically significant. We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **709 branches** total: 425 switched to the new procedure, 284 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks** of data (Weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring in Week 15
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in switched branches against changes in non-switched branches

### Main Results

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 51.2 | 55.1 | +3.9 |
| Non-switched branches | 49.1 | 50.8 | +1.8 |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | | **+2.1** |

- The **2.1-point improvement** is highly statistically significant (p < 0.001)
- The effect remains consistent when controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume
- **85% of switched branches** saw their scores improve, compared to only **68% of non-switched branches**

### Why We're Confident in This Result

1. **Parallel trends before the switch**: In the 9 weeks before the procedure change, both groups showed similar week-to-week patterns, suggesting they were on comparable trajectories.

2. **Robust across specifications**: The 2.1-point effect holds whether we use simple comparisons or sophisticated statistical models with branch and week controls.

3. **Broad-based improvement**: The vast majority of switched branches benefited, not just a few outliers.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to the remaining 284 branches

2. **Monitor implementation**: Track service scores during rollout to ensure consistent results across all branches

3. **Document best practices**: Since 15% of switched branches did not see improvement, investigate what distinguished the high performers to support struggling branches during rollout

4. **Consider complementary investments**: Staffing and visitor volume both positively affect service scores (independent of the procedure). Branches with adequate staffing may see even larger gains from the new procedure.
