# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact Analysis**

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and rotation procedure is associated with a **2.2-point improvement in service scores** (approximately 4% relative to the baseline of 50). However, this effect is **not statistically significant** at conventional confidence levels. The evidence is suggestive but not conclusive. We recommend a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure
- This creates a natural experiment allowing us to isolate the effect of the procedure change

### **Results**

| Group | Pre-Switch Score | Post-Switch Score | Change |
|-------|-----------------|-------------------|--------|
| New Procedure (20 branches) | 51.78 | 52.80 | +1.02 |
| Old Procedure (14 branches) | 52.05 | 50.90 | -1.15 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+2.17** |

### **Statistical Assessment**
- **Point estimate:** +2.2 points (4.3% improvement)
- **Statistical significance:** p-value ≈ 0.13–0.28 (not significant at 5% level)
- **95% confidence interval:** approximately -0.8 to +5.0 points
- **Pre-trend check:** ✓ Passed — both groups had parallel trends before the switch (p=0.95)

### **Interpretation**
1. **Positive direction:** Branches that switched showed score improvements while control branches declined
2. **Limited statistical power:** With only 34 branches, we cannot rule out that the true effect is zero
3. **Practical significance:** If the effect is real, a 2-point improvement represents meaningful service enhancement
4. **No negative effects:** There is no evidence the new procedure harmed service quality

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended Actions**

1. **Proceed with cautious expansion**
   - Roll out the new procedure to additional branches in phases
   - Continue collecting weekly service scores to build a larger evidence base

2. **Extend the evaluation period**
   - Monitor for at least 8–12 more weeks to improve statistical precision
   - Track whether effects persist, grow, or diminish over time

3. **Investigate heterogeneity**
   - Some branches may benefit more than others based on size, staffing, or patron volume
   - Identify characteristics of "high responders" to target rollout strategically

4. **Consider cost-benefit analysis**
   - Weigh the 2-point service improvement against implementation costs
   - Survey branch staff about operational impacts (time savings, workflow changes)

5. **Set a decision checkpoint**
   - Re-evaluate in 3 months with expanded data
   - Define success criteria in advance (e.g., "proceed if effect remains positive and p < 0.10")
