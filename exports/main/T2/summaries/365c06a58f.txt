# Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores

## Bottom Line

The new shelving and rotation procedure is associated with a **modest improvement** in service scores (+1.6 points, or about 3%), but the evidence is **not statistically conclusive**. The effect is positive and consistent with expectations, but we cannot rule out that it occurred by chance. We recommend a **cautious rollout with continued monitoring**.

---

## Key Findings

### 1. Effect Size and Direction
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect of new procedure | **+1.57 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | -0.55 to +3.70 points |
| Statistical significance (p-value) | 0.157 |
| Effect relative to average score | +3.2% |

The new procedure is associated with higher service scores, but the confidence interval includes zero, meaning we cannot be certain the effect is real rather than random variation.

### 2. Branch-Level Patterns
- **18 of 20 switched branches (90%)** showed improvement after adopting the new procedure
- **11 of 14 control branches (79%)** also improved over the same period
- This suggests a general upward trend in service scores, with switched branches improving slightly more

### 3. Pre-Switch Trends Support Validity
Before the switch (weeks 6-14), both groups followed similar patterns, with switched branches averaging only 0.7 points lower than control branches. This supports the validity of the comparison.

### 4. No Confounding Factors Detected
The new procedure did not significantly affect staffing levels or visit volumes, suggesting any service score improvement is attributable to the procedure itself rather than other operational changes.

---

## Next Steps

### Recommended Actions

1. **Proceed with phased rollout** — The positive direction of the effect, combined with no detected downsides, supports expanding the procedure to remaining branches, but do so gradually.

2. **Extend the evaluation period** — Eight weeks of post-switch data may be insufficient to detect a modest effect. Continue collecting data for at least 8-12 more weeks to increase statistical power.

3. **Monitor branch-level variation** — Two switched branches actually saw scores decline. Investigate whether implementation quality, branch size, or local factors explain this variation.

4. **Set a decision threshold** — If after 12 more weeks the effect remains below +1 point or the confidence interval still includes zero, reconsider whether the operational burden of the new procedure justifies the benefit.

5. **Document implementation fidelity** — Ensure all branches are following the new procedure consistently; variation in implementation could be masking a larger true effect.

## Technical Notes

- **Method**: Difference-in-differences analysis comparing switched vs. non-switched branches before and after week 15
- **Data**: 34 branches, 17 weeks (weeks 6-22), 578 observations
- **Controls**: Adjusted for staffing and visit index fluctuations
- **Standard errors**: Clustered by branch to account for repeated measures
