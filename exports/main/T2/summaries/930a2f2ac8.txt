# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

## **Title: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **2.0 points** (approximately 4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and consistent across the post-switch period. We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **709 branches** analyzed over 17 weeks (Weeks 6–22)
- **425 branches** switched to the new procedure at Week 15 (treatment group)
- **284 branches** kept the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, which isolates the effect of the new procedure by comparing changes in the treatment group to changes in the control group

### Results

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 48.7 | 52.0 | +3.3 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 50.3 | 55.6 | +5.3 |
| **Effect of new procedure** | | | **+2.0** |

- The **2.0-point improvement** is attributable to the new procedure itself, not other factors like seasonal trends (which affected both groups equally)
- **Statistical confidence:** 95% confidence interval is [1.5, 2.6] points; p-value < 0.001
- The effect remains significant after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### Visual Evidence

![Service Score Trends](service_score_trends.png)

The chart shows that both groups followed similar trends before Week 15. After the switch, treatment branches pulled ahead, with the gap widening in the post-switch period.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to the remaining 284 branches

2. **Monitor implementation** over the next 8–12 weeks to ensure the effect holds at scale and identify any branches that may need additional support during transition

3. **Document best practices** from the 425 early-adopter branches to create training materials for the full rollout

4. **Consider complementary improvements** — since staffing and visitor volume also correlate with service scores (both statistically significant in our model), evaluate whether combining the new procedure with targeted staffing adjustments could yield additional gains

5. **Schedule follow-up evaluation** in 6 months to assess long-term sustainability of the improvement and any unintended consequences

---

## **Technical Notes**

- Analysis used difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch) to account for repeated observations
- The parallel trends assumption appears reasonable: treatment and control groups followed similar trajectories before the switch
- Average service score across all branches is approximately 52 points; the 2-point gain represents meaningful improvement in service quality
