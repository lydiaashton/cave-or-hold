# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and rotation procedure is associated with a **modest improvement in service scores of approximately 2 points** (on a scale where the average branch scores about 50). However, this effect is **not statistically significant at conventional levels** (p ≈ 0.09), meaning we cannot rule out that the observed improvement occurred by chance. The evidence is suggestive but not conclusive.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** were tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in the treatment group to changes in the control group

### **Results**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated treatment effect | **+1.9 to +2.2 points** |
| Statistical significance | **p = 0.085** (marginally significant) |
| 95% Confidence interval | **−0.3 to +4.6 points** |

### **What This Means**
- Branches using the new procedure saw service scores increase by about **2 points more** than branches using the old procedure
- The effect is in the **positive direction** (higher scores are better)
- However, the confidence interval includes zero, so we **cannot be certain** the new procedure caused the improvement

### **Parallel Trends Check** ✓
- Before the switch (weeks 6–14), the treatment and control groups showed **stable, parallel trends** in service scores
- This supports the validity of the difference-in-differences approach
- No evidence that branches selected for the new procedure were on different trajectories beforehand

### **Event Study Insights**
- No significant differences between groups in any pre-switch week
- Post-switch, **week 16** showed a statistically significant spike (+3.6 points, p=0.038)
- Other post-switch weeks showed positive but non-significant effects
- This pattern suggests the effect may take time to materialize or vary week-to-week

---

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended Actions**

1. **Proceed with Cautious Optimism**
   - The point estimate is positive and meaningful (~4% improvement relative to baseline)
   - The lack of strong statistical significance may reflect limited sample size (34 branches) rather than absence of effect
   - No evidence of harm from the new procedure

2. **Continue Monitoring**
   - Extend data collection for additional weeks to increase statistical power
   - Track whether the effect persists or grows over time

3. **Consider a Phased Rollout**
   - Implement the new procedure in additional branches
   - Use a staggered rollout to continue gathering evidence while expanding benefits

4. **Investigate Heterogeneity**
   - Analyze whether certain branch types (size, location, staffing) benefit more from the new procedure
   - This could inform targeted implementation

5. **Cost-Benefit Assessment**
   - Evaluate implementation costs of the new procedure against the ~2-point service score gain
   - Consider whether the improvement justifies organization-wide adoption

- Results are robust to controlling for staffing and visitor traffic indices
- The parallel trends assumption appears satisfied based on pre-period analysis
