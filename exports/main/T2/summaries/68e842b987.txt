# **Analysis of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure is associated with a statistically significant improvement in service scores of approximately 2 points (95% CI: 1.3 to 2.8 points), representing about a 4% increase over the baseline average.** However, branches that adopted the new procedure were already outperforming control branches before the switch, which introduces uncertainty about how much of the improvement is truly attributable to the procedure itself. **We recommend a cautious, phased rollout with continued monitoring.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **709 branches** analyzed over **17 weeks** (Weeks 6–22)
- **425 branches** switched to the new procedure at Week 15 (treatment group)
- **284 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Service scores average ~51 points (higher is better)

### **Main Results**

| Group | Pre-Switch Average | Post-Switch Average | Change |
|-------|-------------------|---------------------|--------|
| Treatment (switched) | 51.32 | 52.20 | +0.88 |
| Control (not switched) | 50.50 | 49.35 | −1.15 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+2.03** |

The **2.03-point improvement** is:
- **Statistically significant** (p < 0.001)
- **Precisely estimated** (95% confidence interval: 1.31 to 2.75 points)
- **Meaningful in context** (~4% of baseline score)

### **Important Caveat: Pre-Existing Trends**

Branches that switched to the new procedure were **already improving relative to control branches before the switch**. An event study analysis shows:

- **Pre-switch weeks (6–13)**: Treatment branches averaged ~0.9 points higher than expected
- **Post-switch weeks (15–22)**: Treatment branches averaged ~3.0 points higher than expected

This suggests the 2.03-point DiD estimate captures the **additional improvement** after the switch, above and beyond the pre-existing upward trend. The parallel trends assumption required for clean causal inference is not fully satisfied.

### **Supporting Factors**
- The improvement is **consistent across all post-switch weeks** (effects range from 2.0 to 3.7 points)
- Effects **grow over time**, suggesting possible learning or cumulative benefits
- Results hold when controlling for staffing levels and visit volumes

---

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended Actions**

1. **Proceed with phased rollout** to remaining branches, but implement in waves to enable continued evaluation

2. **Establish ongoing monitoring**:
   - Track service scores for newly switched branches
   - Compare against a rotating control group if feasible
   - Monitor for at least 8–12 weeks post-switch per branch

3. **Investigate heterogeneity**:
   - Identify which branch types benefit most (size, location, volume)
   - Understand why some branches were early adopters and whether their characteristics predict success

4. **Consider complementary factors**:
   - The control group's scores declined post-switch (−1.15 points); investigate whether external factors affected them
   - Examine whether staffing or visit changes mediated the effect

5. **Set realistic expectations**:
   - Communicate that the 2-point improvement is the best estimate, but actual effects may range from 1 to 3 points
   - Emphasize that early-adopter branches may have had unmeasured advantages

### **Timeline**
- **Weeks 1–4**: Prepare rollout plan and monitoring dashboard
- **Weeks 5–12**: First wave of rollout (100–150 branches)
- **Weeks 13–20**: Evaluate first wave; adjust if needed
- **Weeks 21+**: Complete rollout to remaining branches
