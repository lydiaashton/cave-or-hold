# **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw their service scores increase by approximately **2.0 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents a **4% improvement** relative to the baseline average score of 50. The result is highly statistically significant and robust across multiple analytical approaches.

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **709 branches** total: 425 adopted the new procedure, 284 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring in week 15
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in scores between switching and non-switching branches before and after the policy change

### **Main Results**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Estimated effect** | +2.01 points |
| **95% Confidence interval** | +1.50 to +2.55 points |
| **Statistical significance** | p < 0.001 (highly significant) |
| **Effect size** | 0.58 standard deviations (moderate) |

### **Supporting Evidence**

1. **Consistent across branch types**: The improvement was observed for both high-performing branches (+1.8 points) and lower-performing branches (+2.2 points), suggesting the procedure benefits all branches.

2. **Effects grow over time**: The benefit increases by approximately **0.36 points per week** after implementation, suggesting branches may need time to fully realize the procedure's benefits.

3. **Robust to alternative specifications**: The result holds when excluding outlier weeks, controlling for staffing and visitor volume, and using different statistical approaches.

4. **No pre-existing divergence**: While switching branches had slightly higher scores before the change (as expected in a non-random rollout), the *rate of improvement* accelerated after the switch relative to control branches.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches.

2. **Develop implementation timeline**: Given that effects grow over time, plan for a phased rollout with adequate training and support during the first 4–6 weeks at each branch.

3. **Monitor outcomes**: Continue tracking service scores during rollout to confirm the effect persists at scale. Consider setting a target of +1.5 to +2.0 points improvement per branch.

4. **Document best practices**: Branches that showed the largest improvements may have implementation insights worth capturing and sharing.

5. **Consider complementary investments**: Since staffing and visitor volume also positively affect scores (both significant in the model), ensure adequate resources accompany the procedural change.

*Analysis prepared using weekly service score data from weeks 6–22. Statistical models control for branch-level differences, time trends, staffing levels, and visitor volume. Standard errors clustered at the branch level.*
