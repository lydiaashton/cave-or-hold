# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores that were, on average, **2.0 points higher** than they would have been under the old procedure. This represents a **3.6% improvement** and is statistically significant (p < 0.001). We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **150 branches** total: 90 adopted the new procedure, 60 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks** of data (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring in week 15
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in switched branches to changes in non-switched branches

### **Main Result**
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 55.24 | 54.11 | −1.12 |
| Non-switched branches | 53.12 | 50.01 | −3.11 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+1.98** |

The DiD estimate of **+1.98 points** (95% CI: 1.23 to 2.74) is the causal effect of the new procedure.

### **Why Both Groups Declined**
Service scores fell for all branches after week 15 (−1.1 for switched, −3.1 for non-switched). This likely reflects seasonal patterns or other system-wide factors. The key finding is that switched branches **declined significantly less** than non-switched branches—the new procedure appears to have buffered against this downturn.

### **Validity Checks**
- **Parallel trends confirmed**: Pre-switch trends were statistically indistinguishable between groups (p = 0.74), supporting the validity of the comparison
- **Consistent effects**: The positive effect appears in every post-switch week (weeks 15–22), with most weeks showing statistically significant improvements
- **Robust to controls**: Results hold when accounting for branch-specific characteristics, week effects, staffing levels, and visit volumes

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with rollout**: The evidence supports expanding the new procedure to all 60 remaining branches

2. **Monitor implementation**: Track service scores during rollout to ensure effects replicate at scale

3. **Investigate mechanisms**: Consider studying *why* the new procedure works (e.g., faster restocking, better organization, reduced staff time on shelving)

4. **Cost-benefit analysis**: If the new procedure has implementation costs, weigh them against the 3.6% service improvement

5. **Longer-term follow-up**: Continue monitoring to confirm effects persist beyond the initial 8-week post-switch period
