# **Board Summary: Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **1.9 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant and represents approximately a **4% gain** relative to the typical branch score of 50. We recommend proceeding with a cooperative-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **709 branches** total: 425 adopted the new procedure (treatment group), 284 kept the old procedure (control group)
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in the treatment group to changes in the control group

### **Main Result**
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 49.6 | 53.5 | +3.9 |
| Non-switched branches | 48.9 | 50.9 | +2.0 |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | | **+1.9** |

- **Effect size:** +1.92 service score points (95% confidence interval: 1.38 to 2.46)
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001 (highly significant)
- **Practical significance:** ~4% improvement on a scale where 50 is average

### **Effect Over Time**
The improvement was sustained across all post-switch weeks:
- Week 15 (first week after switch): +1.6 points
- Weeks 16–17: +5.0 to +5.8 points (peak effect)
- Weeks 18–22: +2.1 to +4.1 points (stabilized)

The effect did not fade over the 8-week post-switch observation period.

### **Robustness**
- Results hold after controlling for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume
- Standard errors account for correlation within branches over time
- The control group also improved slightly (+2.0 points), suggesting some system-wide factors were at play, but the treatment group improved significantly more

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with cooperative-wide rollout** of the new procedure to remaining branches, given the clear positive impact on service scores.

2. **Monitor implementation quality** during rollout. The variation in effect size across post-switch weeks (ranging from +1.6 to +5.8 points) suggests that how well branches implement the procedure may matter. Consider:
   - Providing implementation guidance or training materials
   - Identifying high-performing switched branches as models for others

3. **Track long-term effects.** Our data covers only 8 weeks post-switch. Continue monitoring to ensure:
   - Effects are sustained beyond the initial implementation period
   - No unintended consequences emerge (e.g., staff burden, cost increases)

4. **Investigate mechanisms.** The analysis shows *that* the procedure works, but not *why*. Consider qualitative follow-up with branch managers to understand:
   - Which aspects of the new procedure drive improvements
   - Whether certain branch characteristics (size, location, staffing) moderate the effect

5. **Cost-benefit analysis.** Before full rollout, quantify any additional costs (training, equipment, staff time) associated with the new procedure to confirm the net benefit is positive.

## **Analytical Notes**

- Service scores are an internally calculated index where ~50 represents the average branch; higher scores indicate better service
- The analysis controls for staffing levels and visitor volume, which also affect service scores
- Pre-existing differences between switched and non-switched branches were accounted for in the difference-in-differences design
- All statistical tests use cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch)
