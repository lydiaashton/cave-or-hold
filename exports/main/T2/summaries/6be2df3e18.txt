# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new shelving and stock rotation procedure significantly improved service scores.** Contrary to the expectation that the change would make no difference, branches that adopted the new procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **2.0 points** (approximately 4%) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and robust. **We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect
- **Estimated impact:** +1.99 service score points (95% confidence interval: 1.0 to 3.0)
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001 (highly significant)
- **Practical significance:** Represents a ~4% improvement from the baseline average of 51 points

### 2. Study Design & Validity
- **Method:** Difference-in-Differences analysis comparing 90 switched branches to 60 non-switched branches
- **Data:** 2,550 observations across 150 branches over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- **Parallel trends assumption:** **Satisfied** — treatment and control branches showed statistically indistinguishable trends before the switch (p = 0.95), supporting the validity of our causal estimate

### 3. Service Score Patterns
| Period | Control Branches | Switched Branches | Gap |
|--------|-----------------|-------------------|-----|
| Before switch (weeks 6–14) | 50.2 | 51.7 | 1.4 |
| After switch (weeks 15–22) | 50.1 | 53.5 | 3.4 |

The gap between switched and non-switched branches widened noticeably after week 15, when the new procedure took effect.

### 4. Additional Insights
- **Staffing and visits matter:** Higher staffing levels and branch visits are both associated with better service scores (both p < 0.05), suggesting these factors should be monitored during any rollout
- **Consistent effect:** The improvement appears sustained across the 8-week post-switch period, with no signs of the effect diminishing over time

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with rollout** — The evidence strongly supports expanding the new procedure to all branches

2. **Monitor implementation** — Track service scores during rollout to ensure the effect replicates at scale

3. **Consider staffing interactions** — Since staffing levels affect service scores, ensure adequate staffing during the transition period

4. **Long-term evaluation** — Continue monitoring to confirm the effect persists beyond the initial 8-week post-switch window and to identify any branches that may need additional support

5. **Document best practices** — Interview high-performing switched branches to capture any implementation insights that could help other branches maximize the benefit
