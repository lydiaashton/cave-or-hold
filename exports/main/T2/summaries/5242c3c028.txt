# **MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** [Current Date]  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Shelving Procedure on Branch Service Scores

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**Recommend the new procedure for system-wide rollout.** Branches that adopted the new shelving and stock rotation procedure experienced a statistically significant improvement in service scores of approximately **2.1 points** (about 4% above baseline). This effect is robust, highly significant (p < 0.001), and represents a meaningful enhancement to patron service quality.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### Study Design
- **709 branches** analyzed over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- **425 branches** adopted the new procedure (treatment group)
- **284 branches** retained the old procedure (control group)
- Procedure switch occurred at **week 15**

### Impact on Service Scores

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 49.70 | 50.44 | +0.74 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 50.36 | 53.19 | +2.82 |
| **Net Treatment Effect** | | | **+2.09** |

### Statistical Confidence
- **Treatment effect:** +2.09 points
- **95% Confidence Interval:** [1.52, 2.66]
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001
- Results hold after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume
- Results hold when accounting for fixed differences between individual branches

### Context
- Average service score across all branches is approximately 50
- The 2.1-point improvement represents roughly a **4% gain** in service quality
- Control branches showed modest improvement (+0.74), likely due to seasonal factors or system-wide initiatives
- Treatment branches improved nearly **4 times more** than control branches

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new shelving and stock rotation procedure to all 284 branches currently using the old procedure.

2. **Monitor implementation** over the next 8–12 weeks to ensure:
   - Service score gains are sustained
   - All branches achieve similar improvements
   - No unintended operational burdens emerge

3. **Document best practices** from the 425 early-adopter branches to support training and smooth transition for remaining branches.

4. **Consider complementary investments** in staffing, as our analysis shows staffing levels also positively affect service scores (independent of the procedure change).

---

## **ANALYTICAL NOTES**

*This analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in service scores between branches that switched procedures and those that did not, before and after week 15. The estimation controls for branch-specific characteristics, weekly staffing levels, and visitor volume. Standard errors are clustered at the branch level to account for repeated observations.*
