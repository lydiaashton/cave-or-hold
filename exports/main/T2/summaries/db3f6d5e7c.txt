# Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact Analysis

## Bottom Line

The new shelving and rotation procedure shows a **positive but statistically uncertain effect** on service scores. Branches that switched saw an average improvement of **1.6 points** (about 3% of the typical score), but this result is not statistically significant at conventional levels. The 95% confidence interval ranges from -0.4 to +3.6 points, meaning we cannot rule out either no effect or a modest benefit. **We recommend a cautious, phased rollout with continued monitoring rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.**

---

## Key Findings

### 1. Treatment Effect Estimate
- **Point estimate:** +1.61 points on the service score index
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.11 (not significant at the 0.05 level)
- **95% confidence interval:** [-0.36, +3.58]
- **Effect size:** Approximately 0.14 standard deviations, or 3% of the average score

### 2. What the Data Shows
| Period | Switched Branches | Non-Switched Branches | Difference |
|--------|-------------------|----------------------|------------|
| Before (Weeks 6-14) | 50.38 | 52.30 | -1.92 |
| After (Weeks 15-22) | 51.96 | 52.27 | -0.31 |
| **Change** | **+1.57** | **-0.03** | **+1.61** |

The switched branches improved by 1.6 points while non-switched branches remained essentially flat, yielding the difference-in-differences estimate of +1.61.

### 3. Weekly Pattern After the Switch
The effect was not consistent week-to-week:
- **Weeks 15, 21-22:** Switched branches outperformed control branches
- **Weeks 16-20:** Switched branches underperformed control branches

This variability suggests the effect may still be stabilizing, or that other factors (seasonal patterns, staffing, etc.) are influencing scores.

### 4. Important Caveats
- **Parallel trends concern:** There is some evidence (p = 0.09) that switched branches were on a slightly different trajectory than control branches even before the switch, which complicates causal interpretation.
- **Sample size:** With 34 branches over 17 weeks, the study has moderate statistical power. A larger or longer study would provide more definitive results.
- **Short observation window:** We only have 8 weeks of post-switch data. Effects may evolve over time.

---

## Next Steps

### Recommended Actions

1. **Continue the pilot for 8-12 more weeks** to gather additional data and observe whether the effect stabilizes or grows.

2. **Monitor switched branches closely** for any operational challenges or staff feedback that might explain the week-to-week variability.

3. **Consider a staggered rollout** to remaining branches, treating each new cohort as an additional opportunity to validate the effect.

4. **Track secondary metrics** (staffing index, visits index) alongside service scores to understand the full impact of the procedure change.

5. **Set a decision threshold:** If the effect remains around +1.5 points with narrowing confidence intervals after additional data collection, the benefit may justify system-wide adoption given the likely low implementation costs.

### Decision Framework

| Scenario | Recommendation |
|----------|----------------|
| Effect becomes statistically significant (p < 0.05) with continued positive point estimate | Proceed with full rollout |
| Effect remains positive but uncertain (as now) | Continue phased rollout with monitoring |
| Effect turns negative or confidence interval shifts below zero | Halt rollout and investigate causes |

---

*Analysis based on 578 weekly observations from 34 branches (20 switched, 14 control) over weeks 6-22. Standard errors clustered at the branch level to account for within-branch correlation.*
