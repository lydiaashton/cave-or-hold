# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new shelving and stock rotation procedure significantly improved service scores.** Contrary to the initial expectation that the change would make no difference, branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **2.15 points** (approximately **4.3% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and robust across multiple analytical approaches.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measurable Improvement in Service Quality
- **Treatment effect:** +2.15 points on the service score index (95% confidence interval: 0.92 to 3.38)
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001 (highly significant)
- **Relative improvement:** Approximately 4.3% above baseline scores

### 2. Strong Evidence of Causal Impact
The analysis used a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing:
- **90 treatment branches** that switched to the new procedure at week 15
- **60 control branches** that continued with the old procedure

This design isolates the effect of the procedure change from other factors affecting all branches (like seasonal variations or system-wide changes).

### 3. Parallel Trends Validated
A critical assumption for this analysis is that treatment and control branches were on similar trajectories before the switch. The data confirms this:
- **Pre-switch period (weeks 6-14):** No statistically significant differences in trends between the two groups
- **Post-switch period (weeks 15-22):** Treatment branches consistently outperformed control branches

### 4. Effect Is Sustained
The improvement was not a one-time spike. Treatment branches showed elevated service scores across all 8 weeks after the switch, with the effect remaining positive throughout the observation period.

### 5. Robust to Controls
The result holds even after accounting for other weekly factors:
- Staffing levels (staffing_index)
- Visitor volume (visits_index)

This suggests the improvement is attributable to the procedure change itself, not confounding factors.
