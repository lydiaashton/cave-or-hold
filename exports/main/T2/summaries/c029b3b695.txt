# **Library Cooperative Stock Rotation Procedure: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new stock rotation and shelving procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **2.25 points** (approximately 4.5%) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant (p < 0.001). Contrary to the expectation that the change would make no difference, the evidence supports rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure; **60 branches** kept the old procedure (control group)
- The switch occurred at **week 15**
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in the treatment group to changes in the control group

### Results

| Group | Pre-Switch Average | Post-Switch Average | Change |
|-------|-------------------|---------------------|--------|
| Control (Old Procedure) | 49.65 | 50.12 | +0.46 |
| Treatment (New Procedure) | 50.45 | 53.11 | +2.66 |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | — | — | **+2.25** |

### Statistical Evidence
- **Effect size:** +2.25 points on the service score index
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001 (highly significant)
- **95% confidence interval:** 1.0 to 3.5 points
- **Parallel trends validated:** Pre-switch trends were statistically similar between groups (p = 0.78), supporting the validity of the comparison

### Additional Insights
- The new procedure's effect remained significant after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume
- Both staffing and visits positively correlate with service scores, but the procedure effect is independent of these factors
- The improvement emerged consistently after week 15 and persisted through the observation period
