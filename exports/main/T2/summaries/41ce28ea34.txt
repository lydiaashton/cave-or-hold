# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores approximately **2.15 points higher** than they would have under the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and represents about a **4% improvement** relative to the average branch score. We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Study Design
- **709 branches** total: 425 adopted the new procedure (treatment group), 284 kept the old procedure (control group)
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22), with the procedure switch occurring at week 15
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, which compares changes in the treatment group to changes in the control group

### 2. Main Result: Positive Effect on Service Scores
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect of new procedure | **+2.15 points** |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 (highly significant) |
| 95% Confidence Interval | [1.59, 2.70] points |
| Relative improvement | ~4.1% of average score |

### 3. Important Context: The New Procedure Mitigated a Decline
Both groups experienced a drop in service scores after week 15, but branches using the new procedure declined **less**:
- **Control branches (old procedure)**: Service scores fell by ~3.0 points
- **Treatment branches (new procedure)**: Service scores fell by only ~0.8 points

The new procedure appears to have **protected branches from a broader decline** in service quality, rather than producing an absolute increase.

### 4. Parallel Trends Assumption Holds
Before the switch (weeks 6–14), treatment and control branches had nearly identical service score trends (average difference of only 0.03 points). This validates our analytical approach and increases confidence in the results.

### 5. Effect Is Robust
The estimated effect remains stable (2.15 points) even after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume, suggesting the improvement is attributable to the procedure itself.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to the remaining 284 branches.

2. **Monitor implementation** to ensure the new procedure is being followed consistently across all branches.

3. **Investigate the post-week-15 decline** affecting all branches. Something external (seasonal factors, system-wide changes, etc.) appears to have reduced service scores across the cooperative. Understanding this could help preserve the gains from the new procedure.

4. **Continue tracking** service scores for the next 8–12 weeks after full rollout to confirm the effect persists at scale.

5. **Consider cost-benefit analysis** if the new procedure requires additional resources, though a 4% service score improvement is likely to justify modest implementation costs.

---

*Analysis based on weekly branch data from weeks 6–22, using difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors.*
