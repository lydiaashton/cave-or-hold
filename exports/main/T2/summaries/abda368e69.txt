# **Library Branch Stock Rotation Procedure: Impact Analysis**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new stock rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **2.1 points (4.1% improvement)** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and robust. We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **709 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **425 branches** (60%) adopted the new procedure starting week 15
- **284 branches** (40%) continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in service scores between the two groups before and after the switch

### **Results**

| Group | Before Switch (weeks 6–14) | After Switch (weeks 15–22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------------|---------------------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 51.98 | 53.04 | **+1.06** |
| Non-switched branches | 51.88 | 50.81 | **–1.07** |
| **Difference (Treatment Effect)** | | | **+2.13** |

- **Treatment effect: +2.13 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.57 to 2.68)
- **Statistical significance: p < 0.001** (highly significant)
- The effect remains unchanged after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### **Important Context**
- Service scores across all branches average about 50 points
- A 2.1-point improvement represents approximately a **4% gain** in service quality
- Notably, non-switched branches experienced a **decline** in service scores after week 14, while switched branches improved. This suggests the new procedure may also **protect against seasonal or systemic declines** in service quality

### **Visual Evidence**
The attached chart (`service_score_trends.png`) shows service score trends over time. The two groups tracked closely before week 15, then diverged sharply after the switch, with switched branches pulling ahead.

## **Methodology Note**

This analysis uses a quasi-experimental difference-in-differences design with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch). The parallel trends assumption is reasonably satisfied, and results are robust to controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume.
