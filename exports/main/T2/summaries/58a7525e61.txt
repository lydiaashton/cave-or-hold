# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new shelving and rotation procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **2.1 points** (approximately 4%) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and the analysis supports rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect
- **Estimated impact: +2.13 service score points** (95% confidence interval: +0.98 to +3.28)
- **Statistical significance: p = 0.0003** (highly significant)
- The effect represents a **4.2% improvement** relative to the average baseline score of 50.7

### 2. What Happened to Each Group
| Group | Pre-Switch Avg | Post-Switch Avg | Change |
|-------|---------------|-----------------|--------|
| **Switched branches** (90 branches) | 50.5 | 51.0 | +0.5 |
| **Non-switched branches** (60 branches) | 51.3 | 49.7 | -1.6 |

The difference-in-differences approach isolates the procedure effect by accounting for the fact that *all* branches experienced a general decline after week 15 (possibly due to seasonal factors). The switched branches not only avoided this decline but actually improved.

### 3. Validity Checks
- **Parallel trends assumption holds:** Before the switch (weeks 6-14), there was no significant difference in trends between the two groups (p = 0.20), supporting the validity of the comparison.
- **Robust to controls:** The result holds when accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume.
- **Consistent across specifications:** Results are similar whether using simple averages or models with branch-level fixed effects.

### 4. Visual Evidence
The attached chart (`did_analysis_chart.png`) shows that:
- Both groups followed similar patterns before week 15
- After the switch, treatment branch scores diverged positively from control branches
- The gap widened and remained through the post-switch period

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all 60 branches currently using the old method.

2. **Monitor implementation:** Track service scores for the next 8-12 weeks after rollout to confirm the effect persists at scale.

3. **Document best practices:** Work with the 90 switched branches to capture what made their implementation successful, and share with remaining branches.

4. **Consider cost-benefit:** If the new procedure has any additional costs (training, materials, time), weigh them against the 4% service score improvement to confirm net benefit.

5. **Long-term evaluation:** Re-assess in 6 months to ensure the effect is sustained and identify any branches that may need additional support.

---

## **Methodology Note**

This analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in service scores before and after week 15 between branches that switched procedures (treatment group: 90 branches) and those that did not (control group: 60 branches). The model includes branch fixed effects and clustered standard errors to account for repeated observations. Data covers weeks 6-22 with 2,550 branch-week observations.
