# **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure is associated with a **modest improvement in service scores** (+2.5 points, or approximately 5%), but the evidence is **not statistically conclusive**. The board should consider a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Estimated Impact**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Treatment effect | **+2.45 points** on service score |
| Baseline score | ~50 points |
| Percentage improvement | **~5%** |
| Statistical significance | p = 0.12 (not significant at conventional levels) |
| 95% Confidence Interval | **-0.6 to +5.5 points** |

### **2. Study Design**
- **34 branches** total: 20 switched to new procedure, 14 kept old procedure
- **17 weeks** of data (weeks 6–22), with switch occurring at week 15
- Analysis uses **difference-in-differences** method, comparing changes in switched branches to non-switched branches

### **3. Trend Evidence**
- **Pre-switch (weeks 6–14)**: Both groups had similar average scores (~49.3 vs. 49.2)
- **Post-switch (weeks 15–22)**: Switched branches averaged **51.2**, non-switched averaged **49.0**
- The gap between groups widened after the switch, consistent with a positive treatment effect

### **4. Statistical Uncertainty**
The confidence interval includes zero, meaning we **cannot rule out** that the true effect is:
- No effect at all, or
- A small negative effect (up to -0.6 points), or
- A meaningful positive effect (up to +5.5 points)

This uncertainty is due to the limited sample size and natural week-to-week variation in service scores.

---

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended Actions**

1. **Proceed with cautious expansion**: The point estimate is positive and practically meaningful (~5% improvement). Given the low risk of the procedure change, expanding to more branches is reasonable.

2. **Continue data collection**: Monitor service scores for at least 8–12 more weeks to increase statistical power and confirm the effect.

3. **Track implementation fidelity**: Ensure branches adopting the new procedure are following it consistently—variation in implementation could be masking the true effect.

4. **Consider subgroup analysis**: Investigate whether the procedure works better for certain branch types (e.g., by size, location, or patron volume).

5. **Set a decision checkpoint**: Re-evaluate in 2–3 months with additional data. If the effect remains positive and becomes statistically significant, proceed with full rollout.

---

## **Technical Notes**

- Analysis controls for branch-specific characteristics and week-to-week variation
- Standard errors are clustered at the branch level to account for repeated observations
- Pre-treatment trends between groups were reasonably parallel, supporting the validity of the comparison
