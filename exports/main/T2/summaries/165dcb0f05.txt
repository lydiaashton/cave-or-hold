# **Library Cooperative Branch Procedure Change: Impact Analysis**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new shelving and stock rotation procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.1 points** more than branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant (p < 0.001) and represents a meaningful improvement in branch performance. **We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **709 branches** total: 425 adopted the new procedure, 284 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in switched branches to changes in control branches

### **Results**

| Period | Switched Branches | Control Branches | Gap |
|--------|------------------|------------------|-----|
| **Before switch** (weeks 6–14) | 51.18 | 49.07 | 2.11 |
| **After switch** (weeks 15–22) | 55.09 | 50.85 | 4.24 |
| **Change** | +3.91 | +1.78 | **+2.13** |

- **Estimated effect of new procedure: +2.13 service score points** (95% CI: 1.56 to 2.70)
- The effect is **highly statistically significant** (p < 0.001)
- Results hold after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### **Additional Observations**

1. **Growing benefit over time**: The gap between switched and control branches widened from 3.6 points in week 15 to nearly 6.0 points by week 22, suggesting the benefits of the new procedure may accumulate as staff gain experience.

2. **Parallel trends validated**: Before the switch, the gap between groups remained relatively stable (ranging from 1.3 to 3.2 points), supporting the validity of the comparison.

3. **Both groups improved**: Service scores increased for all branches after week 14 (possibly due to seasonal factors), but switched branches improved substantially more.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new shelving and stock rotation procedure to all 284 branches currently using the old procedure.

2. **Monitor implementation**: Track service scores during rollout to ensure similar gains are realized across all branches.

3. **Document best practices**: Since benefits appear to grow over time, capture training materials and implementation tips from early-adopter branches to accelerate the learning curve for remaining branches.

4. **Consider complementary investments**: Staffing and visitor volume both positively correlate with service scores. Branches implementing the new procedure may benefit from aligned staffing adjustments.

5. **Follow-up evaluation**: Conduct a 3–6 month post-rollout assessment to confirm sustained improvements and identify any branches needing additional support.
