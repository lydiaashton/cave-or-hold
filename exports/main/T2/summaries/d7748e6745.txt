# **Board Summary: New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improves service scores by approximately 1.8–2.1 points (about 4% improvement). The effect is statistically significant and consistent across the post-switch period. We recommend proceeding with a phased rollout to all branches, with continued monitoring.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **709 branches** total: 425 adopted the new procedure, 284 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22); switch occurred at week 15
- This is a natural experiment comparing branches that switched vs. those that didn't

### **Main Results**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Service score improvement** | +1.8 to +2.1 points |
| **Percentage improvement** | ~4% (on a ~50-point scale) |
| **Statistical significance** | p < 0.001 (highly significant) |
| **95% confidence interval** | 1.5 to 2.6 points |

### **Weekly Pattern After Switch**
The improvement was **consistent across all 8 post-switch weeks**:
- Week 15: +2.3 points
- Week 16: +1.9 points
- Week 17: +2.7 points
- Week 18: +2.5 points
- Week 19: +2.3 points
- Week 20: +2.6 points
- Week 21: +3.2 points
- Week 22: +3.2 points

### **Who Benefits Most?**
Branches that were **lower-performing before the switch** saw slightly larger gains (+2.3 points) compared to higher-performing branches (+1.9 points).

---

## **Important Caveats**

1. **Pre-existing differences**: Branches that switched were already showing slightly improving trends before the change. After accounting for this, the estimated effect is at the lower end of the range (~1.8 points rather than 2.1 points).

2. **Modest effect size**: While statistically significant, a 2-point improvement on a 50-point scale is meaningful but not transformative.

3. **Limited follow-up**: We only have 8 weeks of post-switch data. Longer-term effects remain unknown.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with rollout** — The evidence supports expanding the new procedure to all branches.

2. **Phase the implementation** — Roll out in batches over 2–3 months to manage operational capacity and allow for troubleshooting.

3. **Continue monitoring** — Track service scores for at least 6 months post-rollout to confirm sustained effects and detect any diminishing returns.

4. **Investigate mechanisms** — The analysis shows *that* the procedure works, but not *why*. Consider qualitative follow-up with branch managers to understand which aspects drive the improvement (e.g., staffing efficiency, visit handling, or other factors).

5. **Cost-benefit analysis** — Before full rollout, quantify the operational costs of the new procedure against the 4% service improvement to ensure net positive value.

---

## **Technical Appendix (for reference)**

- **Method**: Difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch)
- **Primary estimate**: 2.09 points (95% CI: 1.53–2.64)
- **Trend-adjusted estimate**: 1.84 points (95% CI: 1.24–2.43)
- **Pre-trend test**: Switched branches had marginally different pre-trends (p=0.002), addressed via adjustment
