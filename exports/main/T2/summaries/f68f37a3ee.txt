# **Board Summary: New Branch Procedure Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure shows a **positive but statistically uncertain effect** on service scores. Branches that switched improved by approximately **1.6 points** (about 3% relative to baseline), while non-switching branches remained flat. However, due to limited data, we cannot rule out that this improvement occurred by chance. The effect appears to **grow over time**, suggesting benefits may increase with continued use.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **phased rollout** while continuing to monitor outcomes, rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses difference-in-differences methodology to isolate the procedure's effect

### **Results**

| Metric | Switched Branches | Non-Switched Branches |
|--------|-------------------|----------------------|
| Average score before switch | 50.38 | 52.30 |
| Average score after switch | 51.96 | 52.27 |
| **Change** | **+1.58** | **-0.03** |

**Estimated Treatment Effect: +1.6 points** (95% confidence interval: approximately -0.4 to +3.7)

### **Statistical Confidence**
- The estimated improvement is **not statistically significant at conventional levels** when accounting for repeated measurements per branch (p ≈ 0.12)
- This means we cannot confidently distinguish the observed improvement from random variation
- However, the **direction is consistently positive** across all analytical approaches

### **Encouraging Signal: Effect Grows Over Time**
- The benefit appears to **increase by about 0.35 points per week** after implementation (p = 0.012)
- This suggests branches may need time to fully realize the procedure's benefits
- By week 22 (8 weeks post-switch), switched branches were scoring **~2.8 points higher** than their pre-switch baseline trajectory

### **Other Factors**
- **Visits index** is positively associated with service scores (higher traffic → higher scores)
- **Staffing index** shows a positive but less certain relationship
- These factors were controlled for in the analysis

- **Clustering:** Standard errors clustered by branch to account for repeated observations
