# Impact of New Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores

## Bottom Line

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that switched saw their scores increase by an average of **2.1 points** (95% CI: 1.5 to 2.7 points) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents a **4.2% improvement** relative to the baseline score of 49.5. The result is highly statistically significant (p < 0.001).

**Recommendation: Proceed with rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

## Key Findings

### 1. Treatment Effect
Using a difference-in-differences analysis (the standard method for evaluating policy changes with treatment and control groups), the new procedure increased service scores by **2.10 points** (clustered standard error: 0.29, p < 0.001).

### 2. Branch-Level Impact

| Group | # Branches | Avg. Score Change | % That Improved |
|-------|-----------|-------------------|-----------------|
| Switched branches | 425 | +0.67 points | 58.8% |
| Control branches | 284 | -1.47 points | 33.1% |

### 3. Trend Over Time
The gap between switched and control branches grew consistently after the switch:
- **Before switch** (weeks 6-14): Average gap of 0.30 points
- **After switch** (weeks 15-22): Average gap of 2.44 points
- The gap increased from 1.75 points in week 15 to 3.36 points by week 22, suggesting the benefit may grow over time.

### 4. Validity Check
Statistical testing confirms treatment and control branches had similar trends before the switch (joint pre-trend test p = 0.23), supporting the validity of our analysis.

### 5. Important Context
Control branches experienced a decline in service scores after week 14 (-1.47 points on average), suggesting some external factor may have negatively affected all branches. The new procedure appears to have helped switched branches not only avoid this decline but actually improve their scores.

---

## Next Steps

1. **Proceed with Rollout**: Based on the statistically significant positive effect, we recommend rolling out the new procedure to all 284 remaining branches.

2. **Monitor Implementation**: Track service scores during rollout to ensure the effect is replicated across all branches.

3. **Investigate Variation**: About 41% of switched branches did not see improvement. Investigate what factors (staffing, training, branch characteristics) explain this variation to optimize implementation.

4. **Extend Monitoring Period**: Continue tracking for additional weeks to confirm the effect persists and to detect any long-term trends.

5. **Document Best Practices**: Work with high-performing switched branches to identify implementation practices that could help other branches achieve similar results.
