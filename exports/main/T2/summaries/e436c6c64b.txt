## **Analysis of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

### **Bottom Line**

The new procedure is associated with a **2-point increase in service scores** (from ~50 to ~52), which is statistically significant. However, this estimate comes with an important caveat: branches that adopted the new procedure were already showing improving service scores relative to other branches *before* the switch. When accounting for this pre-existing trend, the estimated effect becomes uncertain. **We recommend a cautious, phased rollout with continued monitoring rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.**

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. Raw Comparison Shows Improvement**
- Branches that switched to the new procedure saw their average service score increase from 50.8 to 51.9 (+1.1 points)
- Branches that kept the old procedure saw their scores decline from 49.4 to 48.6 (-0.8 points)
- The difference-in-differences estimate is **+1.93 points** (p < 0.001)

**2. Important Methodological Concern**
- Treatment branches (those that switched) had higher service scores than control branches even before the switch
- More critically, the gap between treatment and control branches was *widening* before the switch occurred (from 0.4 points in week 6 to 2.7 points in week 14)
- This suggests treatment branches may have been on an improving trajectory regardless of the new procedure
- When statistically controlling for this pre-existing trend, the estimated effect drops to **-0.55 points** (not statistically significant)

**3. Specificity Check**
- The effect appears specific to service scores—no similar patterns were found in staffing or visit indices
- This suggests the finding is not simply a general reporting artifact

**4. Post-Switch Patterns**
- After the switch, the treatment-control gap continued to widen (reaching 4.5 points by week 22)
- However, given the pre-existing trend, we cannot confidently attribute this to the new procedure

---

### **Next Steps**

1. **Do not proceed with immediate system-wide rollout.** The evidence is suggestive but not conclusive.

2. **Extend the observation period.** Continue collecting data for another 8-12 weeks to see if the post-switch improvement persists and to better distinguish the procedure effect from pre-existing trends.

3. **Consider a randomized rollout.** For the next phase, randomly assign remaining branches to switch (rather than letting branches self-select), which would strengthen causal inference.

4. **Investigate why certain branches were selected to switch first.** If higher-performing or better-resourced branches switched first, this could explain the pre-trend. Understanding the selection process is critical.

5. **Monitor implementation fidelity.** Ensure branches that switched are actually implementing the new procedure as intended.
