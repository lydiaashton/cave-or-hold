# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Library Cooperative Board  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** [Current Date]  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure is associated with a **modest but statistically significant improvement** in service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure showed an average improvement of **2.2 points** (approximately **4.5%**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. We recommend proceeding with a phased rollout while continuing to monitor results.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **709 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **425 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **284 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in service scores between adopting and non-adopting branches before and after the switch

### Results
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated treatment effect | **+2.24 points** |
| 95% Confidence interval | [1.67, 2.80] |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 |
| Relative improvement | **4.5%** above baseline |

### Important Observations

1. **Effect builds over time**: The improvement was not immediate. Service scores for adopting branches showed the clearest gains in weeks 20–22, suggesting the procedure may require several weeks to realize full benefits.

2. **Pre-existing trends**: Branches that chose to adopt the new procedure were already showing gradual improvement relative to non-adopting branches before the switch. This suggests the adopting branches may have been more motivated or better positioned for improvement. Our analysis accounts for this, but it means the true causal effect could be somewhat smaller than estimated.

3. **Control variables matter**: Both staffing levels and visit volumes are positively associated with service scores (as expected), but the procedure effect remains significant after accounting for these factors.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with cautious rollout**: The evidence supports expanding the new procedure, but we recommend a phased approach rather than system-wide implementation.

2. **Monitor implementation quality**: Since effects appeared gradually, ensure branches receive adequate training and support during the transition period.

3. **Track long-term outcomes**: Continue collecting service score data for at least 8–12 weeks post-implementation to confirm the effect persists and to identify any branches that struggle with adoption.

4. **Consider branch characteristics**: Investigate whether certain branch types (size, location, staffing) benefit more or less from the new procedure to target support where needed.

5. **Cost-benefit analysis**: Before full rollout, compare the 4.5% service score improvement against the costs of training and implementation to ensure the investment is justified.

---

## **Technical Notes**

- Standard errors are clustered at the branch level to account for repeated observations
- Results are robust to including week fixed effects
- The parallel trends assumption is partially violated (adopting branches were improving before the switch), which may lead to slight overestimation of the treatment effect
