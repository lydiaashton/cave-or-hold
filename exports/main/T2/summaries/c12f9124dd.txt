# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact Analysis**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.05 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and represents a meaningful 4% improvement. We recommend proceeding with a full rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Positive Impact on Service Scores
- **Treatment effect**: +2.05 points (95% confidence interval: 1.51 to 2.60)
- **Statistical significance**: p < 0.001 (highly significant)
- **Effect size**: Approximately 4% improvement relative to the average score of 51, or about 0.21 standard deviations (small-to-moderate effect)

### 2. Analysis Approach
We used a **difference-in-differences** design comparing:
- **Treatment group**: 425 branches that adopted the new procedure
- **Control group**: 284 branches that kept the old procedure
- **Pre-period**: Weeks 6-14 (before the switch)
- **Post-period**: Weeks 15-22 (after the switch)

This approach isolates the effect of the procedure change from other factors affecting all branches (like seasonal variations).

### 3. Supporting Evidence
- **Trend visualization**: Service scores for switched branches rose after week 15, while non-switched branches declined slightly
- **Robustness checks**: Results hold when controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume
- **Event study**: Post-switch effects are consistently positive across all 8 weeks, ranging from +1.8 to +3.2 points

### 4. Pre-Trends Assessment
The parallel trends assumption appears reasonably satisfied. In the 6 weeks before the switch (weeks 6-12), there were no statistically significant differences in trends between the two groups. There was a slight divergence in week 13 (one week before the switch), but the overall pre-period pattern supports the validity of our analysis.

### 5. Practical Impact
- Across the 425 switched branches over 8 weeks, the new procedure generated an estimated **6,984 total service score points**
- If rolled out to all 709 branches, the cooperative could expect similar improvements across the entire system

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with full rollout**: The evidence strongly supports expanding the new procedure to all branches.

2. **Monitor implementation**: Track service scores during rollout to ensure the effect persists at scale. Consider a phased approach to manage the transition.

3. **Investigate mechanisms**: The analysis shows staffing and visitor volume both positively affect service scores. Understanding how the new procedure interacts with these factors could help optimize implementation.

4. **Long-term follow-up**: Continue monitoring to determine if the effect is sustained over time or if there are diminishing returns.

5. **Cost-benefit analysis**: If the new procedure has implementation costs, compare them against the service score improvements to calculate ROI.

---

*Analysis based on 12,053 branch-week observations from 709 branches over 17 weeks (weeks 6-22). Standard errors clustered at the branch level to account for within-branch correlation.*
