# **Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.0 points** (about 4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and consistent across most branches. We recommend proceeding with a full rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect
- **Switched branches** improved by an average of **2.79 points** from before to after the change
- **Non-switched branches** improved by only **0.76 points** over the same period
- **Net effect of the new procedure: +2.0 points** (95% CI: 1.3 to 2.7 points)
- This represents approximately a **4% improvement** over the baseline average service score of 51.4

### 2. Statistical Confidence
- The effect is **highly statistically significant** (p < 0.001)
- Results hold even after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume
- Branch-level fixed effects analysis confirms the finding (effect size: 2.0 points, p < 0.001)

### 3. Branch-Level Impact
- **77% of switched branches** saw service score improvements, compared to 59% of non-switched branches
- The effect is fairly consistent—branches with different baseline scores, staffing levels, and visitor volumes all benefited similarly
- Some branches saw substantial gains (up to +12 points), while a minority saw declines (down to -6 points)

### 4. Timeline
- Data covers weeks 6–22 of the year
- The switch occurred at week 15
- 425 branches adopted the new procedure; 284 branches kept the old procedure
- Service scores were tracked weekly throughout the entire period

---

## **Caveats & Considerations**

1. **Not universal benefit**: About 23% of switched branches saw no improvement or a decline. These branches may need additional support or investigation to understand barriers to success.

2. **Pre-existing differences**: Switched branches started with slightly higher service scores (~1.4 points) than non-switched branches. Our analysis accounts for this, but it's worth noting.

3. **Short-term data**: We have 8 weeks of post-switch data. Longer-term monitoring would confirm whether the effect persists.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with full rollout** to remaining branches, given the clear positive effect.

2. **Investigate underperforming branches**: The ~100 switched branches that saw no improvement (or declines) should be reviewed to identify implementation issues or branch-specific factors.

3. **Monitor ongoing**: Continue tracking service scores for at least 6–12 months to ensure the effect is sustained and to detect any long-term trends.

4. **Document best practices**: Branches with the largest gains (+10+ points) should be studied to identify implementation practices that could be shared during rollout.

5. **Consider complementary factors**: Staffing and visitor volume both positively affect service scores. Ensuring adequate staffing during the rollout may maximize the procedure's impact.

---

*Analysis based on 12,053 weekly observations from 709 branches over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22). Difference-in-differences and fixed effects models used to isolate the procedure's effect.*
