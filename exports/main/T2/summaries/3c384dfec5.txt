# **Impact of New Stock Rotation Procedure on Library Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **1.8 points** (approximately 3.5%) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This improvement is statistically significant and unlikely to be due to chance. **We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measurable Improvement in Service Quality
- **Treatment branches** (90 branches using the new procedure): Service scores increased from 51.2 to 52.4 after the switch (+1.2 points)
- **Control branches** (60 branches keeping the old procedure): Service scores decreased from 51.9 to 51.3 (-0.6 points)
- **Net effect**: +1.8 points attributable to the new procedure

### 2. Statistical Confidence
- The effect is **statistically significant** (p = 0.002)
- **95% confidence interval**: 0.65 to 2.93 points
- This means we can be highly confident the true effect is positive—even in the worst case, the improvement is at least 0.65 points

### 3. Valid Comparison Between Groups
- **Parallel trends verified**: Before the switch (weeks 6–14), treatment and control branches followed similar trends in service scores (p = 0.92), supporting the validity of the comparison
- All 150 branches had data in both pre- and post-switch periods
- The effect is specific to service scores—staffing levels and visitor volumes did not change differently between groups, suggesting the improvement reflects genuine service quality gains rather than external factors

### 4. Study Design
- **Data**: 2,550 weekly observations from 150 branches (weeks 6–22)
- **Switch date**: Week 15
- **Method**: Difference-in-differences analysis with cluster-robust standard errors

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all 60 branches still using the old method

2. **Monitor implementation** during rollout to ensure the procedure is applied consistently and the expected benefits are realized across all branches

3. **Document best practices** from the 90 early-adopter branches to support training and smooth transition for remaining branches

4. **Continue tracking** service scores post-rollout to confirm sustained improvement and identify any branches needing additional support

5. **Consider cost-benefit analysis** if the new procedure involves additional training or resource costs, though the 3.5% service improvement suggests favorable returns
