# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores by approximately 2.2 points (about 4%), and this improvement is statistically significant.** The evidence supports rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **34 branches** were studied over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure at week 15 (treatment group)
- **14 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- This "natural experiment" design allows us to isolate the effect of the new procedure

### Service Score Impact
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Branches with new procedure | 50.8 | 53.9 | +3.2 |
| Branches with old procedure | 52.2 | 53.2 | +1.1 |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | | **+2.1** |

### Statistical Evidence
- **Estimated improvement: +2.2 points** (95% confidence interval: 0.7 to 3.7 points)
- **Statistically significant** (p < 0.01)
- **Pre-existing trends were parallel** between groups before the switch (p = 0.93), validating our analysis approach
- The effect represents approximately a **4% improvement** over the baseline service score of ~52

### Additional Observations
- The improvement was not explained by changes in staffing levels or visitor patterns
- Both groups showed some improvement over time, but the new procedure produced significantly greater gains

---

## **Next Steps**

### Recommendation
**Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new stock rotation and shelving procedure to all 34 branches.

### Implementation Considerations
1. **Expected benefit**: Based on this analysis, the cooperative can expect service scores to improve by approximately 2 points across all branches
2. **Monitoring**: Continue tracking service scores during rollout to confirm the effect holds at scale
3. **Training**: Ensure consistent implementation of the new procedure across all branches to achieve the full benefit
4. **Timeline**: Consider a phased rollout to manage operational capacity, though the data supports full adoption

### Caveats
- The analysis covers an 8-week post-implementation period; longer-term effects should be monitored
- The effect size, while statistically significant, is modest (4%); the board should weigh this against implementation costs
