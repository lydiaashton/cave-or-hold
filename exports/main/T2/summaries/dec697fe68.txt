# **Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure Evaluation**

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure shows a **positive but not statistically significant effect** on service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure maintained their service scores (averaging ~51 points), while control branches using the old procedure experienced a notable decline (from 49 to 47 points). The estimated improvement from the new procedure is approximately **2 points on the service score index**, but this result is not statistically significant at conventional levels (p = 0.15).

**Recommendation:** The evidence is suggestive but not conclusive. We recommend a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Study Design
- **34 branches** total: 20 adopted the new procedure (treatment), 14 kept the old procedure (control)
- **17 weeks of data**: Weeks 6-14 (before switch), Weeks 15-22 (after switch)
- **578 total observations** (weekly scores per branch)

### 2. Service Score Trends

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 49.26 | 47.19 | **-2.07** |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 51.41 | 51.27 | **-0.14** |

**Difference-in-Differences Estimate: +1.93 points**

The treatment group's scores remained stable while the control group declined, suggesting the new procedure may have **protected against deterioration** rather than driving improvement.

### 3. Statistical Analysis
- **DiD coefficient**: 1.93 points (95% CI: -0.63 to 4.49)
- **P-value**: 0.15 (not statistically significant at p < 0.05)
- **Parallel trends assumption**: Validated (p = 0.48 for pre-trend differences)

### 4. Important Context
- The control group's decline (-2 points) is itself statistically significant (p = 0.02), suggesting some system-wide factor may have negatively affected service scores after week 14
- The new procedure appears to have **buffered branches against this decline**
- Covariates (staffing and visits indices) do not materially change the results

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Continue the pilot** for an additional 8-12 weeks to gather more data and improve statistical power

2. **Expand to additional branches** in a second wave, randomly assigning branches to treatment/control to strengthen causal inference

3. **Investigate the control group decline** – understand what caused service scores to drop system-wide after week 14 (seasonal effects? staffing changes? external factors?)

4. **Monitor implementation fidelity** – ensure branches assigned to the new procedure are actually following it consistently

5. **Consider cost-benefit analysis** – if the new procedure requires additional resources, weigh the ~2-point service score benefit against implementation costs

6. **Set a decision threshold** – recommend proceeding with full rollout if future data shows:
   - Statistically significant effect (p < 0.05), OR
   - Consistent positive effect of 2+ points across multiple periods
