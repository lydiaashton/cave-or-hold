# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**
## Analysis for Board Decision on System-Wide Rollout

---

### **BOTTOM LINE**

The new shelving and rotation procedure is associated with a **modest improvement in service scores (+2.3 points, or ~5%)**, but this effect is **not statistically significant** at conventional confidence levels. The data provides suggestive but inconclusive evidence that the new procedure improves service quality. The board may wish to consider a phased rollout with continued monitoring rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.

---

### **KEY FINDINGS**

**1. Study Design**
- **34 branches** analyzed over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22 of the year)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure; **14 branches** kept the old procedure
- Switch occurred at **week 15**; analysis compares before/after for both groups
- This "difference-in-differences" approach isolates the procedure effect from other factors

**2. Service Score Changes**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Treatment (new procedure) | 50.0 | 52.3 | **+2.3** |
| Control (old procedure) | 49.1 | 49.1 | **0.0** |
| **Estimated Effect (DiD)** | | | **+2.3 points** |

**3. Statistical Significance**
- **p-value: 0.11** (above the conventional 0.05 threshold)
- **95% confidence interval: -0.5 to +5.1 points**
- Interpretation: We cannot rule out that the observed improvement is due to random chance, though the point estimate is positive

**4. Validity Checks**
- **Parallel trends confirmed**: No significant difference between groups before the switch (p=0.55), supporting the analysis methodology
- **Effect persistence**: The improvement appears sustained across both early (weeks 15–18) and late (weeks 19–22) post-switch periods
- **Robust to controls**: Results unchanged when accounting for weekly staffing and visit volume

**5. Practical Significance**
- A 2.3-point improvement represents approximately **5% relative to the baseline** service score
- For context: average branch scores around 50; the effect would move a typical branch from 50 to ~52

---

### **NEXT STEPS**

**Recommended Approach: Phased Rollout with Evaluation**

1. **Proceed with cautious expansion**
   - The positive point estimate and lack of negative effects suggest the new procedure is at worst neutral and likely beneficial
   - Consider rolling out to an additional 5–10 branches in the next quarter

2. **Extend the observation period**
   - Current data covers only 8 weeks post-switch; longer-term effects are unknown
   - Collect 12–16 more weeks of data to improve statistical power

3. **Monitor implementation fidelity**
   - Track whether branches are correctly following the new procedure
   - Variation in implementation could be masking true effects

4. **Set clear success criteria**
   - Define minimum acceptable improvement (e.g., +2 points sustained over 12 weeks)
   - Establish a timeline for full rollout decision (e.g., re-evaluate in Q2)

5. **Consider subgroup analysis**
   - Investigate whether effects differ by branch size, location, or baseline performance
   - Target rollout to branches most likely to benefit
