# **Analysis of New Shelving and Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure **improved service scores** by approximately **1.9 points** (on a scale where the average branch scores about 50). This effect is **statistically significant** (p = 0.002), meaning we can be confident the improvement is real and not due to random variation. Contrary to the initial expectation that the change would make no difference, the evidence shows the new procedure had a meaningful positive impact.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15 (treatment group)
- **60 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- This "natural experiment" design allows us to isolate the effect of the new procedure

### Results
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 49.85 | 47.79 | -2.06 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 50.54 | 50.37 | -0.17 |

- **Service scores declined across all branches** after week 15 (likely due to seasonal or external factors)
- However, branches with the new procedure **declined much less** than those without it
- The **net benefit of the new procedure: +1.88 points** (95% confidence interval: 0.72 to 3.05)

### Statistical Confidence
- The analysis used a rigorous difference-in-differences approach with branch and week fixed effects
- Results are **statistically significant at the 1% level** (p = 0.002)
- Pre-switch trends were parallel between groups, validating the comparison

## **Next Steps**

1. **Recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.** The evidence shows a clear, positive impact on service scores.

2. **Monitor implementation closely.** Track service scores at newly converted branches to ensure the benefits observed in this study are replicated.

3. **Investigate why scores declined overall after week 15.** Both groups saw lower scores in the post-period. Understanding this external factor could help maximize the benefits of the new procedure.

4. **Consider cost-benefit analysis.** If the new procedure has additional costs (training, equipment, time), weigh these against the ~1.9 point service score improvement to confirm the rollout is economically justified.
