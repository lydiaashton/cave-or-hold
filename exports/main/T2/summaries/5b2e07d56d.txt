# **Board Summary: New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure Evaluation**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new rotation and shelving procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **2.0 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.5 to 2.6 points) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents approximately a **4% improvement** and is statistically significant. The benefit appears to grow over time, suggesting branches become more effective with the new procedure as they gain experience.

**Recommendation: Proceed with system-wide rollout.**

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect
- **Average improvement: +2.04 service score points** (p < 0.001)
- Switched branches improved from 50.96 to 52.55 (+1.59 points)
- Non-switched branches declined from 49.85 to 49.37 (-0.48 points)
- The difference-in-differences estimate isolates the causal effect of the new procedure

### 2. Effect Grows Over Time

| Week After Switch | Treatment Effect |

This pattern suggests a **learning curve**—branches become more proficient with the new procedure over time.

### 3. Robustness
- Results hold after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume
- Effect is consistent across the 425 branches that switched
- Standard errors are clustered by branch to account for repeated observations
