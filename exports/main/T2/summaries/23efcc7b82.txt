# **Impact of New Shelving Procedure on Branch Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure is associated with a **modest improvement in service scores** (+2.2 points, or approximately 4% of the average score). While this effect is not statistically significant at conventional levels (p=0.10), the evidence suggests the new procedure likely has a positive impact and does not appear to harm service quality. We recommend proceeding with a broader rollout while continuing to monitor outcomes.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** analyzed over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15 (treatment group)
- **14 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in treatment branches to changes in control branches

### **Service Score Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 52.05 | 50.90 | -1.15 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 51.78 | 52.80 | +1.02 |

**Estimated Effect of New Procedure: +2.22 points**
- 95% Confidence Interval: -0.40 to +4.85
- P-value: 0.097 (marginally significant)

### **Interpretation**
1. **Positive direction**: Branches that switched to the new procedure improved their scores relative to branches that did not switch
2. **Statistical uncertainty**: The confidence interval includes zero, meaning we cannot rule out the possibility of no effect. However, the interval also suggests the true effect could be as high as +5 points
3. **Parallel trends validated**: Before the switch, treatment and control branches showed similar score trends, supporting the validity of our comparison
4. **Practical significance**: A 2.2-point improvement represents about 4% of the average service score—a meaningful but modest gain

### **Additional Observations**
- Control branches experienced a slight decline in scores after week 15 (-1.15 points), possibly due to seasonal factors or other external influences
- The new procedure appears to have helped treatment branches maintain and slightly improve their performance during the same period
- Staffing and visit indices were controlled for in the analysis and did not substantially change the results

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with cautious rollout**: The evidence supports expanding the new procedure to remaining branches, with the expectation of modest service score improvements

2. **Continue monitoring**: Track service scores for at least 8–12 weeks post-rollout to confirm the effect persists and to narrow the confidence interval with more data

3. **Investigate variation**: Some weeks showed larger effects than others (e.g., weeks 18 and 22). Understanding what drives this variation could help optimize implementation

4. **Consider complementary measures**: Since the effect is modest, explore whether combining the new procedure with other improvements (e.g., staffing adjustments) could yield larger gains

5. **Cost-benefit analysis**: Before full rollout, assess whether the operational costs of the new procedure are justified by the ~4% service score improvement
