# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure is associated with a **modest improvement in service scores (+2.4 points)**, but this effect is **not statistically significant**. The data do not provide strong evidence that the new procedure improves service quality. The board should consider **extending the evaluation period** before making a system-wide rollout decision.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in switched branches against non-switched branches

### Results

| Group | Pre-Switch Average | Post-Switch Average | Change |
|-------|-------------------|---------------------|--------|
| Switched Branches | 46.0 | 47.1 | +1.1 |
| Non-Switched Branches | 51.4 | 50.1 | −1.3 |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | | | **+2.4** |

### Statistical Assessment
- **Estimated effect**: +2.4 points on the service score index
- **95% Confidence Interval**: −0.15 to +4.59 points
- **p-value**: 0.11 (not significant at conventional thresholds)
- **Interpretation**: We cannot rule out that the true effect is zero or even slightly negative

### Additional Observations
- **Parallel trends assumption**: Pre-switch trends were generally similar between groups, supporting the validity of the comparison
- **Magnitude**: If the effect is real, a 2.4-point increase represents approximately a **5% improvement** relative to the average branch score of ~50
- **Covariates**: Controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume did not materially change the results

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Extend the evaluation period**: Collect 8–12 additional weeks of post-switch data to increase statistical power and determine whether the observed effect stabilizes or grows

2. **Monitor implementation fidelity**: Verify that all switched branches are consistently applying the new procedure as intended

3. **Consider subgroup analysis**: Examine whether the procedure works better in certain branch types (e.g., by size, location, or baseline performance)

4. **Set a decision threshold**: Define the minimum effect size that would justify system-wide rollout costs (e.g., training, materials, transition time)

5. **Revisit in Q2**: With additional data, re-run this analysis to make a more confident go/no-go recommendation

---

## **Recommendation**

**Do not proceed with system-wide rollout at this time.** The current evidence is inconclusive. Continue the pilot with enhanced monitoring and extend the evaluation period before making a final decision.
