# **Analysis of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation procedure is associated with a **modest improvement in service scores** (approximately +2 points or 4% above baseline), but the evidence is **not definitive**. The effect appears delayed and may not be sustained. We recommend **continuing the pilot with expanded monitoring** before committing to a system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Overall Treatment Effect
- **Estimated impact**: +2.0 points on the service score index (p=0.069)
- **95% confidence interval**: -0.2 to +4.2 points
- **Interpretation**: The effect is positive but only marginally statistically significant. We cannot rule out that the true effect is zero.

### 2. What the Data Shows
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 50.1 | 53.3 | +3.2 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 50.6 | 55.5 | +4.9 |

Both groups improved after week 15, but the treatment group improved slightly more.

### 3. Important Patterns in the Data

**The effect was not immediate.** Service scores for treatment branches did not jump right at week 15. Instead:
- Weeks 15-18: Little to no detectable effect
- **Weeks 19-20: Strong positive effect** (+4 to +5 points, statistically significant)
- Weeks 21-22: Effect diminished back to near zero

**Pre-existing differences.** Before the switch, treatment branches tended to score lower than control branches in several weeks. This makes it harder to isolate the true effect of the procedure change.

### 4. Statistical Confidence
- The analysis accounts for branch-level differences and other factors (staffing, visitor volume)
- With proper statistical adjustments, the result is suggestive but not conclusive
- More data would be needed to reach a definitive conclusion

---

## **Next Steps**

### Recommended Actions

1. **Continue the pilot for 8-12 more weeks** to determine whether the effect stabilizes or fades. The pattern in weeks 21-22 raises questions about sustainability.

2. **Monitor branches individually.** Some branches may benefit more than others. Identify which branch characteristics (size, staffing, visitor volume) predict success with the new procedure.

3. **Consider a phased rollout.** If proceeding, start with branches most similar to the successful pilot branches rather than all branches at once.

4. **Track additional metrics.** Service score is one measure; consider tracking patron satisfaction, staff workload, and stock turnover to get a fuller picture.

### Decision Framework

| If the board prioritizes... | Recommendation |
|----------------------------|----------------|
| **Evidence certainty** | Wait for more data before rollout |
| **Potential upside** | Proceed with cautious, phased rollout |
| **Risk mitigation** | Continue pilot; prepare rollout plan contingent on sustained positive results |

## **Technical Notes**

- **Analysis method**: Difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors
- **Sample**: 34 branches (20 treatment, 14 control), 17 weeks, 578 observations
- **Controls**: Staffing index and visitor index included in primary model
