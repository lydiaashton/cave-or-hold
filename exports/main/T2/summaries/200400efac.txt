# **MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2025  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **2.3 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This represents approximately a **4-5% improvement** relative to the typical branch score of 50. The effect is statistically significant and consistent across the post-switch period. **We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### Study Design
- **709 branches** total: 425 adopted the new procedure, 284 continued with the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6-22): 9 weeks before the switch, 8 weeks after
- Analysis used a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in the switched branches against changes in the non-switched branches

### Results

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches (new procedure) | 50.37 | 52.76 | **+2.39** |
| Non-switched branches (old procedure) | 48.68 | 48.79 | +0.11 |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | | **+2.27** |

### Statistical Confidence
- **Estimated effect:** +2.27 points
- **95% confidence interval:** 1.67 to 2.80 points
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001 (highly significant)
- Results remain robust after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### Additional Observations
- The improvement was **sustained** throughout the 8-week post-switch period
- Both groups experienced similar week-to-week fluctuations (e.g., a dip in week 17), but the treatment group consistently maintained higher scores
- The effect size is **practically meaningful**: a 2.3-point gain on a ~50-point scale represents noticeable service improvement

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all 284 remaining branches

2. **Develop implementation timeline** — consider phasing the rollout to manage training and transition logistics

3. **Monitor service scores** during rollout to confirm the effect replicates across all branches

4. **Document the new procedure** — capture what makes it effective to ensure consistent implementation

5. **Continue tracking** staffing and visit metrics to understand whether the procedure's benefits vary by branch characteristics

---

## **METHODOLOGY NOTE**

This analysis compared branches that switched to the new procedure against those that did not, using a quasi-experimental "difference-in-differences" design. This approach isolates the effect of the procedure change from other factors affecting all branches (such as seasonal variations or system-wide trends). Standard errors were clustered at the branch level to account for repeated observations.
