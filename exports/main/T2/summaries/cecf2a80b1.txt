# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **2.2 points** (approximately **4.4% improvement**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This result is statistically significant and robust to controls for staffing levels and visitor volume.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect
- **Estimated improvement:** +2.20 service score points (95% CI: 1.65 to 2.76)
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001 (highly significant)
- **Percentage gain:** ~4.4% above baseline

### 2. Study Design
- **709 branches** total: 425 adopted the new procedure, 284 kept the old one
- **17 weeks of data:** Weeks 6-14 (before), Weeks 15-22 (after)
- **12,053 observations** analyzed using difference-in-differences regression
- Results control for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume

### 3. Score Changes by Group

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 50.23 | 54.28 | +4.06 |
| Non-switched branches | 49.09 | 50.95 | +1.86 |
| **Net effect (DiD)** | | | **+2.20** |

The control branches also improved slightly over time (+1.86 points), likely due to seasonal factors or system-wide trends. The new procedure delivered an **additional 2.20 points** beyond this background improvement.

### 4. Important Caveat
There was a modest pre-existing difference between the two groups before the switch (switched branches averaged 1.14 points higher). However, the gap widened substantially and consistently after week 15, supporting the conclusion that the procedure change drove the improvement.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.** The evidence strongly supports that it improves service scores.

2. **Monitor implementation closely.** Track service scores at newly converted branches to ensure they achieve similar gains.

3. **Investigate mechanisms.** Consider studying *why* the new procedure works (e.g., faster item location, better organization) to refine training and implementation.

4. **Cost-benefit analysis.** If the new procedure has additional costs (training, labor, equipment), weigh them against the 4.4% service improvement to confirm net benefit.

5. **Longer-term evaluation.** Continue tracking scores to ensure the improvement is sustained beyond the initial 8-week post-switch period.
