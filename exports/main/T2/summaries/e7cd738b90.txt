# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure did not produce a statistically significant improvement in service scores.** While branches using the new procedure declined less than those using the old procedure, the difference is not large enough to rule out chance. We recommend **against a system-wide rollout** at this time and suggest further investigation before making a decision.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. No Statistically Significant Effect
- **Estimated effect:** +2.1 points on the service score index
- **95% confidence interval:** -0.5 to +4.6 points
- **P-value:** 0.11 (above the 0.05 threshold for statistical significance)

This means we cannot confidently conclude the new procedure improved scores. The true effect could be zero, slightly negative, or moderately positive.

### 2. Both Groups Experienced Declining Scores
A critical finding is that service scores declined across **all** branches after week 15:

| Group | Pre-Switch Average | Post-Switch Average | Change |
|-------|-------------------|---------------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 50.35 | 46.84 | **-3.52** |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 54.27 | 52.69 | **-1.58** |

The new procedure branches declined less, but they still declined. This suggests an external factor (seasonal patterns, staffing changes, or other system-wide issues) affected all branches after week 15.

### 3. Effect Was Not Consistent Over Time
When examining week-by-week effects after the switch:
- **Week 15:** +5.2 points (positive spike immediately after switch)
- **Weeks 16-22:** Effects ranged from -4.9 to -0.1 points (no sustained benefit)

This pattern suggests any initial benefit was temporary and not maintained.

### 4. Study Design Was Sound
- 34 branches total (20 switched, 14 control)
- 17 weeks of data (weeks 6-22)
- Pre-period trends were similar between groups, supporting the validity of the comparison
- Analysis controlled for branch-specific characteristics and weekly staffing/visit patterns

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Do not proceed with system-wide rollout** based on current evidence. The data does not support the expected improvement.

2. **Investigate the system-wide decline.** Something caused service scores to drop across all branches after week 15. Understanding this is critical before testing any new procedure.

3. **Consider extending the pilot.** Eight weeks of post-switch data may be insufficient to detect a true effect. Continue monitoring the 20 pilot branches for additional weeks.

4. **Gather qualitative feedback.** Survey staff at switched branches about their experience with the new procedure. Quantitative scores may not capture operational benefits or challenges.

5. **Reassess in 3-6 months.** With more data and investigation into the system-wide score decline, the board can make a more informed decision.
