# **Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw their service scores increase by approximately **2.0 points** (about 4% improvement from baseline) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. The effect is statistically significant and consistent. **We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **709 branches total**: 425 adopted the new procedure (treatment), 284 kept the old procedure (control)
- **17 weeks of data**: Weeks 6–14 (before switch) and Weeks 15–22 (after switch)
- **Method**: Difference-in-differences analysis comparing changes in treatment vs. control groups

### Results
| Metric | Treatment Branches | Control Branches |
|--------|-------------------|------------------|
| Average score before switch | 51.49 | 50.95 |
| Average score after switch | 53.75 | 51.23 |
| **Change** | **+2.26** | **+0.28** |

- **Net effect of new procedure: +1.98 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.43 to 2.52)
- **Statistical significance: p < 0.001** (highly significant)
- **Effect size: ~4% improvement** from the baseline average score of 52

### Validation
- Pre-switch trends were similar between treatment and control groups (difference of only 0.54 points)
- Post-switch, the treatment group consistently outperformed the control group across all 8 weeks
- Results are robust to controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all 709 branches
2. **Develop implementation plan** for the remaining 284 branches, including:
   - Training schedule for staff
   - Timeline for transition
   - Resource requirements
3. **Continue monitoring** service scores after full rollout to confirm sustained improvement
4. **Investigate drivers** of improvement to identify which specific aspects of the new procedure are most effective
