# **Analysis of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure is associated with a **modest improvement in service scores** (approximately 2 points, or ~4% relative to the average score of 50). However, the evidence is **not statistically conclusive** at conventional confidence levels. We recommend a **cautious rollout** with continued monitoring.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Estimated Effect on Service Scores
- **Point estimate:** +1.94 points improvement in service scores for branches that adopted the new procedure
- **95% confidence interval:** -0.4 to +4.3 points
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.11 (not significant at the 5% level, but suggestive at the 10% level)

### 2. Treatment vs. Control Comparison
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Treatment (New Procedure) | 47.5 | 48.8 | +1.3 |
| Control (Old Procedure) | 51.2 | 50.6 | -0.7 |

The difference-in-differences estimate (+1.94) accounts for the underlying time trend (control branches declined slightly).

### 3. Branch-Level Results
- **15 of 20 treatment branches** (75%) showed score improvements after switching
- **6 of 14 control branches** (43%) showed improvements in the same period
- Treatment branch improvements ranged from -5.7 to +6.1 points

### 4. Validity Checks
- **Parallel trends assumption:** Supported. Treatment and control branches showed similar trends in the 9 weeks before the switch (p = 0.28 for differential pre-trends)
- **Confounding factors:** No systematic differences in staffing or visit patterns between treatment and control branches after the switch

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with measured rollout** — The point estimate is positive and meaningful, though statistical certainty is limited. The potential upside (up to ~4 point improvement) likely outweighs the risk of no effect.

2. **Extend the evaluation period** — Current data covers only 8 weeks post-switch. Continue tracking for 3-6 more months to:
   - Reduce uncertainty around the effect estimate
   - Assess whether effects persist or grow over time
   - Identify any branches that struggle with implementation

3. **Monitor implementation quality** — The variation in branch-level effects (-5.7 to +6.1) suggests implementation matters. Consider:
   - Documenting best practices from high-performing branches
   - Providing additional support to branches showing declines

4. **Track secondary outcomes** — While service scores are the primary metric, also monitor staffing efficiency and patron satisfaction to capture the full impact.

---

**Analysis notes:** Difference-in-differences regression with branch fixed effects and cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch). Data covers 34 branches over 17 weeks (weeks 6-22), with the procedure switch occurring at week 15.
