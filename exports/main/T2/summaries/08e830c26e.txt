# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores that were **2.3 points higher** (approximately 4% improvement) than they would have been without the change. This effect is statistically significant and robust. We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Measurable Positive Impact
- The new procedure increased service scores by **2.26 points** on average (95% confidence interval: 0.72 to 3.81 points)
- This represents a **4.4% improvement** relative to the baseline average score of 51.9
- The result is statistically significant (p = 0.004), meaning there's less than a 1% chance this finding is due to random variation

### 2. Study Design Strength
- **150 branches** were analyzed over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure; **60 branches** continued with the old procedure (serving as a control group)
- The switch occurred at **week 15**, providing 9 weeks of "before" data and 8 weeks of "after" data
- Pre-switch trends were parallel between the two groups, validating our comparison approach

### 3. What Would Have Happened Without the Change
- Non-switched branches experienced a **2.85-point decline** in service scores after week 15
- Switched branches experienced only a **0.57-point decline**
- The new procedure appears to have **protected branches from a broader downward trend** in service quality, rather than driving scores up in absolute terms

### 4. Additional Insights
- Higher visitor volumes are associated with higher service scores (independent of the procedure change)
- The effect holds even after accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all 60 remaining branches

2. **Monitor implementation** over the next 8–12 weeks to confirm the effect persists at scale

3. **Investigate the broader decline** observed in non-switched branches to understand what may be driving service scores down across the system

4. **Document the new procedure** in detail to ensure consistent implementation across all branches

5. **Consider a follow-up evaluation** in 6 months to assess long-term sustainability of the improvement

---

## **Methodology Note**

This analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, which compares the change in service scores for switched branches against the change for non-switched branches. This method isolates the effect of the new procedure from other factors that may have affected all branches during the same period (such as seasonal variations or system-wide challenges).
