# **Analysis of New Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure had service scores approximately **2 points higher** than would be expected based on the trend in branches that kept the old procedure. This difference is statistically significant (p=0.002), and we recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **150 branches** total: 90 adopted the new procedure (treatment group), 60 kept the old procedure (control group)
- **17 weeks of data**: Weeks 6-14 (before switch) and Weeks 15-22 (after switch)
- **2,550 observations** (weekly scores for each branch)

### Results

| Group | Pre-Switch Mean | Post-Switch Mean | Change |
|-------|-----------------|------------------|--------|
| **New Procedure** (90 branches) | 50.91 | 51.10 | +0.19 |
| **Old Procedure** (60 branches) | 49.22 | 47.36 | -1.87 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+2.05** |

- **Treatment Effect: +2.05 points** (95% CI: 0.77 to 3.33)
- **Statistical Significance: p = 0.002** (highly significant)

### Interpretation

While branches using the new procedure showed only a modest improvement (+0.19 points), branches keeping the old procedure experienced a notable decline (-1.87 points). The new procedure appears to have **protected branches from a broader downward trend** in service scores during this period, resulting in a net benefit of about 2 points relative to the control group.

### Robustness Checks

- **Staffing levels**: No significant difference between groups after the switch, ruling out staffing as an explanation
- **Visit volumes**: Slightly lower in treatment branches after the switch (not statistically significant at conventional levels), which would typically *reduce* service scores—making our positive finding more conservative
- **Pre-trends**: Both groups showed parallel weekly fluctuations before the switch, supporting the validity of the comparison

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches
2. **Monitor implementation** to ensure consistent adoption and identify any branches needing additional support
3. **Continue tracking** service scores for 8-12 weeks post-rollout to confirm the effect persists at scale
4. **Document best practices** from high-performing switched branches to support training materials

---

## **Technical Notes**

Analysis used a difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch) to account for within-branch correlation over time. The parallel trends assumption appears satisfied based on pre-period patterns.
