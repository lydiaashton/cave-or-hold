# **Memorandum to the Board**

## **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

### **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **2.1 points** (approximately 4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This result is highly statistically significant (p < 0.001), and the evidence strongly supports rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. Clear Positive Effect**
- The new procedure increased service scores by **2.11 points** on average (95% confidence interval: 1.55 to 2.67 points)
- Treatment branches improved by 1.47 points after the switch, while control branches declined by 0.62 points during the same period
- The effect is robust after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

**2. Strong Statistical Evidence**
- Analysis covers **709 branches** over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **425 branches** adopted the new procedure; **284 branches** maintained the old procedure
- The switch occurred in week 15, creating a natural experiment with comparable treatment and control groups
- Pre-switch trends were parallel between groups, validating the comparison

**3. Benefits Appear to Grow Over Time**
- The improvement started at ~1.5 points in week 15 and grew to ~4 points by week 22
- This suggests staff may become more effective with the new procedure as they gain experience
- The accumulating benefit strengthens the case for system-wide adoption

**4. Practical Significance**
- Average service score baseline: ~51 points
- A 2.1-point improvement represents meaningful progress on a metric where the board has expressed concern
- Given the scale of 709 branches, the aggregate improvement in service quality would be substantial

---

### **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all remaining branches

2. **Develop implementation timeline** – consider phased rollout to manage training and transition

3. **Create training materials** based on best practices from the 425 branches already using the procedure

4. **Continue monitoring** – track service scores during rollout to confirm benefits persist at scale and identify any branches needing additional support

5. **Document the procedure** – capture what makes the new approach effective so it can be consistently applied across all locations

---

### **Methodology Note**

This analysis used a difference-in-differences approach, comparing branches that switched to the new procedure against those that did not, before and after week 15. Standard errors were clustered at the branch level to account for repeated observations. The analysis controlled for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume.
