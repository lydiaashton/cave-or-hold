## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Contrary to the expectation that the change would make no difference, branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.45 points** (on a scale where the average branch scores about 50). This effect is statistically significant (p < 0.001) and represents a meaningful improvement in service quality.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect on Service Scores
- **Estimated impact:** +2.45 points increase in service score
- **95% Confidence Interval:** 1.20 to 3.70 points
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001 (highly significant)
- This represents approximately a **5% improvement** relative to the average branch score of 50

### 2. Study Design & Sample
- **60 branches** continued with the old procedure (control group)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure (treatment group)
- Data covers **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- **2,550 total observations** (one per branch per week)

### 3. Validity of the Analysis
- **Parallel trends confirmed:** Before the switch, both groups followed similar trends (p = 0.754), supporting the validity of the comparison
- **No confounding factors detected:** The improvement was not accompanied by significant changes in staffing levels (p = 0.24) or visitor volume (p = 0.19), suggesting the effect is attributable to the procedure change itself

### 4. Visual Evidence
The attached chart shows service scores over time for both groups. Notice that:
- Before week 15, both groups track closely (treatment group consistently ~1.3 points lower)
- After week 15, the treatment group's scores rise relative to the control group
