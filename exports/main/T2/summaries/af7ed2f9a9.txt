# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new shelving and rotation procedure significantly improved service scores.** Contrary to the expectation of no difference, branches that switched to the new procedure saw service scores increase by an average of **2.1 points** (approximately 4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and consistent across most branches. **We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Treatment Effect**
- **Estimated impact**: +2.12 service score points (95% confidence interval: 0.9 to 3.3 points)
- **Statistical significance**: p < 0.001 (highly significant)
- **Relative improvement**: ~4% increase relative to the average service score of 51

### **2. Study Design**
- **150 branches** total: 90 switched to the new procedure, 60 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data**: 9 weeks before the switch (weeks 6-14), 8 weeks after (weeks 15-22)
- **Method**: Difference-in-differences analysis with branch fixed effects and clustered standard errors

### **3. Branch-Level Results**
- **85.6% of switched branches** (77 of 90) showed improvement in service scores
- Average improvement for switched branches: +3.2 points
- Average change for non-switched branches: +1.1 points (likely due to seasonal or system-wide factors)

### **4. Parallel Trends Validation**
Before the switch, the two groups had similar trends with an average difference of 0.8 points. After the switch, the gap widened to 2.9 points, supporting the conclusion that the procedure change caused the improvement.

### **5. What Did NOT Drive the Effect**
The improvement does not appear to operate through changes in staffing levels or visit volumes—neither measure changed significantly for switched branches relative to controls.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** of the new procedure to all remaining branches.

2. **Monitor implementation** to ensure the new procedure is being followed consistently across branches.

3. **Track service scores** for the next 8-12 weeks post-rollout to confirm the effect persists at scale.

4. **Investigate mechanisms**: Since staffing and visits didn't change, further research could identify *why* the new procedure improves service scores (e.g., faster item location, better organization, staff efficiency).

5. **Document best practices** from the highest-performing switched branches to support training during rollout.

## **Technical Notes**

- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences design, the standard approach for evaluating policy changes with treatment and control groups
- Standard errors are clustered at the branch level to account for within-branch correlation over time
- Results are robust to controlling for staffing and visit indices
