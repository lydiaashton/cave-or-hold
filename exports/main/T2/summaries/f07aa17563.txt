# **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure shows a **positive but statistically inconclusive effect** on service scores. Branches that switched to the new procedure improved by an estimated **1.5 points more** than branches that kept the old procedure (approximately 3% improvement relative to baseline). However, this difference is not statistically significant—we cannot rule out that the observed improvement occurred by chance. The board should consider a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure (treatment group); **14 branches** kept the old procedure (control group)
- Data covers **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- **578 total observations** (weekly scores for all branches)

### Results
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 47.3 | 51.6 | +4.3 |
| Non-switched branches | 48.0 | 50.8 | +2.8 |
| **Difference** | | | **+1.5** |

- **Effect estimate:** +1.53 points (95% confidence interval: -0.59 to +3.65)
- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.16 (not statistically significant at conventional levels)
- **Pre-trend validation:** No evidence of differential trends before the switch (p = 0.52), supporting the validity of the comparison

### Branch-Level Patterns
- **18 of 20** switched branches showed improvement (mean +4.3 points)
- **11 of 14** non-switched branches also improved (mean +2.8 points)
- Both groups improved overall, but switched branches improved more on average
- Two switched branches saw declines (-1.3 and -5.3 points), suggesting the procedure may not benefit all branches equally

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Continue monitoring:** Extend the evaluation period to increase statistical power. With more weeks of data, we could detect smaller effects with greater confidence.

2. **Investigate heterogeneity:** Examine why some switched branches saw large gains (up to +9.9 points) while others saw declines. Branch characteristics (size, staffing, visit volume) may moderate the procedure's effectiveness.

3. **Consider phased rollout:** Given the positive but uncertain results, expand the new procedure to additional branches while maintaining a control group for ongoing evaluation.

4. **Cost-benefit analysis:** Before full implementation, assess whether the operational costs of the new procedure are justified by the modest (~3%) service score improvement observed.

5. **Supplementary metrics:** Evaluate the new procedure's impact on other outcomes (e.g., staffing efficiency, patron satisfaction) beyond the service score index.
