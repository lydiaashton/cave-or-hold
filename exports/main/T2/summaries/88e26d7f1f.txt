# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

## **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

### **Bottom Line**

The new procedure **significantly improved service scores**. Branches that switched to the new procedure saw their service scores increase by approximately **2.5 points** (about 5% above baseline), while branches that kept the old procedure showed no change. This result is statistically significant and contradicts the initial expectation that the procedure change would have no effect on service quality. **We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.**

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. Study Design**
- Analyzed 150 branches over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- 90 branches adopted the new procedure at week 15; 60 branches kept the old procedure
- Used a difference-in-differences approach to isolate the effect of the procedure change

**2. Service Score Results**

| Group | Before Switch (Weeks 6–14) | After Switch (Weeks 15–22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------------|---------------------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 50.13 | 50.14 | +0.01 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 50.81 | 53.39 | +2.58 |

**3. Estimated Effect**
- The new procedure increased service scores by **2.50 points** (95% confidence interval: 1.18 to 3.82)
- P-value < 0.001 (highly statistically significant)
- Effect represents approximately **5% improvement** over baseline scores

**4. Robustness Checks**
- Result holds after controlling for weekly staffing levels and visitor volume
- Pre-switch trends were parallel between groups, supporting the validity of the analysis
- Standard errors account for repeated observations at each branch

### **Analytical Note**

This analysis used a quasi-experimental difference-in-differences design, which compares the change in outcomes for branches that adopted the new procedure against the change for branches that did not. This approach controls for both time-invariant branch characteristics and common time trends, providing a credible estimate of the causal effect of the procedure change.
