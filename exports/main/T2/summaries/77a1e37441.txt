# **Impact of New Stock Rotation Procedure on Library Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure significantly improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.1 points** (about 4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This finding is statistically significant (p = 0.003) and contradicts the initial expectation that the change would make no difference.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **150 branches** total: 90 adopted the new procedure, 60 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** per branch (weeks 6–22)
- Switch occurred at week 15, providing 9 weeks of "before" and 8 weeks of "after" data
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in switched branches against changes in control branches

### Results
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Treatment Effect** | +2.11 service score points |
| **95% Confidence Interval** | [0.74, 3.48] |
| **Statistical Significance** | p = 0.003 |
| **Relative Improvement** | ~4% above baseline |

### What the Numbers Mean
- **Switched branches** improved from 48.6 (before) to 51.7 (after) = +3.2 points
- **Control branches** improved from 47.2 (before) to 48.4 (after) = +1.1 points
- The **net effect attributable to the new procedure** is 2.1 points

### Robustness Checks
- Results hold when controlling for staffing levels and visit volumes
- Results hold when accounting for branch-specific characteristics (branch fixed effects)
- Pre-switch trends between groups were reasonably parallel, supporting the validity of the analysis

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with system-wide rollout** — The evidence supports expanding the new procedure to all branches, with an expected service score improvement of ~2 points per branch.

2. **Monitor implementation** — Track service scores during rollout to ensure the effect replicates across all branches.

3. **Investigate mechanisms** — Consider studying *why* the new procedure improved scores (e.g., faster item location, better organization, staff efficiency) to optimize training.

4. **Cost-benefit analysis** — Quantify the operational costs of the new procedure against the service quality gains to confirm net benefit.

5. **Long-term tracking** — Continue monitoring to ensure the improvement is sustained over time and does not diminish as staff adapt.

---

*Analysis based on weekly service score data from 150 branches, weeks 6–22. Statistical model: difference-in-differences regression with branch fixed effects and clustered standard errors.*
