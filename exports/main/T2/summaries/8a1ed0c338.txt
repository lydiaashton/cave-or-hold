# **Memorandum to the Board**

## **Title: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure is associated with a **modest improvement in service scores**—approximately **2 points higher** (a 4% increase from baseline) for branches that adopted the new procedure compared to those that kept the old one. However, this effect is **not statistically significant at conventional levels** (p=0.12), meaning we cannot rule out that the observed improvement occurred by chance. The evidence suggests the new procedure likely helps, but we recommend a cautious, phased rollout with continued monitoring rather than an immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** switched to the new procedure at week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in service scores between the two groups before and after the switch

### **Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 49.2 | 51.1 | +1.9 |
| Treated (new procedure) | 47.0 | 51.0 | +3.9 |

- **Estimated treatment effect: +2.04 points** (95% confidence interval: -0.5 to +4.6)
- Both groups improved after week 15, but branches using the new procedure improved slightly more
- The effect represents approximately a **4% increase** relative to the baseline service score

### **Statistical Confidence**
- The result is **not statistically significant at the 5% level** (p=0.12)
- The 95% confidence interval includes zero, meaning the true effect could range from slightly negative to moderately positive
- The analysis meets the **parallel trends assumption**: both groups had similar trajectories before the switch (p=0.59), supporting the validity of the comparison

### **Event Study Insights**
- Service scores for treated branches showed **consistently positive effects** in all 8 weeks after the switch
- The average post-switch effect was approximately **5.5 points**, though this varied week-to-week
- No systematic differences were observed between groups before the switch

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with Cautious Rollout**: The evidence points in a positive direction, but the uncertainty warrants a measured approach. Consider expanding the new procedure to additional branches while continuing to collect data.

2. **Extend the Evaluation Period**: Service scores may take time to stabilize. Continue monitoring for at least 8–12 more weeks to obtain more precise estimates.

3. **Investigate Heterogeneous Effects**: Some branches may benefit more than others. Analyze whether branch characteristics (size, staffing, visit volume) moderate the effect to identify which branches would benefit most.

4. **Monitor Implementation Fidelity**: Ensure branches adopting the new procedure are implementing it consistently. Variation in execution could mask true effects.

5. **Set a Decision Threshold**: Define in advance what level of improvement (e.g., +3 points with p<0.05) would justify full system-wide adoption.

---

## **Technical Notes**

- Analysis controls for branch fixed effects and uses cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch)
- Covariates (staffing index, visits index) were tested and did not materially change the results
- All 34 branches have complete data for all 17 weeks; no data quality issues identified
