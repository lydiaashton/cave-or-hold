# **Memorandum to the Board**

## **Title: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

### **Bottom Line**

**Yes, the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2.3 points** (about 4.5% relative to the baseline average of 51) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and consistent across the post-switch period. We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. Study Design and Sample**
- 150 branches total: 90 adopted the new procedure, 60 kept the old procedure
- Data covers 17 weeks (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in service scores between switching and non-switching branches before and after the procedure change

**2. Main Results**
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 51.81 | 49.45 | −2.36 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 51.24 | 51.19 | −0.05 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+2.31** |

- The treatment effect of **+2.31 points** is statistically significant (p < 0.001)
- Results hold when controlling for staffing levels and visitor traffic (+2.21 points, p = 0.001)

**3. Parallel Trends Validation**
- An event study analysis confirms that switching and non-switching branches had similar trends in service scores before the procedure change (all pre-period coefficients statistically insignificant)
- After the switch, treatment branches consistently outperformed control branches by 2–3 points per week

**4. Context**
- Control branches experienced a notable decline in service scores after week 15 (−2.36 points), possibly due to seasonal factors or other system-wide pressures
- Switching branches essentially maintained their pre-switch performance (−0.05 points), suggesting the new procedure helped buffer against whatever factors were depressing scores elsewhere

---

### **Next Steps**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** of the new rotation and shelving procedure to all 60 branches still using the old method

2. **Monitor implementation** over the next 8–12 weeks to confirm the effect persists at scale and identify any branches that may need additional support during transition

3. **Investigate the control group decline**: The drop in service scores at non-switching branches warrants attention. Consider whether the new procedure should be prioritized for branches showing the steepest declines

4. **Document best practices** from the 90 early-adopter branches to support training and smooth implementation at remaining locations

---

*Analysis based on weekly service score data from weeks 6–22. Standard errors clustered at the branch level to account for within-branch correlation.*
