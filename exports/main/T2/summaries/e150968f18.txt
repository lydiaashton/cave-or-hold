# **Analysis of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

The new stock rotation and shelving procedure shows a **positive but statistically inconclusive effect** on service scores. Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores improve by approximately **2 points (4% improvement)** relative to branches that kept the old procedure. However, this result is **not statistically significant** at conventional levels, meaning we cannot confidently rule out that the observed improvement occurred by chance. **We do not recommend a full rollout at this time** without additional evidence.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Treatment Effect Estimate**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated improvement | **+2.0 points** |
| Relative improvement | **+4.1%** of average score |
| Statistical significance | **Not significant** (p = 0.14) |
| 95% Confidence Interval | **-0.6 to +4.7 points** |

The confidence interval tells us the true effect could range from a small negative impact to a meaningful positive impact of nearly 5 points.

### **2. What Actually Happened**
- **Switched branches** (20 branches): Scores rose from 47.9 to 49.6 (+1.7 points)
- **Non-switched branches** (14 branches): Scores fell slightly from 51.1 to 50.8 (-0.3 points)

The difference-in-differences approach isolates the procedure's effect by comparing these changes, yielding the +2.0 point estimate.

### **3. Data Quality & Validity**
- **Parallel trends assumption appears reasonable**: Before the switch, both groups showed similar week-to-week patterns in service scores
- **Analysis controls for**: Branch-specific characteristics (some branches consistently score higher/lower) and week-specific factors (seasonal variations)
- **Sample size**: 34 branches over 17 weeks (578 observations)

### **4. Why the Result is Inconclusive**
The main limitation is **statistical power**. With only 34 branches and substantial week-to-week variation in service scores (standard deviation ~10 points), the study cannot detect effects smaller than about 3-4 points with confidence. The observed 2-point improvement could be:
- A real but modest benefit from the new procedure
- Random fluctuation in the data

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended Actions**

1. **Do not proceed with full rollout yet** — The evidence is suggestive but not strong enough to justify system-wide implementation costs.

2. **Extend the evaluation period** — Continue monitoring both groups for an additional 8-12 weeks. More post-switch data would increase statistical power and clarify whether the effect persists or grows.

3. **Consider a phased expansion** — If operational capacity allows, switch an additional 10-15 branches and compare against the current control group. This would strengthen the evidence base.

4. **Investigate heterogeneity** — Analyze whether the procedure works better for certain branch types (e.g., by size, location, or baseline performance). Targeted rollout to high-benefit branches may be warranted even without system-wide evidence.

5. **Review cost-benefit tradeoffs** — Even if the 2-point improvement is real, the board should weigh this against implementation costs, staff training time, and any operational disruptions.
