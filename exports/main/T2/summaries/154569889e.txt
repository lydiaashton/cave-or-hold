# **Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that switched to the new rotation and shelving procedure saw their service scores increase by an average of **1.8 points** (approximately 4% improvement) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant and represents a meaningful improvement in service quality.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **150 branches** total: 90 switched to the new procedure, 60 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach, comparing changes in switched branches against non-switched branches while controlling for branch-specific characteristics and week-to-week variation

### **Results**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated improvement from new procedure | **+1.84 points** |
| 95% Confidence interval | **1.08 to 2.60 points** |
| Statistical significance | **p < 0.001** (highly significant) |
| Effect size | **3.7%** of average service score |

### **Supporting Evidence**
- **Parallel trends validated**: Before the switch, switched and non-switched branches showed no significant difference in their service score trends (p=0.61), supporting the validity of the comparison
- **Consistent across models**: The estimated effect remains stable (~1.8 points) whether we use simple comparisons or control for branch and week fixed effects
- **Visual pattern**: Service scores for switched branches rose noticeably after week 15, while control branch scores remained relatively flat or declined slightly

### **What This Means**
The data contradicts the initial expectation that the new procedure would make no difference. Instead, the new rotation and shelving procedure produced a **reliable, positive improvement** in service scores across the 90 branches that adopted it.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Recommend rollout**: Based on this evidence, we recommend extending the new procedure to all remaining branches. The consistent positive effect and strong statistical support justify system-wide adoption.

2. **Monitor implementation**: As additional branches switch, continue tracking service scores to confirm the effect holds across different branch contexts and over longer time periods.

3. **Investigate mechanisms**: Consider studying *why* the new procedure improved scores (e.g., faster item location, better stock organization, staff efficiency) to optimize training and implementation.

4. **Cost-benefit analysis**: If the new procedure involves additional costs (training, labor, equipment), compare those against the 3.7% service score improvement to confirm the change delivers value.

5. **Timeline**: A phased rollout over 4–8 weeks would allow for monitoring and adjustment while minimizing disruption.
