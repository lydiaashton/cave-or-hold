**Prepared for the Board of Directors**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure produced a modest but statistically significant improvement in service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **1.8 points** (about **3.7%**) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. However, the effect appears strongest in the first two weeks after implementation and becomes less certain thereafter.

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Statistically Significant but Modest Effect
- **Overall treatment effect:** +1.80 points (95% CI: 0.61 to 2.99)
- **Statistical significance:** p=0.003
- **Effect size:** 0.18 standard deviations (modest by social science standards)

### 2. Effect May Diminish Over Time
| Period | Average Effect | Statistically Significant? |
|--------|---------------|---------------------------|
| Weeks 15-16 (immediate) | +1.98 points | Yes |
| Weeks 17-22 (sustained) | +1.37 points | No (CI includes zero) |

This pattern suggests either:
- A short-term "novelty effect" that fades, or
- Genuine improvement with insufficient data to confirm persistence

### 3. Valid Comparison Between Groups
Pre-switch trends were parallel (p=0.44), supporting the causal interpretation that the procedure change—not pre-existing differences—drove the improvement.

### 4. Effect Holds After Controlling for Other Factors
The result persists when accounting for staffing levels and visitor volumes, suggesting the effect is attributable to the procedure itself.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve phased rollout** rather than immediate system-wide adoption. Start with 20-30 additional branches.

2. **Extend monitoring period** to 12+ weeks post-implementation to determine if effects persist or fade.

3. **Track implementation fidelity** to ensure branches are correctly following the new procedure.

4. **Conduct cost-benefit analysis** before full rollout—weigh the modest service score gain against any additional labor or training costs.

5. **Reconvene in 3 months** to review extended data before deciding on system-wide adoption.
