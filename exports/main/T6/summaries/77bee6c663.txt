# **MEMORANDUM**

**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence is inconclusive.** While branches using the new procedure do show higher service scores, we cannot determine whether the procedure *caused* this improvement. Branches that adopted the new procedure were already outperforming others before the switch. We recommend **additional analysis before proceeding with system-wide rollout**.

---

## **Key Findings**

### What the Data Shows

| Metric | Result |
|--------|--------|
| Service score difference (post-switch) | +4.0 points |
| Standard DiD estimate | +2.2 points |
| **Reliability of estimate** | **Low – key assumption violated** |

### The Core Problem: Pre-Existing Trends

Branches that adopted the new procedure were already improving relative to other branches *before* the switch:

| Week | Gap (Adopters – Non-adopters) |
|------|-------------------------------|
| Week 6 | 0.4 points |
| Week 10 | 1.9 points |
| Week 14 | 3.6 points |

This gap grew by **0.4 points per week** during the pre-period. This suggests adopters differed systematically from non-adopters—perhaps more motivated management, better resources, or greater capacity for change.

### What Happened After the Switch?

| Period | Average Gap |
|--------|-------------|
| Pre-switch (weeks 6–14) | 1.7 points |
| Post-switch (weeks 15–22) | 4.0 points |

The gap increased by only **0.4 points** after the switch—consistent with the pre-existing trend continuing, not a new treatment effect.

---

## **Why This Matters**

The standard analysis method (difference-in-differences) assumes treatment and control groups would have followed similar paths absent the intervention. **This assumption does not hold here.** The estimated 2.2-point improvement likely reflects:

1. **Selection bias**: Better-performing or more motivated branches chose to adopt early
2. **Pre-existing momentum**: These branches were already improving
3. **Unknown confounders**: Other factors we cannot measure may explain both adoption and improvement

---

## **Recommendation**

**Do not proceed with system-wide rollout yet.** Instead:

1. **Extend the observation period**: Collect 8–12 more weeks of data to see if the gap stabilizes, grows, or shrinks

2. **Consider a randomized rollout**: For remaining branches, randomly assign adoption timing to enable cleaner causal inference

3. **Investigate adopter characteristics**: Understand what distinguished early adopters—this may reveal who benefits most

4. **Pilot with matched comparison**: Select new adopters that closely match non-adopters on pre-period performance

---

## **Bottom Line Restated**

The branches using the new procedure have higher scores, but we cannot confidently attribute this to the procedure itself. Rolling out system-wide based on current evidence risks investing in a change that may not deliver the expected benefits. A more rigorous evaluation is warranted before committing cooperative-wide resources.
