# **Board Memorandum**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure had a measurable positive effect, but not in the way we expected.** It did not raise service scores in absolute terms. Instead, it **protected branches from a decline** that affected branches using the old procedure. Switched branches held steady while non-switched branches dropped 2.4 points. The causal benefit is **+2.3 points relative to what would have happened otherwise**. We recommend rollout, but should recalibrate expectations: this procedure maintains service quality rather than enhancing it.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
- **709 branches**: 425 adopted new procedure, 284 kept old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22), switch occurred at week 15
- Groups were **statistically comparable before the switch** (p=0.46)

### What Actually Happened

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| New procedure | 51.92 | 51.75 | -0.17 (stable) |
| Old procedure | 52.11 | 49.67 | -2.44 (declined) |

### The Causal Effect
**+2.27 points** (95% CI: +1.70 to +2.81, p < 0.001)

This means: without the new procedure, switched branches would likely have declined by ~2.4 points like the control group. The new procedure prevented that decline.

### Why This Matters
Something caused system-wide service score deterioration during this period. The new procedure provided **operational resilience** that the old procedure lacked. This is valuable—but it's different from the improvement we anticipated.

---

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with rollout**, but with adjusted expectations:
- The new procedure is worth adopting (preventing a 2.4-point decline is meaningful)
- It will likely **maintain** service quality, not enhance it
- If we want actual score improvements, we need to investigate what caused the system-wide decline and address it separately

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Approve rollout** to remaining 284 branches to prevent further service quality erosion.

2. **Investigate the system-wide decline** affecting non-switched branches. What drove the 2.4-point drop? This could reveal larger improvement opportunities.

3. **Set realistic targets**: Frame the new procedure as quality maintenance, not quality enhancement.

4. **Monitor post-rollout** for 8–12 weeks to confirm the protective effect persists at scale.

---

*Analysis based on difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors. The parallel trends assumption is satisfied (pre-switch groups statistically equivalent, p=0.46).*
