# **Revised Memorandum to the Board of Directors**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** [Current Date]  
**Subject:** Results of New Branch Procedure Pilot — **Inconclusive Evidence**

---

## **Executive Summary**

**We cannot confidently conclude the new procedure improved service scores.** While branches using the new procedure showed higher scores after the switch, **this difference was already emerging before the procedure changed**. The apparent 2-point improvement is largely explained by pre-existing trends, not the procedure itself.

**Bottom Line:** The evidence is **inconclusive**. We recommend **additional evaluation before system-wide rollout**.

---

## **Critical Finding: Parallel Trends Assumption Violated**

The standard analysis method (difference-in-differences) requires that treatment and control groups would have followed similar trends absent the intervention. **This assumption does not hold in our data.**

| Metric | Finding |
|--------|---------|
| **Pre-period gap trend** | Widening by 0.24 points per week (p = 0.001) |
| **Expected gap increase** (from pre-trend alone) | +1.91 points over 8 weeks |
| **Actual gap increase** (post-period) | +2.00 points over 8 weeks |
| **Effect attributable to procedure** | **~0.09 points** (not statistically distinguishable from zero) |

In other words: **Almost the entire observed "effect" was already happening before the switch.**

---

## **Why This Matters**

Branches selected for the pilot may have differed systematically from control branches:
- They may have had more resources, better management, or more motivated staff
- They may have been on an upward trajectory regardless of the procedure
- The selection process itself (not random) introduces bias we cannot fully adjust for

The statistical models that produced the "2-point improvement" estimate **do not account for diverging pre-trends**, making that estimate unreliable.

---

## **Recommendation**

**Do not proceed with system-wide rollout based on current evidence.**

Instead:

| Action | Rationale |
|--------|-----------|
| **Pause rollout decision** | Evidence does not support the expected benefit |
| **Extend evaluation period** | Collect more post-switch data to see if trends diverge further |
| **Consider randomized rollout** | Future pilots should use random assignment to ensure valid comparison |
| **Investigate selection criteria** | Understand why pilot branches were outperforming before the switch |
| **Gather qualitative feedback** | Survey staff and patrons about perceived changes from the new procedure |

---

## **Questions for Discussion**

1. How were branches selected for the pilot? Were they chosen because they were already high-performing?
2. Should we run a randomized controlled trial with proper assignment before full rollout?
3. Are there other metrics (staff efficiency, cost savings, patron satisfaction) that might show clearer benefits?
4. What is the cost of the new procedure, and does even a small potential benefit justify implementation?

---

## **Bottom Line (Restated)**

We wanted to find that the new procedure works. The data does not support that conclusion with confidence. **The apparent improvement existed before the procedure changed.** Rolling out system-wide based on current evidence would be premature.
