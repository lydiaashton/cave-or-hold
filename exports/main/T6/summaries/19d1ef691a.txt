# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Library Cooperative Board  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**RE:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**We cannot confidently conclude the new procedure caused the improvement.** While branches that adopted the new procedure showed higher service scores after the switch, these same branches were **already improving faster than others before they switched**. The observed improvement is largely consistent with this pre-existing trend rather than the procedure itself. **We recommend pausing the rollout and conducting a more rigorous evaluation.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### What the Data Shows

| Measure | Finding |
|---------|---------|
| Raw score difference (post-switch) | +2.3 points higher for adopting branches |
| Pre-existing trend (before switch) | Gap was already widening by **0.38 points/week** |
| Post-switch trend | Gap widened by only **0.21 points/week** (slower than before) |
| Estimated causal effect | **Uncertain – possibly zero** |

### The Critical Issue: Selection Bias

Branches that chose to adopt the new procedure were **already on an upward trajectory** before the switch:

| Week | Treatment-Control Gap |
|------|----------------------|
| 6 | 0.36 points |
| 10 | 1.87 points |
| 14 (last pre-switch week) | 3.60 points |
| 22 (final week) | 4.65 points |

The gap grew by **3.24 points** during the pre-period (weeks 6–14) and only **1.05 points** during the post-period (weeks 15–22). This pattern suggests the adopting branches were different from the start—perhaps more motivated, better staffed, or better managed—not that the procedure caused improvement.

### Statistical Assessment

When we account for the pre-existing trend:
- **Expected** gap increase (based on pre-period trend): 3.07 points
- **Actual** gap increase: 2.27 points
- **Unexplained portion** (potential treatment effect): **-0.80 points**

This suggests the new procedure may have had **no effect or even a slight negative effect** once we account for the fact that adopting branches were already improving.

---

## **Recommendations**

| Priority | Action |
|----------|--------|
| **1** | **Do not proceed with system-wide rollout** until causality is established |
| **2** | **Conduct a randomized trial**: Randomly assign some branches to adopt the procedure while others wait, ensuring comparable groups |
| **3** | **Investigate why early adopters were already improving**: Interview these branches to understand what else may have driven their gains |
| **4** | **Continue monitoring**: Keep collecting data to see if the pattern changes over time |
| **5** | **Consider qualitative feedback**: Survey staff at adopting branches about whether they perceive the procedure as helpful |

---

## **Why This Matters**

Rolling out a procedure that doesn't actually improve service would:
- Waste training resources and staff time
- Disrupt operations without benefit
- Miss the opportunity to identify what's *actually* driving improvement at the high-performing branches

---

## **Conclusion**

The data does not support the conclusion that the new procedure improves service scores. The apparent improvement is better explained by pre-existing differences between branches that chose to adopt versus those that didn't. Before investing in a system-wide rollout, we need stronger evidence—ideally from a randomized evaluation—that the procedure itself causes improvement.

---

*Analysis based on branches.csv (12,053 observations, 709 branches, weeks 6–22). The key limitation is non-random adoption of the new procedure, which prevents causal inference.*
