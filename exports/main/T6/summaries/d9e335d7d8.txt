# **Board Summary: Impact of New Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure on Service Scores**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure produced a small but statistically reliable improvement in service scores—contrary to our expectation of no effect.** Branches that switched saw scores increase by approximately **2 points** (about 4%) relative to branches that kept the old procedure. 

**Recommendation:** The effect is real but modest. Proceed with rollout **if** the operational benefits (faster shelving, better inventory management, etc.) justify the transition costs. Do not expect dramatic service improvements, but also do not expect service degradation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Statistically Significant, Practically Modest Effect
| Metric | Result | Interpretation |
|--------|--------|----------------|
| **Estimated effect** | +2.05 points | Real, detectable improvement |
| **95% Confidence interval** | 0.8 to 3.3 points | Even the lower bound is positive |
| **Statistical significance** | p = 0.001 | Very unlikely to be random chance |
| **Effect size** | 0.21 standard deviations | Small-to-moderate |
| **As % of average score** | ~4% | Modest in practical terms |

### 2. What the Data Shows
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches (90) | 51.42 | 52.55 | **+1.14** |
| Control branches (60) | 52.12 | 51.20 | **-0.91** |

The switched branches improved slightly; control branches declined slightly. The **net effect is +2.05 points**.

### 3. Important Context
- **Week-to-week volatility averages 3 points**—larger than the treatment effect. The improvement is detectable statistically but would not be obvious week-to-week at any single branch.
- **99.7% of score variation** is explained by factors other than the procedure change (staffing, visits, random variation, etc.).
- **61% of switched branches** improved vs. 40% of control branches—consistent but not universal.

### 4. Why We're Confident This Is Real (Not Noise)
- **Parallel pre-trends**: Before week 15, switched and control branches moved together (no significant difference in trends)
- **Clear divergence post-switch**: The gap opened after week 15 and persisted
- **Robust to controls**: Effect holds after accounting for staffing and visitor volume
- **Appropriate statistical methods**: Standard errors account for repeated observations per branch

---

## **Next Steps**

### If Proceeding with Rollout:
1. **Set accurate expectations**: Communicate that branches should expect a 1-3 point improvement, not a transformation.
2. **Monitor for 8-12 weeks**: Confirm the effect holds during system-wide implementation.
3. **Investigate the mechanism**: Since we didn't expect this effect, understand *why* it happened (staff efficiency? better findability?) to inform future decisions.

### If Pausing to Reconsider:
1. **Cost-benefit analysis**: Does a 2-point service score improvement justify the transition costs?
2. **Operational benefits**: The procedure may still be worthwhile for non-service reasons (inventory accuracy, staff time savings, etc.).
3. **Pilot expansion**: Consider switching 15-20 more branches and monitoring before full rollout.

---

## **Bottom Line for Decision-Makers**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did the procedure change service scores? | **Yes, positively** |
| Is the finding statistically reliable? | **Yes** (p=0.001) |
| Is the effect large? | **No**—about 4% improvement |
| Should we expect service degradation? | **No evidence of this** |
| Should we roll out system-wide? | **Depends on transition costs** and whether operational benefits alone justify it |
