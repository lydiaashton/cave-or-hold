## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure protected service scores from a system-wide decline and should be rolled out to all branches.**

After week 15, service scores dropped across the system. Branches using the new procedure held steady while others declined by 2.4 points. The net benefit is **preventing a 2.3-point drop**—a meaningful protection of service quality.

---

## **Key Findings**

### What the Data Shows

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|----------------|---------------|--------|
| **New Procedure** | 51.92 | 51.75 | -0.17 |
| **Old Procedure** | 52.11 | 49.67 | **-2.44** |

### The Effect

- **Treatment benefit:** +2.27 points relative to non-adopters (95% CI: 1.8 to 2.7; p < 0.001)
- **In practical terms:** The new procedure prevented decline, it did not create improvement
- **System-wide context:** Something caused scores to drop after week 15 (seasonal? operational?); the new procedure shielded branches from this

### Why We're Confident

1. **Parallel pre-trends:** Both groups moved together before week 15
2. **Robust to controls:** Effect holds accounting for staffing, visits, and branch characteristics
3. **Clean identification:** 425 branches switched, 284 did not—natural experiment design

---

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with system-wide rollout.** Even though the procedure doesn't boost scores above baseline, it provides important protection against declines. In a period where service scores are vulnerable, this resilience is valuable.
