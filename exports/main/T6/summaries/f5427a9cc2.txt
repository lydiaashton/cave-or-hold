## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The evidence does not support system-wide rollout of the new procedure.** 

While branches that adopted the new procedure showed higher service scores (+1.9 points), **critical methodological concerns undermine this finding**:

| Issue | Concern |
|-------|---------|
| **Pre-trend violation** | Switched branches were already on different trajectories before the change |
| **Statistical uncertainty** | p = 0.09; 95% CI includes zero (-0.3 to +4.2) |
| **Small effect** | Even if real, effect size is modest (d = 0.22) |

**Recommendation:** Do not proceed with rollout. Consider discontinuing the procedure or conducting a proper randomized trial.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **1. Primary Analysis Results**

| Measure | Value | Interpretation |
|---------|-------|----------------|
| Estimated effect | +1.94 points | Potentially overstated |
| 95% Confidence interval | -0.3 to +4.2 | Includes no effect |
| P-value | 0.09 | Not statistically significant |
| Effect size (Cohen's d) | 0.22 | Small |

### **2. Critical Methodological Concern**

**Pre-Trend Violation:** Our event study analysis examined week-by-week differences between switched and control branches. Results show:

- **Weeks -8, -6, -5, -4, -3** (all BEFORE the switch) showed statistically significant differences
- This indicates switched branches were **already performing differently** before adopting the new procedure
- The difference-in-differences method assumes parallel pre-trends—**this assumption is violated**
- **Conclusion:** The +1.94 point estimate likely captures pre-existing differences, not the procedure's effect

### **3. Branch-Level Outcomes**

| Group | Branches | % Improved | Avg. Change |
|-------|----------|------------|-------------|
| Switched | 20 | 70% | +1.9 |
| Control | 14 | 50% | -0.1 |

While more switched branches improved, this pattern existed before the switch as well.

### **4. Study Limitations**

- Only 34 branches total (20 treatment, 14 control)
- Only 8 weeks of post-switch data
- Non-random assignment to treatment (branches self-selected or were selected for switching)
- Pre-existing differences between groups

## **NEXT STEPS**

### **Option A: Discontinue the Procedure**
If the new procedure requires ongoing training, monitoring, or operational changes, the weak evidence does not justify continued investment.

### **Option B: Conduct a Proper Randomized Trial**
If the board believes the procedure has potential:

| Step | Action |
|------|--------|
| 1 | Randomly assign 20-30 branches to treatment vs. control |
| 2 | Verify parallel pre-trends over 8+ weeks |
| 3 | Implement procedure only in treatment group |
| 4 | Collect 12+ weeks of post-implementation data |
| 5 | Re-analyze with valid causal inference |

### **Option C: Hybrid Approach**
- Maintain current procedure in already-switched branches (avoid disruption)
- Do not expand to additional branches
- Continue monitoring for 6 months
- Re-evaluate if clear patterns emerge

## **CONCLUSION**

The initial analysis suggested a positive effect, but **deeper examination reveals the evidence is not reliable**. The pre-trend violation is a fundamental flaw that cannot be corrected with the existing data. 

The board should **not approve system-wide rollout** based on this evidence. A decision to continue, discontinue, or properly re-test the procedure should be made based on operational considerations (cost, staff feedback, implementation feasibility) rather than the inconclusive service score data.
