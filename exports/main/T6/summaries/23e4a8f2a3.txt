# **REVISED MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD OF DIRECTORS**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure  
**Recommendation:** **Do Not Proceed** with System-Wide Rollout Based on Current Evidence

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The analysis does NOT demonstrate that the new procedure caused the improvement in service scores.** While branches using the new procedure did show higher score gains, this appears to reflect **pre-existing differences** between those branches and the control group—not a causal effect of the intervention.

**We cannot recommend system-wide rollout based on this evidence.**

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### 1. The Core Problem: Parallel Trends Violation

The standard method for this analysis (Difference-in-Differences) requires that treatment and control branches were on similar trajectories before the intervention. **This assumption is violated:**

| Period | Treatment-Control Gap | Trend |
|--------|----------------------|-------|
| Weeks 6-11 | -0.76 to +0.70 | Treatment catching up |
| Weeks 12-14 | +1.71 to +2.23 | **Gap widening by 0.37 points/week** |
| Weeks 15-22 | +1.89 to +3.14 | Gap widening by 0.15 points/week |

**Critical observation:** The gap between treatment and control branches was already widening **before** the switch occurred. By week 14 (the week before switching), treatment branches were already outperforming control branches by 2.23 points.

### 2. No Discontinuous Jump at Intervention

If the new procedure caused an improvement, we would expect to see a sudden increase in the gap at week 15. Instead:

- **Week 14 gap:** 2.23 points
- **Week 15 gap:** 2.08 points (actually **decreased**)
- **Week 16 gap:** 2.62 points

There is no evidence of a level shift attributable to the intervention.

### 3. Counterfactual Analysis

If we project the pre-intervention trend forward, the predicted gaps are very similar to (actually larger than) the observed gaps:

| Week | Actual Gap | Predicted Gap (if pre-trend continued) |
|------|------------|----------------------------------------|
| 15 | 2.08 | 2.55 |
| 18 | 2.55 | 3.65 |
| 22 | 2.93 | 5.12 |

**Average difference:** Actual gaps were 1.21 points **lower** than predicted. This suggests the intervention may have had **no positive effect**—and possibly even slowed the improvement trend.

### 4. What the Original +1.90 Estimate Actually Captures

The initial Difference-in-Differences estimate of +1.90 points does not represent a causal treatment effect. Instead, it captures:
- Pre-existing differences between branches that chose to adopt vs. those that didn't
- An ongoing convergence/divergence pattern that began before the intervention
- **Not** the effect of the new procedure itself

---

## **WHY DID TREATMENT BRANCHES IMPROVE MORE?**

Several alternative explanations are plausible:

1. **Selection bias:** Branches that volunteered/were selected for the new procedure may have been systematically different (e.g., more motivated staff, better management, more resources)

2. **Regression to the mean:** Some treatment branches had lower scores in early weeks and were naturally recovering

3. **Secular trends:** Treatment branches may have been implementing other improvements simultaneously

4. **The procedure itself may have no effect:** The observed differences may be entirely due to branch characteristics, not the procedure

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

### Recommended Actions

1. **Do NOT proceed with system-wide rollout** based on current evidence

2. **Conduct a proper randomized controlled trial:**
   - Randomly assign branches to treatment and control
   - Ensure parallel trends before intervention
   - Measure outcomes over a longer period

3. **Gather qualitative data:**
   - Interview staff at treatment branches about implementation
   - Survey patrons about their experience
   - Identify any operational challenges or benefits not captured in service scores

4. **Consider alternative metrics:**
   - Item retrieval time
   - Patron satisfaction surveys
   - Staff efficiency measures

### If Proceeding Anyway

If the Board wishes to proceed despite the weak evidence:
- Implement as a **pilot** with rigorous monitoring
- Use **random assignment** for the next phase
- Pre-register success criteria before expansion
- Budget for evaluation and potential reversal

---

## **CONCLUSION**

The data shows that branches using the new procedure had higher service score gains. However, **this correlation does not establish causation.** The branches that adopted the new procedure were already on a different trajectory before the intervention, and there is no clear evidence that the procedure itself caused any improvement.

**Rolling out the new procedure system-wide based on this analysis would be premature and potentially wasteful.** We recommend either:
1. Conducting a properly designed randomized trial, or
2. Gathering additional evidence before making an expansion decision
