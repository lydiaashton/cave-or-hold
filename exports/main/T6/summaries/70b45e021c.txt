# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Contrary to expectations, the data suggests a modest improvement in service scores**, but the evidence has limitations that warrant caution. Branches that switched showed a **2.5-point increase** relative to non-switched branches (statistically significant, p < 0.001). However, given the high week-to-week volatility in scores and only 8 weeks of post-switch data, we recommend **a phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than immediate system-wide adoption.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. **Statistically Significant but Modest Effect**
- Difference-in-differences estimate: **+2.48 points** (95% CI: 1.6 to 3.5)
- This finding contradicts our prior expectation of no impact
- Effect size is meaningful but modest relative to typical week-to-week variation (~3.3 points)

### 2. **Important Context on the Comparison**
- Before the switch, treatment branches scored **1.2 points LOWER** than control branches on average
- After the switch, treatment branches scored **1.3 points HIGHER**
- The 2.5-point "effect" represents treatment branches moving from slightly worse to slightly better relative to control

### 3. **High Volatility in Service Scores**
- Week-to-week fluctuations are large (3–9 point swings are common)
- This makes it harder to isolate the procedure effect from normal variation
- The signal is detectable but operates against considerable noise

### 4. **Limited Post-Switch Observation Period**
- Only 8 weeks of data after the switch
- Cannot assess whether the effect persists, grows, or diminishes over time
- Cannot assess seasonal effects (data spans weeks 6–22 only)

---

## **Next Steps**

### Recommended Approach: **Cautious Phased Rollout**

1. **Expand to additional branches** — switch 20–30 more branches over the next 4–6 weeks

2. **Extend the observation period** — collect at least 12–16 weeks of post-switch data to confirm the effect persists

3. **Monitor for heterogeneity** — check whether the effect is consistent across different branch types, sizes, and regions

4. **Assess implementation costs** — before full rollout, quantify training time, labor hours, and any equipment needs

5. **Investigate the mechanism** — the unexpected service score improvement deserves understanding. Consider brief staff surveys to identify what changed (e.g., faster item retrieval, more patron interaction time)

6. **Revisit the decision** — after 12–16 weeks of additional data, reconvene to decide on system-wide rollout

---

## **Technical Note**

Analysis uses difference-in-differences regression with standard errors clustered at the branch level. The parallel trends assumption appears satisfied (no differential pre-trends detected, p = 0.87). Bootstrap validation confirms the DiD estimate is stable. However, the low R-squared (~0.01) indicates most score variation is unexplained by the model, reflecting high inherent volatility in weekly service scores.

---

**Bottom line for the board**: The data provides evidence of improvement, but given the unexpected nature of the finding and data limitations, a measured approach with continued evaluation is prudent before committing to system-wide rollout.
