## **Key Findings**

### **The Apparent Effect Disappears When Accounting for Pre-Existing Trends**

| Analysis | Estimated Effect |

### **Event Study Shows No Treatment Effect**

The gap between switched and non-switched branches:
- **Before switch (weeks 6-14)**: Widening by ~1.5 points
- **After switch (weeks 15-22)**: Widening by ~0.5 points
- **At the switch point**: No observable jump or change in trajectory

This pattern indicates the difference reflects **which branches chose to switch**, not the effect of switching itself.

### **What This Means**

Branches that volunteered for the new procedure may have:
- More engaged management
- Better baseline operations
- Greater capacity for implementation

These factors—not the procedure itself—likely explain the score differences.

---

## **Recommendations**

### **1. Do Not Approve System-Wide Rollout Yet**

The current evidence does not support a causal claim that the new procedure improves service.

### **2. Conduct a Randomized Controlled Trial**

To properly evaluate the procedure:
- Randomly assign remaining branches to adopt or not adopt
- This eliminates selection bias
- Provides a clean causal estimate

### **3. Alternative: Staggered Rollout with Rigorous Monitoring**

If randomized assignment is not feasible:
- Roll out in random order across branches
- Collect several weeks of data before and after each branch's switch
- Use modern causal inference methods (e.g., Callaway-Sant'Anna estimator)

### **4. Investigate Why Some Branches Chose to Switch**

Understanding which branches self-selected may reveal:
- Characteristics associated with better performance
- Opportunities to support lower-performing branches
- Whether the procedure works better for certain branch types

---

## **Bottom Line Restated**

**The new procedure may help, may hurt, or may have no effect—we simply cannot tell from this data.** Rolling it out system-wide based on the current evidence risks implementing an ineffective change across all branches. A proper evaluation is needed before making that decision.
