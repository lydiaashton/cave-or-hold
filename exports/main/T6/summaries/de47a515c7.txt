# Board Summary: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## Bottom Line

The new procedure **likely improves service scores**, with an estimated gain of about **2 points** (4% improvement). While the result falls just short of conventional statistical significance (p = 0.09), there is approximately a **95% probability the true effect is positive**. The main limitation is sample size, not lack of effect.

**Recommendation:** The evidence supports moving forward with broader implementation, while continuing to monitor outcomes to confirm the effect persists at scale.

---

## Key Findings

### Results Summary

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect | **+1.9 points** |
| P-value | **0.092** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | **-0.3 to +4.1** |
| Probability effect is positive | **~95%** |
| Effect size (Cohen's d) | **0.20** (small-to-moderate) |

### What the Data Shows

1. **Treatment branches improved** relative to control branches after the switch (+1.9 points)
2. **The effect is in the expected direction** and substantively meaningful
3. **Statistical uncertainty remains** due to limited sample size (34 branches, 17 weeks)
4. **The confidence interval is mostly positive**—only a small portion includes zero or negative values

### Why This Isn't "Statistically Significant" at p<0.05

This is a **power issue**, not an evidence issue. With only 34 branches and modest week-to-week variation in service scores, the study lacks the precision to rule out random chance at the 5% threshold. However, the **weight of evidence favors a real positive effect**.

---

## Recommendation

**Proceed with rollout, with monitoring:**

1. **Expand implementation** to remaining branches over the next 1–2 quarters
2. **Continue tracking service scores** to verify the effect persists
3. **Document any implementation challenges** that could affect outcomes
4. **Re-evaluate after 6 months** with the larger dataset

The risk of adopting a procedure that shows a 95% probability of improvement is low, while the potential benefit to service quality is meaningful.

---

## Decision Context

| If we rollout and effect is real | If we rollout and effect is zero | If we delay and effect is real |
|----------------------------------|----------------------------------|--------------------------------|
| ✓ Service improves ~4% | ~ No harm, minor cost | ✗ Missed opportunity for improvement |
| ✓ Validated innovation | ✓ Learning opportunity | ✗ Branches continue with inferior process |

The asymmetry favors action: the cost of being wrong is low, but the cost of delay is continued lower service scores at branches that could benefit.
