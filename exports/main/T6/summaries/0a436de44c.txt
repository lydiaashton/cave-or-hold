## **CRITICAL FINDING: PRE-EXISTING TREND**

The fundamental assumption of our analysis—that treatment and control branches were on similar trajectories before the switch—**was violated**.

### Pre-Switch Trend (Weeks 6-14)

| Week | Treatment-Control Gap |
|------|----------------------|
| 6 | -0.36 |
| 9 | +0.86 |
| 12 | +1.30 |
| 14 | +2.11 |

**The gap was already widening by 0.28 points per week** (p < 0.001) before any branch switched procedures. This suggests branches that chose to switch were already on an upward trajectory.

### What This Means

If the pre-switch trend had continued:
- **Predicted** post-switch gap: **3.27 points**
- **Actual** post-switch gap: **2.90 points**
- **Effect attributable to new procedure: -0.37 points**

In other words, after accounting for the pre-existing trend, there is **no evidence the new procedure provided additional benefit**.

---

## **WHY THE INITIAL ANALYSIS WAS MISLEADING**

The standard difference-in-differences method assumes parallel pre-trends. When this assumption is violated:
- The treatment effect is **biased upward**
- What looks like a "treatment effect" may just be **continuation of an existing trend**
- Branches that volunteered to switch may have been **more motivated or better-managed** from the start

---

## **RECOMMENDATIONS**

### **1. Pause System-Wide Rollout**
Do not proceed with full implementation until we have clearer evidence of causality.

### **2. Improve the Evaluation Design**
Options for more rigorous testing:
- **Randomized assignment**: Randomly assign branches to switch (not self-selection)
- **Staggered rollout with better controls**: Use branches switching in later waves as controls for earlier waves
- **Longer baseline**: Collect more pre-switch data to better establish trends

### **3. If Proceeding, Monitor Closely**
If the board decides to proceed despite uncertainty:
- Set clear success metrics (e.g., +1.0 point minimum improvement)
- Track each branch for 12 weeks post-switch
- Be prepared to revert branches that don't show improvement

---

## **CONCLUSION**

We share the board's expectation that the new procedure should help. However, **the data does not support a confident conclusion that it does**. The apparent improvement is explained by pre-existing differences between branches that chose to switch and those that didn't.

**Recommendation: Pause and redesign the evaluation before committing resources to a full rollout.**
