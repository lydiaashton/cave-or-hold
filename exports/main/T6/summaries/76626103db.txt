# **REVISED BOARD MEMORANDUM**

---

## **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

### **Bottom Line**

**We cannot confidently conclude the new procedure improved service scores.** While branches that adopted the new procedure showed 2.2 points higher scores after the switch, these same branches were **already improving faster before the change**. The pre-existing trend fully explains the observed difference. 

**Recommendation:** Do **not** proceed with a full rollout based on this evidence. Either (a) run a randomized trial to get credible causal evidence, or (b) gather more data with better controls before expanding.

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. What the Raw Data Shows**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Branches keeping old procedure | 50.45 | 50.85 | +0.40 |
| Branches adopting new procedure | 51.40 | 53.99 | +2.59 |
| **Apparent effect** | | | **+2.19** |

**2. The Critical Problem: Pre-Existing Trends**

Branches that switched were already on a stronger improvement trajectory **before** the procedure change:

| Week | Treatment-Control Gap |
|------|----------------------|
| Week 6 | -0.83 |
| Week 10 | +1.06 |
| Week 14 (just before switch) | +2.51 |
| Week 22 (8 weeks after) | +4.20 |

- Treatment branches were gaining **0.31 points per week faster** than control branches even before the switch (p < 0.001)
- Over 8 weeks, this pre-trend accounts for **~2.5 points** of apparent gain
- The observed 2.2-point effect is **fully explained by pre-existing differences**

**3. What This Means**

The branches that chose to adopt the new procedure were systematically different—perhaps better managed, better staffed, or more motivated. These underlying factors, not the procedure itself, likely drove the score improvements.

---

### **Next Steps**

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| **1** | **Pause rollout** | Current evidence does not support expansion |
| **2** | **Design a randomized trial** | Randomly assign branches to new vs. old procedure to eliminate selection bias |
| **3** | **Investigate adoption criteria** | Understand what distinguished early-adopting branches; these factors may matter more than the procedure |
| **4** | **Continue monitoring** | If rollout proceeds for other reasons, track outcomes carefully to detect any negative effects |

### **Questions for Discussion**

2. Should we test the new procedure on a random sample of branches before wider adoption?

### **Summary of the Revision**

My original bottom line was **too confident**. The difference-in-differences method requires that treatment and control groups would have followed parallel trends absent the intervention. The data clearly violates this assumption—treatment branches were already improving faster before the switch. After adjusting for this pre-trend, there is **no detectable causal effect** of the new procedure on service scores.
