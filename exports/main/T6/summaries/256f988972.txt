# **Board Summary: Impact of New Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure shows a statistically significant but modest improvement in service scores.** The estimated effect is **+2 points** (95% confidence interval: 0.8 to 3.3 points). However, the effect is not universal—about 40% of switched branches saw no improvement—and we have only 8 weeks of post-switch data.

**Recommendation: Proceed with rollout, but with measured expectations and continued monitoring.** This is a low-risk operational change with evidence of benefit, but it is not a transformative intervention.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Statistically Significant, Modest Effect
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches (90) | 51.4 | 52.6 | +1.1 |
| Control branches (60) | 52.1 | 51.2 | -0.9 |

**Net effect: +2.05 points** (p = 0.001; 95% CI: 0.8 to 3.3)

### 2. Important Limitations
- **Not all branches benefited**: 61% improved, but 39% did not
- **Short observation window**: Only 8 weeks of post-switch data
- **Control group declined**: Some of the effect reflects control branches getting worse, possibly due to seasonal factors
- **Small effect size**: 0.21 standard deviations; most score variation is due to other factors

### 3. What This Means in Practice
- A branch scoring 50 might expect to reach **51–53** after switching
- Some branches may see no benefit
- The effect may diminish or grow with more time—we don't yet know

## **Questions the Board May Have**

**Q: Is a 2-point improvement worth it?**
A: Without cost data, we can't calculate ROI. However, if this is primarily a procedural change (training and workflow) rather than a capital investment, the benefit likely justifies the cost.

**Q: Why did control branches decline?**
A: We don't know. It could be seasonal (later weeks in the year), regression to the mean, or external factors. This uncertainty means we should interpret the 2-point effect cautiously.

**Q: Should we roll out to all 60 branches at once?**
A: A phased rollout would allow continued learning and adjustment. Consider rolling out to 20–30 branches first, monitor for 4–8 weeks, then proceed with the remainder.
