# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD OF DIRECTORS**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Office  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure is associated with improved service scores, but we recommend a phased rollout with monitoring rather than immediate system-wide adoption.**

Branches that adopted the new procedure showed service score increases of **1.8 to 2.5 points** compared to branches that kept the old procedure. However, branches that chose to switch were already showing stronger improvement trends before the switch, which limits our confidence in attributing the full effect to the procedure itself.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### Study Overview
| | |
|---|---|
| Branches analyzed | 709 |
| Treatment branches (new procedure) | 425 |
| Control branches (old procedure) | 284 |
| Study period | 17 weeks (weeks 6–22, 2026) |
| Switch date | Week 15 |

### Results Summary

| Estimate | Effect Size | 95% Confidence Interval |
|----------|-------------|------------------------|
| Main analysis | +1.8 points | [1.3, 2.4] |
| Excluding weeks 11-14* | +2.5 points | [1.8, 3.2] |

*Using only weeks 6-10 as baseline (before treatment-control divergence)

### Important Caveat: Pre-Existing Trends

**Treatment branches were already improving faster than control branches before the switch.**

| Period | Treatment-Control Gap |
|--------|----------------------|
| Weeks 6-10 (early pre-period) | ≈ 0 points |
| Weeks 11-14 (late pre-period) | Widened to 2.5 points |
| Weeks 15-22 (post-switch) | 2.0 to 2.9 points |

This pattern suggests either:
- **Anticipation effects**: Branches prepared for the switch before week 15, OR
- **Selection bias**: Branches that opted into the new procedure were already on an upward trajectory

Because branches were not randomly assigned to switch, we cannot definitively separate the procedure's effect from pre-existing differences.

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

### Recommended Actions

| Priority | Action | Timeline |
|----------|--------|----------|
| 1 | **Phased rollout** to 50-100 additional branches | 4-8 weeks |
| 2 | Continue monitoring service scores weekly | Ongoing |
| 3 | Document implementation practices from early adopters | 4 weeks |
| 4 | Re-evaluate after 8 weeks of phased rollout data | 12 weeks |
| 5 | Consider full rollout if phased results confirm findings | After evaluation |

### Why a Phased Approach?

1. **Uncertainty about causal effect**: The pre-trend violation means the true effect could be smaller than observed
2. **Implementation risk**: Rolling out to all 284 remaining branches at once carries operational risk
3. **Learning opportunity**: A phased approach allows us to refine training and identify which branches benefit most
4. **Cost management**: Staged rollout spreads implementation costs over time

### Success Metrics for Phased Rollout

- Service score improvement of **1.0+ points** in phased branches
- No significant operational disruptions
- Staff feedback on procedure feasibility

---

## **CONCLUSION**

The evidence suggests the new procedure has a **positive association** with service scores, but non-random assignment limits our ability to claim definitive causal impact. The observed 1.8-2.5 point improvement may partially reflect pre-existing differences between branches that chose to switch versus those that did not.

**We recommend a cautious, phased expansion** that allows the cooperative to confirm the effect while managing implementation risk. If the phased rollout confirms similar improvements, full system-wide adoption would be warranted.
