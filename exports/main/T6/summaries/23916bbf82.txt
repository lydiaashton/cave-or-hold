# **Memorandum to the Board of Directors**

## **Bottom Line**

**Recommendation: Proceed with rollout, with measured expectations.**

The new procedure is associated with improved service scores, particularly for branches that were previously underperforming. The evidence supports adoption, but the effect is modest—approximately a 2-point improvement on a 50-point scale.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Important Context: Switched Branches Started Behind

Before the procedure change, branches that switched scored **1.2 points lower** on average than branches that kept the old procedure. This suggests the new procedure may have been adopted by branches seeking improvement, or was assigned to struggling branches.

### The Procedure Helped Close the Performance Gap

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| 90 branches (new procedure) | 51.1 | 53.6 | +2.5 |
| 60 branches (old procedure) | 52.3 | 52.8 | +0.5 |

**Net effect: +2.0 points** — switched branches closed the gap and now perform slightly better than non-switched branches.

### Statistical Confidence

- **95% Confidence Interval:** 1.2 to 2.9 points
- **Statistical Significance:** p < 0.001
- **Effect Size:** 0.2 standard deviations (modest but meaningful)

### What This Means

The new procedure appears to help branches improve relative to their peers. It did not produce dramatic gains, but it enabled lower-performing branches to catch up. There is no evidence of harm, and the statistical evidence for a positive effect is strong.

---

## **Recommendation & Next Steps**

1. **Approve rollout** to remaining branches, with realistic expectations (~2 point improvement).

2. **Prioritize branches with lower service scores** for early adoption, as they may benefit most.

3. **Monitor for 8–12 weeks post-rollout** to confirm effects persist at scale.

4. **Investigate what else distinguishes high-performing branches**, as the procedure alone does not explain all performance variation.

---

## **Caveats**

- This is observational data, not a randomized trial. While the analysis controls for many factors, unobserved differences between branches could influence results.
- The effect is statistically significant but modest in practical terms.
- Week-to-week variability remains substantial even after the procedure change.
