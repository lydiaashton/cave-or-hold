# **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Recommendation: Proceed with rollout, but with tempered expectations.**

The new procedure has a **statistically significant effect** (p=0.001), but the benefit is primarily **protective rather than transformative**. Treatment branches improved by only **+0.19 points**, while the 2.1-point gap emerged because control branches declined by **-1.87 points**. The new procedure appears to shield branches from deterioration rather than drive substantial gains.

**Key consideration for the board:** If the factors causing control-branch decline (possibly seasonal) will affect all branches going forward, the new procedure is valuable protection. If not, the actual benefit may be only ~0.2 points.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
| | |
|---|---|
| **Branches studied** | 150 (90 treatment, 60 control) |
| **Observation period** | 17 weeks (weeks 6–22) |
| **Switch date** | Week 15 |
| **Method** | Difference-in-differences analysis |

### What Actually Happened

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Actual Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|---------------|
| Control (Old Procedure) | 49.22 | 47.36 | **−1.87** |
| Treatment (New Procedure) | 50.91 | 51.10 | **+0.19** |

**The 2.12-point "treatment effect" decomposes as:**
- Treatment gain: +0.19 points
- Control loss: −1.87 points
- **Net gap: 2.12 points**

### Interpretation

The new procedure's value is **defensive**:
- ✓ **Prevents decline** when external pressures hit (seasonal? operational?)
- ✓ **Statistically robust** effect (p=0.001, clustered SE)
- ⚠️ **Modest active improvement** (+0.19 points is small on a ~50-point scale)

### Why Did Control Branches Decline?

Looking at weekly patterns, **both groups show similar week-to-week fluctuations** (both dip around weeks 16–17, both peak around week 21). This suggests a **systemic factor** affecting all branches—possibly:
- Seasonal visitor patterns
- Staffing changes
- Budget cycles

The new procedure appears to **insulate branches from these pressures**.

### Validity Assessment

| Check | Result |
|-------|--------|
| Parallel pre-trends | ✓ Passed (p=0.97 for differential trends) |
| Statistical significance | ✓ Strong (p=0.001) |
| Controls for confounders | ✓ Effect persists with staffing/visit controls |
| Practical significance | ⚠️ Modest (+0.19 actual gain) |

## **Next Steps**

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| 1 | **Investigate the control-group decline** before full rollout | Understand what caused the −1.87 drop; if it's temporary or fixable, the new procedure's value proposition changes |
| 2 | **Assess implementation costs** | A 0.2-point gain may not justify high costs; protection value depends on whether decline pressures persist |
| 3 | **Pilot at 10–15 control branches** | Test whether they also show protection (vs. just selection effects from early adopters) |
| 4 | **If proceeding, set realistic expectations** | Frame as "maintaining service quality" not "significant improvement" |

---

## **Decision Framework for the Board**

| If... | Then... |
|-------|---------|
| The control decline reflects ongoing pressures (seasonality, staffing constraints) | **Roll out**—the protection is valuable |
| The control decline was a one-time anomaly | **Reconsider**—actual benefit is only ~0.2 points |
| Implementation costs are low | **Roll out**—even modest gains may justify it |
| Implementation costs are high | **Pause**—investigate further before committing |
