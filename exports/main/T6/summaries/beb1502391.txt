# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure prevented service scores from declining.** Branches that switched maintained their service levels (essentially unchanged), while branches keeping the old procedure saw scores drop by nearly 2 points. The statistical evidence is strong that the new procedure provides protection against this decline. **We recommend rolling out the new procedure to all branches to prevent further deterioration.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. What Actually Happened
- **Switched branches**: Service scores remained stable (+0.1 points, not statistically meaningful)
- **Non-switched branches**: Service scores declined (-1.9 points)
- **Net effect**: +2.04 points relative to control (95% CI: 1.47 to 2.61, p < 0.001)

### 2. Interpretation
The new procedure doesn't appear to *boost* scores—it **prevents them from falling**. This suggests the old procedure may have an inherent problem that causes gradual deterioration, or the new procedure provides resilience against factors that normally drag scores down.

### 3. Effect Size
- 2-point prevention of decline on a ~50-point scale
- Represents 21% of one standard deviation
- Meaningful for maintaining service quality

### 4. Data Scope
- **709 branches** over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- Switch at **week 15** (8 weeks of post-switch data)
- **60% of branches** adopted the new procedure

---

## **Important Caveats**

1. **We don't know why the control group declined**. This could be seasonal, due to external factors, or inherent to the old procedure. Understanding this would strengthen the recommendation.

2. **Only 8 weeks of post-switch data**. We need longer-term monitoring to confirm the effect persists.

3. **Switched branches started slightly higher**. Our analysis controls for this, but it's worth noting.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with rollout**—the evidence supports preventing decline across all branches

2. **Investigate why non-switched branches declined**—is this seasonal? A problem with the old procedure? External factors?

3. **Monitor for 3–6 months** post-rollout to confirm sustained protection

4. **Document the procedure** carefully to ensure consistent implementation

5. **Consider a cost-benefit analysis**—if the new procedure requires more resources, weigh that against the value of preventing score decline

---

## **Analytical Method**

Difference-in-differences analysis with cluster-robust standard errors, controlling for staffing and visitor volume. This isolates the effect of the procedure change from other factors affecting all branches.
