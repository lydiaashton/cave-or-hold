# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure produced a modest but statistically significant improvement in service scores.** Contrary to our expectation of no change, switched branches gained approximately **2.3 points** (5% improvement) relative to non-switched branches. The effect is real and sustained, but whether it justifies system-wide rollout depends on implementation costs and operational considerations not captured in this analysis.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **150 branches**: 90 switched to new procedure, 60 kept the old
- **17 weeks of data**: Weeks 6–14 (before), Weeks 15–22 (after)
- Difference-in-differences analysis comparing changes between groups

### **Results**

| Group | Before | After | Change |
|-------|--------|-------|--------|
| Switched branches | 49.75 | 53.60 | +3.85 |
| Non-switched branches | 49.73 | 51.08 | +1.35 |
| **Net effect** | | | **+2.50** |

Regression estimate (controlling for staffing and visits): **+2.33 points**  
95% confidence interval: **1.0 to 3.7 points**  
p-value: **0.001**

### **Evidence Quality**

| Strength | Limitation |
|----------|------------|
| Statistically significant (p < 0.01) | Observational — branches not randomly assigned |
| Effect sustained across all 8 post-switch weeks | Only 9 pre-switch weeks for trend comparison |
| Parallel pre-trends confirmed | Unobserved factors could influence results |
| Robust to controls (staffing, visits) | Effect size is modest (0.23 standard deviations) |

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Assess implementation costs** — compare training, labor, and material costs of new vs. old procedure
2. **Consider a phased rollout** — expand to additional branches while continuing to monitor
3. **Gather qualitative feedback** — survey staff and patrons about perceived service changes
4. **Decide based on cost-benefit** — if costs are low, the 5% improvement likely justifies rollout; if costs are high, further evaluation may be warranted
