You're right to question this. Let me be more careful about the evidence.

**The bottom line is still supported, but with important nuance:**

### Why I'm Confident the Effect is Real:

1. **Consistent across specifications**: The effect is ~2.1 points in every model (basic DiD, with controls, with branch fixed effects, with week fixed effects)

2. **Highly statistically significant**: p < 0.001 across all specifications

3. **Plausible dynamics**: The effect was zero in weeks 1-2 after the switch, then grew steadily. This pattern is exactly what you'd expect from a real operational change that requires learning—not what you'd expect from a spurious correlation

4. **Pre-trends look reasonable**: In the two weeks immediately before the switch (weeks 13-14), switched and control branches showed no statistically significant divergence in the event study

### Important Caveats I Should Have Emphasized:

1. **Part of the effect comes from control group decline**: Non-switched branches' scores dropped by 0.62 points after week 15. The DiD attributes this to the treatment effect, but it could reflect external factors (seasonal changes, budget cuts, etc.) that happened to affect non-switched branches more

2. **Early pre-period differences**: In weeks 6-12, switched branches sometimes performed worse than control branches. However, these differences resolved before the switch occurred

3. **Only 8 weeks of post-data**: We don't know if the effect persists, grows, or fades over time

### Revised Bottom Line:

> **"The evidence indicates the new procedure improved service scores by approximately 2 points (4%). The effect is statistically robust and shows a plausible pattern of growing benefits over time. However, part of the estimated effect reflects a decline in non-switched branches' scores, which may involve external factors. We recommend proceeding with rollout while continuing to monitor outcomes."**

This is more measured but still supports moving forward with the rollout.
