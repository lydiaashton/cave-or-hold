# **Memorandum to the Board**

## **Title: Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure helped branches maintain service scores during a period of system-wide decline.** While we expected no difference, the data shows that switching branches experienced a smaller drop in service scores than non-switching branches after week 15. The estimated protective effect is **2 points** (statistically significant, p = 0.002). However, **neither group improved in absolute terms**—both declined, but treatment branches declined less. **We recommend proceeding with rollout, but with tempered expectations: the procedure appears to buffer against score declines rather than boost scores outright.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect
| Measure | Value |
|---------|-------|
| Estimated protective effect | +2.0 points (vs. control group decline) |
| 95% confidence interval | 0.8 to 3.3 points |
| Statistical significance | p = 0.002 |
| Absolute change (treatment group) | −0.5 points (not an improvement) |

### 2. What the Data Shows

| Group | Before Switch (Weeks 6-14) | After Switch (Weeks 15-22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------------|---------------------------|--------|
| **Control** (kept old procedure) | 51.6 | 49.1 | **−2.5** |
| **Treatment** (new procedure) | 53.1 | 52.6 | **−0.5** |

**Critical observation:** Service scores dropped system-wide after week 15. The new procedure appears to have ** cushioned** this decline, not reversed it.

### 3. Week-by-Week Pattern
The gap between treatment and control branches averaged ~1.5 points before week 15, then widened to ~3.5 points after week 15. This widening is the 2-point DiD effect.

### 4. Validity Checks
| Check | Result |
|-------|--------|
| Parallel trends (pre-switch) | ✓ Confirmed (p = 0.42) |
| Robustness to controls | ✓ Effect holds with staffing/visitor controls |
| Sample | 150 branches (90 switched, 60 did not) |

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Investigate the system-wide decline** — Why did scores drop for all branches after week 15? This could be seasonal, operational, or external. Understanding this is critical before rollout.

2. **Proceed cautiously with rollout** — The new procedure shows a protective effect, but it does not appear to improve scores in absolute terms. Set realistic expectations.

3. **Monitor closely during implementation** — Track whether the protective effect holds as more branches adopt the procedure.

4. **Consider cost-benefit** — If the procedure requires significant investment, weigh whether buffering against declines justifies the cost, versus other interventions that might actually improve scores.
