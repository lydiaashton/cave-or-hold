# MEMORANDUM

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## Bottom Line

**The evidence is inconclusive.** The new procedure is associated with a +2 point improvement in service scores, but this effect is **not statistically significant** at conventional levels (p = 0.10). The data cannot distinguish between no effect and a meaningful positive effect (up to +4 points). Before rolling out to all branches, we recommend **collecting additional data** to reduce uncertainty.

---

## Key Findings

### Study Overview
- **34 branches**: 20 adopted the new procedure, 14 maintained the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data**: Weeks 6–14 (before) and Weeks 15–22 (after)
- Analysis uses difference-in-differences to isolate the procedure's effect

### Results

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched Branches | 47.90 | 48.53 | +0.63 |
| Non-Switched Branches | 51.95 | 50.86 | −1.09 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+2.01** |

### Statistical Confidence

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect | +2.01 points |
| Standard error | 1.21 |
| p-value | 0.098 |
| 95% Confidence Interval | −0.37 to +4.40 |

**What this means:** There is approximately a 10% probability of observing this result (or larger) if the procedure truly has no effect. The confidence interval includes zero, so we **cannot reject the possibility that the procedure has no impact** on service scores.

### Important Context

1. **Baseline differences**: Switching branches started with lower scores (47.9 vs. 51.9). Our analysis controls for this.

2. **Pre-switch trends were similar**: Both groups showed comparable week-to-week patterns before the change, supporting the validity of the comparison.

3. **Limited statistical power**: With only 34 branches, detecting small effects is difficult. A larger sample or longer observation period would provide more definitive answers.

4. **The control group declined**: Non-switching branches saw scores drop after week 15. The net effect reflects both the treatment group's slight improvement AND this decline.

---

## Next Steps

### Recommended Actions

1. **Pause the full rollout pending more evidence**
   - The current data does not provide strong enough evidence to justify system-wide adoption
   - Continue the procedure at the 20 branches already using it

2. **Collect 8–12 additional weeks of data**
   - This would approximately double the post-switch observations
   - Expected to narrow the confidence interval by ~30%
   - Would provide clearer evidence of whether the effect is real

3. **Monitor implementation fidelity**
   - Ensure the 20 switched branches are executing the procedure consistently
   - Document any variations that could affect outcomes

4. **Track additional outcomes**
   - Service score is one metric; also monitor staff time, operational costs, and patron satisfaction
   - Even a small service score improvement may be worthwhile if costs are low

5. **Reconvene in Q1 2027**
   - Review updated analysis with extended data
   - Make final rollout decision based on stronger evidence

### Decision Framework

| If the true effect is... | Rollout recommendation |
|--------------------------|------------------------|
| Zero or negative | Do not roll out |
| +1 to +2 points | Consider rollout if costs are low |
| +3 or more points | Roll out recommended |

Current data cannot distinguish between these scenarios. Additional data will clarify.
