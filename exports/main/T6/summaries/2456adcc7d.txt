# **REVISED MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** Library Service Procedure Change – Impact Analysis and Recommendation  
**Date:** November 2026

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**We cannot conclusively attribute the score improvement to the new procedure.** While branches that switched show higher scores after week 15, those same branches were already outperforming the control group—and the gap was widening—before the switch occurred. This suggests **selection bias**: branches that chose (or were chosen) to switch may have been on a better trajectory regardless of the procedure change.

**Recommendation: Do not approve system-wide rollout yet.** Instead, run a randomized pilot or gather more evidence before committing.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **What the Data Shows**
- Switched branches have higher scores after week 15 (+1.9 points vs. control)
- **However**, switched branches were already pulling ahead before the switch
- The gap between groups grew from 1.3 to 2.5 points in the pre-switch period alone

### **Why This Matters**
The standard analysis method (difference-in-differences) requires that treatment and control groups follow parallel trends before the intervention. **This assumption is not met.** The observed "effect" could reflect:
1. A real procedure benefit (what we hoped)
2. Pre-existing differences between branches that switched vs. didn't
3. Unmeasured factors affecting the groups differently

### **What We Can Say**
✓ Branches using the new procedure have higher scores  
✗ We cannot prove the procedure *caused* the improvement  
✗ Rolling out system-wide risks investing in something that may not work
