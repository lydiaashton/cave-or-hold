## **Key Findings**

### **What the Data Shows**

|--------|-----------------|---------------|

### **Statistical Certainty**

| Statistic | Value | Interpretation |
|-----------|-------|----------------|
| Point estimate | +2.0 points | Positive, as expected |
| P-value | 0.096 | Not significant at 0.05 level |
| 95% Confidence Interval | -0.35 to +4.35 | Includes zero |

**What this means:** The most likely effect is positive (~2 points), but we cannot be 95% confident the effect is different from zero. The true effect could reasonably be:
- A modest improvement (up to +4 points)
- No effect (0 points)
- A very small decline (down to -0.35 points)

### **Why the Uncertainty?**

- **Limited sample:** 34 branches over 17 weeks provides moderate statistical power
- **Natural variation:** Service scores fluctuate week-to-week (standard deviation ~10 points)
- **Short post-period:** Only 8 weeks of data after the switch

### **Supporting Evidence**

Despite statistical uncertainty, several factors support proceeding:

1. **Directional consistency:** The point estimate is positive and matches expectations
2. **No detected harms:** No branch showed systematic decline attributable to the new procedure
3. **Staffing effect validated:** Higher staffing correlates with better scores (p < 0.01), confirming our measures capture real variation
4. **Operational rationale:** The procedure change was designed to improve efficiency; even a small service score gain may justify adoption
