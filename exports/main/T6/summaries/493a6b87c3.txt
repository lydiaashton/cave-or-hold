Looking at the evidence more critically:

1. **Statistical significance ≠ practical importance**: A 2.1-point increase on a ~50 point scale is about 4%. Is this meaningful for patrons, or just statistically detectable?

2. **Effect attenuation**: The effect was +3.0 points in weeks 15-18, but dropped to +1.3-1.8 points in weeks 19-22 (some not statistically significant). This could indicate the effect fades.

3. **Observational design**: Branches weren't randomly assigned to switch. There could be unobserved differences between branches that chose to switch early vs. those that didn't.

4. **Their expectation matters**: If domain expertise suggests no effect should exist, a surprising positive finding warrants extra scrutiny before acting on it.

## **Subject: Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

### **Bottom Line**

**The data show a statistically significant improvement, but we recommend cautious interpretation.** Branches using the new procedure had service scores **2.1 points higher** than expected (p=0.001). However, given that we anticipated no effect, and the observed effect weakened in later weeks, we recommend **additional validation before full rollout**.

### **Key Findings**

| Metric | Result |
|--------|--------|
| Estimated effect | +2.1 points (95% CI: 0.9 to 3.4) |
| Statistical significance | p = 0.001 |
| Effect size | ~4% of average score |
| Pattern over time | Stronger early (+3.0 pts weeks 15-18), weaker later (+1.5 pts weeks 19-22) |

---

### **Reasons for Caution**

1. **Unexpected direction**: We hypothesized no effect on service scores. Unexpected findings require extra validation.

2. **Attenuation**: The effect diminished after the first month. This could indicate:
   - Initial enthusiasm/training effect that faded
   - Normalization of the procedure
   - Random variation

3. **Non-random assignment**: Branches self-selected into early adoption. Unobserved factors (motivated staff, better management) could explain part of the effect.

4. **Magnitude**: A 2-point change may not translate to meaningful patron experience improvements.

---

### **Recommendation**

**Do not proceed with full rollout yet.** Instead:

1. **Extend the observation period** – Monitor current branches through week 30+ to see if effects stabilize or continue fading

2. **Consider a randomized rollout** – Randomly assign remaining branches to switch now vs. later to eliminate selection bias

3. **Gather qualitative data** – Interview staff and patrons at switched branches to understand what (if anything) changed in their experience

4. **Define success thresholds** – Before rollout, agree on what magnitude of improvement justifies the operational change

5. **Revisit in 8-12 weeks** – With more data, we can provide a more confident recommendation
