# **Memorandum to the Board**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors
**From:** Data Analysis Team
**Date:** November 15, 2026
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Recommendation: Do NOT proceed with system-wide rollout at this time.** While branches using the new procedure show higher service scores, our analysis indicates this improvement **cannot be confidently attributed to the procedure change itself**. The branches that adopted the new procedure were already improving faster than other branches before the switch occurred.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Observed Improvement Exists, But Attribution Is Uncertain
Branches using the new procedure show service scores approximately **1.8 points higher** after the switch. However, this difference **may not be caused by the new procedure**.

### 2. Critical Problem: Pre-Existing Trends
Treatment branches were already on a more positive trajectory **before** adopting the new procedure:
- They were improving **0.28 points per week faster** than control branches prior to the switch
- This pre-trend is statistically significant (p < 0.001)
- Over 8 weeks, this trend alone would predict a **2.3 point gap increase**—larger than the 1.8 points we actually observed

### 3. Placebo Test Fails
When we artificially pretend the switch happened earlier (week 11, using only pre-switch data), we still detect a significant "effect" of 1.4 points. This indicates the analysis method cannot distinguish the procedure change from pre-existing differences between branches.

### 4. No Clear Jump at Implementation
If the new procedure had an immediate impact, we would expect to see a discontinuity at week 15. We found **no evidence of such a jump** (p = 0.79).

### 5. What This Means
The data is consistent with two explanations:
- **Optimistic**: The new procedure provides modest benefits (but we cannot isolate them from pre-existing trends)
- **Concerning**: The observed improvement reflects nothing more than which branches were selected to switch (these branches were already improving)

---

## **Recommendation**

**Pause the rollout and gather more evidence:**

1. **Extend the observation period** – Continue monitoring both groups for 3-6 more months. If the new procedure has a real effect, the gap should continue widening beyond what pre-trends predict.

2. **Consider a randomized rollout** – For the remaining branches, randomly assign which adopt the procedure next. This would eliminate selection bias and allow for a cleaner causal estimate.

3. **Investigate branch selection** – Understand why certain branches were chosen for the initial rollout. Were they higher-performing? Better-staffed? More motivated? This information is critical for interpretation.

4. **Pilot with volunteer branches** – If randomization is not feasible, recruit a new set of volunteer branches and compare their before/after changes to a matched control group.

---

## **Bottom Line Restated**

The current evidence is **insufficient to justify system-wide investment**. The branches that switched may simply be branches that were already improving. We recommend additional data collection before making a rollout decision.

---

**Questions for the Board:**

1. What were the criteria for selecting which branches switched to the new procedure?
2. Is there budget available for a randomized controlled rollout?
3. What is the cost of delaying rollout versus the risk of implementing an ineffective procedure system-wide?
