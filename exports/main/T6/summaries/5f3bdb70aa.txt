## **BOTTOM LINE**

**Do NOT approve full rollout yet.** While switched branches show higher service scores after implementation, **we cannot confidently attribute this improvement to the new procedure.** The branches that switched were already on a more positive trajectory before the change, which undermines our ability to claim causation.

**Recommendation:** Conduct a more rigorous evaluation before system-wide implementation.

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### The Observed Pattern

| Measure | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Raw difference (switched vs. control)** | +1.94 points post-switch |
| **Statistical significance** | p < 0.001 |
| **BUT: Pre-existing trend difference** | 0.36 points/week (p < 0.001) |

### The Critical Problem: Non-Parallel Trends

The fundamental assumption for causal inference is that treatment and control groups would have followed similar trends absent the intervention. **This assumption is violated:**

| Period | Switched Branches | Control Branches | Gap |
|--------|------------------|------------------|-----|
| Week 6 (pre) | 53.26 | 54.38 | **-1.12** |
| Week 14 (pre) | 47.10 | 45.34 | **+1.75** |
| **Change in gap BEFORE switch** | | | **+2.88 points** |

**The gap between groups widened by 2.88 points during the pre-period alone**—before any branches switched procedures. This suggests switched branches differed systematically from control branches in ways that affected service scores.

### What This Means

If the pre-period trend differential (0.36 points/week) continued into the post-period, it would account for approximately **2.9 points** of the observed 1.9-point effect. This implies:

- The **true causal effect could be near zero**
- The observed improvement may reflect **selection bias**, not the procedure itself
- Branches that volunteered/qualified for the pilot may have been better positioned to improve regardless of the intervention

## **RECOMMENDATION**

### Do Not Proceed With Full Rollout Yet

| Issue | Concern |
|-------|---------|
| **Causal identification** | Compromised by non-parallel pre-trends |
| **Effect attribution** | Cannot separate procedure effect from selection bias |
| **Risk** | System-wide rollout may not deliver expected improvements |

### Proposed Next Steps

1. **Pause rollout decision** pending further analysis

2. **Conduct a randomized controlled trial:**
   - Randomly assign remaining branches to treatment/control
   - This eliminates selection bias and ensures valid causal inference

3. **Alternatively, use matching methods:**
   - Match switched branches to similar control branches on pre-period characteristics
   - Re-estimate the effect with better-comparable groups

4. **Gather qualitative data:**
   - Interview branch managers about why some branches switched and others didn't
   - Identify unobserved factors that may explain the differential trends

5. **Reconvene in 8-12 weeks** with stronger evidence before making rollout decision
