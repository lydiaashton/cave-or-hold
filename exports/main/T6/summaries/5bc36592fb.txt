### **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure produced a small but statistically reliable improvement in service scores.** Switched branches improved by **2.1 points** (approximately **4%**) relative to control branches. **What this means:**
- The improvement is **real**, not random noise
- The improvement is **small**—about 2 points on a ~50-point scale
- The result is **robust** to alternative specifications and controls
- There is **no evidence** the effect was present before the switch

### **Context**

The board's expectation that this change would have no measurable impact was reasonable—rotation and shelving procedures are operational details that often don't translate into patron-facing service improvements. The fact that we detected an effect suggests either:

1. Staff can execute the new procedure more efficiently, freeing time for patron service
2. The new arrangement makes materials easier to locate
3. There is a Hawthorne effect (branches performed better because they knew they were part of a change)

**Proceed with rollout, but:**

| Action | Rationale |
