# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

**Date:** November 2026

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

The new procedure **produced a statistically significant improvement** in service scores of approximately 2.1 points (about 4% relative to the baseline average of 50). This finding contradicts the expectation that the procedure would make no difference. However, the effect size is modest.

**Recommendation:** The new procedure improves service scores and can be recommended for rollout **provided implementation costs are reasonable**. The Board should weigh the modest but real service improvement against any associated costs before approving system-wide adoption.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### Study Overview
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **60 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Analysis uses difference-in-differences, the standard method for evaluating policy changes with staggered adoption

### Main Results

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated treatment effect | **+2.1 points** on service score |
| 95% Confidence interval | [0.8, 3.4] points |
| Statistical significance | p = 0.002 |
| Effect size (Cohen's d) | 0.22 (small-to-moderate) |
| Relative improvement | ~4% above baseline |

### What the Numbers Mean

Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores **increase by 1.4 points** after the switch, while control branch scores **declined by 0.7 points**. The net effect of **+2.1 points** is statistically distinguishable from zero with high confidence.

### Why We Trust These Results

1. **Parallel trends validated**: Pre-switch trends showed no significant differences between groups (all p > 0.24), confirming the control group provides a valid comparison.

2. **Consistent over time**: The positive effect appeared immediately and persisted across all 8 post-switch weeks with no signs of fading.

3. **Robust to controls**: Results hold when accounting for staffing levels, visitor volume, branch-specific factors, and week-to-week variation.

### Important Caveats

- **Modest magnitude**: A 4% improvement is meaningful but not transformative. Expectations should be calibrated accordingly.
- **Short-term evidence**: We observe only 8 weeks post-switch; long-term sustainability is unknown.
- **Costs not analyzed**: This evaluation measures service impact only. Implementation costs (training, labor, equipment) should be factored into the rollout decision.

## **NEXT STEPS**

1. **Cost-benefit assessment**: Quantify implementation costs per branch and compare against the value of a 4% service score improvement.

2. **If costs are reasonable, approve rollout** to remaining 60 branches with monitoring.

3. **Track implementation**: Monitor service scores during rollout to confirm effects persist and identify branches needing support.

4. **Document lessons learned**: Capture implementation experiences from the 90 switching branches to support training.

5. **Longer-term evaluation**: Reassess after 6–12 months to determine whether effects are sustained.
