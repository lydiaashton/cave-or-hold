# **REVISED MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

## **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

### **BOTTOM LINE**

**The evidence is suggestive but not conclusive.** Branches that adopted the new procedure showed stronger service score improvements than non-switching branches. However, **the switching branches were already outperforming non-switchers before the change**, which limits our ability to attribute the improvement solely to the new procedure. We recommend a **cautious, monitored expansion** rather than immediate full rollout.

---

### **KEY FINDINGS**

**What the Data Shows**

| Metric | Finding |
|--------|---------|
| **Observed Difference** | +1.94 points higher for switched branches |
| **Statistical Significance** | p < 0.001 |
| **Critical Limitation** | Parallel trends assumption **not satisfied** |

**The Problem: Pre-Existing Differences**

The branches that switched were already on a stronger trajectory **before** adopting the new procedure:

| Week | Treatment - Control Gap |
|------|------------------------|
| Week 6 | +1.07 points |
| Week 10 | +2.44 points |
| Week 14 (last pre-switch) | +3.74 points |

**The gap was widening by ~0.35 points/week even before the switch** (p < 0.001). This means we cannot confidently separate the procedure's effect from the pre-existing trend.

---

### **INTERPRETATION**

**Two Possible Explanations:**

1. **Optimistic:** The new procedure added ~1.9 points on top of an already-positive trajectory
2. **Conservative:** The observed improvement is largely continuation of pre-existing momentum; the procedure itself may have little or no effect

**We cannot distinguish between these with the current data.**

---

### **RECOMMENDATIONS**

| Priority | Action |
|----------|--------|
| **1** | **Pause full rollout** pending stronger evidence |
| **2** | **Expand pilot** to a randomized subset of remaining branches |
| **3** | **Collect 3–6 more months of data** to assess sustained effects |
| **4** | **Re-analyze** with randomized design for causal clarity |

---

### **CONCLUSION**

The new procedure is associated with improved service scores, but **selection bias** (branches that chose to switch may differ systematically from those that didn't) prevents a definitive causal conclusion. A randomized expansion would provide the evidence needed for a confident system-wide decision.

### **Note to Board**

This is a common challenge in program evaluation. When branches self-select into a new procedure, we cannot assume they were comparable to non-switchers. Random assignment in the next phase would resolve this uncertainty.
