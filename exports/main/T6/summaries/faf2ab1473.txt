## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure shows a **positive effect on service scores** (+2.2 points, approximately 4% improvement), consistent with expectations. However, the evidence is **not statistically conclusive** at conventional confidence levels. We recommend **continuing the pilot with expanded monitoring** before committing to a full system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure at week 15; **14 branches** maintained the old procedure as a comparison group
- Service scores average ~50 points; higher scores indicate better service

### Results Summary

| Finding | Result |
|---------|--------|
| **Estimated improvement** | **+2.18 points** |
| **95% Confidence Interval** | **−0.34 to +4.70 points** |
| **Statistical significance** | **p = 0.089** (not significant at 0.05 level) |
| **Probability effect is positive** | **95.5%** |
| **Branches that improved** | **70%** of switched vs. **50%** of control |

### What the Data Shows

1. **The point estimate is positive and meaningful.** Switched branches improved by 2.7 points on average versus 0.5 points for control branches—a difference of 2.2 points, or roughly 4%.

2. **Statistical uncertainty remains.** The confidence interval includes zero, meaning the data is also compatible with no effect (or a very small negative effect up to −0.3 points).

3. **Most switched branches improved.** 14 of 20 switched branches (70%) showed score gains after adoption, compared to 7 of 14 control branches (50%).

4. **No evidence of harm.** The lower bound of the confidence interval (−0.34) suggests any negative effect would be negligible.

### Why Results Are Not Statistically Conclusive

- **Only 8 weeks of post-switch data**—insufficient to overcome week-to-week variability
- **Service scores are noisy**—standard deviation of ~10 points
- **34 branches provides moderate power**, but not enough to detect a 4% effect with high confidence

## **Recommendation**

**Continue and expand the pilot; defer full rollout pending additional evidence.**

The data is encouraging and consistent with expectations, but does not yet provide the statistical certainty needed for a board-level commitment to system-wide implementation. We recommend:

1. **Maintain the current 20 branches** on the new procedure
2. **Optionally add 5–10 branches** to the pilot to increase statistical power
3. **Collect 8–12 more weeks of data** to improve precision
4. **Reconvene in Q2 2026** for a final rollout decision
