## **Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

---

### **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure helped branches maintain service quality during a period of system-wide decline.** While service scores fell across the cooperative after week 15, branches using the new procedure declined significantly less than those keeping the old procedure. The evidence supports rolling out the new procedure to all branches.

---

### **KEY FINDINGS**

**1. The new procedure had a statistically significant protective effect**
- Branches using the new procedure declined by only **0.6 points** after week 15
- Branches keeping the old procedure declined by **2.9 points**
- The net difference of **+2.3 points** is statistically significant (p < 0.001)

**2. Something caused system-wide scores to fall after week 15**
- Both groups declined, suggesting an external factor affected all branches (seasonal patterns, staffing changes, etc.)
- The new procedure appears to have buffered branches against this decline

**3. The analysis is methodologically sound**
- 150 branches studied (90 switched, 60 control)
- Pre-switch scores were nearly identical (~51.8–51.9), confirming comparable groups
- No differential changes in staffing or visits explain the results

**4. Practical significance**
- A 2.3-point relative advantage represents approximately **4–5% better service** compared to the old procedure
- This is meaningful for library patrons
