## **Revised Bottom Line**

**The data suggests the new procedure improved service scores by approximately 2.1 points (4%), and this effect is statistically significant.** However, there are important limitations to consider before approving a full rollout:

- **Selection concern:** Branches that switched were already outperforming control branches by 3.2 points (6.7%) before the change. While our analysis controls for this baseline gap, it raises the possibility that better-performing branches were selected to pilot the new procedure.
- **Modest effect size:** A 2.1-point gain is meaningful but not transformative.
- **Limited post-switch data:** Only 8 weeks of data after implementation.
- **Unknown costs:** We have not analyzed the implementation costs or operational burden of the new procedure.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with **cautious expansion** rather than immediate full rollout. Consider a second-phase pilot with random assignment to strengthen the evidence base before committing all 60 remaining branches.

### **Important Caveats**

| Concern | Evidence | Implication |
|---------|----------|-------------|
| **Baseline differences** | Switched branches scored 3.2 points higher before the switch | Possible selection bias; better branches may have been chosen to pilot |
| **Parallel trends** | Pre-switch gap was stable (no significant trend, p=0.115) | Supports validity of comparison, but doesn't eliminate selection concerns |
| **Post-switch duration** | Only 8 weeks of data after implementation | Effect may not persist; longer-term monitoring needed |
| **Cost-benefit unknown** | No cost data in this analysis | Board should weigh 4% gain against implementation costs |

## **Revised Next Steps**

1. **Do not approve immediate full rollout** — evidence is promising but not definitive

2. **Run a second-phase pilot** with 20–30 additional branches, ideally with **random assignment** to strengthen causal inference

3. **Collect cost data** on implementation time, training requirements, and ongoing operational burden

4. **Extend monitoring period** to 16–24 weeks post-switch to assess whether effects persist

5. **Return to the board** in one quarter with:
   - Results from the expanded pilot
   - Full cost-benefit analysis
   - Recommendation on full rollout
