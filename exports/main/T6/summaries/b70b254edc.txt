# MEMORANDUM

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## Bottom Line

The new procedure shows a **positive trend** in service scores, but the evidence is **not statistically conclusive**. We observed a +2.3 point improvement, but the data are also consistent with **no real effect**. The 95% confidence interval ranges from **-0.5 to +5.1 points**, meaning we cannot rule out that the procedure has zero or even slightly negative impact.

**Recommendation:** Do not proceed with full rollout yet. **Extend the pilot** to gather more data before making a system-wide decision.

---

## Key Findings

### 1. What the Data Show

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Observed improvement (point estimate) | +2.3 points |
| 95% Confidence Interval | **-0.5 to +5.1 points** |
| P-value | **0.12** (not significant at 0.05 level) |
| Conclusion | **Cannot confirm** the procedure causes improvement |

### 2. Why We Can't Be Confident

- The confidence interval **includes zero**, meaning no effect is a plausible explanation
- With only 34 branches (20 switched, 14 control), the study has **limited statistical power**
- To achieve statistical significance with this sample size, we would have needed an effect of approximately **+2.8 points or higher**; we observed +2.3 points

### 3. What We Can Say

- The effect is in the **expected direction** (positive)
- There is **no evidence of harm** (lower bound is only -0.5 points)
- If there is a real effect, it is likely **modest** (probably under 5 points)

### 4. Branch Variation

Switched branches showed considerable variation in outcomes:
- Range: **-2.5 to +8.9 points** change
- This suggests **local factors** (staffing, training, implementation quality) may matter more than the procedure itself

---

## Recommendations

### 1. Extend the Pilot (Primary Recommendation)

| Action | Rationale |
|--------|-----------|
| Continue current pilot for 8–12 more weeks | More data will narrow the confidence interval |
| Add 5–10 more branches to the switched group | Increased sample size improves statistical power |
| Re-analyze after extension | Make rollout decision with greater confidence |

### 2. If Proceeding Despite Uncertainty

If operational considerations favor rollout despite inconclusive evidence:

- Implement **gradually** (not all branches at once)
- Establish **clear success metrics** and stopping rules
- Monitor for branches that **underperform** after adoption
- Be prepared to **revert** branches that show declining scores

### 3. Investigate Variation

- Identify characteristics of **high-performing** switched branches
- Determine whether **training, staffing, or branch size** explains the variation
- Use findings to improve implementation protocol

---

## Decision Framework

| Scenario | Recommended Action |
|----------|-------------------|
| Effect confirmed (p < 0.05) in extended pilot | Full rollout |
| Effect remains inconclusive but positive | Phased rollout with monitoring |
| Effect reverses or shows harm | Halt and investigate |

- **Analysis method:** Difference-in-differences with cluster-robust standard errors
- **Observations:** 578 weekly records from 34 branches

- **Limitation:** Sample size provides approximately 50–60% power to detect a 2-point effect

**Bottom line for the board:** The data are **encouraging but not definitive**. We recommend gathering more evidence before committing to a system-wide rollout.
