# **MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2025  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The data show a **positive effect consistent with expectations**, but the evidence is **not statistically definitive** at conventional standards. The new procedure is associated with a **+2.2 point improvement** in service scores, but we cannot rule out the possibility that the true effect is zero or very small. We recommend **expanding the pilot** rather than immediate full rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### What the Data Shows

| Metric | Result | Interpretation |
|--------|--------|----------------|
| **Point estimate** | +2.18 points | Direction matches expectations |
| **95% confidence interval** | −0.34 to +4.70 | Includes zero |
| **90% confidence interval** | +0.07 to +4.30 | Excludes zero |
| **p-value** | 0.089 | Marginally significant |
| **Probability effect is positive** | 95.5% | One-sided test |

### What This Means

- **The effect is in the expected direction.** Switched branches improved more than control branches.
- **The evidence is suggestive, not conclusive.** At the standard 95% confidence level, we cannot rule out no effect.
- **At 90% confidence, the effect is positive.** If the board is comfortable with this lower threshold, the data support a positive conclusion.
- **The practical impact is modest.** A 4.4% improvement is meaningful but not transformative.

### Why the Uncertainty?

1. **Limited post-switch data:** Only 8 weeks of observations after the procedure change.
2. **Natural variability:** Service scores fluctuate week-to-week (standard deviation ~10 points).
3. **Sample size:** 20 treatment branches and 14 control branches provide moderate statistical power.

---

## **Recommendation**

**Expand the pilot program while continuing to monitor.**

Given the positive direction of the effect and the operational expectations, we recommend:

1. **Roll out to 5–10 additional branches** in the next quarter to increase sample size.
2. **Continue monitoring all switched branches** for at least 12 more weeks.
3. **Re-evaluate in Q2 2026** with the expanded dataset. If the effect remains positive and precision improves, proceed with full rollout.

This approach balances the promising early results with appropriate statistical caution.

---

## **Decision Framework**

| If the board prioritizes... | Recommended action |
|----------------------------|-------------------|
| **Statistical certainty** | Wait for more data before expanding |
| **Operational momentum** | Proceed with phased expansion now |
| **Risk mitigation** | Expand to 5–10 branches, then reassess |

---

## **Monitoring Plan**

Track the following metrics monthly:

- Average service scores (treatment vs. control)
- Branch-level variation (identify underperformers)
- Staffing and visit patterns (assess interaction effects)

---

## **Conclusion**

The new procedure shows promise and aligns with operational expectations. However, the statistical evidence does not yet meet the conventional threshold for definitive conclusions. A phased expansion with continued monitoring offers the best balance between acting on promising results and maintaining analytical rigor.
