## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure **likely improves service scores on average** (94% probability of positive effect, estimated +2.2 points), but **benefits are highly uneven across branches**. We recommend a **targeted rollout**: expand to branches with characteristics similar to successful adopters, while offering branches that struggled an option to revert or receive implementation support.

## **Key Findings**

### **1. The Average Effect Is Positive**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated improvement | **+2.2 points** |
| Probability effect is positive | **94.6%** |
| Probability effect exceeds 1 point | **80.7%** |
| Effect size | **4.2% of average score** |

The data supports your expectation that the procedure would improve scores—but the improvement is **modest, not transformative**.

### **2. But Branch-Level Results Vary Widely**

Among the 20 branches that switched:
- **11 branches improved** (average gain: +4.8 points)
- **9 branches declined** (average loss: -3.4 points)

| Top Gainers | Change | Biggest Declines | Change |
|-------------|--------|------------------|--------|
| Branch 27 | +8.1 | Branch 32 | -5.1 |

| Branch 20 | +7.1 | Branch 18 | -3.5 |
| Branch 12 | +6.3 | Branch 10 | -3.3 |

**The median switched branch saw only +0.2 points improvement.** The average is pulled up by a few big winners.

### **3. What This Means**

The procedure is **not a universal improvement**. It appears to work well in some contexts and poorly in others. Rolling it out everywhere would:
- **Help** roughly half the branches (meaningful gains)
- **Hurt** roughly half the branches (modest declines)
- **Net positive** overall, but with losers

---

## **Recommendations**

### **1. Do Not Do Uniform System-Wide Rollout**

Forcing all branches to adopt the same procedure ignores the clear variation in outcomes.

### **2. Instead, Pursue Targeted Expansion**

| Action | Rationale |
|--------|-----------|
| **Identify success factors** — Analyze what distinguishes branches that improved (e.g., size, layout, staffing patterns, visitor volume) from those that declined | Enables prediction of which branches will benefit |
| **Prioritize rollout to likely beneficiaries** — Expand first to branches matching the profile of successful adopters | Maximizes gains, minimizes disruption |
| **Offer flexibility to current adopters** — Allow the 9 branches that declined to revert or receive implementation coaching | Respects branch-level evidence, maintains trust |
| **Continue monitoring all branches** — Track whether effects persist, grow, or diminish over time | Ensures decisions remain evidence-based |

### **3. Set a Review Date**

Reconvene in **12 weeks** with:
- Additional data (weeks 23-34)
- Findings from branch interviews
- Recommendations for which branches should adopt, adapt, or avoid the new procedure
