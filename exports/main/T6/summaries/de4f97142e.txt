You're right to question this. Let me be more precise about what the evidence actually tells us.

**The statistics are solid:**
- Effect estimate: **+1.88 points** (p = 0.002)
- 95% confidence interval: **0.69 to 3.06 points**
- This is statistically significant even at the 1% level

**However, there are important caveats:**

1. **The effect is modest** — even the lower bound of the confidence interval is only a 1.5% improvement on the baseline score of ~47

2. **Meaningful heterogeneity** — 21% of treatment branches saw no improvement or decline, suggesting the procedure doesn't work equally well everywhere

3. **Limited follow-up** — We only have 8 weeks of post-switch data; we don't know if effects persist, grow, or fade

4. **No cost data** — We can't assess whether the improvement justifies implementation costs

---

## **Revised Bottom Line**

**The new procedure produced a statistically significant improvement in service scores (+1.9 points, p=0.002), but the effect is modest and not universal.** 

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rollout, but with conditions:
- Implement with careful documentation and training to minimize variation
- Monitor outcomes closely, especially for branches that don't show improvement
- Conduct a cost-benefit analysis before full commitment

---

The evidence supports adoption, but this is a **cautious yes** rather than an enthusiastic one. The board should weigh the ~4% average improvement against implementation costs and the fact that roughly 1 in 5 branches may not benefit.
