# **BOARD MEMORANDUM**

---

**TO:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors

**RE:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure  
**DATE:** January 2026

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**We cannot conclude the new procedure improved service scores.** While branches that switched showed higher scores after the change, they were **already improving faster than other branches before the switch occurred**. After accounting for this pre-existing trend, there is **no statistical evidence** that the new procedure itself caused any improvement—and the effect could even be slightly negative.

**Recommendation: Do not proceed with system-wide rollout until further evidence is gathered.**

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### The Critical Issue: Pre-Existing Trends

| Week | Treatment vs. Control Gap |
|------|--------------------------|
| Week 6 | -0.63 (treatment behind) |
| Week 10 | +0.25 |
| Week 14 | +2.51 (treatment ahead) |

**The treatment group was already catching up and surpassing the control group BEFORE the switch.** This trend was statistically significant (p < 0.001), gaining approximately **0.32 points per week** on the control group.

### Why the Simple Comparison is Misleading

| | |
|---|---|
| **Simple DiD estimate** | +1.86 points (appears positive) |
| **Expected from pre-trend alone** | +2.56 points over 8 weeks |
| **Adjusted effect** | **-0.70 points** (no benefit detected) |

The apparent improvement is fully explained by the momentum these branches already had.

### Discontinuity at Switch Point

At the exact moment of switch (week 14 → 15):
- Control group jumped: **+3.31 points**
- Treatment group jumped: **+2.62 points**
- **Treatment actually jumped 0.69 points LESS than control**

This provides no evidence of an immediate treatment benefit.

---

## **WHAT THIS MEANS**

1. **Branches selected for the new procedure were already on an upward trajectory** — possibly due to better management, more motivated staff, or other unmeasured factors.

2. **The new procedure did not accelerate improvement** — if anything, it may have slowed the momentum these branches had built.

3. **The +1.86 point "effect" is confounded** — it reflects pre-existing differences, not causal impact.

---

## **RECOMMENDED NEXT STEPS**

| Priority | Action | Timeline |
|----------|--------|----------|
| **1** | **Pause system-wide rollout** | Immediate |
| **2** | Investigate why some branches were selected early — what characteristics distinguish them? | 2–4 weeks |
| **3** | Consider a randomized pilot: randomly assign remaining branches to new vs. old procedure | Next quarter |
| **4** | If proceeding, implement rigorous A/B testing with random assignment | Ongoing |
| **5** | Report back with cleaner causal evidence | Quarter 3 |

---

## **LESSONS FOR FUTURE EVALUATIONS**

1. **Random assignment** would have prevented this ambiguity
2. **Pre-trend analysis** should always be conducted before interpreting DiD results
3. **Selection bias** is a real risk when branches self-select or are selected for new procedures

---

## **CONCLUSION**

We entered this analysis expecting to confirm the new procedure's benefits. The data does not support that conclusion. **Branches using the new procedure perform well, but they were already improving before adoption.** Rolling out system-wide based on this evidence would be premature and may not deliver the expected returns.

We recommend gathering cleaner causal evidence before making a cooperative-wide investment.
