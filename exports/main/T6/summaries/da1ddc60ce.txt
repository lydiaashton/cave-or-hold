# **Library Cooperative Procedure Change: Impact Analysis**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure shows a statistically significant positive effect, but the practical impact is modest.** The analysis finds a **2.2-point improvement** in service scores for switched branches relative to control branches (p < 0.001). However, switched branches improved by only **0.79 points in absolute terms**, and just **51% of individual branches** saw any improvement at all.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with caution. The evidence supports rollout, but expectations should be tempered. Consider a phased rollout with close monitoring rather than an immediate system-wide mandate.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Statistically Significant, But Modest in Practice

| Measure | Value | Interpretation |
|---------|-------|----------------|
| DiD treatment effect | +2.21 points | Statistically significant (p = 0.0005) |
| 95% Confidence Interval | [0.99, 3.43] | Does not include zero |
| Absolute change (switched branches) | +0.79 points | Modest improvement |
| Absolute change (control branches) | -1.33 points | Decline occurred |
| Effect size | 0.23 SD | Small-to-moderate |

**Important context:** The board expected no difference. The positive finding is genuine, but the magnitude is smaller than a "clear win." About half of the DiD effect reflects control branches declining, not just treatment branches improving.

### 2. Only Half of Branches Benefited

| Group | % with Score Improvement |
|-------|--------------------------|
| Switched branches | 51% |
| Control branches | 28% |

Switched branches were more likely to improve, but **49% saw no benefit or declined**. This suggests the procedure's impact varies by branch context (staffing, layout, volume, etc.).

### 3. Benefits Took Time to Materialize

| Period | Effect vs. Control | Significance |
|--------|-------------------|--------------|
| Weeks 15-18 | -0.54 points | Not significant |
| Weeks 19-22 | +1.54 points | p = 0.03 |

Branches needed **4+ weeks** to realize gains. This is consistent with a learning curve but means short-term evaluations would miss the effect.

### 4. Research Design Is Sound

- **Parallel pre-trends confirmed** (p = 0.36 for differential trends before switch)
- **150 branches, 2,550 observations** provides good statistical power
- **Clustered standard errors** account for within-branch correlation
- **Controls for staffing and visits** don't change the conclusion

## **Next Steps**

1. **Phased rollout** rather than immediate system-wide mandate:
   - Start with 15-20 volunteer branches
   - Monitor for 8 weeks before expanding
   - Allow branches to opt out if no benefit emerges by week 6

2. **Set realistic expectations:**
   - Communicate that ~50% of branches may see little to no improvement
   - Emphasize the 4-week adjustment period
   - Frame this as a low-cost experiment, not a guaranteed win

3. **Investigate heterogeneity:**
   - Which branch characteristics predict benefit? (size, staffing, urban/rural, etc.)
   - Target rollout to branches most likely to benefit first

4. **Monitor for 6 months post-rollout** to confirm effects persist and identify any unintended consequences.

5. **Consider qualitative follow-up** to understand *why* the effect exists (unexpected given prior expectations) and why it varies across branches.
