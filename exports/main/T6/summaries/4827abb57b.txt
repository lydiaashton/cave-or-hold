# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The data shows a statistically detectable difference, but the practical impact is modest.** Branches using the new procedure maintained service scores better than those using the old procedure during a period when scores declined system-wide. The estimated effect is **+2.3 points** (4% of baseline), but the true effect could reasonably be as small as **+0.7 points** or as large as **+3.8 points**. 

Given the board's expectation that this change would not affect service scores, this finding warrants consideration—but the decision to roll out should weigh this modest benefit against implementation costs.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. What Changed
| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|---------------|---------------|--------|
| Switched branches (n=90) | 51.82 | 51.25 | -0.57 |
| Non-switched branches (n=60) | 51.93 | 49.08 | -2.85 |
| **Difference** | | | **+2.28** |

**Interpretation**: All branches saw scores decline after week 15. Switched branches declined *less*—by about 2.3 points less than non-switched branches.

### 2. Uncertainty Range
| Confidence Level | Effect Estimate |
|-----------------|-----------------|
| 95% confidence interval | +0.72 to +3.81 points |
| Point estimate | +2.26 points |
| Statistical significance | p = 0.004 |

**Interpretation**: We are 95% confident the true effect lies between 0.7 and 3.8 points. There is a small chance the true effect is outside this range.

### 3. Effect Size Context
- **4.4%** improvement relative to baseline (~52 points)
- **0.23 standard deviations** (small-to-medium in social science terms)
- Service scores naturally vary by about **10 points** week-to-week across branches

### 4. Study Strengths & Limitations
**Strengths:**
- Large sample (150 branches, 2,550 observations)
- Pre-switch trends were parallel between groups
- Effect persists after controlling for staffing and visitor volume

**Limitations:**
- Only 8 weeks of post-switch data
- Observational (not randomized) design
- Cannot rule out unmeasured factors that differed between groups
- Effect may not persist long-term

---

## **Recommendation**

**Consider a phased rollout with continued monitoring**, rather than immediate system-wide implementation.

**Rationale**: The evidence suggests a real but modest benefit. Before committing all 60 remaining branches:
- The effect size may not justify significant implementation costs
- More data would reduce uncertainty about the true effect
- A phased approach allows for course correction if benefits don't materialize

---

## **Next Steps**

| Action | Purpose |
|--------|---------|

| Pilot in 10–15 additional branches | Gather more data before full rollout |
| Monitor for 12+ weeks post-implementation | Assess whether effect persists |

---

## **Bottom Line (Restated)**

The new procedure **did make a difference**—just not necessarily the kind of difference that automatically justifies system-wide rollout. The board should decide whether a **1–4 point service score benefit** is worth the cost of changing procedures at 60 branches.
