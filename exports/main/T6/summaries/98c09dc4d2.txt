# **Library Cooperative Shelving Procedure Evaluation**
## Analysis of New Branch Rotation and Stocking Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

The evidence for the new procedure is **inconclusive**. While branches using the new procedure showed a 2.5-point improvement relative to control branches, two important limitations prevent a confident conclusion:

1. **Selection bias concern**: Treatment branches had significantly lower baseline scores (46.0 vs. 51.4), with 13 of 20 below the control average. Some observed improvement may reflect natural regression to the mean rather than the procedure itself.

2. **Limited statistical power**: The study design could only reliably detect effects of 4.7 points or larger. The observed 2.5-point effect, while promising, falls below this threshold.

**Recommendation:** Do **not** proceed with system-wide rollout at this time. Instead, treat this as a **pilot study** and gather more evidence through either (a) extending monitoring of current branches, or (b) a more rigorous randomized trial with additional branches.

## **Interpretation**

**Why we can't conclude the procedure works:**
- The baseline imbalance creates ambiguity about whether improvement reflects the procedure or natural regression toward average performance
- The confidence interval includes zero, meaning no effect is compatible with the data
- The study lacked power to confirm an effect of this magnitude

**Why we can't conclude the procedure doesn't work:**
- The point estimate is positive and meaningful (2.5 points ≈ 5% improvement)
- The one-sided p-value (0.052) is borderline, suggesting the effect may be real
- Post-switch trends show treatment branches improving while control branches declined

**Bottom line:** The data is **suggestive but not sufficient** for a system-wide decision.

## **Next Steps**

### Option 1: Extend Current Monitoring (Lower Cost)
- Continue tracking all 34 branches for an additional 8–12 weeks
- This would increase statistical power and clarify whether effects persist
- Decision point: Re-evaluate in Q2 with expanded dataset

### Option 2: Randomized Expansion (More Rigorous)
- Randomly assign 10–15 additional branches to treatment or control
- Randomization eliminates selection bias concerns
- Provides stronger evidence for eventual system-wide decision

### Option 3: Targeted Implementation (Pragmatic)
- Offer the new procedure to low-performing branches only
- Acknowledge this is a remedial intervention, not a universal improvement
- Monitor whether effects are larger for this subgroup

### Recommended Decision Framework
| If follow-up shows... | Then... |
|----------------------|---------|
| Effect grows to 4+ points with p < 0.05 | Proceed with system-wide rollout |
| Effect remains 2–3 points with p < 0.10 | Consider targeted rollout to struggling branches |
| Effect shrinks or becomes negative | Discontinue new procedure |

## **Conclusion**

The pilot provides **intriguing but inconclusive** evidence. The observed improvement could reflect a genuine benefit from the new procedure, or it could reflect natural recovery of underperforming branches. The board should **pause system-wide rollout** and invest in additional data collection before making a final decision.
