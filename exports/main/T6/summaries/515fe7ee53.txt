# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team

**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure  

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence for a positive effect is weaker than it initially appears.** While branches that adopted the new procedure showed higher service scores after the switch, further analysis reveals these branches were already improving relative to other branches *before* the procedure changed. After accounting for this pre-existing trend, the data does not clearly demonstrate that the new procedure caused the improvement. **We recommend additional data collection before making a system-wide rollout decision.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **What We Observed (Surface Level)**
- Branches using the new procedure had service scores **2.0 points higher** after the switch compared to branches using the old procedure
- This appeared statistically significant in initial analysis

### **What Deeper Analysis Revealed**

**The treated branches were already on an upward trajectory before the switch:**

| Period | Treated-Control Gap | Trend |
|--------|---------------------|-------|
| Week 6 (start) | -1.33 points | Treated were *worse* than control |
| Week 14 (before switch) | +1.46 points | Treated had *caught up and surpassed* control |
| Week 22 (end) | +3.29 points | Gap continued widening |

**Critical finding:** The gap between treated and control branches was **already growing by 0.30 points per week** before the procedure change (p=0.002). This pre-existing trend explains most—or possibly all—of the observed "effect."

### **What This Means**

When we extrapolate the pre-switch trend forward:
- **Predicted gap at Week 22** (if trend continued without treatment): 3.89 points
- **Actual gap at Week 22**: 3.29 points
- **Attributable to treatment**: -0.60 points (possibly negative)

The new procedure may have had **no effect** or even a **slight negative effect** relative to what would have happened anyway.

### **Alternative Interpretation**

The event study analysis shows small positive effects emerging only in the final 2 weeks of observation (Weeks 21-22). This could indicate:
1. A **delayed benefit** that needs more time to materialize, OR
2. **Random variation** in a noisy measure (service scores fluctuate ~10 points week-to-week)

We cannot distinguish between these with only 8 weeks of post-switch data.

---

## **Recommendations**

1. **Do NOT proceed with system-wide rollout at this time.** The evidence does not support a clear causal benefit.

2. **Continue monitoring** the 425 branches already using the new procedure for at least 8-12 more weeks to determine if benefits emerge with more time.

3. **Consider a randomized rollout** for remaining branches if you wish to obtain clearer causal evidence. Randomly assign half to adopt the procedure now and half to wait, then compare.

4. **Investigate why treated branches were improving before the switch.** Were they systematically different (e.g., more motivated staff, better management)? Understanding this selection effect is important.

5. **Review the service score metric** to ensure it captures what matters most to patrons and is not overly sensitive to weekly fluctuations.
