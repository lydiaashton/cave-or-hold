# **Board Memorandum (REVISED)**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**We cannot conclude the new procedure caused the improvement.** While branches using the new procedure did achieve higher service scores, our analysis reveals that these branches were **already improving faster before they switched**. This suggests the observed gains reflect pre-existing differences between branches—not the procedure itself.

**Recommendation: Do not proceed with system-wide rollout based on current evidence.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **What the Data Shows**

| Measure | Result |
|---------|--------|
| Raw difference (switched vs. control) | +1.9 points |
| **After accounting for pre-trends** | **Not statistically significant** |
| Pre-existing trend advantage | +0.27 points/week |

### **The Critical Issue: Selection Bias**

Branches that chose to adopt the new procedure were **already on an upward trajectory** before the switch:

| Week | Difference (Switched − Control) |
|------|---------------------------------|
| 6 | −0.88 |
| 8 | −0.04 |
| 10 | −0.25 |
| 12 | +0.80 |
| 14 | **+1.50** (before switch) |
| 15 | +1.35 (after switch) |
| 22 | +3.22 |

The gap was **already widening before week 15**. When we statistically account for this pre-existing trend, the estimated effect of the new procedure becomes:
- **−0.26 points** (not +1.91)
- **P-value: 0.56** (not statistically significant)
- **95% CI: [−1.14, +0.61]** (includes zero)

### **What This Means**

The branches that volunteered to switch were likely:
- Better managed
- More motivated to improve
- Located in improving neighborhoods
- Already implementing other positive changes

The new procedure may still be beneficial, but **this analysis cannot isolate its effect** from these pre-existing advantages.

---

## **Recommendation**

**Do not approve system-wide rollout based on this evidence alone.**

The apparent 4% improvement is likely attributable to selection bias, not the procedure itself. Rolling out system-wide would be premature without stronger causal evidence.

---

## **Next Steps**

| Action | Rationale |
|--------|-----------|
| **Pause rollout decision** | Current evidence does not support causal claim |
| **Consider randomized trial** | Randomly assign branches to new procedure to eliminate selection bias |
| **Gather qualitative feedback** | Interview switched branches about their experience |
| **Monitor switched branches** | Continue tracking to see if gains persist or fade |
| **Review implementation costs** | If procedure is low-cost, limited rollout may still be worthwhile |

---

## **Lessons Learned**

This analysis demonstrates why **randomized assignment** is the gold standard for program evaluation. When branches self-select into a new procedure, we cannot distinguish the procedure's effect from the characteristics of branches that chose it.

---

*I apologize for the initial conclusion. The pre-trend analysis reveals the evidence is not strong enough to support a system-wide rollout recommendation.*
