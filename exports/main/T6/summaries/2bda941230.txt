# **Board Summary: New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure Evaluation**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**We cannot confidently conclude the new procedure caused the observed improvement.** While branches that switched show higher service scores after the change, our analysis reveals the treatment and control groups were already on different trajectories *before* the switch. This violates a key assumption needed to attribute the difference to the procedure itself.

**Recommendation: Do not proceed with system-wide rollout based on this evidence alone.** Additional evaluation with stronger methodology is needed.

---

## **What We Found**

| | Simple Comparison | Causal Estimate* |
|---|---|---|
| **Apparent Effect** | +1.96 points | **Unreliable** |
| **Statistical Significance** | p < 0.001 | **Not trustworthy** |
| **Confidence** | High | **Low** |

*The causal estimate is compromised by methodological issues described below.

---

## **Critical Concerns**

### 1. **Pre-Existing Differences Between Groups**

The event study shows **5 of 8 pre-switch weeks** had statistically significant differences between treatment and control branches:

| Week (relative to switch) | Coefficient | Significant? |
|---|---|---|
| -9 | -0.31 | No |
| -8 | **+5.05** | **Yes** |
| -7 | +0.60 | No |
| -6 | **+1.91** | **Yes** |
| -5 | **-2.20** | **Yes** |
| -4 | +0.07 | No |
| -3 | **+1.82** | **Yes** |
| -2 | **+1.27** | **Yes** |

**Implication:** Branches that chose to switch were already systematically different from those that didn't. The observed "effect" may reflect these pre-existing differences rather than the procedure itself.

### 2. **Inconsistent Post-Switch Pattern**

After the switch, effects were volatile:
- Week 0: **+6.99** (large positive)
- Week 1: **+1.79** (moderate positive)
- Week 2: **-5.85** (large **negative**!)
- Weeks 3-7: +1.63 to +5.35 (positive)

**Implication:** This volatility is unusual and suggests other factors (seasonal, operational, or measurement issues) may be driving the results.

---

## **Why This Matters**

The analysis used a "difference-in-differences" approach, which requires that treatment and control groups would have followed **parallel trends** in the absence of the intervention. Our event study shows this assumption is **violated**.

**Likely explanation:** Branches that volunteered (or were selected) to switch may differ in unobserved ways—better management, more resources, more motivated staff—that also affect service scores.

---

## **Recommendations**

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Pause system-wide rollout** | Current evidence does not support causal claim |
| **2** | **Continue monitoring** existing switched branches | Gather more data over time |
| **3** | **Design stronger evaluation** for future changes | Use random assignment or careful matching |
| **4** | **Qualitative investigation** | Interview branch managers to understand implementation differences |

---

## **Path Forward**

To properly evaluate the new procedure, we recommend:

1. **Randomized rollout** – Assign remaining branches to switch (or not) randomly
2. **Propensity score matching** – Match switched branches to similar non-switched branches on observable characteristics
3. **Longer follow-up period** – Track scores for 6+ months to assess sustainability
4. **Implementation fidelity check** – Verify the procedure was actually implemented as intended
