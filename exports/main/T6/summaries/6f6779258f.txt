## **BOTTOM LINE**

**Do not approve system-wide rollout at this time.** While the new procedure shows promise, the evidence is not strong enough to justify expanding to all 284 remaining branches. The analysis reveals significant methodological concerns that undermine confidence in the estimated effect. **Recommend extended monitoring and a more rigorous evaluation before proceeding.**

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### What the Data Shows

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Simple DiD Estimate | +2.09 points |
| Statistical Significance | p < 0.001 |
| Weeks with Clear Positive Effect | 1 of 8 post-switch weeks |
| Weeks with Negative/No Effect | 7 of 8 post-switch weeks |

### Critical Concern: Parallel Trends Violation

The difference-in-differences method requires that treatment and control branches were on similar trajectories before the switch. **This assumption is violated:**

| Issue | Evidence |
|-------|----------|
| Pre-trend test | Significant (p < 0.001) |
| Pre-switch pattern | Treatment branches systematically underperforming control |
| Event study (pre-period) | All 8 weeks show negative coefficients (-3.18 to -8.71) |

### Event Study Results (Relative to Week 14)

| Period | Weeks | Pattern |
|--------|-------|---------|
| **Pre-Switch** | 6-13 | All negative, all significant (treatment underperforming) |
| **Post-Switch** | 15-21 | Mostly negative or no effect |
| **Post-Switch** | 22 | First clear positive effect (+3.76) |

**Interpretation:** The "treatment effect" appears to reflect treatment branches *catching up* from a pre-existing performance gap, not the procedure *causing* improvement. Only week 22 shows unambiguous positive impact.

---

## **ALTERNATIVE EXPLANATIONS**

The observed pattern could reflect:

1. **Regression to the mean** – Treatment branches were in a temporary dip before the switch and naturally recovered
2. **Selection bias** – Branches that switched may have been those struggling most, creating artificial "improvement"
3. **Seasonal factors** – Week 22 (year-end?) may have unique characteristics unrelated to the procedure
4. **Learning curve** – Benefits may take longer to materialize; 8 weeks may be insufficient

---

## **RECOMMENDATION**

| Action | Rationale |
|--------|-----------|
| **Pause system-wide rollout** | Evidence too weak to justify expanding to 284 branches |
| **Extend monitoring period** | Collect 8-12 additional weeks of data to see if week 22 pattern continues |
| **Investigate pre-switch underperformance** | Understand why treatment branches were struggling before the switch |
| **Consider randomized rollout** | Randomly assign remaining branches to treatment/control for cleaner causal evidence |
| **Review week 22 specifically** | Determine if there were special circumstances that drove that week's strong result |

---

## **WHAT WOULD CHANGE OUR RECOMMENDATION**

We would support rollout if future data shows:

- ✓ Multiple consecutive post-switch weeks with positive, significant effects
- ✓ Parallel pre-trends confirmed in extended data
- ✓ Consistent effects across different branch types and regions
- ✓ No alternative explanations for the observed pattern

---

## **CONCLUSION**

The new procedure is **promising but unproven**. Week 22's strong result (+3.76 points) is encouraging, but one week does not establish a reliable pattern. The board should request additional data before committing resources to system-wide implementation.
