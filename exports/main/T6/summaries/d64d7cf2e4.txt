# REVISED MEMORANDUM

**To:** Board of Directors

**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Revised Recommendation on New Shelving & Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## Bottom Line

**Recommendation: Proceed with caution; do NOT assume the full 2–4 point gain will replicate.**

While switched branches did show higher post-switch scores, **branches that chose to switch were already improving faster than control branches before the procedure change**. This selection bias means we cannot confidently attribute all observed gains to the procedure itself. The true causal effect is likely **smaller than 2 points**, possibly much smaller.

---

## Critical Finding: Pre-Existing Trends

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Pre-switch performance gap | +1.59 points (treatment branches already ahead) |
| Pre-switch gap growth rate | +0.26 points/week (p = 0.0002) |
| **Parallel trends assumption** | **VIOLATED** |

The treatment branches were **already outperforming and improving faster** before they adopted the new procedure. This suggests branches with stronger momentum self-selected into the switch.

---

## What This Means for the Effect Estimate

| Scenario | Estimated Effect |
|----------|-----------------|
| Naive DiD (ignores pre-trends) | +2.02 points |
| Accounting for pre-trend continuation | ~+1.0 point |
| True causal effect | **Uncertain—could be near zero** |

The standard analysis shows a 2-point effect, but if the pre-existing trend had simply continued, we would have expected about 1 point of gap growth anyway. The **incremental effect attributable to the procedure is unclear**.

---

## Revised Recommendations

### Option 1: Phased Rollout with Rigorous Monitoring (RECOMMENDED)
- Roll out to remaining branches in **batches**
- Track service scores **weekly** during implementation
- Be prepared to **pause or adjust** if gains don't materialize
- Set realistic expectations: **0–2 points**, not 2–4 points

### Option 2: Randomized Controlled Trial
- Randomly assign remaining branches to switch now vs. later
- This would provide **clean causal evidence**
- Delays full rollout but reduces risk of wasted investment

### Option 3: Proceed as Planned (NOT RECOMMENDED)
- Risk: May not achieve expected gains
- Could waste resources on a procedure with limited benefit

## Bottom Line for Decision-Makers

The new procedure is **not harmful** and may provide modest benefits. However, the **evidence for a 2–4 point improvement is weaker than we initially believed**. Branches that volunteered to switch were already on an upward trajectory. 

**We recommend a cautious, monitored rollout rather than a confident system-wide implementation.** Set expectations at 0–2 points of improvement, not 2–4 points.

*This revision reflects a more careful assessment of causal inference limitations. The original analysis did not adequately account for selection bias and non-parallel pre-trends.*
