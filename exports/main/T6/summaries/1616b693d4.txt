# **Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**
## **REVISED ANALYSIS**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Do NOT proceed with system-wide rollout based on this evidence.** While switched branches show higher service scores, the data does not demonstrate that the new procedure *caused* the improvement. Branches that chose to switch were already outperforming others before the change, and the gap continued growing at the same rate after. This suggests selection bias rather than a true procedure effect.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. No Clear Break at the Switch Point
| Week | Treatment-Control Gap |
|------|----------------------|
| Week 14 (last before switch) | +2.70 |
| Week 15 (first after switch) | +2.50 |
| **Change at switch** | **-0.20** |

If the procedure caused improvement, we'd expect a jump upward at week 15. Instead, the gap actually dipped slightly.

### 2. The Gap Was Already Growing Before the Switch
- **Week 6:** Treatment branches were 0.12 points *behind* control
- **Week 14:** Treatment branches were 2.70 points *ahead*
- **Pre-switch growth:** 2.83 points over 8 weeks (0.32 points/week)

### 3. Post-Switch Trend Continues Unchanged
- **Post-switch growth rate:** 0.32 points/week (identical to pre-switch)
- The divergence between groups continued at the same pace before and after

### 4. What This Means
The standard difference-in-differences estimate of 2.1 points **assumes** treatment and control branches would have followed parallel trends without the intervention. This assumption is **violated** in our data. The branches that volunteered to switch appear to have been systematically different—perhaps more motivated, better managed, or better resourced.

---

## **Recommendation**

| Option | Assessment |
|--------|------------|
| **System-wide rollout** | ❌ Not supported by evidence |
| **Continue monitoring current switchers** | ✅ Gather more data |
| **Randomized controlled trial** | ✅ Best path forward—randomly assign some remaining branches to switch, others to wait |
| **Qualitative follow-up** | ✅ Interview switched branches about implementation challenges and perceived benefits |

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Pause rollout decision** until stronger evidence is available

2. **Design a randomized trial:**
   - Randomly assign 100-150 of the remaining 284 branches to switch now
   - Keep the rest as control for 8-12 weeks
   - This eliminates selection bias

3. **Continue tracking current switchers** to see if effects persist or fade

4. **Conduct branch interviews** to understand:
   - What motivated some branches to switch early?
   - Do staff perceive real benefits?
   - What implementation challenges emerged?

---

## **Data Summary**
- **709 branches** (425 switched, 284 control)
- **17 weeks** of data (switch at week 15)
- **Method:** Difference-in-differences with parallel trends validation

---

**Bottom line:** The original analysis was statistically sound but relied on an assumption (parallel trends) that the data does not support. The honest conclusion is that we cannot distinguish the procedure's effect from pre-existing differences between branches. A randomized trial would resolve this uncertainty.
