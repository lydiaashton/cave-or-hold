# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**We cannot determine whether the new procedure improves service scores.** The estimated effect is +1.9 points, but the result is **not statistically significant** (p = 0.14). The true effect could be zero, slightly negative, or moderately positive. **More data is needed** before deciding on systemwide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Service Score Performance**

| Group | Before (Weeks 6-14) | After (Weeks 15-22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------|---------------------|--------|
| **Switched Branches** (20 branches) | 51.4 | 51.3 | −0.1 |
| **Non-Switched Branches** (14 branches) | 49.3 | 47.2 | −2.1 |

### **Statistical Results**

| Metric | Value | Interpretation |
|--------|-------|----------------|
| Estimated Effect | +1.9 points | Point estimate |
| 95% Confidence Interval | −0.6 to +4.5 | **Includes zero** |
| P-value | 0.14 | **Not significant** (needs < 0.05) |
| Minimum effect for significance | 2.6 points | Our estimate falls short |

### **What the Data Actually Tells Us**

1. **We cannot confirm the procedure works** — The confidence interval includes zero, meaning no effect is entirely plausible.

2. **We cannot rule out benefit** — Effects up to +4.5 points are also consistent with the data.

3. **We cannot rule out harm** — Small negative effects (down to −0.6) are also possible.

4. **The study is underpowered** — With 34 branches and only 8 weeks of post-switch data, we lack precision to detect effects of this magnitude.

5. **No red flags** — There is no evidence the procedure harms service scores.

---

## **Recommendation**

**Do not proceed with systemwide rollout yet.** Instead:

1. **Continue the pilot** with current switched branches
2. **Collect 12-16 more weeks of data** to improve statistical power
3. **Re-analyze** once we have sufficient evidence
4. **Then decide** whether to roll out, modify, or abandon the procedure

---

## **Rationale**

Rolling out an unproven procedure systemwide carries risks:
- Implementation costs across all branches
- Potential disruption if the procedure doesn't deliver
- Opportunity cost if resources could be better used elsewhere

The cost of waiting for more data is low compared to the cost of a mistaken rollout decision.
