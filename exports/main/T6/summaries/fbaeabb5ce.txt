# **REVISED MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving Procedure – Service Score Impact

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The data does not clearly support rolling out the new procedure.** While branches that switched show higher service scores after the change, this difference was already emerging *before* they switched. The apparent 2.2-point improvement is likely due to pre-existing differences between branches, not the procedure itself. **We cannot confidently attribute score improvements to the new procedure.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **The Parallel Trends Problem**

Difference-in-differences analysis requires that treatment and control groups would have followed similar trends without the intervention. **This assumption is violated in our data:**

| Period | Treatment vs. Control Gap |
|--------|---------------------------|
| Week 6 (start) | Treatment **1.5 points behind** |
| Week 14 (before switch) | Treatment **1.0 points ahead** |
| Week 15–22 (after switch) | Treatment **2.2 points ahead** (average) |

The treatment group gained **2.5 points on the control group during the pre-period alone**—before any procedure change occurred.

### **What This Means**

If the pre-switch trend had continued naturally:
- **Expected gap:** ~3.5 points by end of study
- **Actual gap:** ~2.2 points
- **Residual:** The new procedure may have actually **reduced** the gap by ~1.3 points relative to the expected trend

### **Possible Explanations**

1. **Selection bias:** Branches chosen (or choosing) to switch may have been on an upward trajectory for other reasons (new management, recent renovations, community factors)

2. **The procedure itself:** May have no effect, or possibly a modest negative effect that offset the continuing upward trend

3. **Confounding factors:** Unmeasured variables affecting both the decision to switch and service scores

---

## **Recommendation**

**Do not proceed with cooperative-wide rollout based on current evidence.**

The analysis cannot isolate the procedure's effect from pre-existing branch differences. Rolling out based on this data risks:
- Implementing a change that provides no benefit
- Disrupting operations without measurable gain
- Missing the actual drivers of service score improvements

---

## **Next Steps**

| Action | Rationale |
|--------|-----------|
| **1. Pause rollout decision** | Current evidence is inconclusive |
| **2. Investigate why some branches were selected to switch** | Understand selection criteria and potential confounders |
| **3. Consider a randomized trial** | Randomly assign remaining branches to treatment/control to establish causality |
| **4. Gather qualitative feedback** | Interview branch staff about the procedure's practical impact |
| **5. Analyze other outcomes** | Examine staffing efficiency, patron wait times, or other metrics not captured in service scores |
| **6. Extend monitoring period** | Collect more post-switch data to see if effects emerge over time |

---

## **Technical Appendix**

**Why the Initial Analysis Was Misleading:**

The standard difference-in-differences estimator assumes parallel pre-trends. Our event-study analysis shows:
- Pre-period trend: Treatment gained 0.28 points/week on control (p < 0.001)
- This trend alone explains ~2.2 points over the 8-week post-period
- The DiD estimate conflates pre-existing momentum with treatment effect

**What Would Strengthen the Analysis:**
- Randomized assignment of branches to treatment
- Longer pre-period to establish stable trends
- Branch-level fixed effects with more time periods
- Instrumental variables or matching approaches to address selection
