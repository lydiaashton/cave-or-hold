# **Revised Board Summary: New Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure Evaluation**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**We cannot confidently conclude the new procedure caused the improvement.** While service scores did rise more in treatment branches after the switch, the treatment and control groups were already on different trajectories *before* the intervention began. When accounting for these pre-existing trends, the estimated effect of the new procedure is **near zero and not statistically significant**.

**Recommendation: Do not proceed with system-wide rollout yet. Conduct additional evaluation.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **The Core Problem: Pre-Existing Trends**

| Week | Treatment vs. Control Difference |
|------|----------------------------------|
| Week 6 (start) | -0.70 (treatment worse) |
| Week 14 (pre-switch) | +0.80 (treatment better) |
| Week 22 (end) | +2.69 (treatment better) |

**The treatment group was already improving relative to control BEFORE the switch.** The gap widened by 1.5 points during the pre-period alone—before any intervention occurred.

### **Revised Effect Estimates**

| Analysis | Estimated Effect | Statistical Significance |
|----------|------------------|--------------------------|
| Simple DiD (ignoring trends) | +1.84 points | p < 0.001 |
| DiD **controlling for pre-trends** | **+0.02 points** | **p = 0.97** |
| Week 14→15 jump only | +0.75 points | p = 0.058 (marginal) |

### **What This Means**

- The apparent 1.8-point improvement is **largely explained by pre-existing differences** between the groups, not the new procedure
- When we account for the diverging pre-trends, the treatment effect **disappears**
- There may be a small immediate effect at the switch point (~0.75 points), but this is only marginally significant and could be due to chance

---

## **Why This Matters**

The difference-in-differences method relies on the **parallel trends assumption**: treatment and control groups should have followed similar trajectories absent the intervention. **This assumption is violated here.** The groups selected for early adoption may differ systematically from those that weren't (e.g., more motivated staff, better management, different community demographics).
