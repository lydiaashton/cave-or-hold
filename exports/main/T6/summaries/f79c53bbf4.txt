# **REVISED MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence is inconclusive.** We cannot confidently determine whether the new procedure improves service scores. The estimated effect is positive but not statistically significant, and branches using the new procedure **still score below** non-switched branches after the change. **We recommend against a systemwide rollout until more data is collected.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### Service Score Impact

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| **Branches that switched** (20 branches) | 48.2 | 50.3 | +2.0 |
| **Branches that did not switch** (14 branches) | 51.6 | 51.6 | 0.0 |
| **Estimated effect** | | | **+2.0 points** |

**Critical context:**
- Statistical significance: **p = 0.105** (not significant at 5% level)
- 95% confidence interval: **-0.4 to +4.5 points** (includes no effect)
- **After the switch, treatment branches still score 1.3 points BELOW control branches**
- Only **1 of 8 post-switch weeks** showed treatment branches outperforming control branches

### What This Means

The +2.0 point estimate reflects that switched branches improved *relative to their own past* and relative to the control group's flat performance. However:

1. **Switched branches remain behind:** They started 3.4 points below control and ended 1.3 points below—still behind, just less so.
2. **High uncertainty:** The data is compatible with no effect, a modest positive effect, or even a small negative effect.
3. **Inconsistent weekly performance:** Treatment branches outperformed control in only 12.5% of post-switch weeks.

### Why the Evidence Is Weak

| Issue | Impact |
|-------|--------|
| **Not statistically significant** | 10.5% chance of seeing this result even if the procedure has no effect |
| **Only 8 post-switch weeks** | Limited data reduces confidence in the estimate |
| **Treatment still trails control** | Switched branches have not caught up to non-switched branches |
| **High branch-level variation** | Effects ranged from -5.9 to +9.5 points across branches |

---

## **Recommendations**

### 1. **Do Not Proceed with Systemwide Rollout**
The current evidence does not support a confident decision. Rolling out systemwide based on this data would be premature.

### 2. **Collect More Data Before Deciding**
- Gather **12–16 additional weeks** of data to increase statistical power
- Re-analyze once there are 20+ post-switch weeks
- This would narrow the confidence interval and provide clearer guidance

### 3. **Investigate Why Some Branches Struggled**
- 35% of switched branches saw service scores *decline* after adoption
- Understanding which branches struggled and why could inform implementation support

### 4. **Consider a Structured Pilot**
If the cooperative wishes to continue testing:
- Randomly assign additional branches to adopt the procedure
- Pre-specify success criteria (e.g., "adopt if effect is +1.5 points with p<0.05")
- Set a decision date in advance

### 5. **Reconvene in 12–16 Weeks**
Return to the board with updated analysis to make a final adoption decision based on stronger evidence.

---

## **Conclusion**

The data shows a **positive signal but not proof**. The new procedure may improve service scores by approximately 2 points, but we cannot rule out no effect or even small negative effects. Given the uncertainty and the fact that switched branches still underperform non-switched branches, **a systemwide rollout is not justified at this time.** The prudent course is to gather more data before committing the entire cooperative to the new procedure.
