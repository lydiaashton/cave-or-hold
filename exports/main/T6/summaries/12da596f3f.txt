# **REVISED MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Impact of New Branch Shelving and Rotation Procedure on Service Scores

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence is mixed.** Branches that adopted the new procedure maintained their service scores while non-switched branches declined. However, switched branches were already improving relative to non-switched branches *before* the procedure change, which makes it difficult to isolate the true effect of the new procedure.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with caution. Consider a controlled pilot expansion with rigorous monitoring before system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. What the Data Shows

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|----------------|---------------|--------|
| **Branches with new procedure** | 52.4 | 52.6 | +0.2 |
| **Branches with old procedure** | 51.0 | 49.1 | −1.9 |

Switched branches held steady; non-switched branches declined. The raw difference suggests a ~2 point benefit.

### 2. Important Caveat: Pre-Existing Trends

**Critical issue:** Switched branches were already pulling ahead *before* the new procedure:

| Week | Gap (Switched − Non-Switched) |
|------|-------------------------------|
| 6 | −0.2 |
| 9 | +1.5 |
| 12 | +2.5 |
| 14 | +2.3 |

The gap was growing by **0.35 points per week** before the switch (p<0.001). This pattern makes it difficult to attribute the post-switch gap entirely to the new procedure.

### 3. Revised Effect Estimate

Accounting for pre-existing trends, the **conservative estimate** of the procedure's effect is approximately **+1.0 to +1.5 points** (rather than +2.0), and the true effect could be smaller.

### 4. Benefits Still Appeared Across Branch Types

Despite the methodological concerns, switched branches outperformed across all subgroups (staffing levels, visit volumes), suggesting the procedure is not harmful and may provide modest benefits.

---

## **Next Steps**

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| 1 | **Do not proceed with immediate system-wide rollout** | Evidence is not strong enough to justify full deployment |
| 2 | **Expand pilot to additional volunteer branches** | Gather more data with better pre/post comparison |
| 3 | **Randomize future rollout** | Random assignment would enable cleaner causal inference |
| 4 | **Investigate why non-switched branches declined** | May reveal operational issues unrelated to the procedure |
| 5 | **Re-analyze after 8-12 more weeks of data** | More post-switch data will improve estimate precision |

---

## **Bottom Line (Restated)**

The new procedure **did not harm** service scores and branches using it performed better than those that did not. However, because switched branches were already improving before the change, we cannot confidently attribute the full performance gap to the procedure itself. A more cautious, evidence-building approach is warranted before committing to system-wide implementation.

---

*Analysis based on weekly service score data from 709 branches, weeks 6–22. The key limitation is that treatment and control groups were not on parallel trends before the intervention, which undermines causal claims.*
