# **Board Memorandum**

## **New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure: Impact Assessment**

---

### **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure shows a positive effect, but with important limitations.** Branches that switched show a **+2 point improvement** in service scores, and this result is statistically reliable. However, because branches were not randomly assigned to switch, we cannot be certain the full 2-point gain is caused by the procedure itself. We recommend **proceeding with rollout, but in phases with continued evaluation** rather than immediate system-wide implementation.

---

### **Key Findings**

| Metric | Result |
|--------|--------|
| **Measured Impact** | +2.07 points (p < 0.001) |
| **Confidence Interval** | 1.91 to 2.23 points |
| **Relative Improvement** | 4% above baseline |
| **Branches Analyzed** | 709 (425 switched, 284 control) |

**What Supports the Finding:**
- The result is **highly statistically significant**—unlikely to be random noise
- Switched branches improved while control branches declined slightly
- The effect held after controlling for staffing and visitor volume
- The gap between groups **widened over the 8 weeks post-switch**

**What Limits Our Confidence:**
- **Selection bias concern**: Branches chosen to switch first were already on a slightly stronger trajectory before the change (pre-trend p = 0.010)
- **Causal uncertainty**: The true effect may be smaller—our conservative estimate ranges from **+0.5 to +2.0 points**
- **Limited follow-up**: Only 8 weeks of post-switch data; long-term sustainability is unknown

---

### **Recommendation**

**Approve phased rollout with monitoring.** The evidence supports a real positive effect, and the direction matches expectations. However, given the methodological limitations and modest effect size, we recommend:

1. Expand to remaining branches over 2–3 months (not all at once)
2. Continue weekly score tracking throughout rollout
3. Conduct a formal review after 6 months to confirm effects persist
4. Document implementation practices to maximize benefit at each branch

---

### **Bottom Line (Restated)**

The new procedure **does improve service scores**, but the gain is **modest** and our confidence in the exact magnitude is **limited by the study design**. This supports rollout, but argues for a measured approach rather than treating the 2-point estimate as certain.
