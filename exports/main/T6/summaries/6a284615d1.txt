# **Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure Evaluation**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure shows a statistically significant positive effect, but the practical benefit is modest.** Treatment branches scored approximately **2 points higher** than control branches after implementation. However, **57% of treatment branches still saw their scores decline**—the new procedure mainly helped branches decline less than those using the old procedure.

**Recommendation: Proceed cautiously.** Approve a **phased expansion** (not full rollout) while gathering more data on costs, long-term effects, and the cause of the system-wide score decline observed after week 15.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect: Real but Modest

| Metric | Value | Interpretation |
|--------|-------|----------------|
| Estimated impact | +1.96 points | Statistically significant (p < 0.01) |
| Effect size | 0.22 standard deviations | Small-to-moderate |
| Relative improvement | 3.9% of baseline | Modest |
| 95% Confidence interval | +0.78 to +3.13 | Uncertainty range |

### 2. What Actually Happened at Branch Level

| Outcome | Treatment Branches | Control Branches |
|---------|-------------------|------------------|
| **Improved** after week 15 | 43% | 25% |
| **Declined** after week 15 | 57% | 75% |

**Key insight:** The new procedure did not make most branches better—it protected them from a system-wide decline that affected all branches after week 15.

### 3. The Unexplained Decline

Both groups experienced a drop in service scores after week 15:

| Group | Before | After | Change |
|-------|--------|-------|--------|
| Control | 49.14 | 46.92 | **−2.22** |
| Treatment | 49.86 | 49.56 | **−0.31** |

**Critical question:** What caused this system-wide decline? (Seasonal pattern? External factor? Data artifact?) The answer matters for estimating the new procedure's true value.

### 4. Analysis Strengths and Limitations

**Strengths:**
✓ Parallel trends confirmed before switch (p = 0.41)  
✓ Effect consistent across all 8 post-switch weeks  
✓ Robust to controls for staffing and visitor volume  

**Limitations:**
⚠ Only 8 weeks of post-treatment data  
⚠ No cost or resource requirement data  
⚠ Cause of system-wide decline unknown  
⚠ Effect size modest—practical significance unclear

## **Recommendations**

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| 1 | **Do NOT approve full rollout yet** | Evidence is promising but incomplete |
| 2 | **Expand to 20–30 additional branches** | Gather more data before committing all 150 branches |
| 3 | **Investigate the week-15 decline** | Understand whether this was temporary or ongoing |
| 4 | **Quantify implementation costs** | Determine if 2-point gain justifies resource investment |
| 5 | **Extend monitoring to 6+ months** | Assess whether effect sustains, grows, or fades |
| 6 | **Define "meaningful" service score change** | Establish what magnitude of improvement warrants rollout |

---

## **Decision Framework**

**Proceed to full rollout IF:**
- Cost-benefit analysis shows positive ROI
- Effect persists beyond 3–6 months
- System-wide decline is understood and likely to recur

**Reconsider or modify procedure IF:**
- Implementation costs are high relative to benefit
- Effect fades over time
- Decline was a one-time anomaly unlikely to repeat
