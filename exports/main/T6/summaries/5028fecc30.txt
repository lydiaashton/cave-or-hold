# **Impact of New Stock Rotation Procedure on Library Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**We cannot determine whether the new procedure affected service scores.** While branches using the new procedure showed higher scores after the switch, the data reveals that treatment branches were already on a different trajectory before the change—they were recovering from a mid-period dip while control branches remained stable. This violates the key assumption needed to isolate the procedure's effect. **We recommend against system-wide rollout based on this evidence alone** and suggest gathering additional data before making a decision.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Observed Difference Exists, But Causation Is Unclear
| Group | Pre-Switch Avg | Post-Switch Avg | Change |
|-------|---------------|-----------------|--------|
| Treatment (New Procedure) | 51.2 | 52.4 | +1.2 |
| Control (Old Procedure) | 51.9 | 51.3 | -0.6 |
| **Raw Difference** | | | **+1.8** |

The 1.8-point gap is statistically significant in a simple model (p = 0.002), **but** this does not prove causation.

### 2. Critical Problem: Pre-Existing Diverging Trends
The event study reveals treatment branches were **already trending differently** before the switch:

| Period | Treatment-Control Gap |
|--------|----------------------|
| Weeks 6-9 | Treatment 1.3–2.1 points **behind** |
| Weeks 10-14 | Treatment gradually **catching up** |
| Week 14 (immediately before switch) | Treatment 0.4 points **ahead** |
| Weeks 15-22 (after switch) | Treatment 0.4–1.6 points **ahead** |

The post-switch "effect" appears to be a continuation of a pre-existing recovery trend, not a response to the procedure change.

### 3. Placebo Tests Confirm the Problem
When testing fake switch dates in the pre-period, we still find "significant effects":
- Fake switch at week 12: p = 0.009
- Fake switch at week 13: p = 0.004
- Fake switch at week 14: p = 0.002

This pattern indicates the model is capturing underlying trend differences, not a true treatment effect.

### 4. What We Can Say
- Service scores at treatment branches improved relative to control branches after week 15
- **However**, treatment branches were already improving relative to control branches before week 15
- We cannot disentangle the procedure effect from pre-existing trends with this data

---

## **Recommendation**

**Do not proceed with system-wide rollout based on this analysis.** The evidence is insufficient to conclude the new procedure causes service score improvements. The observed difference may reflect pre-existing branch characteristics or trends unrelated to the procedure change.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Extend the observation period**: Collect more pre-switch data to better establish whether trends were truly parallel before the intervention

2. **Consider a stepped-wedge design**: Randomly assign remaining branches to switch at different dates, allowing cleaner causal inference

3. **Investigate branch characteristics**: Examine whether treatment and control branches differ systematically (size, location, staffing, patron demographics) in ways that might explain diverging trends

4. **Gather qualitative evidence**: Interview staff at treatment branches to understand whether they perceive the new procedure as affecting their work or patron interactions

5. **Re-evaluate in 6-12 months**: With more data and potentially a cleaner identification strategy, reassess whether the procedure has a causal effect

---

*Analysis based on 2,550 weekly observations from 150 branches (weeks 6–22). The parallel trends assumption required for causal inference appears violated, limiting confidence in any estimated effect.*
