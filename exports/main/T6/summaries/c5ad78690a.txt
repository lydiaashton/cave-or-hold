# Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## Bottom Line

**The evidence is inconclusive.** While the statistical model suggests a +2.3 point average effect, this finding has important limitations: treatment branches started with systematically lower scores, only 65% of switched branches actually improved, and post-switch performance gaps remain negative in most weeks. **I cannot recommend a full rollout without further investigation.**

---

## Key Findings

### Study Design
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** switched to the new procedure at week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure
- **Critical concern**: Treatment branches had significantly lower baseline scores (average gap: -3.5 points before switch)

### Main Results

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| DiD estimate | +2.29 points |
| 95% Confidence Interval | [0.68, 3.89] |
| Statistical significance | p = 0.005 |
| Branches that improved | 13 of 20 (65%) |
| Branches that declined | 7 of 20 (35%) |

### Why the Result Is Uncertain

1. **Selection bias concern**: Branches that switched were systematically lower-performing before the change. The apparent "gain" may reflect regression to the mean rather than a true treatment effect.

2. **Post-switch reality**: In 6 of 8 weeks after the switch, treatment branches still scored BELOW control branches. The DiD measures relative improvement from baseline, not absolute superiority.

3. **Inconsistent effects**: Over one-third of switched branches saw score declines, with some dropping 5+ points.

4. **Statistical limitations**: Standard errors are not clustered by branch. With only 34 branches, proper cluster-robust inference would likely widen confidence intervals substantially.

5. **Short follow-up**: Only 8 weeks of post-switch data. We don't know if effects persist, grow, or fade.

---

## Next Steps

1. **Pause full rollout** — The current evidence does not support system-wide implementation.

2. **Investigate why some branches declined** — Understand what distinguishes the 7 branches that got worse from the 13 that improved (training, layout, staffing, leadership).

3. **Consider randomized rollout** — If proceeding, randomly assign remaining branches to treatment vs. control to eliminate selection bias.

4. **Extend monitoring period** — Collect at least 6 more months of data to assess whether effects stabilize or fade.

5. **Re-analyze with proper methods** — Use cluster-robust standard errors and consider matching treatment branches to similar control branches based on pre-switch performance.

6. **Define success criteria** — What magnitude of improvement justifies the cost of changing procedures? Is 2 points meaningful for patron experience?

---

*Analysis based on weekly service scores from 34 branches, weeks 6–22. The difference-in-differences method controls for branch-specific and week-specific factors, but selection bias and regression to the mean remain concerns.*
