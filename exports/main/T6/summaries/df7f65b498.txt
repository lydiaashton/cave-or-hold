# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure  
**Date:** November 15, 2026

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The evidence suggests the new procedure improves service scores, but with important caveats.** Branches that switched showed a **~2 point improvement** (3.8%), and this effect was consistent across all post-switch weeks. However, pre-switch differences between groups introduce some uncertainty about the exact causal effect. **We recommend a phased rollout with close monitoring** rather than immediate systemwide implementation.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### 1. Positive Association with Service Scores

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect | **+1.97 points** |
| 95% confidence interval | 1.62 to 2.31 points |
| Relative improvement | **3.8%** above average |

All 8 post-switch weeks showed positive, statistically significant effects ranging from +1.1 to +2.1 points.

### 2. Important Caveat: Pre-Existing Differences

The treatment and control branches showed some differences **before** the switch:
- Week 8 before switch: treatment group scored 1.5 points lower (statistically significant)
- Joint statistical test indicates groups were not on perfectly parallel trajectories

**What this means:** The true causal effect could be somewhat smaller (or larger) than our estimate. We cannot rule out that some portion of the observed improvement reflects pre-existing differences rather than the procedure itself.

### 3. Branch-Level Variation

- **66% of switched branches** improved after adoption
- **34% saw no improvement or decline**
- This variation suggests implementation quality or local conditions matter

### 4. Effect Pattern Over Time

| Weeks After Switch | Estimated Effect |
|-------------------|------------------|
| Weeks 1–3 | +1.1 to +1.2 points |
| Weeks 4–6 | +2.0 to +2.1 points |
| Weeks 7–8 | +1.7 to +1.8 points |

Effects appeared immediately and remained elevated throughout observation.

## **RECOMMENDATION**

**Proceed with a phased rollout, not immediate systemwide implementation.**

**Rationale:**
- The weight of evidence favors the new procedure (consistent positive effects, large sample, robust to controls)
- But pre-trend differences create uncertainty about the exact causal magnitude
- A phased approach allows learning and adjustment before full commitment
