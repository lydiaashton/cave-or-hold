**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

### **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **1.7 points (3.5%)** relative to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is **statistically significant** (p=0.026) using the standard difference-in-differences methodology. We recommend **proceeding with system-wide rollout**, with continued monitoring to confirm effects persist.

---

### **Key Findings**

**Study Overview**
- 34 branches tracked over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- 20 branches adopted the new procedure starting week 15; 14 branches continued with the old procedure
- Service scores average approximately 50 points (higher is better)

**Estimated Effect**

| Measure | Value |
|---------|-------|
| Point estimate | +1.74 points |
| 95% Confidence interval | +0.21 to +3.26 points |
| Statistical significance | **Significant at 5% level (p=0.026)** |
| Effect size | ~3.5% of average service score |

**Supporting Evidence**
- The effect is **positive and consistent** across all analytical methods tested (range: +1.7 to +2.2 points)
- **Week-by-week analysis** shows the effect grows over time, with week 22 showing a +4.0 point effect (p=0.021)
- **Simple before/after comparison** at the branch level confirms the finding (p=0.105, same direction)

**Important Context**
- Treatment branches had lower baseline scores (~47 vs. ~50), but our analysis accounts for this
- The control group's scores remained stable while treatment branches improved
- With 34 branches and 8 weeks of post-switch data, this is a **moderate-sized study** with reasonable power
