**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

## **Bottom Line**

**Proceed with caution toward system-wide rollout.** The data suggest the new procedure is associated with higher service scores (+2.3 points, p < 0.001). However, branches that adopted the new procedure were underperforming relative to control branches in the weeks immediately before the switch, which introduces uncertainty about whether the effect is causal or partly reflects natural recovery from a low point. We recommend a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than immediate full implementation.

### The Positive Signal
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect | +2.3 points |
| 95% Confidence Interval | [1.0, 3.6] |
| Statistical significance | p = 0.0005 |
| Practical interpretation | Treatment branches maintained scores; control branches declined |

### The Concern
| Period | Treatment − Control |
|--------|---------------------|
| Weeks 6-10 | +0.07 (equal) |
| Weeks 11-14 | **−1.39** (treatment worse) |
| Weeks 15-22 | **+1.73** (treatment better) |

The treatment group was underperforming just before adoption. This pattern could reflect:
- **Mean reversion** (natural bounce-back from a low point)
- **Selection effects** (struggling branches may have been more likely to switch)
- **A genuine treatment effect** (the procedure helped)

We cannot definitively distinguish among these explanations with the current data.

## **Bottom Line (Restated)**

The evidence is **suggestive but not definitive**. The new procedure is associated with better service scores, and there is no evidence of harm. However, given the pre-trend concerns, a cautious phased approach is warranted rather than immediate system-wide adoption. If the effect holds across additional branches and time, full rollout should proceed.
