# **Library Branch Stock Rotation Procedure: Impact Analysis**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**We cannot confidently conclude the new procedure caused the improvement.** While switched branches showed better outcomes, they were already improving faster than other branches *before* adopting the procedure. This selection bias means the observed 1.7-point gain may reflect pre-existing differences rather than the procedure's effect. Statistical adjustment suggests the true causal effect could be **substantially smaller—or even zero**.

**Recommendation: Do not approve system-wide rollout yet.** Instead, conduct a **randomized controlled trial** to isolate the procedure's true effect before making a system-wide decision.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. The Core Problem: Pre-Existing Trends
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Switched branches' pre-switch trend | +0.59 points/week |
| Non-switched branches' pre-switch trend | +0.32 points/week |
| **Difference (before any switch)** | **+0.27 points/week** |
| Statistical significance | p = 0.004 |

Branches that chose to switch were **already improving faster** before the procedure change. This violates the key assumption needed to attribute post-switch gains to the procedure itself.

### 2. What This Means for the Estimate
- **Observed DiD estimate:** +1.7 points
- **Expected gap increase from pre-trend alone:** +2.2 points (over 8 weeks)
- **Implied causal effect:** Approximately **−0.4 points** (not statistically distinguishable from zero)

The data are consistent with the procedure having **no effect** or even a **slight negative effect** once we account for pre-existing trends.

### 3. More Reassuring (But Still Uncertain) Signal
Looking only at the 5 weeks immediately before the switch (weeks 10-14), the pre-trend difference is smaller and not statistically significant (p=0.14). This leaves open the possibility of a modest positive effect, but we cannot quantify it reliably.

### 4. What We Know With Confidence
- Switched branches are higher-performing overall
- Switched branches were on a stronger improvement trajectory before the change
- The procedure *might* help, but current evidence cannot confirm this

## **Next Steps**

### For Board Decision
1. **Do not authorize system-wide rollout** based on current evidence
2. **Approve a randomized controlled trial** to establish causal impact

### For Management Execution
3. **Randomly assign 50-100 branches** to adopt the procedure (treatment) vs. continue current practice (control)
4. **Monitor for 12-16 weeks** with weekly service score tracking
5. **Return to the board** with definitive evidence before making a rollout decision

### Alternative Path
If a randomized trial is not feasible:
- Proceed with a **phased, optional rollout** allowing branches to adopt at their discretion
- **Track outcomes carefully** and be prepared to discontinue if no benefit materializes
- **Document implementation quality** to identify whether the procedure works when properly executed
