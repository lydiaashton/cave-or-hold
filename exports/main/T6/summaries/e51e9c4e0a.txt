# **REVISED MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative

**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure  

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The evidence is inconclusive. We do NOT recommend a full rollout at this time.**

While branches that switched to the new procedure showed higher service scores after the change, **these same branches were already improving faster before the switch**. We cannot determine whether the improvement was caused by the new procedure or by pre-existing differences between branches. A more rigorous test is needed before system-wide implementation.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **The Core Problem: Pre-Existing Differences**

| Week | Gap (Switched − Control) |
|------|--------------------------|
| Week 6 | +0.17 points |
| Week 10 | +0.48 points |
| Week 14 (last pre-switch) | +2.07 points |
| Week 15 (first post-switch) | +2.43 points |
| Week 22 | +3.40 points |

**Critical observation:** The gap widened by **1.9 points BEFORE the switch** but only **0.35 points AT the switch**. This suggests the improvement trend predates the procedure change.

### **What the Standard Analysis Shows**

| Measure | Estimate |
|---|---|
| Standard DiD estimate | +2.1 points |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 |

**However**, this estimate is likely biased because the fundamental assumption of difference-in-differences (parallel pre-trends) is violated.

### **More Conservative Estimates**

| Approach | Estimate |
|---|---|
| Immediate jump at switch (Week 14→15) | +0.35 points |
| Change in trend (post vs. pre) | Not statistically distinguishable from zero |
| Adjusted for pre-trend | +0.5 to +1.0 points (highly uncertain) |

---

## **WHY THIS MATTERS**

Branches that chose to switch may differ systematically from those that didn't:
- Better-managed branches may have been more likely to adopt the new procedure
- These branches may have been on an improvement trajectory regardless of the procedure
- We cannot separate selection effects from the procedure's actual impact

---

## **RECOMMENDATIONS**

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Pause full rollout** | Current evidence does not support the cost of system-wide change |
| **2** | **Run a randomized trial** | Randomly assign remaining branches to switch or not; this would establish causality |
| **3** | **Extend monitoring** | Continue tracking current switched branches for 6+ more months |
| **4** | **Gather qualitative data** | Interview branch managers about what changed and whether it feels beneficial |
| **5** | **Revisit in 6 months** | With more data and possibly a randomized test, make a better-informed decision |

---

## **CONCLUSION**

The new procedure **may** improve service scores, but the current data cannot confirm this. The branches that switched were already outperforming the control group before the change. Rolling out the procedure system-wide based on this evidence risks implementing a change that provides little or no benefit.

A randomized controlled trial or a longer observation period with better controls would provide the evidence needed for a confident decision.

## **Summary of the Revision**

My original bottom line was too confident. The key issue is:

1. **Pre-trends were not parallel** — Switched branches were already improving faster before the procedure changed (p=0.002 for pre-trend test)
2. **The actual "jump" at switch was tiny** — Only 0.35 points from week 14 to 15, not 2+ points

The honest conclusion is that we **cannot confidently attribute the improvement to the procedure**. A more rigorous test is needed before recommending rollout.
