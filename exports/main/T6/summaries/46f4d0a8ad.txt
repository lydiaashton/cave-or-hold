# **Revised Memorandum to the Board of Directors**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** [Current Date]  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure  

---

## **Bottom Line**

**We cannot confidently conclude that the new procedure caused the observed improvement in service scores.** While branches that adopted the new procedure showed higher scores after the switch, those same branches were already improving faster *before* they switched. This pre-existing difference means the observed 2-point gap may reflect branch characteristics rather than the procedure itself.

**Recommendation:** Do not proceed with immediate cooperative-wide rollout. Instead, implement a **randomized phased rollout** for the remaining 284 branches to generate more reliable evidence before making a final decision.

---

## **Key Findings**

### What the Data Shows

| Observation | Value |
|-------------|-------|
| Raw difference after switch | +2.1 service score points |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 |
| **BUT: Pre-treatment trend difference** | **+0.27 points/week** |

### The Critical Problem

The branches that chose to adopt the new procedure early were **already improving faster** than other branches before the switch occurred:

- **Control branches** (old procedure): -0.04 points/week trend before switch
- **Treatment branches** (new procedure): +0.23 points/week trend before switch

Over the 9-week pre-treatment period, this trend difference accumulates to approximately **2.4 points**—which fully explains the observed 2.1-point "treatment effect."

### What This Means

When we statistically account for these pre-existing trends, the estimated effect of the new procedure drops to **0.2 points** and is **no longer statistically distinguishable from zero** (p = 0.52).

**In plain terms:** The branches that switched may have been better-managed, better-staffed, or otherwise positioned to improve regardless of the procedure change. We cannot separate the procedure's effect from these underlying differences.

---

## **Recommended Next Steps**

### 1. Randomized Rollout (Preferred Approach)
For the remaining 284 branches that have not yet switched:
- **Randomly assign** half to adopt the new procedure immediately
- **Randomly assign** half to continue with the old procedure for 8–12 more weeks
- This creates a clean experimental comparison that eliminates selection bias

### 2. If Randomization Is Not Feasible
- **Delay full rollout** and continue monitoring current branches
- **Collect additional data** on branch characteristics (staffing levels, budget, patron demographics)
- **Re-analyze** with better controls for branch differences

### 3. Regardless of Approach
- **Document implementation fidelity**—ensure branches are actually following the new procedure
- **Track costs** associated with the new procedure
- **Gather qualitative feedback** from branch staff about the change

---

## **Conclusion**

The initial analysis appeared to show a clear benefit from the new procedure. However, upon closer examination, the evidence is **not sufficient to support a causal claim**. The branches that adopted the new procedure were already on a more positive trajectory before the switch, and this pre-existing difference likely explains most or all of the observed improvement.

A more rigorous evaluation—ideally using random assignment for the remaining branches—is needed before committing the cooperative to a full rollout. This approach will provide the board with reliable evidence for a final decision.
