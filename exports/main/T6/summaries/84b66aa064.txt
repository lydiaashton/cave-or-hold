# **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative
**From:** Data Analysis Team
**Date:** November 2025
**Subject:** Recommendation on System-Wide Procedure Rollout

---

## **Bottom Line**

The data shows **inconclusive evidence** about whether the new procedure improved service scores. While the estimated effect is positive (+2.5 points), the result is **not statistically significant** and the confidence interval includes zero. Additionally, the treatment and control groups showed different trends even before the switch, which weakens our ability to attribute any change to the procedure itself.

**Recommendation: Do not proceed with system-wide rollout yet. Gather more data first.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Statistical Evidence Does Not Support a Confident Conclusion**

| Measure | Result | Interpretation |
|---------|--------|----------------|
| Estimated effect | +2.5 points | Point estimate is positive |
| P-value | 0.12 | **Not statistically significant** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | -0.6 to +5.5 | **Includes zero and negative values** |

The confidence interval means the true effect could plausibly be:
- A small harmful effect (-0.6 points)
- No effect at all (0 points)
- A meaningful positive effect (+5.5 points)

We cannot distinguish among these possibilities with the current data.

### **2. Pre-Existing Differences Between Groups**

The switched and non-switched branches did **not** follow parallel trends before the procedure change:

| Pre-Switch Week | Difference (Switched − Non-Switched) |
|-----------------|-------------------------------------|
| Week 6 | -4.30 (significant) |
| Week 10 | -5.69 (significant) |
| Week 11 | -5.00 (significant) |
| Week 12 | -4.75 (significant) |

This violates a key assumption of our analysis method. The groups were systematically different before the intervention, making it harder to attribute post-switch differences to the procedure itself.

### **3. Inconsistent Post-Switch Pattern**

After the switch, the week-by-week effects were erratic:

| Post-Switch Week | Effect | Significance |
|------------------|--------|--------------|
| Week 15 | +0.14 | Not significant |
| Week 16 | -4.30 | **Significantly negative** |
| Week 17 | -5.51 | **Significantly negative** |
| Week 18 | +5.38 | **Significantly positive** |
| Weeks 19-22 | Mixed | Mostly not significant |

This pattern does not show a clear, sustained improvement.

---

## **Recommendation**

**Pause the system-wide rollout and collect more evidence.**

**Rationale:**
1. The current evidence does not meet standard thresholds for statistical confidence
2. Pre-existing differences between groups undermine causal inference
3. Post-treatment effects are inconsistent, not steadily positive
4. Rolling out an unproven procedure system-wide risks disrupting operations without guaranteed benefit

---

## **Next Steps**

| Action | Timeline | Purpose |
|--------|----------|---------|
| Continue monitoring both groups | 8-12 more weeks | Increase statistical power |
| Investigate why groups differed pre-switch | Immediate | Understand selection bias |
| Consider randomized rollout for remaining branches | Next quarter | Enable cleaner causal inference |
| Re-evaluate with additional data | 3 months | Make informed rollout decision |

---

## **What Would Change Our Recommendation?**

We would recommend rollout if future data shows:
- Statistically significant positive effect (p < 0.05)
- Confidence interval entirely above zero
- Consistent positive effects across multiple post-switch weeks
- Evidence that pre-treatment differences are accounted for

---

*Analysis based on 578 weekly observations from 34 branches, weeks 6–22. Difference-in-differences estimation with branch and week fixed effects, clustered standard errors.*
