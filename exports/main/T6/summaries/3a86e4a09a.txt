## Bottom Line

**The new procedure improved service scores on average, but effects vary significantly across branches.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **2 points (4%)** on average compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant (p=0.002).

**However, 43% of branches that switched saw their scores decline.** The procedure benefits most branches but may not suit all locations.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rollout, but implement monitoring to identify branches where the procedure is not working and provide targeted support.

---

## Key Findings

### 1. Positive Average Effect
- Average improvement: **+2.0 points** on the service score index
- 95% confidence interval: **0.7 to 3.3 points**
- Represents approximately **4% improvement** over the baseline average of 50.6

### 2. Significant Variation Across Branches
| Outcome | Treatment Branches | Control Branches |
|---------|-------------------|------------------|
| Scores improved | 57% | 45% |
| Scores declined | 43% | 55% |

- The new procedure shifts the odds in favor of improvement, but **not all branches benefit**
- Control branches declined slightly on average (-0.5 points), while treatment branches improved (+1.6 points)

### 3. Strong Statistical Evidence
- Result is highly statistically significant (p=0.002)
- Pre-trend analysis confirms both groups were on parallel trajectories before the switch
- Analysis controlled for staffing levels and visitor volume

### 4. Modest Effect Size
- The 2-point improvement equals about **0.20 standard deviations**—a modest but meaningful effect
- For context: 31% of treatment branches saw improvements greater than 4 points

## Next Steps

1. **Approve rollout with monitoring** — Proceed with system-wide implementation but track branch-level outcomes closely

2. **Identify at-risk branches** — After 4-6 weeks, flag any branches where scores decline for additional support or procedure adjustment

3. **Investigate heterogeneity** — Analyze whether branch characteristics (size, urban/rural, staffing levels) predict which branches benefit most

4. **Allow flexibility** — Consider permitting branches that show sustained decline to modify or revert the procedure

5. **Set success metrics** — Define what constitutes acceptable performance (e.g., system-wide average improvement, maximum % of branches declining)

## Appendix: Methodology

**Data:** 2,550 weekly observations from 150 branches over 17 weeks (weeks 6-22), with the switch occurring in week 15.

**Analysis:** Difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch), controlling for staffing index and visits index.

**Limitation:** This analysis identifies the average effect but cannot predict which specific branches will benefit without further investigation.
