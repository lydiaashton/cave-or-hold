# **REVISED MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Rotation Procedure  
**Date:** Analysis of weeks 6-22 data

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The evidence is inconclusive.** While branches that switched show higher service scores after the change, **those branches were already improving faster before the switch**. The statistical method we used assumes both groups were on parallel trajectories—they were not. This means we cannot confidently attribute the improvement to the new procedure.

**Recommendation: Do NOT proceed with system-wide rollout yet.** Instead, gather more evidence before making a decision.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **The Core Problem: Pre-Existing Trends**

| Week | Treatment vs. Control Gap |
|------|--------------------------|
| Week 6 (start) | Treatment **0.42 points behind** |
| Week 14 (before switch) | Treatment **2.09 points ahead** |
| Week 22 (end) | Treatment **3.75 points ahead** |

**The treatment group went from behind to ahead BEFORE the procedure changed.** The gap was already widening by **0.21 points per week** (p=0.005) before the switch. If this trend simply continued, it would explain most of what we observe.

### **What Happened After the Switch?**

| Period | Effect vs. Expected Trend | Statistical Significance |
|--------|--------------------------|-------------------------|
| Weeks 15-18 | No detectable effect | Not significant (p > 0.28) |
| Weeks 19-22 | Gap widens further | Significant (p < 0.05) |

**There was no immediate effect** in the first 4 weeks after switching. The divergence only became apparent 5+ weeks later. This pattern is inconsistent with a direct procedure effect and more consistent with:
- Continuation of pre-existing trends, OR
- Some other factor affecting branches differently over time

### **The Numbers (With Important Caveats)**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 51.05 | 54.75 | +3.70 |
| Non-switched branches | 50.17 | 51.92 | +1.75 |
| **Raw difference** | | | **+1.95** |

⚠️ **This +1.95 point estimate cannot be interpreted as causal** because the parallel trends assumption is violated.

---

## **WHAT THIS MEANS**

**We do not have reliable evidence that the new procedure causes improvement.** The branches that switched may have been:
- Better-managed branches that were already improving
- Branches selected for the change because they were on an upward trajectory
- Branches with other unmeasured advantages

Without random assignment or parallel pre-trends, we cannot separate the procedure effect from these other factors.

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

1. **Pause the rollout decision.** Do not commit to system-wide implementation based on this evidence alone.

2. **Gather stronger evidence:**
   - **Extend the study period:** Collect more weeks of data to see if the pattern persists or reverses
   - **Consider random assignment:** If feasible, randomly assign remaining branches to switch now vs. later
   - **Collect qualitative data:** Interview branch staff about implementation quality and perceived effects

3. **If proceeding despite uncertainty:**
   - Implement as a **pilot** with careful monitoring
   - Set clear success metrics and a review date (e.g., 12 weeks post-implementation)
   - Be prepared to reverse course if results don't materialize

4. **Document selection criteria:** Understand why certain branches were chosen to switch first—this may explain the pre-existing trend difference.

---

**Bottom line:** The data shows correlation, not causation. The board should not approve a system-wide rollout based on this analysis alone.
