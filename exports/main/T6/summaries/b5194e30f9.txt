### **Key Findings**

**Study Overview**
- 150 branches tracked over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- 90 branches adopted the new procedure at week 15; 60 branches continued with the old procedure
- Service scores average around 50 points system-wide (higher is better)

**Results**

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|---------------|---------------|--------|
| Branches that switched | 48.55 | 51.72 | +3.17 |
| Branches that did not switch | 47.24 | 48.35 | +1.12 |
| **Net effect of new procedure** | | | **+2.04** |

- **Estimated impact: +2.0 points** (95% confidence interval: 0.7 to 3.4)
- **Statistical significance: p = 0.003**
- **Effect size: ~4% improvement** relative to baseline; about 0.2 standard deviations

**Why We Trust This Result**
- Branches that switched and those that did not showed parallel trends before the change
- The improvement appeared consistently across all post-switch weeks
- The analysis controls for branch-specific factors and week-to-week variation

**Important Caveat**
This outcome was not expected. Before committing to full rollout, we should investigate *why* the procedure had this effect—it may reveal insights about what actually drives service scores.

### **Next Steps**

1. **Approve a phased rollout** to additional branches over the next 4–6 weeks, with careful monitoring.

2. **Gather qualitative feedback** from staff at switched branches to understand what changed in their workflows and patron interactions.

3. **Continue tracking service scores** to confirm the effect persists and doesn't fade over time.
