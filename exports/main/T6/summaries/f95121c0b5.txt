# **REVISED MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** New Shelving Procedure – Service Score Impact Analysis

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence is inconclusive.** While branches that switched procedures showed higher service scores afterward, those same branches were already improving faster *before* the switch. This undermines our ability to claim the procedure change caused the improvement. The 2.2-point estimate likely overstates the true effect.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **What the Raw Numbers Show**

| Group | Before | After | Change |
|-------|--------|-------|--------|
| Branches keeping old procedure (284) | 51.03 | 49.87 | −1.16 |
| Branches adopting new procedure (425) | 51.00 | 52.02 | +1.02 |
| **Raw difference-in-differences** | | | **+2.19** |

### **The Critical Problem: Pre-Existing Trends**

Branches selected for the new procedure were **already improving faster** before the switch:

- Treatment branches gained **0.26 points per week more** than control branches during Weeks 6–14
- This pre-trend difference is **statistically significant (p = 0.0001)**
- Over 9 weeks, this accounts for approximately **2.3 points** of the observed effect

**This means the 2.2-point "treatment effect" may be entirely explained by pre-existing differences, not the procedure change itself.**

### **Why This Matters**

The standard analysis method (difference-in-differences) requires that treatment and control groups follow similar trends before the intervention. This assumption is **violated** in our data. Branches were not randomly assigned to switch—some systematic factor (management quality, staff engagement, local conditions) likely influenced both the selection to switch AND the service score trajectory.

---

## **Recommendation**

**Do not approve cooperative-wide rollout based on this analysis alone.**

We cannot confidently attribute the score improvement to the procedure change. Rolling out based on potentially inflated expectations risks:
- Disappointing results that don't match the 2.2-point projection
- Unnecessary disruption and training costs across 284 additional branches
- Loss of credibility if the effect doesn't materialize

## **Alternative Path Forward**

If the board wishes to proceed despite the uncertainty:

1. **Randomize the remaining rollout** – Assign remaining 284 branches randomly to switch now vs. later, creating a cleaner experimental design
2. **Set conservative expectations** – Plan for 0 to 1.5 point improvement, not 2.2 points
3. **Monitor closely** – Track weekly scores and be prepared to pause if results don't materialize
