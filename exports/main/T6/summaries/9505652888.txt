# **BOARD SUMMARY: New Stock Rotation Procedure Evaluation**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**We cannot conclude the new procedure caused the improvement.** While service scores did rise more at switched branches after the change, our analysis reveals that these branches were **already improving faster than others before the switch**. This pre-existing trend fully accounts for the observed difference. **We cannot recommend rollout based on this evidence alone.**

---

## **What We Found**

| Metric | Result |
|--------|--------|
| **Raw DiD estimate** | +1.98 service score points |
| **Pre-switch trend difference** | +0.28 points/week (treatment vs. control) |
| **Implied bias over 8 weeks** | +2.24 points |
| **Bias-adjusted estimate** | **-0.26 points** |
| **Statistical conclusion** | Pre-trends violate causal inference assumptions |

**The critical issue:** Treatment branches were gaining 0.28 points per week MORE than control branches even before they switched. Over the 8-week post-switch period, this differential trend alone would produce a 2.24-point gap—**more than the entire 1.98-point effect we observed.**

---

## **What This Means**

1. **The parallel trends assumption is violated.** This is the foundation of difference-in-differences analysis. Without it, we cannot attribute the score difference to the procedure.

2. **The observed improvement may have nothing to do with the new procedure.** Branches that chose to switch may have been:
   - More motivated to improve generally
   - Better managed
   - Experiencing other positive changes

3. **The true effect could be zero—or even negative.** After accounting for pre-existing trends, the bias-adjusted estimate is slightly negative.

---

## **Recommendations**

| Action | Priority |
|--------|----------|
| **Do NOT proceed with full rollout** | High |
| **Run a randomized controlled trial** | High |
| **If proceeding, implement rigorous monitoring** | High |
| **Investigate why some branches chose to switch** | Medium |

---

## **Next Steps for Valid Evidence**

1. **Randomized rollout:** Randomly assign remaining branches to switch now vs. later. This eliminates selection bias.

2. **Staggered implementation:** If randomization isn't feasible, use a phased rollout with clear timing to enable stronger causal analysis.

3. **Collect more data:** Extend the observation period to see if patterns stabilize.

4. **Qualitative investigation:** Interview branch managers to understand why some switched and others didn't—this may reveal confounding factors.

---

## **Bottom Line Restated**

The data shows switched branches have higher scores, but **we cannot tell if the procedure caused this or if better-performing branches were simply more likely to switch**. Rolling out based on this evidence risks implementing a change that may not deliver the expected benefits.
