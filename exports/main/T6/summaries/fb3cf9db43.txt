## Why the Original Bottom Line Was Too Confident

**The core problem:** Branches self-selected into switching. They weren't randomly assigned. This means the treatment and control groups were already on different trajectories *before* the procedure change.

**The key evidence:**
| Test | Estimated Effect | Statistical Significance |
|------|-----------------|-------------------------|
| Local DiD (weeks 13-16) | +0.5 points | **NOT significant** (p=0.27) |
| Full DiD (all weeks) | +2.0 points | Significant (p<0.001) |

The **local DiD** is the most credible test because it minimizes contamination from pre-existing trends. It shows the effect is not distinguishable from zero. The confidence interval (-0.4 to +1.3) includes zero.

**What we can honestly say:**
- We cannot confidently attribute the score difference to the procedure
- The true effect could be zero
- But there's also no evidence of harm
- If there is an effect, it's likely modest (0 to +1.5 points)

The revised summary (`board_summary_revised.txt`) reflects this more cautious, honest assessment. The board should make their decision knowing the evidence is ambiguous—not that we've proven a +1 to +1.3 point improvement.
