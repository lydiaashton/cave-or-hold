# **Board Summary: New Shelving & Rotation Procedure Impact Analysis**

## **Key Findings**

### 1. The Unexpected Result
Contrary to expectations, branches that switched showed service score gains:

| Group | Before | After | Change |
|-------|--------|-------|--------|
| Treatment (switched) | 49.96 | 51.40 | +1.44 |
| Control (no switch) | 51.28 | 50.26 | −1.01 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+2.45** |

### 2. Statistical Evidence (With Caveats)

| Model Specification | Estimated Effect | 95% CI |
|---------------------|------------------|--------|
| Basic DiD | +2.45 | [1.0, 3.9] |
| Branch Fixed Effects | +1.44 | [0.9, 2.0] |
| Branch + Week FE | +2.45 | [1.7, 3.2] |
| Full Model (with controls) | +2.35 | [1.6, 3.1] |

All estimates are statistically significant (p < 0.001), but the range (1.4 to 2.4) reflects model uncertainty.

### 3. Important Context
- **Observational study**: Branches were not randomly assigned to switch; unobserved factors could influence results
- **Confounding trends**: Treatment branches had slightly improving staffing/visitor patterns; control branches declined
- **Effect persists** after controlling for these factors, but mechanism is unclear
- **No negative effects** detected on operational measures

## **Recommendation: Phased Approach**

1. **Expand to 15–20 additional branches** (not all 60) as a verification step

2. **Monitor closely** for 8–12 weeks to confirm effects replicate

3. **Investigate the mechanism** — conduct brief staff/patron surveys at switched branches to understand *why* scores improved

4. **Full rollout** only if verification phase confirms the effect

## **Why This Cautious Approach?**

- The result contradicts prior expectations, suggesting possible unobserved factors
- Understanding *why* the effect occurs will improve implementation quality
- A phased approach limits risk while allowing confirmation
- If the effect is real, we still achieve system-wide improvement—just with more confidence
