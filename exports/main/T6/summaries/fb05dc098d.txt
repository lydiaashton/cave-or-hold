# **Board Memorandum (REVISED)**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors
**From:** Data Analysis Team
**Date:** November 2025
**Subject:** Results of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure Pilot

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence is mixed.** While branches using the new procedure do show higher service scores, **we cannot confidently attribute this to the procedure itself**. The treatment branches were already improving relative to control branches *before* the switch occurred, which undermines our ability to isolate the procedure's effect. 

**Recommendation: Proceed with caution.** Consider a more rigorous pilot design before system-wide rollout, or proceed with enhanced monitoring to confirm the effect.

---

## **Key Findings**

### What the Data Shows

| Period | Treatment - Control Gap |
|--------|------------------------|
| Weeks 6-9 (Early Pre) | +0.05 points (essentially equal) |
| Weeks 10-14 (Late Pre) | +1.96 points (treatment pulling ahead) |
| Weeks 15-22 (Post) | +3.18 points (treatment further ahead) |

### The Problem

**The treatment group began outperforming the control group 5 weeks BEFORE the switch.** The gap grew from near-zero (weeks 6-9) to +2 points (weeks 10-14) without any intervention. This pattern suggests:

1. **Selection bias**: Branches chosen to switch may have been on an upward trajectory already
2. **Anticipation effects**: Staff may have changed behavior in expectation of the new procedure
3. **Confounding factors**: Something else may have changed for treatment branches around week 10

### Revised Effect Estimate

When accounting for the pre-existing trend (using weeks 10-14 as baseline):
- **Adjusted treatment effect: +1.22 points** (95% CI: +0.62 to +1.82)
- This is **40% smaller** than the initial 2.07-point estimate
- Still statistically significant, but less certain to be causal

---

## **Why This Matters**

The standard analysis method (difference-in-differences) assumes treatment and control groups would have followed parallel trends absent the intervention. **This assumption is violated in our data.** The initial 2.07-point estimate likely overstates the true effect.

---

## **Recommendations**

### Option A: Proceed with Rollout (Lower Confidence)
If the board wishes to move forward despite the uncertainty:
- Implement in phases (50 branches at a time)
- Track service scores closely for each cohort
- Be prepared to pause if effects don't materialize

### Option B: Run a Cleaner Pilot (Higher Confidence)
- Randomly assign a new set of branches to treatment/control
- Ensure parallel trends hold before switching
- Measure for 8-12 weeks post-switch before deciding on rollout

### Option C: Hybrid Approach
- Proceed with rollout **and** embed a randomized evaluation
- Randomize rollout timing across remaining branches
- Use staggered implementation to strengthen causal inference

## **Conclusion**

The new procedure **may** improve service scores by 1-2 points, but the evidence is not as definitive as initially appeared. The treatment branches were already improving before the switch, which clouds our ability to claim the procedure caused the gain. 

Given the cooperative's confidence in this initiative, a **phased rollout with rigorous monitoring** is the most prudent path forward. This balances the desire to improve service with the need for evidence-based decision-making.
