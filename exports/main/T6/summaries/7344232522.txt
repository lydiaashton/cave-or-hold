# **REVISED MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure  
**Date:** November 2025  
**Prepared by:** Data Analysis Team

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The data do not provide reliable evidence that the new procedure improved service scores.** While a simple comparison suggests a +2.5 point gain, deeper analysis reveals the treatment and control branches had different performance patterns *before* the switch, undermining the comparison. Post-switch results are inconsistent—some weeks show gains, others show losses. **We recommend against a system-wide rollout until more conclusive evidence is available.**

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **1. The Core Problem: Incomparable Groups**

The branches that switched procedures did not follow the same performance trajectory as those that didn't—**even before the switch**. This violates the key assumption needed to attribute any difference to the procedure change.

| Pre-Switch Weeks | Treatment vs. Control Difference |
|------------------|----------------------------------|
| Week 6 | -4.3 points ** |
| Week 10 | -5.7 points *** |
| Week 11 | -5.0 points *** |
| Week 12 | -4.8 points *** |

(** p<0.05, *** p<0.01)

The treatment branches were systematically underperforming in several pre-switch weeks, meaning any "improvement" could simply reflect a return to normal performance rather than a procedure effect.

### **2. Post-Switch Results Are Inconsistent**

| Post-Switch Week | Effect | Significance |
|------------------|--------|--------------|
| Week 15 | +0.1 | Not significant |
| Week 16 | **-4.3** | Significant negative *** |
| Week 17 | **-5.5** | Significant negative *** |
| Week 18 | **+5.4** | Significant positive *** |
| Week 19 | -3.3 | Marginally negative * |
| Weeks 20-22 | Near zero | Not significant |

There is **no consistent pattern** of improvement. Two weeks show significant *declines*, one week shows a gain, and the rest show no clear effect.

### **3. Simple vs. Rigorous Analysis**

| Method | Estimated Effect | Conclusion |
|--------|------------------|------------|
| Simple before-after comparison | +2.5 points | Appears positive |
| Difference-in-Differences (with fixed effects) | +2.4 points, p=0.12 | Not statistically significant |
| Event study (week-by-week) | Average -0.9 points | No consistent effect |

The simpler analyses are misleading because they don't account for pre-existing differences between branches.

---

## **RECOMMENDATIONS**

### **1. Do Not Proceed with System-Wide Rollout**
The evidence does not support expanding the procedure to all branches. The observed gains may be statistical noise or regression to the mean rather than a true procedure effect.

### **2. Extend the Evaluation Period**
Collect 12–20 additional weeks of data to determine whether any pattern emerges over time. Eight weeks post-switch is insufficient for a reliable conclusion.

### **3. Investigate Implementation Variation**
The volatile week-to-week results suggest branches may be implementing the procedure inconsistently. Audit a sample of switched branches to verify adherence.

### **4. Consider Alternative Evaluation Approaches**
- Match treatment and control branches more carefully on pre-switch performance
- Use a staggered rollout design for future procedure changes
- Track additional outcomes (patron satisfaction, circulation, staff time)

### **5. Revisit in 3–4 Months**
Schedule a follow-up review once more data are available. If a consistent positive effect emerges, reconsider expansion.
