# **REVISED MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure  

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**Recommendation: Proceed with caution; evidence is suggestive but not definitive.**

The new procedure is associated with higher service scores, but **we cannot confidently attribute this to the procedure itself.** Branches that switched were already improving relative to other branches *before* the change occurred. The true causal effect is uncertain and likely smaller than initial estimates suggested.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### 1. **Critical Limitation: Pre-Existing Trends**

The difference-in-differences method requires that treatment and control groups follow parallel trends before the intervention. **This assumption is violated in our data:**

| Period | Treatment-Control Gap |
|--------|----------------------|
| Weeks 6-10 (early pre-period) | +0.15 points |
| Weeks 11-14 (late pre-period) | +1.52 points |
| Weeks 15-22 (post-period) | +2.88 points |

The treatment group was **already pulling ahead by 1.4 points** before the switch happened. This suggests branches selected for the new procedure may have been systematically different (better managed, more motivated, or facing different conditions).

### 2. **Revised Effect Estimate**

| Estimate Type | Value | Interpretation |
|---------------|-------|----------------|
| Initial DiD estimate | +2.12 points | Likely overstated |
| Adjusted for pre-trend | +1.36 points | Still uncertain |
| True causal effect | **Unknown** | Cannot be determined from this data |

### 3. **What We Can Say With Confidence**

- Branches using the new procedure **do have higher service scores** than those using the old procedure
- The gap widened after the switch (from ~1.5 to ~2.9 points)
- However, we cannot rule out that this reflects **ongoing selection effects** rather than the procedure itself

### 4. **Event Study Raises Concerns**

Two weeks *before* the switch (weeks 12-13), the treatment group already showed statistically significant improvements relative to baseline. This is inconsistent with a clean causal interpretation.

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

### Recommended Actions

1. **Do NOT proceed with immediate system-wide rollout**
   - The evidence does not support a confident recommendation

2. **Consider a randomized rollout**
   - Randomly assign remaining branches to treatment/control
   - This would allow for cleaner causal inference

3. **If proceeding with rollout, implement rigorous monitoring**
   - Track each branch for 12+ weeks post-implementation
   - Compare against matched control branches
   - Be prepared to halt if benefits don't materialize

4. **Investigate why switched branches were already improving**
   - Were they selected based on prior performance?
   - Did they receive additional support or training?
   - Understanding this could inform rollout strategy

5. **Conduct qualitative follow-up**
   - Interview branch managers at switched vs. non-switched locations
   - Identify confounding factors that may explain the pre-trends

---

## **CONCLUSION**

The initial analysis was overly optimistic. While the new procedure is associated with better outcomes, **we cannot confidently claim it causes those improvements.** The board should either:

- **Option A:** Delay rollout and design a randomized evaluation
- **Option B:** Proceed with rollout but treat it as a pilot with careful monitoring and willingness to reverse course

We apologize for the initial overconfidence. Rigorous causal inference requires careful attention to assumptions that our data do not fully satisfy.
