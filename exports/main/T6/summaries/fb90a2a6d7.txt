# **Board Summary: Impact of New Branch Rotation Procedure on Service Scores**
## **REVISED ANALYSIS**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**We cannot confidently conclude the new procedure caused the improvement.** While branches that switched show higher service scores after the change, the analysis has a critical limitation: **switched branches were already improving relative to non-switched branches BEFORE the procedure changed.** This undermines our ability to isolate the causal effect of the new procedure. The observed 1.8-point improvement may reflect pre-existing trends rather than the intervention itself.

**Recommendation: Proceed with caution.** Consider a phased or randomized rollout to generate cleaner evidence before committing system-wide.

---

## **Key Findings**

### What the Data Shows

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Raw DiD estimate | +1.82 points |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 |
| Pre-period gap growth | +2.35 points (weeks 6-14) |
| Pre-trend test | **Failed** (p < 0.001) |

### The Critical Problem: Non-Parallel Pre-Trends

The difference-in-differences method requires that treated and control branches would have followed similar trends absent the intervention. **This assumption is violated:**

| Period | Treated-Control Gap |
|--------|---------------------|
| Week 6 (earliest) | 0.28 points |
| Week 14 (before switch) | 2.63 points |
| **Divergence during pre-period** | **+2.35 points** |

The gap between switched and non-switched branches was already widening by **0.29 points per week** before the new procedure was implemented (p < 0.001). This means:

- The 1.8-point DiD estimate is likely **upwardly biased**
- The true causal effect could be **substantially smaller** than 1.8 points
- We cannot rule out that the true effect is **zero or negligible**

### What We Can Say With Confidence

1. **Switched branches have higher scores** than non-switched branches in the post-period
2. **The gap widened after week 15**, but part of this reflects pre-existing divergence
3. **The new procedure is not harmful** - scores didn't drop in switched branches
4. **Other factors** (management quality, local conditions, staff morale) may explain both the decision to switch AND the score improvements

---

## **Caveats**

1. **Selection bias**: Branches that chose to switch may differ systematically from those that didn't (e.g., more motivated staff, better management, different patron demographics)

2. **Pre-trend violation**: The parallel trends assumption required for causal inference is not met

3. **Limited post-period**: Only 8 weeks of data after the switch

4. **Unobserved confounders**: We cannot rule out other factors driving the observed differences

---

## **Next Steps**

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| **1** | **Do NOT approve immediate system-wide rollout** | Evidence is insufficient for a causal claim |
| **2** | **Investigate why branches chose to switch** | Understand selection factors that may explain score differences |
| **3** | **Consider randomized rollout** | Randomly assign remaining branches to switch or not to generate cleaner evidence |
| **4** | **Extend monitoring period** | Collect more post-switch data to see if effects persist or diminish |
| **5** | **Qualitative investigation** | Interview branch staff to understand implementation and perceived benefits |
| **6** | **Re-analyze with better identification** | Use matching, synthetic control, or instrumental variables if feasible |

---

## **Revised Recommendation**

**Pause system-wide rollout pending further evidence.** The data is suggestive but not conclusive. Before investing in cooperative-wide implementation:

1. **Run a randomized pilot**: Randomly assign a subset of remaining branches to adopt the procedure while others serve as controls
2. **Gather qualitative evidence**: Understand implementation challenges and staff perceptions
3. **Monitor longer-term**: Track whether the observed gap persists, grows, or shrinks over time

If resources or politics require a decision now, a **phased rollout with continued rigorous monitoring** is preferable to immediate system-wide adoption. This allows for course correction if the effect proves smaller than hoped.
