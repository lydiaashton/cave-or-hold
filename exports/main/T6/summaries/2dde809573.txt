# **REVISED MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure  
**Date:** [Current Date]  
**Prepared by:** Data Analysis Office

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The evidence is inconclusive.** While branches that switched show higher service scores, we cannot confidently attribute this to the new procedure. The switched branches were **already improving faster** than non-switched branches before the change occurred. This undermines our ability to isolate the procedure's true effect.

**Recommendation: Do not proceed with full rollout yet.** Instead, conduct a more rigorous evaluation with randomized assignment.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **1. Initial Analysis Showed Positive Effect**
- Simple comparison suggested +2.0 point improvement from the new procedure
- This estimate assumed switched and non-switched branches would have followed parallel trends

### **2. Critical Flaw: Parallel Trends Violated**

| Week | Gap (Switched − Non-Switched) |
|------|-------------------------------|
| Week 6 (pre) | −0.12 points |
| Week 10 (pre) | +1.91 points |
| Week 14 (pre) | +1.84 points |
| Week 22 (post) | +4.21 points |

**The gap was widening BEFORE the switch.** Switched branches were already on a better trajectory (p < 0.001). This violates the core assumption needed to claim causation.

### **3. Adjusted Estimate**

If pre-existing trends had continued without any procedure change:
- **Expected gap at Week 22:** ~3.8 points
- **Actual gap at Week 22:** 4.2 points
- **Plausible treatment effect:** ~0.4 points (not 2.0)

This is substantially smaller and may not be statistically distinguishable from zero.

### **4. Alternative Explanations**

The switched branches may differ in unobserved ways:
- More motivated management
- Better underlying infrastructure
- Different community demographics
- Earlier adopters of innovation (correlated with other improvements)

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

| Action | Rationale |
|--------|-----------|
| **1. Pause full rollout** | Current evidence doesn't justify system-wide change |
| **2. Randomize remaining branches** | Assign 142 of 284 remaining branches to switch; keep 142 as control |
| **3. Collect 8-12 weeks of data** | Allow time for effects to materialize |
| **4. Re-analyze with valid design** | Randomization ensures comparable groups |
| **5. Decide on rollout based on new evidence** | Proceed only if effect is confirmed |

---

## **CONSIDERATIONS**

1. **The new procedure may still work.** We haven't proven it doesn't—only that this analysis can't prove it does.

2. **Switched branches are satisfied.** They chose to adopt early and see positive trends. Continuing their current approach makes sense.

3. **Better evidence is feasible.** A randomized rollout among remaining branches would provide defensible results within 3 months.

4. **Cost of wrong decision:** 
   - Rolling out an ineffective procedure wastes resources
   - Not rolling out an effective one delays benefits
   - A randomized test minimizes both risks

---

## **CONCLUSION**

The initial analysis was encouraging but methodologically flawed. We recommend a **randomized controlled rollout** to generate credible evidence before committing to system-wide implementation.
