# **REVISED MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Office  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Impact of New Branch Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure — **CAUTION ADVISED**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**We cannot confidently conclude the new procedure caused the improvement.** While service scores did rise in switched branches, those branches were already improving relative to control branches *before* the switch. The observed post-switch gain is consistent with continuation of this pre-existing trend rather than a causal effect of the procedure. **Do not approve system-wide rollout without further evidence.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. The Core Problem: Pre-Existing Trends Violate Our Analysis Assumptions

The standard difference-in-differences method assumes treatment and control groups would have followed parallel trends absent the intervention. **This assumption is violated:**

| Period | Treatment-Control Gap |
|--------|----------------------|
| Week 6 | −0.12 |
| Week 9 | +0.04 |
| Week 12 | +1.15 |
| Week 14 (last pre-switch) | +2.70 |

The gap was already widening by **~0.3 points per week** before the switch (p < 0.001). This suggests branches that chose to switch were systematically different—perhaps more motivated, better managed, or better resourced.

### 2. The "Effect" Disappears When Accounting for Pre-Trends

- **Pre-trend projection:** If the pre-switch trend continued for 8 weeks, we'd expect the gap to grow by **+2.52 points**
- **Actual observed DiD:** **+2.09 points**
- **Unexplained portion:** **−0.43 points**

In other words, the post-switch improvement is *smaller* than what the pre-trend alone would predict. There is no statistical evidence the procedure itself added value.

### 3. Timing Pattern Raises Further Doubts

If the procedure caused the improvement, we'd expect the gap to jump immediately after week 14. Instead:

| Weeks | Average Gap |
|-------|-------------|
| 15–17 (immediate post) | +2.26 |
| 18–22 (later post) | +3.46 |

The gap actually *declined* in the first 3 weeks post-switch compared to week 14 (+2.70), then rose later. This pattern is inconsistent with a clean treatment effect.

### 4. What We Can Say

- Branches that adopted the new procedure **did** see service score improvements
- However, we cannot distinguish whether this was caused by the procedure or by underlying branch characteristics that led them to adopt it in the first place
- The 284 control branches provide an imperfect counterfactual because they differ systematically from the 425 switched branches

---

## **Recommendation**

**Do NOT proceed with system-wide rollout based on this evidence alone.** The data is suggestive but not conclusive.

---

## **Next Steps**

| Action | Rationale |
|--------|-----------|
| **Run a randomized trial** | Randomly assign a subset of remaining branches to switch vs. stay; this would restore causal identification |
| **Match branches on pre-trends** | Use statistical matching to create better comparison groups before estimating effects |
| **Gather qualitative data** | Interview switched branches about implementation challenges and perceived benefits |
| **Monitor longer-term** | Extend observation period to see if patterns stabilize or diverge further |
| **Consider phased rollout with evaluation** | If proceeding, treat it as a learning opportunity with rigorous tracking |

---

## **Appendix: Analysis Details**

- **Data:** 12,053 weekly observations from 709 branches (weeks 6–22)
- **Method:** Difference-in-differences with cluster-robust standard errors; pre-trend diagnostics
- **Key limitation:** Non-random selection into treatment; parallel trends assumption violated
- **Service score scale:** Average branch scores ~50; higher is better
