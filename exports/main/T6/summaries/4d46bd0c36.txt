# **Library Branch Rotation & Shelving Procedure: Impact Analysis**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure produced a small but statistically significant improvement in service scores—unexpectedly.** While we anticipated no measurable change, branches that adopted the new procedure achieved service scores **2.5 points higher (approximately 5%)** than comparable branches keeping the old procedure.

**Recommendation: The evidence supports proceeding with rollout, but with continued monitoring to confirm the effect persists.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure; **60 branches** kept the old procedure
- Switch occurred in **week 15**

### Results Summary

| Group | Pre-Switch Average | Post-Switch Average | Change |
|-------|-------------------|---------------------|--------|
| Treatment (New Procedure) | 50.21 | 52.55 | +2.34 |
| Control (Old Procedure) | 49.82 | 49.64 | -0.18 |

**Difference-in-Differences Estimate: +2.53 points**

### Statistical Confidence
- **95% Confidence Interval:** 1.22 to 3.83 points
- **p-value:** < 0.001
- **Pre-trend test:** No significant difference in trends before the switch (p = 0.39) ✓
- **Effect size:** 0.25 standard deviations (small-to-medium)

### Important Caveats
1. **Unexpected finding:** This procedure was not designed to affect service scores
2. **Limited follow-up:** Only 8 weeks of post-switch data; long-term effects unknown
3. **Observational design:** Branches were not randomly assigned to treatment
4. **Modest magnitude:** 2.5 points on a ~50-point scale may or may not be operationally meaningful

---

## **Next Steps**

### Recommended Actions
1. **Proceed with rollout** — the evidence is sufficiently strong to justify expansion
2. **Implement in phases** — allows for course correction if issues emerge
3. **Continue monitoring** — track service scores for 6–12 months post-rollout to confirm persistence

### Questions for the Board
- Is a 5% improvement in service scores meaningful for our strategic goals?
- Are there any operational costs or staff burden not captured in this analysis?
- Should we set a target date to re-evaluate whether the effect has persisted?
