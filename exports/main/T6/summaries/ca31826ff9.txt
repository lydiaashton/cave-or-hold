# **REVISED BOARD MEMORANDUM**

---

**TO:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** December 2025  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure  

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**Do NOT approve full rollout based on current evidence.** 

While branches with the new procedure showed higher service scores, **we cannot confidently attribute this improvement to the procedure itself**. Branches that adopted the new procedure were already improving faster than other branches *before* the switch occurred. This suggests the observed gains may reflect pre-existing differences rather than the procedure's effect.

**Recommendation:** Conduct a randomized trial or phased evaluation before system-wide commitment.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### What the Data Shows

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Branches with **new procedure** | 49.7 | 54.8 | **+5.0** |
| Branches with **old procedure** | 49.2 | 52.4 | +3.2 |
| **Raw difference** | | | **+1.8** |

### The Critical Problem: Non-Parallel Pre-Trends

**Difference-in-differences analysis requires that treatment and control groups follow similar trends before the intervention.** This assumption is **violated** in our data:

| Week | Gap (Treatment − Control) |
|------|---------------------------|
| Week 6 | −0.63 points |
| Week 10 | +0.25 points |
| Week 14 | **+2.51 points** |

**The treatment group was already gaining 3.1 points on the control group BEFORE the switch.** This divergence is statistically significant (p < 0.001).

### What This Means

- Branches selected for the new procedure may have had **better management, more motivated staff, or other advantages**
- The 1.8-point "effect" could be entirely explained by these pre-existing differences
- **We cannot isolate the causal impact of the procedure** from this observational data

---

## **IMPLICATIONS**

| Scenario | True Procedure Effect | Risk of Full Rollout |
|----------|----------------------|---------------------|
| Optimistic | ~1.5–2.0 points | Low |
| Realistic | ~0.5–1.0 points | Moderate |
| Pessimistic | ~0 points | High (costs without benefits) |

**We simply do not know which scenario is correct.**

---

## **RECOMMENDED NEXT STEPS**

| Action | Rationale |
|--------|-----------|
| **1. Pause full rollout** | Avoid committing resources to an unproven intervention |
| **2. Randomize remaining branches** | Assign 50% of remaining 284 branches to new procedure, 50% to control. This enables causal inference. |
| **3. Extend evaluation period** | Collect 12+ weeks of post-switch data to assess durability |
| **4. Investigate selection criteria** | Understand why certain branches were chosen for the new procedure; this may reveal confounding factors |
| **5. Consider qualitative research** | Interview staff at high-performing branches to identify what actually drives improvement |

---

## **CONCLUSION**

The new procedure is **associated with** higher service scores, but we cannot conclude it **causes** higher service scores. The branches that adopted it were already on a stronger trajectory before implementation.

**A randomized evaluation of the remaining branches would provide definitive evidence** and allow the Board to make an informed decision about system-wide rollout.
