# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative

**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence suggests the new procedure improved service scores, but we cannot be fully certain.** Branches that switched showed a +2.1 point improvement relative to control branches. However, treatment and control branches were on different trajectories even before the switch, which limits our ability to claim causality. **We recommend a cautious, phased rollout with close monitoring** rather than immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Positive Association, But Causality Is Uncertain

| Group | Before (Weeks 6-14) | After (Weeks 15-22) | Change |
|-------|--------------------|--------------------|--------|
| Treatment (425 branches) | 52.5 | 53.6 | +1.0 |
| Control (284 branches) | 50.6 | 49.5 | -1.1 |
| **Difference** | | | **+2.1** |

**Important limitation:** Treatment branches were on a different trajectory than control branches even before the switch. This means part of the observed "effect" may reflect pre-existing trends rather than the procedure itself.

### 2. Event Study Shows a Suggestive Pattern

- **Weeks 6-13 (pre-switch):** Treatment branches underperformed by 0.6 to 2.5 points, but were converging toward control
- **Weeks 15-17:** No clear effect (adjustment period)
- **Weeks 18-22:** Treatment branches outperformed by 1.1 to 1.8 points (statistically significant)

The post-switch effects are larger than pre-switch trends would predict, which is **consistent with** a treatment benefit—but does not prove it.

### 3. No Evidence of Harm

No switching branches experienced sustained score declines after implementation. At minimum, the new procedure does not appear to damage service quality.

---

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with a phased rollout, not immediate system-wide implementation:**

1. **Expand to 50-100 additional branches** over the next quarter
2. **Monitor service scores closely** at new adopters
3. **If effects replicate**, proceed with full rollout
4. **If effects do not replicate**, pause and investigate further

This approach balances the suggestive evidence of benefit against the analytical uncertainty.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Select diverse branches** for phase 2 (varying sizes, locations, performance levels)

2. **Establish clear success metrics** before expansion (e.g., minimum +1.0 point improvement)

3. **Document implementation details** to identify what drives success

4. **Reconvene in 12 weeks** to review phase 2 results before deciding on full rollout

---

## **Technical Note**

The difference-in-differences design requires "parallel trends"—treatment and control groups must be on similar trajectories before the intervention. Our pre-trends test rejected this assumption (p < 0.001). While the post-period pattern is encouraging, we cannot rule out that pre-existing trends explain part of the observed effect. A phased rollout with monitoring is the appropriate response to this uncertainty.
