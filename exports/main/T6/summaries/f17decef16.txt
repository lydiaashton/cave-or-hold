# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

---

## **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Prepared by:** Data Analysis Team

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure is associated with improved service scores, but important caveats apply.** The evidence supports expanding the procedure, though the board should consider implementation costs and monitor results carefully.

Branches using the new procedure achieved **2.16 points higher** service scores than comparable branches using the old procedure. However, approximately **1 in 5 branches saw no improvement**, and we cannot rule out that early-adopting branches were systematically different from later adopters.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **What the Data Shows**

| Metric | Value | Interpretation |
|--------|-------|----------------|
| **Estimated Effect** | +2.16 points | Statistically significant (p < 0.001) |
| **Confidence Interval** | [1.60, 2.71] | 95% confident true effect is in this range |
| **Relative Improvement** | 4.4% | Small-to-moderate effect (0.23 SD) |
| **Branches Benefiting** | 80.5% | But 19.5% saw no improvement or decline |

### **Important Limitations**

| Concern | Details | Implication |
|---------|---------|-------------|
| **Selection bias** | Treatment branches scored 0.6 points higher at baseline | Early adopters may have been more motivated or better-resourced |
| **Pre-trend divergence** | Treatment branches were gaining slightly before Week 15 | Some of the effect may reflect existing momentum |
| **Short follow-up** | Only 8 weeks of post-switch data | Long-term effects unknown; could fade or grow |
| **No cost data** | Implementation costs not analyzed | Cannot assess cost-effectiveness |
| **Heterogeneous effects** | Effects ranged from -8.15 to +14.62 across branches | One size may not fit all |

### **What We Can and Cannot Conclude**

| We CAN conclude | We CANNOT conclude |
|-----------------|-------------------|
| ✓ A positive association exists between the new procedure and scores | ✗ The procedure *caused* the full 2.16-point gain |
| ✓ Most branches (80%) improved after switching | ✗ All branches will benefit equally |
| ✓ The effect is unlikely to be random chance | ✗ The effect will persist long-term |
| ✓ The analysis methods are sound | ✗ The effect justifies any implementation cost |

---

## **RECOMMENDATIONS**

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| **1** | **Proceed with cautious rollout** | Evidence is positive but not definitive |
| **2** | **Phase implementation** over 3–6 months | Allows monitoring and adjustment |
| **3** | **Track costs** alongside scores | Enable cost-benefit analysis |
| **4** | **Identify support needs** for struggling branches | 20% may need additional help |
| **5** | **Re-evaluate after 6 months** | Confirm effects persist before full commitment |

---

## **DECISION FRAMEWORK FOR THE BOARD**

**Approve rollout if:**
- Implementation costs are low or moderate
- The cooperative can monitor and support branches during transition
- A 2-point average improvement is considered meaningful for patron service

**Delay or modify rollout if:**
- Implementation requires significant investment
- Staff capacity is already constrained
- The board prefers stronger causal evidence before system-wide change
