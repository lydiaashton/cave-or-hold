# **Board Summary: Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence is mixed.** Branches using the new procedure showed higher service score improvements, but we cannot confidently attribute this to the procedure itself. The treatment branches were already improving faster than control branches *before* the switch, which undermines our ability to isolate the procedure's effect. 

**Recommendation:** Proceed with caution. Consider a more controlled rollout with additional data collection before committing to system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. What the Data Shows**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| **New Procedure** (425 branches) | 51.05 | 54.75 | +3.70 |
| **Old Procedure** (284 branches) | 50.17 | 51.92 | +1.75 |
| **Apparent Effect** | — | — | **+1.95** |

On the surface, this suggests the new procedure added ~2 points to service scores.

### **2. Critical Limitation: Pre-Existing Differences**

⚠️ **The treatment and control groups were already on different trajectories before the switch.**

| Week | Treatment Score | Control Score | Gap |
|------|-----------------|---------------|-----|
| Week 6 (start) | 49.00 | 49.42 | -0.42 |
| Week 14 (pre-switch) | 53.52 | 51.42 | +2.09 |

**The treatment group was already pulling ahead by 2+ points before the new procedure began.** This pattern violates the key assumption needed to claim the procedure *caused* the improvement.

### **3. What This Means**

| Possibility | Explanation |
|-------------|-------------|
| **Optimistic** | The new procedure did help, and the effect is real (~2 points) |
| **Concerning** | Treatment branches were already improving faster for other reasons (better management, more resources, etc.) |
| **Most Likely** | The true effect is **smaller than 1.95 points**, possibly much smaller |

### **4. Encouraging Signs**

Despite the limitation, some patterns are noteworthy:
- Post-switch effects were **larger and more consistent** than pre-switch differences
- 7 of 8 post-switch weeks showed statistically significant gains
- No negative effects were observed

---

## **Recommendation**

**Do not approve full system-wide rollout yet.** Instead:

1. **Extend the evaluation period** — Collect 8-12 more weeks of data to see if the effect stabilizes
2. **Randomize the remaining rollout** — Assign the remaining 284 branches to treatment/control randomly to get cleaner causal evidence
3. **Investigate why treatment branches were already improving** — Understanding this could inform implementation strategy

---

## **Next Steps**

| Action | Timeline | Rationale |
|--------|----------|-----------|
| **1. Pause full rollout decision** | Immediate | Avoid committing to a procedure with uncertain benefits |
| **2. Extend data collection** | 8-12 weeks | More post-switch data strengthens inference |
| **3. Randomize remaining branches** | Next quarter | Clean causal identification for final decision |
| **4. Qualitative follow-up** | Ongoing | Interview branch managers to understand non-procedure factors |

---

## **Bottom Line for Decision-Makers**

| If you... | Then... |
|-----------|---------|
| **Need high confidence** before system-wide change | **Wait** for better evidence |
| **Are comfortable with some uncertainty** and the procedure has low implementation costs | **Proceed cautiously** with phased rollout |
| **Want to maximize learning** | **Randomize** the remaining rollout |
