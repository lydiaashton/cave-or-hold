# **Library Cooperative Branch Procedure Change: Impact Analysis**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure likely improved service scores, but the effect is smaller and less certain than initial analysis suggests.** After accounting for pre-existing trends, the estimated effect is approximately **1.3–1.5 points** rather than 2.1 points. The evidence supports a **cautious rollout** with close monitoring, rather than an immediate system-wide mandate.

**Recommendation: Expand to additional branches as a pilot, with rigorous tracking, before committing to full rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **709 branches** total: 425 adopted the new procedure, 284 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15

### **Critical Limitation: Pre-Existing Trends**

The switched branches were already outperforming control branches *before* the intervention, and that gap was widening:

| Week | Gap (Switched − Control) |
|------|-------------------------|
| 6    | 1.29 |
| 10   | 1.53 |
| 14   | 3.23 |

**Pre-switch trend: +0.21 points/week (p=0.004)**

This violates the key assumption needed for clean causal inference. Some of the observed "effect" may simply be continuation of this pre-existing trend.

### **Adjusted Results**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Raw DiD estimate | +2.13 points |
| Expected from pre-trend alone | ~0.8 points |
| **Adjusted effect estimate** | **~1.3–1.5 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | 0.7 to 2.0 points (approximate) |

### **Supporting Evidence**

1. **Post-switch acceleration**: After week 15, the gap grew faster than the pre-trend would predict (especially weeks 20–22).
2. **Consistent direction**: Every post-switch week shows a larger gap than the pre-switch average.
3. **Controls don't explain it**: The effect persists after adjusting for staffing and visitor volume.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Staged expansion**: Roll out to 50–100 additional branches (not all 284 at once) to gather more evidence.

2. **Randomize where possible**: If feasible, randomly assign the next cohort to strengthen causal inference.

3. **Extend observation period**: Monitor for 12+ weeks post-implementation to distinguish temporary effects from sustained improvement.

4. **Track implementation fidelity**: Document how thoroughly each branch adopts the new procedure—effects may vary by execution quality.

5. **Re-evaluate in 3 months**: Use expanded data to produce a more definitive estimate before deciding on full system-wide rollout.

---

*Analysis based on weekly service score data from 709 branches, weeks 6–22. The violated parallel trends assumption introduces uncertainty; the adjusted effect estimate accounts for pre-existing divergence between groups.*
