# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD OF DIRECTORS**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

**Date:** November 15, 2026

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure produced a modest but statistically reliable improvement in service scores.** Branches using the new procedure averaged **1.77 points higher** (3.6% improvement) than those keeping the old procedure. This contradicts our initial expectation of no effect. However, the improvement is modest, and **38% of branches saw no benefit or declined**.

**Recommendation:** The board should weigh the **modest benefit** against implementation costs before approving system-wide rollout. If costs are low, proceed. If costs are substantial, consider targeted rollout or further piloting.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### 1. Statistically Significant but Modest Effect

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Net effect (DiD estimate) | **+1.77 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | **+0.58 to +2.96 points** |
| P-value | **0.003** |
| Effect size | **0.17 standard deviations** (small-to-moderate) |

### 2. Mixed Results Across Branches

| Group | Mean Change | Median Change | % Improved |
|-------|-------------|---------------|------------|
| Treatment (New Procedure) | +1.44 | +1.58 | 62% |
| Control (Old Procedure) | -0.34 | -0.04 | 50% |

**Notable:** Over one-third of treatment branches (34 of 90) did not benefit from the new procedure.

### 3. Not Driven by Staffing or Traffic

The improvement is not explained by changes in staffing levels (p=0.51) or visitor volume (p=0.75), suggesting the effect reflects genuine operational changes from the procedure itself.

### 4. Pre-Switch Trends Were Parallel

Treatment and control branches showed similar service score trends before the switch (p=0.25), supporting the validity of the comparison.
