# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2025  
**Subject:** Assessment of New Shelving Procedure – Proceed with Caution

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence is suggestive but not conclusive.** While branches that adopted the new procedure showed a **2.1-point average improvement** in service scores, **serious analytical limitations prevent a confident recommendation for system-wide rollout.** The branches that chose to switch differed systematically from those that did not, and the statistical evidence of improvement is weaker than initially apparent. We recommend **expanding the pilot with proper randomization** before committing to full rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### What the Numbers Show

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 49.47 | 52.31 | +2.84 |
| Non-switched branches | 48.47 | 49.24 | +0.77 |
| **Raw Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+2.06** |

### Critical Limitations

**1. Parallel Trends Assumption Violated**

The difference-in-differences method requires treatment and control groups to follow similar trends before the intervention. **This assumption does not hold:**

| Pre-Switch Week | Treatment vs. Control Difference | Statistically Significant? |
|-----------------|----------------------------------|---------------------------|
| Week 6 | -2.15 points | **Yes** |
| Week 7 | -2.36 points | **Yes** |
| Week 8 | -1.64 points | **Yes** |
| Week 9 | -1.73 points | **Yes** |
| Week 10 | -1.83 points | **Yes** |
| Week 11 | -1.65 points | **Yes** |
| Week 12 | -1.01 points | No |
| Week 13 | -1.13 points | **Yes** |

**7 of 8 pre-switch weeks** show significant differences between groups. This suggests branches that chose to switch were systematically different from those that did not.

**2. Weak Post-Switch Evidence**

| Post-Switch Week | Estimated Effect | Statistically Significant? |
|-----------------|------------------|---------------------------|
| Week 16 | +0.27 points | No |
| Week 17 | +0.19 points | No |
| Week 18 | +0.41 points | No |
| Week 19 | +0.72 points | No |
| Week 20 | +0.79 points | No |
| Week 21 | +1.08 points | **Yes** |
| Week 22 | +0.86 points | No |

Only **1 of 7 post-switch weeks** shows a statistically significant improvement. The positive trend is encouraging but not robust.

**3. Selection Bias**

Branches **self-selected** into the new procedure. Those that opted in may have been:
- More motivated to improve
- Better managed overall
- Experiencing different operational challenges

These unobserved factors could explain the observed improvement rather than the procedure itself.

**4. Limited Explanatory Power**

The statistical model explains only **~2% of the variation** in service scores, indicating many other factors influence performance.

---

## **Recommendation**

**Do not proceed with immediate system-wide rollout.** Instead:

### Option A: Randomized Expansion (Preferred)
- Randomly assign 100–150 additional branches to adopt the procedure
- Keep a comparable control group
- Measure outcomes over 8–12 weeks
- This would provide causal evidence suitable for a rollout decision

### Option B: Staged Rollout with Monitoring
- Roll out to 50–75 branches per month
- Track service scores closely at each cohort
- Pause if improvements do not materialize
- Accept higher risk of ineffective implementation

### Option C: Further Analysis
- Investigate what distinguishes early-adopter branches
- Identify which branch characteristics predict success
- Target rollout to branches most likely to benefit

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Discuss risk tolerance** – How confident must we be before system-wide implementation?
2. **Consider operational constraints** – Is there pressure to decide quickly?
3. **Evaluate costs** – What are the costs of rollout vs. costs of gathering more evidence?
4. **Decide on path forward** – Randomized expansion, staged rollout, or additional analysis

---

**Bottom line restated:** The +2.1 point estimate is encouraging, but the methodological limitations mean we cannot confidently attribute this improvement to the new procedure. A more rigorous evaluation before full rollout would reduce the risk of implementing an ineffective change across all 709 branches.
