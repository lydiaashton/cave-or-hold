# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Office  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Analysis of New Branch Procedure – Recommendation Deferred

---

## **Bottom Line**

**We cannot confidently conclude the new procedure improved service scores.** While switched branches showed higher scores after the change, they were already improving faster than control branches *before* the switch. After accounting for this pre-existing trend, the estimated effect is small (~0.4 points) and **not statistically significant**. **We recommend against system-wide rollout until further evidence is gathered.**

---

## **What We Found**

### Initial (Uncorrected) Analysis
| | Switched Branches | Control Branches |
|---|---|---|
| Average score **before** switch | 51.2 | 49.1 |
| Average score **after** switch | 55.1 | 50.8 |
| **Apparent improvement** | **+3.9** | **+1.8** |

This suggested a **2.1-point effect** – but this analysis was flawed.

### The Problem: Pre-Existing Trends

The gap between switched and control branches was **already widening before the switch**:

| Week | Gap (Switched − Control) |
|------|-------------------------|
| 6 | 1.3 |
| 10 | 1.5 |
| 14 | 3.2 |
| 22 | 6.0 |

**The gap grew by 0.21 points per week even before the new procedure.** This violates the key assumption needed to attribute the improvement to the procedure change.

### Corrected Estimate

| Measure | Value |
|---------|-------|
| Raw DiD estimate | 2.13 points |
| Pre-trend adjustment | −1.67 points |
| **Adjusted effect** | **~0.46 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | −0.33 to +1.24 |
| **Statistically significant?** | **No** |

---

## **Interpretation**

Three possibilities are consistent with the data:

1. **The procedure has a small positive effect** (~0.5 points) that we cannot precisely estimate
2. **The procedure has no effect** – switched branches were simply on a better trajectory
3. **Branch selection bias** – branches expected to improve were chosen to switch first

We cannot distinguish among these with the current data.

---

## **Recommendation**

**Do not proceed with system-wide rollout at this time.** Instead:

1. **Extend the evaluation period** – collect 8–12 more weeks of data to see if the gap continues widening or stabilizes

2. **Consider a randomized rollout** – randomly assign remaining branches to switch in waves, enabling cleaner causal inference

3. **Investigate selection criteria** – understand why certain branches were chosen to switch first; this may reveal confounding factors

4. **Monitor early adopters** – if the 6.0-point gap at week 22 continues growing, confidence in the procedure will increase

---

## **Bottom Line for Decision-Makers**

The data are **inconclusive**. The apparent 2-point improvement is largely explained by pre-existing differences between branches. Rolling out system-wide based on current evidence risks investing in a procedure that may not deliver meaningful benefits.

---

*Analysis based on 12,053 weekly observations from 709 branches (weeks 6–22, 2026). Pre-trend violation identified; causal claims not supported.*
