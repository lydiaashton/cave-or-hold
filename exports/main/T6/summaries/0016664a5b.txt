# **REVISED MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure  
**Date:** November 2025  
**Prepared by:** Data Analysis Team

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**Recommendation: Pause rollout pending further analysis.**

While the initial analysis suggested a 1.8-point improvement, **the key assumption underlying this estimate is violated**. Branches that switched were already on a different trajectory before the procedure change. The immediate effect at the switch point is negligible (0.04 points). We cannot confidently attribute the observed differences to the new procedure.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### What We Found

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Raw DiD estimate | +1.82 points |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 |
| **Parallel trends test** | **FAILED (p < 0.001)** |
| Immediate effect (weeks 13-14 → 15-16) | **+0.04 points** |
| Pre-switch gap trend | Already widening by 0.29 pts/week |

### The Critical Issue

**The parallel trends assumption is violated.** This is the foundation of difference-in-differences analysis. Here's what the data shows:

| Period | Average Gap (Switched − Non-Switched) |
|--------|--------------------------------------|
| Week 6 | 0.28 points |
| Week 10 | 1.66 points |
| Week 14 | 2.63 points |
| Week 15 | 2.60 points |
| Week 16 | 2.79 points |

**The gap was already widening before the switch.** From week 6 to week 14, the gap grew from 0.28 to 2.63 points—**without any intervention**. The switch occurred at week 15, and the gap barely changed in the immediate aftermath.

### What This Means

The 1.8-point "effect" likely reflects **pre-existing differences** between branches that chose to switch and those that didn't, rather than the procedure itself. Branches that volunteered (or were selected) to switch may have been:
- Better managed overall
- More motivated to improve
- Already implementing other improvements

---

## **RECOMMENDATION**

| Action | Rationale |
|--------|-----------|
| **Do not proceed with system-wide rollout yet** | Evidence is inconclusive; may waste resources on ineffective change |
| **Conduct a randomized trial** | Randomly assign remaining branches to switch or not; this ensures valid comparison |
| **Gather qualitative feedback** | Interview staff at switched branches about actual operational changes |
| **Extend monitoring period** | Collect more post-switch data to see if effects persist or grow |
| **Consider branch heterogeneity** | The procedure may help some branch types but not others |

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

1. **Design a randomized controlled trial** for the remaining branches (if feasible)
2. **Interview branch managers** to understand what else changed during this period
3. **Analyze subgroups** (e.g., by branch size, urban/rural, staffing levels)
4. **Reconvene in 4-6 weeks** with additional evidence before making a rollout decision

---

**Bottom line:** The data does not provide reliable evidence that the new procedure caused the improvement. Proceeding with a full rollout based on this analysis would be premature.
