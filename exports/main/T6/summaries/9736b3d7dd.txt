## **Critical Findings**

### 1. The "Effect" Is Not What It Appears

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Branches that **switched** | 50.54 | 50.37 | **−0.17 (no change)** |
| Branches that **did not switch** | 49.85 | 47.79 | **−2.06 (declined)** |

**Key Insight:** Branches using the new procedure did **not improve**. They simply **did not decline** like the control group did. The apparent "benefit" comes entirely from control group deterioration, not treatment group gains.

### 2. Statistical Assumptions Are Violated

The difference-in-differences method requires that treatment and control groups follow **parallel trends** before the intervention. Our analysis shows:

- **4 out of 8 pre-switch weeks** show statistically significant differences between groups
- Treatment branches had **consistently higher scores** even before switching (+0.69 points on average)
- This violates the parallel trends assumption, **undermining causal inference**

### 3. Possible Selection Bias

Treatment branches had higher baseline service scores before the switch, suggesting:
- Better-performing branches may have been **more likely to volunteer** for the new procedure
- The observed difference may reflect **pre-existing branch quality**, not the procedure itself

### 4. Unexplained Control Group Decline

The 2-point drop in control branches requires investigation:
- Was there a system-wide factor (seasonal, staffing, budget cuts)?
- Did something negative happen to non-switching branches?
- Without understanding this, we cannot isolate the procedure's true effect
