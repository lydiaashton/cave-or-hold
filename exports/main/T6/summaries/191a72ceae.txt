# **MEMORANDUM**

## **Bottom Line**

**We cannot determine whether the new procedure improved service scores.** The branches selected for the pilot were already outperforming and improving faster than other branches before the change. After accounting for this pre-existing advantage, the data show **no clear evidence of benefit** from the procedure itself.

**Recommendation:** Do not proceed with system-wide rollout until stronger evidence is available. Consider a randomized trial or extend the pilot with more rigorous evaluation.

---

## **What the Data Show**

### **Switched Branches Are Outperforming—But Were Already Ahead**

| Period | Switched Branches | Non-Switched Branches | Gap |
|--------|------------------|----------------------|-----|
| Before (weeks 6-14) | 52.5 | 50.6 | +1.9 points |
| After (weeks 15-22) | 53.6 | 49.5 | +4.1 points |

The gap widened by about 2 points—but this largely reflects **pre-existing trends**, not the procedure's effect.

### **The Critical Problem: Non-Comparable Groups**

Before the switch, the gap was already **widening by 0.29 points per week**. If this trend had continued unchanged, we would have expected:

- **Predicted post-period gap:** 4.39 points
- **Actual post-period gap:** 4.05 points
- **Difference:** -0.33 points (actual was slightly *below* expected)

This suggests the procedure **did not accelerate improvement** and may have slightly slowed it—though the difference is small and not statistically conclusive.

### **Standard Analysis Would Be Misleading**

A simple difference-in-differences model produces a statistically significant coefficient of **+2.12 points** (p<0.001). However, this estimate is **not causal**—it reflects pre-existing differences between the groups, not the procedure's effect.

---

## **Why We Can't Draw a Firm Conclusion**

Valid causal inference requires that treatment and control groups would have followed similar trends absent the intervention (the "parallel trends assumption"). This assumption is **clearly violated** in our data:

- Treatment branches were selected based on characteristics that made them higher-performing
- They were already improving faster before the procedure was implemented
- We cannot separate the procedure's effect from these pre-existing differences

| Priority | Action | Rationale |

| **2** | **Continue monitoring pilot branches** | Collect more data; look for longer-term effects or patterns |
| **3** | **Investigate selection criteria** | Understand why certain branches were chosen and what characteristics predict success |
| **4** | **Consider a randomized trial** | Random assignment would enable credible causal inference |
| **5** | **Evaluate non-score outcomes** | Consider costs, staff satisfaction, patron feedback, and operational efficiency |

## **What the Board Should Know**

- **Switched branches ARE performing better**—just not demonstrably because of the procedure

- **A proper evaluation is still possible** with better experimental design
