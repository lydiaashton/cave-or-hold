# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

**Prepared for the Board of Directors**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence supports rolling out the new procedure.** Branches that adopted the new shelving and rotation procedure showed significantly larger improvements in service scores than branches that kept the old procedure. The estimated effect is **+2.4 points** (95% CI: 1.1 to 3.6), statistically significant at p < 0.001.

Notably, branches selected for the new procedure started with lower service scores but not only caught up—they exceeded the control group after implementation. This suggests the new procedure may be particularly beneficial for lower-performing branches.

**Recommendation: Approve system-wide rollout, with monitoring to confirm effects persist.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect
| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|---------------|---------------|--------|
| Control (Old Procedure) | 49.4 | 50.7 | +1.3 |
| Treatment (New Procedure) | 47.7 | 51.4 | +3.7 |
| **Difference** | **-1.7** | **+0.7** | **+2.4** |

The **2.4-point difference-in-differences** estimate isolates the effect of the new procedure from general time trends affecting all branches.

### 2. Statistical Confidence
- **Highly significant**: p < 0.001
- **95% confidence interval**: 1.1 to 3.6 points
- **Parallel trends verified**: Pre-switch trends were similar between groups (p = 0.87)
- **Robust to controls**: Effect persists after adjusting for staffing and visitor volume

### 3. Important Context
- Treatment branches started at a **disadvantage** (1.7 points lower at baseline)
- The new procedure helped these branches **close the gap and surpass** control branches
- Effect size: approximately **5% of baseline** service score, or **0.24 standard deviations**

### 4. What We Cannot Rule Out
- This is **observational data**, not a randomized trial
- Unmeasured factors correlated with branch selection could partially explain results
- Only **8 weeks of post-implementation data**—longer-term effects unknown
