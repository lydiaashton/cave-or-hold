# **REVISED MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD OF DIRECTORS**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 2025  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure  

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence suggests the new procedure improved service scores, but with important caveats.** Our best estimate is a **~2 point improvement**, however this estimate has limitations because branches that adopted the new procedure were already showing different performance patterns before the switch. The post-switch gains are larger than pre-switch fluctuations, suggesting a real effect exists, but we cannot precisely isolate its magnitude. **We recommend proceeding with rollout, but with careful monitoring.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Observed Improvement (With Caveats)**

| Group | Before Switch (Weeks 6-14) | After Switch (Weeks 15-22) | Change |
|-------|---------------------------|---------------------------|--------|
| **New Procedure** (425 branches) | 51.05 | 54.75 | +3.70 |
| **Old Procedure** (284 branches) | 50.17 | 51.92 | +1.75 |
| **Observed Difference** | — | — | **+1.95** |

⚠️ **Important:** This 1.95-point estimate may include pre-existing differences between the groups, not just the effect of the new procedure.

### **2. Analytical Limitation: Non-Random Assignment**

The branches that switched to the new procedure were **not randomly selected**. Our analysis shows:

- Treatment and control groups were already on **different trajectories** before the switch (p = 0.001)
- **5 of 8 pre-period weeks** showed statistically significant differences between groups
- This suggests switching branches may have differed systematically (e.g., more motivated staff, better management, more resources)

**What this means:** The true effect of the new procedure is likely **smaller than 1.95 points**, but we cannot determine exactly how much smaller.

### **3. Evidence Supporting a Real Effect**

Despite the limitation above, several findings suggest the procedure **did** have a positive impact:

| Period | Effect Size Range | Pattern |
|--------|------------------|---------|
| **Pre-switch** (weeks 6-14) | -2.4 to +1.7 points | Volatile, no clear trend |
| **Post-switch** (weeks 15-22) | +1.3 to +6.9 points | Consistently positive, larger magnitude |

- Post-switch effects are **larger and more sustained** than pre-switch fluctuations
- The effect **grew over time** (consistent with staff learning the new procedure)
- **6 of 8 post-switch weeks** show statistically significant improvements

### **4. Practical Significance**

- Average service score: ~50 points
- Estimated improvement: ~2 points (**~4% gain**)
- No evidence of negative consequences or increased score variability

---

## **Recommendations**

### **Proceed with Rollout, But Cautiously**

1. **Approve phased rollout** to remaining 284 branches, but implement in batches to allow for ongoing evaluation.

2. **Set conservative expectations:** Communicate to branch managers that improvements of 1-2 points are anticipated, but individual results may vary.

3. **Invest in training:** The 2-3 week adjustment period suggests adequate training support will accelerate benefit realization.

### **Strengthen Evidence Base**

4. **Continue monitoring:** Track service scores for at least 6 months post-rollout to confirm effects persist.

5. **Consider a randomized pilot** for future procedure changes to enable cleaner causal inference.

6. **Gather qualitative feedback** from branch staff about what aspects of the new procedure are most helpful.

---

## **Analytical Transparency**

This evaluation used a **Difference-in-Differences** design. While this is a standard approach for quasi-experimental data, it relies on the assumption that treatment and control groups would have followed parallel trends absent the intervention. **This assumption was not met in our data.** We have therefore presented a more conservative interpretation than the raw statistics might suggest.
