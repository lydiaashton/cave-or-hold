# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure protected branches from a service score decline.** After week 15, non-switched branches experienced a significant drop in service scores (-2.2 points), while switched branches maintained their levels. The new procedure did not improve scores above baseline—it prevented deterioration.

**Recommendation: Proceed with rollout, but investigate the underlying decline.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### What Happened

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Treatment (switched) | 52.15 | 52.30 | +0.15 (ns) |
| Control (not switched) | 52.72 | 50.56 | **−2.17*** |

*Control decline is statistically significant (p < 0.001)

### The Estimated Effect

- **Difference-in-Differences: +2.30 points** (95% CI: 1.0 to 3.6)
- **Interpretation:** Switched branches avoided a ~2-point decline that affected non-switched branches
- **Statistical significance:** p < 0.001

### Why This Matters

1. **Something changed at week 15** that negatively affected service scores system-wide (seasonal factors? staffing changes? increased demand?)

2. **The new procedure acted as a buffer**—branches using it were insulated from whatever caused the decline

3. **No evidence of active improvement:** Switched branches didn't exceed their pre-switch performance

### Analysis Validity

- Parallel trends confirmed before the switch (p = 0.80)
- Effect robust across specifications (simple DiD, controls, fixed effects)
- Effect emerges only after week 15 in event study

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with rollout**—the procedure provides meaningful protection against service score deterioration.

2. **Urgently investigate the control group decline.** What happened at week 15 that caused non-switched branches to drop? This could reveal:
   - Seasonal patterns requiring proactive management
   - External pressures the new procedure mitigates
   - Unrelated factors that need separate attention

3. **Set realistic expectations.** Communicate to stakeholders that the procedure maintains service levels rather than boosting them.

4. **Monitor post-rollout.** Confirm the protective effect holds when all branches switch (no control group remaining).

5. **Consider complementary measures.** If the goal is actual improvement (not just preventing decline), additional interventions may be needed alongside this procedure.
