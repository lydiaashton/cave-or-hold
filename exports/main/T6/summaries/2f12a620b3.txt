## **BOTTOM LINE**

The new procedure is associated with a **2.3-point improvement in service scores**, but this result is **not statistically significant at conventional levels** (p=0.10). The 95% confidence interval (-0.4 to +5.1 points) includes zero, meaning we cannot rule out no effect. However, there is a **95% probability the true effect is positive**. 

**Recommendation:** The board faces a trade-off. If the new procedure is low-cost and low-risk, the evidence supports proceeding. If rollout requires significant investment, **collect 6–8 more weeks of data** to reach statistical certainty before committing system-wide.

### **What the Confidence Interval Means**

The true effect could reasonably be:
- **As high as +5.1 points** (meaningful improvement)
- **Around +2.3 points** (our best estimate)
- **As low as -0.4 points** (essentially no effect, possibly slightly negative)

We **cannot confidently distinguish** the new procedure from having no effect.

### **Why the Uncertainty?**

- Only **8 weeks of post-switch data** per branch
- Service scores have natural week-to-week variation
- **34 branches** provides moderate but not definitive statistical power

### **What Would Strengthen the Evidence?**

| Scenario | Additional Data Needed |
|----------|----------------------|
| If effect is real and persists | ~7 more weeks to reach p < 0.05 |
| To detect smaller effects | More weeks or more branches |
