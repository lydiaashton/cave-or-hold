# **Memorandum to the Board**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure

**Date:** November 15, 2026

---

## **Bottom Line**

**We cannot confidently conclude the new procedure caused the improvement.** While service scores at switched branches are higher than control branches post-switch, this difference was already emerging *before* the procedure change. The data does not support a clear causal claim. We recommend **additional evaluation before system-wide rollout**.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Pre-Existing Trends Undermine Causal Inference

The standard evaluation method (difference-in-differences) requires that treatment and control branches were on parallel trajectories before the switch. **This assumption is violated:**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Pre-switch trend (treatment vs. control) | +0.32 points/week |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 |
| Implication | Treatment branches were already improving relative to control *before* the switch |

Over the 9-week pre-period, this trend alone would produce a ~2.9 point gap—**larger than the 2.1 point "effect"** we observe post-switch.

### 2. No Immediate Jump at Implementation

If the procedure caused the improvement, we would expect a noticeable jump in the gap at week 15. Instead:

- **Week 14 gap (pre-switch):** +2.70 points
- **Week 15 gap (first week post-switch):** +2.50 points
- **Change:** −0.21 points (the gap actually *narrowed*)

Both groups experienced a sharp score drop at week 15 (likely seasonal), but treatment branches did not outperform.

### 3. Later Weeks Show Larger Gaps—But Interpretation Is Unclear

| Period | Average Gap |
|--------|-------------|
| Weeks 6–14 (pre) | +0.92 |
| Weeks 15–19 (early post) | +2.40 |
| Weeks 20–22 (late post) | +4.04 |

The widening gap in weeks 20–22 could indicate:
- A **delayed treatment effect** (branches improved after learning the new procedure)
- **Continuation of pre-existing trends** (branches that adopted early were already on upward trajectories)
- **Selection bias** (higher-performing branches self-selected into the new procedure)

We cannot distinguish among these explanations with the current data.

---

## **Recommendation**

**Do not proceed with system-wide rollout yet.** The evidence is inconclusive.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Extend the evaluation period** – Collect 8–12 more weeks of data to see if the late-post gains persist or revert
2. **Consider a randomized rollout** – For remaining branches, randomly assign implementation timing to enable cleaner causal inference
3. **Investigate selection factors** – Analyze whether branches that opted in differed systematically (size, staffing, management) from those that did not
4. **Pilot with matched pairs** – If proceeding, pair similar branches and stagger implementation to isolate the effect
