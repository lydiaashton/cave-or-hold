# **Memorandum to the Board of Directors**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Office  
**Date:** [Current Date]  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Rotation Procedure  

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The data are inconclusive.** Branches that adopted the new procedure showed a 2.4-point improvement in service scores compared to branches that did not, but this result is **not statistically significant** (p = 0.12). The confidence interval ranges from -0.6 to +5.4 points, meaning we cannot rule out no effect—or even a small negative effect. 

**Recommendation:** Do **not** make a system-wide rollout decision based on this evidence alone. Instead, **continue the pilot for 6 more months** to gather additional data before committing cooperative-wide resources.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. What We Estimated**

| Metric | Value | Interpretation |
|--------|-------|----------------|
| Point estimate | **+2.4 points** | Direction is positive |
| p-value | **0.12** | Not statistically significant |
| 95% confidence interval | **-0.6 to +5.4** | Includes zero |
| Probability effect ≤ 0 | **~6%** | Low, but not negligible |

### **2. What This Means**

The data are consistent with **three possibilities**:

| Scenario | Plausibility |
|----------|--------------|
| The procedure has **no real effect** | ✓ Compatible with data |
| The procedure has a **small negative effect** (up to -0.6) | ✓ Compatible with data |
| The procedure has a **meaningful positive effect** (up to +5.4) | ✓ Compatible with data |

**We cannot distinguish among these possibilities with current data.**

### **3. Why the Uncertainty?**

- **Only 34 branches** participated (20 switched, 14 did not)
- **Only 8 weeks** of post-switch data per branch
- **High variability** in weekly service scores (standard deviation ≈ 10 points)

With this sample size, the study can only detect fairly large effects with confidence. Smaller effects—positive or negative—cannot be reliably identified.

### **4. What We Can Say with Confidence**

✓ **No evidence of harm:** The lower bound (-0.6) suggests any negative effect would be small.

✓ **Pre-trends were parallel:** Branches that switched did not differ systematically from those that didn't before the change (p = 0.39).

✓ **The analysis is valid:** The methodology is sound; the limitation is sample size, not design.

## **Next Steps**

### **Recommended Action: Extend the Pilot**

| Action | Timeline | Rationale |
|--------|----------|-----------|
| **1. Continue current pilot** as-is | Months 1-6 | Gather more data without committing |
| **2. Do NOT expand** to remaining branches | Until re-evaluation | Avoid scaling an unproven change |

| **4. Board decision** on system-wide rollout | Month 7 | Based on stronger evidence |

### **What Additional Data Will Tell Us**

With 6 more months of data:
- Confidence interval will narrow by approximately **40%**
- We will be able to detect effects as small as **1.5 points** (vs. 2.4+ currently)
- Statistical significance will be more informative

### **Success Metrics for Re-evaluation**

| Outcome | Recommended Decision |
|---------|---------------------|
| Effect > 0 and statistically significant (p < 0.05) | Proceed with full rollout |
| Effect > 0 but still not significant | Consider targeted rollout to high-need branches |
| Effect ≈ 0 or negative | Discontinue new procedure; revert to old method |

---

## **Conclusion**

The board asked whether the new procedure **changed** service scores. The honest answer is: **We don't know yet.** 

The positive point estimate is encouraging, and there is no signal of meaningful harm. However, the evidence is too weak to justify a cooperative-wide commitment. Extending the pilot for 6 months is the prudent course—it preserves the option to adopt if benefits are confirmed while avoiding the cost of scaling an ineffective change.

**We recommend approval to extend the pilot with a formal re-evaluation in 6 months.**
