**The new procedure provides meaningful protection against service score declines.** While scores dropped system-wide after week 14, branches using the new procedure declined only **0.8 points** compared to **3.0 points** for branches using the old procedure. ## **Key Findings**

### Relative Benefit, Not Absolute Improvement

| Group | Before | After | Change |
|-------|--------|-------|--------|
| Switched branches (425) | 53.09 | 52.29 | −0.80 |
| Control branches (284) | 53.06 | 50.10 | −2.96 |
| **Difference (Treatment Effect)** | | | **+2.16** |

The +2.1 point treatment effect is statistically significant (p < 0.001), but it represents **avoided decline**, not absolute improvement.

### System-Wide Decline After Week 14

Service scores dropped across all branches after the switch date, suggesting external factors (possibly seasonal patterns, as weeks 16, 19, 21, and 22 all show lower scores). The new procedure appears to have **buffered** branches against this decline.

### Benefits Accumulate Over Time

| Weeks After Switch | Relative Benefit |
|-------------------|------------------|
| Week 1 | +0.85 |
| Week 4 | +1.84 |
| Week 8 | +3.18 |

The protective effect grows as staff become more proficient with the new procedure.

### Consistent Across Conditions

The benefit held regardless of staffing levels (+2.0 to +2.3 points across all terciles).

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with rollout, but set appropriate expectations.** The evidence shows:

1. The procedure has a **real, measurable benefit** (+2.1 points relative to counterfactual)
2. It **protects against declines** rather than driving absolute score increases
3. Benefits **grow over time** as staff gain experience
4. Effects are **consistent** across different branch conditions

The board should understand that rolling out the procedure will not necessarily raise service scores above current levels, but it will help maintain scores better than the old procedure during periods of system-wide pressure.
