# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

## **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

### **Bottom Line**

**The data suggest a modest positive effect on service scores, though this was not expected.** Branches that switched to the new procedure saw service scores improve by approximately **1.5 to 2 points** relative to branches that kept the old procedure. The effect is statistically distinguishable from zero, but it is modest in magnitude (about 3% of the average score).

**Recommendation:** Proceed with the rollout, but temper expectations. This procedure change should not be viewed as a major service quality intervention. The primary justification should remain operational efficiency, with any service score improvement treated as a potential co-benefit that warrants monitoring.

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. Study Design**
- 150 branches total: 90 adopted the new procedure, 60 kept the old procedure
- 17 weeks of data (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- **Important limitation:** Branches were not randomly assigned to switch. Switched branches had lower baseline service scores (about 3 points lower on average), suggesting they may have been selected because they were underperforming.

**2. Service Score Impact**
| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches | 51.2 | 51.7 | +0.5 |
| Non-switched branches | 54.2 | 52.9 | −1.3 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+1.7** |

The switched branches improved relative to the control group. However, the effect is modest and there is week-to-week variation in the post-switch period.

**3. Statistical Assessment**
- **Effect size:** +1.76 points (p = 0.001)
- **95% confidence interval:** 0.69 to 2.84 points
- The lower bound of the confidence interval (0.7 points) suggests the true effect could be quite small
- Results are robust to controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

**4. Important Caveats**
- This was **not a randomized experiment** — branches were selected to switch, and the selection criteria may be related to service scores
- The effect, while statistically significant, represents only about **3% of the average service score**
- We have only 8 weeks of post-switch data; longer-term effects are unknown
- The mechanism is unclear — we cannot explain why a shelving/rotation procedure would affect service scores

---

### **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with rollout** — The procedure change appears harmless and may provide a small service benefit, but this should not be the primary justification

2. **Set appropriate expectations** — Communicate to stakeholders that any service score improvement is likely to be modest

3. **Continue monitoring** — Track service scores for 6–12 months after full rollout to confirm the effect persists and to rule out short-term novelty effects

4. **Investigate the mechanism** — If resources allow, conduct qualitative research (staff interviews, patron surveys) to understand whether and how the procedure affects service delivery

5. **Document for future reference** — This finding suggests operational changes can have unintended service quality effects, which should be considered in future procedure evaluations

---

### **Methodology Note**

This analysis uses a difference-in-differences approach. The key assumption is that switched and non-switched branches would have followed similar trends absent the intervention. The pre-period gap between groups was relatively stable, which supports this assumption. However, the non-random selection of branches into the treatment group means we cannot rule out all confounding factors. Results should be interpreted as suggestive rather than definitive.
