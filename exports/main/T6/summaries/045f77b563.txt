# **MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure shows a real but modest improvement in service scores.** We recommend the board consider a **phased rollout** rather than immediate system-wide adoption. The evidence supports continuing the new procedure, but the 4% improvement should be weighed against implementation costs and the fact that roughly one in four switched branches saw no benefit.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **Study Design**
- **709 branches** participated (425 switched to new procedure; 284 kept old procedure)
- **17 weeks** of data (weeks 6–22); switch occurred at week 15
- Analysis controlled for branch-specific characteristics and weekly trends

### **Service Score Impact**

| Metric | Switched Branches | Control Branches |
|--------|-------------------|------------------|
| Before switch (avg) | 51.5 | 51.0 |
| After switch (avg) | 53.7 | 51.2 |
| **Change** | **+2.3** | **+0.3** |

- **Net improvement: +2.0 points** (95% CI: 1.6 to 2.3; p < 0.001)
- **Relative improvement: ~4%** over baseline

### **Important Caveats**

| Consideration | Finding |
|---------------|---------|
| **Not universal** | 27% of switched branches saw no improvement or decline |
| **Some negative effects** | A few branches dropped up to 10 points after switching |
| **Short-term data** | Only 8 weeks of post-switch observation |
| **Modest effect** | 2 points on a 50-point scale is meaningful but not transformative |
| **Non-random assignment** | Branches were not randomly assigned to switch, which could bias results |

### **Validation**
- Pre-switch trends were parallel between groups (supports causal interpretation)
- Staffing and visit patterns did not differ between groups (suggests genuine service improvement)

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

1. **Request cost estimates** for system-wide implementation (training, materials, staff time)

2. **Conduct cost-benefit analysis** comparing the 4% service improvement against implementation expenses

3. **Consider phased expansion** to additional branches while monitoring results

4. **Investigate underperforming branches** to understand why 27% saw no benefit

5. **Extend monitoring period** to determine if effects persist beyond 8 weeks

---

## **RECOMMENDATION**

The new procedure **does improve service scores**, and the evidence is statistically sound. However, the effect is modest and not universal. We recommend:

- **If implementation costs are low:** Proceed with rollout
- **If implementation costs are significant:** Consider targeted rollout to branches most likely to benefit, or invest in understanding why some branches did not improve
