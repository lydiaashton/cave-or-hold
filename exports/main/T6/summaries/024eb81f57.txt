## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence is inconclusive.** While branches using the new procedure outperformed control branches by about 2 points after the switch, this difference appears driven by a decline in the control group rather than improvement in the treatment group. The treatment branches themselves showed virtually no change (+0.2 points). Given that we expected no effect, and that treatment branches were already outperforming control branches before the switch, we **cannot confidently attribute the gap to the new procedure**. We recommend **against a full rollout** until further evidence is gathered.

## **Key Findings**

### Critical Context
- Treatment branches scored **~1.7 points higher** than control branches throughout the pre-period (weeks 6-14)
- This pre-existing gap suggests systematic differences between the two groups
- The parallel trends assumption underlying our analysis may not hold

### What Changed After the Switch

| Group | Before | After | Change |
|-------|--------|-------|--------|
| New Procedure | 50.91 | 51.10 | **+0.19** |
| Old Procedure | 49.22 | 47.36 | **−1.87** |

The treatment group's service scores remained essentially flat. The statistical "effect" comes from the control group's decline.

### Possible Explanations
1. **Selection bias:** Better-performing branches may have self-selected into the new procedure
2. **External factors:** Control branches may have faced unrelated challenges during weeks 15-22
3. **True effect:** The new procedure may have protected branches from decline (but this is less supported by the data)

---

## **Recommendation**

**Do not proceed with system-wide rollout at this time.** Instead:

1. **Extend the evaluation period** by 8-12 weeks to see if patterns persist
2. **Investigate why control branches declined** during the post-period
3. **Consider a randomized trial** for future procedure changes to avoid selection bias
4. **Revisit the decision** after additional data is collected

---

## **Bottom Line for Decision-Makers**

The new procedure did not demonstrably improve service scores. At best, it may have helped branches maintain existing performance during a period when other branches declined. Given implementation costs and uncertainty, a cautious approach is warranted.
