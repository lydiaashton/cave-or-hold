# Library Cooperative Branch Procedure Change: Impact Analysis

**Date:** November 2026  
**Prepared for:** Board of Directors

---

## Bottom Line

The new procedure shows a **positive trend** (+2 points, ~4% improvement), but the evidence is **not statistically significant** (p=0.10). The 95% confidence interval ranges from **-0.4 to +4.5 points**, meaning we cannot rule out no effect. **We recommend gathering more data before deciding on system-wide rollout.**

---

## Key Findings

### Service Score Results

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 50.89 | 50.90 | +0.01 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 49.69 | 51.72 | +2.03 |
| **Net Effect (DiD)** | | | **+2.02** |

### Statistical Evidence

| Metric | Value | Interpretation |
|--------|-------|----------------|
| Effect estimate | +2.02 points | Positive direction |
| p-value | 0.105 | Not significant at 0.05 level |
| 95% Confidence Interval | -0.4 to +4.5 | Includes zero |
| Branches improving | 70% treatment vs. 50% control | Consistent pattern |

### What This Means

- **The data is inconclusive.** The observed improvement could be due to the new procedure, or it could be random variation.
- **The direction is encouraging.** No branches showed systematic declines, and the point estimate is positive.
- **More data is needed.** With only 8 weeks of post-switch observations across 34 branches, we lack statistical power to detect modest effects reliably.

---

## Recommendations

### 1. Do Not Yet Approve System-Wide Rollout
The evidence does not meet the standard for a confident decision. Rolling out based on this data alone risks implementing a change that may have no real benefit.

### 2. Extend the Evaluation Period
Continue collecting data for at least 12 more weeks. This will:
- Increase statistical power
- Narrow the confidence interval
- Clarify whether the effect is real

### 3. Consider a Staged Rollout
If there is operational pressure to proceed, expand to 10–15 additional branches while keeping 10+ branches as controls. This preserves the ability to measure the effect while allowing some branches to benefit if the procedure is effective.

### 4. Investigate Implementation Variation
Some branches gained 5–10 points while others saw no change or slight declines. Understanding what differentiates high-performing adopters could improve training and increase the likelihood of success.

---

## Conclusion

The board's expectation that the new procedure would improve service scores is **not confirmed by this analysis**. The data shows a positive signal, but it is too weak to support a confident rollout decision. The responsible course is to **gather more evidence before committing system-wide resources.**

**Recommended decision:** Continue the pilot with extended monitoring. Revisit the rollout decision after 12 additional weeks of data collection.

---

*Note: This analysis uses standard difference-in-differences methods with cluster-robust standard errors. The parallel trends assumption appears satisfied based on pre-switch data.*
