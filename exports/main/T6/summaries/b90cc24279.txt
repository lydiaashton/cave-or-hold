## **Bottom Line**

The data suggests the new procedure **likely improved service scores by approximately 2 points**, though we have moderate (not definitive) confidence in this conclusion. The branches selected to switch were already higher-performing, which complicates interpretation. We recommend **a phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than immediate system-wide adoption.

## **Key Findings**

### **The Numbers**

| | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Branches with new procedure** | 52.2 | 53.4 | +1.2 |
| **Branches with old procedure** | 48.7 | 48.0 | −0.7 |

### **Statistical Assessment**

| Metric | Value | Interpretation |
|--------|-------|----------------|
| Effect estimate | +1.91 points | Modest improvement |
| 95% confidence interval | [0.83, 2.99] | Does not include zero |
| p-value | 0.0005 | Statistically significant |
| Effect size | 0.19 standard deviations | Small-to-moderate |

### **Important Caveats**

1. **Selection bias concern**: Branches chosen to switch scored 3.5 points higher than control branches even before the change. They may have been better-managed branches overall.

2. **Pre-trend variability**: The treatment-control gap fluctuated considerably before the switch (ranging from 1.8 to 4.9 points). Week 13 showed the smallest gap, which may have been an anomaly.

3. **Control group decline**: Non-switched branches declined slightly (−0.75 points). The DiD assumes switched branches would have declined similarly without treatment—this is unprovable.

## **Bottom Line Restated**

The evidence points toward a **real but modest benefit** from the new procedure. It is unlikely to harm service quality, and likely provides some improvement. However, given the selection concerns and modest effect size, a **measured rollout with ongoing evaluation** is more appropriate than treating this as a confirmed success.
