# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact Analysis**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure is associated with improved service scores, but confidence is limited.** Branches that adopted the new procedure showed a **1.9-point improvement** relative to non-switched branches. However, the switched branches were already improving relative to controls before the change, making it difficult to isolate the procedure's true effect.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with caution. Consider a phased rollout with careful monitoring, or run a more controlled pilot before system-wide adoption.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Observed Improvement, but Pre-Trends Are Concerning

| Period | Switched Branches | Non-Switched Branches | Gap |
|--------|------------------|----------------------|-----|
| Before (Weeks 6–14) | 51.64 | 50.18 | 1.46 |
| After (Weeks 15–22) | 52.36 | 49.00 | 3.36 |
| **Change** | **+0.72** | **-1.18** | **+1.90** |

**Important caveat:** The gap between groups was already widening before week 15. Statistical testing confirms a significant pre-existing differential trend (p=0.003). This means the 1.9-point estimate likely **overstates** the true effect of the procedure change.

### 2. The Gap Accelerated After the Switch

| Period | Average Gap (Treatment − Control) |
|--------|----------------------------------|
| Weeks 6–9 (early pre) | 0.84 points |
| Weeks 10–14 (late pre) | 1.76 points |
| Weeks 15–22 (post) | 3.36 points |

The gap roughly doubled after the switch, but it was already growing. A reasonable estimate is that **some portion** of the 1.9-point effect is attributable to the procedure, but the exact magnitude is uncertain.

### 3. Control Group Declined While Treatment Held Steady

Non-switched branches saw service scores drop by 1.18 points after week 15, while switched branches increased by 0.72 points. The new procedure may have **protected branches from decline** even if the absolute improvement is smaller than the raw DiD suggests.

### 4. Analysis Limitations

- **Parallel trends violation:** Treatment and control groups were diverging before the intervention
- **Non-random assignment:** Branches self-selected (or were selected) to switch; they were already higher-performing
- **Short post-period:** Only 8 weeks of data after the change
- **Unobserved confounders:** Other differences between switching and non-switching branches may exist

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Do not approve immediate system-wide rollout.** The evidence is suggestive but not definitive.

2. **Run a randomized controlled trial:** Randomly assign a new set of branches to switch vs. stay, ensuring comparable groups and valid causal inference.

3. **If proceeding with rollout, implement gradually:** Switch branches in waves and monitor each cohort carefully before expanding.

4. **Investigate why control branches declined:** Understanding this drop may reveal opportunities to improve all branches regardless of procedure.

5. **Gather qualitative feedback:** Interview staff at switched branches about implementation challenges and perceived benefits.
