# **REVISED MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure  
**Date:** November 2025

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The evidence is inconclusive.** While switched branches showed stronger post-switch performance, they were already improving faster before the procedure change. The pre-existing trend alone could explain the entire observed effect. **We cannot confidently attribute the improvement to the new procedure.**

**Recommendation:** Do not proceed with systemwide rollout based on this evidence alone. Instead, implement a **randomized controlled trial** to obtain credible evidence before making a systemwide decision.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **1. The Critical Problem: Violated Assumption**

The difference-in-differences method requires that treatment and control groups would have followed **parallel trends** absent the intervention. This assumption is **violated** in our data:

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Pre-switch differential trend | **+0.29 points/week** (p < 0.001) |
| Over 9 pre-switch weeks | Accounts for **2.63 points** of gap |
| Over 8 post-switch weeks | Would account for **2.34 points** of gap |
| Estimated treatment effect | **1.94 points** |
| **Residual after accounting for trend** | **-0.39 points** |

**Interpretation:** If the pre-existing trend continued unchanged, we would expect a 2.34-point gap increase. The observed 1.94-point increase is actually *smaller* than this. The data does not demonstrate a positive treatment effect beyond the pre-existing divergence.

### **2. No Discontinuous Jump at Switch Point**

If the procedure caused an immediate improvement, we should see a sharp gap increase at Week 15:

| Comparison | Gap Change |
|------------|------------|
| Week 14 → Week 15 (single week) | **+0.11 points** |
| Last 3 pre-weeks → First 3 post-weeks | **+0.37 points** |

These minimal changes suggest no clear break attributable to the procedure change.

### **3. What the Data Does Show**

| Period | Non-Switched | Switched | Gap |
|--------|-------------|----------|-----|
| Pre-switch | 48.85 | 50.75 | 1.91 pts |
| Post-switch | 48.27 | 52.14 | 3.87 pts |

Switched branches ARE performing better post-switch, but we cannot determine whether this is due to:
- The new procedure, OR
- Better management/practices that led to their early selection, OR
- Both factors combined

### **4. Likely Selection Bias**

Branches selected for early adoption were probably:
- Better-managed
- Better-staffed
- More innovative
- Already on an improvement trajectory

This selection bias confounds the analysis and cannot be fully adjusted for statistically.

---

## **RECOMMENDED NEXT STEPS**

### **1. Pause Systemwide Rollout**
Do not commit resources to systemwide implementation until we have credible evidence of effectiveness.

### **2. Conduct a Randomized Controlled Trial**
- Randomly assign remaining 284 non-switched branches to treatment (new procedure) or continued control
- This eliminates selection bias and restores the validity of causal inference
- Monitor for 8-12 weeks post-implementation

### **3. If Rollout Proceeds Anyway**
Should the board decide to proceed despite inconclusive evidence:
- Implement **phased rollout** with continued monitoring
- Establish **clear success metrics** and thresholds for continuation
- Be prepared to **reverse course** if scores do not improve

### **4. Qualitative Investigation**
Interview early-adopter branches to understand:
- What practices accompany the new procedure
- Whether high-performing branches do other things differently
- Implementation challenges and support needs

---

## **ANALYSIS SCOPE**

- **Data:** 12,053 weekly observations from 709 branches
- **Treatment Group:** 425 branches that switched (Week 15 onward)
- **Control Group:** 284 branches that did not switch
- **Method:** Difference-in-differences regression with cluster-robust standard errors
- **Key Limitation:** Non-random selection into treatment violates parallel trends assumption
