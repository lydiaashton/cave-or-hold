# **Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure Evaluation**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores—an unexpected but welcome finding.** Branches that adopted the new rotation and shelving procedure saw service scores increase by **1.8 to 1.9 points** (approximately 4%) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This effect is statistically significant (p = 0.002) and robust to multiple verification checks. Despite initial expectations that the procedure change would have no effect on service quality, the data clearly show a positive impact. We recommend proceeding with a system-wide rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Statistically Significant Improvement

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect | **+1.9 points** on service score |
| 95% Confidence Interval | 0.7 to 3.1 points |
| Statistical Significance | p = 0.002 |
| Relative Improvement | ~4% above baseline |

### 2. Strong Causal Evidence

The analysis used a rigorous difference-in-differences design:
- **Parallel trends confirmed**: Before the switch, treatment and control branches had nearly identical scores (47.13 vs 47.05)
- **Placebo test passed**: No significant difference found when testing fake switch dates in the pre-period
- **Effect persists** after controlling for staffing levels and visitor volume

### 3. Most Branches Benefited

| Outcome | Treatment Branches | Control Branches |
|---------|-------------------|------------------|
| Improved after period | 79% | 60% |
| Declined after period | 21% | 40% |

### 4. Why This Matters

This finding is notable because **the procedure change was not expected to affect service scores**. The improvement may stem from:
- Staff having easier access to materials
- Reduced time searching for items
- Better organization leading to faster patron service

---

## **Recommendations**

1. **Approve system-wide rollout** to all 150 branches
2. **Document implementation practices** from high-performing branches
3. **Monitor outcomes** post-rollout to confirm the effect persists
4. **Investigate the 21% of branches** that did not improve to identify barriers

---

## **Technical Summary**

- **Data**: 2,550 observations (150 branches × 17 weeks)
- **Method**: Difference-in-differences with cluster-robust standard errors
- **Verification**: Manual calculation, regression, and placebo tests all confirm the result
