# **REVISED MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD OF DIRECTORS**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure  

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**Recommendation: Proceed with caution. The evidence is promising but not definitive.**

The new procedure is associated with a **1.9 point improvement** in service scores, but there are **important limitations** that reduce confidence in this estimate:

1. **Pre-existing trends**: Branches that switched were already improving faster than control branches *before* the switch (the gap grew from -0.35 to +1.45 points over weeks 6-14, p = 0.009)
2. **System-wide improvement**: *All* branches improved after week 15 (~2 points), suggesting something else changed that affected everyone
3. **Small effect size**: 1.9 points represents only 0.19 standard deviations—conventionally considered a "small" effect
4. **Weekly estimates not significant**: Individual week-by-week effects are not statistically distinguishable from zero

**Revised recommendation**: Consider a **phased rollout with continued evaluation** rather than immediate system-wide implementation.

### What Supports a Positive Conclusion

- Post-switch coefficients are **all positive** and **increase over time** (+0.17 to +1.92)
- Pre-switch coefficients are **all negative** (relative to reference week)
- The pattern shows a **clear break** at the switch point
- Effect is **not explained** by staffing or visit changes

| Action | Rationale |
|--------|-----------|
| **1. Continue monitoring** for 8-12 more weeks | Determine if the effect persists and grows, or if pre-trends continue |
| **2. Investigate week-15 system-wide change** | Understand what caused *all* branches to improve (seasonal? measurement?) |
| **3. Consider phased rollout** | Expand to additional branches while maintaining some controls for ongoing evaluation |
| **4. Conduct cost-benefit analysis** | Is a ~2 point improvement worth the implementation costs? |

---

## **CONCLUSION**

The data show a **positive association** between the new procedure and service scores, but **causal confidence is limited** by pre-existing differences between groups and a system-wide improvement at the same time as the switch. 

We recommend **continued evaluation** before committing to full rollout. If the effect persists and grows over the next 8-12 weeks while pre-trends do not continue, confidence in the procedure's effectiveness would increase substantially.
