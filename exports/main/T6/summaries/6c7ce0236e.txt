## **Bottom Line**

The evidence **suggests the new procedure improved service scores**, though statistical certainty is moderate. Branches that adopted the new procedure gained approximately **2.2 points** on the service score index compared to branches that kept the old procedure. The 90% confidence interval (0.02 to 4.43 points) indicates we can be reasonably confident the effect is positive, though it does not meet the stricter 95% confidence standard. Given the expected direction, meaningful magnitude (~4% improvement), and no detected harms, we recommend proceeding with the rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **What We Measured**
- **34 branches** tracked over 17 weeks (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** switched to the new procedure in week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Service scores averaged around 50 points across the cooperative (higher is better)

### **Results**

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|----------------|---------------|--------|
| Branches with **new procedure** | 51.78 | 52.80 | **+1.02** |
| Branches with **old procedure** | 52.05 | 50.90 | **-1.15** |

**Net effect of new procedure: +2.22 points**

### **Statistical Confidence**

| Confidence Level | Interval | Includes Zero? |
|-----------------|----------|----------------|
| 90% | 0.02 to 4.43 | No ✓ |
| 95% | -0.40 to 4.85 | Yes |

**Interpretation:** We can be 90% confident the true effect is positive, but not 95% confident. The p-value of 0.097 indicates the result is "marginally significant."

### **What This Means**

1. **The direction matches expectations.** Treatment branches improved relative to control branches, consistent with the hypothesized benefit.

2. **The magnitude is operationally meaningful.** A 2.2-point gain equals about 4% of the average service score.

3. **Statistical certainty is moderate, not high.** There is approximately a 1 in 10 chance the observed effect could occur even if the procedure has no true impact.

4. **No negative effects detected.** No branches showed score declines attributable to the change.

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with systemwide rollout, with continued monitoring.**

The weight of evidence favors a positive effect, and the risk of harm appears minimal. However, given the statistical uncertainty, we recommend:

1. Approving the rollout to remaining branches
2. Collecting additional data post-rollout to narrow the confidence interval
3. Re-evaluating after 12 weeks with the larger dataset
