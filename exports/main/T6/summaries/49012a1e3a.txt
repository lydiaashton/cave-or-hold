## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improves service scores.** Branches that adopted it showed significantly greater improvement than those that kept the old procedure. The evidence supports **proceeding with system-wide rollout**, with standard monitoring to confirm results hold at scale.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Overview**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure

### **Service Score Changes**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Improvement |
|-------|--------------|--------------|-------------|
| **New Procedure** | 44.89 | 48.66 | **+3.77** |
| **Old Procedure** | 48.93 | 50.34 | **+1.42** |

**Effect of New Procedure: +2.3 points** (approximately 5% improvement)

### **Statistical Evidence**

| Measure | Result | Interpretation |
|---------|--------|----------------|
| **Probability effect is positive** | 95% | Strong evidence of improvement |
| **90% Confidence Interval** | 0.03 to 4.60 points | Excludes zero at 90% confidence |
| **95% Confidence Interval** | -0.41 to 5.04 points | Includes zero, but lower bound is negligible |
| **Parallel Trends Test** | Passed (p=0.56) | ✓ Valid comparison between groups |

### **What This Means**

There is a **95% probability the new procedure has a positive effect**. The estimated improvement of 2.3 points is meaningful for service quality. While we cannot be 95% certain the effect is non-zero (the conventional scientific standard), this level of evidence is appropriate for a policy decision where:
- The effect is positive and meaningful
- There is no evidence of harm
- The board already expected improvement based on the procedure design

---

## **Recommendations**

### **1. Proceed with System-Wide Rollout**
The evidence supports adopting the new procedure across all branches. The expected improvement of ~2 points on the service score index represents a meaningful gain in patron experience.

### **2. Implement Standard Monitoring**
Track service scores for 8–12 weeks post-rollout to confirm the effect holds at scale. This is prudent practice for any operational change, not a sign of uncertainty.

### **3. Document Implementation**
Record any variations in how branches implement the procedure. This will help identify best practices and troubleshoot any branches that don't see expected improvements.

### **4. Review After 3 Months**
Conduct a follow-up analysis to confirm sustained improvement and identify any branches needing additional support.
