# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 2025  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure  

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure did not improve service scores.** After accounting for pre-existing differences between branches, the estimated effect is essentially zero (−0.58 points, not statistically significant). **We recommend against a system-wide rollout.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
- **709 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **425 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **284 branches** continued with the old procedure

### Why the Initial Numbers Were Misleading

A simple comparison suggested switched branches gained 1.86 points. However, this ignored a critical fact: **branches that switched were already improving faster before the change.**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Apparent effect (unadjusted) | +1.86 points |
| Pre-trend (switched branches improving faster) | +0.29 points/week |
| **True effect (adjusted for pre-trend)** | **−0.58 points** |
| Statistical significance | **Not significant (p=0.42)** |

### The Gap Actually Shrunk at the Switch

| Week | Gap (Switched − Non-Switched) |
|------|-------------------------------|
| Week 14 (last week before) | 3.37 points |
| Week 15 (first week after) | 3.04 points |
| **Change** | **−0.33 points** |

If the procedure had an immediate positive effect, we would expect the gap to jump up at week 15. It did not.

---

## **Conclusion**

The evidence indicates **no meaningful benefit** from the new procedure. The apparent improvement was entirely due to pre-existing differences between branches that chose to switch versus those that did not.

---

## **Recommendations**

1. **Do not proceed with system-wide rollout.** The procedure shows no measurable benefit.

2. **Consider discontinuing the new procedure** at switched branches unless there are other unmeasured benefits (e.g., staff efficiency, cost savings).

3. **If pursuing further evaluation:**
   - Use randomized assignment rather than self-selection
   - Define clear success metrics before implementation
   - Allow sufficient time for effects to materialize
