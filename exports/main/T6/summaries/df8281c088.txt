# REVISED MEMORANDUM

**TO:** Board of Directors  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## Bottom Line

**We cannot conclude the new procedure improved service scores.** While initial analysis suggested a +2.0 point improvement, deeper examination reveals the treatment and control branches had systematically different performance patterns *before* the switch. The event study shows no clear positive effect after adoption. **We recommend against system-wide rollout until more rigorous evidence is obtained.**

---

## Key Findings

### 1. Initial DiD Estimate (Now Questioned)
| Metric | Initial Result |
|--------|---------------|
| Treatment effect | +2.0 points |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 |

### 2. Event Study Results (More Reliable)
| Period | Average Effect | Significant Weeks |
|--------|---------------|-------------------|
| **Pre-switch** (weeks -9 to -1) | -1.92 | 6 of 9 weeks |
| **Post-switch** (weeks 0 to 7) | **-0.90** | 4 of 8 weeks |

**The post-switch effect is negative, not positive.**

### 3. Why the Initial Analysis Was Misleading

The difference-in-differences method requires that treatment and control groups follow parallel trends before the intervention. Our data shows:

- Treatment branches had **wildly different weekly patterns** before switching (effects ranging from -7.5 to +3.8)
- This suggests **selection bias**: branches that chose to switch were systematically different from those that didn't
- The +2.0 estimate likely captures these pre-existing differences, not a causal effect

---

## Recommendation

**Do not approve system-wide rollout at this time.**

---

## Next Steps

| Action | Rationale |
|--------|-----------|
| **Pause rollout plans** | Current evidence does not support effectiveness |
| **Conduct randomized trial** | Randomly assign remaining branches to treatment/control to eliminate selection bias |
| **Extend observation period** | 8 weeks post-switch may be too short to detect effects |
| **Investigate heterogeneity** | The procedure may work for some branch types but not others |
| **Qualitative feedback** | Survey staff at switched branches about operational impacts |

## What We Know

- 425 branches adopted the new procedure; 284 did not
- Switched branches had higher baseline scores (~53 vs ~51)
- No clear evidence the procedure caused score improvements
- The observed "effect" likely reflects which branches chose to switch, not the procedure itself
