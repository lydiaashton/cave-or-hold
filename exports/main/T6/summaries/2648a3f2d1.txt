# **Memorandum to the Board**

## **Title: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

### **Bottom Line**

**We cannot conclude the new procedure improved service scores.** While the raw analysis shows a +2.3 point difference, branches that switched were already experiencing declining service scores relative to other branches *before* the procedure change. This pre-existing trend makes it impossible to separate the procedure's effect from natural regression to the mean. **We do not recommend a system-wide rollout based on this evidence.**

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. What the Simple Analysis Shows**
- Switching branches: +2.3 points relative to non-switching branches after the change
- Statistically significant in a basic difference-in-differences model (p < 0.001)

**2. Why We Cannot Trust This Result**
- **Pre-trend violation**: In the weeks before the switch, treatment branches were already falling behind control branches
  - Weeks 11-14: treatment group significantly lower than control (all p < 0.01)
  - This violates the key assumption needed for causal inference
- The apparent "gain" likely reflects the treatment group **recovering from their pre-existing decline**, not a benefit from the new procedure

**3. What This Means**
- The analysis is consistent with the new procedure having **no effect** (as we originally expected)
- It is also consistent with a modest positive or negative effect—we simply cannot tell from this data
- The observational nature of the rollout (branches self-selected or were selected to switch) introduces confounding we cannot adjust for

---

### **Next Steps**

1. **Do not proceed with system-wide rollout** based on current evidence

2. **Consider a randomized trial**: Randomly assign remaining branches to adopt or not adopt the procedure, then measure outcomes with proper experimental design

3. **Investigate why some branches were selected to switch first**: If selection was based on poor performance, that explains the pre-trend pattern

4. **Gather qualitative feedback**: Interview staff at switching branches about whether the new procedure changed their workflow, even if service scores didn't clearly improve

---

*The statistical evidence does not support the conclusion that the new procedure improved service quality. The apparent effect is confounded by pre-existing differences between switching and non-switching branches.*
