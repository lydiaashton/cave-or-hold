## **Executive Summary**

The new procedure shows **promising but not statistically definitive** evidence of improving service scores. The estimated improvement is **2.2 points** (approximately 5%), but the confidence interval includes the possibility of no effect. The evidence leans positive, but uncertainty remains.

**Recommendation: Consider a broader pilot before full rollout, or proceed with rollout accompanied by rigorous monitoring.**

---

## **Bottom Line**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did the new procedure improve service scores? | **Suggests yes, but not certain** |
| Is the evidence statistically conclusive? | **No** – p-value = 0.085 (above 0.05 threshold) |
| What's the most likely outcome? | **Modest improvement** (~2 points) |
| What's the range of plausible outcomes? | **No effect to +5 points** (95% confidence) |
| Should we roll out to all branches? | **Proceed with caution** – monitor closely |

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Point Estimate Is Positive, But Uncertainty Is Material**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect | **+2.2 points** |
| Standard error | 1.30 |
| 95% Confidence Interval | **-0.3 to +4.8** |
| P-value | **0.085** |

**What this means:** The best estimate is a 2.2-point improvement, but we cannot rule out the possibility that the true effect is zero (or even slightly negative). The evidence is **suggestive but not conclusive** by conventional statistical standards.

### **2. Direction of Evidence Is Consistently Positive**

While not statistically definitive, multiple analyses point in the same direction:

| Analysis | Estimated Effect |
|----------|------------------|
| Basic difference-in-differences | +2.0 points |
| DiD with staffing/visits controls | +2.2 points |
| Excluding first 2 weeks post-switch | +2.6 points |
| Branch-level average changes | +2.0 points |

**All estimates are positive.** None suggest harm.

### **3. Benefits May Grow Over Time**

| Period | Estimated Effect |
|--------|------------------|
| Weeks 15–18 (early) | +0.7 points (p = 0.56) |
| Weeks 19–22 (later) | +3.3 points (p = 0.06) |

The effect appears to strengthen as staff gain familiarity with the procedure.

### **4. No Evidence of Harm**

- Zero branches showed catastrophic decline attributable to the procedure.
- The lower bound of the confidence interval (-0.3) suggests any negative effect would be trivial.

---

## **Why Isn't This Statistically Significant?**

| Factor | Impact |
|--------|--------|
| **Sample size** | 34 branches is modest for detecting small effects |
| **Natural variation** | Service scores vary week-to-week (SD ≈ 9.8 points) |
| **Effect size** | The ~2-point effect is real but modest relative to variation |
| **Observation period** | Only 8 weeks of post-switch data per branch |

**Bottom line:** The study may be **underpowered** to detect a real but modest effect with high confidence. Absence of evidence is not evidence of absence.

## **Conclusion**

The data shows **encouraging but not definitive** evidence that the new procedure improves service scores. The estimated effect is positive and meaningful (~2 points), all sensitivity analyses point in the same direction, and there is no evidence of harm. However, statistical uncertainty means we cannot be fully confident the effect is real rather than due to chance.

**Given the low risk and potential upside, proceeding with rollout accompanied by close monitoring is reasonable.** If the board prefers greater certainty before committing, an extended pilot would provide more conclusive evidence.
