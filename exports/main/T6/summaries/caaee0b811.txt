# **BOARD SUMMARY: New Shelving & Stock Rotation Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

The data shows a **positive association** between the new procedure and service scores (+2 points), **but this result is not statistically significant** at conventional standards. We cannot confidently conclude the procedure caused the improvement. The board faces a decision under uncertainty: proceed based on suggestive evidence, or gather more data before committing.

---

## **What the Data Shows**

| Metric | Result | Interpretation |
|--------|--------|----------------|
| **Estimated effect** | +1.9 points | Direction matches expectations |
| **95% confidence interval** | -0.7 to +4.5 | Includes zero; effect could be null |
| **P-value** | 0.15 | Not statistically significant (threshold: 0.05) |
| **Effect size** | 0.18 standard deviations | Small-to-moderate if real |

**Branch Performance:**
- **20 switched branches:** 49.9 → 52.0 (+2.1 points)
- **14 control branches:** 49.2 → 49.3 (+0.2 points)

The pattern is consistent with a beneficial effect, but the evidence is **suggestive, not conclusive**.

---

## **Why the Uncertainty?**

1. **Limited sample size:** 34 branches over 17 weeks provides moderate statistical power
2. **Natural variability:** Service scores fluctuate week-to-week (SD ≈ 10 points)
3. **Short post-switch window:** Only 8 weeks of data after implementation

With more data, the confidence interval would narrow. Currently, it spans from a small negative effect to a meaningful positive one.

## **Recommendation**

Given the **directionally positive but inconclusive** evidence:

### **If implementation costs are LOW:**
→ **Proceed with phased rollout** while continuing to monitor. The risk of harm is minimal, and branches may benefit.
