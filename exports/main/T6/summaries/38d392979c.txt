## **Executive Summary**

The new shelving and stock rotation procedure shows **promising evidence of improvement** in service scores, with switched branches gaining approximately **2.3 points** (a **5% relative improvement**) compared to control branches. However, the result **does not reach conventional statistical significance** (p=0.10). The data suggest the procedure likely helps, but we cannot rule out the possibility of no effect. We recommend a **phased rollout with continued monitoring** to build stronger evidence.

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Measured Impact**

| Metric | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |

| **Switched Branches** (20 branches) | 50.25 | 52.63 | **+2.38** |

| **Net Effect of New Procedure** | — | — | **+2.31** |

### **2. Statistical Certainty**

| Confidence Level | Conclusion |
|-----------------|------------|
| **90%** | Effect is positive (CI: +0.01 to +4.61) |
| **95%** | Cannot confirm (CI: -0.43 to +5.05 includes zero) |

**What this means:** There is approximately a **95% probability the true effect is positive**, but the evidence falls short of the 95% confidence standard typically required for definitive conclusions.

### **3. Why We Don't Have Stronger Evidence**

- **Limited post-switch data:** Only 8 weeks of observations after the change
- **Natural variation:** Service scores fluctuate week-to-week for many reasons
- **Sample size:** 34 branches is moderate; more would increase precision

**This does NOT mean the procedure failed.** It means we need more time to confirm the effect with greater certainty.

- ✓ Parallel trends validated before the switch
- ✓ Practical significance is meaningful (~5% improvement)

The data provide **encouraging but not definitive** evidence that the new procedure improves service scores.
