# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure shows a **positive but statistically inconclusive effect** on service scores. Switched branches improved by an estimated **2.9 points** relative to control branches, but the statistical evidence is too weak (p = 0.11) to rule out chance. The 95% confidence interval ranges from **-0.7 to +6.4 points**, meaning the true effect could be slightly negative, zero, or moderately positive. 

**Recommendation:** Do not mandate a full rollout based on this evidence alone. Instead, extend the trial period to gather more data, or proceed with a voluntary phased expansion while continuing to monitor outcomes closely.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** switched to the new procedure at week 15; **14 branches** kept the old procedure
- Analysis compares score changes in switched branches against changes in non-switched branches

### **Statistical Certainty**

| Metric | Value | Interpretation |
|--------|-------|----------------|
| Point estimate | +2.87 points | Best guess of the effect |
| p-value | 0.11 | Not statistically significant (threshold is 0.05) |
| 95% Confidence Interval | -0.67 to +6.41 | True effect could be negative, zero, or positive |
| 90% Confidence Interval | -0.03 to +5.77 | Still includes zero |

### **What This Means**
- The observed improvement **could be real** (up to ~6 points)
- The observed improvement **could be due to chance** (p > 0.05)
- The study had **limited statistical power** due to the number of branches and weeks observed

## **Next Steps**

1. **Extend the Trial**: Continue monitoring for 8–12 additional weeks to increase statistical power and reduce uncertainty.

2. **If Proceeding with Rollout, Make It Voluntary**: Given the inconclusive evidence, allow branches to opt in rather than mandating the change system-wide.

3. **Track Implementation Quality**: Document how faithfully each branch implements the new procedure—variation in execution may explain mixed results.

4. **Set a Decision Threshold**: Define in advance what level of evidence would justify full rollout (e.g., p < 0.05 with a minimum 2-point effect).

5. **Consider Branch-Level Analysis**: Some branches may benefit more than others. Identifying characteristics of successful adopters could inform targeted rollout.
