## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure shows promising but inconclusive evidence of improvement.** Service scores at switched branches increased by an estimated 1.6 points relative to non-switched branches, with a 94% probability the effect is positive. However, this falls just short of conventional statistical certainty. We recommend **collecting 8–12 more weeks of data** before deciding on a system-wide rollout, rather than proceeding immediately or abandoning the procedure.

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Overview**
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure
- Data covers **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring in **week 15**

### **Results**

| Metric | Value | Interpretation |
|--------|-------|----------------|
| Estimated effect | **+1.6 points** | In expected direction |
| 95% Confidence interval | **-0.4 to +3.5** | Includes zero (inconclusive) |
| 90% Confidence interval | **-0.1 to +3.2** | Barely includes zero |
| Probability effect is positive | **94%** | Suggestive evidence |
| One-sided p-value | **0.062** | Close to 0.05 threshold |
| Effect size | **Small** (0.15 SD) | Modest practical impact |

### **Branch-Level Outcomes**

| Group | Improved | Declined | % Improved |
|-------|----------|----------|------------|
| Switched branches | 11 | 9 | 55% |
| Non-switched branches | 5 | 9 | 36% |

### **What This Means**

1. **The evidence leans positive.** There is a 94% probability the procedure improved scores, but we cannot claim statistical certainty at the conventional 95% confidence level.

2. **More data would clarify the picture.** With just 2–3 additional weeks of data (assuming the effect persists), we could reach conventional statistical significance.

3. **The effect is modest.** Even if real, a 1.6-point improvement represents about 3% of the average service score.

4. **Results varied across branches.** Nearly half of switched branches (9 of 20) did not improve, suggesting the procedure may work better in some contexts than others.

## **Recommendations**

### **Primary Recommendation**
**Continue the pilot and collect 8–12 more weeks of data before making a rollout decision.** This balances the promising signals against the current uncertainty.

### **Rationale**

| Option | Pros | Cons |
|--------|------|------|
| **Roll out now** | Captures benefits sooner if real; shows decisiveness | Risk of costly change if effect is illusory; 45% of pilot branches didn't improve |
| **Abandon procedure** | Avoids unnecessary change | May discard a genuinely beneficial procedure |
| **Continue pilot (recommended)** | Reduces uncertainty; identifies which branches benefit most | Delays potential benefits; requires continued monitoring |

### **Next Steps**

1. **Extend data collection through Q1 2026.** An additional 8–12 weeks would provide substantially more statistical power.

2. **Investigate variation across branches.** Understand why 11 branches improved while 9 did not. Branch size, staffing, or local factors may matter.

3. **Gather staff feedback.** Operational challenges or training gaps may explain mixed results.

4. **Re-evaluate in Q2 2026.** With additional data and qualitative insights, make a final rollout decision.
