# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** New Branch Shelving & Stock Rotation Procedure – Impact Assessment  
**Date:** November 2026

---

## **Bottom Line**

**We cannot conclude the new procedure improved service scores.** Branches that adopted the procedure were already on an upward trajectory before the switch. After accounting for this pre-existing trend, the post-switch results show **no detectable benefit**—and possibly a slight underperformance relative to what would have been expected anyway. **We do not recommend system-wide rollout based on this evidence.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Pre-Existing Trends Complicate Interpretation
| Period | Treatment-Control Gap |
|--------|----------------------|
| Week 6 (earliest) | -0.12 |
| Week 14 (just before switch) | +2.70 |
| **Pre-switch trend** | **+0.32 points/week** (p < 0.001) |

Branches that volunteered for the new procedure were already improving faster than control branches—**before** the procedure was implemented.

### 2. Post-Switch Results Don't Exceed Pre-Trend Expectations
- **Expected gap** (if pre-trend continued): +4.12 points
- **Actual gap** (observed post-switch): +3.01 points
- **Shortfall**: -1.11 points

The treatment branches did not maintain their pre-switch momentum after adopting the new procedure.

### 3. Standard DiD Estimate Is Misleading
The +2.1 point "effect" from a basic difference-in-differences model **assumes parallel pre-trends**, which is violated here. When we account for the pre-existing differential trend, the estimated effect becomes **negative** (-0.4 points).

---

## **Recommendation**

**Do not proceed with system-wide rollout** based on current evidence. Instead:

1. **Pause the rollout decision** until more data is collected
2. **Investigate why** treatment branches were on a stronger pre-trend (selection bias? motivated managers? other factors?)
3. **Consider a randomized controlled trial** for future procedure changes to avoid selection bias
4. **Continue monitoring** both groups for another 8–12 weeks to see if effects emerge with more time

---

## **Lessons for Future Evaluations**

- **Random assignment** of branches to new procedures would eliminate selection bias
- **Longer pre-period data** would help establish whether trends are parallel
- **Staggered rollout** with randomization could provide cleaner causal evidence
