# **REVISED MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure  
**Recommendation:** **Do NOT proceed with rollout based on current evidence**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**We cannot conclude the new procedure improved service scores.** While branches using the new procedure did achieve higher scores after the switch, **this improvement was already underway before they switched.** The data do not support a causal claim that the procedure itself caused the improvement.

**We recommend against rollout until stronger evidence is obtained.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### The Critical Problem: Pre-Existing Trends

| Week | Treatment Avg | Control Avg | Gap |
|------|---------------|-------------|-----|
| Week 6 (start) | 51.7 | 51.5 | **0.17** |
| Week 14 (before switch) | 54.8 | 52.8 | **2.07** |
| Week 22 (end) | 58.6 | 55.2 | **3.40** |

**The gap grew by 1.9 points BEFORE the switch.** It grew by only 1.0 points AFTER. The treatment branches were already improving faster than control branches before adopting the new procedure.

### Two Different Estimates

| Analysis | Estimated Effect | Statistical Significance |
|----------|------------------|--------------------------|
| Simple comparison (ignores pre-trend) | +2.09 points | p < 0.001 ✓ |
| **Proper analysis (accounts for pre-trend)** | **+0.23 points** | **p = 0.52 ✗** |

**The proper analysis shows no statistically detectable effect.**

---

## **Why the Initial Analysis Was Misleading**

The standard difference-in-differences method assumes treatment and control groups would have followed **parallel trends** without the intervention. This assumption is **violated** in our data:

- Treatment branches were gaining ~0.24 points/week on control branches **before** the switch
- This pre-existing trend explains most of the observed "effect"
- After accounting for it, the procedure itself shows no detectable impact

---

## **What This Means**

Possibilities consistent with the data:
1. The new procedure has **no effect** on service scores
2. The new procedure has a **small positive effect** (too small to detect with this data)
3. Branches that chose to switch were **already better performers** on an upward trajectory (selection bias)

We cannot distinguish among these with the current data.

---

## **Next Steps**

| Action | Rationale |
|--------|-----------|
| **Pause rollout** | Current evidence does not support the investment |
| **Run a randomized trial** | Randomly assign branches to new vs. old procedure to eliminate selection bias |
| **Gather qualitative data** | Interview branch staff about whether the procedure changes their work |
| **Re-analyze after 6+ months** | More post-switch data may clarify the effect |

---

## **Conclusion**

**The data do not support rolling out the new procedure.** The observed improvement in service scores was already happening before branches switched. We cannot attribute the gain to the procedure itself.

Before investing in a cooperative-wide rollout, we need stronger evidence—ideally from a randomized evaluation where branches are randomly assigned to adopt (or not adopt) the new procedure.
