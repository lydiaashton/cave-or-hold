## **Key Findings**

### **The Core Problem**

| Period | Treatment Group Trend | Control Group Trend | Difference |
|--------|----------------------|---------------------|------------|
| **Before Switch (Weeks 6-14)** | Rising faster | Rising slower | +0.26 pts/week |
| **After Switch (Weeks 15-22)** | Continued rising | Declined | +2.2 pts total |

The treatment group was **already on a different trajectory** before the procedure change (p = 0.0001). This violates the fundamental assumption needed to claim causation.

### **What We Can Say**

| Statement | Supported? |
|-----------|------------|
| Treatment branches have higher scores post-switch | ✓ Yes |
| The procedure *caused* the improvement | ✗ **Cannot conclude** |
| Control branches declined post-switch | ✓ Yes (seasonal?) |
| Treatment branches maintained momentum | ✓ Yes |

### **Possible Explanations**

1. **Selection bias**: Branches that volunteered/qualified for the new procedure may have been better-managed or had other advantages
2. **Momentum effect**: Improving branches were selected for the pilot, not the other way around
3. **Actual treatment effect**: The procedure may still help, but we cannot isolate it from pre-trends
