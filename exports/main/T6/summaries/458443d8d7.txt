Thank you for pushing back. You're right to question my confidence.

**The honest answer: No, I'm not sure.**

The key problem is that branches **self-selected** into switching. Those that chose to switch were already on an upward trend (+0.22 points/week) while control branches were declining (-0.21 points/week) **before** the procedure change. This violates the fundamental assumption needed for causal inference.

When I use the most credible method (comparing only weeks 13-16, closest to the switch), the effect is **+0.45 points with a p-value of 0.29** — not statistically significant. The 95% confidence interval is **-0.39 to +1.30**, which includes zero.

**What this means:** The data is compatible with:
- No effect at all
- A small positive effect (up to ~1 point)
- We simply cannot tell with confidence

**What we CAN say:**
- The procedure appears safe (no branches showed score declines)
- Switched branches did outperform in all 8 post-weeks
- 60% of branches voluntarily adopted it

**My revised recommendation:** Proceed with rollout, but set modest expectations (0.5-1.0 points at most), and build in rigorous monitoring to confirm whether the effect is real. The procedure may help, but the data does not prove it.
