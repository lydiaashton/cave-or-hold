# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD OF DIRECTORS**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Office  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The data suggest a positive effect, but with important caveats.** Branches that adopted the new procedure show service scores approximately **2 points higher** than the control group after the switch. However, treatment branches had systematically higher scores even before the change, which limits our confidence that the procedure itself caused the improvement. 

**Recommendation: Proceed cautiously with rollout while monitoring closely.** The evidence is suggestive but not definitive.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Overview**
- **150 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **90 branches** adopted the new procedure at week 15; **60 branches** kept the old procedure
- Analysis uses difference-in-differences methodology

### **Results**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated treatment effect | +2.0 points |
| Statistical significance | p = 0.002 |
| 95% Confidence interval | +0.8 to +3.3 points |
| Effect size | ~4% of baseline score |

### **Important Limitations**

| Concern | Finding | Implication |
|---------|---------|-------------|
| **Pre-existing differences** | Treatment branches scored ~1.5 points higher even before the switch (p = 0.065) | Groups were not perfectly comparable |
| **Selection possibility** | Higher-performing branches may have been chosen for the pilot | Effect could reflect branch quality, not procedure |
| **Placebo test** | A fake "treatment" at week 12 also shows significance (p = 0.025) | Suggests underlying trend differences |
| **Unexpected result** | Staff expected no effect on service scores | Warrants extra scrutiny |

### **What the Data Show**

The gap between treatment and control branches widened after week 15:
- **Before switch**: Treatment branches led by ~1.5 points (stable)
- **After switch**: Treatment branches led by ~3.0 points

The statistical model attributes this widening to the procedure change, but the pre-existing gap means we cannot be fully confident in this attribution.

---

## **Interpretation**

This analysis yields an **unexpected positive signal** that requires careful interpretation:

1. **Optimistic view**: The new procedure genuinely improves service quality, and the 2-point gain is real.

2. **Cautious view**: Higher-performing branches were selected for the pilot, and the apparent effect reflects this selection rather than the procedure itself.

3. **Middle ground**: The procedure may help, but the true effect is likely smaller than 2 points.

---

## **Next Steps**

| Action | Rationale |
|--------|-----------|
| **1. Approve limited rollout** | Evidence is positive but not definitive; expand gradually |
| **2. Randomize remaining branches** | Future phases should use random assignment to eliminate selection bias |
| **3. Monitor closely during rollout** | Track whether lower-performing branches see similar gains |
| **4. Re-evaluate after 6 months** | Confirm effects persist and are consistent across branch types |
| **5. Investigate mechanisms** | Understand *why* scores might improve (staff efficiency? patron experience?) |

---

## **Conclusion**

The analysis reveals a statistically significant association between the new procedure and higher service scores. However, because treatment branches were higher-performing from the start, we cannot definitively attribute the improvement to the procedure itself. A cautious, monitored rollout with randomized assignment for remaining branches is the prudent path forward.
