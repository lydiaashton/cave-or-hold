# **Library Cooperative Branch Procedure Change: Impact Analysis**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence is suggestive but not conclusive.** Branches that adopted the new procedure showed an average improvement of approximately **2 points** on the service score index. However, when accounting for the statistical uncertainty inherent in this study design, the effect is **not statistically significant** (p = 0.11). The true effect could reasonably range from a slight decline (-0.5 points) to a meaningful gain (+4.5 points).

**Recommendation: Proceed with caution.** Consider a **phased rollout** with continued monitoring rather than immediate cooperative-wide adoption.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Overview
| Component | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Branches studied** | 34 total (20 switched, 14 did not) |
| **Time period** | 17 weeks (weeks 6–22) |
| **Switch date** | Week 15 |
| **Post-switch data** | 8 weeks |
| **Analysis method** | Difference-in-differences with branch and week controls |

### Results Summary
| Metric | Estimate |
|--------|----------|
| **Point estimate** | +2.04 points |
| **Statistical significance** | p = 0.11 (not significant at 95% confidence) |
| **95% Confidence interval** | -0.49 to +4.56 points |
| **Relative improvement** | ~4% of average score (if real) |

### What the Data Shows

1. **Positive point estimate**: Switched branches improved from 48.2 to 50.3, while control branches remained flat at 51.6

2. **Substantial uncertainty**: The confidence interval is wide enough to include zero effect and even small negative effects

3. **No consistent post-switch pattern**: Week-by-week comparison shows switched branches sometimes outperform controls and sometimes underperform after the switch

4. **Limited post-period data**: Only 8 weeks of data after the switch limits statistical power

5. **Non-randomized design**: Branches were not randomly assigned to switch, so unobserved factors could influence results

---

## **Next Steps**

### Recommended Approach: Phased Rollout with Monitoring

1. **Expand to 5–7 additional branches** rather than all 14 remaining branches
   - This increases statistical power while limiting risk if the procedure doesn't work

2. **Extend monitoring period** to at least 12–16 weeks post-switch for each cohort
   - More data will narrow the confidence interval

3. **Establish decision criteria** before expansion:
   - What effect size would justify full rollout?
   - What level of statistical confidence is required?

4. **Document implementation details** from early adopters
   - Identify which specific practices drive any improvements
   - Capture lessons learned for training

### If Proceeding to Full Rollout

1. **Plan for evaluation** — build in continued data collection and analysis
2. **Set realistic expectations** — communicate that benefits are uncertain
3. **Monitor staffing** — ensure adequate resources during transition
4. **Be prepared to adjust** — have a process for reverting if scores decline
