# MEMORANDUM

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2025  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## Executive Summary

**The new procedure shows a positive effect on service scores, but with important caveats. We recommend a phased rollout with close monitoring rather than immediate system-wide implementation.**

Branches that adopted the new procedure showed service scores **2.1 points higher** (approximately 4%) than control branches after the switch. However, the evidence has limitations that warrant caution before full rollout.

---

## Key Findings

### 1. Positive Effect, But Modest in Size

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated improvement | **+2.1 points** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | 1.5 to 2.7 points |
| Statistical Significance | p < 0.001 |
| Effect Size | **0.21 standard deviations** (small-to-moderate) |

### 2. Important Concerns

| Issue | Details |
|-------|---------|
| **Pre-existing trends** | Treatment branches were already improving relative to control branches *before* the switch (gap grew from -0.54 to +1.33 points in weeks 6-14) |
| **Only 59% benefited** | 175 of 425 switched branches (41%) saw no improvement or declined |
| **Limited data** | Only 8 weeks of post-switch observation |
| **Unknown mechanism** | We cannot explain *why* the procedure works, making implementation risks higher |

### 3. Encouraging Signs

- All 8 post-switch weeks show statistically significant positive effects
- The effect **grew over time** (1.45 points in week 15 → 3.07 points in week 22)
- The effect is not explained by changes in staffing or visit patterns
- Formal pre-trend test is not statistically significant (p = 0.23)

### 4. Control Group Decline

Control branches declined by 1.5 points after week 14. The new procedure appears to have helped switched branches avoid this decline. However, we do not know what caused this system-wide pattern.

---

## Recommendation

**Proceed with a PHASED ROLLOUT, not immediate system-wide implementation.**

### Phase 1 (Next 8 weeks)
- Roll out to 50-75 additional branches (volunteers preferred)
- Provide enhanced training and implementation support
- Monitor service scores weekly

### Phase 2 (Evaluation)
- Assess whether Phase 1 branches replicate the original effect
- Investigate why 41% of original switched branches did not improve
- Identify implementation best practices from high-performing branches

### Phase 3 (Decision)
- If Phase 1 results are positive: proceed with full rollout
- If Phase 1 results are mixed or negative: pause and investigate further

---

## Monitoring Requirements

1. **Track service scores weekly** for all branches during rollout
2. **Document implementation quality** at each branch (training hours, staff feedback, physical constraints)
3. **Set a success threshold**: Require at least 60% of rollout branches to show improvement before proceeding to full implementation
4. **Extend observation period** to 6 months to assess long-term effects

---

## Bottom Line

The evidence suggests the new procedure **likely has a positive effect**, but it is not definitive. The modest effect size, substantial branch-level variation, and pre-existing trend differences mean we should proceed carefully. A phased rollout with rigorous monitoring balances the potential benefit against implementation risks.
