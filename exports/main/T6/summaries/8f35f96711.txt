# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

---

## **Subject: Impact of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure on Service Scores**

**Date:** November 2026  
**Analysis Period:** Weeks 6–22 (17 weeks)  
**Data:** 709 branches, 12,053 weekly observations

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure is associated with improved service scores, but we recommend a cautious, phased rollout rather than immediate systemwide adoption.** Branches that switched to the new procedure showed a statistically significant gain of **1.9 points** on the service score index. However, the analysis has important limitations: only 8 weeks of post-switch data, non-random assignment of branches to the new procedure, and uncertainty about long-term sustainability. A phased rollout with continued monitoring would allow the cooperative to capture benefits while managing risk.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **Study Overview**
- **425 branches** adopted the new procedure at week 15; **284 branches** retained the old procedure
- **9 weeks** of pre-switch data (weeks 6–14) and **8 weeks** of post-switch data (weeks 15–22)
- Branches were **not randomly assigned** to switch—this was an operational decision, which introduces potential selection bias

### **Service Score Results**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|:-------------:|:------------:|:------:|
| Control (old procedure) | 49.4 | 48.6 | −0.8 |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 50.8 | 51.9 | +1.1 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+1.9** |

**Important note:** Part of the measured effect reflects that control branches declined slightly after week 15, not solely that treatment branches improved. This could reflect seasonal patterns or external factors affecting all branches.

### **Statistical Confidence**
- **Estimated effect:** +1.93 points
- **95% Confidence Interval:** 0.86 to 3.01 points
- **P-value:** 0.0004
- **Interpretation:** The effect is statistically significant, but the true impact could reasonably be as modest as 0.9 points or as large as 3.0 points

### **Validation Checks**
| Check | Result | Implication |
|-------|--------|-------------|
| Parallel pre-trends | ✓ Passed | Treatment and control branches moved similarly before the switch |
| Covariate balance | ✓ Passed | Staffing and visitor volume were similar across groups |
| Random assignment | ✗ Not met | Branches self-selected or were chosen to switch; unobserved differences may exist |
| Long-term data | ✗ Limited | Only 8 weeks post-switch; sustainability unknown |

### **Practical Significance**
- Average branch scores approximately **50** on the service index
- A **1.9-point gain** represents roughly **4% improvement**
- The effect is real but modest in magnitude

---

## **RECOMMENDATION**

**Approve a phased rollout** with the following approach:

1. **Expand to 50–100 additional branches** in the next quarter (not all 284 remaining)
2. **Monitor closely** for 12 weeks to confirm effects persist and identify implementation challenges
3. **Document lessons learned** before proceeding to full systemwide adoption
4. **Revisit the board** in Q2 2027 with updated evidence before committing to complete rollout

This approach balances the opportunity to improve service scores against the risks of scaling a change with limited long-term evidence.

## **LIMITATIONS TO ACKNOWLEDGE**

1. **Non-random assignment:** Branches that switched may differ systematically from those that didn't in ways we cannot observe
2. **Short post-period:** Eight weeks may not capture long-term effects, adaptation costs, or staff fatigue
3. **External factors:** We cannot rule out that something else changed at week 15 that affected switching branches differently
4. **Cost-benefit unknown:** We have not analyzed the operational costs of the new procedure

---

## **APPENDIX: METHODOLOGY NOTE**

*Analysis employs difference-in-differences regression with branch fixed effects and cluster-robust standard errors (clustered at branch level). This approach controls for time-invariant branch characteristics but cannot address unobserved time-varying confounders or selection bias from non-random treatment assignment.*
