# Library Cooperative Board Memorandum

**To:** Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## Bottom Line

The evidence **suggests the new procedure improves service scores**, but the result is not statistically conclusive. Branches using the new procedure saw service scores increase by approximately **1.9 points** (about 4% improvement). There is roughly a **95% probability this effect is positive**, though we cannot rule out no effect at conventional confidence levels. The study was **underpowered** to detect modest improvements, meaning a real benefit may exist even though statistical significance wasn't achieved. 

**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **phased rollout** while continuing to collect data. The positive signal, combined with the likelihood that the study missed a real effect due to limited sample size, supports expanding the procedure — provided implementation costs are reasonable.

---

## Key Findings

### Study Design
- **34 branches** total: 20 adopted the new procedure (treatment), 14 kept the old procedure (control)
- **17 weeks of data** (weeks 6–22), with the switch occurring at week 15
- Analysis uses a **difference-in-differences** approach, comparing changes in service scores between the two groups before and after the switch

### Results

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| Treatment (new procedure) | 47.52 | 48.95 | +1.43 |
| Control (old procedure) | 49.28 | 48.81 | −0.47 |
| **Estimated Effect** | | | **+1.90** |

- **Point estimate:** +1.93 points improvement in service scores
- **95% confidence interval:** −0.31 to +4.12 points
- **P-value:** 0.092 (marginally significant; significant at 10% level)
- **Probability effect is positive:** ~95%

### Interpretation

1. **Evidence leans positive:** The point estimate is positive, the p-value is borderline (0.092), and there is a 95% probability the true effect exceeds zero.

2. **Study was underpowered:** With 34 branches over 17 weeks, this analysis could only reliably detect effects larger than ~3.2 points. A real 1.9-point improvement would often go undetected in a study of this size.

3. **Effect size is meaningful:** A ~2-point gain on a ~50-point scale represents a 4% improvement — potentially meaningful for patron experience if sustained.

4. **No negative signals:** There is no evidence the new procedure harms service scores; the worst plausible effect (lower bound of CI) is essentially zero.

---

## Next Steps

### Recommended Actions

1. **Proceed with phased rollout:** Expand the new procedure to additional branches while monitoring outcomes. The positive evidence outweighs the statistical uncertainty, especially given the low risk of negative effects.

2. **Continue data collection:** As more branches adopt the procedure and more weeks of data accumulate, the estimate will become more precise.

3. **Track implementation costs:** Ensure the operational costs of the new procedure are justified by the service improvement.

4. **Set a review checkpoint:** Re-evaluate after 6–12 months with expanded data. If the effect persists and remains around 2 points, consider system-wide adoption.

5. **Monitor for heterogeneity:** Track whether certain branch types benefit more than others to optimize implementation.

---

## Technical Notes

- Analysis uses difference-in-differences with cluster-robust standard errors (clustered by branch)
- Service scores average approximately 50 points across all branches; higher scores indicate better service
- The marginal significance (p = 0.092) reflects limited statistical power, not necessarily absence of an effect
