# **REVISED MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

## **New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure: Impact on Service Scores**

---

### **BOTTOM LINE**

**We cannot confidently determine whether the new procedure improved service scores.** While branches that adopted the new procedure showed higher score gains than control branches, those same branches were already improving faster *before* the switch occurred. This suggests the branches selected for early adoption may have been systematically different (e.g., better managed or more motivated) in ways that drove improvement regardless of the procedure. **We recommend additional analysis before approving a system-wide rollout.**

---

### **KEY FINDINGS**

**1. Observed Scores Increased—But Why Is Unclear**

| Period | Control Branches | Treatment Branches | Gap |
|--------|-----------------|-------------------|-----|
| Before switch (weeks 6–14) | 51.54 | 51.83 | +0.29 |
| After switch (weeks 15–22) | 52.45 | 54.57 | +2.12 |

The gap widened by **1.8 points** after the switch. However, this alone does not prove the procedure caused the improvement.

**2. Critical Problem: Pre-Existing Trends**

The treatment branches were already pulling ahead *before* adopting the new procedure:

| Pre-Period | Treatment-Control Gap |
|------------|----------------------|
| Weeks 6–10 (early) | −0.33 points |
| Weeks 11–14 (late) | +1.07 points |

**The gap widened by 1.4 points before the switch even happened.** This trend, if continued, could explain the entire observed "effect."

**3. What This Means**

- The statistical method used (difference-in-differences) requires that treatment and control branches would have followed similar trends absent the intervention
- **This assumption is violated** in our data
- Branches selected for early adoption appear to have been on a stronger trajectory already
- We cannot separate the procedure's effect from pre-existing branch differences

**4. What We Can Say**

- There is a **positive association** between adopting the new procedure and service score gains
- The association is statistically significant
- But **causation is not established**

---

### **NEXT STEPS**

| Action | Rationale |
|--------|-----------|
| **1. Do not approve full rollout yet** | Evidence is insufficient to conclude the procedure itself drives improvement |
| **2. Investigate selection criteria** | Understand why certain branches were chosen for early adoption; this may reveal confounding factors |
| **3. Consider a randomized trial** | Randomly assign remaining branches to treatment/control to establish causality |
| **4. Gather qualitative data** | Interview branch staff to understand whether the procedure changed operations in expected ways |
| **5. Extend monitoring period** | More post-switch data may help distinguish procedure effects from pre-existing trends |

---

### **ALTERNATIVE INTERPRETATIONS**

| Possibility | Implication |
|-------------|-------------|
| The procedure works | Rollout would benefit all branches |
| Only high-performing branches benefit | Rollout should target similar branches; effect may not generalize |
| The procedure has no effect | Resources would be better spent on other improvements |

The initial analysis reported a positive treatment effect. Upon deeper examination of pre-trends, we found the parallel trends assumption was violated. This revision reflects a more accurate interpretation of what the data can—and cannot—tell us.
