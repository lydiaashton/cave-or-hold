# **Memorandum to the Board**

## **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

### **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure shows a statistically significant but modest improvement in service scores.** The treatment effect is **2.1 points** (approximately **4% improvement**), which is real but relatively small. The effect also appears to diminish over time—from about 3 points in the first month to about 1 point by week 8. We recommend a **cautious, phased rollout** with close monitoring, rather than immediate system-wide adoption.

---

### **Key Findings**

| Finding | Detail | Implication |
|---------|--------|-------------|
| **Treatment Effect** | +2.13 points (p < 0.001) | Statistically significant |
| **Effect Size** | 0.21 standard deviations | Small-to-moderate practical impact |
| **Effect Over Time** | Week 15-18: ~3 pts; Week 21-22: ~1 pt | Effect may fade; needs monitoring |
| **Control Group** | Also improved 0.9 pts after week 14 | Some improvement occurs naturally |
| **Model Fit** | R² = 0.018 | Service scores driven by many factors |
| **Pre-Trends** | Groups were similar before switch | Valid comparison |

**Important Caveats:**

1. **Diminishing returns**: The gap between treatment and control branches narrowed from 3.3 points (week 15) to 1.1-1.4 points (weeks 21-22). This could indicate the effect fades, or it could be random variation with only 8 weeks of post-switch data.

2. **Modest absolute impact**: A 2-point gain on a 50-point scale may or may not be noticeable to patrons, depending on how the score translates to actual service quality.

3. **Unknown costs**: This analysis measures service scores only. Implementation costs, staff training time, and operational impacts were not assessed.

---

### **Recommendation**

**Proceed with a monitored, phased rollout—not an immediate mandate.**

The evidence supports a real positive effect, but the modest size and potential decay suggest we should:
- Implement in stages to verify the effect replicates
- Track whether the effect persists beyond 8 weeks
- Assess implementation costs before full commitment

---

### **Next Steps**

1. **Pilot expansion**: Switch 15-20 additional branches over the next 4-6 weeks and monitor closely.

2. **Extend observation period**: Track the original 90 treatment branches for another 8-12 weeks to determine if the effect stabilizes or continues to fade.

3. **Cost-benefit analysis**: Estimate staff training time, operational changes, and any costs associated with the new procedure.

4. **Patron feedback**: Supplement service scores with direct patron satisfaction measures to assess whether the statistical improvement translates to noticeable quality gains.

5. **Decision point**: After 12 weeks of additional data, reconvene to decide on full rollout based on effect persistence and cost-benefit assessment.
