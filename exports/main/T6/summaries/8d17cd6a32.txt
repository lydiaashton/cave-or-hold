# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**The data show a statistically significant improvement in service scores, though this was not expected.** Branches that adopted the new procedure showed service scores approximately **2 points higher** than would have been expected based on trends at branches that kept the old procedure. The effect is real but modest. Before deciding on a full rollout, we recommend investigating *why* a stock rotation procedure affected service scores.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Statistical Evidence Is Clear
- **Estimated effect: +2.1 points** (95% confidence interval: 0.9 to 3.3 points)
- **P-value: 0.001** — the result is unlikely to be due to chance
- The confidence interval excludes zero, indicating a genuine effect

### 2. Effect Size Is Modest
- Represents about **4% improvement** on the service score scale (average ~50)
- Equivalent to **0.21 standard deviations** — small-to-moderate in practical terms
- **73% of switched branches** improved vs. 48% of control branches

### 3. The Finding Was Unexpected
- The procedure change was designed for stock management, not service delivery
- There is no obvious theoretical reason why shelving procedures would affect service scores
- This warrants investigation before committing to system-wide implementation

### 4. Possible Explanations (To Investigate)
- Staff may spend less time searching for items, freeing time for patron assistance
- Better organization may reduce frustration and improve staff-patron interactions
- The change may have coincided with other unmeasured factors at switched branches

---

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed cautiously.** The evidence supports a real effect, but given the unexpected nature:

1. **Do investigate first** — Conduct brief interviews at 5–10 switched branches to understand *why* service scores improved
2. **Consider phased rollout** — Implement at additional branches while monitoring results
3. **Set clear success criteria** — Define what magnitude of improvement justifies the cost of system-wide change
4. **Revisit in 3 months** — Decide on full rollout after gathering qualitative insights
