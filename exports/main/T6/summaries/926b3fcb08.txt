# **Board Summary: New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence suggests the new procedure improved service scores, but we cannot precisely quantify the effect.** Branches that switched showed stronger score gains than non-switched branches, but those branches were already on a more positive trajectory before the switch. The likely improvement is **positive but smaller than the raw 2.1-point difference**—perhaps in the range of 1.0 to 1.5 points. We recommend a **cautious rollout with continued monitoring**.

---

## **Key Findings**

| Metric | Control Branches | Switched Branches |
|--------|-----------------|-------------------|
| Before switch | 48.9 | 50.7 |
| After switch | 49.3 | 53.3 |
| **Change** | **+0.4** | **+2.6** |

**Raw difference: +2.1 points** — but this likely overstates the true effect.

### Important Caveat
Switched branches were already improving faster than control branches before the intervention. This pre-existing difference means we cannot confidently attribute the full 2.1-point gap to the new procedure alone.

### What Supports a Positive Effect
- Post-switch improvements are **larger and more sustained** than pre-switch trends
- The effect **grows over time** (weeks 19-22 show the strongest gains), consistent with staff gaining proficiency
- Results hold when controlling for staffing and visitor volume

### What Limits Our Confidence
- **Parallel trends assumption violated**: Treatment and control groups had different pre-intervention trajectories
- We cannot rule out that some unmeasured factor drove both the switch decision and the score improvements

---

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with rollout, but treat it as a continued pilot with rigorous monitoring.** The weight of evidence points to a beneficial effect, but we should:

1. Roll out in phases rather than all at once
2. Establish clear success metrics before each phase
3. Be prepared to adjust or pause if results don't materialize
