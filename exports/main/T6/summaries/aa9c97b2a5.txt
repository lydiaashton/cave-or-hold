# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**The data provides evidence that the new procedure improved service scores, though the effect is modest.** Branches that adopted the new procedure showed service score increases of approximately **2 points (about 4%)** relative to branches that kept the old procedure. This difference is statistically significant (p = 0.001) and consistent across the 8-week post-switch period.

**Recommendation: Proceed with rollout, but monitor closely.** The evidence supports adoption, though expectations should be calibrated to a modest improvement rather than a transformational change.

---

## **Key Findings**

### What the Data Shows
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect | **+2.04 points** on service score |
| Statistical significance | **p = 0.001** |
| 95% Confidence Interval | **[0.81, 3.27]** |
| Relative improvement | **~4%** from baseline |

### Study Strengths
- **Comparable groups**: Treatment and control branches had similar service scores before the switch (51.4 vs. 52.1)
- **Consistent effect**: Positive differences observed in all 8 post-switch weeks
- **Robust to controls**: Effect persists when accounting for staffing and visitor volume

### Limitations to Consider
- **Modest magnitude**: The effect is statistically significant but small in practical terms
- **Observational design**: Branches were not randomly assigned; unobserved factors could influence results
- **Short follow-up**: Only 8 weeks of post-switch data; long-term effects unknown
- **Limited explanatory power**: The procedure explains <1% of score variation (many other factors matter)
