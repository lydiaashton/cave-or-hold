# **Memorandum to the Board of Directors**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** [Current Date]  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Stock Rotation Procedure  

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure produced a statistically significant but modest improvement in service scores.** Branches that switched saw scores increase by approximately **2 points (4%)** relative to branches that kept the old procedure. The effect is real and unlikely to be due to chance. However, the practical impact is modest, and we lack data on implementation costs. 

**Recommendation: Consider a phased rollout with continued monitoring, weighing the modest service improvement against implementation costs.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Treatment Effect
| Metric | Value | Interpretation |
|--------|-------|----------------|
| Estimated impact | +1.99 points | Statistically significant (p < 0.001) |
| 95% Confidence Interval | 1.0 to 3.0 points | Effect is reliably positive |
| Effect size (Cohen's d) | 0.18 SD | Small-to-moderate practical impact |
| Percentage improvement | ~4% | Modest but meaningful |

### 2. Study Design & Validity
- **Method:** Difference-in-Differences analysis (90 switched vs. 60 non-switched branches)
- **Data:** 2,550 observations across 150 branches over 17 weeks
- **Parallel trends assumption:** **Satisfied** (p = 0.95) — supports causal interpretation
- **Limitation:** Only 8 weeks of post-switch data; long-term effects unknown

### 3. Service Score Patterns

| Period | Control Branches | Switched Branches | Gap |
|--------|-----------------|-------------------|-----|
| Before switch (weeks 6–14) | 50.2 | 51.7 | 1.4 |
| After switch (weeks 15–22) | 50.1 | 53.5 | 3.4 |

The treatment-control gap widened after the switch, consistent with a positive treatment effect.

### 4. Important Considerations

| Factor | Status |
|--------|--------|
| Statistical evidence | Strong (p < 0.001) |
| Practical significance | Modest (~4% improvement) |
| Long-term sustainability | Unknown (only 8 weeks observed) |
| Implementation costs | **Not analyzed** — critical missing information |
| Potential downsides | None detected in available data |

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Assess implementation costs** — Before any rollout decision, quantify the labor, training, and resource costs of the new procedure

2. **Consider phased rollout** — Rather than immediate system-wide adoption, expand to additional branches while continuing to monitor effects

3. **Define success thresholds** — Determine whether a 4% service score improvement justifies the expected costs

4. **Extend monitoring period** — Track switched branches for 6–12 months to confirm effects persist and don't diminish

5. **Gather qualitative feedback** — Survey staff at switched branches about workload, feasibility, and any unintended consequences
