# **Memorandum to the Library Cooperative Board**

**To:** Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The data suggests the new procedure may improve service scores, but we cannot confirm this with statistical confidence.**

Branches that adopted the new procedure showed a **2.2-point higher improvement** than control branches. However, this result is **not statistically significant** (p=0.10), meaning there is approximately a 10% chance we would observe this difference even if the procedure had no real effect. The true effect could be zero, modestly positive (up to ~5 points), or slightly negative.

**Recommendation:** The board faces a decision under uncertainty. Given the positive direction of results, absence of evidence for harm, and operational expectations, a **cautious rollout with continued monitoring** is reasonable. However, the board should understand this is a **policy judgment, not a data-proven conclusion**.

---

## **What the Data Shows**

| Finding | Evidence |
|---------|----------|
| Point estimate | **+2.24 points** improvement |
| Statistical significance | **Not significant** (p=0.104) |
| 95% confidence interval | **-0.46 to +4.94 points** |
| Direction of effect | **Positive** (as expected) |
| Evidence of harm | **None detected** |

**Group Performance:**
- Switched branches: 45.0 → 47.8 (+2.8 points)
- Control branches: 48.7 → 49.2 (+0.5 points)
- Net difference: +2.2 points

---

## **What This Means**

### **We Can Say:**
✓ The observed effect is in the expected direction  
✓ The data is *consistent with* a modest improvement  
✓ There is no statistical evidence the procedure causes harm  
✓ Pre-switch trends were parallel (validating the comparison)

### **We Cannot Say:**
✗ The procedure definitively improved service scores  
✗ We have proven the new procedure works  
✗ The effect is distinguishable from random variation  

---

## **Why the Evidence Is Inconclusive**

1. **Limited sample:** Only 20 branches adopted the new procedure
2. **Short evaluation window:** Only 8 weeks of post-switch data
3. **Natural variability:** Service scores fluctuate due to many factors (staffing, visits, seasonal patterns)
4. **Effect size:** The observed effect (~2 points) is smaller than what this study could reliably detect (~4 points)

---

## **Recommendations**

### **Option 1: Phased Rollout (Recommended)**
Expand the procedure to remaining branches while continuing to track scores. This balances potential benefits against uncertainty.

| Action | Timeline |
|--------|----------|
| Roll out to remaining branches | Next 6 weeks |
| Continue weekly tracking | Ongoing |
| Re-evaluate with expanded data | 3 months post-rollout |

### **Option 2: Extended Evaluation**
Keep the current split (20 switched, 14 control) for 8-12 more weeks before deciding. This would provide stronger evidence but delays potential benefits.

### **Option 3: Full Rollout**
Adopt system-wide immediately. This is reasonable if the board weighs the positive direction and lack of harm as sufficient, but it forecloses the opportunity for clearer evidence.

---

## **Decision Framework**

| Consideration | Question for the Board |
|---------------|------------------------|
| **Risk tolerance** | Are you comfortable acting on suggestive but unproven evidence? |
| **Cost of reversal** | How difficult would it be to revert if the procedure proves ineffective? |
| **Opportunity cost** | What is the cost of delaying potential benefits while gathering more data? |
| **Stakeholder expectations** | How will staff and patrons perceive a rollout that later proves ineffective? |

- **Sample:** 34 branches, 578 observations (17 weeks each)

- **Statistical power:** ~80% to detect effects of 3.85+ points
