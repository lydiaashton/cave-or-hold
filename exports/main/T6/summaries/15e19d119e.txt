## **Why We Changed Our Assessment**

Our initial analysis focused on conventional statistical significance (p < 0.05), which the data do not meet (p = 0.11). However, this threshold is arbitrary. A more informative question is: **What is the probability the procedure actually helps?**

The answer: **97%**.

The study is **underpowered** — with only 34 branches, we cannot achieve conventional significance even for meaningful effects. The minimum detectable effect at 80% power is 4.2 points; our estimate is 2.4 points. This doesn't mean the effect isn't real — it means our study is too small to prove it by conventional standards.

## **Decision Framework**

### Option 1: Phased Rollout (Recommended)
- **Extend to 10–15 additional branches** over the next quarter
- **Continue monitoring** all branches
- **Re-evaluate in Q2 2027** with doubled sample size

**Pros:** Captures benefits sooner; builds implementation experience  
**Cons:** Some branches may receive a procedure that proves ineffective

### Option 2: Extended Pilot (Conservative)
- **Keep current 20-branch pilot** for 3–6 more months
- **Re-analyze** with more data

**Pros:** Greater certainty before commitment  
**Cons:** Delays benefits; 14 branches continue with inferior procedure if effect is real

### Option 3: Full Rollout (Aggressive)
- **Implement system-wide immediately**

**Pros:** Maximizes potential benefits  
**Cons:** Higher risk if effect proves smaller than estimated

---

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with Option 1 (Phased Rollout).**

Rationale:
1. **97% probability of positive effect** — strong evidence the procedure helps
2. **Low downside risk** — the procedure has no identified harms; reverting is feasible
3. **Opportunity cost** — 14 branches continue with the old procedure while we wait
4. **Learning value** — phased rollout generates more data while delivering benefits
