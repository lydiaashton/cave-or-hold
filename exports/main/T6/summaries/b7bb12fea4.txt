# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

---

## **Subject: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

The evidence is **inconclusive**. The new procedure shows a positive point estimate (+2.3 points, ~4% improvement), but the effect is not statistically significant at conventional levels (p=0.088). The 95% confidence interval ranges from -0.3 to +5.0 points, meaning the true effect could be zero, slightly negative, or meaningfully positive. **We recommend expanding the pilot and collecting more data before committing to a full rollout.**

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### Study Overview
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** adopted the new procedure starting week 15; **14 branches** continued with the old procedure

### Results Summary

| Metric | Value | What It Means |
|--------|-------|---------------|
| Estimated effect | **+2.32 points** | Best estimate of improvement |
| 95% Confidence interval | **-0.34 to +4.99** | True effect could be negative or positive |
| P-value | **0.088** | Not significant at 5% threshold |
| Relative improvement | **~4.4%** | If effect is real, this is the magnitude |

### What the Data Shows

1. **Switched branches** held relatively steady after week 15 (declined 0.45 points)
2. **Control branches** declined more sharply after week 15 (declined 2.78 points)
3. The difference (+2.32 points) is promising but **too uncertain to act on confidently**

### Why the Uncertainty?
- Only 8 weeks of post-switch data
- Natural week-to-week variation in service scores
- 34 branches provides limited statistical power

---

## **RECOMMENDATION**

**Do not proceed with full rollout yet.** Instead:

1. **Expand the pilot** — Invite some or all control branches to adopt the procedure
2. **Collect 8–12 more weeks of data** — This will narrow the confidence interval
3. **Re-evaluate** — With more data, we can make a clearer go/no-go decision

This approach minimizes risk while the evidence remains uncertain. If the effect is real, we'll detect it more clearly. If not, we avoid locking in a procedure that may not deliver benefits.
