# **Memorandum to the Board**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Shelving and Rotation Procedure  
**Date:** November 2026

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence is inconclusive.** Branches that adopted the new procedure show a 2.1-point improvement relative to control branches (statistically significant, p<0.001). However, these branches were already outperforming and improving faster *before* the switch, making it impossible to confidently attribute the gain to the procedure itself. 

**Recommendation:** Proceed with caution. If the board wishes to continue, implement a **properly randomized trial** to obtain credible evidence before system-wide rollout.

---

## **What the Data Show**

### **Top-Line Results**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Treatment branches (new procedure) | 425 branches |
| Control branches (old procedure) | 284 branches |
| Treatment branch scores: Before → After | 52.5 → 53.6 (+1.0) |
| Control branch scores: Before → After | 50.6 → 49.5 (−1.1) |
| **Difference-in-Differences estimate** | **+2.12 points** |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 |

### **The Complication: Pre-Existing Trends**

The treatment branches were already on a stronger trajectory before the procedure change:

| Period | Treatment-Control Gap | Weekly Change |
|--------|----------------------|---------------|
| Weeks 6-13 (pre-switch) | 1.79 points avg | +0.29 pts/week |
| Week 14 (last pre-switch) | 3.12 points | — |
| Weeks 15-22 (post-switch) | 4.05 points avg | +0.27 pts/week |

**Key observation:** The gap continued widening after the switch at roughly the same rate as before. There was **no discontinuous jump** at week 15 when the procedure changed (local effect: +0.27 points, p=0.47).

### **Two Interpretations**

| Interpretation | Evidence |
|---------------|----------|
| **The procedure works** | Treatment branches improved while control declined; DiD estimate is significant |
| **Selection bias explains results** | Treatment branches were already gaining ~0.29 pts/week; expected 8-week gain = 2.32 points, which matches the observed 2.12 points |

---

## **Why We Can't Be Certain**

This analysis relies on the **"parallel trends" assumption**: that treatment and control branches would have followed similar trajectories absent the intervention. The data show this assumption is **violated**—treatment branches were already pulling ahead before the switch.

Without random assignment, we cannot disentangle:
- The procedure's actual effect
- The continuation of pre-existing momentum at treatment branches

---

## **Recommendations**

| Priority | Action | Rationale |
|----------|--------|-----------|
| **1** | **Do not proceed with immediate system-wide rollout** | Evidence is ambiguous; full implementation carries risk |
| **2** | **Continue monitoring current pilot** | Additional weeks of data may clarify the picture |
| **3** | **Design a randomized controlled trial** | Random assignment would eliminate selection bias and yield credible causal estimates |
| **4** | **Investigate why treatment branches were selected** | Understanding selection criteria may reveal confounding factors |
| **5** | **Gather qualitative feedback** | Staff and patron experiences may complement quantitative findings |

---

## **If the Board Wishes to Proceed**

Should the board decide the potential benefit warrants implementation despite inconclusive evidence:

1. **Phase the rollout** – Implement in waves with monitoring between stages
2. **Set clear success criteria** – Define minimum acceptable improvement in advance
3. **Build in evaluation** – Allocate resources for continued data collection and analysis
4. **Plan for reversal** – Establish conditions under which the procedure would be abandoned
