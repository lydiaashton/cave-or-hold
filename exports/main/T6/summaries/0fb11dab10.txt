# **To: Library Cooperative Board**
# **From: Data Analysis Team**
# **Subject: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure – Evaluation Results**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**We cannot confidently conclude the new procedure improved service scores.** The initial analysis suggested a 2-point gain, but this appears to reflect pre-existing differences between the branches that switched versus those that didn't—not a true effect of the procedure itself. When we account for these differences, the estimated effect drops to essentially zero.

**Recommendation: Do not proceed with full rollout yet.** Additional evidence is needed.

---

## **What Changed Our Assessment**

The key assumption in this type of analysis is that treatment and control groups were on **parallel trajectories** before the intervention. They were not:

| Week | Treatment vs. Control Difference |
|------|----------------------------------|
| Week 6 (earliest) | -0.11 points |
| Week 14 (just before switch) | +2.00 points |

**The treatment group was already pulling ahead by 2 points during the pre-switch period.** This suggests the branches that chose to switch were already on an upward trajectory independent of the new procedure.

---

## **Revised Estimates**

| Model | Estimated Effect | Statistical Significance |
|-------|-----------------|-------------------------|
| Simple comparison (unadjusted) | +2.03 points | Significant (p < 0.001) |
| Adjusted for pre-trends | **-0.13 points** | **Not significant (p = 0.86)** |

Once we account for the diverging pre-trends, there's no detectable benefit from the new procedure.

---

## **What This Means**

1. **The 425 branches that switched** may differ systematically from the 284 that didn't (e.g., more motivated staff, better management, different patron demographics)

2. **The apparent improvement** likely reflects these underlying differences rather than the procedure itself

3. **We cannot rule out** that the procedure has no effect, a small positive effect, or even a small negative effect

---

## **Recommended Next Steps**

1. **Pause full rollout** until we have stronger evidence

2. **Consider a randomized trial**: Randomly assign remaining branches to switch or not switch, which would eliminate selection bias

3. **Investigate why branches chose to switch**: Understanding the selection process may reveal confounding factors

4. **Gather qualitative data**: Interview staff at switched vs. non-switched branches about implementation experience and perceived benefits

5. **Extend the observation period**: More post-switch weeks may provide clearer signal

6. **Examine other outcomes**: Service score is one metric; consider circulation, patron satisfaction, or staff time savings

---

## **Bottom Line for Decision-Making**

| If you... | Then... |
|-----------|---------|
| Require strong evidence before system-wide change | **Do not rollout** – evidence is insufficient |
| Are willing to accept some uncertainty for potential gain | **Consider limited pilot expansion** with rigorous monitoring |
| Want definitive answers | **Run a randomized controlled trial** |

---

**We apologize for the initial overconfident assessment.** The data structure made the parallel trends assumption appear reasonable at first glance, but closer examination reveals it was violated. We recommend caution before committing cooperative-wide resources to this change.
