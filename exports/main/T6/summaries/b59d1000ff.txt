# **REVISED MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Branches using the new procedure do have higher service scores, but we cannot confidently attribute this to the procedure itself.** The data shows a 2-point association, but branches that switched were already improving faster than other branches *before* the change. **We recommend a cautious, phased rollout with continued evaluation** rather than immediate system-wide implementation.

---

## **Key Findings**

### What the Data Shows

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Observed score difference (post-switch) | **+2.5 points** for switched branches |
| Simple DiD estimate | **+1.98 points** |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 |

### The Critical Limitation

**The parallel trends assumption is violated.** This is the core requirement for claiming causation in this type of analysis.

| Period | Gap Between Groups (Treatment − Control) |
|--------|------------------------------------------|
| Week 6 (start) | −0.18 points |
| Week 14 (just before switch) | +1.66 points |
| **Pre-switch gap widening** | **+1.84 points** |
| Week 22 (end) | +3.41 points |

**The gap was already widening by 1.84 points BEFORE the switch.** This means:

- Branches that switched were already on an upward trajectory
- The estimated "treatment effect" of 1.98 points may largely reflect pre-existing differences
- We cannot separate the procedure's impact from the branches' underlying improvement trend

---

## **Possible Explanations**

1. **Selection bias:** Branches that adopted the new procedure may have been more motivated, better managed, or already improving
2. **The procedure does work** — but the true effect is smaller than 2 points (we cannot quantify how much smaller)
3. **The procedure has no effect** — the observed difference is entirely due to pre-existing trends

**The data cannot distinguish between these explanations.**

---

## **Recommendations**

| Priority | Action |
|----------|--------|
| **1** | **Pause full rollout** — the evidence does not support confident system-wide implementation |
| **2** | **Continue monitoring** switched branches to see if the gap stabilizes or grows |
| **3** | **Consider a randomized trial** for remaining branches to get cleaner causal evidence |
| **4** | **Investigate why** certain branches were selected/chose to switch — were they systematically different? |
| **5** | **If proceeding**, do so as a pilot with explicit acknowledgment of uncertainty |

---

## **What Would Strengthen the Evidence**

- **Randomized assignment** of the procedure to branches (not self-selection or administrative selection)
- **Longer pre-period data** to better establish baseline trends
- **Branch-level fixed effects** to control for time-invariant differences (requires different modeling approach)
- **Qualitative data** on why branches switched and how faithfully they implemented the procedure

---

## **Conclusion**

The new procedure is associated with higher service scores, but **we cannot claim it causes the improvement**. The branches that switched were already improving faster than others. Proceeding with full rollout carries the risk of investing in a procedure that may not deliver the expected benefits. A more cautious, evidence-building approach is warranted.
