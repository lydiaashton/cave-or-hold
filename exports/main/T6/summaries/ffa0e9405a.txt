# **MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 15, 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Impact of New Branch Procedure on Service Scores  

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence is inconclusive.** While the statistical analysis shows a positive association between the new procedure and service scores, **methodological concerns prevent us from confidently attributing causation**. We recommend a **cautious, phased rollout with continued monitoring** rather than immediate system-wide adoption.

---

## **Key Findings**

### What the Numbers Show

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **Aggregate DiD estimate** | +1.8 service score points |
| **Statistical significance** | p < 0.01 (aggregate) |
| **Individual post-weeks significant** | 0 of 8 weeks |
| **Effect size** | 0.18 standard deviations (small) |

### Critical Concerns

**1. Parallel Trends Assumption Violated**

The difference-in-differences method requires that treatment and control groups would have followed similar trajectories absent the intervention. Our event study analysis shows:

- Weeks 10-13 (BEFORE the switch) showed statistically significant differences between groups
- This suggests the groups were already diverging before the procedure change
- **This undermines confidence in the causal interpretation**

**2. Inconsistent Post-Switch Effects**

| Week | Treatment vs. Control Difference |
|------|----------------------------------|
| 15 | +2.24 |
| 16 | +0.72 |
| 17 | +0.77 |
| 18 | **-0.39** |
| 19 | +2.10 |
| 20 | +2.02 |
| 21 | +1.77 |
| 22 | +1.39 |

- Week 18 shows treatment branches performing **worse** than control
- No individual post-switch week reaches statistical significance on its own
- The effect is not stable or consistent

**3. Small Practical Significance**

- 1.8 points on a ~50-point scale may not be noticeable to patrons
- We have no established threshold for what constitutes a "meaningful" improvement
- The organization's prior expectation was **no effect**—this finding contradicts that belief, warranting extra scrutiny

**4. Alternative Explanations**

- Treatment branches started with slightly lower pre-switch scores (50.8 vs. 51.2)
- Some observed "improvement" could reflect mean reversion rather than treatment effect
- Unobserved factors (staffing changes, local events) may explain the divergence

---

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with caution. Do not mandate system-wide rollout at this time.**

Instead:

1. **Continue the pilot** with the current 90 branches for an additional 8-12 weeks
2. **Gather qualitative feedback** from branch staff about the new procedure
3. **Re-analyze with more data** to determine if effects stabilize and strengthen
4. **Consider a stepped-wedge rollout** to remaining branches, allowing continued evaluation

---

## **Rationale**

| Factor | Assessment |
|--------|------------|
| Statistical evidence | Mixed (significant aggregate, non-significant weekly) |
| Methodological validity | Concerning (pre-trend violation) |
| Effect size | Small (0.18 SD) |
| Risk of rollout | Low (procedure already in use, no harm detected) |
| Risk of premature conclusion | Moderate (may adopt ineffective procedure) |

Given that the procedure is already operational in 60% of branches with no detected harm, there is no urgency to decide. The prudent course is to **gather more evidence before committing the cooperative to a system-wide change**.
