# **Revised Memorandum to the Board of Directors**

**Subject:** New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure – Impact Assessment  
**Date:** November 2026  
**Prepared by:** Data Analysis Office

---

## **Bottom Line**

**We cannot confidently conclude the new procedure caused the improvement.** While switched branches showed higher service scores after the change, they were **already improving faster than other branches before the switch**. The data is equally consistent with two explanations:

1. The procedure improved scores, OR
2. Motivated/high-performing branches chose to switch and continued their existing upward trajectory

**Recommendation: Do NOT proceed with system-wide rollout yet.** Instead, conduct a **randomized controlled trial** to isolate the true causal effect.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. The Parallel Trends Assumption Is Violated

For our analysis to prove causation, switched and non-switched branches should have been on similar trajectories before the switch. **They were not.**

| Period | Gap Trend (Switched vs. Non-Switched) |
|--------|---------------------------------------|
| **Before switch** (weeks 6-14) | +0.27 points/week *(p=0.004)* |
| **After switch** (weeks 15-22) | +0.25 points/week *(p<0.001)* |

**The gap widened at essentially the same rate before and after the procedure change.** This suggests the new procedure may have added little or nothing beyond pre-existing momentum.

### 2. What the 1.7-Point Estimate Actually Means

The initial 1.7-point "effect" likely **overstates** the true impact:

- Switched branches were gaining ~0.27 points/week on non-switched branches **before** the procedure
- Over 8 post-switch weeks, this pre-existing trend alone would produce ~2.2 points of apparent "effect"
- **Adjusted estimate: Possibly zero or even negative**

### 3. Self-Selection Bias Is the Likely Explanation

Branches chose whether to adopt the new procedure. Those that opted in were probably:
- Better managed
- More motivated staff
- Already experimenting with improvements
- More responsive to any change

These branches would likely have outperformed regardless of the procedure.

### 4. What We Can Say With Confidence

| Finding | Confidence |
|---------|------------|
| Switched branches have higher scores after week 15 | ✓ Certain |
| The procedure *caused* the improvement | ✗ **Not established** |
| Branches that volunteer for changes outperform others | ✓ Supported |
| A randomized trial would give a definitive answer | ✓ Recommended |

---

## **Revised Recommendations**

### Do Not Roll Out System-Wide (Yet)

| Action | Timeline | Responsibility |
|--------|----------|----------------|
| **1. Pause system-wide rollout** | Immediate | Operations |
| **2. Design randomized trial** with 100+ branches randomly assigned to treatment/control | 60 days | Data Office + Operations |
| **3. Implement with treatment group only** | Q2 2027 | Operations |
| **4. Measure impact over 12+ weeks** | Q2-Q3 2027 | Data Office |
| **5. Return to Board with causal evidence** | Q4 2027 | Director |

### Why a Randomized Trial?

- **Eliminates self-selection bias** – branches cannot choose their assignment
- **Provides defensible evidence** for system-wide investment decisions
- **Identifies which branches benefit most** – effect may vary by branch characteristics
- **Low cost compared to failed system-wide rollout**

---

## **Risk of Proceeding Without Better Evidence**

| Risk | Impact |
|------|--------|
| Procedure has no real effect | Wasted staff time and training resources |
| Procedure has negative effect in some contexts | Service quality declines at some branches |
| Opportunity cost | Resources diverted from interventions that would actually work |
| Credibility loss | Board and staff lose confidence in data-driven decisions |

---

## **Conclusion**

The Board's intuition that this procedure would help may still be correct. **But this dataset cannot prove it.** The branches that switched were already on a better trajectory, and we cannot separate the procedure's effect from their pre-existing momentum.

**We recommend investing in a proper randomized trial before committing to system-wide rollout.** This is the responsible approach to evidence-based decision-making.

---

*Analysis based on 12,053 weekly observations from 709 branches. Key limitation: non-random adoption of the new procedure violates the parallel trends assumption required for causal inference.*
