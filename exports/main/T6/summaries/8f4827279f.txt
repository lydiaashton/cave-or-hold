# **Memorandum to the Board**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**We cannot confidently conclude the new procedure caused the improvement.** While branches that switched show higher service scores after the change, our analysis reveals a critical issue: **treatment branches were already improving relative to control branches BEFORE the switch occurred**. This pre-existing trend makes it impossible to isolate the procedure's true effect. **We recommend against system-wide rollout until further evidence is gathered.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **The Surface-Level Result**
- Treatment branches averaged **2.1 points higher** than control branches after the switch
- This appears statistically significant (p < 0.001) using standard methods

### **The Critical Problem: Pre-Existing Trends**

| Week | Treatment-Control Difference |
|------|------------------------------|
| 6 | -1.06 |
| 9 | +0.03 |
| 12 | +0.58 |
| 14 (last pre-switch week) | +0.99 |
| 22 (last post-switch week) | +3.02 |

**The gap was already widening before the switch.** Statistical analysis shows:
- Pre-switch trend: **+0.29 points per week** (p = 0.0004)
- This trend explains most of the post-switch difference
- When we account for the pre-trend, the actual post-switch effect is **-0.30 points** (not positive)

### **What Actually Happened**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Treatment branches | 51.98 | 53.04 | +1.06 |
| Control branches | 51.88 | 50.81 | **-1.07** |

The control group's scores **declined** after week 15. The apparent "treatment effect" is largely driven by control branches getting worse, not treatment branches getting better. This could reflect:
- Seasonal patterns (later weeks may naturally have lower scores)
- External factors affecting all branches differently
- Control branches being systematically different from treatment branches

---

## **Recommendations**

| Priority | Action |
|----------|--------|
| **1** | **Do NOT proceed with system-wide rollout** based on current evidence |
| **2** | **Investigate why treatment branches were already improving** before the switch (selection bias?) |
| **3** | **Gather more data** – extend the observation period to see if patterns persist |
| **4** | **Consider a randomized pilot** – randomly assign some remaining branches to switch and others to wait, ensuring comparable groups |
| **5** | **Review the branch selection process** – were higher-performing or improving branches chosen to switch first? |

---

## **Bottom Line Restated**

The data shows correlation, not causation. Branches that adopted the new procedure have higher scores, but we cannot determine whether this is **because of** the procedure or because those branches were **already on an upward trajectory**. Rolling out the procedure system-wide based on this evidence risks implementing a change that may not deliver the expected benefits.
