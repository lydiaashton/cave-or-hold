# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure is associated with higher service scores, but the board should weigh this finding against implementation costs before approving system-wide rollout.** Branches using the new procedure showed service scores approximately **2 points higher** than expected—a statistically significant but modest improvement. Given this was an unexpected finding, we recommend a **phased expansion with continued monitoring** rather than immediate full rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **What the Data Show**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Old Procedure (Control) | 48.92 | 48.59 | −0.34 |
| New Procedure (Treatment) | 48.64 | 50.40 | +1.76 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+2.10** |

- **Statistical significance:** p = 0.001 (the effect is unlikely to be due to chance)
- **95% Confidence Interval:** +0.84 to +3.36 points
- **Effect size:** ~4% above baseline, or about 22% of one standard deviation

### **Why This Is Surprising**

The board's expectation was that stock rotation and shelving procedures would have **no measurable impact** on service scores. The data contradict this assumption. Possible explanations include:

- Staff spend less time searching for items, freeing time for patron assistance
- Materials are more readily available when patrons request them
- The procedural change signaled increased attention to service quality

However, **we have not verified the mechanism**, and the analysis does not capture potential costs (training time, workflow disruption, etc.).

### **Analytical Strengths**

✓ Parallel pre-trends confirm the groups were comparable before the switch  
✓ Effect persists after controlling for staffing and visitor volume  
✓ Consistent positive differences in all 8 post-switch weeks  
✓ Clustered standard errors account for repeated observations per branch  

### **Limitations to Consider**

⚠️ Only 8 weeks of post-switch data—longer-term effects unknown  
⚠️ Effect size is modest—may not justify significant implementation costs  
⚠️ No cost data included—operational burden not measured  
⚠️ Unexpected finding—warrants replication or deeper investigation  

---

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with cautious, phased expansion—not immediate full rollout.**

| Option | Pros | Cons |
|--------|------|------|
| **Full immediate rollout** | Captures benefits sooner | Risks costs outweighing modest benefits; no chance to course-correct |
| **Phased expansion** (recommended) | Allows monitoring; identifies implementation issues; manages resource constraints | Delays potential benefits |
| **No expansion** | Avoids implementation costs | Forgoes documented service improvement |

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Request cost estimates** from operations for system-wide implementation (training, materials, staff time).

2. **Conduct a cost-benefit analysis** comparing implementation costs against the value of a 2-point service score improvement.

3. **Expand to 15–20 additional branches** over the next quarter with:
   - Clear implementation protocols
   - Pre/post service score tracking
   - Staff feedback collection

4. **Investigate the mechanism** through:
   - Staff surveys at switched branches
   - Patron satisfaction data (if available)
   - Operational metrics (shelving time, item availability)

5. **Reconvene in 3 months** to review expanded data and cost analysis before deciding on full system-wide rollout.

---

## **Bottom Line (Restated)**

The new procedure **does** appear to improve service scores—contrary to expectations. However, the effect is modest, the finding is unexpected, and implementation costs are unknown. A **measured, evidence-based expansion** is more appropriate than immediate system-wide adoption.
