# **REVISED MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

## **Subject: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

**Date:** November 2025  
**Prepared by:** Data Analysis Team

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The evidence is inconclusive.** While branches that adopted the new procedure showed higher service scores after the switch, we cannot confidently attribute this improvement to the procedure itself. The treatment and control groups were on **different trajectories before the switch**, which undermines our ability to isolate the procedure's effect.

**Recommendation: Do NOT proceed with system-wide rollout based on this evidence alone.** Additional analysis or a more rigorous evaluation design is needed.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **1. Observed Improvement, But Causality Uncertain**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|---------------|--------------|--------|
| Switched branches (425) | 51.3 | 53.5 | +2.3 |
| Non-switched branches (284) | 51.0 | 51.2 | +0.2 |
| **Simple difference** | | | **+2.0** |

**However**, this simple comparison does not account for pre-existing differences in trends between the groups.

### **2. Critical Problem: Parallel Trends Assumption Violated**

A valid difference-in-differences analysis requires that treatment and control groups would have followed similar trends absent the intervention. **This assumption is not met:**

| Test | Result | Interpretation |
|------|--------|----------------|
| Pre-trend coefficients significant? | 5 of 8 weeks (p<0.05) | **Groups were diverging before switch** |
| Joint F-test (all pre-trends = 0) | p = 0.000 | **Reject parallel trends** |
| Post-trend coefficients significant? | 2 of 8 weeks (p<0.05) | Weak evidence of treatment effect |
| Joint F-test (all post-trends = 0) | p = 0.128 | **Cannot confirm treatment effect** |

### **3. What the Data Actually Shows**

The gap between treatment and control groups was **already widening before the switch**:

| Period | Treatment-Control Gap |
|--------|----------------------|
| Week 6 | -1.33 points |
| Week 14 | +1.46 points |
| Week 22 | +3.29 points |

The treatment group started ~1.3 points **behind** the control group and was already catching up before adopting the new procedure. This suggests:
- **Regression to the mean**: Lower-performing branches may have been assigned to switch first
- **Selection bias**: Branches chosen to switch may have been on an improvement trajectory regardless

### **4. Alternative Explanations for the Observed Pattern**

1. **Selection bias**: Branches selected for the new procedure may have been those already showing improvement potential
2. **Regression to the mean**: Branches with unusually low scores naturally tend to improve over time
3. **Unobserved confounders**: Other factors (management changes, local initiatives) may differ between groups

---

## **WHAT WE CAN CONFIDENTLY SAY**

✓ Branches that switched procedures saw service scores increase by ~2.3 points  
✓ Branches that did not switch saw minimal change (~0.2 points)  
✓ 73% of switched branches improved their scores  
✓ The improvement is **correlated with** the procedure change  

## **WHAT WE CANNOT CONFIDENTLY SAY**

✗ The procedure **caused** the improvement  
✗ The same improvement would occur if rolled out system-wide  
✗ The effect size would be consistent across all branches

## **METHODOLOGICAL NOTE**

The original analysis used a standard difference-in-differences approach, which assumes parallel pre-trends. Our event study analysis revealed this assumption was violated (p<0.001), meaning the estimated treatment effect likely captures pre-existing differences rather than the causal impact of the procedure. A randomized controlled trial or carefully matched comparison design would be needed to establish causality.
