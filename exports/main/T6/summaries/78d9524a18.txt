# **Board Summary: New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**We cannot confidently conclude the new procedure improved service scores.** While our initial analysis showed a statistically significant +2.1 point difference, closer examination reveals this finding is **not robust** and may reflect pre-existing differences between branches rather than a true treatment effect. We recommend **against systemwide rollout** pending further evidence.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. The Initial Result Looks Positive—but Is Fragile
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| DiD estimate | +2.1 points |
| Statistical significance (basic model) | p < 0.01 |
| Treatment branches that improved | 61% |
| Control branches that improved | 43% |

### 2. Critical Concerns

**a) The effect is driven partly by control branch decline**
- Treatment branches: +1.42 points
- Control branches: -0.68 points
- The "effect" is as much about control branches getting worse as treatment branches getting better. This suggests external factors (seasonality, staffing changes, etc.) may be at play.

**b) High variability across branches**
- Standard deviation of branch-level changes: ~4 points
- This exceeds the treatment effect itself (2.1 points), indicating substantial noise.

**c) Placebo test raises doubts**
- When we fake a "treatment date" at week 12 (before the actual switch), we still see a +1.25 point "effect" (p=0.13).
- This suggests treatment and control branches may have underlying differences not captured by our model.

**d) Methodological limitations**
- Branches were **not randomly assigned** to the new procedure
- Proper clustering of standard errors by branch produced model convergence issues
- Only 8 weeks of post-switch data

### 3. What We Can Say With Confidence
- Pre-switch trends were parallel between groups (supporting basic comparability)
- The service score difference is consistent across post-switch weeks
- No obvious data quality issues

---

## **Next Steps**

### Recommended Approach: **Do Not Roll Out—Gather Better Evidence**

1. **Pause systemwide implementation**. The current evidence does not justify the cost and disruption of rolling out to all 150 branches.

2. **Run a proper randomized trial**:
   - Randomly assign a new set of branches to treatment vs. control
   - This eliminates selection bias concerns
   - Minimum 12 weeks of post-switch observation

3. **Investigate the mechanism**:
   - If shelving/rotation changes affect service scores, *how*?
   - Conduct staff and patron interviews at treatment branches
   - Review whether service score methodology inadvertently captures back-office metrics

4. **Extend current monitoring**:
   - Continue tracking existing treatment and control branches
   - Determine if the effect persists, grows, or fades
