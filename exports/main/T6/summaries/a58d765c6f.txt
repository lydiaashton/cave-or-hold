# **Library Branch Procedure Change: Impact on Service Scores**

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence supports rollout, but with measured expectations.** The new procedure produced a **statistically significant 2-point improvement** in service scores (approximately 4%). While this effect was unexpected and the mechanism is unclear, there is no evidence of harm. The board should approve rollout if implementation costs are modest relative to a 4% service improvement.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Positive Effect Detected (Contrary to Expectations)

| Group | Pre-Switch | Post-Switch | Change |
|-------|-----------|-------------|--------|
| Switched branches (90) | 51.1 | 53.6 | +2.5 |
| Non-switched branches (60) | 52.3 | 52.8 | +0.5 |

**Net effect: +2.0 points** (95% CI: 1.35 to 2.70)

### 2. Statistically Robust

- **p < 0.001** – less than 0.1% probability this is due to chance
- Effect persists after controlling for staffing, visitor volume, and branch-specific factors
- Pre-switch trends were parallel between groups (validating the comparison)

### 3. Effect Size Context

- **4% improvement** over baseline scores (~50 points average)
- **0.21 standard deviations** – a modest but meaningful shift
- No detected negative effects on service scores

### 4. Important Caveat

**We do not yet understand why this worked.** The procedure change was not expected to affect service scores. This suggests there may be secondary benefits (e.g., staff efficiency, item findability) that warrant investigation before or during rollout.

---

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with rollout, conditional on:**

1. **Cost assessment** – Ensure implementation costs are proportionate to a 4% service gain
2. **Monitoring plan** – Track scores for 8–12 weeks post-rollout to confirm effect persists at scale
3. **Mechanism investigation** – Gather staff/patron feedback to understand *why* scores improved

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Quantify rollout costs** (training, materials, staff time)
2. **Approve rollout** if costs are reasonable relative to benefit
3. **Document implementation** to identify any variations that enhance or diminish the effect
4. **Schedule follow-up review** after full rollout to assess actual impact
