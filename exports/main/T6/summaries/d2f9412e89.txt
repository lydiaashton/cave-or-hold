# **Board Summary: Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence is inconclusive.** The new procedure shows a positive point estimate (+1.8 service score points), but this effect is not statistically distinguishable from zero given our sample size. We cannot confidently recommend either a full rollout or abandonment. The board should weigh the cost of implementation against the possibility of a modest benefit that this study could not definitively confirm.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Study Design**
- **34 branches** tracked over **17 weeks** (weeks 6–22)
- **20 branches** switched to the new procedure in week 15; **14 branches** kept the old procedure
- Service scores average around 50 (higher is better)

### **What the Data Shows**

| Group | Pre-Switch Avg (Weeks 6–14) | Post-Switch Avg (Weeks 15–22) | Change |
|-------|----------------------------|------------------------------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 52.31 | 49.93 | **−2.38** |
| Treatment (new procedure) | 50.48 | 49.91 | **−0.57** |

**Difference-in-Differences Estimate: +1.81 points** (95% CI: −0.5 to +3.9; p = 0.13)

### **Critical Interpretation**

The treatment group's scores **declined less** than the control group's after the switch. While this pattern is consistent with a beneficial effect, the uncertainty is too large to rule out no effect (or even a small negative effect).

**What would it take to be confident?** With our current sample, we could only detect effects larger than about ±2.5 points. A true benefit of 1-2 points would likely go undetected.

### **Week-by-Week Pattern (Event Study)**

Effects vary considerably:
- **Weeks 17 & 19**: Treatment branches scored significantly worse (−4.4 points each)
- **Week 21**: Treatment branches scored significantly better (+3.9 points)
- **Other weeks**: No significant difference

This inconsistency could reflect:
- Random variation (most likely, given small sample)
- Learning/adaptation effects over time
- Interaction with other weekly factors (staffing, visits, etc.)

### **Parallel Trends Assumption: Supported**

Pre-switch trends were similar between groups (p = 0.90), validating our comparison approach.

### **Control Variables**

Staffing levels matter: each unit increase in the staffing index is associated with about +1.0 point in service score (p < 0.01). Visit volume had no significant effect.

## **Recommendations / Next Steps**

### **Option A: Cautious Expansion (Recommended if implementation costs are low)**
- Proceed with phased rollout to additional branches
- Continue monitoring with the same metrics
- Accept that the true effect may be modest (0 to +3 points)

### **Option B: Pause and Investigate (Recommended if implementation costs are high)**
- Do not expand further at this time
- Gather qualitative feedback from the 20 branches already using the new procedure
- Extend the evaluation period to 6-12 more months

### **Option C: Targeted Implementation**
