# MEMORANDUM

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative

**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## BOTTOM LINE

The analysis shows a **positive but statistically uncertain effect**. Branches that switched to the new procedure improved by approximately 2 points relative to those that did not, but **we cannot confidently attribute this improvement to the procedure itself**. The key limitation is that switched branches were systematically different from non-switched branches from the start, which undermines our ability to isolate the procedure's true effect. 

**Recommendation: Do not proceed with system-wide rollout yet. Instead, extend the pilot and gather more evidence before making a commitment.**

---

## KEY FINDINGS

### What the Numbers Show

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| 20 branches that switched | 46.7 | 48.5 | +1.8 |
| 14 branches that did not | 50.3 | 49.8 | −0.4 |
| **Estimated effect** | | | **+2.2 points** |

### Why We Can't Be Confident

| Issue | What It Means |
|-------|---------------|
| **P-value = 0.10** | A result this large could occur by chance 10% of the time even if the procedure has no real effect |
| **95% CI: −0.4 to +4.7** | The true effect could be zero or slightly negative |
| **Parallel trends violated (p < 0.001)** | Switched and non-switched branches followed different trajectories *before* the procedure changed |
| **Systematic baseline differences** | Switched branches scored 3–5 points lower throughout the entire period, suggesting they were selected *because* they were underperforming |

### What This Means for Causal Inference

The fundamental problem: **we cannot distinguish whether the improvement was caused by the new procedure or by other factors** that differ between switched and non-switched branches. For example:

- Switched branches may have had more motivated staff
- They may have received additional attention from management
- They may have been on an improvement trajectory already
- Regression to the mean (low performers naturally improve over time)

---

## RECOMMENDATION

**Pause system-wide rollout. Extend the evaluation period and improve the research design.**

### Rationale

| Consideration | Assessment |
|--------------|------------|
| Current evidence quality | **Insufficient** for system-wide decision |
| Risk of rolling out ineffective procedure | Wasted staff time, training costs, disruption |
| Risk of delaying rollout | 14 branches continue with old procedure (moderate) |
| Value of better evidence | High — informs future operational decisions |

---

## NEXT STEPS

### Immediate Priorities

1. **Extend data collection** — Continue tracking all branches for at least 12 more weeks to capture longer-term effects

2. **Improve the comparison** — Either:
   - Randomly assign remaining branches to switch now vs. later, OR
   - Use statistical matching to create more comparable treatment/control groups

3. **Document implementation details** — Track which branches are faithfully implementing the procedure vs. those with partial adoption

### Decision Timeline

| Milestone | Target Date |
|-----------|-------------|
| Collect 12 additional weeks of data | Week 34 |
| Re-analyze with improved design | Week 36 |

### If Board Wishes to Proceed Anyway

If the board determines the potential upside justifies proceeding despite uncertain evidence:

1. **Phase the rollout** — 5–7 branches per month, not all at once
2. **Set clear success metrics** — Define what improvement level constitutes success
3. **Build in an off-ramp** — Commit to re-evaluating after 3 months with authority to pause if results disappoint
