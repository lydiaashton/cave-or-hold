# **REVISED MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Library Cooperative Board  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** January 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**We cannot confidently conclude the new procedure caused the improvement.** While branches that adopted the new procedure do show higher service scores, the evidence suggests this difference existed *before* the switch occurred. The standard analysis indicates a 2.2-point improvement, but this likely reflects pre-existing differences between branches rather than a causal effect of the procedure. **We recommend against a full rollout until further evidence is gathered.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### The Core Problem: Parallel Trends Violation

For a valid comparison, the treatment and control groups should have been on similar trajectories before the intervention. **They were not.**

| Week | Treatment-Control Gap |
|------|----------------------|
| Week 6 | 0.36 points |
| Week 10 | 1.87 points |
| Week 14 (last pre-switch) | **3.60 points** |

The gap was **already widening by 0.38 points per week** before any branch switched procedures. This suggests branches that volunteered to adopt the new procedure were already on an upward trajectory.

### Immediate Pre/Post Comparison

When we look at the actual switch point:

| Period | Treatment-Control Gap |
|--------|----------------------|
| Week 14 (last week before switch) | 3.60 points |
| Week 15 (first week after switch) | 3.66 points |
| **Change** | **+0.06 points** |

The gap barely moved at the moment of implementation.

### Why the Standard Analysis is Misleading

The standard difference-in-differences estimate of +2.24 points compares average pre-period scores to average post-period scores. But because the treatment group was already pulling ahead before the switch, this method attributes their pre-existing momentum to the intervention.

When we account for the pre-period trend, the **adjusted estimate is essentially zero** (possibly even negative).

---

## **What We Can Say**

1. **Branches using the new procedure do have higher service scores** — but we cannot determine if the procedure caused this or if better-performing branches were more likely to adopt it.

2. **No discontinuous jump at implementation** — If the procedure had an immediate effect, we would expect to see a clear break at week 15. We do not.

3. **Later post-period weeks show larger gaps** — Weeks 20–22 show gaps of 4.4–4.9 points, but this could reflect continued pre-existing trends rather than treatment effects.

---

## **Recommendation**

**Do not proceed with system-wide rollout at this time.** Instead:

| Action | Rationale |
|--------|-----------|
| **Pause expansion** | Current evidence does not support causal claims |
| **Extend the study** | Collect more post-implementation data (8–12 additional weeks) |
| **Consider random assignment** | For future pilots, randomly assign branches to treatment/control to ensure valid comparison |
| **Qualitative investigation** | Interview early-adopter branches to understand what else may have changed |
| **Re-analyze after 6 months** | With more data and potentially better identification strategy |

---

## **Lessons for Future Evaluations**

1. **Random assignment** prevents selection bias when branches self-select into treatment
2. **Pre-trend analysis** should be conducted before drawing conclusions
3. **Immediate pre/post comparison** is more credible than long-window averages when trends are diverging
