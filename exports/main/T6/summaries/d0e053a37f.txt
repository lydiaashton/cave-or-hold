# **Board Summary: Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence is inconclusive.** While branches using the new procedure show higher service scores, we cannot confidently attribute this to the procedure itself. The branches that adopted the new procedure were already improving faster than other branches *before* the switch occurred. This pre-existing difference makes it impossible to isolate the true effect of the new procedure with the current data.

**We do not recommend a system-wide rollout based on this analysis alone.** Additional evidence is needed.

---

## **Key Findings**

### What the Data Shows

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| **New procedure** (425 branches) | 51.40 | 53.99 | +2.59 |
| **Old procedure** (284 branches) | 50.45 | 50.85 | +0.40 |
| **Raw difference-in-differences** | | | **+2.19** |

### Critical Limitation: Pre-Existing Differences

The statistical method used (difference-in-differences) requires that both groups were on similar trajectories before the intervention. **This assumption is violated:**

| Week | Gap (Treatment − Control) |
|------|---------------------------|
| Week 6 | −0.83 (treatment behind) |
| Week 10 | +1.06 (treatment ahead) |
| Week 14 | +2.51 (treatment ahead) |

**The treatment group was already pulling ahead by 3.34 points during the pre-switch period.** This suggests:
- The branches selected for early adoption may differ systematically from others
- The observed improvement may reflect these pre-existing differences, not the procedure itself
- The true effect of the new procedure could be smaller, zero, or even negative

### Week 15 Anomaly

Both groups showed an unusual spike in week 15 (the switch week), with scores 7-8 points higher than surrounding weeks. This affects the analysis and suggests external factors influenced all branches that week.

---

## **Recommendation**

**Do not proceed with system-wide rollout based on current evidence.** The analysis cannot distinguish whether the new procedure caused the improvement or whether branches predisposed to improve were simply selected for early adoption.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Investigate branch selection criteria**
   - Why were these 425 branches chosen for early adoption?
   - Were they systematically different (size, location, management, baseline performance)?

2. **Gather qualitative evidence**
   - Survey staff at treatment branches about perceived benefits
   - Identify specific mechanisms by which the procedure might improve service

3. **Consider a randomized rollout**
   - Randomly assign remaining branches to adopt now vs. later
   - This would enable a cleaner causal estimate

4. **Extend the observation period**
   - Collect more post-switch data to see if the gap stabilizes or continues widening
   - Monitor whether the effect persists over time

5. **Analyze branch-level heterogeneity**
   - Do some types of branches benefit more than others?
   - This could inform targeted rather than universal rollout

---

## **Bottom Line Restated**

The new procedure is associated with higher service scores, but **association is not causation**. The branches that adopted it were already on an upward trajectory. Rolling out system-wide based on this evidence risks investing in a procedure that may not deliver the expected benefits. More rigorous evidence is needed before committing cooperative-wide resources.
