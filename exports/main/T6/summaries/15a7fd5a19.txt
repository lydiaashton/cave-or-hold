# Board Summary: New Shelving & Stock Rotation Procedure

**Date:** November 2026  
**Analysis Period:** Weeks 6–22 (17 weeks of data)  
**Sample:** 709 branches (425 switched, 284 control)

---

## Bottom Line

**Recommendation: Do NOT proceed with system-wide rollout yet. Conduct a randomized controlled trial first.**

While service scores did improve in branches that switched, **we cannot confidently attribute this to the new procedure.** The branches that chose to switch were already on an improving trajectory before the change was implemented. This violates a key assumption required for causal inference, meaning the observed improvement may reflect pre-existing differences rather than the procedure's effect.

**Estimated true effect: Approximately 0.5 points (highly uncertain), not the 2–4 points initially reported.**

---

## Critical Finding: Parallel Trends Violated

The difference-in-differences analysis relies on the assumption that treatment and control groups would have followed similar trends absent the intervention. **This assumption is violated in our data:**

| Test | Result |
|------|--------|
| Pre-trend interaction p-value | **0.0002** (statistically significant) |
| Pre-period gap slope | **+0.21 points per week** (widening before switch) |
| Gap at week 14 (last pre) | 3.05 points |
| Gap at week 15 (first post) | 2.96 points |
| **Change at switch point** | **-0.09 points** (no jump) |

**Interpretation:** The treatment group was already improving faster than controls before the procedure was implemented. Over the 8-week pre-period, this accounts for approximately 1.7 points of the observed gap. After adjusting for this pre-existing trend, the estimated treatment effect shrinks from ~2.2 points to approximately **0.5 points**.

## What the Data Actually Shows

| Metric | Initial Estimate | Revised Estimate |

| Raw DiD effect | +2.02 points | Not causal |
| Branch FE effect | +3.62 points | Confounded |
| Event study (Post - Pre) | +2.21 points | Includes pre-trend |
| **Adjusted for pre-trends** | — | **~0.5 points** |

### Event Study Pattern

```
Pre-Switch Weeks (6-14):  Gap already widening from 0.25 → 2.37 points
Switch Point (14→15):     Gap actually decreased slightly (-0.09 points)
Post-Switch Weeks (15-22): Gap continued widening to 4.43 points
```

The pattern suggests the gap was widening throughout the study period, not that there was a discrete jump at the switch point.

---

## Why This Matters

Branches **self-selected** into the new procedure. Those that chose to switch may have:
- Better management or staff
- More resources to implement changes
- Higher motivation to improve
- Other unobserved advantages

These same factors likely drove their pre-existing improvement trend. Without random assignment, we cannot separate the procedure's effect from these confounding factors.

## Next Steps

| Action | Priority | Rationale |
|--------|----------|-----------|
| **Pause system-wide rollout** | High | Evidence insufficient for confident decision |
| **Design randomized trial** | High | Randomly assign branches to new vs. old procedure |
| **Run trial for 8-12 weeks** | Medium | Allow time for effect to stabilize |
| **Re-analyze with valid causal design** | High | Only then can we estimate true effect |
| **If effect confirmed, proceed with rollout** | Conditional | Depends on trial results |

---

## What We Still Don't Know

1. **Does the procedure actually cause improvement?** Current evidence is correlational, not causal.

2. **What is the true effect size?** Could be 0, could be 2+ points. We need a proper experiment.

3. **Are there subgroups that benefit more?** Some branch types may respond differently.

4. **What is the cost-benefit ratio?** Implementation costs must be weighed against uncertain benefits.

---

## Conclusion

The initial analysis was **overly optimistic** because it did not adequately account for pre-existing differences between branches that chose to switch and those that did not. 

**We cannot recommend system-wide rollout based on this evidence.** The board should approve a properly randomized controlled trial to establish whether the procedure genuinely improves service scores before committing cooperative-wide resources.
