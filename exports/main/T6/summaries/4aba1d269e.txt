# **Memorandum to the Board**

## **Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure**

### **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure shows promise, but warrants a cautious, monitored rollout.** Service scores improved by an average of **2.1 points** (4%) in branches that adopted the change—contrary to the expectation of no effect. However, **27% of switching branches saw their scores decline**, suggesting the procedure's impact varies by branch. We recommend proceeding with rollout, but with close monitoring and support for struggling branches.

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. Positive Average Effect—With Important Variation**
- **Average improvement**: +2.1 points (95% CI: 1.2 to 3.0 points)
- **73% of switching branches** improved; **27% declined**
- Control branches showed essentially no change (+0.1 points)
- Effect was **persistent across all 8 weeks** post-switch, with no sign of fading

**2. Results Are Statistically Robust**
- Highly significant (p < 0.001) after controlling for branch differences, staffing, and visitor volume
- Pre-switch trends were parallel between groups, supporting the validity of the comparison

**3. Why the Unexpected Result?**
- The procedure change was not expected to affect service scores
- The positive effect suggests the new rotation/shelving method may improve patron experience in ways not anticipated
- The variation in effects (some branches declined) suggests **branch-specific factors** moderate the impact—possibly size, layout, staffing, or patron demographics

**4. Limitations to Consider**
- Only **8 weeks of post-switch data**—long-term effects unknown
- **One outcome measure** (service score); other metrics (costs, staff satisfaction, efficiency) not analyzed
- **27% of branches saw declines**—understanding why is critical before full rollout

---

### **Next Steps**

1. **Proceed with rollout, but phased**—implement in waves to allow for monitoring and course-correction

2. **Investigate heterogeneous effects**—analyze which branch characteristics predict success or decline with the new procedure

3. **Establish monitoring thresholds**—flag any branch whose score drops more than X points post-implementation for immediate support

4. **Track additional metrics**—staff time, operational costs, patron complaints—to ensure no unintended negative consequences

5. **Document implementation practices** from high-performing switching branches to support training

6. **Re-evaluate after 6 months**—assess whether effects persist and whether the 27% decline rate has been reduced through learning and support

---

### **Analytical Approach**

Difference-in-differences design comparing 90 switching branches against 60 control branches over 17 weeks. Pre-trend tests confirm parallel trends before the switch (p = 0.21), supporting causal interpretation.
