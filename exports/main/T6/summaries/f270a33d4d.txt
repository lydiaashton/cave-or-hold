## **The Parallel Trends Assumption Is Violated**

The event study showed that **all 8 pre-period weeks had statistically significant negative coefficients** (ranging from -3.9 to -12.9 points, all p < 0.01). The joint F-test for pre-trends had a p-value of 0.0000.

This means the treatment and control groups were **not on parallel trajectories before the switch**. Looking at the raw weekly differences:

| Period | Average Difference (Treatment − Control) |
|--------|------------------------------------------|
| Weeks 6–13 | −0.48 (treatment was *lower*) |
| Week 14 | +0.33 (treatment was *higher*) |
| Weeks 15–22 | +1.81 (treatment was *higher*) |

The treatment group was actually underperforming relative to control in most pre-period weeks. The apparent "improvement" could reflect:
- **Mean reversion** (treatment group returning to normal after a slump)
- **Pre-existing differences** between groups rather than the procedure change
- **Week 14 being an outlier** that distorts the comparison

## **Revised Bottom Line**

**The evidence is inconclusive.** While branches that switched procedures showed higher service scores afterward, we cannot confidently attribute this to the procedure change itself because the treatment and control groups were on different trajectories before the switch. The observed ~2-point improvement may reflect pre-existing differences rather than a causal effect.

**Recommendation:** Do **not** proceed with system-wide rollout based on this evidence alone. Instead:

1. **Extend the evaluation period** to gather more pre- and post-switch data
2. **Consider a randomized rollout** to remaining branches to create a cleaner comparison
3. **Investigate why** treatment branches underperformed in the pre-period
4. **Pilot at additional branches** with proper random assignment before full implementation
