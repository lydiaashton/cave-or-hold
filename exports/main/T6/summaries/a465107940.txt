# **MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure  
**Recommendation:** Approve Rollout with Monitoring Conditions

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure improved service scores on average, but the effect is modest and not universal.** Branches that adopted the new procedure saw service scores increase by **1.85 points** (3.6%) compared to branches that kept the old procedure. This is statistically significant but represents a small absolute improvement. Importantly, **about 1 in 4 treatment branches showed no benefit**. We recommend **conditional approval**: proceed with rollout but implement monitoring to identify and address branches that don't benefit.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### What the Data Show

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Treatment effect (DiD estimate) | +1.85 points |
| 95% Confidence Interval | [0.97, 2.72] |
| Statistical significance | p < 0.001 |
| Effect size | 3.6% of baseline, 0.18 standard deviations |
| Branches with positive effect | 76% of treatment branches |
| Branches with no/negative effect | 24% of treatment branches |

### What This Means

- **The effect is real**: The statistical evidence is strong; this is unlikely to be random variation
- **The effect is modest**: A 1.85-point improvement on a ~50-point scale is meaningful but not transformative
- **The effect is not universal**: Roughly 1 in 4 branches saw no improvement, suggesting implementation or contextual factors matter

### Study Strengths

- Strong research design (difference-in-differences with 150 branches)
- Parallel pre-switch trends support causal interpretation
- Effect persisted across all 8 post-switch weeks
- Result holds after controlling for staffing and visitor volume

### Limitations & Uncertainties

- We don't know implementation costs (training, materials, disruption)
- We don't know if the effect will persist long-term
- We don't know why 24% of branches didn't benefit
- The effect size may not justify rollout if costs are high

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

| Action | Rationale |
|--------|-----------|
| **1. Approve phased rollout** | Start with 25-30% of remaining branches to monitor real-world implementation |
| **2. Define success metrics** | Set minimum improvement threshold (e.g., +1 point) to evaluate each branch |
| **3. Investigate non-responders** | Analyze why ~24% of treatment branches didn't benefit; identify modifiable factors |
| **4. Track costs** | Document training time, materials, and any service disruption during transition |
| **5. Re-evaluate after 6 months** | Assess whether effects persist and whether costs justify benefits |

---

## **DECISION FRAMEWORK FOR THE BOARD**

| If... | Then... |
|-------|---------|
| Implementation costs are low | Rollout is likely worthwhile |
| Implementation costs are high | Consider targeting only branches similar to the 76% that benefited |
| Long-term persistence is uncertain | Build in periodic re-assessment |
| Equity across branches is a priority | Investigate and address the 24% that didn't benefit |

---

## **CONCLUSION**

The original expectation—that the procedure change would make no difference—was incorrect. The data show a **real, positive effect**. However, the effect is **modest in magnitude** and **not universal across branches**. 

**Recommendation:** Approve the rollout with explicit monitoring conditions. This allows the cooperative to capture the average benefit while learning why some branches don't benefit and whether the improvement justifies the investment.
