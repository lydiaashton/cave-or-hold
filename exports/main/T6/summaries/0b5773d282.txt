## **Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

### **Bottom Line**
We cannot confidently conclude the new procedure improved service scores. While a simple analysis suggests a +1.9 point benefit, **deeper examination reveals serious concerns**: treatment branches had systematically lower scores before the switch, and week-by-week effects are mostly negative after the initial two weeks. We recommend **pausing the rollout** pending further analysis.

---

### **Key Findings**

**1. The Initial Result Is Misleading**
- Simple analysis shows +1.86 points (p=0.001)
- **However**, this relies on the assumption that treatment and control branches would have followed parallel trends—which they did not

**2. Pre-Existing Differences Between Groups**
- Treatment branches had **consistently lower service scores** than control branches throughout the pre-switch period
- Average pre-switch gap: approximately 4 points lower for treatment branches
- This violates the key assumption needed to interpret the DiD as a causal effect
- The "positive" result may reflect **mean reversion** (lower-scoring branches naturally improving) rather than a treatment effect

**3. Week-by-Week Effects Are Mostly Negative**
| Period | Average Effect |
|--------|---------------|
| Weeks 15-16 (immediate) | +1.15 |
| Weeks 17-22 (sustained) | -2.33 |
| **All 8 weeks combined** | **-1.46** |

- Only the first two weeks show positive effects
- Weeks 17, 19, and 21 show **significantly negative** effects
- This pattern suggests any initial benefit did not persist

**4. What We Actually Know**
- Treatment branches went from 0.9 points *below* control (pre) to 0.9 points *above* control (post)
- But because treatment branches were systematically different from the start, we cannot attribute this to the procedure
- The event study—which makes fewer assumptions—shows mostly negative effects

---

### **Next Steps**

1. **Do NOT proceed with systemwide rollout** at this time

2. **Investigate why treatment and control branches differed** before the switch
   - Were different types of branches selected for early adoption?
   - Are there unmeasured factors (staff experience, facility age, community demographics)?

3. **Extend the observation period** to determine if negative later-week effects persist or reverse

4. **Consider a randomized rollout** for remaining branches to enable cleaner causal inference

5. **Return to the board in 8-12 weeks** with:
   - Extended time-series data
   - Analysis of branch selection bias
   - Clearer evidence on whether effects persist
