# **Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure Evaluation**

---

## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure shows a **positive effect of approximately 2.6 points** on service scores, but the evidence is **not statistically conclusive** at conventional confidence levels. The 95% confidence interval ranges from -0.4 to +5.7 points, meaning we cannot rule out no effect. However, there is roughly a **95% probability the true effect is positive**, and the result would be significant if we had pre-specified a directional hypothesis (which aligns with the board's expectation of improvement).

**Recommendation:** Proceed with a **cautious, monitored expansion** rather than full immediate rollout. Collect more data to strengthen the evidence base before committing cooperative-wide.

### **What This Means**

- **Best case:** The new procedure improves scores by up to 5-6 points
- **Most likely:** The improvement is around 2-3 points
- **Worst case (within 95% confidence):** The procedure has no effect or a negligible negative effect
- **Probability the effect is actually positive:** ~95%

## **Recommendations**

### **1. Do NOT Approve Full Rollout Yet**
The evidence is promising but does not meet the conventional threshold for statistical certainty. A full cooperative-wide commitment is premature.

### **2. Expand to Additional Branches (Phased Approach)**
Given the high probability of a positive effect and the relatively low implementation risk, extend the new procedure to 5-10 additional branches while continuing to monitor.

### **3. Extend the Evaluation Period**
Eight weeks post-switch provides limited statistical power. Collect **8-12 more weeks of data** to narrow the confidence interval and reach a more definitive conclusion.

### **4. Set a Clear Decision Threshold**
Reconvene when:
- The 95% confidence interval no longer includes zero, **OR**
- At least 16 weeks of post-switch data are available

Then decide on full rollout based on stronger evidence.

### **5. Document Implementation Variations**
Track how different branches implement the procedure. If some achieve larger gains than others, identify and share best practices.
