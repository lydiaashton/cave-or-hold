## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence suggests the new procedure likely improved service scores, but with meaningful uncertainty.** Branches that adopted the new procedure showed an average improvement of approximately **2 points** (about 4%), and this effect is statistically significant. However, week-to-week results were inconsistent, and we cannot rule out a smaller true effect. We recommend a **phased rollout** with continued monitoring rather than immediate system-wide adoption.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Estimated Impact
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated improvement | **+2.05 points** |
| 95% confidence interval | +0.3 to +3.8 points |
| Statistical significance | p = 0.02 |
| Relative improvement | ~4% |

### Important Caveats
- **Inconsistent weekly results**: In 2 of 8 post-switch weeks, treatment branches performed worse than control branches
- **Pre-existing differences**: Treatment branches had slightly lower scores than control branches before the switch, which may inflate the estimated effect
- **Substantial variability**: Week-to-week fluctuations are large relative to the estimated effect

### Study Overview
- **34 branches**: 20 adopted the new procedure, 14 maintained the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data**: Weeks 6–22; procedure change at week 15
- Analysis controls for branch characteristics, staffing, and visitor volume

---

## **Recommendations**

1. **Proceed with cautious expansion** – Extend the new procedure to additional branches, but not all at once
2. **Continue monitoring** – Track service scores closely for 8–12 weeks at each new branch
3. **Set clear success criteria** – Define what magnitude of improvement justifies full rollout (e.g., consistent 1.5+ point gains)
4. **Document implementation factors** – Record which branches adopt the procedure most faithfully to identify best practices
5. **Re-evaluate in 3 months** – With more data, reassess whether to proceed with system-wide adoption or modify the procedure
