# **Board Memorandum (Revised)**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2025  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence suggests the new procedure improved service scores, but we cannot be as confident as we hoped.** 

Branches that adopted the new procedure showed service scores **1.0 to 1.8 points higher** than expected, but a critical limitation complicates this conclusion: the switched branches were already improving relative to non-switched branches *before* the procedure changed. This makes it difficult to isolate how much of the improvement is due to the new procedure versus pre-existing trends.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with caution. The weight of evidence points to a positive effect, but the magnitude is uncertain. Consider a phased rollout with continued monitoring.

---

## **What We Found**

| Metric | Result |
|--------|--------|
| **Raw improvement estimate** | +1.8 points |
| **Adjusted estimate** | +1.0 to 1.3 points |
| **Statistical significance** | Yes (p < 0.001) |
| **Key limitation** | Pre-existing upward trend in switched branches |

---

## **The Complication**

**The switched branches were already outperforming before the change.**

| Period | Gap (Switched − Non-Switched) |
|--------|-------------------------------|
| Week 6 | +0.3 points |
| Week 10 | +1.7 points |
| Week 14 (last pre-switch) | +2.6 points |
| Week 18 | +4.2 points |
| Week 22 (last observed) | +3.8 points |

The gap was widening by **0.29 points per week** even before the switch (p < 0.001). This violates a key assumption of our analysis method and means the 1.8-point estimate likely overstates the true effect.

---

## **Why We Still Think There's a Real Effect**

1. **Post-switch gaps remain elevated** — All 8 post-switch weeks show gaps above 2.6 points, higher than any pre-switch week.

2. **Immediate pre/post comparison** — Comparing the 4 weeks immediately before and after the switch shows a **1.1-point increase** in the gap, which is more conservative and may better reflect the true effect.

3. **Consistency across branches** — The effect is observed across many branches, not driven by a few outliers.

4. **Controls don't explain it** — The result holds after accounting for staffing levels and visitor volume.

---

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with a phased rollout, not a full immediate rollout.**

| Action | Rationale |
|--------|-----------|
| Roll out to additional branches | Evidence points to positive effect |
| Monitor closely for 3–6 months | Confirm effect persists and quantify more precisely |
| Track implementation fidelity | Ensure branches are actually adopting the procedure as designed |
| Be prepared to adjust | If effects are smaller than expected, refine the procedure |

---

## **What We Need to Learn**

1. **Why were switched branches already improving?** Were they systematically different (e.g., better management, more resources, more motivated staff)?

2. **Does the effect persist?** We only have 8 weeks of post-switch data.

3. **What is the true causal effect?** A more rigorous evaluation (e.g., randomized rollout) would provide clearer answers.

---

## **Bottom Line for Decision-Making**

| If the Board... | Then... |
|-----------------|---------|
| Is risk-tolerant and values innovation | Proceed with rollout; likely benefit outweighs uncertainty |
| Is risk-averse and needs certainty | Pilot further; gather more data before full commitment |
| Wants a middle ground | **Phased rollout with monitoring** (recommended) |
