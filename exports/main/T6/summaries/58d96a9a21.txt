**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure provided measurable protection against service score decline.** Contrary to our initial expectation that the change would make no difference, the data shows a clear effect: branches using the new procedure declined only slightly (-0.3 points) while control branches dropped substantially (-2.2 points). The net benefit is **+1.9 points of decline avoided**, which is statistically significant (p = 0.002). However, the procedure did not improve scores in absolute terms—both groups declined. **We recommend rollout, but with tempered expectations: the benefit is protective rather than enhancing.**

### Critical Interpretation
- **Did scores improve?** No—treatment branches also declined, just less severely
- **What was the benefit?** The new procedure shielded branches from approximately 1.9 points of decline that affected the control group
- **Why did everyone decline?** Something affected the entire cooperative after week 15 (possibly seasonal factors, external conditions, or measurement changes)
- **Is the effect real?** Yes—statistically significant (p = 0.002) with a 95% confidence interval of +0.7 to +3.1 points

### Effect Size Context
- **Magnitude:** 1.9 points on a ~50-point scale (approximately 4%)
- **Relative to variability:** About 0.22 standard deviations—a modest but meaningful effect
- **Practical value:** Preventing decline may be as valuable as generating improvement, depending on board priorities
