You raise a fair question. Let me be more precise about what the evidence actually tells us:

**What I'm confident about:**
- The point estimate is +2.18 points (positive)
- There's no evidence of harm
- 65% of treatment branches improved

**What I'm NOT confident about:**
- The p-value of 0.089 means we **cannot reject the possibility of no effect** at conventional standards
- The 95% confidence interval [-0.34, 4.70] **includes zero**
- With only 34 branches, this study is **underpowered** to detect modest effects reliably

**The honest bottom line:**

> The data **suggests** the new procedure may improve service scores by about 2 points, but the evidence is **too weak to be certain**. The result is consistent with both a meaningful improvement AND with no effect at all. Given the low risk and potential upside, a cautious expansion is reasonable—but the board should not expect the same confidence they had before seeing the data.

**Should I revise my recommendation?**

I think "phased rollout" is still appropriate because:
1. No evidence of harm
2. Potential upside is meaningful (4% improvement)
3. Cost of monitoring is modest
4. We'll learn more with expanded implementation

But I should be clearer that this is **exploratory**, not confirmatory. The board is essentially approving a **larger pilot**, not a proven improvement.
