# **REVISED MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD OF DIRECTORS**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** November 2026  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Executive Summary**

**We cannot confidently conclude that the new procedure caused the observed improvement in service scores.** While branches that switched procedures showed higher scores after the change, those same branches were **already improving relative to other branches before they switched**. This pre-existing trend makes it impossible to isolate the procedure's true effect from the characteristics of branches that chose to adopt early.

**Recommendation:** **Do NOT proceed with system-wide rollout yet.** Instead, conduct a **randomized controlled trial** or gather more data with better identification before making a cooperative-wide decision.

---

## **Critical Finding: The Parallel Trends Problem**

The standard analysis method (Difference-in-Differences) requires that treatment and control branches would have followed similar trends absent the intervention. **This assumption is violated in our data.**

### **Pre-Switch Gap Between Groups**

| Week | Treatment − Control Gap |
|------|------------------------|
| 6 | −0.11 points |
| 8 | −0.08 points |
| 10 | +0.92 points |
| 12 | +1.38 points |
| 14 | +2.00 points |

**The gap grew by 2.11 points during the pre-switch period alone.** Branches that volunteered to switch were already pulling away from control branches before the procedure began.

### **Post-Switch Gap**

| Week | Treatment − Control Gap |
|------|------------------------|
| 15 | +1.92 points |
| 17 | +2.66 points |
| 20 | +2.83 points |
| 22 | +3.63 points |

**Concerning observation:** The gap actually **decreased slightly** from Week 14 (2.00) to Week 15 (1.92) — the first week after the switch.

---

## **What This Means**

### **The 2.0-Point Estimate Is Likely Inflated**

The standard DiD estimate of +2.03 points attributes **all** post-switch gap growth to the procedure. But our analysis shows:

1. **Pre-trend extrapolation:** If the pre-switch trend had continued, the gap at Week 22 would have been ~4.1 points. The actual gap was 3.6 points — suggesting the procedure may have had **no additional effect** or even a slightly negative one.

2. **Selection bias:** Early-adopter branches likely differ systematically from non-adopters (more motivated staff, better management, more resources). These unmeasured factors explain both their willingness to switch AND their better performance.

3. **Control group decline:** Control branches' scores dropped 1.15 points post-switch. This could reflect demoralization from not being selected, external factors, or random variation — but it inflates the apparent treatment effect.

### **Honest Assessment of the Evidence**

| Possibility | Plausibility |
|-------------|--------------|
| Procedure has large positive effect (~2 points) | **Low** — pre-trends explain most of the gap |
| Procedure has modest positive effect (~0.5-1 point) | **Possible** — cannot rule out |
| Procedure has no effect | **Possible** — gap growth matches pre-trend |
| Procedure has negative effect | **Possible** — Week 15 gap dipped |

---

## **Recommendations**

### **1. Pause System-Wide Rollout**

Given the uncertainty, rolling out to all 709 branches risks:
- Implementing a procedure that may not work
- Disruption costs with no guaranteed benefit
- Missing the opportunity to learn what actually drives improvement

### **2. Conduct a Randomized Controlled Trial**

The cleanest way to answer this question:
- Randomly assign ~100 branches to switch now
- Randomly assign ~100 branches to remain on old procedure (control)
- Track scores for 12+ weeks
- This eliminates selection bias

### **3. If Rollout Proceeds, Implement Rigorous Monitoring**

If the Board decides to proceed despite uncertainty:
- Roll out in small waves (50-100 branches per wave)
- Compare each wave to contemporaneous non-switched branches
- Be prepared to halt if effects don't materialize
- Set a minimum effect threshold (e.g., +1.0 point) for continuation

### **4. Investigate Why Early Adopters Were Different**

Understanding what distinguished early-adopter branches could:
- Help target the procedure to branches most likely to benefit
- Reveal whether the procedure works only under certain conditions
- Inform implementation support needs

---

## **Bottom Line (Revised)**

**The data does not support a confident conclusion that the new procedure improves service scores.** The observed improvement is real, but we cannot attribute it to the procedure rather than to pre-existing differences between early-adopter and other branches.

**We recommend against system-wide rollout until stronger evidence is obtained.**
