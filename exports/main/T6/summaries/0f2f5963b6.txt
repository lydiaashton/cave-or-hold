# **REVISED Board Memorandum**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Office  
**Subject:** Assessment of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**We cannot confidently conclude the new procedure caused the improvement.** While service scores did increase more in switched branches, those branches were **already on a better trajectory before the switch**. The observed improvement may reflect pre-existing differences rather than the procedure itself.

**Recommendation: Do not proceed with full rollout yet. Gather stronger evidence first.**

---

## **Critical Finding: Parallel Trends Violation**

The difference-in-differences method requires that treatment and control groups follow similar trends *before* the intervention. **This assumption is violated in our data:**

| Metric | Control Branches | Treatment Branches |
|--------|-----------------|-------------------|
| Pre-treatment trend (weeks 6-14) | -0.43 points/week | -0.22 points/week |
| **Difference in slopes** | | **+0.21 points/week (p=0.003)** |

The treatment group was already improving faster than control **before** the switch occurred. This means:

- The gap between groups was **widening before the intervention** (from 0.7 points in week 6 to 2.5 points in week 14)
- Much of the post-switch gap increase likely reflects **continuation of this pre-existing trend**, not the procedure itself
- The naive 2.1-point estimate is probably **substantially overstated**

---

## **What the Data Actually Shows**

| Period | Treatment-Control Gap |
|--------|----------------------|
| Week 6 | 0.7 points |
| Week 14 (pre-switch) | 2.5 points |
| Week 22 (post-switch) | 5.2 points |

The gap grew by 1.8 points before the switch and 2.7 points after. **We cannot disentangle how much is due to the procedure versus the pre-existing trend.**

---

## **Recommendations**

1. **Pause system-wide rollout** until we have stronger causal evidence

2. **Consider a randomized rollout**: Randomly assign remaining branches to switch now vs. later. This would restore the validity of the comparison.

3. **Alternative: Staggered rollout analysis**: Use branches that haven't switched yet as a cleaner control group with more recent methods (e.g., Callaway-Sant'Anna estimator)

4. **If proceeding anyway**: Frame expectations cautiously—the true effect could be near zero, or it could be positive but smaller than 2 points

---

## **Bottom Line for Decision-Makers**

The data is **suggestive but not conclusive**. The branches that chose (or were chosen) to switch early may have been systematically different—perhaps better-managed, better-staffed, or more motivated to improve. Without random assignment, we cannot separate selection effects from the procedure's actual impact.

**Prudent course: Test with a randomized subset before committing system-wide resources.**
