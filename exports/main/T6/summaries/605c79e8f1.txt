# **REVISED MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The evidence is mixed.** Branches using the new procedure had service scores about **2 points higher** than expected based on control branches, but there are important caveats:

1. **Scores declined across the system** — even branches with the new procedure saw absolute scores drop
2. **Treatment branches were already improving faster** before the switch, which complicates the analysis
3. **We cannot confirm the improvement was caused by the procedure** rather than pre-existing differences between branches

**Recommendation: Proceed with caution.** Consider a phased rollout with careful monitoring rather than immediate system-wide adoption.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### **1. What the Numbers Show**

| Group | Before Switch | After Switch | Change |
|-------|--------------|--------------|--------|
| New procedure branches | 53.02 | 51.91 | **-1.11** |
| Old procedure branches | 51.98 | 48.87 | **-3.10** |
| **Difference** | | | **+1.96** |

The new procedure branches declined **less** than control branches, resulting in a 2-point relative advantage. However, **their scores still fell in absolute terms.**

### **2. Critical Concern: Pre-Existing Trends**

Treatment branches were already gaining on control branches **before** the switch:

| Week | Gap (Treatment − Control) |
|------|--------------------------|
| Week 6 | -0.56 |
| Week 10 | +1.73 |
| Week 14 | +2.40 |
| Week 22 | +3.42 |

The gap widened by ~1.8 points before the switch and ~1.0 points after. This suggests **part of the "effect" may reflect pre-existing differences**, not the procedure itself.

### **3. Why This Matters**

We don't know how branches were selected for the new procedure:
- **If assigned randomly**: The 2-point estimate is reasonably credible
- **If branches volunteered**: Better-managed branches may have opted in, and their superior performance might reflect management quality, not the procedure

The data cannot distinguish between these scenarios.

### **4. Effect Size Context**

- **2 points** on a ~52-point average scale = **~4% improvement**
- **0.20 standard deviations** = small-to-moderate effect
- **Not large enough** to overcome system-wide score declines

---

## **NEXT STEPS**

| Action | Rationale |
|--------|-----------|
| **1. Do NOT approve immediate system-wide rollout** | Evidence is insufficiently conclusive |
| **2. Request information on selection process** | Was switching voluntary or assigned? This affects interpretation |
| **3. Extend the study period** | More post-switch data would strengthen conclusions |
| **4. Consider a randomized pilot** | Randomly assign remaining branches to isolate the true effect |
| **5. Investigate system-wide score decline** | All branches scored lower after week 15 — this needs attention regardless of procedure |

---

## **REVISED RECOMMENDATION**

**Pause and gather more evidence before system-wide rollout.** The statistical analysis shows a positive association between the new procedure and service scores, but we cannot confidently attribute this to the procedure itself given:

- Pre-existing trend differences between groups
- Unknown selection mechanism (voluntary vs. assigned)
- Absolute score declines even in treatment branches

**Suggested path forward:**
1. Clarify how branches were selected for the new procedure
2. Run a randomized controlled trial with remaining branches if feasible
3. Monitor current treatment branches for 8-12 more weeks to see if effects persist
4. Investigate what drove the system-wide score decline after week 15

The new procedure **may** be beneficial, but the board should not commit to system-wide implementation based on this evidence alone.

---

*Analysis based on branches.csv containing weekly service scores for 709 branches, weeks 6-22. Difference-in-differences regression with branch-clustered standard errors.*
