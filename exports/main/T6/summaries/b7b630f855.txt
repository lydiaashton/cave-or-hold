# **Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure Evaluation**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure improved service scores by 2 points (4%), and the evidence is statistically solid.** However, whether this justifies system-wide rollout depends on implementation costs we haven't measured. We recommend **proceeding with rollout IF the new procedure does not increase operational costs**. If it requires additional staff time or resources, the modest 2-point gain may not justify the expense.

---

## **Key Findings**

### What We Know

| Finding | Evidence |
|---------|----------|
| **Statistically significant improvement** | +2.0 points, p < 0.001, 95% CI [1.3 to 2.8] |
| **Effect is modest in practical terms** | 0.2 standard deviations; 4% of baseline |
| **Effect is stable** | Consistent across all 8 post-switch weeks |
| **Causal interpretation is credible** | Pre-switch trends were parallel; reversal in treatment-control gap occurred at switch |

### What We Don't Know

| Question | Status |
|----------|--------|
| Does the new procedure require more staff time? | Not measured |
| Are there training or implementation costs? | Not measured |
| Will the effect persist beyond 8 weeks? | Unknown |
| Will remaining 60 branches see the same gain? | Plausible but untested |

---

## **Recommendation**

**Conditional approval for rollout:**

1. **If the new procedure is cost-neutral or cost-saving** → Proceed with rollout. A 4% service improvement at no additional cost is a clear win.

2. **If the new procedure increases operational costs** → Conduct a cost-benefit analysis before proceeding. Quantify staff time, training, and any other resource requirements.

3. **Either way** → Monitor service scores during rollout to confirm the effect replicates.

---

## **Why We're More Confident Than Expected**

The board expected no difference. The data shows otherwise. The key reason to trust this result:

- **Pre-switch**: Treatment branches scored ~1 point **below** control branches
- **Post-switch**: Treatment branches scored ~1 point **above** control branches

This 2-point reversal at the exact moment of the procedure change is unlikely to be coincidence. The statistical model confirms this (t-statistic = 5.3).
