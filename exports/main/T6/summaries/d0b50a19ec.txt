## **Important Caveat: Parallel Trends Violation**

The difference-in-differences method requires that treatment and control groups were on **parallel trends before the intervention**. My analysis shows this assumption is **violated**:

| Week | Treatment Score | Control Score | Gap |
|------|-----------------|---------------|-----|
| 6 | 55.8 | 55.0 | 0.8 |
| 10 | 53.8 | 52.1 | 1.7 |
| 14 | 50.4 | 47.0 | 3.4 |

**The gap was already widening by 0.32 points per week BEFORE the switch (p < 0.001).** This means switched branches were already outperforming non-switched branches and pulling away before the new procedure was implemented.

---

## **Revised Bottom Line**

**The evidence is suggestive but not conclusive.** Branches that adopted the new procedure showed improved service scores relative to non-adopters. However, because the two groups were already diverging before the switch, we cannot confidently attribute the full 1.9-point difference to the new procedure alone.

**What we can say:**
- Switched branches improved slightly (+0.7 points) after adoption
- Non-switched branches declined (-1.2 points) during the same period
- The new procedure is **associated with** better outcomes, but selection bias may explain part of this effect

**What we cannot say:**
- The exact causal impact of the procedure (the true effect is likely smaller than 1.9 points)
- That rollout to all branches will produce the same magnitude of improvement

---

## **Revised Recommendation**

**Proceed with caution.** Consider a **phased rollout with rigorous monitoring** rather than immediate system-wide implementation:

1. **Randomize the rollout** to remaining branches to enable cleaner causal inference
2. **Set clear success metrics** and stop/go decision points
3. **Investigate why** certain branches chose to switch early – there may be unobserved characteristics driving both adoption and performance

This is a promising intervention, but the evidence does not support the strong conclusion I initially provided.
