# **Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure**

## **Bottom Line**

**Evidence suggests the new procedure improved service scores, contrary to expectations.** Branches that switched saw scores increase by approximately **2.2 points** (95% CI: 0.9 to 3.5) relative to control branches. The effect is statistically significant but modest in magnitude, and about 30% of switched branches saw little or no improvement. 

**Recommendation**: Proceed with rollout, but implement in phases with close monitoring to confirm benefits persist at scale and to identify which branches benefit most.

## **Key Findings**

### The Numbers
| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|-------|---------------|---------------|--------|
| Switched branches (n=90) | 50.65 | 52.54 | +1.89 |
| Control branches (n=60) | 50.84 | 50.51 | -0.34 |
| **Net effect** | | | **+2.23** |

- **Statistical confidence**: p=0.001 (cluster-robust); 95% CI: 0.9 to 3.5 points
- **Practical magnitude**: ~4% improvement; about 0.2 standard deviations
- **Consistency**: 70% of switched branches improved vs. 43% of control branches

### Important Caveats
- **Not universal**: 27 of 90 switched branches (30%) saw no improvement or decline
- **Most variation unexplained**: Service scores vary widely week-to-week; the procedure explains only a small portion
- **Unexpected finding**: We hypothesized no effect; this result should be validated during broader rollout

### What We Can Rule Out
The improvement is not explained by changes in staffing levels or visitor volumes—neither changed systematically for switched branches.
