# Library Branch Procedure Change: Service Score Analysis

**To:** Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2025  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## Bottom Line

**We cannot determine whether the new procedure improved service scores.** The branches that switched were already improving faster than non-switching branches *before* the change occurred. At the actual switch point, there was no meaningful jump in performance. The observed difference reflects pre-existing trends, not a causal effect of the procedure. **We do not recommend system-wide rollout based on this evidence.**

---

## Key Findings

### 1. No Evidence of Causal Effect

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Gap at week 14 (last week before switch) | +2.55 |
| Gap at week 15 (first week after switch) | +2.62 |
| **Immediate change at switch** | **+0.08** |

The +0.08 point change is negligible and consistent with random variation. There is no discontinuity suggesting the procedure caused improvement.

### 2. Pre-Existing Trend Explains the Pattern

- The gap between switching and non-switching branches was **already growing by +0.32 points per week** before the switch
- If this pre-trend had continued, the expected gap at week 15 would have been +2.87
- The actual gap (+2.62) was slightly *below* this expectation

This indicates switching branches were on a stronger improvement trajectory for reasons unrelated to the procedure—possibly better management, more resources, or other unmeasured factors.

### 3. Why the Standard Analysis is Misleading

A naive difference-in-differences calculation produces an estimate of +1.85 points. However, this method relies on the **parallel trends assumption**—that treatment and control groups would have followed similar trajectories absent the intervention. **This assumption is violated here.** The +1.85 estimate captures pre-existing differences, not the procedure's effect.

### 4. Data Overview

- **709 branches** analyzed (425 switched, 284 did not)
- **17 weeks** of data (weeks 6-22)
- Switch occurred at week 15
- Service scores average ~50 across all branches

---

## Next Steps

### Recommended Actions

1. **Do not proceed with rollout.** The current evidence does not support that the new procedure causes improvement.

2. **Investigate selection bias.** Understand why certain branches were chosen to switch first. Were they:
   - Higher-performing branches to begin with?
   - Better staffed or resourced?
   - Led by more experienced managers?
   - Self-selected volunteers?

3. **Design a proper evaluation:**
   - Randomly assign branches to switch (eliminates selection bias)
   - Collect baseline data for several weeks before any switches
   - Ensure parallel pre-trends before attributing changes to the procedure

4. **Consider alternative explanations.** The switching branches' improvement may reflect:
   - Management quality differences
   - Community demographics
   - Branch size or resource advantages
   - Other concurrent initiatives

5. **If proceeding despite uncertainty:**
   - Implement as a pilot with rigorous monitoring
   - Set clear success criteria in advance
   - Be prepared to discontinue if no effect is observed

---

## Conclusion

The data does not support the conclusion that the new procedure improves service scores. The observed differences reflect pre-existing trends rather than causal effects. Before investing in system-wide implementation, the cooperative should conduct a properly randomized evaluation to determine whether the procedure actually works.
