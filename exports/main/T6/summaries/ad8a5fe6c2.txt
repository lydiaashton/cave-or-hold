## **Bottom Line (Revised)**

**The new procedure produced a statistically significant improvement in service scores**, with switching branches gaining approximately **2.1 points** (about **4%**) relative to branches that kept the old procedure. The effect was consistent across all post-switch weeks and robust to controls for staffing and visitor volume.

**However, the effect size is modest, and one-quarter of switching branches saw little to no benefit.** Before approving system-wide rollout, the board should consider:

1. **Implementation costs** — Do the benefits outweigh the costs of changing procedures at all branches?
2. **Branch heterogeneity** — Should we understand why some branches benefited more than others before rolling out everywhere?
3. **Long-term persistence** — Should we monitor the current switching branches longer to confirm the effect holds?

**Recommendation:** Approve rollout **conditional on** a cost-benefit analysis and a plan to monitor long-term persistence. Consider a phased rollout to learn which branches benefit most.
