# Library Cooperative Shelving Procedure Evaluation
## Board Summary Report

---

## BOTTOM LINE

**The evidence is inconclusive.** The new procedure *may* provide a modest improvement in service scores (estimated 0 to +1.5 points), but we cannot confidently distinguish this from pre-existing differences between branches that chose to switch and those that didn't. 

**Recommendation:** Do not proceed with a blind systemwide rollout. Instead, either (a) gather better evidence through a randomized pilot, or (b) if rollout costs are low, proceed cautiously with continued monitoring to confirm benefits.

---

## KEY FINDINGS

### Study Overview
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Total branches | 709 |
| Branches adopting new procedure | 425 (60%) |
| Branches keeping old procedure | 284 (40%) |
| Observation period | 17 weeks (weeks 6–22) |
| Procedure switch date | Week 15 |
| Service score scale | Average ~50; higher is better |

### What the Data Shows

**1. Surface-Level Result**
- Switching branches showed service scores **+2.1 points higher** after the switch
- 95% confidence interval: +1.4 to +2.8 points
- This would represent a meaningful ~4% improvement if causal

**2. The Complication**
- **Before the switch**, switching branches were already improving faster than others
- The performance gap widened by **0.31 points per week** during the pre-switch period
- This suggests switching branches differed systematically from non-switching branches

**3. Adjusted Estimates (Accounting for Pre-Trends)**
| Method | Effect Estimate | 95% CI | Interpretation |
|--------|-----------------|--------|----------------|
| Simple comparison | +2.12 pts | [+1.4, +2.8] | Likely **overstates** true effect |
| Pre-trend adjusted | -0.30 pts | [-1.7, +1.1] | Likely **understates** true effect |
| Recent baseline only (weeks 12-14) | +1.22 pts | [+0.3, +2.2] | Most plausible estimate |

**4. Bottom-Line Estimate**
- The true effect likely falls between **0 and +1.5 points**
- This represents **0% to 3%** of the average service score
- We cannot rule out zero effect, but we also cannot rule out a modest positive effect

### Why the Uncertainty?

Branches **self-selected** into the new procedure rather than being randomly assigned. Branches that volunteered to switch were likely already better-managed, better-staffed, or otherwise on an upward trajectory. This **selection bias** makes it impossible to cleanly separate the procedure's effect from pre-existing branch differences.

## RECOMMENDATIONS

### Primary Recommendation

| If rollout costs are... | Then... |
|------------------------|---------|
| **LOW** (minimal training, easy to reverse) | Proceed with cautious rollout; continue monitoring to confirm benefits |
| **HIGH** (extensive training, disruptive, hard to reverse) | Pause; gather better evidence before deciding |

### Options for Better Evidence

1. **Randomized Pilot** (Gold Standard)
   - Randomly assign a subset of remaining branches to adopt the procedure
   - Provides credible causal evidence
   - Can be completed in 8-12 weeks

2. **Extended Monitoring**
   - Continue tracking current switch branches for additional weeks
   - Look for sustained vs. temporary effects
   - Lower cost but still subject to selection bias

3. **Qualitative Assessment**
   - Survey staff on operational efficiency, workload, and satisfaction
   - Gather patron feedback on service experience
   - May reveal benefits not captured in service scores

4. **Cost-Benefit Analysis**
   - Quantify training, implementation, and ongoing costs
   - Weigh against the estimated 0 to +1.5 point benefit
   - Determine if modest gains justify the investment

---

## METHODOLOGICAL NOTE

This analysis used **difference-in-differences** methodology. The key assumption—that treatment and control groups would have followed parallel trends absent the intervention—was **violated** (p < 0.001). When this assumption fails, causal claims become unreliable.

**For future evaluations:** Randomized assignment is strongly preferred. When voluntary adoption is necessary, collect sufficient pre-intervention data to validate parallel trends, or use methods that explicitly model pre-existing differences.
