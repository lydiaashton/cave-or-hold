# **Memorandum to the Board**

**Subject:** Analysis of New Branch Shelving and Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure is associated with higher service scores, but the board should weigh this against implementation costs before deciding on rollout.**

The data shows a **statistically significant 2-point improvement** in service scores at branches that adopted the new procedure. However, this outcome was not anticipated, and the decision to expand should consider factors beyond this analysis.

---

## **Key Findings**

### What the Data Shows

| Finding | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Estimated effect** | +2.03 service score points |
| **Statistical confidence** | 95% CI: 1.15 to 2.90 (does not include zero) |
| **P-value** | < 0.001 |
| **Effect size** | 0.2 standard deviations (~4% of baseline) |
| **Post-switch observation period** | 8 weeks |

### What Changed When

| Group | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Difference |
|-------|---------------|---------------|------------|
| Switched branches (n=90) | 51.1 | 53.6 | +2.5 |
| Non-switched branches (n=60) | 52.3 | 52.8 | +0.5 |
| **Net effect** | | | **+2.0** |

### Why This Analysis Is Credible

- **Parallel pre-trends**: Both groups moved together before the switch (p = 0.84)
- **Controls applied**: Results account for staffing, visitor volume, and branch-specific factors
- **Consistent signal**: Effect persists across the 8-week post-switch period

---

## **Important Considerations**

### Reasons for Caution

1. **Unexpected finding**: This procedure change was not designed to affect service scores. The mechanism is unclear and warrants investigation.

2. **Limited follow-up**: Only 8 weeks of post-implementation data. Effects could fade, grow, or prove unstable.

3. **Small-to-moderate effect**: 2 points on a ~50-point scale is meaningful but not transformative.

4. **Unknown costs**: This analysis does not address implementation costs, staff training time, or operational burden.

5. **No control group post-rollout**: If all branches switch, we lose the ability to monitor ongoing effects.

### Reasons for Confidence

1. **Statistically robust**: Multiple model specifications yield consistent results.

2. **Valid comparison**: Pre-treatment trends were parallel between groups.

3. **Practical significance**: A 4% improvement in service quality may matter to patrons.

---

## **Recommended Next Steps**

| Action | Rationale |
|--------|-----------|
| **Do not rush a decision** | Unexpected results deserve careful review |
| **Quantify implementation costs** | Compare against the 2-point benefit |
| **Interview staff at switched branches** | Understand what changed operationally |
| **Consider a phased rollout** | Switch remaining branches in stages to monitor effects |
| **Track additional metrics** | Staff satisfaction, operational efficiency, patron feedback |
| **Extend observation period** | Confirm effect persists beyond 8 weeks |

---

## **Conclusion**

The analysis provides credible evidence that the new procedure improved service scores by approximately 2 points. Whether this justifies system-wide adoption depends on:

- Implementation and ongoing operational costs
- Staff capacity and training requirements
- Board priorities regarding service quality vs. other goals

We recommend the board discuss these tradeoffs before authorizing rollout.

---

*Analysis based on 2,550 branch-week observations using difference-in-differences regression with branch fixed effects. Technical documentation available upon request.*
