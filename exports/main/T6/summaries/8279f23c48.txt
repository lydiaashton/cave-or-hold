## **Bottom Line**

The new procedure is associated with **higher service scores**, but the evidence is **less definitive than expected**. 

| Finding | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| **Observed improvement** | +2.2 points for switched branches |
| **Accounting for pre-existing trends** | Likely **+0.4 to +1.2 points** true effect |
| **Statistical certainty** | Pre-trends weaken causal confidence |
| **Recommendation** | **Proceed with caution** – pilot expansion, not full rollout |

---

## **What We Found**

Branches that switched to the new procedure showed a 2.2-point service score increase relative to non-switching branches. **However**, the analysis revealed a complication:

**The treatment-control gap was already widening before the switch.** In the 9 weeks prior to implementation, switching branches were already improving relative to control branches by about 0.2 points per week. This suggests branches that volunteered to switch may have been more motivated or better-managed from the start.

| Interpretation | Estimated Effect |
|----------------|------------------|
| Simple comparison (not recommended) | +2.2 points |
| Adjusted for pre-trend | **+0.4 to +1.2 points** |

---

## **Key Concerns**

1. **Parallel trends assumption violated**: The standard DiD method assumes treatment and control groups would have followed similar paths without the intervention. Our data shows they were already diverging.

2. **Selection bias likely**: Branches that chose to switch may differ systematically from those that didn't (e.g., more engaged leadership, better resources).

3. **True effect uncertain**: The actual causal impact is probably positive but smaller than the raw 2.2-point estimate.

---

## **Recommendation**

**Do not proceed with immediate system-wide rollout.** Instead:

| Action | Rationale |
|--------|-----------|
| **Expand pilot to 50–100 additional branches** | Randomly assign to get cleaner causal evidence |
| **Require random assignment** | Eliminates selection bias |
| **Monitor for 8–12 weeks** | Confirm effect persists and grows |
| **Revisit full rollout decision** | Based on randomized evidence |
