## **Why We Recommend Proceeding**

1. **Directional hypothesis confirmed:** The Board expected improvement, and the data supports this (96% confidence the effect is positive).

2. **Consistency matters:** All 8 post-implementation weeks showed positive effects. Random noise typically doesn't persist in one direction.

3. **Clean study design:** Control branches showed no improvement while pilot branches did, isolating the procedure as the likely cause.

4. **Low downside risk:** The procedure doesn't require additional staffing or resources. If the effect is smaller than expected, the loss is minimal.

5. **Practical significance:** A 2.5-point gain on a 50-point scale represents meaningful service improvement for patrons.

---

## **Uncertainties to Acknowledge**

- There remains approximately a **4% chance** the observed improvement is due to random variation
- The true effect could be **smaller than 2.5 points** (possibly near zero)
- We have only **8 weeks of post-implementation data**; long-term effects are unknown
- Effects at scale may differ from the pilot (20 branches)

---

## **Recommendations**

### **Proceed with Rollout**
1. Approve implementation at remaining 14 branches
2. Use a **phased approach** (3–4 branches per week) to manage risk
3. Complete rollout within 8–12 weeks

### **Monitor Closely**
1. Track service scores weekly during rollout
2. Set a **trigger threshold**: if any branch drops 3+ points, pause and investigate
3. Report to the Board after full rollout with 12 weeks of system-wide data

### **Success Criteria**
- **Met:** System-wide average increases by 1.5+ points within 12 weeks
- **Exceeded:** System-wide average increases by 2.5+ points
- **Not met:** No measurable improvement; reconsider procedure

## **Conclusion**

The evidence is **suggestive but not definitive**. However, given the directional confirmation, low implementation risk, and potential patron benefit, we recommend proceeding with a monitored rollout rather than waiting for more certainty.
