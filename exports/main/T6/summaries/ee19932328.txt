# **REVISED MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

---

## **New Shelving Procedure: Impact on Service Scores**

---

### **BOTTOM LINE**

**The evidence suggests a modest improvement, but with important caveats.** Branches that adopted the new procedure showed higher service score gains than control branches, but the treatment branches were already outperforming controls before the switch. After accounting for this pre-existing trend, the new procedure likely improved scores by **1 to 1.5 points** (approximately 2–3%), with the effect becoming clearer 3–4 weeks after implementation.

**Recommendation: Proceed with caution.** The new procedure appears beneficial, but the evidence is not as strong as a simple comparison suggests. Consider a phased rollout with continued monitoring.

---

### **KEY FINDINGS**

**Study Overview**
- 709 branches: 425 adopted the new procedure, 284 kept the old procedure
- 17 weeks of data (weeks 6–22); switch occurred in week 15

**Critical Observation: Pre-Existing Differences**

Treatment branches were already gaining on control branches *before* the switch:

| Period | Treatment Avg | Control Avg | Gap |
|--------|---------------|-------------|-----|
| Weeks 6–10 | 51.2 | 49.8 | 1.4 |
| Weeks 11–14 | 54.2 | 51.6 | 2.6 |
| Weeks 15–17 | 53.0 | 50.1 | 3.0 |
| Weeks 18–22 | 54.7 | 50.5 | 4.2 |

The gap was widening throughout the study period. The first week after the switch (week 15) actually showed a *smaller* gap than week 14.

**Estimated Impact**

| Method | Estimated Effect |
|--------|------------------|
| Simple difference-in-differences | +2.1 points |
| Adjusted (using recent pre-trend) | +1.4 points |
| Effect in weeks 18–22 only | +1.5 to +2.0 points |

The true effect is likely in the **1 to 1.5 point range**, becoming apparent only after branches had 3–4 weeks to adjust.

**Why the Uncertainty?**

1. **Non-parallel pre-trends**: Treatment branches were already improving relative to controls before the switch
2. **Delayed effect**: No measurable impact in weeks 15–17; effect emerged in weeks 18+
3. **Control branch decline**: Control branches' scores dropped after week 14, which may reflect external factors unrelated to the procedure

---

### **NEXT STEPS**

1. **Approve a phased rollout** rather than system-wide implementation. Start with 50–100 additional branches and monitor closely.

2. **Set realistic expectations**. Expect 1–1.5 point improvements, not 2+ points. Allow 4 weeks per branch for the effect to materialize.

3. **Investigate why treatment branches were already outperforming.** Were they systematically different (e.g., better staffed, more resources)? This matters for predicting rollout success.

4. **Understand the control branch decline.** Scores dropped ~1 point after week 14. Was this seasonal? Staffing changes? Visitor patterns? Addressing this could improve all branches.

5. **Continue rigorous monitoring.** Track service scores weekly during rollout. Be prepared to pause or adjust if results don't match expectations.
