# **Board Summary: New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure Evaluation**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence is mixed and inconclusive.** While the initial analysis suggested a 1.9-point improvement, a more rigorous examination reveals that branches selected for the new procedure were already on different performance trajectories before the switch. When we compare only the weeks immediately surrounding the change, the effect shrinks to **0.6 points and is not statistically significant** (p=0.10).

**Recommendation: Do not proceed with full rollout yet. Gather more data and consider a randomized evaluation.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Initial Analysis Was Misleading

| Analysis | Estimated Effect | Statistical Significance |
|----------|-----------------|-------------------------|
| Full-sample DiD (all 17 weeks) | +1.89 points | p < 0.001 ✓ |
| Limited-window DiD (weeks 12-18 only) | +0.60 points | p = 0.10 ✗ |

The large effect from the full sample appears to be driven by pre-existing differences between branches, not the procedure itself.

### 2. Critical Problem: Parallel Trends Violated

The difference-in-differences method requires that treatment and control branches were on similar trajectories before the switch. **This assumption is violated:**

| Week (relative to switch) | Difference from Control | Significant? |
|---------------------------|------------------------|--------------|
| Week -9 | +0.50 | No |
| Week -8 | -0.11 | No |
| Week -7 | **+6.23** | **Yes** |
| Week -6 | **+2.33** | **Yes** |
| Week -5 | **+1.62** | **Yes** |
| Week -4 | +0.05 | No |
| Week -3 | **+4.01** | **Yes** |
| Week -2 | **+5.68** | **Yes** |

**5 of 8 pre-switch weeks** show statistically significant differences between the groups. This means branches selected for the new procedure were systematically different from control branches—even before the change.

### 3. What We Can Say

- All post-switch coefficients are positive (ranging from +1.6 to +8.7 points)
- This is encouraging but **cannot be confidently attributed to the procedure** given the pre-existing differences
- The true effect could be smaller than estimated, zero, or even negative

### 4. Why This Matters

Branches were not randomly assigned to switch. The 425 branches that adopted the new procedure may differ from the 284 control branches in unobserved ways (management quality, staff experience, local demographics, etc.). These differences—not the procedure itself—may explain the observed gap.

---

## **Next Steps**

### Recommended Actions

1. **Pause the full rollout** until we have more credible evidence of the procedure's effect.

2. **Collect additional data** over more weeks to see if the post-switch gap persists or narrows as pre-existing differences fade.

3. **Consider a randomized evaluation** for the remaining 284 branches—randomly assign half to adopt the procedure and half to continue as controls. This would provide much more credible causal evidence.

4. **Investigate branch selection criteria**—understand why certain branches were chosen for early adoption. This may reveal confounding factors.

5. **If proceeding despite uncertainty**, implement a **phased rollout with rigorous monitoring**:
   - Track service scores monthly for each branch
   - Set a minimum effect threshold (e.g., +1.0 point sustained over 3 months)
   - Be prepared to pause or reverse if effects don't materialize

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did service scores improve after the switch? | Yes, but we can't be sure why |
| Was the improvement caused by the new procedure? | **Uncertain**—pre-existing differences confound the analysis |
| Should we roll out to all branches now? | **No**—evidence is too weak to justify system-wide change |
