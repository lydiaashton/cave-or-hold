# **Board Memorandum**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 2025  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Branches that adopted the new procedure show higher service scores, but we cannot confidently attribute this to the procedure itself.** The observed 1.8-point difference is statistically significant, **however**, branches that switched were already improving faster than other branches before the change. This suggests the new procedure may have helped, but the evidence is not strong enough to justify system-wide rollout without further testing. **We recommend a controlled pilot expansion before full implementation.**

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. Observed Difference**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Switched branches (after) | 52.4 |
| Non-switched branches (after) | 48.9 |
| **Raw difference** | **+3.5 points** |
| **Adjusted difference (DiD)** | **+1.8 points** |

### **2. Critical Limitation: Pre-Existing Trends**
Before the switch (weeks 6-14), the two groups were already diverging:

| Group | Weekly Trend (Pre-Switch) |
|-------|---------------------------|
| Switched branches | **+0.16 points/week** |
| Non-switched branches | **-0.07 points/week** |

This difference is **statistically significant (p = 0.0004)**. In plain terms: branches that chose to switch were already on an upward trajectory, while non-switched branches were flat or declining.

### **3. What This Means for the 1.8-Point Estimate**
Because the groups were not on parallel paths before the switch:
- The 1.8-point estimate **likely overstates** the true effect
- Some or all of the improvement may reflect **pre-existing differences** between branches (e.g., management quality, staffing stability, community engagement)
- We **cannot isolate** the procedure's effect from these other factors

### **4. What the Data Does Show**
- Switched branches are performing better after adoption
- The gap has not narrowed—it has continued to widen
- No negative effects were observed
- The procedure appears safe to implement

---

## **Recommendations**

### **Do Not Proceed with Full Rollout Yet**
Given the uncertainty about causality, a system-wide rollout risks:
- Implementing a procedure that may not deliver expected benefits
- Disruption costs without guaranteed returns
- Missing the opportunity to learn what actually drives improvement

### **Instead, Pursue Rigorous Testing**
1. **Randomized expansion**: Randomly assign remaining branches to adopt now vs. later. This creates comparable groups and enables causal inference.

2. **Extended monitoring**: Continue tracking all branches for 12+ more weeks to see if trends persist or converge.

3. **Investigate selection factors**: Analyze whether switched branches differ systematically (size, location, budget, staff tenure). This may reveal what actually drives improvement.

4. **Implementation fidelity check**: Verify the new procedure is being applied consistently across switched branches.

### **Decision Timeline**
- **3 months**: Complete randomized pilot with 50-100 additional branches
- **6 months**: Re-evaluate with stronger causal evidence
- **12 months**: Make rollout decision based on robust findings

---

## **Conclusion**

The new procedure is **promising but unproven**. The observed improvement is real, but we cannot confirm the procedure caused it. A more rigorous evaluation will protect the cooperative from investing in changes that may not deliver results, while still allowing us to capture benefits if the procedure does work.
