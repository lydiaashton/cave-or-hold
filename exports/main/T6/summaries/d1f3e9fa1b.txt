# **Library Branch Rotation & Shelving Procedure: Impact Analysis**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**We found an unexpected 2.5-point improvement in service scores** at branches that adopted the new procedure. However, given that we anticipated no effect, this result warrants careful interpretation before committing to system-wide rollout.

**Recommendation: Proceed cautiously.** Consider a phased expansion with continued monitoring, rather than immediate full rollout. Investigate *why* scores improved before scaling.

---

## **Key Findings**

### The Numbers

| Group | Pre-Switch | Post-Switch | Change |
|-------|-----------|-------------|--------|
| New Procedure (90 branches) | 50.21 | 52.55 | +2.34 |
| Old Procedure (60 branches) | 49.82 | 49.64 | -0.18 |

**Estimated Effect: +2.5 points (95% CI: 1.2 to 3.8)**

### What Supports This Result

- **Parallel pre-trends**: Treatment and control groups tracked together for 9 weeks before the switch (p=0.49 for difference in pre-switch means)
- **Statistically significant**: p < 0.001
- **Consistent direction**: Treatment group outperformed control in all 8 post-switch weeks

### What Gives Us Pause

- **Unexpected finding**: We hypothesized no effect; finding one requires extra scrutiny
- **Modest effect size**: 2.5 points is ~5% of the baseline—meaningful but not transformative
- **Limited post-switch data**: Only 8 weeks; we don't know if this persists
- **Week 19 anomaly**: Both groups spiked unusually high that week, which may affect estimates
- **Possible Hawthorne effect**: Staff may have temporarily increased effort due to awareness of the change

---

## **Interpretation**

The data suggest the new procedure *may* have a positive effect on service scores, but we cannot rule out:

1. **Temporary effort effect** – staff tried harder during the transition
2. **Unmeasured confounders** – something else differed between groups post-switch
3. **Regression to the mean** – though pre-trends argue against this

The fact that we expected no effect makes this finding both interesting and uncertain.

---

## **Next Steps**

### Recommended Approach: Cautious Expansion

1. **Expand to 20–30 additional branches** over the next 8 weeks (not all 60 at once)
2. **Continue monitoring** service scores with the same methodology
3. **Qualitative investigation**: Survey staff at treatment branches to understand *why* service may have improved
   - Is it faster material retrieval?
   - More time for patron interaction?
   - Better organization overall?

### Decision Points

- **If effect persists** at 3-month mark with expanded sample → proceed to full rollout
- **If effect diminishes** → the improvement may have been temporary; reconsider rollout priorities
- **If mechanism is identified** → refine the procedure to maximize the benefit

### What We Need From the Board

- Approval for **phased expansion** (not full rollout)
- Resources for **staff surveys/interviews** to understand the mechanism
- Commitment to **3-month review** before final rollout decision

---

## **Bottom Line for the Board**

We tested a procedural change expecting neutral impact. Instead, we found a modest but statistically significant improvement in service scores. Before committing to system-wide rollout, we recommend:

1. **Verify the effect persists** with additional branches
2. **Understand why it happened** through staff feedback
3. **Then decide** on full adoption

This is a promising finding—but unexpected results deserve careful verification before scaling.
