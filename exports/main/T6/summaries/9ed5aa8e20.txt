# **Library Cooperative Procedure Change: Impact Analysis**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The new procedure is associated with improved service scores, but we cannot definitively prove causation.** Branches that adopted the new procedure showed service scores that were **2.1 points higher** than expected based on control branch trends (p = 0.001). However, because branches were not randomly assigned to switch, unobserved factors could explain part of this difference. The board should weigh this **modest but statistically significant improvement** against implementation costs and operational considerations before deciding on a full rollout.

---

## **Key Findings**

### Study Design
- **150 branches**: 90 adopted the new procedure, 60 kept the old procedure
- **17 weeks of data**: 9 weeks before (weeks 6–14), 8 weeks after (weeks 15–22)
- **Important limitation**: Branches were not randomly assigned to switch; this is observational data

### Results

| Group | Before | After | Change |
|-------|--------|-------|--------|
| Control (old procedure) | 49.55 | 48.87 | −0.68 |
| Switched (new procedure) | 49.36 | 50.78 | +1.42 |
| **Difference-in-Differences** | | | **+2.10** |

- **Estimated effect: +2.10 points** (95% CI: 0.83 to 3.38)
- **P-value: 0.001** (statistically significant)
- **Effect size: 4.3%** improvement from baseline, or about 21% of one standard deviation

### Strengths of the Evidence
✓ Switched and control branches had **similar scores before the switch** (difference of −0.19, p = 0.89)  
✓ Effect is **statistically significant** at the 1% level  
✓ Effect is **robust** when controlling for staffing and visit volumes  
✓ Effect is **consistent** across all 8 post-switch weeks (all positive)

### Limitations
⚠️ **Not randomized**: Branches self-selected or were selected to switch; unobserved differences could explain results  
⚠️ **Modest effect**: 2 points on a ~50-point scale may or may not justify rollout costs  
⚠️ **Short timeframe**: Only 8 weeks of post-switch data; long-term effects unknown

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Consider a phased rollout** rather than immediate system-wide adoption, allowing for continued monitoring.

2. **Investigate why some branches switched and others didn't**—if switched branches were systematically different (e.g., more motivated staff, better management), this could explain the results.

3. **Calculate implementation costs** and compare against the value of a 4% service score improvement.

4. **Gather qualitative feedback** from the 90 switched branches about operational impacts beyond the service score.

5. **Continue monitoring** to assess whether the effect persists over time.
