# **REVISED BOARD MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure  

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The evidence is inconclusive.** While branches that adopted the new procedure have higher service scores than control branches, this difference largely reflects pre-existing trends rather than the procedure itself. Treatment branches were already improving relative to control branches *before* the switch occurred. We **cannot confidently attribute the improvement to the new procedure** and do not recommend system-wide rollout based on this evidence alone.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### 1. **Pre-Existing Trends Undermine Causal Claims**

The fundamental assumption for causal inference—that treatment and control branches were on parallel trajectories before the intervention—is **violated**:

| Week | Gap (Treatment − Control) |
|------|---------------------------|
| Week 6 | 0.01 |
| Week 10 | 0.40 |
| Week 14 (last pre-switch) | 1.64 |

The gap widened by **1.63 points before the switch ever happened**. This suggests branches selected for the pilot were already on an upward trajectory.

### 2. **Post-Switch Patterns Match Pre-Existing Trends**

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Simple DiD estimate | +1.89 points |
| Pre-trend bias | ~1.63 points |
| **Adjusted estimate** | **~0.26 points** |

The post-switch gap evolution closely matches what the pre-trend would have predicted. By week 22, the expected gap (if pre-trend continued) was 3.28 points; the actual gap was 3.31 points.

### 3. **What We Can Say**

- Treatment branches *do* have higher scores than control branches in the post-period (average gap: 2.27 points)
- This difference is statistically significant
- **However**, we cannot determine how much (if any) is caused by the new procedure versus pre-existing differences

### 4. **Likely Explanation: Selection Bias**

Branches were not randomly assigned to adopt the new procedure. Early adopters may have been:
- Better-managed branches
- Branches with more engaged staff
- Branches already experiencing improvement
- Branches with different patron demographics

---

## **RECOMMENDATIONS**

### **Do Not Proceed with System-Wide Rollout Yet**

The current evidence does not support a confident conclusion that the new procedure causes improvement.

### **Next Steps**

| Action | Rationale |
|--------|-----------|
| **1. Randomized rollout** to remaining branches | Random assignment would eliminate selection bias and provide clean causal evidence |
| **2. Continue monitoring** current pilot branches | Track whether the gap stabilizes, grows, or shrinks over more weeks |
| **3. Qualitative investigation** | Interview staff at switched vs. control branches to understand operational differences |
| **4. Re-analyze in 8-12 weeks** | More post-switch data may clarify whether the procedure has delayed effects |

---

## **METHODOLOGICAL NOTES**

- **Design**: Difference-in-differences with 709 branches over 17 weeks
- **Problem**: Parallel trends assumption violated (pre-switch divergence)
- **Implication**: Cannot isolate causal effect of the procedure from selection effects
- **Solution needed**: Randomized assignment or natural experiment

---

**Bottom line:** The new procedure is *associated* with higher scores, but we cannot say it *causes* higher scores. A randomized rollout would resolve this uncertainty within 2-3 months.
