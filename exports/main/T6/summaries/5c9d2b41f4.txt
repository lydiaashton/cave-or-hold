## **REVISED BOTTOM LINE**

**The evidence is inconclusive.** While branches that adopted the new procedure did see service score improvements, **these branches were already outperforming the control group before the switch occurred.** This violates the key assumption needed to claim the new procedure *caused* the improvement.

The observed 2-point gap could reflect:
1. **Selection bias:** Branches chosen for the new procedure may have been on an upward trajectory already
2. **Mean reversion:** Underperforming branches naturally improving over time
3. **A genuine treatment effect:** The new procedure may still help, but we cannot isolate its impact from pre-existing trends

**Recommendation:** **Do not proceed with a full rollout yet.** Instead:
1. Run a **randomized pilot** at additional branches to establish causal evidence
2. Or, implement more gradually with careful monitoring and a control group
3. Investigate *why* treatment branches were improving before the switch

### **What Changed in My Assessment**

My initial analysis used a standard difference-in-differences model, which assumes treatment and control branches would have followed parallel trends absent the intervention. The event study **rejects this assumption** — the groups were diverging before week 15.

This doesn't prove the new procedure *doesn't* work. It means we **cannot confidently claim it does** based on this data alone.
