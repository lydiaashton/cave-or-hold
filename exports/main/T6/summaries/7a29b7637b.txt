## **Bottom Line**

**We cannot definitively conclude the new procedure caused the improvement.** While switched branches outperformed after the change, they were already on a stronger trajectory before switching. The observed 2-point gap increase may reflect the procedure's effect, pre-existing differences, or both. 

**Recommendation:** Proceed with rollout **but with tempered expectations** and rigorous monitoring to isolate the true effect.

## **Key Findings**

### **What the Data Shows**

| Metric | Result |
|--------|--------|
| Switched branches (before → after) | 50.75 → 52.14 (**+1.39**) |
| Non-switched branches (before → after) | 48.85 → 48.27 (**-0.58**) |
| **Difference-in-differences** | **+1.9 points** |
| **Statistical significance** | p < 0.001 |

### **The Critical Caveat: Pre-Existing Trends**

The gap between groups was **already widening before the switch**:

| Week | Gap (Switched − Non-Switched) |
|------|-------------------------------|
| 6 | 0.2 pts |
| 10 | 2.0 pts |
| 14 (last pre-switch week) | 2.6 pts |
| 18 | 3.8 pts |
| 22 | 4.9 pts |

The gap grew by **2.4 points** before the switch and **2.3 points** after. This pattern suggests the divergence may have continued regardless of the procedure change.

### **What This Means**

| Scenario | Plausibility |
|----------|--------------|
| Procedure caused full 1.9-pt gain | Uncertain |
| Procedure caused partial gain (~1 pt) | Plausible |
| Procedure had no effect; trends continued | Plausible |
| Procedure had negative effect masked by trends | Unlikely but possible |

---

## **Why This Happened**

The 425 branches selected for early adoption differed systematically from the 284 that weren't. They were already improving faster before the procedure change. This is a common challenge in non-randomized rollouts.

---

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with rollout, but:**

1. **Set modest expectations** — The true effect may be smaller than 1.9 points, possibly zero.

2. **Track each branch's before/after change** — Focus on within-branch improvement rather than treatment vs. control comparisons.

3. **Identify non-responders early** — Branches that don't improve after switching may need additional support or the procedure may not work for them.

4. **Plan a formal evaluation in 6 months** — With all branches switched, compare actual performance to pre-switch projections.
