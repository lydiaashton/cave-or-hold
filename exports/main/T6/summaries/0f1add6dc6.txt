# **REVISED MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** [Current Date]  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure  

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence is inconclusive.** While branches that switched to the new procedure have higher service scores, this appears to reflect **pre-existing differences** between branches rather than a causal effect of the procedure change. The branches that chose to switch were already trending upward relative to others before the intervention. **We cannot confidently attribute the score difference to the new procedure.**

**Recommendation: Do not proceed with full rollout yet.** Instead, conduct a randomized trial to properly isolate the procedure's effect.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. The Critical Problem: Non-Parallel Pre-Trends

| Week | Treatment - Control Gap |
|------|------------------------|
| 6 | -0.11 |
| 10 | +0.92 |
| 14 (last week before switch) | +2.00 |

**The gap was already widening by 2.1 points BEFORE the switch.** This violates the core assumption needed to claim the procedure caused the improvement.

### 2. No Immediate Effect at Switch Point

| Transition | Gap Change |
|------------|------------|
| Week 14 → Week 15 | -0.08 points (gap actually narrowed) |
| Weeks 13-14 avg → Weeks 15-16 avg | +0.21 points |

If the procedure had a real effect, we would expect a noticeable jump at week 15. There isn't one.

### 3. What the 2-Point "Effect" Actually Represents

The difference-in-differences estimate of +2.03 points largely captures:
- **Pre-existing branch differences** (treatment branches were already ~0.8 points higher)
- **Continuation of pre-switch divergence trend** (gap was already growing ~0.23 points/week)
- **Unknown factors** affecting control branches negatively after week 14

### 4. Control Group Decline Is Unexplained

Control branches declined by 1.15 points after week 14, but overall system-wide scores remained stable (50.99 before vs 51.06 after). Something specific affected control branches, but we don't know what.

### 5. Branch Selection Bias Is Likely

Branches self-selected into the new procedure (425 switched, 284 did not). Branches that chose to switch may differ systematically:
- More motivated leadership
- Better baseline operations
- Different community demographics
- Greater capacity for change

---

## **Recommendation**

**Do not proceed with system-wide rollout based on current evidence.**

The observed association between the new procedure and higher scores cannot be distinguished from pre-existing branch differences. Rolling out system-wide based on this analysis risks:
- Implementing a procedure that may not actually improve service
- Unnecessary disruption and training costs across 709 branches
- Missing the opportunity to identify what actually drives service score improvements

---

## **Next Steps**

| Action | Rationale | Timeline |
|--------|-----------|----------|
| **Randomized controlled trial** | Randomly assign 100-200 branches to switch; this eliminates selection bias | 4-8 weeks to design |
| **Qualitative investigation** | Interview switched vs non-switched branches to understand why some chose to switch and what else differs | 2-4 weeks |
| **Identify confounding factors** | Analyze what distinguishes branches that chose to switch (size, location, staffing, community) | 2-3 weeks |
| **Re-analyze with better controls** | Use matching or instrumental variables to account for selection | 3-4 weeks |

### If Board Wishes to Proceed Despite Uncertainty

If the Board decides the potential benefit justifies the risk:

1. **Phased rollout with embedded evaluation** - Continue tracking scores and stop if no improvement emerges
2. **Target branches similar to early adopters** - Don't assume the effect generalizes to all 709 branches
3. **Set clear success metrics** - Define what improvement level would justify continued investment
4. **Budget for reversal** - Be prepared to revert if the procedure proves ineffective

---

## **Appendix: Why the Initial Analysis Was Misleading**

The difference-in-differences method requires that treatment and control groups would have followed **parallel trends** absent the intervention. This data shows they did not:

- Pre-switch gap trend: **+2.11 points** over 9 weeks
- Post-switch gap trend: **+1.71 points** over 8 weeks

The post-switch divergence is actually **slower** than the pre-switch divergence. The statistical significance of the DiD coefficient reflects the precision of the estimate, not the validity of the causal claim.
