# **Revised Memorandum to the Board**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** [Current Date]  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure — **Inconclusive Results**

---

## **Bottom Line**

**Do not proceed with systemwide rollout based on current evidence.** The apparent 2-point improvement cannot be confidently attributed to the new procedure. Statistical testing reveals that branches selected for the new procedure were already improving faster than control branches *before* the switch. After accounting for this pre-existing trend, the estimated effect of the procedure itself is approximately **0.16 points** — statistically indistinguishable from zero.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **Critical Issue: Parallel Trends Assumption Violated**

Difference-in-differences analysis requires that treatment and control groups follow similar trends before the intervention. **This assumption is violated in our data:**

| Test | Result |
|------|--------|
| Pre-trend differential | +0.24 points/week (p = 0.0004) |
| Trend over 8 weeks | Would explain 1.91 points of the "effect" |
| Raw DiD estimate | 2.07 points |
| **Adjusted estimate** | **0.16 points** |

### **What the Gap Data Actually Shows**

The gap between treated and control branches was widening steadily before week 15:

| Period | Average Gap |
|--------|-------------|
| Week 6 | 1.54 points |
| Week 14 | 3.15 points |
| Week 22 | 4.36 points |

When we extrapolate the pre-treatment trend forward, the **actual post-treatment gaps match what we would have predicted without any intervention**:

| Week | Predicted Gap (from pre-trend) | Actual Gap | Difference |
|------|-------------------------------|------------|------------|
| 15 | 3.08 | 3.16 | +0.08 |
| 16 | 3.32 | 2.66 | −0.66 |
| 17 | 3.56 | 4.08 | +0.52 |
| 18 | 3.80 | 4.27 | +0.47 |
| 19 | 4.04 | 3.99 | −0.05 |
| 20 | 4.27 | 4.14 | −0.13 |
| 21 | 4.51 | 4.42 | −0.09 |
| 22 | 4.75 | 4.36 | −0.39 |

**In 5 of 8 post-treatment weeks, the actual gap was BELOW what the pre-trend predicted.**

### **Selection Bias Concern**

Branches that switched to the new procedure:
- Had higher service scores throughout the study (average 52.8 vs. 50.9 before switch)
- Were improving faster even before the intervention
- Were likely **not randomly selected** — possibly higher-performing branches were chosen first

This selection bias makes it impossible to isolate the procedure's true effect from the existing differences between branches.

---

## **Recommendations**

| Priority | Action |
|----------|--------|
| **1** | **Pause systemwide rollout** — Current evidence does not support the expected benefit |
| **2** | **Conduct a randomized trial** — Randomly assign remaining branches to treatment/control to eliminate selection bias |
| **3** | **Extend observation period** — Collect 6+ months of data to assess longer-term patterns |
| **4** | **Investigate selection criteria** — Understand why certain branches were chosen for the initial rollout |
| **5** | **Consider qualitative feedback** — Survey branch staff about perceived benefits/challenges of the new procedure |

---

## **What Would Constitute Convincing Evidence?**

A proper evaluation would require:
1. **Random assignment** of branches to treatment and control groups
2. **Demonstrated parallel pre-trends** between groups before intervention
3. **Sustained effect** over multiple months, not just 8 weeks
4. **Replication** across different types of branches (urban/rural, large/small, etc.)
