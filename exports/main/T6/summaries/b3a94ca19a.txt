## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence suggests the new procedure improved service scores, but with important caveats.** Branches that switched showed approximately **2 points higher service scores** after the change compared to branches that did not. However, because branches were not randomly assigned to switch, and because treatment branches were already trending differently before the switch, we cannot be fully certain the improvement was caused by the procedure itself. **We recommend a cautious rollout with close monitoring**, rather than an immediate system-wide mandate.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. Observed Improvement (With Uncertainty)

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Estimated effect | +1.93 service score points |
| 95% Confidence interval | +0.70 to +3.16 points |
| Statistical significance | p = 0.002 |
| Relative improvement | ~4% above baseline |

The effect is statistically significant, but statistical significance does not guarantee causation in observational data.

### 2. Important Limitation: Pre-Existing Trends

**Treatment branches were already diverging from control branches before the switch:**
- Average difference pre-switch (weeks 6–14): **-0.73 points** (treatment lagging)
- Average difference post-switch (weeks 15–22): **+1.23 points** (treatment ahead)
- Pre-switch trend: Treatment branches were declining relative to control by ~0.22 points/week (p=0.013)

This pattern raises the possibility that some of the observed improvement reflects **mean reversion** (branches returning to their typical performance) rather than a true treatment effect.

### 3. Supporting Evidence

- The improvement persisted across **all 8 post-switch weeks**
- The effect was **not explained by staffing or visitor volume** changes
- Results were consistent whether or not we controlled for other factors

### 4. What We Cannot Rule Out

- Branches that chose to switch may differ systematically from those that did not
- Unobserved factors (management quality, staff morale, local conditions) could explain both the decision to switch and the score improvement
- The true causal effect could be smaller than our estimate—or possibly zero

---

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with a phased rollout rather than immediate system-wide implementation:**

1. **Expand to additional branches** (perhaps 20–30 more) with careful tracking
2. **Consider random assignment** for the next phase to establish causality more confidently
3. **Monitor service scores closely** during expansion to verify the effect replicates
4. **Document implementation fidelity** to ensure branches are actually adopting the new procedure

This approach balances the promising evidence against the uncertainty inherent in observational data.

---

## **Next Steps**

1. **Discuss with the board** whether the observed improvement (even if partially causal) justifies rollout costs
2. **Design a randomized pilot** for the next expansion phase if feasible
3. **Gather qualitative feedback** from switched branches about implementation experience
4. **Re-analyze data** after additional branches adopt the procedure to strengthen evidence
