# **REVISED Board Memorandum**

**To:** Library Cooperative Board of Directors  
**From:** Data Analysis Team  
**Date:** November 15, 2026  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation Procedure

---

## **Executive Summary**

Branches that adopted the new procedure show **higher service scores after the switch**, but we **cannot confidently attribute this improvement to the procedure itself**. The treatment and control branches were on different trajectories *before* the switch occurred, which undermines our ability to isolate the procedure's causal effect.

**Recommendation:** Proceed with caution. The evidence is **suggestive but not conclusive**. Consider a more rigorous evaluation design for future changes.

---

## **Bottom Line**

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Did service scores improve in switching branches? | **Yes** (+1.9 points vs. control) |
| Can we credit the new procedure? | **Uncertain** — pre-existing trends confound the result |
| How confident are we? | **Moderate** — association is clear, causation is not |
| Should we roll out to all branches? | **Proceed, but monitor closely** |

---

## **Key Findings**

### **1. The Raw Numbers Look Positive**
- Treatment branches: 50.61 → 52.58 (+1.96 points after switch)
- Control branches: 49.33 → 49.44 (+0.11 points after switch)
- **Simple difference-in-differences: +1.85 points**

### **2. BUT: The Parallel Trends Assumption Is Violated** ⚠️

The fundamental requirement for causal inference is that treatment and control groups would have followed similar trends *absent the intervention*. **This assumption fails in our data:**

| Period | Treatment vs. Control Gap |
|--------|---------------------------|
| Week 6 | -2.65 points (treatment behind) |
| Week 10 | -0.69 points |
| Week 13 | +0.25 points (treatment ahead) |
| Week 14 | +0.33 points |
| Week 15 | +0.53 points (switch occurs) |
| Week 22 | +1.20 points |

**The treatment group was already gaining ~0.32 points/week on the control group BEFORE the switch.** This pre-existing trend (p < 0.001) means the observed post-switch improvement may reflect continuation of this pattern rather than the procedure's effect.

### **3. What This Means**
- The ~1.85 point DiD estimate likely **overstates** the true treatment effect
- Some portion of the improvement would have occurred anyway
- We cannot determine how much is attributable to the procedure vs. other factors (e.g., treatment branches may have had more motivated management, better resources, or other unobserved advantages)

---

## **Revised Recommendation**

| Action | Rationale |
|--------|-----------|
| **Proceed with rollout** | The association is positive; no evidence of harm; branches chose to adopt early suggesting perceived benefit |
| **Monitor outcomes closely** | Track whether late-adopting branches show similar gains; watch for branches that underperform |
| **Document implementation factors** | Capture what distinguishes branches with stronger vs. weaker improvements |
| **Use randomized rollout for future changes** | If operationally feasible, randomize adoption order to enable cleaner causal inference |

---

## **Conclusion**

The data shows that branches adopting the new procedure experienced service score improvements relative to those that did not. However, because early-adopting branches were already on an upward trajectory before the switch, we cannot definitively conclude the procedure caused the improvement.

The board should interpret the 1.85-point estimate as an **upper bound** on the true effect. The actual causal impact is likely smaller, though probably still positive given the continuation of gains post-switch.

**Proceed with rollout, but temper expectations and invest in ongoing monitoring.**
