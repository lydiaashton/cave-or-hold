# **REVISED MEMORANDUM TO THE BOARD**

**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure  
**Date:** November 15, 2026

---

## **BOTTOM LINE**

**The new procedure has a measurable positive effect, but the board should consider costs before approving full rollout.** Branches using the new procedure maintained service scores that were **~2 points higher** than comparable branches using the old procedure. This effect is statistically reliable (p < 0.01). However, the improvement is modest, and we lack cost data to determine whether the benefit justifies systemwide adoption.

---

## **KEY FINDINGS**

### The Numbers

| | Before Week 15 | After Week 15 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| **New Procedure** (90 branches) | 49.6 | 48.9 | −0.7 |
| **Old Procedure** (60 branches) | 50.6 | 48.0 | −2.6 |
| **Net Effect** | | | **+1.9** |

### What This Means

1. **The new procedure did not increase scores in absolute terms** — both groups saw declines after week 14. The new procedure simply **reduced the magnitude of decline**.

2. **The effect is statistically significant but modest**:
   - 95% confidence interval: +0.7 to +3.0 points
   - Effect size: approximately 4% of the average score
   - Consistent across multiple analytical approaches

3. **Pre-switch trends were similar** between groups, supporting the validity of the comparison.

4. **We do not know why scores dropped for everyone** after week 14. This could reflect seasonal patterns, budget cycles, or other external factors.

---

## **RECOMMENDATION**

**Proceed cautiously.** The evidence supports a positive effect, but before committing to full rollout:

1. **Obtain cost data** — If the new procedure requires additional staff time, training, or equipment, calculate whether a ~2-point service score improvement justifies the expense.

2. **Consider a phased rollout** — Expand to the remaining 60 branches in stages while monitoring outcomes, rather than all at once.

3. **Investigate the week-14 decline** — Understand why service scores dropped across all branches before making major procedural changes.
