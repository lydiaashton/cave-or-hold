## **REVISED ASSESSMENT**

### **Key Concern: Pre-Existing Trends**

The week-by-week data reveals a problem for our analysis:

| Week | Treatment - Control Difference |
|------|-------------------------------|
| 6 | -0.83 |
| 8 | +0.82 |
| 10 | +1.06 |
| 13 | +1.52 |
| 14 | +2.51 |

**Before the switch even happened**, treatment branches were already gaining ground on control branches—the gap widened by **3.3 points** during the pre-period alone. This violates the "parallel trends" assumption that DiD relies on.

**What this means**: Some (possibly much) of the 2.2-point "effect" may reflect a pre-existing upward trend in treatment branches, not the new procedure itself.

---

### **Additional Concern: Week 15 Anomaly**

Week 15 (the first post-switch week) shows an unusual spike for **both** groups:
- Control: jumped from ~50 to 58.3, then dropped back to 49.5 in week 16
- Treatment: jumped from 52.4 to 59.96

This suggests an external factor affected all branches in week 15, which complicates interpretation.

---

### **Revised Bottom Line**

**The evidence is suggestive but not conclusive.** The new procedure *may* improve service scores, but we cannot confidently separate the treatment effect from pre-existing trends.

**Recommendation: Proceed with caution.**

---

### **Revised Next Steps**

1. **Do NOT approve immediate system-wide rollout**

2. **Extend the evaluation period** — collect 8-12 more weeks of data to see if the treatment effect persists and widens

3. **Investigate the pre-trend** — why were treatment branches already improving before the switch? Were they selected because they were improving?

4. **Consider a randomized rollout** for remaining branches to enable cleaner causal inference

5. **If proceeding**, do a **phased rollout** with careful monitoring, not a blanket implementation

---

### **Bottom Line (Revised)**

> The data shows switched branches improved more than non-switched branches, **but** treatment branches were already on an upward trajectory before the switch. We cannot confidently attribute the full 2.2-point difference to the new procedure. **Recommend a phased rollout with continued evaluation rather than immediate system-wide implementation.**
