# **REVISED MEMORANDUM**

**TO:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**FROM:** Data Analysis Team  
**DATE:** December 2025  
**SUBJECT:** Evaluation of New Branch Stock Rotation and Shelving Procedure

---

## **Bottom Line**

**The evidence is suggestive but not definitive.** The new procedure is associated with higher service scores, but branches that adopted it early were already improving faster than other branches before the change. After accounting for this, the procedure may have added roughly **0.5 to 1.5 points** to service scores. **We recommend a cautious rollout with close monitoring**, rather than a full immediate deployment.

---

## **Key Findings**

### 1. The Raw Numbers

| Period | Switched Branches | Control Branches | Gap |
|--------|-------------------|------------------|-----|
| Before (Weeks 6–14) | 51.05 | 50.17 | +0.88 |
| After (Weeks 15–22) | 54.75 | 51.92 | +2.83 |
| **Simple difference** | | | **+1.95** |

### 2. The Complication: Pre-Existing Trends

The gap between switched and control branches was **already widening before the switch**:

| Week | Gap (Switched − Control) |
|------|--------------------------|
| 6 | −0.42 |
| 10 | +0.56 |
| 14 (last pre-switch) | +2.09 |
| 15 (first post-switch) | +1.69 |
| 22 (final week) | +3.75 |

**Concern:** Week 15 showed a *smaller* gap than week 14. The simple 1.95-point estimate likely overstates the effect because it compares post-weeks to an early pre-period when switched branches hadn't yet pulled ahead.

### 3. A More Conservative Estimate

Comparing post-switch weeks to the **last pre-switch week** (a better counterfactual given the trend):

- **Average post-week gap minus week 14 gap:** +0.74 points
- **Later weeks only (19–22):** +1.4 points above week 14

This suggests the true effect is likely between **0.5 and 1.5 points** (1% to 3% improvement), not the full 1.95 points.

### 4. Why Branches May Have Been Selected

The cooperative likely chose branches for early adoption based on factors like:
- Management capacity and readiness
- Existing performance trajectories
- Staff availability for training

These same factors may have driven continued improvement, independent of the procedure change.

---

## **Recommendation**

**Proceed with a phased rollout, not a full immediate deployment.**

| Action | Rationale |
|--------|-----------|
| **Expand to 50–100 additional branches** over 4–6 weeks | Test whether similar gains occur in branches not selected for the initial pilot |
| **Track weekly service scores** during rollout | Detect early if effects are weaker than the pilot suggested |
| **Document implementation conditions** at each branch | Identify what enables success (training, staffing, leadership) |
| **Pause and reassess after first phase** | Decide on full rollout based on actual results, not pilot alone |
| **Set realistic expectations** | Anticipate 0.5–1.5 point gains, not 2+ points |

---

## **Bottom Line Restated**

The new procedure probably helps, but we cannot be certain how much. The pilot branches were already on an upward trajectory. A cautious, monitored expansion will tell us whether the procedure itself drives improvement or whether early success reflected branch selection. **Invest in learning before investing in full deployment.**
