# **REVISED BOARD MEMORANDUM**

**To:** Board of Directors, Library Cooperative  
**Subject:** Evaluation of New Branch Rotation and Shelving Procedure  
**Date:** November 2026

---

## **Bottom Line**

**We cannot confidently conclude the new procedure improved service scores.** While branches that switched do show higher scores after week 15, the gap between switching and non-switching branches was already widening **before** the switch occurred. This pattern suggests the observed difference reflects pre-existing trends rather than the treatment effect.

**Recommendation:** Do not proceed with system-wide rollout until we can better isolate the procedure's actual impact.

---

## **Key Findings**

### **The Problem: Pre-Existing Trends**

| Period | Treatment-Control Gap |
|--------|----------------------|
| Week 6 | -0.4 |
| Week 9 | +0.9 |
| Week 14 (last pre-switch week) | +2.1 |
| Week 15 (first post-switch week) | +3.0 |
| Week 22 | +3.8 |

**Critical observation:** The gap grew by **+2.5 points** in the 9 weeks BEFORE the switch, but only **+1.7 points** in the 8 weeks AFTER. The divergence started around week 10—five weeks before any branch changed procedures.

### **What the Pre-Trend Predicts**

If the pre-switch trend had continued unchanged:
- **Predicted gap at week 15-22:** +2.3 to +4.3 points
- **Actual gap at week 15-22:** +2.3 to +3.8 points
- **Result:** Actual gaps are slightly **below** what the pre-trend predicted

This suggests the new procedure may have had **no effect** or even a **slight negative effect** relative to the existing trajectory.

### **Why This Matters**

The difference-in-differences method relies on the assumption that treatment and control branches would have followed parallel trends absent the intervention. **This assumption is violated.** Treatment branches were already pulling ahead before the switch, likely due to unobserved factors (e.g., more motivated staff, better management, or selection bias in which branches volunteered to switch first).

---

## **What We Can Say**

1. **Branches that adopted the new procedure have higher service scores** than those that didn't (+2.9 points on average post-switch).

2. **However, this gap existed before the switch** and was already growing. We cannot attribute it to the procedure itself.

3. **The data does not rule out a positive effect**, but it also does not provide credible evidence of one.

---

## **Next Steps**

| Action | Rationale |
|--------|-----------|
| **Pause the rollout** | Current evidence is insufficient to justify system-wide change |
| **Investigate selection bias** | Determine why some branches switched and others didn't; were they systematically different? |
| **Consider a randomized trial** | Randomly assign branches to switch now vs. later to establish causality |
| **Gather qualitative data** | Interview branch staff about whether they perceive the new procedure as helpful |
| **Re-analyze in 3-6 months** | With more post-switch data, we may be able to better separate treatment effects from pre-trends |

---

## **Methodology Note**

The initial analysis reported a +2.05 point treatment effect. However, this estimate is biased by non-parallel pre-trends. When we extrapolate the pre-switch trend forward, the observed post-switch gaps are actually **smaller than expected** (average -0.37 points below prediction), suggesting the procedure itself may not be driving the improvement.

---

*Analysis based on 12,053 weekly observations from 709 branches, weeks 6-22.*
