gap continued along roughly the same trajectory. There is no clear evidence 
that the procedure itself caused any additional improvement.

Recommendation: Do NOT base a system-wide rollout decision on this evidence.
If the board wishes to proceed, it should be for reasons other than 
demonstrated service score improvements.

================================================================================
KEY FINDINGS
================================================================================

1. WHAT THE DATA SHOWS

   • Treatment branches had higher service scores than control branches 
     throughout the entire observation period
   
   • The gap was WIDENING before the switch:
     - Week 6:  0.2 points
     - Week 14: 2.6 points (just before switch)
     - Week 22: 3.8 points (end of data)
   
   • The post-switch gap follows the same trajectory as the pre-switch trend

2. WHY THE STANDARD ANALYSIS IS MISLEADING

   • The difference-in-differences method assumes treatment and control 
     branches would have followed parallel trends without the intervention
   
   • This assumption is violated: treatment branches were already improving 
     relative to control branches before the procedure changed
   
   • The 1.73 point "effect" likely reflects pre-existing differences in 
     branch performance trends, not the procedure itself

3. WHAT WE CAN CONCLUDE

   • The new procedure did not HARME service scores at adopting branches
   
   • We cannot determine whether it HELPED, because the analysis cannot 
     separate the procedure's effect from pre-existing trends
   
   • Branches selected for early adoption appear to have been on a stronger 
     improvement trajectory regardless of the procedure

4. PRACTICAL IMPLICATIONS

   • A 1.7 point improvement would represent a 3% increase (meaningful but 
     modest) IF it were causal
   
   • However, the evidence does not support a causal interpretation
   
   • Rolling out the procedure system-wide based on this evidence would be 
     premature

================================================================================
NEXT STEPS
================================================================================

1. DO NOT ROLLOUT BASED ON THIS EVIDENCE ALONE

   The data does not demonstrate that the new procedure improves service 
   scores. A system-wide rollout should not be justified by these findings.

2. IF PROCEEDING, TREAT AS A PILOT WITH PROPER EVALUATION

   • Randomly assign remaining branches to new vs. old procedure
   • Or match branches on pre-trends before assigning treatment
   • Collect at least 12-16 weeks of post-implementation data
   • Pre-register the analysis plan to avoid data dredging

3. INVESTIGATE WHY EARLY ADOPTERS WERE DIFFERENT

   • Document the criteria used to select branches for early adoption
   • Understand what made these branches improve faster
   • Consider whether those factors (not the procedure) explain the results

4. CONSIDER OTHER OUTCOMES

   • The procedure may have benefits not captured in service scores
     (staff efficiency, cost savings, patron satisfaction)
   • Evaluate these separately with appropriate methods

METHODOLOGY NOTES
================================================================================

Data:        709 branches, 17 weeks (weeks 6-22), 12,053 observations
Method:      Difference-in-Differences with cluster-robust standard errors
Key Issue:   Parallel trends assumption violated (pre-trends divergent)

The DiD estimator is biased when treatment and control groups have different 
pre-intervention trends. In this case, treatment branches were improving 
faster than control branches even before the procedure change, making it 
impossible to isolate the procedure's causal effect.
